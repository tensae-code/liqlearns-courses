# About the Technical Drawing — Ethiopia course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from a government teacher guide —
Ethiopia's own Ministry of Education Grade 12 Technical
Drawing teacher guide. The one exception is the **Tip
boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the guide's.

## This course stands on a teacher guide alone — said plainly

This course is built from the Grade 12 teacher guide
**alone**. No Technical Drawing student book for Ethiopia
is in hand, and nothing in this course dresses the guide
up as a student book: every lesson's `lesson/sources.md`
carries the book as a teacher guide, with its printed
pages, marked "(teacher guide)".

The build stands on the owner's ruling of 2026-11-24:
teacher guides CAN be used to create courses — Ethiopian
teacher guides explain the student textbook, so a guide
carries the teaching. The lessons here are built from the
guide's own explanations, learning competencies, teaching
notes and printed solution drawings.

**Why this is its own course:** Technical Drawing is a
standalone subject in Ethiopia's own curriculum, with its
own book. Under the owner's standing rule, a subject that
exists as its own book gets its own course and is never
silently folded into another course. Before this build,
this guide's teaching stood only as a named strand inside
the Technology & Design world course; that strand **stays
exactly as it is** (see the overlap note below) — this
course is Ethiopia's own, beside it.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Ethiopia inside the Technical
Drawing folder (one course per country). Inside it, the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's
place is found by what they prove, and each level stands
on the ones before it as a recommended-first path — never
a forced gate. The school grade the book comes from
appears only in each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as
provenance. The five levels follow the guide's own
five-unit progression (see CURRICULUM.md). Lessons teach
through their text first: every picture's and GIF's
teaching is also in the text, so a lesson is fully
learnable by listening to its text read aloud.

## The guide's way — the course's shape (placement 1)

Five units climb the drafter's ladder: free-hand sketching
and visualisation first (Unit 1), then the
descriptive-geometry core — auxiliary views for inclined
surfaces (Unit 2) and sectional views for interiors
(Unit 3) — then dimensioning, the language that makes a
drawing a complete set of instructions (Unit 4), and
finally the sheet-metal geometry of developments and
intersections (Unit 5), which alone takes 21 of the
course's 58 periods and ends in pages of worked solution
drawings. Every lesson in the guide is scripted the same
way — periods (split theory/practice, practice always the
larger share), Learning Competencies, "Planning ahead",
Hints for Teaching, Teaching Notes, checkpoints and
exercises, assessments, and printed solutions. That
demonstrate-then-practise, solution-carrying pattern is
preserved in this course's shape: every lesson opens with
doing, teaches, and ends in a drawing task judged against
a stated standard. The guide's philosophy stands at the
course's head: sketching is essential even for an engineer
stranded alone on an island; it requires no artistic
ability — once the basic techniques are known, the limit
on a sketch is not the ability to draw but the ability to
think through the details of a design.

**The course-intro picture** (teacher guide, printed
pp. 1–105): the five-unit ladder — sketch, auxiliary view,
section, dimension, development — drawn as five
drawing-board sheets. **Course-intro video idea:** one
short script walking the five units in order (described
slot; filming is a later pass).

## Excluded by name

Nothing that teaches was left out. The following are
excluded **as lessons**, by name, because they are
apparatus, answer material or pedagogy for the teacher —
not teaching items for the learner:

- The guide's per-lesson **"Planning ahead"** preparation
  lists and **"Hints for Teaching"** scripts (teacher
  apparatus; their demonstrate-then-practise pattern is
  carried in the course shape above, not stocked as
  lessons).
- The guide's **assessment scripts and marking
  administration** as standalone items — except where the
  marking standard itself teaches, which IS stocked: the
  isometric-sketch marking scale (50/25/20/5) stands inside
  the Pictorial Sketching lesson, and Unit 3's continuous
  assessment against a minimum level stands inside the
  Conventional Practice lesson.
- The **answer keys as bare keys**: exercise answers are
  stocked only where the worked drawing itself teaches —
  the solution plates (placement 18) are a lesson,
  "Worked Developments — The Answer Plates", because they
  are the course's visible drawing standard.
- The **Reference bibliography** (placement 19) is filed
  above as course About material, not built as a lesson.
- Front matter (title, acknowledgements/copyright,
  foreword, contents pages) — apparatus only.

## Overlap with Technology & Design — noted, not merged

The Technology & Design world course holds a Technical
Drawing strand (`technology-and-design/technical-drawing/`)
in which this same guide's placements were stocked earlier
as world-course lessons, merged with other countries'
books (for example "Free-hand Sketching — Principles and
Place in Design" there stands on several countries'
books). That strand **stays as-is — nothing in it was
deleted or edited by this build.** The overlap is real and
is stated here and in `technical-drawing/README.md`: the
same Ethiopian guide feeds both courses — the world course
by subject, this course by country, as Ethiopia's own
standalone subject. Lesson titles coincide where the thing
taught is the same; the courses are different shelves, and
each lesson's sources say which shelf it stands on.
