# Surface Development — Flat Sheets That Become Things

## Do it first

Before you read, try the lesson's task once with what you already know: Choose one sheet-made object at home. Sketch its flat development with fold lines drawn, and name the hems or joints its edges would need. Keep your first attempt — you will compare it with your work at the end.

## ℹ Info

Surface development lays a solid's surface out flat. Many objects — cardboard and metal boxes, tin cans, funnels, cake pans, furnace pipes, elbows, ducts and roof gutters — are made from flat sheet material folded, formed or rolled into shape, and sheet-metal workers work from developments.

## What development is for

A development is the flat pattern of a solid's surface: cut it out, fold, form or roll it, and the solid stands up. Learn the use of surface development through the things made this way all around you — boxes, cans, funnels, pans, pipes, elbows, ducts and gutters.

## Surfaces, solids, hems and joints

Identify the different types of surfaces and solids — the shape of the solid decides how it can be developed. Identify also the types of hems and joints used in sheet-metal drawing: the edges of the flat pattern are finished and joined in set ways, and the pattern must allow for them.
