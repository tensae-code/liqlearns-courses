# Hands-on — Surface Development — Flat Sheets That Become Things

The lesson with your hands, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Choose one sheet-made object at home. Sketch its flat development with fold lines drawn, and name the hems or joints its edges would need.

## Show it with real things
Work the lesson's drawing task on real paper, naming each step aloud as you do it: what you are drawing, why this step comes here, and what the finished drawing must show. Compare the result against the lesson's one thing: Surface development lays a solid's surface out flat. Many objects — cardboard and metal boxes, tin cans, funnels, cake pans, furnace pipes, elbows, ducts and roof gutters — are made from flat sheet material folded, formed or rolled into shape, and sheet-metal workers work from developments.

## Check it for one day
For one day, look for this lesson's teaching in real made things around you — drawings, packages, sheet-metal objects, buildings. Each time you see it, note what you saw and what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
