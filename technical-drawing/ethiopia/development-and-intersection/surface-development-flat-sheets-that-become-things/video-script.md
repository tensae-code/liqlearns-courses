# Video script — Surface Development — Flat Sheets That Become Things

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The lesson is text-first: this script says nothing the lesson text does not already teach.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Surface development lays a solid's surface out flat. Many objects — cardboard and metal boxes, tin cans, funnels, cake pans, furnace pipes, elbows, ducts and roof gutters — are made from flat sheet material folded, formed or rolled into shape, and sheet-metal workers work from developments.

Watch the drawing being made, step by step, in the order the lesson gives.

A development is the flat pattern of a solid's surface: cut it out, fold, form or roll it, and the solid stands up. Learn the use of surface development through the things made this way all around you — boxes, cans, funnels, pans, pipes, elbows, ducts and gutters.

Identify the different types of surfaces and solids — the shape of the solid decides how it can be developed. Identify also the types of hems and joints used in sheet-metal drawing: the edges of the flat pattern are finished and joined in set ways, and the pattern must allow for them.

Remember it this way: Surface development lays a solid's surface out flat. Many objects — cardboard and metal boxes, tin cans, funnels, cake pans, furnace pipes, elbows, ducts and roof gutters — are made from flat sheet material folded, formed or rolled into shape, and sheet-metal workers work from developments.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
