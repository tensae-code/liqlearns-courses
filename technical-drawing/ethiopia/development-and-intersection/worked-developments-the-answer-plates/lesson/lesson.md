# Worked Developments — The Answer Plates

## Do it first

Before you read, try the lesson's task once with what you already know: Take one development you drew earlier in this level and lay it beside the matching solution plate's method. List every difference you find — stations, fold lines, true lengths — and redraw yours to the plate's standard. Keep your first attempt — you will compare it with your work at the end.

## ℹ Info

The teacher guide prints full-page worked solution drawings, lettered A to J, as the answers to the development exercises. They are the only place the course's expected drawing standard is fully visible, and they set the accuracy bar for all the development work in this level.

## Reading the plates

Read the solutions as drawings, not pictures: solution B shows developments of hexagonal prisms with their fold lines and numbered stations; solution C shows a transition development built by projecting a grid from the elevation, with a divided circle below giving the true lengths; solution D shows a pointed solid's development with numbered division points around its plan circle.

## The harder plates

Solution G shows a cone development carrying an intersecting solid, its intersection curve constructed by concentric arcs. Solutions H and I show sheet-metal developments of duct-like solids with diamond-shaped cutouts and mating pieces. The remaining lettered solutions are in the same family. Compare your own developments from the earlier lessons in this level against these plates: same stations, same fold lines, same standard.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Draw first, judge after. A drawing you can compare against the lesson's standard teaches more than a perfect copy — keep every attempt and mark what changed.
