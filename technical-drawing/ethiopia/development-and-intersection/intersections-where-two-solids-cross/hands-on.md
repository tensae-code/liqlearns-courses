# Hands-on — Intersections — Where Two Solids Cross

The lesson with your hands, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a cylinder piercing a prism. Find the piercing points, draw the line of intersection showing hidden parts correctly, then develop one of the solids with the intersection curve in its pattern.

## Show it with real things
Work the lesson's drawing task on real paper, naming each step aloud as you do it: what you are drawing, why this step comes here, and what the finished drawing must show. Compare the result against the lesson's one thing: Where two solids meet, their surfaces cross in a line of intersection that must be found before either can be made: find the piercing points and the visible and hidden lines of intersection, determine the line of intersection of two solids such as prisms and cylinders, and carry the intersection curve into the development of each solid.

## Check it for one day
For one day, look for this lesson's teaching in real made things around you — drawings, packages, sheet-metal objects, buildings. Each time you see it, note what you saw and what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
