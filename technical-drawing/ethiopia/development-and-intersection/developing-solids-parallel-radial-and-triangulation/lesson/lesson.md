# Developing Solids — Parallel Line, Radial Line, Triangulation

## Do it first

Before you read, try the lesson's task once with what you already know: Develop three solids, one by each method: a prism by parallel lines, a cone by radial lines, and one oblique solid by triangulation. Label the method on each pattern. Keep your first attempt — you will compare it with your work at the end.

## ℹ Info

Three construction methods develop any solid: parallel-line development for prisms and cylinders, radial-line development for pyramids and cones, and the rule of true length by triangulation for solids whose edges no view shows truly.

## Parallel-line development

For platonic solids with parallel edges — prisms and cylinders — lay the surface out by parallel lines: stations along a stretch-out line, each station carrying its true height. Perform it on different types of prisms and cylinders.

## Radial-line development

For pyramids and cones, develop from an apex: the surface sweeps open around the apex point by radial lines. Perform radial-line development for pyramids and cones, keeping every edge at its true length from the apex.

## Triangulation and true length

Some solids have edges that no view shows at true length. The rule of true length by triangulation finds each edge's true length first, then builds the surface triangle by triangle. Ten of this lesson's twelve periods are drawing practice — the methods are learned by the hand.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Draw first, judge after. A drawing you can compare against the lesson's standard teaches more than a perfect copy — keep every attempt and mark what changed.
