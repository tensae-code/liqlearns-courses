# Lesson plan — Developing Solids — Parallel Line, Radial Line, Triangulation

**Course:** Technical Drawing — Ethiopia · **Level folder:** Level 5 — Development and Intersection · **Lesson:** Developing Solids — Parallel Line, Radial Line, Triangulation
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 5 — Development and Intersection (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Three construction methods develop any solid: parallel-line development for prisms and cylinders, radial-line development for pyramids and cones, and the rule of true length by triangulation for solids whose edges no view shows truly.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 5 — Development and Intersection (recommended, never forced).
**Guide time for this lesson:** 12 periods (2 theory, 10 practice) (teacher guide, printed pp. 87-89) — in the guide, practice takes the larger share wherever the lesson is split.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The lesson teaches through its text first: every picture's and GIF's teaching is also in the text, so the lesson is fully learnable by listening to its text read aloud.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Three construction methods develop any solid: parallel-line development for prisms and cylinders, radial-line development for pyramids and cones, and the rule of true length by triangulation for solids whose edges no view shows truly.
2. Do the lesson's drawing task (Do It Yourself, below) to the standard the lesson sets.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper (grid paper where the lesson says so), a pencil, and a ruler for checking — the drawing itself is free-hand or construction as the lesson directs. Nothing else.

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- IMAGE — a picture of three solids — prism, cone, oblique pyramid — each beside its development → the student studies the picture and names what it shows, part by part, in the lesson's own words.
- GIF — the movement named in the lesson text, shown moving → the student watches, then describes the movement in words before drawing.
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later) → the student listens and answers along. The lesson is fully learnable from its text alone; the video adds nothing the text does not teach.

## If the learner is stuck

Work the Do it first act again, slowly, naming each step aloud as you draw it.
