# Video script — Conventional Practice in Sectioning — The Agreed Exceptions

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The lesson is text-first: this script says nothing the lesson text does not already teach.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Beyond the standard types there are other sectional view representations, and a conventional representation of section: agreed exceptions and simplifications — parts and features conventionally not section-lined or drawn uncut — that keep engineering drawings consistent.

Watch the drawing being made, step by step, in the order the lesson gives.

Some interiors are better shown by other sectional view representations than by the standard types. Learn when they are needed and use them when they are.

By convention, certain parts and features are not section-lined, or are drawn uncut, even when the cutting plane passes through them. These are not mistakes: they are agreed exceptions every reader of drawings expects, and applying them in your own section drawings keeps your drawings consistent with everyone else's. In this unit the teacher assesses each student's work continuously over the whole activity, against the described minimum level, with extra help for those below it — check your own work against that minimum as you go.

Remember it this way: Beyond the standard types there are other sectional view representations, and a conventional representation of section: agreed exceptions and simplifications — parts and features conventionally not section-lined or drawn uncut — that keep engineering drawings consistent.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
