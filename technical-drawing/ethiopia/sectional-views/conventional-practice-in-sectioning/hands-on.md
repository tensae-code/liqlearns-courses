# Hands-on — Conventional Practice in Sectioning — The Agreed Exceptions

The lesson with your hands, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a section of an assembly and deliberately leave the conventionally uncut parts unhatched. List the parts you left uncut and the reason the convention leaves them so.

## Show it with real things
Work the lesson's drawing task on real paper, naming each step aloud as you do it: what you are drawing, why this step comes here, and what the finished drawing must show. Compare the result against the lesson's one thing: Beyond the standard types there are other sectional view representations, and a conventional representation of section: agreed exceptions and simplifications — parts and features conventionally not section-lined or drawn uncut — that keep engineering drawings consistent.

## Check it for one day
For one day, look for this lesson's teaching in real made things around you — drawings, packages, sheet-metal objects, buildings. Each time you see it, note what you saw and what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
