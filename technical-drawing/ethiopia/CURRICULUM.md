# Technical Drawing — Ethiopia course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Ethiopia inside the Technical Drawing folder,
organised by **levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when
they have proved the level, not when a school calendar says
so. The school grade the book comes from appears only in
each lesson's sources (provenance), never as the course's
organisation.

**How the levels derive:** the course stands on one book —
Ethiopia's Grade 12 *Basic Technical Drawing* **teacher
guide** (first edition, 2003 E.C.) — and its ladder is the
guide's own progression, its five units in order: free-hand
sketching and visualisation first; then the
descriptive-geometry core, auxiliary views for inclined
surfaces and sectional views for interiors; then
dimensioning, the language that makes a drawing a complete
set of instructions; and finally the sheet-metal geometry
of developments and intersections, the unit the guide
spends the most time on (21 of the course's 58 periods).
Five levels, in the book's own order. The ladder is a
recommended path, never a forced gate: every lesson stands
alone for a learner who lands on it directly.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from a government teacher guide —
Ethiopia's own Ministry of Education Grade 12 Technical
Drawing teacher guide. No student book for this subject is
in hand, and the course says so plainly: by the owner's
ruling of 2026-11-24, Ethiopian teacher guides can be used
to create courses, because the guide explains the student
textbook and so carries the teaching. The one exception to
"the lessons are the book's" is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in
a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are
ours; the lessons are the guide's. See ABOUT.md for the
full book record and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **Level 1 — Sketching and Visualisation** — 3 lessons:
   Free-hand Sketching — Principles and Place in Design;
   Sketching Techniques — Lines, Circles and Proportion;
   Pictorial Sketching — Isometric Sketching Someone Else
   Could Build From.

2. **Level 2 — Auxiliary Views** — 3 lessons: Orthographic
   Projection — Points, Lines, Planes and Surfaces; Drawing
   an Auxiliary View — The True Shape of an Inclined
   Surface; Secondary Auxiliary Views — Projecting a
   Projection.

3. **Level 3 — Sectional Views** — 3 lessons: Sectioning —
   Words and Common Practice; Types of Section — Choosing
   and Drawing the Right Cut; Conventional Practice in
   Sectioning — The Agreed Exceptions.

4. **Level 4 — Dimensioning** — 4 lessons: Dimensioning —
   Lines, Symbols and Reading Direction; Size and Location
   Dimensions — The Theory; Arranging Dimensions — The Two
   Methods; Placing Dimensions — Diameters, Radii, Holes
   and Notes.

5. **Level 5 — Development and Intersection** — 4 lessons:
   Surface Development — Flat Sheets That Become Things;
   Developing Solids — Parallel Line, Radial Line,
   Triangulation; Intersections — Where Two Solids Cross;
   Worked Developments — The Answer Plates.

**The whole course stands at 17 lessons.**
