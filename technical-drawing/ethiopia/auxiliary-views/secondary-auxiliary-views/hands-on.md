# Hands-on — Secondary Auxiliary Views — Projecting a Projection

The lesson with your hands, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Work one oblique-faced object through the full chain: principal views, primary auxiliary view, secondary auxiliary view. Circle the view where the true shape finally appears.

## Show it with real things
Work the lesson's drawing task on real paper, naming each step aloud as you do it: what you are drawing, why this step comes here, and what the finished drawing must show. Compare the result against the lesson's one thing: For a surface inclined to all the principal planes — an oblique surface — even a first auxiliary view does not show the true shape. A secondary auxiliary view, projected again from the primary auxiliary view, gives the true shape no earlier view can show.

## Check it for one day
For one day, look for this lesson's teaching in real made things around you — drawings, packages, sheet-metal objects, buildings. Each time you see it, note what you saw and what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
