# Drawing an Auxiliary View — The True Shape of an Inclined Surface

## Do it first

Before you read, try the lesson's task once with what you already know: Draw one object with an inclined face in its principal views, then construct its auxiliary view step by step and label the face that now shows its true shape. Keep your first attempt — you will compare it with your work at the end.

## ℹ Info

To draw an auxiliary view of a whole object, project the object's features onto a plane parallel to its inclined surface; the drawing then describes the true shape of that surface.

## Placing the auxiliary plane

Set the auxiliary plane parallel to the inclined surface whose true shape you need. Parallel is the whole secret: only a plane parallel to a surface receives that surface's projection at its true size and shape.

## Projecting the object onto it

Project the object's features, point by point, onto the auxiliary plane, carrying distances truly with folding and reference lines from the principal views. The result is the auxiliary view drawing of the object, with the inclined surface shown truly.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Draw first, judge after. A drawing you can compare against the lesson's standard teaches more than a perfect copy — keep every attempt and mark what changed.
