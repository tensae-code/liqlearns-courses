# Hands-on — Drawing an Auxiliary View — The True Shape of an Inclined Surface

The lesson with your hands, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw one object with an inclined face in its principal views, then construct its auxiliary view step by step and label the face that now shows its true shape.

## Show it with real things
Work the lesson's drawing task on real paper, naming each step aloud as you do it: what you are drawing, why this step comes here, and what the finished drawing must show. Compare the result against the lesson's one thing: To draw an auxiliary view of a whole object, project the object's features onto a plane parallel to its inclined surface; the drawing then describes the true shape of that surface.

## Check it for one day
For one day, look for this lesson's teaching in real made things around you — drawings, packages, sheet-metal objects, buildings. Each time you see it, note what you saw and what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
