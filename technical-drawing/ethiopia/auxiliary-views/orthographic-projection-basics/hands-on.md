# Hands-on — Orthographic Projection — Points, Lines, Planes and Surfaces

The lesson with your hands, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Take an object with one slanted face. Draw its three principal views and point to where the slanted face is distorted in each. Then state, in one sentence, what an auxiliary view would do about it.

## Show it with real things
Work the lesson's drawing task on real paper, naming each step aloud as you do it: what you are drawing, why this step comes here, and what the finished drawing must show. Compare the result against the lesson's one thing: Orthographic projection shows an object in principal views, but when an object has inclined or oblique planes or features, no principal view shows their true shape. The auxiliary view is the special orthographic view that answers this, built with folding lines and reference lines from the projection of points, lines and planes in space.

## Check it for one day
For one day, look for this lesson's teaching in real made things around you — drawings, packages, sheet-metal objects, buildings. Each time you see it, note what you saw and what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
