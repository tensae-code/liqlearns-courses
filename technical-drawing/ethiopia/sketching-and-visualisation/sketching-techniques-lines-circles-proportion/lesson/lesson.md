# Sketching Techniques — Lines, Circles and Proportion

## Do it first

Before you read, try the lesson's task once with what you already know: Sketch a straight line and mark its midpoint by eye, then check it. Draw one circle by each of the four methods. Then sketch one solid object in two views, keeping the proportions the same in both. Keep your first attempt — you will compare it with your work at the end.

## ℹ Info

The hand skills of sketching are demonstrable procedures: sketch straight lines and long lines free-hand, find the midpoint of a line, draw a circle by the guide's four methods, sketch angles and arcs, lay out a sketch using proportion, and sketch multi-view drawings of three-dimensional objects.

## Lines, midpoints, angles and arcs

Sketch straight lines and long lines free-hand with a steady, single movement of the hand. Find the midpoint of a line by judgement and checking. Sketch angles and arcs the same free-hand way, practising until the hand obeys the eye.

## The four methods of drawing a circle

The guide teaches four methods of drawing a circle free-hand. Practise each one: the point of having four is that different sizes and situations suit different methods, and a sketcher who knows all four is never stuck.

## Proportion and multi-view sketching

Lay out a sketch using proportion: judge each part's size against the whole before drawing it, so the finished sketch is true in its relations even when it is not measured. Then sketch multi-view drawings of 3D objects — the same object shown from more than one side, each view in its place.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Draw first, judge after. A drawing you can compare against the lesson's standard teaches more than a perfect copy — keep every attempt and mark what changed.
