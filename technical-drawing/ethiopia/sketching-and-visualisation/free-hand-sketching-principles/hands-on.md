# Hands-on — Free-hand Sketching — Principles and Place in Design

The lesson with your hands, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Open a notebook page. Sketch one object you know three times, each time thinking through one more detail of it, and write beside the last sketch what the sketch has now fixed about the design.

## Show it with real things
Work the lesson's drawing task on real paper, naming each step aloud as you do it: what you are drawing, why this step comes here, and what the finished drawing must show. Compare the result against the lesson's one thing: Free-hand sketching is a fast form of graphical communication: it organises thoughts, generates ideas, records dimensions and transfers information, and it is the beginning phase of design, where a design's final geometric parameters and constraints are first fixed. It needs no artistic ability — once the basic techniques are known, the limit on a sketch is not the ability to draw but the ability to think through the details of a design.

## Check it for one day
For one day, look for this lesson's teaching in real made things around you — drawings, packages, sheet-metal objects, buildings. Each time you see it, note what you saw and what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
