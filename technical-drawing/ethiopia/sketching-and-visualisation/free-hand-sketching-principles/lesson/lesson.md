# Free-hand Sketching — Principles and Place in Design

## Do it first

Before you read, try the lesson's task once with what you already know: Open a notebook page. Sketch one object you know three times, each time thinking through one more detail of it, and write beside the last sketch what the sketch has now fixed about the design. Keep your first attempt — you will compare it with your work at the end.

## ℹ Info

Free-hand sketching is a fast form of graphical communication: it organises thoughts, generates ideas, records dimensions and transfers information, and it is the beginning phase of design, where a design's final geometric parameters and constraints are first fixed. It needs no artistic ability — once the basic techniques are known, the limit on a sketch is not the ability to draw but the ability to think through the details of a design.

## What sketching is for

Sketching communicates ideas, designs and manufacturing or construction procedures by combining words, numbers and visual images. A designer sketches to think: the sketch organises a thought so it can be examined, changed and shared, long before any instrument drawing is made.

## Sketching inside the design process

Sketching belongs at the beginning of the design process. In that beginning phase the sketch fixes the design's final geometric parameters and constraints — the sizes, shapes and limits everything later must respect. Instrument drawing comes afterwards, to make the settled idea exact.

## Materials and the sketching habit

The lesson covers free-hand sketching materials and their proper use, and starts from the sketching learners have already done. Keep sketch ideas in a notebook: an idea kept can be returned to, compared and improved; an idea not kept is lost.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: Draw first, judge after. A drawing you can compare against the lesson's standard teaches more than a perfect copy — keep every attempt and mark what changed.
