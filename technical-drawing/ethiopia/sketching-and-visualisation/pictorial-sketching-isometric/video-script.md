# Video script — Pictorial Sketching — Isometric Sketching Someone Else Could Build From

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. The lesson is text-first: this script says nothing the lesson text does not already teach.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
A pictorial sketch is good when it carries enough detail that someone else can make a similar object from it: features on every side, basic dimensions and basic material descriptions, drawn as an isometric sketch on isometric grid paper.

Watch the drawing being made, step by step, in the order the lesson gives.

Choose an object and sketch it free-hand with enough detail that someone else can make a similar object. Compare your sketch against a detailed technical sketch of the same kind of object and note what yours is missing.

A complete pictorial sketch shows the features on every side of the object, gives its basic dimensions, and adds basic material descriptions. Use isometric and orthographic graphing paper scaled to the object to keep the sketch true.

The guide marks an isometric sketch out of 100: concepts and principles of isometric sketches 50 points, accuracy 25, line weight, technique and neatness 20, lettering 5. Measurement accuracy and line-work and lettering quality are assessed across the whole unit — judge your own sketch by the same scale before anyone else does.

Remember it this way: A pictorial sketch is good when it carries enough detail that someone else can make a similar object from it: features on every side, basic dimensions and basic material descriptions, drawn as an isometric sketch on isometric grid paper.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
