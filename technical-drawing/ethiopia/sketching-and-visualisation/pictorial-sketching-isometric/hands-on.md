# Hands-on — Pictorial Sketching — Isometric Sketching Someone Else Could Build From

The lesson with your hands, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Choose a small object. Make its isometric sketch on grid paper with features on every side, basic dimensions and a material note. Then mark it yourself out of 100 using the guide's scale above.

## Show it with real things
Work the lesson's drawing task on real paper, naming each step aloud as you do it: what you are drawing, why this step comes here, and what the finished drawing must show. Compare the result against the lesson's one thing: A pictorial sketch is good when it carries enough detail that someone else can make a similar object from it: features on every side, basic dimensions and basic material descriptions, drawn as an isometric sketch on isometric grid paper.

## Check it for one day
For one day, look for this lesson's teaching in real made things around you — drawings, packages, sheet-metal objects, buildings. Each time you see it, note what you saw and what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
