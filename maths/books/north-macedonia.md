### Everything taught (own summary; details from the sight-read pages)

- **Position, movement, comparison of size (pp. 9–23).** The book opens with geometry/measurement, not numbers. On printed p. 10 (sight-read; checker-corrected) position words are taught on a playground scene, in word pairs: ПРЕД/ЗАД (in front of / behind), ПОД/НАД (under / above), ДО/НА/ВО (beside / on / in), ГОРЕ/ДОЛУ (up / down), НАПРЕД/НАЗАД (forwards / backwards), БЛИСКУ/ДАЛЕКУ (near / far). (The page prints ДО, not кај; меѓу does not appear on it; there are no completion sentences.) Comparing by size/length/depth/weight follows (big–small, long–short, deep–shallow, heavy–light), always on pictures.
- **Lines and 2D shapes (pp. 25–32).** Straight vs curved lines; open vs closed lines; then one shape per spread — circle (p. 27 sight-read; checker-corrected: the find-and-colour-the-circles task is task 2 among mixed 2D shapes, and the page's top art is everyday round objects — a hoop, a coin, a target, a bicycle; there is no basketball picture), square, triangle, rectangle; making models/pictures from 2D shapes; flat vs curved surfaces as the bridge to solids.
- **3D shapes (pp. 33–39).** Ball (sphere), cube, cuboid, cylinder, cone — recognised in everyday objects and sorted by surface: the survey spread (p. 33, sight-read) already groups objects under the five solids and classifies solids as having only flat surfaces, only a curved surface, or both flat and curved (cylinder, cone); the block ends with finding the 2D shapes (faces) on 3D shapes.
- **Counting to 10 before any numeral is taught (pp. 40–44).** More/less comparison of pictured sets, counting sets to 10, counting backwards.
- **Numbers 1–5, one at a time (pp. 45–54).** Each number gets its own spread with multiple representations side by side — sight-read for 1 (p. 45, headed "БРОЈОТ ЕДЕН 1"; checker-corrected): a raised finger, one dot on a die face, one tally stroke, and the singleton pictures — a sun, an apple, a butterfly — with the digit traced/written (no balloon and no elephant on the page; the book leans on finger, dice-dot and tally representations throughout). Then writing 1–5, comparing numbers to 5 with the signs taught pictorially through a crocodile (p. 51 sight-read: the worked comparisons 2<4, 2>1, 4=4; the page carries no number line — the 0–5 number line is on printed p. 64 — and no printed rule sentence), and 1 more / 1 less.
- **Addition and subtraction to 5 (pp. 55–61).** Addition is joining pictured sets (sight-read p. 55; checker-corrected: kangaroos 1+1=2 and apples 2+1=3, then ball box-sums; no train carriages and no printed "addend + addend = sum" scaffold). Subtraction's model story is ice-cream scoops, 5−2=3 (p. 58 sight-read; checker-corrected), with take-away tasks of cats leaving a sofa (3−1) and pigeons; there is no cross-out instruction on the page. The block closes by linking addition and subtraction as inverse operations on the same picture (p. 60 sight-read; checker-corrected: parrots in cages, with the written equalities 2+1=3, 3−2=1, 3−1=2 — 1+2=3 does NOT appear).
- **Zero, 6–10, number line (pp. 62–84).** Zero is taught as "nothing": its spread (p. 62 sight-read, headed "БРОЈОТ НУЛА 0"; checker-corrected) shows zero through an empty toy box, a closed fist (0 fingers) and an empty frame, beside the digit 0 — there is no empty plate on the page. A 0–5 number line follows (p. 64). Numbers 6, 7, 8, 9, 10 get the same one-by-one treatment as 1–5, with comparing/adding/subtracting consolidated at 6 (p. 67) and word problems ("story problems") for addition and subtraction to 10 (pp. 80–81), counting-on to a target (p. 82).
- **Operations to 10 consolidated (pp. 85–99).** Number bonds on the opener page itself (p. 85 sight-read: pairs of numbers whose sum is a given number); adding in a different order (commutativity, p. 86); the addition–subtraction link again (p. 88). **Money**: denars introduced as pictures on the money itself (p. 91 sight-read: coins 1, 2, 5, 10 денари and the 10-denar banknote, front and back, with a circle-the-coin matching task), coin values (p. 93) and a school-bazaar buying context (p. 94). **Even and odd to 10** (p. 96) and **doubling and halving to 10** (p. 98 sight-read: doubling as making pairs — socks, aeroplane wings; halving as sharing fairly between two children; closing table links double and half: 0→0, 1→2, 3→6 implied, 4→8, 5→10). A table-reading lesson (p. 99) bridges to data.
- **Numbers to 30, counting to 100, place value (pp. 101–123).** The opener is a full **1–100 hundred square** (p. 101 sight-read, rows colour-banded by ten). Numbers 11–20 ("the second ten"), estimating then counting, and **decomposing into tens and ones** (p. 109 sight-read: "10 единици = 1 десетка (10 Е = 1 Д)"; ten 1-denar coins = one 10-denar coin = one 10-denar note; solved example 1 ten + 1 one = 11 on a Десетки|Единици (tens|ones) chart). Comparing and ordering to 20 and 30, even/odd to 20, skip-counting by 2s to 20 and by 10s to 100, ±2 and ±10 neighbours, counting on/back in tens from any number to 100.
- **Operations to 20 (pp. 124–137).** Addition/subtraction inside the second ten first, then to 20 generally; adding three numbers by first making 10 (p. 128) and grouping when adding (p. 129); **missing-number equations** (p. 134 sight-read: 7+□=12, 12−□=7, □+9=16, 16−□=9, each pictured with dot arrays and checked on a 0–20 number line — pre-algebra in box notation, no letter unknowns). Market word problems close the block (p. 136).
- **Measurement, time and data (pp. 138–158).** Symmetry first (p. 139 sight-read: complete the other half of a car/butterfly across a dashed line of symmetry; judge which dotted lines are lines of symmetry; draw mirror halves on squared paper). Length: estimate then measure, with the body units named and pictured — прст (finger), педа (span), лакт (cubit), стапало (foot), чекор (step) — and an explicit "non-standard units for length" task measuring a goal width in feet and steps and classroom objects in spans (p. 144 sight-read). Capacity and mass are estimate-and-compare work (pp. 145–148). Time: months, days of the week, parts of the day; the analogue clock (p. 152 sight-read: clock has two hands and numbers 1–12; the small/short hand shows the hour, the big/long hand the minutes; the worked clock shows 4:00); duration ("how much time is needed?"). Data: grouping into a **Venn diagram** by one criterion (p. 156 sight-read: sea animals grouped by colour/form/size; triangles grouped in overlapping and separate loops), then **pictogram, bar chart and Carroll diagram** (pp. 157–158; p. 158 sight-read: count-and-compare questions on the data, and completing a Carroll diagram — риба / не е риба (fish / not a fish) × colour splashes).

### Honest negatives — what a Grade 1 book might teach that this one does NOT

Basis: the book's own complete contents (PDF pp. 5–7) contains no entry for any of the following, and none appeared in the openers/sampled interiors. (Contents-based negatives; a stray picture mention inside an unsampled exercise cannot be excluded, but no topic below is *taught* anywhere in the book.)

- **No multiplication or division** in any form — no times tables, no grouping/sharing written as × or ÷. (Doubling and halving to 10/20, pp. 98/112 context, are taught as pairs and fair sharing only.)
- **No fractions** — no halves/quarters notation, no fraction of a shape or quantity as a written fraction. Halving is sharing objects fairly (p. 98).
- **No numbers above 100**, and arithmetic is capped: written addition/subtraction only to 20; numbers to 30 are read, decomposed, compared and ordered but **not added or subtracted in writing**; to 100 there is counting (by 1s on the hundred square, by 10s) only.
- **No standard measuring units** — no cm, m, kg, litre anywhere in the contents: length, capacity and mass use non-standard/body units and direct comparison only.
- **No perimeter or area**, no angle work, no 2D-shape property counts (no "a square has 4 sides/4 corners" lesson in the contents) — shapes are recognised, drawn and sorted, not analysed by sides/vertices.
- **No ordinal numbers as a topic** (no "first, second, third" lesson in the contents).
- **No negative numbers, no decimals, no percentages, no ratio** — as expected at this grade, recorded for completeness of the filing.
- **No digital time** — time is the analogue clock, full hours in the sampled lesson (p. 152); no half/quarter hours appear in the contents.
- **No separate workbook/answers section** — the book ends with the cut-out money appendix (p. 159) and a congratulations page; there are no printed answers.

### Everything taught (own summary)

- **Ratio and proportion (Theme 1, units 1–5).** Ratio of two numbers and of two segments; the value of a ratio; equal ratios, inverse ratio and continued ratio (a : b : c); commensurable and incommensurable segments (the side and diagonal of a square, whose ratio is √2 — stated, not developed). Proportion and its terms; finding an unknown term; proportional segments and the coefficient of proportionality; the geometric mean of two segments (a : x = x : b, x² = ab; sight-read example: 6 is the geometric mean of 4 and 9, printed p. 10). Dividing a segment into equal parts and in a given ratio by construction. **Thales' theorem** on proportional segments, with its converse, and its form inside a triangle; construction of the fourth proportional.
- **Similarity (Theme 1, units 6–9).** Similar figures and similar triangles defined by equal corresponding angles and proportional corresponding sides, similarity coefficient k. First criterion (two equal angles, AA), second and third criteria (proportional sides with included angle; three proportional sides). Perimeters of similar triangles are in the ratio of corresponding sides; **areas are in the ratio of the squares** of corresponding sides (printed p. 35).
- **Right-triangle similarity and Pythagoras (Theme 1, units 10–12).** Euclid's theorems for the right-angled triangle: each leg is the geometric mean of the hypotenuse and its projection on it (a² = cp, b² = cq), and the altitude to the hypotenuse is the geometric mean of the two projections (h² = pq), printed p. 38. **Pythagoras' theorem** stated and proved, with its converse (if a² + b² = c² the triangle is right-angled); finding any side from the other two; applications (diagonals, altitudes, distances, shadow/ladder problems); Pythagorean triples, including the generating forms in the optional material at printed p. 44. Sight-read worked example (printed p. 42): diagonal of a rectangle with sides 6 dm and 11 cm — converted to 60 cm and 11 cm, d² = 60² + 11² = 3721, d = 61 cm (correct; see Confirmed book errors for the one defect on this same page).
- **Population and sample (Theme 1, unit 13).** Population vs sample; why sampling is used; a sample must be **representative**, chosen **randomly**; judging given sampling plans (the bus-stop survey is criticised as unrepresentative); reading survey tables and carrying a sample percentage back to the whole population (e.g. a sample that is 10% of the population, printed pp. 50–51).
- **Linear equations in one unknown (Theme 2, units 1–7).** Equality, equation, unknown, domain of an equation; an **identity** (true for every value) vs a contradictory/impossible equation (true for none), printed p. 58. Equations classified by number of unknowns and by degree — linear (1st), quadratic (2nd), cubic (3rd) are **named only** here. Solution/root; checking a value; **equivalent equations** and the two theorems (add/subtract the same number or expression to both sides; multiply/divide both sides by a non-zero number) with their corollaries (moving terms across, clearing denominators). General form **ax + b = 0** with the three cases (a ≠ 0: unique solution x = −b/a; a = 0, b ≠ 0: no solution; a = 0, b = 0: infinitely many solutions). Word-problem applications with a printed check step.
- **Linear inequalities in one unknown (Theme 2, units 8–12).** Inequality statement vs inequality in an unknown; solution set; **intervals** (open, closed, half-open, infinite rays) and number-line representation; basic inequalities in solved form; theorems on equivalent inequalities, including **reversing the sign when multiplying or dividing by a negative number**; solving linear inequalities; a **system of two linear inequalities** solved as the intersection of the two solution intervals, including the no-solution (empty intersection) case.
- **Linear functions (Theme 2, units 13–17).** Linear function **y = kx + n**: argument and coefficient; the **zero of the function** (the x-value where y = 0, printed p. 106). Graphing from a table of values; checking whether a point lies on the graph; graphs with equal k are **parallel**; graphs with equal n meet the y-axis at the same point (0, n); a function is **increasing** for k > 0, **decreasing** for k < 0, constant for k = 0; solving a linear equation graphically as the x-intercept of its graph.
- **Random events and statistical probability (Theme 2, unit 18).** Certain, impossible and possible (random) events; equally likely outcomes; probability as a number between 0 and 1 (also as %). **Statistical probability** from experiment: sight-read table (printed p. 121) of five series of 100 coin tosses giving relative frequencies 0.52, 0.49, 0.53, … settling near 0.5. (The formula-based **mathematical probability** V(A) = m/n is taught later, in Theme 4 unit 15.)
- **Systems of two linear equations (Theme 3, units 1–7).** A linear equation in two unknowns, ax + by = c; its solution as an **ordered pair**, with infinitely many solutions shown on its graph line. Equivalent equations in two unknowns. A **system** of two such equations and its solution as the common ordered pair. Graphical solving with the three cases (one solution / no solution / infinitely many). Solving by the **substitution method** (unit 5) and by the **opposite-coefficients (elimination) method** (unit 6). Applications: number, money/coin, age and mixture problems (the mixture lesson works with % concentrations, printed p. 150).
- **Dirichlet's principle (Theme 3, unit 8).** The pigeonhole principle used as a proof method: sight-read examples — in a class of 30 pupils whose test errors fall into 9 possible counts (0–8), at least 4 pupils share the same count (30 = 3·9 + 3, printed pp. 154–155); birthdays, initials, divisibility of differences and angle arguments.
- **Space geometry foundations (Theme 4, units 1–5).** Point, line and plane in space; coplanar points/lines; two lines — intersecting, parallel, coincident or **skew**; two planes — intersecting (in a line) or parallel; a line and a plane; a line **perpendicular** to a plane and its piercing point. **Parallel projection and orthogonal projection** of a point, segment and figure onto a plane. Drawing solids (cube, cuboid) on paper.
- **Prism (Theme 4, units 6–9).** Prism and its elements (bases, lateral faces/edges, height); kinds (right/oblique, regular, n-gonal); diagonal sections. Parallelepiped, cuboid and cube, including the cuboid's space diagonal. The **net** of a right prism and its **surface area P = 2B + M** (B base area, M lateral area). **Volume axioms** (positive; congruent solids have equal volumes; volumes add over parts; unit cube), printed pp. 184–185; volume of a cuboid **V = abc** and of a cube **V = a³**; also V = B·H; cubic units and the litre (1 l = 1 dm³). Volume of any right prism, **V = B·H**.
- **Pyramid, cylinder, cone, sphere (Theme 4, units 10–14).** Pyramid and its elements (including the apothem of a regular pyramid); surface area P = B + M; **volume V = ⅓B·H** (printed p. 194). Cylinder: bases, axis, axial section, net; **P = 2Rπ(R + H)**, **V = R²πH**. Cone: generatrix s with s² = H² + R²; net as circle + circular sector; **P = Rπ(R + s)**, **V = ⅓R²πH**. Sphere (surface) vs ball (solid), great circle; **P = 4R²π**, **V = ⁴⁄₃R³π**, with the book's derivation V = ⅓PR (printed p. 204).
- **Mathematical probability (Theme 4, unit 15).** For equally likely outcomes, **V(A) = m/n** (favourable over all possible cases), printed pp. 206–207, applied to dice, cards and spinners; an impossible event has probability 0 and a certain event 1 in the same notation.

### Honest negatives — what a Grade 8 book might teach that this one does NOT

Basis: the four printed contents pages plus full-text searches of the extracted text layer (563,638 characters) for the relevant Macedonian terms, all returning zero or name-only hits as noted.

- **No quadratic equations are solved** — no quadratic formula, no factorising quadratics, no vertex/parabola work. "Квадратна" (quadratic) occurs only where equations are *classified by degree* (Theme 2 unit 2, and the glossary index entries pointing to pp. 60/86); the graph drawn for every function in this book is a straight line.
- **No trigonometry** — no sine, cosine or tangent of an angle anywhere (full-text search: 0 hits for the terms). Right-angled triangles are handled only by similarity, Euclid's theorems and Pythagoras.
- **No circle theorems** — no inscribed/central angle theorems, no tangent properties, no chord theorems. Circles appear only as bases of cylinders/cones, as the great circle of a sphere, and via circumference/area facts used inside the solids formulas.
- **No sequences or series as a topic** — no arithmetic/geometric progressions (the linear function is never re-framed as an arithmetic sequence).
- **No powers/indices or roots as topics** — no index laws, no square-root arithmetic as a skill (√ appears inside Pythagoras/Euclid results), no standard form, and **no negative-numbers topic** (negatives occur inside equations/inequalities only).
- **No statistics beyond sampling and probability** — no mean/median/mode, no spread, no data charts or graphs as a taught skill (survey results stay in tables); probability is the statistical (relative-frequency) form in Theme 2 and the m/n form in Theme 4, with no combined-events rules.
- **No vectors, matrices, logarithms, complex numbers, or calculus** — as expected at this grade, recorded for completeness of the filing.
- **No transformations beyond projection** — no translation/rotation/reflection/enlargement strand; similarity is treated through triangles and Thales, not as a transformation, and congruence appears only in passing (congruent solids in the volume axioms; congruent triangles inside similarity work).
