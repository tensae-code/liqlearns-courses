## The set at a glance

- **One publisher, one language, three stages.** All 21 books are
  Republic of Iraq, Ministry of Education, General Directorate of
  Curricula books, all in Arabic, all distributed free by law (sale
  and trading in markets prohibited — printed on the title pages).
  Grades 1–6 are the primary-stage series (student book + Exercises
  Book per grade); Grades 7–9 are the intermediate stage
  (الأول–الثالث المتوسط, 1st–3rd intermediate year, student book
  only); Grades 10–12 are the preparatory stage, split into a
  **Scientific** track (العلمي) and a **Literary** track (الأدبي)
  from Grade 10 on. No teacher guides exist in the set, and no
  exercises books exist for Grades 7–12.
- **Grades 1–4:** numbers to 9,999,999, addition and subtraction
  with regrouping, multiplication to 2-digit × 2-digit, division by
  a 1-digit number with remainders, fractions then decimals to
  hundredths, angles, congruence and similarity, perimeter and area
  of square and rectangle, picture graphs then bar graphs, and a
  first probability vocabulary (certain / possible / impossible).
  No regrouping in Grade 1; no division until Grade 3; no decimals
  until Grade 4; no money as a topic anywhere in the band.
- **Grades 5–6:** whole numbers to billions, decimals to
  thousandths, fractions and mixed numbers through all four
  operations, primes, squares and cubes, GCD and LCM; then integers
  and all four operations on them, first algebra (order of
  operations, expressions, substitution, one-step equations), ratio,
  percent, proportion and scale, bar and pie charts, outliers, the
  circle's elements, the coordinate plane, and circle circumference
  and area. No negatives until Grade 6; no probability as a number
  P(E) anywhere in the band.
- **Grades 7–9:** integers deepened (powers, scientific notation,
  absolute value, roots), rational numbers and percent in use
  (profit, proportional division), polynomials and functions in
  tables, sets and inequalities, geometry (polygons, solids,
  coordinate plane, transformations, congruence/similarity),
  measurement (volumes, surface areas, scale effects, paving),
  statistics and probability (frequency polygons, stem-and-leaf,
  experimental outcomes, comparing probabilities); then real
  numbers and Pythagoras, factoring including difference of two
  squares, multi-step equations in R, quadratics by square root and
  the zero-product property, cylinder and sphere, linear functions,
  box-and-whisker plots and formal probability P(E) = m/n; then
  relations and inequalities in real numbers, mappings and
  functions, arithmetic sequences, factoring by identities and
  trial, systems of two linear equations, quadratics by completing
  the square and the quadratic formula, coordinate geometry,
  trigonometric ratios, polygons and solids, circles, and
  permutations, combinations and compound events. No trigonometry
  beyond right-triangle ratios; no logarithms, calculus, vectors,
  complex numbers or matrices anywhere in the band.
- **Grades 10–12, Scientific:** mathematical logic, equations and
  inequalities, indices and roots, trigonometry through the unit
  circle and circular applications, plane vectors, coordinate
  geometry, statistics; then logarithms, sequences, conic sections
  (circle only), circular functions, limits and continuity,
  derivatives, space geometry, counting and probability, matrices;
  then complex numbers, conic sections (parabola, ellipse,
  hyperbola), applications of differentiation, integration,
  ordinary differential equations, and space geometry. The calculus
  capstone of the set.
- **Grades 10–12, Literary:** real functions, equations and
  inequalities, trigonometry, coordinate geometry, statistics; then
  logarithms, sequences (with present value, annuity and insurance),
  matrices and determinants, statistics (standard deviation,
  Pearson and Spearman correlation, regression); then counting and
  the binomial theorem, limits and continuity, derivatives
  (including economics applications), and integration (areas only).
  No logic, indices/roots, vectors, circular functions, conics,
  space geometry, complex numbers, differential equations, volumes
  of revolution, or probability anywhere in the Literary track.
- **Track split in one line:** the Literary track never meets logic,
  indices/roots, vectors, circular functions, conic sections, space
  geometry, complex numbers, differential equations, volumes of
  revolution, or probability; the Scientific track never meets
  Spearman rank correlation, regression, present value / annuity /
  insurance, or economics applications of the derivative.
- **No ISBN is printed in any of the 21 books.** Citations use
  country + book + grade + page + edition/year only.
- **Confirmed book errors:** the G3/G4 exercises introductions

## Band C cross-notes (for course building)

### Scientific vs Literary track split

The two tracks share a spine (functions, equations, trigonometry,
coordinate geometry, statistics, logarithms, sequences, matrices,
limits, derivatives, integration) but the Scientific track goes deeper
and wider at every grade, and the Literary track drops whole strands:

| Vectors | Sci only (Ch 5, plane vectors) | — | — |
| Trigonometry | Both (Sci: unit circle, circular applications, calculator; Lit: basic ratios, special angles, unit circle) | Sci only (Ch 4: full circular functions — winding mapping, cot/sec/csc, identities, sum/difference, double/half-angle, trig equations, graphing) | — |

**Track gaps in one line:** the Literary track never meets mathematical
logic, indices/roots, vectors, circular functions, conic sections,
space geometry, complex numbers, differential equations, volumes of
revolution, or probability (it meets counting and the binomial theorem
in G12 instead); the Scientific track never meets Spearman rank
correlation, regression, present value / annuity / insurance, or
economics applications of the derivative (those are Literary-only).

### Cross-grade patterns

- **Logarithms** appear in G11 in both tracks (one chapter each, same
  applications — compound interest, geometric mean, pH, Richter scale,
  rocket speed), then only as the natural logarithm inside G12
  Scientific integration.
- **Sequences** run G9 (arithmetic only) → G11 (both tracks, arithmetic
  + geometric + sums + infinite geometric); the Literary track adds
  financial applications (present value, annuity, insurance) the
  Scientific track does not have.
- **Matrices** run G11 in both tracks (Scientific full, Literary
  partial) and then stop — G12 has no matrices in either track.
- **Counting** runs G9 (permutations / combinations / probability, both
  tracks) → G11 Scientific (counting, permutations, combinations,
  probability, binomial theorem) → G12 Literary (counting, permutations,
  combinations, binomial theorem, no probability). The binomial theorem
  thus appears in G11 Scientific and again in G12 Literary, with the
  same worked example (101³ = 1,030,301) in both books.
- **Statistics thread:** G9 (surveys, permutations / combinations,
  probability) → G10 (central tendency, dispersion, correlation) → G11
  Literary (SD, Pearson, Spearman, regression) / G11 Scientific
  (counting, probability, binomial) → G12 Literary (counting / binomial
  only, no statistics). G12 Scientific has no statistics at all.
- **Calculus thread:** G11 Scientific limits + derivatives → G12
  Scientific applications of differentiation + integration + ODEs; G12
  Literary limits + derivatives + integration (lighter — no trig
  functions, no volumes of revolution, no ODEs).
- **Geometry thread:** G9 coordinate geometry + circle → G10 vectors
  (Scientific only) + coordinate geometry → G11 Scientific circle +
  space geometry → G12 Scientific conics + space geometry. G12 Literary
  has no geometry at all.
- **Shared exercises:** the two G10 books share exercise numbers in
  trigonometry (same r = 30 cm, L = 18.84 cm answers; same mountain
  elevation example, 30° in Literary vs 70° in Scientific). The two G11
  books share the logarithm calculator values (ln7 = 1.945910149 in
  both).
- **English glosses:** every book carries English lesson titles / term
  glosses, and every book has spelling slips in them (see the per-book
  lists). The Literary books have the most (G11 Literary: "sequense",
  "Colum Matrix", "Disperssion", "correlarion", glossary "amatrix",
  "Invers", "Simultaneou", "devintion", "coefficent"; G12 Literary
  glossary: "Margina", "Defenite", "Bionnomial"). The Scientific books
  have fewer but real ones (G10 Scientific: "Analgtic Geometrey",
  "Conorlical Vector"; G9: "Spicial", "Slop", "Probe and Error"). G11
  Scientific had none found.
- **Convention worth noting for course-building:** G12 Literary defines
  convex "محدب" as f″ < 0 and concave "مقعر" as f″ > 0 — the opposite
  of the convention used in many other books. Teach it as the book's
  convention, and do not silently "correct" it.
- **Teaching imprecision worth noting:** G11 Literary (pp. 95–96) states
  that if Δ = 0 a system has no solution — Δ = 0 can also mean
  infinitely many solutions. Teach the fuller statement.

### Gaps across the band (what no book in Band C teaches)

No book in Grades 9–12 teaches: 3D vectors or vector geometry in space
(space geometry is synthetic throughout); probability distributions
(only elementary probability in G9 and G11 Scientific); numerical
methods; linear programming; transformational geometry beyond dilation;
or any statistics in G12 Scientific. The Literary track ends without
ever meeting complex numbers, conics, matrices (beyond G11), or
differential equations.
