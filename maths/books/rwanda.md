### 3. What the pupil's books teach, unit by unit

The two pupil's books teach identical mathematics (twin detail in §5). The summary below is of the shared course, with printed page ranges from the English book (Kinyarwanda ranges in the placements file placements-p2-sb-kin.md).

- **Preliminary Activities (EN pp. 1–6).** Revision of P1: missing numbers in 0–100 grids along odd and even patterns; comparing and ordering two-digit numbers; expanded form (tens + ones) both ways; digits under Tens/Ones; adding and subtracting two-digit numbers in a place-value table (no carrying/borrowing); missing-number sentences; mixed add/subtract with brackets; common differences and missing terms in short patterns; one-step addition word problems; a ×2 / ÷2 table; shading and naming one half and one quarter.
- **Unit 1 — Whole numbers 0–200 (EN pp. 7–48).** Counting, reading and writing 0–200 (collections, backwards counting, 100–200 chart, number lines, numbers in words and figures). Place value H/T/O including abacus work and rebuilding a number from jumbled groups. Comparing with <, >, =; ordering ascending/descending. Addition to a sum ≤ 200: mental, without carrying, with carrying — the standard written (column) method is named and its rule stated (start at ones; write the ones, carry the ten). Addition word problems via "number sentence". Subtraction within 200: mental tens, without borrowing, with borrowing (borrow 1 ten = 10 ones). Subtraction word problems with a vocabulary note (subtract / take away / minus / difference / how many left). Multiplication by 2 and by 3, each built as repeated addition with the full × table, missing-factor items, comparing repeated addition with the product; multiplying a two-digit number by 2 and by 3 in the place-value table; word problems. Division by 2 and by 3 without remainder: equal sharing, ÷ tables, then the standard written (long division) method for two-digit and three-digit dividends, including the rule for when the first digit is too small (take two digits); word problems. End Unit Assessment 1 (pp. 47–48).
- **Unit 2 — Whole numbers 0–500 (EN pp. 49–87).** The same architecture extended: count/read/write 0–500; place value; comparing; ordering; addition (mental / no carry / carry) and subtraction (mental / no borrow / borrow) with sums and minuends ≤ 500 and parallel word problems; then the ×4 and ×5 cycles (tables, two-digit × 4 and × 5, division by 4 and by 5 without remainder by the standard written method, word problems); a combined ×2–×5 table. End Unit Assessment 2 (pp. 85–87).
- **Unit 3 — Whole numbers 0–1000 (EN pp. 88–120).** Count/read/write 0–1000 (the chart work concentrates on 500–1000); place value to 999; comparing; ordering; addition and subtraction within 999 with the same three-step treatment; the ×6 cycle (table, two- and three-digit × 6 including a carrying example 25 × 6, division by 6 without remainder, word problems); multiplication by 10 and by 100 (append one/two zeros) with missing-factor items and mixed tables. End Unit Assessment 3 (pp. 118–120).
- **Unit 4 — Fractions 1/2, 1/4, 1/8 (EN pp. 121–132).** Each fraction is built the same way: fold a sheet into 2, 4 or 8 equal parts, name the part (half, quarter, eighth), read/write the symbol, shade it on shapes and on a circle. Then: parts of a fraction (numerator, denominator, fraction bar); comparing the unit fractions and, in the exercises, other fractions with the same denominator (the stated rule covers only the same-denominator case: bigger numerator = bigger fraction); joining parts to remake a whole; why fractions matter (equal sharing). End Unit Assessment 4 (pp. 131–132).
- **Unit 5 — Length measurements (EN pp. 133–148).** Preliminary work in whole metres. Measuring with a metre ruler and 30 cm ruler. The metre divided into 10 decimetres (1 m = 10 dm); the decimetre into 10 centimetres (1 dm = 10 cm; 1 m = 100 cm); the metre named as the standard unit. Conversion with a conversion table both ways. Comparing and ordering lengths after converting to the smaller/smallest unit. Adding, subtracting, multiplying and dividing lengths by whole numbers, always converting to the required unit first, with worked examples in an m/dm/cm table. Measuring "the length round" objects by adding side lengths (informal perimeter, preparation for Unit 12). Word problems; uses of length units and tools (metre ruler, tailor's tape, folding ruler, yard stick). End Unit Assessment 5 (pp. 147–148).
- **Unit 6 — Litre, the standard unit of capacity (EN pp. 149–158).** The litre as standard unit; containers and their litre capacities; measuring by filling (how many 1-litre bottles fill 2, 5, 10 litre jerry cans); comparing and ordering capacities; adding capacities in litres (to three digits, with carrying); subtracting; multiplying and dividing capacities by whole numbers; word problems throughout; importance of capacity measurement. (In the English book the unit's closing assessment items are folded into the general review pages at printed pp. 157–158 shared with Unit 7's opening in the Kinyarwanda twin; see §5.)
- **Unit 7 — Kilogram, the standard unit of mass (EN pp. 159–172).** The kilogram as standard unit of mass; balances and their types (electronic, beam, string, double-beam); measuring masses in kg and judging heavier/lighter; importance of the kg; comparing masses; adding, subtracting, multiplying and dividing masses in kg, each with word problems. End unit assessment material at pp. 171–172.
- **Unit 8 — Rwandan currency 1 Frw–1000 Frw (EN pp. 173–190).** A preliminary money unit (8.0), then: features of Rwandan coins and notes; importance of money; sources of money; buying and selling; exchanging currency (composing 1000, 500, 100 Frw from smaller denominations); listing items needed before buying; good use and management of money; the saving habit; starting a small income-generating project; comparing amounts ≤ 1000 Frw; adding/subtracting amounts; multiplying/dividing an amount by a whole number; word problems. End Unit Assessment (pp. 189–190).
- **Unit 9 — Hour, months of the year and days of each month (EN pp. 191–207).** Reading and telling time: clock hands (hour, minute, second), 1 hour = 60 minutes, 1 day = 24 hours, day/night and the two 12-hour halves; exact hours (o'clock) on analogue and digital clocks; half past the hour (analogue and digital, e.g. 9:30). The calendar: 7 days in a week, working days and weekend; the full 2017 calendar printed month by month; months and their day counts, February 28/29, common year 365 days and leap year 366, 12 months and 52 weeks in a year. School activities and timetable; preparing a weekly activity plan. End Unit Assessment 9 (p. 207).
- **Unit 10 — Types of lines and angles (EN pp. 208–217).** Preliminary naming of lines/shapes/angles. Straight lines: horizontal, vertical, oblique right, oblique left; closed lines; open (non-closed) lines; curved lines; zigzag lines. Angles: right angle (defined by horizontal + vertical), acute angle (less than a right angle), obtuse angle (greater than a right angle); comparing the three (obtuse > right > acute). End Unit Assessment 10 (p. 217).
- **Unit 11 — Grid (EN pp. 218–223).** What a grid is: vertical lines called posts and horizontal lines called crossing bars. Constructing grids with given numbers of posts and bars. Putting a point on a grid and locating a point by post number first, then crossing-bar number (an ordered-pair idea in words, without coordinate notation). End Unit Assessment 11 (p. 223).
- **Unit 12 — Square, rectangle and triangle (EN pp. 224–234).** Properties: square (4 equal sides, 4 right angles), rectangle (4 sides, opposite sides equal, 4 right angles; length and width), triangle (3 sides, 3 angles); making each shape by folding/cutting paper; identifying the shapes among mixed figures. Perimeter of each: square = side × 4 (worked example: side 23 cm → 92 cm); rectangle = (L + W) × 2 (worked example: 8 cm × 4 cm → 24 cm); triangle = sum of the three sides (worked example: 30 + 25 + 35 = 90 cm). End Unit Assessment 12 (pp. 233–234).
- **Unit 13 — Missing numbers in addition, subtraction, multiplication or division (EN pp. 235–241).** Finding the unknown in each position: missing addend (subtract the known addend from the sum — see §6 for the book's reversed wording), missing subtrahend, missing minuend (add difference + subtrahend); missing digits in vertical additions/subtractions column by column; missing factor (divide the product) and missing dividend/divisor in × and ÷ sentences. Number patterns: finding the (additive or subtractive) common difference and finding missing terms. End Unit Assessment 13 (p. 241).
- **Unit 14 — Pictographs (EN pp. 242–245).** Grouping objects by kind and showing the counts on a pictograph whose columns match numbers 1–10; describing and interpreting pictographs (the book's examples match counts of leaves, books, cars, sweets, apples to their numbers); drawing a pictograph for given counts. End Unit Assessment 14 (p. 245).
- **End of Year Assessment (EN pp. 246–250).** Twenty questions revisiting the whole year: writing numbers, place value, comparing/ordering, the four operations, ×10/×100, patterns, perimeters of pictured figures, naming angles, length/capacity/mass/money computations and conversions, comparing measurements, a calendar month, telling the time, and ten word problems.

### 4. Honest negatives — what the P2 pupil's books do NOT teach

- No multiplication tables beyond ×6 (no ×7, ×8, ×9), and no division by 7, 8, 9, 10 or 100 (division by 10/100 does not appear; only multiplication by 10/100).
- No division with a remainder anywhere — every division in the book is exact.
- No multiplication of a number by a two-digit number; the largest multiplier is a single digit (2–6, 10, 100).
- Fractions stop at unit fractions 1/2, 1/4, 1/8 as taught concepts: no thirds, fifths, sixths etc. as concepts, no fraction of a quantity (e.g. 1/2 of 12), no adding or subtracting fractions (parts are only joined physically to remake a whole), no equivalent fractions as a topic — although comparison exercises use non-unit fractions such as 2/4, 4/8, 8/8 without ever teaching them.
- No decimals, no percentages, no ratio.
- No negative numbers; no number work beyond 1000; place value stops at hundreds (no thousands column is taught even though 1000 itself is counted to).
- Measurement: length only in m/dm/cm (no mm, no km); capacity only in whole litres (no half-litre or millilitre work); mass only in whole kilograms (no grams). No conversion between capacity or mass units is needed because only one unit is used for each.
- No area (perimeter only; "length round" is taught as adding sides). No volume.
- Time: no quarter past / quarter to, no minutes past the hour other than half past, no elapsed-time calculations, no seconds in any computation.
- Geometry: no circles as a shape topic, no naming of 3-D solids, no symmetry, no angle measurement in degrees (angles are only compared with the right angle).
- Data: pictographs only with one picture per object and counts to 10; no bar charts, no tally charts, no tables of data beyond harvest/marks comparison tables in number work.
- Money stops at 1000 Frw (the 2000 and 5000 Frw notes are not covered); no change-giving computation beyond simple subtraction word problems.

**Unit 1 — Whole numbers from 0 up to 2 000 (pp. 1–46).** Reading numbers
aloud from cards and pictures and writing them in figures and words
(§§1.1–1.2), with number-line completion in steps of 100–300. Place value
to thousands: the value of a digit is the digit times 1 000, 100, 10 or 1;
expansion of a number as a sum and as (digit × place) products, and

problems (§1.15). Quick multiplication by 100 and 1 000 by appending zeros

whole number — multiply by the numerator, divide by the denominator

**Unit 5 — The relationship between length measurements (pp. 155–179).**

  for an unknown index under each law. LCM by listing multiples and by prime
  factorisation; GCF by listing factors and by prime factorisation/factor trees;
  word problems for both; finding an unknown number from a given LCM and GCF using
  the rule that the product of two numbers equals LCM × GCF. (The unit's
  introduction prints the Earth–Sun distance in index form — see suspected errors.)
- **Unit 4 — Operations on fractions (pp. 55–68).** Multiplication only:
  whole number × fraction, fraction × whole number (including finding a fraction
  of a quantity), fraction × fraction including mixed numbers, then word problems.
  Reciprocals of whole numbers, fractions and mixed numbers. Division in all
  combinations by multiplying by the reciprocal: whole ÷ fraction, fraction ÷
  whole, fraction ÷ fraction including mixed numbers, word problems (finding the
  whole from a part), and finally mixed multiplication-and-division expressions.
- **Unit 5 — Rounding and converting decimals and fractions (pp. 69–82).** Rounding
  decimals step by step to the nearest tenth, hundredth, thousandth, ten
  thousandth, hundred thousandth and millionth, with word problems. Converting
  fractions to decimals by division and decimals to fractions; converting
  recurring decimals to fractions by the multiply-and-subtract method
  (x = 0.777…, 10x − x = 7, x = 7/9), and word problems mixing both directions.
- **Unit 6 — Ratios, proportions, percentages and mixtures (pp. 83–118).**
  Percentages converted to and from decimals (including recurring decimals) and
  fractions (including fractional and over-100% percentages); one quantity as a
  percentage of another; finding the whole from a percentage of it; increasing and
  decreasing quantities by a percentage (two methods each); recovering the
  original quantity after a percentage change; percentage increase and decrease;
  percentage profit and loss on the buying price. Ratios and simplifying them;
  sharing in a ratio; increasing and decreasing quantities in a ratio; ratio of
  increase and of decrease; inverse (indirect) proportion problems. Mixtures:
  average price of a two-ingredient mixture; quantity of one ingredient from the
  mixture price by balancing profit and loss; price of one ingredient; and both
  quantities from the total mass and price.
- **Unit 7 — Volume, capacity and mass (pp. 119–126).** Revision-style unit:
  mass units from tonne to milligram with conversions (including hectogram,
  decigram, centigram) and adding masses; capacity units from kilolitre to
  millilitre with conversions; volume units mm³–dam³ with conversions; then the
  combined relationship for water, 1 litre = 1 kg = 1 dm³, and conversion between
  units of volume, capacity and mass from one combined table.
- **Unit 8 — Speed, distance and time (pp. 127–150).** The 12-hour and 24-hour
  clocks compared; converting between them in both directions. Time zones at one
  hour per zone, adding travelling east and subtracting travelling west, with
  named-place problems. Speed as distance ÷ time; converting km/h to m/s and back;
  distance as speed × time; time as distance ÷ speed; two bodies moving towards
  each other (meeting time, distance covered by each); bodies moving in the same
  direction (catching-up and overtaking time); average speed as total distance ÷
  total time, including return journeys.
- **Unit 9 — Simple interest and saving (pp. 151–172).** Simple interest
  I = PRT/100, including fractional rates and time given in months; then each
  variable in turn found by rearranging the formula — rate, principal, time — each
  with its own word-problem section; amount as principal + interest, found and
  used in problems. Saving money in a bank and investing (shares, SACCOs) and
  reasons for saving, with problems on monthly savings offers.
- **Unit 10 — Equivalent expressions and number sequences (pp. 173–186).** Writing
  algebraic expressions from word statements (sum, difference, product, quotient
  language); equivalent expressions tested by opening brackets and simplifying,
  with pupils devising their own tests. Linear sequences: finding missing
  consecutive numbers, fractions and decimals from the common difference; finding
  the general term (difference × n ± constant) for number and fraction sequences;
  finding missing terms and the nth term; generating a sequence from a given rule.
- **Unit 11 — Simple algebraic equations and inequalities (pp. 187–204).**
  Collecting like terms; unlike terms; substituting values into expressions under
  addition/subtraction, multiplication and division (including negative values);
  solving one-unknown equations by inverse operations; fractional equations
  cleared by multiplying through; forming and solving equations from word
  problems (ages, perimeters, sharing money); solving one-unknown inequalities
  including compound (double) inequalities; solution sets, finite and infinite,
  shown on number lines; word problems with equations and inequalities.
- **Unit 12 — Regular polygons and bearings (pp. 205–228).** Polygons defined and
  named by sides from triangle to decagon; interior and exterior angles at a
  vertex summing to 180°; sums of interior and exterior angles found by measuring,
  then the formula (n − 2) × 180° proved three ways including triangulation;
  number of sides from one exterior angle; sides and apothem; perimeter as sides ×
  side length; area of a regular polygon as (apothem × perimeter) ÷ 2. Compass
  points and turns; three-figure bearings measured clockwise from north, including
  back bearings; tiling a plane/floor with regular polygons.
- **Unit 13 — Construction of polygons and nets (pp. 229–260).** Drawing triangles,
  a square, a rectangle, a regular pentagon and a regular hexagon with ruler and
  protractor from given sides and angles (using the interior angles 108° and
  120°); constructing triangles (SSS, SAS), a rectangle and a square with ruler
  and compasses only; the centre angle of a regular polygon (360° ÷ sides, equal
  to the exterior angle); constructing regular pentagon, hexagon, septagon
  (heptagon), octagon, nonagon and decagon inside a circle from the centre angle;
  designing nets of cuboids, cubes and prisms by unfolding.
- **Unit 14 — Area bounded by a circle, surface area of cuboids, volume of a
  cylinder (pp. 261–278).** The area of a circle estimated by counting squares on
  squared paper, leading to A = πr²; area from radius, from diameter and from
  circumference; radius recovered from area. Surface area of a cuboid from its
  net, 2lw + 2lh + 2wh (cube as 6l²), then the formula rearranged in turn to find
  length, width or height from the surface area. Volume of a cylinder as base
  area × height, V = πr²h, with problem solving.
- **Unit 15 — Statistics (pp. 279–292).** Collecting data for an investigation;
  tallying into frequency tables and interpreting them; drawing bar charts with a
  chosen scale and interpreting them; drawing pie charts with each share as a
  fraction of 360° and interpreting pie charts (amounts and percentages from
  sector angles).
- **Unit 16 — Probability (pp. 293–299).** The language of chance — impossible,
  certain, equally likely, likely, unlikely, event, chance — sorted in activities;
  probability as a number between 0 and 1, found from data and from equally likely
  outcomes (dice, cards, pens in a box). A glossary (p. 298) and references
  (p. 299) close the book.

#### What is taught, unit by unit

**Unit 1 — Complex Numbers (pp. 1–80).** Concepts: real and imaginary parts, purely imaginary numbers, ℝ ⊂ ℂ, equality. Algebraic form: powers of i; Argand diagram and affix; modulus and its properties, |z_B − z_A| as distance; loci related to distances (|z| = R, |z − z₁| = R, |z − z₁| = |z − z₂| the perpendicular bisector); addition and subtraction with the parallelogram interpretation; conjugate and opposite, extracting Re/Im via the conjugate; multiplication; inverse and division via zz̄ = a² + b²; collinearity and perpendicularity criteria via (z_C − z_A)/(z_B − z_A) real or purely imaginary; square roots of a complex number (formula with the sign rule on b); linear equations in ℂ, including equations involving z̄; quadratic equations in ℂ (discriminant cases, Viète relations, complex-coefficient discriminants via square roots); polynomials in ℂ (Fundamental Theorem of Algebra stated, factorisation by synthetic division). Polar form: argument with the piecewise principal-argument definition; loci related to angles (arg(z) = θ half-lines, sector regions); polar form r cis θ; multiplication and division in polar form; exact values cos(π/12) = (√6+√2)/4 and sin(π/12) = (√6−√2)/4 deduced; De Moivre's theorem (integer and fractional n); nth roots, roots of unity and their sum, the exact value cos(2π/5) = (√5−1)/4 derived; construction of regular polygons from roots of unity. Exponential form: Euler's formula from series, z = re^{iθ}, Euler's formulae for cos and sin. Applications: multiple-angle formulae via De Moivre and the binomial theorem; linearisation via Euler (product-to-sum); solving a cos x + b sin x = c by complex numbers; alternating-current problems (impedance, phasors, R–L and R–C series circuits, the parallel impedance formula).

**Unit 2 — Logarithmic and Exponential Functions (pp. 81–142).** Natural logarithm: domain, range, properties, the number e as a limit; limits and asymptotes of ln (L'Hôpital's rule used); derivative of ln x and ln u; full curve studies of ln x and of f(x) = (1 + 2 ln x)/x (second derivative, inflection at e). Logarithm to any base: log_a x = ln x/ln a, change of base, limits by base, derivative 1/(x ln a), logarithmic differentiation (including x^x), curve studies of log₂ x and log_{1/2} x. Exponential base e: domain, range, limits, derivatives, curve as the reflection of ln. Exponential to any base: a^x properties, limits by base, the indeterminate forms 0⁰, 1^∞, ∞⁰ resolved via e^{g ln f}, (a^x)' = a^x ln a, curves of 2^x and (1/2)^x. Applications: compound interest A = P(1 + r/k)^{kn}; mortgage payments P = (rM/n)/(1 − (1 + r/n)^{−nt}); population growth and doubling time; depreciation/decay; the Richter scale; carbon-14 dating; decibels; pH.

**Unit 3 — Taylor and Maclaurin's Expansions (pp. 143–186).** Finite series by the method of differences (partial fractions). Infinite series: partial sums, convergence defined by the limit of partial sums; convergence tests (comparison, limit comparison, ratio, nth root). Power series: centre and radius of convergence. Taylor and Maclaurin series: Taylor's formula with remainder, Taylor polynomials, standard Maclaurin series (e^x, sin, cos, ln(1+x), the binomial series (1+x)^m), obtaining Taylor series from Maclaurin series by substitution; the Lagrange form of the remainder (in the unit summary). Applications: limits via Maclaurin expansion; estimating e; estimating π via the arctan series; estimating trigonometric values, irrational numbers (√2, ∛5) and logarithms (via the ln((1+x)/(1−x)) series); estimating roots of equations.

**Unit 4 — Integration (pp. 187–288).** Differentials: ∆y ≈ f'(x)dx, relative and percentage change. Indefinite integrals: antiderivatives, properties, basic results. Methods: substitution; rational functions (∫dx/x, ∫dx/(a²+x²), ∫u'/u, ∫u'/u²; long division for improper fractions; partial fractions with distinct and repeated linear factors and with irreducible quadratic factors); trigonometric integrals (standard table incl. ∫sec x dx; product-to-sum integrals; ∫sin^m x cos^n x dx rules; ∫tan^m x sec^n x dx rules; the t-formulae substitution u = tan(x/2) for sin/cos denominators; u = tan x for sin²x/cos²x denominators); irrational functions (ⁿ√(ax+b) substitution; √(ax²+bx+c) denominators by completing the square; trigonometric substitutions x = k sin θ / k tan θ / k sec θ); integration by parts (with a choice-of-u table and the cyclic ∫e^x sin x dx); reduction formulae; integration by Maclaurin series. Definite integrals: via antiderivatives and via Riemann sums; absolute-value integrands; properties (permutation of bounds, Chasles relation, positivity/comparison); the Mean Value Theorem for integrals and average value; root mean square value (unit summary). Improper integrals: infinite limits with comparison theorems and absolute convergence; discontinuous integrands via one-sided limits. Applications: area under and between curves (including integration with respect to y); volumes of revolution by the disc, washer and shell methods; arc length; work done by a variable force with Hooke's law; motion (displacement and velocity by integration).

**Unit 5 — Differential Equations (pp. 289–328).** Definition and classification (order, degree, ordinary vs partial); forming an ODE by eliminating arbitrary constants. First-order equations: separable variables; homogeneous equations (homogeneous functions, substitution z = y/x); linear equations solved by the y = uv product method (the integrating factor is derived but not named as such); particular solutions and initial-value problems. Second-order linear equations with constant coefficients: homogeneous via the auxiliary/characteristic equation (three discriminant cases); non-homogeneous by undetermined coefficients (r(x) = Pe^{αx} with the root-multiplicity rule; r(x) = Pe^{αx}cos βx + Qe^{αx}sin βx with its rule); variation of parameters with the Wronskian. Applications: Newton's law of cooling; RL circuit current growth and the time constant; RC capacitor charging.

**Unit 6 — Intersection and Sum of Subspaces (pp. 329–344).** Vector space and subspace definitions and tests; intersection of subspaces is a subspace, found by solving systems; dimension of an intersection; sum of subspaces as the smallest subspace containing both; Grassmann's dimension formula dim(F + G) = dim F + dim G − dim(F ∩ G); complementary (supplementary) subspaces; direct sums with uniqueness of decomposition. (Deepens Senior Five Unit 6.)

**Unit 7 — Transformation of Matrices (pp. 345–386).** Introduction via matrix cryptograms (letter–number coding with a 2×2 matrix; worked encode and decode). Kernel and range of a linear map; nullity and rank; singular vs non-singular; theorems (f one-to-one iff Ker f = {0}; rank–nullity dim Ker f + dim Im f = dim E; isomorphism). Elementary row/column operations and equivalence; similarity B = P⁻¹AP. Eigenvalues and eigenvectors; eigenspaces; characteristic polynomial det(A − λI); trace = sum of eigenvalues, determinant = product of eigenvalues; properties (A and Aᵀ share eigenvalues; triangular matrices; λ = 0 iff singular); the Cayley–Hamilton theorem and inverse via the characteristic polynomial. Diagonalisation: seven-step procedure, the criterion of n linearly independent eigenvectors, symmetric matrices diagonalisable. Applications: echelon forms (row echelon, reduced row echelon, row canonical form, pivots); matrix inverse by Gauss–Jordan on (A|I); rank of a matrix; solving linear systems by Gauss elimination with the rank criteria; powers of a matrix Aⁿ = PDⁿP⁻¹.

**Unit 8 — Conics (pp. 387–443).** Generalities: sections of a double cone; the focus–directrix definition PF = e·PM; eccentricity classification (e < 1 ellipse, e = 1 parabola, e > 1 hyperbola, e = 0 circle); degenerate conics; the general second-degree equation. Parabola: derivation of y² = 4ax and x² = 4ay; shifted vertex forms; parametric form; focal distance, focal chord, latus rectum; tangent and normal; tangency condition c = a/m. Ellipse: focus–directrix derivation of x²/a² + y²/b² = 1; sum-of-distances definition; centre, vertices, axes; e = √(a²−b²)/a; latus rectum 2b²/a derived; shifted and parametric forms; the vertical-major case; tangent and normal; tangency condition c² = a²m² + b². Hyperbola: difference-of-distances definition; x²/a² − y²/b² = 1; transverse and conjugate axes; asymptotes; shifted and parametric forms; the rectangular hyperbola xy = c²; tangent, tangency condition c² = a²m² − b², normals. Polar coordinates: pole and polar axis, equivalent representations of a point, conversion to and from Cartesian, sketching polar curves (cardioid, four-petal rose). Polar equations: of a conic r = ep/(1 + e cos θ) with focus at the pole; of a straight line; of a circle via the cosine law. Applications: planetary eccentricity, parabolic and semi-elliptical arches, cooling tower, whispering gallery.

**Unit 9 — Random Variables (pp. 445–493).** Discrete random variables and probability mass functions; cumulative distribution F(x) = P(X ≤ x) and recovering the pmf from it; expected value, E(g(X)), variance Var(X) = E(X²) − [E(X)]², standard deviation. Binomial distribution: Bernoulli trials, P(X = r) = C(n,r)p^r q^{n−r}, expected frequencies N(p + q)ⁿ, fitting a binomial, mean np, variance npq. Poisson distribution: motivated from the Maclaurin series of e^θ and as the binomial limit (n → ∞, np = λ); P(X = x) = e^{−λ}λ^x/x!; mean = variance = λ; the recurrence P(X = x+1) = [λ/(x+1)]P(X = x); fitting a Poisson. Continuous random variables: probability density functions (probability as area), cumulative distribution as an integral, expectation and variance as integrals; the uniform distribution with mean (a + b)/2 and variance (b − a)²/12 derived.

**Summative Evaluations 1–4 (pp. 494–506)** — cross-year exam papers over S4–S6 content; **References (pp. 507–508)** — 26 entries.
