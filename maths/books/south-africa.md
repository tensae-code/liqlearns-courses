South Africa runs one national maths course in this set:
the Department of Basic Education (DBE) **Rainbow
Workbooks** — learner workbooks, not explanatory textbooks.
Two books per grade, one per half-year (Book 1 = Terms 1–2,
Book 2 = Terms 3–4), in the South African four-term year.
Grades 1–3 (Foundation Phase) are held in up to eleven
official languages per book; Grades 4–6 (Intermediate) and
7–9 (Senior) in English and Afrikaans. The bodies descend
from 2014 CAPS-aligned originals, reissued as 15th edition
2025 (Grade 2 English editions) or 16th edition 2026
(everything else held). Every book carries "This book may
not be sold" and was made available free, funded by the
National Treasury. Money throughout is South African rand
and cents, decimal fractions use the comma (R12,50), and
the contexts are South African (tuck shops, taxis, soccer,
stokvels, spaza shops). The honest shape of the whole set:
topics are practised, often introduced by a single worked
example, while the explanation itself is left to the teacher
and the class discussion each worksheet opens with — and
the Senior Phase learner books contain no answers at all.

**THE GAP, PROVEN FROM THE BOOKS: the in-hand set contains
no Grade 10, 11 or 12 mathematics of any kind.** The set
tops out at Grade 9, and no teacher book or teacher guide
for any grade is in the record at all — the workbooks assume
a teacher leading discussion and marking each worksheet, but
no guide is held. Language gaps inside the band are not

## Band A — Grades 1–3

**Scope.** 62 files held of 63 expected in Band A (Grade 1: 21/21; Grade 2:
19/20; Grade 3: 22/22). They resolve into **6 distinct works** — two workbooks
per grade, one per half-year — each held in several language editions. Content
is filed **once per work** below, from the English edition where one is held
(all six works have an English edition in hand), with the other language
editions spot-checked against it.

**What these books are.** All six are Department of Basic Education (DBE)
**Rainbow Workbooks** — learner workbooks, not explanatory textbooks. Every
unit is normally two pages: a first page led by the teacher (pictures,
counting, discussion, "we have done the first one for you" worked starts) and
a second practice page, usually carrying a Teacher / Sign / Date box. There
is no running exposition: a topic is introduced by a worked example, a
picture, or an instruction, and then practised. Where a topic is practised
but never explained, or where the book plainly assumes the teacher's spoken
explanation, the filing says so. Word problems in Grade 1 are explicitly
read aloud by the teacher or parent ("Your teacher/parent will read this to
you"). All six books say "This book may not be sold" and were made available
free in all official languages, funded by the National Treasury; the contents
pages name the Minister and Deputy Minister of Basic Education.

**Front matter, uniform.** In every file: PDF p1 is a full cover spread
(back cover left, front cover right); PDF p2 is the contents list plus the
imprint; PDF p3 is the title page ("This book belongs to:"); the body
starts at PDF p4. Body page citations below use the **contents printed
page** (the page number the book's own contents list gives for the unit's
first page). In the numbered body, PDF page = contents page + 2, verified
at unit starts across all six books — **except in the tail sections**
flagged per work, where the held PDF's pagination disagrees with its own
contents list; there, cite by **unit number**, not by page. The books'
bodies all descend from 2014 CAPS-aligned originals (InDesign stems dated
April–July 2014 run in the footers of the Grade 2 and Grade 3 books), reissued
as 15th edition 2025 (Grade 2 English) or 16th edition 2026 (everything else
in the band). The Grade 3 Book 2 front cover carries a "Revised and CAPS
aligned" badge.

**How the read was done.** Each English base was read page by page from its
text layer, with picture-led pages checked visually (the books are heavily
pictorial; a text dump alone understates them). Every other language edition
was checked at the first page (PDF p4, unit 1 / unit 65) and at a mid-book
unit page (PDF p70), and two full unit-header sequences were compared
page-for-page (Grade 2 Workbook 1 Afrikaans: 70/70 headers identical to
English; Grade 1 Workbook 1 Sepedi: 69/69 identical). Every held edition
matches its English base at every checked point: same unit numbers, same
translated titles, same number ranges. No translation mismatch was found.
Apparent mismatches in the machine header comparison (a handful of single
tokens in isiZulu, Sepedi and Tshivenda files) were inspected individually
and are content numbers misread as headers (e.g. the answer "15" on a bead
page, "Half of 4 / 6 / 8 / 2" labels), not different content. One
qualification: Grade 2's English editions are 15th edition 2025 while its
other-language editions are 16th edition 2026 — the structure is identical
at every checked point, but no 16th-edition English copy was held to diff
against, so a small wording change in the 16th edition English cannot be
ruled out. Grade 3 Workbook 2 isiZulu has a largely image-based text layer
(its cover page yields only ~109 characters), so its match rests on the
p4/p70 unit checks, the identical 138-page count, and its contents
worksheet numbers (65+), plus its title-page evidence (see the table notes).

## Work 1 — Grade 1 Workbook 1 (Terms 1–2)

**Identity.** DBE Rainbow Workbook, Mathematics Grade 1 Book 1, Terms 1 & 2.
English edition: 16th edition 2026, ISBN 978-1-920458-82-9 (30,061,568
bytes, MD5 0f409402e896eb4adea600dc8ed18971). All 10 held language editions
are also 16th edition 2026. **Held files: 10** — English + Afrikaans,
IsiNdebele, IsiXhosa, Sepedi, Sesotho, Setswana, Siswati, Tshivenda,
Xitsonga — each 142 PDF pages. **No isiZulu edition is held**, and the ISBN
sequence confirms the gap: the held ISBNs run …0082-9 (English), …0083-6,
…0084-3, then …0086-7, with the …0085… slot unheld — the slot the isiZulu
edition would occupy. Body: PDF pp4–138 (contents printed pp2–138, with the
tail caveat below); cut-outs: PDF pp139–142. Units 1–64b (numbered 1–64 with
12a/12b, 24a/24b, 48a/48b, 60a/60b, 64a/64b splits). Term 1 = units 1–32;
Term 2 = units 33–64b (Term 2 starts at unit 33, "Six").

**How it teaches.** This is a Grade R recap followed by first numbers. The
first eight units are explicitly "Revision". Every number unit follows the
same drill: count objects in a picture, trace the number name, then on the
second page colour in that many shapes, copy and draw that many more,
practise writing the symbol, and colour shapes as you count. Instructions
are imperatives addressed to the child ("Draw 6 circles in the block",
"Colour in 5 in each block"); the only teaching voice is the worked first
item ("We have done the red shapes as an example for you"). There is no
explanation of what addition or subtraction *is* — sums appear as pictures
to complete ("and makes", "take away") and only gradually as symbols
(the first body-unit symbol sum, 2+3=5, is in unit 43, "Adding"; the cover spread also carries a decorative 2+3=5). A number line (1–10, then
1–20) runs as a footer strip on every body page, and the book leans on
cut-outs at the back (shapes, a dice with a dice game, number-board
cut-outs) for the sorting, shape and number-board units.

**Content, unit by unit (contents printed page in brackets).**
- *School readiness / revision (Term 1).* 1 Tracking and matching [2]:
  follow a dashed path (maze) and match identical pictures. 2 Sorting
  colours and tracing patterns [4]: sort cut-out shapes by colour; trace
  patterns on a whale. 3 Colours and patterns [6]: match same-coloured
  clothes; copy small patterns. 4 Sorting and matching shapes [8]: place
  cut-out circles, squares, triangles, rectangles. 5 Find and count [10]:
  count kinds of animals, record with dots. 6 Positions [12]: circle the
  animal in the same position as a coloured block; trace patterns.
  7 Time [14]: tick what takes longer / cross what takes shorter
  (walking vs driving, etc.). 8 Shapes, sizes and colours [16]: circle the
  biggest object; circle things of a given colour.
- *Numbers 1–5 (Term 1).* 9 One [18], 10 Two [20], 11 Three [22]: count,
  trace the number name, colour/copy/draw, practise the symbol. 12a Length
  and position [24] and 12b Length [26]: tick shortest/longest trains,
  circle the shorter object, short/long, tall/short, wide/narrow.
  13 Compare numbers 1 to 3 [28]: same number of objects, more objects,
  one more / one less. 14 Four [30] (same number drill). 15 Add and
  subtract up to 4 [32]: complete pictures ("and makes"), draw counters
  to make 4, "how many counters are left". 16 Time [34]: order pictures
  1–4 (growing up), fastest/slowest, yesterday / today / tomorrow
  drawings. 17 Five [36] (number drill). 18 Revise numbers 1 to 5 [38]:
  write symbols 1–5, circle the correct symbol for a count, trace number
  words. 19 Add up to 5 [40]: draw more beads, "and 2 and 2 makes 4" sums.
  20 Subtract from 5 and add up to 5 [42]: draw less, "5 take away 1 is 4",
  subtract by counting back. 21 Add and subtract up to 5 [44]:
  "and give me" picture sums; second page: word problems read aloud by teacher/parent (Lisa's pears). 22 Addition and subtraction 1 to 5 [46]:
  bead-colouring for bonds of 5 (2 and 3 is 5; 5 take away 2 is 3), number
  lines.
- *Space, sharing, time (Term 1).* 23 Balls and boxes [48]: which pictures
  look like boxes (square mark) or balls (circle mark); trace the words
  "box", "ball". 24a Left and right [50]: left/right hands and feet on
  pictured children, "stand like the boy and show your right hand".
  24b Direction [52]: rabbit to carrots, turn left or right; match arrows
  to above / left / below / right. 25 Building up and breaking down
  numbers [54]: fill in bonds ("2 and 1 is 3"). 26 Addition doubles [56]:

**How it teaches.** Three named methods carry the year's arithmetic.
(1) Number-line jumps for two-digit addition/subtraction: 46 + 24 is

80 − 50 + 7 − 3 versus 80 + 7 − 50 + 3 — the two orders give the same
answer, and learners are told to choose the way they know and like best
(unit 38, "Solve it!"). Place-value blocks (a small block = 1 unit, a rod

concrete. Division is taught three ways at once: sharing sweets, number
blocks, and repeated subtraction on a number line (16 − 2 − 2 … = 0, so

symbols (one half, one quarter, one third, one fifth, three quarters,

**How it teaches.** Grade 3's second half is the band's most method-rich
book. Its signature devices: (1) Error-spotting — "The teacher asks
Peter to show 537 with his cards and blocks. This is what Aakar showed.
What did he do wrong?" (units 66, 69, 71, 98, 99, 101, 104, with Peter/
Aakar/Ben/Gugu/Andile/Jabu rotating as the mistaken child); learners
must diagnose place-value errors, not just compute. (2) Flow diagrams
(input → rule → output) for × and ÷, with tables to complete
(×1–20, then ×1–21). (3) The distributive method made routine:
24 × 3 = (20 + 4) × 3 = (20 × 3) + (4 × 3) = 60 + 12 = 72; division the
same way (63 ÷ 3 = (60 + 3) ÷ 3 = 20 + 1 = 21), then division with
remainders (65 ÷ 3 = 21 rem 2, checked as 21 × 3 + 2 = 65). (4) "Halving
to subtract" / near-doubles strategies (34 − 18 via 34 − 17 = 17, then
17 − 1 = 16; 340 + 341 via double 340 + 1). (5) "What makes 1 000" and
"add forward from 600" jump chains. Rounding is taught as a rule with a
number line (4 and below round to 0, 5 and above round to 10) before it
is used for estimation (spaza-shop income: estimate, then calculate
exactly). Fractions graduate to strip kits the child makes and folds
(unit 91), a fraction wall, sharing with remainders expressed as
fractions (25 sugar strips between 10 children), and tangram fractions.
Measurement becomes standard: centimetres with rulers, metres vs
centimetres choice, kilograms and grams (1,5 kg = 1 kg 500 g; 1000 g =
1 kg; spring scale in 10 g steps), litres in jugs, perimeter defined
("the distance around"), area by covering with squares, and volume as
cup intervals ("the capacity of the cup is 10 and the volume of the cup
that is filled is 1").

## Band A — cross-cutting summary

**Progression in one paragraph.** Grade 1 (280 PDF pages across its two
works) teaches numbers 1–20, bonds to 10, doubling/halving, grouping in
2s–5s as repeated addition, comparative length, full/empty capacity,
months/days/duration, sorting and pictographs, and money to R20 with
change — all by picture, tracing and cut-outs, with no symbols beyond
+ and − and no definitions. Grade 2 (284 PDF pages) extends to 50 then
200, tens/units (then hundreds/tens/units readings), decomposition
addition/subtraction without carrying/borrowing, ×2–×5 as groups +
repeated addition + × sign, sharing with a labelled ÷ sign, halves and
quarters, hours and minutes separately then quarter past/to, litres and
kilograms named, and bar graphs. Grade 3 (280 PDF pages) reaches 1000,
regrouping tens into hundreds, number-line jumps, Busi/Dumi written
methods, division by sharing/blocks/repeated subtraction and by
distribution with remainders, fractions to eighths via strips and
sharing, centimetres/metres, kilograms/grams, perimeter and square-
counted area, rounding to tens, flow diagrams, and error-spotting as a
teaching device. Total Band A body: 844 PDF pages (62 files).

**What a course-builder should not import uncritically.** (a) These are
workbooks: almost every "explanation" is a worked first item or a
teacher-led discussion prompt, so a course built from them must supply
its own explanations — the books alone do not define addition,
subtraction, multiplication, even/odd, litre, kilogram (except Grade 3's
one kg definition and its mass/weight distinction), or symmetry (except
Grade 3's one folding definition). (b) Money is South African rand and
cents throughout (notes to R100, coins 1c–50c, R1/R2/R5, comma decimals
R12,50); the stokvel, spaza shop, taxi and police-station contexts are
South African. (c) The term labels are the South African four-term year
(Terms 1–4 across two workbooks per grade). (d) Tail sections of Grade 1
Workbook 1 (units 64a/64b), Grade 2 Workbook 1 (unit 64) and Workbook 2
(unit 128), and Grade 3 Workbook 1 (units 54–64) and Workbook 2 (units
115–128b) must be cited by unit number, not by contents page, in the
held PDFs. (e) The one file still owed is Grade 2 Workbook 2 isiZulu;
Grade 1 Workbook 1 isiZulu, Grade 2 Workbook 1 Sepedi and Xitsonga are
also unheld but their content is fully covered by sibling editions.

**No contradictions.** No held file's contents contradict its slot
(grade/workbook/language). The only slot evidence that rests on
title-page rather than cover evidence is the four image-based covers

## Band B — what this means for the courses

1. **The spine is continuous and cumulative.** Number ranges grow
   steadily (1 000 → 2 000 → 5 000 → 10 000 → 20 000 → 50 000 → 100 000 →
   200 000 → 1 000 000 → 9-digit work), and every method is introduced
   once, by example, then reused: breaking-down addition/subtraction
   (G4) → column method named (G5 Book 2 ws 84–85) → column multiplication
   (G6 Book 1 ws 31) → 4-digit by 3-digit column products and 4-digit by
   3-digit division (G6 Book 2). A course built from these books should
   teach in that order, not import the column method into Grade 4.
2. **Several topics are practised before they are defined.**
   Ratio (G4 ws 61, by picture), mixed numbers (G4 Book 2 ws 74, one
   line), fractions as division (G4), decimals as mass notation
   (6,5 kg in G5 Book 2) all precede their definitions (ratio problems
   G5–G6; mixed/improper definitions G6 Book 1 ws 9; decimals G6 Book 1
   ws 52). Course text must supply the definition the workbook omits,
   and must not assume the workbook's one-line naming was teaching.
3. **Discussion-first design.** Worksheets open with "Talk about…" and
   end with a teacher Sign/Date box. The books assume a teacher leading
   the discussion and marking each worksheet. A self-study course cannot
   copy that structure silently — it needs to put the discussion's
   content (the rule, the reason) into the lesson itself.
4. **Comma decimals, rand money, South African contexts** (tuck shops,
   taxis, soccer, provincial book deliveries) throughout. Decimal
   teaching must use the comma as printed, and flag it for learners used
   to the point.
5. **Defects to carry forward, not silently fix:** the crossed Grade 5
   Book 2 covers/contents (cite ISBNs by edition language, never by file);
   the contents-page misprints (G4 Book 1 R13; G5 Book 1 R11–R13;
   G6 Book 1 R8a); the stray term markers (G4 Book 2 ws 117/120/126;
   G6 Book 2 ws 118); the "Refection" and "approximiation"/"Enlargment"
   spellings, plus contents-only misspellings — "Reflectection" for
   Grade 6 Book 2 ws 137a and "Perpective" for ws 142b (worksheet
   headers read "Reflection" and "Perspective" correctly) and
   "Tesselation" for Grade 5 Book 2 ws 90 in its contents (worksheet
   header "Tessellation"); the dated calendars (Dec 2015; 2012/2013/2015).

**What it actually teaches, honestly.** The capstone book: factorisation
through trinomials and difference of squares, solving quadratics by
factorising, gradient from rise over run, scatter plots, nets construction,
and compound probability. The teaching pattern is unchanged — discussion
opener, worked example, drill, problem-solving footer — but the content is
the most demanding in the band. What is *practised but never explained*:
why the factorisation patterns work beyond the worked examples, why
gradient is defined as rise over run rather than derived, and why the
counting principle multiplies (stated via possibility spaces, not proved).
What *assumes the teacher*: the polyhedron discussions, the visualisation
exercises, the perspective enrichment, and the compound-probability
classifications, which are introduced as definitions to apply.

**Honest negatives.** No trigonometry. No simultaneous equations. No
quadratic formula, no completing the square, no factorising beyond
trinomials/difference of squares. No function notation (graphs remain
tables of ordered pairs). No circle theorems. No volume of spheres or cones
(prisms and cylinder only). No statistics beyond mean/median/mode/range
(no quartiles, no standard deviation, no box-and-whisker). No constructions
as new content in Book 2 (all in Book 1).

## Band C — closing notes

**Coverage in one paragraph.** Grades 7–9 in this band are a single
progression, not three separate courses: Grade 7 establishes number sense,
fractions/decimals, measurement and pattern-description; Grade 8 adds
exponent laws, symbolic algebra, constructions, Pythagoras and the
Cartesian plane; Grade 9 completes exponent laws, adds factorisation and
quadratic-type solving, gradient, scatter plots, nets construction and
compound probability. Data handling recurs every year (Grade 7 Term 4,
Grade 8 Terms 3–4, Grade 9 Term 4) at increasing depth; probability likewise
(single events in Grades 7–8, compound events in Grade 9). What the band
never teaches: trigonometry, simultaneous equations, the quadratic formula,
function notation, circle theorems, spheres/cones, and any statistics
beyond mean/median/mode/range.

edition — contents, revision part, every numbered worksheet's opener,
