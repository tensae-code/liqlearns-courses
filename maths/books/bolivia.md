### (d) Confirmed book errors (each recomputed; taught corrected; † = not independently re-verified at citation check — stands on the reader's recomputation)

   divides by +√13, then claims cos ω=2/√13, **sen ω=3/√13** (its
   own equation has −3/√13), ω=56°18′36″, and a final equation
   with constant +5/√13 (i.e. ρ negative, contradicting ρ>0). →
   Correct normal form: **−(2/√13)x + (3/√13)y − 5/√13 = 0,
   ω ≈ 123°41′**.
2. **P. 62 (distance, ex. 3).** The problem states the point's
   ordinate is **4**; the working uses **y=3** and answers
   **x=5/2, −15/2** → for the stated y=4: **x=7/4, −33/4** (the
   printed answers belong to y=3).
3. **P. 63 (parallel distance, ex. 3).** L1 is printed as
   **3x+4y+4=0** and L2 as 9x−12y−4=0 — as printed they are **not
   parallel**; the working silently rewrites L1 as 3x−4y+4=0 and
   gets 16/15, valid only for the changed line.
4. **P. 65 (applied Problem 1, depreciation).** Laptop Bs 4950 →
   Bs 495 in 5 years: the book computes the slope as **m=+891**
   (sign dropped) and then uses **495** as the slope, giving
   **V=495t+4950** → **m=−891, V=4950−891t** (check: V(5)=495).

6. **P. 80 (horizontal parabola y²=4x).** The text calls the axis
   of symmetry "**vertical**" and prints the directrix as **D:
   y=−1** → the axis is horizontal (the line y=0) and the
