### C1 what the book teaches, chapter by chapter

- **Ch 1 Compare (pp. 1–4).** Comparing by sight and touch before any
  numbers: more/less, small/large, short/tall, near/far (from a lamp
  post), light/heavy. Tick the less/more, small/large, short/tall,
  light/heavy object in picture pairs; compare objects around the learner
  with the same words. No numbers yet.
- **Ch 2 Counting (pp. 5–8).** Counting collections of objects (flags,
  fruit, birds, flowers, tigers); matching groups with equal numbers of
  pictures; counting different objects around the learner and saying the
  number. No numerals written yet.
- **Ch 3 Numbers 1 to 10 (pp. 9–28).** Recognise and read 1–5, then 6–10;
  count pictures and match/circle/underlined the matching number; count,
  colour, read and write the numerals; **zero** (p. 20: count mangoes on
  plates, the empty plate is "zero", read "zero" and write "0"); arrange
  5, 8 and 10 objects in different ways; comparison of numbers 0–10 —
  smaller/greater, circling the smaller/greater, ordering smaller to
  greater and greater to smaller, writing the number in between, the
  previous and the next number; join-the-dots in number order to draw a
  shape.
- **Ch 4 Concept of Addition (pp. 29–42).** Putting groups together:
  flowers at the Shaheed Minar, bananas, pineapples, cats, mangoes,
  balloons, children in a park, tomatoes, cars, mangoes, birds, brinjals.
  Introduces "+" ("plus"), "=" ("equal") and the "mathematical
  statement" (e.g. 3 + 1 = 4, read "Three plus one equal to four"); the
  result of addition is called the "summation". Addition stories with a
  start ("at first there were… then… more came"); making addition
  stories for given sums (5 + 2, 3 + 5, 4 + 2); addition number cards
  arranged by equal answers, noticing patterns in the cards; adding two
  numbers to make 10, marking pairs that sum to 10. All sums within 10.
- **Ch 5 Concept of Subtraction (pp. 43–53).** Taking away: birds flying
  off (5 − 2), eating apples (6 − 2 = 4), moving balls away from a group

  plates/cakes, "Rafi has 6 flowers and Tuli has 3 flowers — how many
  more?"; word problems (Nidhi's 8 toys, give 5; 7 birds, 4 fly away;
  Ruma's 8 sheets, write on 3; 9 students, 5 girls — how many boys?;
  10 flowers, give 5; 9 boats, 4 leave; 9 fish vs 5 fish); expressing a
  subtraction with a picture; **subtraction of zero**
  (p. 50: 4 chocolates, eat 4/3/2/1/0, how many remain); making
  subtraction stories (6 − 4); a card game (take 2 cards, make a
  subtraction). All within 10.
- **Ch 6 Numbers 11 to 20 (pp. 54–57).** Eleven to twenty as one ten and
  ones (1 ten 1 = 11 "eleven", up to 2 tens = 20); trace, read and write

- **Ch 7 Addition 11 to 20 (pp. 58–65).** Adding within 20, e.g. Mithu's
  12 pencils plus 4 more from her father; 13 friends + 5 relatives;
  6 + 11 mangoes; 10 + 9 colour pencils; the **making-10 strategy** is
  taught explicitly (9 + 4: 9 needs 1 to make 10, split 4 into 1 and 3,
  10 + 3 = 13 — and two ways to make the 10 are shown, 6 + 4 and
  1 + 9; same for 8 + 5, 8 + 3, 7 + 6, 6 + 5); sums-to-15 game;
  adding adjacent numbers. Tens-and-ones
  layout for the sums.
- **Ch 8 Subtraction 11 to 20 (pp. 66–68).** Subtracting within 20, e.g.
  14 balls in the almirah, 4 taken to play with (14 − 4 = 10);
  17 chocolates, 10 eaten (17 − 10 = 7); 13 toy cars, 9 sold;
  subtracting within 20 (14 − 6, 15 − 9, 12 − 7, 16 − 8 …); how many
  more (15 eggs vs 9 bananas; Bijoy's 19 pencils vs Anita's 8);
  show 15 − 7 with a picture; make a story for 13 − 6; circle pairs
  whose difference is 12. Short chapter (3 pages).
- **Ch 9 Numbers 21 to 40 (pp. 69–73).** Twenty-one to forty as tens and
  ones (2 tens 1 = 21 "twenty one", 3 tens = 30 "thirty"); read, write

- **Ch 10 Place Value (pp. 74–78).** Groups of ten: count marigold
  flowers by making bundles of 10; tens and ones as the place value of a
  two-digit number.
- **Ch 11 Do myself (pp. 79–81).** Mixed revision: number sequences and

- **Ch 12 Geometry (pp. 82–85).** Shapes of everyday objects (book,

  quadrangular shapes in things around the learner.
- **Ch 13 Pattern (pp. 86–91).** Repeating patterns: bat–ball, square–
  triangle, colour/shape sequences; completing and making patterns.
- **Ch 14 Numbers 41 to 100 (pp. 92–96).** Number chart 41–100 with
  Bengali/English number names (forty-one … one hundred); counting and

- **Ch 15 Addition (pp. 97–100).** Two-digit addition without carry in

- **Ch 16 Subtraction (pp. 101–104).** Two-digit subtraction without

### C2 what the book teaches, chapter by chapter

- **Ch 1 Numbers and place values (pp. 1–39).**
  - *Read numbers and write in words 21–100 (pp. 1–7):* counting blocks
    and writing numbers 21–100 in digits and words (twenty one …
    one hundred); a 51–100 word table; "Let us do" with family sizes,
    fruit trees in a house, and boys/girls classwise in a school.
  - *Count (pp. 8–13):* grouping by tens to count flowers; blocks of
    100, 10 and 1 (345 = 3 hundreds, 4 tens, 5 ones); counting cards of
    100/10/1; reading and writing 101–1000, sorting numbers into
    101–500 and 501–1000.
  - *Comparison of numbers (pp. 14–16):* compare by blocks of tens, then
    hundreds, then ones (62 vs 45; 434 vs 253; 354 vs 325); circle the
    larger/smaller number.
  - *Place value (pp. 17–21):* bundles of 10 sticks; hundreds, tens,
    ones; thousands (2442 = 2 thousands, 4 hundreds, 4 tens, 2 ones);
    place value of each digit of 47, 352, 1000; fill-in-the-blanks
    (56 = 5 tens 6 ones; 2 tens 8 ones = 28).
  - *Comparison using place values (pp. 22–24):* compare hundreds, then
    tens, then ones (460 vs 630; 562 vs 548); arrange 223, 232, 239 in
    ascending/descending order; sort 1000, 780, 949.
  - *Even-odd numbers and number patterns (pp. 25–33):* pairing objects
    (pairs of earrings, pigeons, socks); even numbers end in 2, 4, 6, 8
    or 0, odd in 1, 3, 5, 7 or 9; identify even/odd 1–50; number
    patterns with a fixed difference (2, 4, 6, 8…; 15, 20, 25…;
    21, 19, 17, 15…; 0, 5, 10, 15…; 3, 6, 9…); make your own patterns
    (start at 3, +4; start at 47, decreasing odd numbers).
  - *Ordinal number (pp. 34–39):* 1st–5th, then 1st–10th (first …
    tenth); positions from the left/right/front/back; "Savita is second
    from the front and 5 more children are behind her — how many in the
    line?"; "Raju is third from the front and fifth from the back";
    "Tamim is the third youngest and fourth oldest in his family";
    seven people in a circle, seventh from the right vs seven from the
    right.
- **Ch 2 Addition and Subtraction (pp. 40–72).** Note: the chapter title
  says "(Without carry)" but the chapter does teach carrying and
  borrowing in its second half — the title describes only Addition (1)
  and Subtraction (1).
  - *Addition (1) (pp. 40–45):* 43 + 32 cards; add ones first, then
    tens; 32 + 16; two-digit + two-digit without carry; then **with
    carry**: 38 + 24 (8 + 4 = 12 = 1 ten 2 ones); 23 + 18, 45 + 27,
    52 + 38, 78 + 8, 26 + 37; three-number addition (35 + 6 + 6);
    money word problems (fish Tk 45 + vegetables Tk 38; ferry groups
    35 + 27; library books 54 + 38).
  - *Subtraction (1) (pp. 46–52):* 36 − 13 sheets of paper; subtract
    ones, then tens; subtract 10 from 47, 25 from 45; subtract a
    1-digit number from a 2-digit number (64 − 3 …); then **with
    borrowing**: 45 − 18 = 27 (take 1 ten to the ones place,
    15 − 8 = 7, 3 − 1 = 2); 97 − 59, 71 − 44, 75 − 47, 60 − 53;
    subtract from 30/34/25 etc.; subtraction word problems (75 vs 49
    marbles; mother/daughter ages sum 70).
  - *Mathematical Relation (pp. 53–54):* inverse operations — mangoes
    sold (□ − 5 = 6, so 6 + 5 = 11 at first); fill in missing numbers
    (9 + □ = 16; □ − 39 = 45; 12 + 9 − □ = 13); two-step problems
    (Bijoy's mangoes: +15, sold 33, left 12 — how many at first?
    100 lichis, give 25 and 23 — how many left?).
  - *Addition (2) (pp. 55–63):* adding tens and hundreds: 80 + 50,
    100 + 300, 140 + 20; 73 + 54 with carry into hundreds (7 + 5 = 12
    tens = 1 hundred 2 tens); three-digit addition 216 + 142,
    134 + 145 flowers; 526 + 253, 552 + 436, 872 + 127; **with carry
    into hundreds**: 468 + 394 = 862; 267 + 145 = 412; missing-digit
    additions; pair game adding two 3-digit numbers.
  - *Subtraction (2) (pp. 64–68):* 120 − 50; 170 − 40 … 520 − 30;
    three-digit with borrowing across places (125 − 43; 785 − 421;
    241 − 115 = 126, borrowing a ten into the ones); 325 − 13 …
    930 − 40; word problems (Tk 385 − Tk 253; 542 students, 290 girls;
    200 tilapia, 165 sold; Tk 342 vs Tk 315).
  - *Problems related to addition and subtraction (pp. 69–72):*
    two-step village/school/money problems: 673 people + 117 came −
    105 left = 685; 625 students + 275 admitted − 35 left; sum of two
    numbers 840, one is 527; 9 numbers sum 360, first four sum 152,
    last four sum 164 — find the 5th (44); ages after 10 years;
    fruit-shop price list (mango Tk 100/kg, apple Tk 220, orange
    Tk 160); make a story for 975 + 325.
- **Ch 3 Multiplication (pp. 73–101).** Concept: equal groups as
  repeated addition (5 plates of 4 fish = 4 + 4 + 4 + 4 + 4); the × sign;
  "3 × 4 = 12, three multiplied by four equals twelve"; multiplication
  tables of 5, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 1 and 0 built with the 100-dot
  table and L-shaped paper; multiplication cards game (4 games);
  splitting a factor (6 × 3 = 2 × 3 + 4 × 3 = 6 + 12 = 18); the full
  1–10 table (p. 97) with patterns in the ones places; commutativity
  (4 × 3 = 3 × 4, factors of 16, 24, 36, 63, 72); two-digit × one-digit
  without carry (12 × 4; practice 14 × 2, 23 × 3, 11 × 7, 32 × 3,
  34 × 2, 21 × 4 — none of them carries, and no with-carry
  two-digit × one-digit example is taught in the chapter); mixed practice
  and word problems (benches × students, books at Tk 22, seating dots).
  Multiplication by 0 is 0; multiplication by 1 leaves the number.
  **No division is taught in Class 2.**
- **Ch 4 Geometric Shapes and Patterns (pp. 102–106).** Shapes around
  us: round, triangular, quadrangular; coin (round), matchbox surface
  (quadrangular); match shapes to objects (triangle, quadrangle/
  rectangle/square, circle/sphere/cylinder); shapes of foods (samosa,
  bread, roshogolla, biscuit, moa); shading triangular and quadrangular
  shapes; counting triangles/quadrangles/circles in a cartoon drawing;
  patterns: zebra crossing, a zebra's stripes, making patterns with
  circles, triangles and quadrangles, permuting pictures into a pattern.
- **Ch 5 Measurement (pp. 107–120).**
  - *Length (pp. 107–110):* compare pencils; metre for long, centimetre
    for short; 1 metre = 100 centimetres; using a ruler (zero end at
    one end of the object, read the other end — pencil 8 cm); measure
    pen, pencil, notebook, geometry box, blackboard, bench.
  - *Weight (pp. 111–113):* heavier/lighter by hand (duster vs pen);
    kilogram (kg) and gram (gm); 1 kg = 1000 gm; balance/scale/digital
    balance; standard weights 5, 10, 20, 50, 100, 200, 500, 1000 gm;
    making weights with batkhara (50 + 20 + 20 + 10 …); weigh 3–4
    objects on a balance and a digital balance and compare.
  - *Volume of liquid (pp. 114–116):* which bottle holds more water
    (pour from one to the other, mark levels); compare with smaller
    jars; litre (L/l) as the unit; a bucket holds so many 1-litre
    bottles.
  - *Time (pp. 117–120):* analogue clock (hour hand at 3, minute hand
    at 12); set the clock to 8, 2, 10, 3 o'clock; digital watch
    (11:15 = 15 minutes past 11); days of the week, day before/after;
    Bengali months (Chaitro, Jaishthya, Poush, Falgun, Augrohayon,
    Ashwin …) and English months (January–December), month before/
    after, month of your birth.
- **Ch 6 Currency (pp. 121–124).** The currency of Bangladesh is the
  taka (Tk); coins and paper notes (pictures of both sides); exchanging
  notes (Tk 200 = Tk 100 + two Tk 50, or two Tk 50 + five Tk 20, etc.;
  Tk 500 and Tk 1000 the same way); paying Tk 30 with 1/2/5/10 taka
  pieces in different combinations; exchanging a Tk 50 note with
  1/2/5/10/20; paying fruit prices with notes; word problems (4 eggs
  Tk 40 + biscuits Tk 65; notebook + pen Tk 105, pen Tk 15 — price of
  the notebook; Tk 100, father gives Tk 50, geometry box Tk 120 —
  money left).
- **Ch 7 Data (pp. 125–127).** Tally marks (four vertical strokes, the
  fifth diagonal across them = a bundle of five); counting fruits with
  tallies; vehicles on a street in a day (tally sheet and counts);
  domestic animals on the board; class survey (favourite subject among
  Bangla/English/Mathematics; favourite bird among crow/cuckoo/pigeon/
  martin; favourite colour among red/blue/green/purple); leaves of 3–4

### C3 honest negatives and confirmed errors

- **No decimals, no area/perimeter, no triangles, no lines-and-angles
  beyond the angle/quadrilateral introduction** in Class 3. Division
  stops at 2-digit ÷ 1-digit with remainders; no long division of
  3-digit numbers; fractions are like-denominator only (no unlike
  denominators, no mixed numbers). Time is durations and clock reading,
  not timetables or calendars beyond months (months were in Class 2).

  English translation (both versions wrong; correct values given):**
  1. Printed p. 39, multiplication table of 1–10 (recall table): the

  original is correct in each case:**
  3. Printed p. 70, division remainder rule. English: "The remainder
     is smaller than the dividend — remainder < dividend." **Correct:
     the remainder is smaller than the divisor — remainder < divisor.**
     The Bangla (p. 70) says "ভাগের অবশিষ্ট ভাজক থেকে ছোটো হবে /
     অবশিষ্ট < ভাজক" (the remainder will be smaller than the divisor).

     19 ÷ 3 = 5 remainder 4 is wrong precisely because the remainder
     4 is bigger than the divisor 3; the correct answer is 6 remainder 1.
     The English rule as printed would bless the wrong attempt (4 < 19).

     numerator is obtained by subtracting the numerator of minuend

     numerator of the subtrahend from the numerator of the minuend**

### C4 what the book teaches, chapter by chapter

- **Ch 1 Large numbers and Place Value (pp. 2–18).**
  - *§1.1 Five-digit numbers:* cricket tickets in packets of 1000
    (10 packets = 10,000 "ten thousand"; 2 ten thousands = 20,000;
    23,425 tickets in total); the ten-thousands place is called
    **ajut** (this book's spelling; Class 3 English spells it "Ayut").
  - *§1.2 Six, seven and eight-digit numbers:* a new place, **lac**
    (1 lac = 10 ajut = 100,000; motor vehicles registered in
    Bangladesh in 2013: 137,109); **nijut** (ten lac = 1,000,000,
    also called one million; 2,463,751); **crore** (1 crore = 10 nijut
    = 10,000,000; primary students in Bangladesh in 2013:
    19,584,972).
  - *Commas:* South Asian grouping (1,00,000; 1,95,84,972) to make
    large numbers readable.
  - *Number line:* intervals of 1000 (A = 13,000, B = 26,000);
    numbers increase to the right.
  - *§1.5 Comparison:* compare the number of digits first, then the
    biggest place downwards (38,000 > 36,000; 371,020 > 39,000 —
    the book stages the misreading first, then corrects it with
    commas and the number line); ordering (5238 < 6137 < 7325 <
    7329 < 8324, bicycle prices); largest/smallest numbers from
    digit cards (0 cannot lead; smallest 4-digit example 1479);
    successor and predecessor (999 → 1000; before 10,000).
- **Ch 2 Addition and Subtraction (pp. 19–33).**
  - *§2.1 Addition up to four-digit numbers; §2.2 up to five-digit
    numbers:* 45,736 + 48,797 = 94,533 (males/females in a city);
    candy-shop monthly sales 34,295 + 13,720 + 14,853 + 20,582 +
    12,376 = Tk 95,826; sidewise addition with a tick mark over each

## Confirmed book errors — stated as errors, with the correct value

**Checker correction (2026-11-05): they are NOT all in the Bangla original.** Twelve of them are Bangla-original errors carried into English (E1–E4, E6, E10, the E11 proof label-swap, E13–E16, E18). Seven differ between the versions — E5, E7, E8, E9, the E11 third bullet, E12 and E17 are wrong only in the English translation, and the Bangla original is correct on those points (E12 excepted: the Bangla line has a different defect, a missing operator, not the wrong sign). Each affected item below says which version(s) it is in. Do not teach any of them as fact.

- **E1 — Ch. 1, p. 26 (§1.10, item 4c):** "n(A\B) = n(A) – n(A ∪ B)" is **wrong**. Correct: **n(A\B) = n(A) − n(A∩B)**.
- **E2 — Ch. 1, p. 27 (Exercise Q4c):** "(A ∪ B) ∩ C = (A ∩ C) ∩ (B ∩ C)" is **wrong**. Correct: **(A∪B)∩C = (A∩C)∪(B∩C)**. With the question's own sets (A = {1,3,5,7}, B = {0,2,4,6}, C = {3,4,5,6,7}) the left side is {3,4,5,6,7} and the printed right side is ∅. The correct version is printed as Q7c on the same page.
- **E3 — Ch. 1, p. 28 (Exercise Q9d):** "(P × Q) ∩ R" is a **type error**: P × Q is a set of ordered pairs and R = {0, 1, a} is a set of elements, so the intersection as written cannot equal (c) P × (Q∩R), a set of pairs. Presumably intended: (P×Q) ∩ (P×R).
- **E4 — Ch. 2, p. 41 (geometric-sequence table, item ii):** "3x, 9x², 81x³, …" is **not a geometric sequence** — 9x²/3x = 3x but 81x³/9x² = 9x — yet the book asks for its common ratio and next three terms. For r = 3x the third term should be **27x³**. Visually confirmed in both versions.
- **E5 — Ch. 2, p. 53 (Apu's savings) — ENGLISH ONLY; the Bangla original is correct:** the English says the series "2 + 4 + 8 + 16 + … + 8192" has "the number of terms is n = 12", which is **internally inconsistent**: with n = 12 the last term is 2¹² = **4096** and the sum is **8190**; the last term 8192 needs n = **13** (sum **16382**). The Bangla original says n = **13** (and "13 weeks"), consistent with 8192 — visually confirmed by the checker.
- **E6 — Ch. 2, p. 58 (Exercise Q7(iii)):** Σₖ₌₁ⁿ (−8)(0.5)ᵏ⁻¹ = −16 has **no finite solution**: the partial sum is −16(1 − (1/2)ⁿ), which equals −16 only in the limit n → ∞. The item as set is defective.

The Bangla says to join the points with a smooth (মসৃণ) curve — visually confirmed by the checker.

- **E10 — Ch. 4, p. 99 (§8.4):** "The degree of R(x) is less than the degree of q(x)" — **wrong**; the remainder's degree is less than the degree of the **divisor d(x)**, not the quotient.
- **E11 — Ch. 4, p. 100 (§9) — SPLIT BY VERSION (checker):** the third bullet is wrong **in the English only** — "If the value of x is −2 … divide p(x) by x+1. The remainder will be p(−1)". For p(x) = x⁴ − 3x² + 2x − 2, x = −2 goes with divisor **x + 2** and remainder **p(−2) = −2** (p(−1) = −6). The Bangla bullet is correct (x = −1, divide by x+1, remainder p(−1)). The proof on the same page **is** a Bangla-original error carried into English: it swaps the labels, calling the divisor q(x) ("If the divisor is q(x) = x − a … As the degree of q(x) is 1").
- **E12 — Ch. 5, p. 121 (graphical method) — DIFFERS BY VERSION (checker):** the English says "From equation (i) we get, y = 4x + 5" for 4x − y = 5 — **wrong sign**; correct is **y = 4x − 5**. The Bangla line prints no operator at all — "y = 4x 5", a missing-operator typeset defect (confirmed at 300 dpi) — while its table gives the correct formula "y = 4x − 5". The English table on the same page follows the correct formula (x = 0 → y = −5; y = 7 → x = 3).
- **E13 — Ch. 5, p. 138 (team project):** "Match the solution [p = 1, q = 3 or p = 2, q = 4]" — **neither pair satisfies** q = p² − 2p + 44 (p = 1 gives q = 43; p = 2 gives q = 44). In fact p² − 3p + 42 = 0 has no real solution, so the system has **no real market-equilibrium point at all**.
- **E14 — Ch. 6, p. 156 (Exercise Q3):** "∠B = 90°, AC = 12 cm, BC = 13 cm" — **impossible**: the hypotenuse (12 cm) cannot be shorter than a leg (13 cm). The triangle does not exist.

- **E16 — Ch. 9, p. 223:** "In 1983, Karl Pearson introduced the concept of standard deviation" — **wrong year**; Pearson introduced standard deviation in **1893**.
- **E17 — Ch. 9, p. 225 — ENGLISH ONLY; the Bangla original is correct:** the English says "the average age of Thomas's family members has a much larger age dispersion than that of Aleya's" — **wrong, and it contradicts p. 224**: Aleya's variance is 1750/6 ≈ **291.67**, Thomas's is 2480/31 = **80** — Aleya's dispersion is the larger one (also by range: 50 vs 30). The Bangla sentence correctly says Aleya's dispersion is much larger than Thomas's — visually confirmed by the checker.

(a) Determine the standard deviation of Sobuj and Mouli." — **defective as stated**: one value each has no meaningful standard deviation (a one-value set has SD 0); the question needs a series of daily spends.

## Muddled statements, historical slips and defective wording (not hard maths errors, but do not teach as written)

- **M1 — Ch. 3, pp. 61, 64:** "John Napier first expressed the logarithm considering e as the base" / Napierian logarithm = e-based — historically inaccurate; Napier's logarithms were not base-e (the natural logarithm came later).
- **M2 — Ch. 3, p. 74:** "R = 0 expresses that no earthquake took place" — misleading: magnitude 0 means the amplitude equals the standard (1 micron), not that there was no earthquake. Same page: "The instrument used for measuring earthquake … was invented by Charles Francis Richter" — the seismograph was not invented by Richter.
- **M3 — Ch. 3, p. 80 (Exercise Q6):** asks for "the depreciation of the land" while the scenario is fertiliser *increasing* fertility by 3% a year — depreciation vs appreciation confused; as worded it has no well-defined answer.
- **M4 — Ch. 4, p. 83 (§2):** the degree of a term is defined as "the summation of every term of a variable's index" — garbled wording for "the sum of the exponents of the variables in the term".
- **M5 — Ch. 4, p. 102 (§10.1):** "x − a will be the factor of p(x) if only p(a) = 0" — should be "**if and only if**".
- **M6 — Ch. 4, pp. 108, 111:** "When the degree of p(x) is less than the degree of q(x), there will be no common factor" (and the parallel claim for improper fractions) — false as a general statement; properness says nothing about common factors.
- **M7 — Ch. 5, p. 132:** roots are called "rational" when the discriminant is positive and a perfect square — true only when a, b, c are rational; the book's standard form only says they are real.
- **M8 — Ch. 6, p. 142:** "'gon' (side)" in the etymology of trigonometry — wrong: Greek *gōnia* means **angle**, not side.
- **M9 — Ch. 6, p. 152 (§7):** "geosynchronous line" for the horizontal line of sight — wrong word (should be "horizontal line"); the depression-angle definition on the same page is muddled (it describes the angle between the line of sight and a line on the ground, not between the line of sight and the horizontal).
- **M10 — Ch. 8, pp. 180–181:** "take any two points A and B. This segment AB is an arc" — the straight segment AB is a **chord**, not an arc; and the book calls sector area "circular segment area" throughout the arc/sector section (the area (θ/360)πr² is a **sector**, not a segment).
- **M11 — Ch. 8, p. 186:** the cone curved-surface derivation assumes the paper sector is one-fourth of the circle (l = 4r) and presents πrl as a general derivation — it only holds for that particular 90° sector. The result πrl is correct; the derivation is not general.
- **M12 — Ch. 8, p. 194:** the hemisphere's surface area is "proved" as "sum of area of two circles = πr² + πr² = 2πr²" — the reasoning is wrong (a hemisphere's curved surface is not two circles), and the book never states the total surface area of a solid hemisphere (**3πr²**), although Exercise Problem 2 (p. 195) asks for it.
- **M13 — Ch. 8, p. 198:** right/oblique and regular/irregular prisms are conflated — "when the lateral sides … are equilateral polygons or regular polygons, it is known as a right prism", "if the base … is not an equilateral polygon, it is called an oblique prism", "another name for a right prism is a regular prism". Correct: right/oblique turns on whether the lateral edges are perpendicular to the base; regular/irregular turns on whether the base is a regular polygon. A right prism need not be regular; an oblique prism need not be irregular.
- **M14 — Ch. 8, p. 205:** the pyramid's "apothem" is defined as "the line segment connecting the apex … and any angular point on the base" — that is a **lateral edge**, not the apothem (slant height); "the point where the hypotenuses of a regular pyramid intersect is called the incenter" is meaningless; "the number of edges in the apothem is twice the number of sides" is garbled (a pyramid on an n-gon base has 2n edges in total).
- **M15 — Ch. 8, p. 206:** lateral surface area = (1/2) × base perimeter × slant height is claimed "applicable to all pyramids" — it holds only for **regular** pyramids (one common slant height); a general pyramid's faces have different heights.
- **M16 — Ch. 8, p. 207 (Problems 1 and 3):** the total surface area of a non-regular triangular pyramid cannot be found from base sides + vertical height alone — the slant heights of the three different faces are not determined by the data. Underdetermined as stated (the volumes are determinable).
- **M17 — Ch. 8, p. 208:** Exercise Q2 asks for the "total surface area of a cuboid … slant height is 26 cm" and to "paint the curved surface of the cuboid" — a cuboid has no curved surface and no slant height; the item does not match its figure. Exercise Q1 on the same page asks to "determine the nutritional content of the carrot" — not a mathematics question.
- **M18 — Ch. 9:** p. 219, "mean deviation determined from the range (Mo)" — should be "**mode** (Mo)"; p. 223, "the absolute value of the deviation was used to determine standard deviation" — wrong, SD uses **squared** deviations, not absolute values; p. 230, the SD definition sentence is garbled ("the square root of the value obtained by dividing the sum of the squares of the arithmetic mean of the values …"). P. 216, Single task 2 is garbled ("tallest … is 75.06 cm tall and the plants range in height from 15.37 cm" — presumably range = 15.37 cm, smallest = 59.69 cm, but as printed it is unclear).
- **M19 — Ch. 2, p. 43 (hint box):** "b = c or b² = ac" — garbled; it should be b/a = c/b (i.e. b² = ac). The printed "b = c" is wrong.
- **M20 — Small slips:** Ch. 3, p. 79, "Diving the both sides by 10" (twice; should be "Dividing"); Ch. 4, p. 82, "Minmhaj's" (typo for Minhaj's); Ch. 7, p. 161, "the rays OA and OB meet at two points O"; Ch. 7, p. 166, "the point P(1, 1) on the positive side of the x-axis" (P(1, 1) is not on the x-axis); Ch. 9, p. 232, the SD line prints "= 1.7748 = 1.33 × 4" with the square-root sign missing (√1.7748 ≈ 1.33; the result is ≈ 5.33, the book says 5.32 — a rounding slip).
- **M21 — Units and implausible numbers, Ch. 8:** p. 197 hollow-sphere painting priced in "**Rs** 1.75" while the rest of the book uses Taka; p. 203 Problem 4, "base edge 12 cm, height of the prism pole 17 m" (mixed cm/m); p. 207 Problem 4, "base side 23 cm, height 3.7 meters" (mixed cm/m); p. 209 Exercise Q8, pyramid base side 6 m with slant height **67 m** (height ≈ 66.93 m — implausibly needle-like; likely a misprint); p. 210 Exercise Q13 numbering jumps (i), (ii), (iv), (v) — (iii) is missing; Ch. 2, p. 56, Exercise Q4 has two items both labelled "(a)".
- **M22 — Terminology/wording:** Ch. 4, p. 112, Exercise Q2 uses "three dimensional" to mean "degree three" (and "quadrimonial" for quadrinomial); Ch. 4, p. 112, Exercise Q7, "the length of a cubic reservoir is the inverse multiple of the length of another" (presumably reciprocal — unclear); Ch. 9, p. 234, Exercise Q6, "33.2 is the arithmetic mean deviation of the mass number in the following table. Find the value of p by finding the arithmetic mean" (garbled instruction); Ch. 9, p. 235, Exercise Q9, "another member of 15 values" (should be "another member with value 15").

## Honest negatives — what this book does NOT teach

(Checked by full-text search of the English work; the Bangla work mirrors it.)

- **No probability at all** (0 mentions), no permutations, no combinations.
- **No functions.** The word "function" occurs once, only in "annual function" (a school event). No function concept, domain/range, inverse or composite functions, no graphs presented as functions.
- **No completing the square** as a method (the quadratic formula is derived that way, but completing the square is never taught as a solving technique); quadratics are solved only by middle-term factorisation, the quadratic formula, and graphs.
- **No trigonometric identities.** sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 (and friends) never appear; the only double-angle material is two prove-that items in the Ch. 6 exercise.
- **No straight-line equation** (y = mx + c), no gradient/slope, no distance or midpoint formula, no circle equation. Coordinate geometry is limited to plotting points, polynomial graphs (Ch. 4) and polar coordinates (Ch. 7).
- **No matrices, vectors, complex numbers** (the word "complex" occurs twice, both meaning "complicated"), **no surds, no binomial theorem, no inequalities**, no indices beyond the exponent laws.
- **Logarithms:** natural log is named but never used; **no log tables, no characteristic/mantissa, no antilog** — values come from a calculator/device (p. 70).
- **Mensuration gaps:** **no cylinder formulas at all** — cylinders are explicitly assumed from earlier classes ("you have studied cylinders in detail in previous classes", p. 188) and used only as a prop in the cone-volume experiment. No cuboid/cube surface-area or volume formulas are stated in this book (a cuboid appears only in the garbled Exercise Q2, p. 208). No frustum, no torus.
- **Statistics gaps:** mean, median and mode are **used** (as reference points for mean deviation) but **never defined in this book** — assumed from earlier classes. **Quartile deviation is named only in the Ch. 9 opener diagram (p. 211) and never defined or taught anywhere in the chapter.** No frequency polygons, histograms, ogives, cumulative frequency, coefficient of variation, skewness, quartiles or percentiles.
- **Geometry gaps:** no congruence/similarity theorems, no circle theorems (chord/tangent/angle theorems), no constructions beyond drawing angles with a protractor; polygons appear only via the regular-polygon area formula.
- **Sets:** De Morgan's laws are exercised but never named; no three-set Venn beyond the sports problem; no relations or functions built from sets.
- **No Class 11–12 mathematics in this set** — this is the Class 9 book only, and the C10 files are the same book (see Identity). The book never mentions SSC or any examination.
