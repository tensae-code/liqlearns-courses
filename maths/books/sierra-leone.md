# Sierra Leone — Maths books

**Sierra Leone is CLOSED in the library, and its maths set is 36 files
in hand of 36 recorded — 18 teacher + 18 student, Primary 1–6, JSS 1–3
and SSS 1–3, all in English.** The 36 held files were read and filed
below in four bands on 2026-11-04: **5,227 PDF pages** — Band A
(Primary 1–3) 1,076, Band B (Primary 4–6) 1,167, Band C (JSS 1–3)
1,510, Band D (SSS 1–3) 1,474. Every band's filing then passed a
separate citation check (checker ≠ reader): **662 anchors checked in
total — 609 passed outright, 53 corrected, 0 not found** (Band A 170:
149/21/0; Band B 165: 150/15/0; Band C 165: 156/9/0; Band D 162:
154/8/0). The per-band counts stand in the check block at the end of
each band below. Where this header and a band section disagree, the
section wins: the header was cross-checked against the sections, and
the sections carry the per-book evidence.

**Provenance note on the Band C page total.** The Band C reader's
report stated "1,610 pages", but its own per-file numbers add to
1,510 (142 + 210 + 146 + 170 + 198 + 126 + 154 + 210 + 154), and the
grab record also says 1,510 — an arithmetic slip in the reader's
headline, repeated once in the Band C check file. This filing uses
1,510 throughout, including in the corrected Band C count line.

**Composition — 36 term files forming 12 class/year volumes.** The
set is one programme in two kinds of book, three terms per class:

- **18 Primary teacher Mathematics Lesson Plans** — Primary 1–6 ×
  Terms 1–3, 150 scripted 35-minute lessons per grade (60 + 60 + 30),
  numbered continuously across the year. Each lesson is a
  teacher script (learning outcomes, teaching aids, preparation, and
  Opening / Introduction to the New Material / Guided Practice /
  Independent Practice / Closing, with answers supplied).
- **18 pupil Mathematics Handbooks** — JSS 1–3 + SSS 1–3 × Terms 1–3.
  JSS: 150 lessons per class (term splits vary by class: JSS 1
  45 + 60 + 45, JSS 2 55 + 60 + 35, JSS 3 45 + 60 + 45). SSS: 140 lessons per class
  (48 + 48 + 44), numbered L1–L140 per class, with no L141–L144 in
  any class. Each handbook lesson teaches through Learning Outcomes,
  an Overview with Solved Examples, and a Practice set, with answers
  in a complete answer key at the end of the book.

**Teacher vs pupil — read this before citing anything.** Primary has
NO pupil book in the record: pupils work orally, on the board, in
pairs and in exercise books, and the plans are teacher evidence only
— they fill no pupil-book row, and the plans' generic line about
using "other textbooks" never names, quotes or paginates any pupil
book. The JSS/SSS handbooks ARE pupil books, each with a complete
answer key covering all its lessons, giving final answers only;
construction/diagram answers are often just "Diagram:" (or a
"see diagram"-style note).

**Dating and attribution — only what is printed, and one discrepancy
flagged.** NO year, edition, ISBN or imprint is printed in any of the
36 files, so no citation from this set carries a year. What IS
printed:

- Primary plans print "Ministry of Education, Science and
  Technology" (MEST) on the covers, under "President's Recovery
  Priorities — Education"; the back covers carry UKaid / IRC /
  British Council credits; the foreword is by Dr. Minkailu Bah,
  Minister of Education, Science and Technology, referencing the
  August 2015 Accelerated Teaching Syllabi (the Ebola-response
  catch-up). That August 2015 reference gives the earliest possible
  context, not a publication date.
- JSS/SSS handbooks print MBSSE (Ministry of Basic and Senior
  Secondary Education) and "Free Quality School Education", with a
  foreword by Minister Alpha Osman Timbo thanking DFID (the JSS
  foreword presents the books as support for self-study aimed at
  BECE performance; the SSS foreword cites the national curriculum
  and WAEC examinations).
- **"Leh Wi Lan" and "SSEIP" appear NOWHERE in the printed text of
  any of the 36 files.** The set is NOT attributed to Leh Wi
  Lan/SSEIP as printed fact anywhere in this filing. Any
  programme-level attribution of that kind comes from the download
  list / source provenance only and is unverified against print.
- JSS PDF metadata dates (created July 2018, modified January 2019;
  JSS 3 Term 3 March 2020) are metadata only, never a printed
  publication date, and are not used as one here.

**Page rules.** JSS/SSS handbooks: printed page = PDF page − 5
throughout — checker-verified across all 450 JSS lessons (every
lesson-header PDF page minus 5 equals its contents printed page,
zero mismatches) and across the SSS sweep (including the body-heading
sweep of all 420 SSS lessons). Primary plans cite printed pages

every contents lesson is present in the body.

**Gaps proven from the books:**

- **Primary (all six grades):** no pupil book; no homework or
  assessment sections in the plans; money lessons use
  pre-redenomination Leone and need re-basing before reuse. The
  plans are complete as teacher scripts (no lesson missing from any
  file), but they are teacher evidence only.
- **JSS — never taught:** surds, logarithms, quadratic formula,
  simultaneous equations, sine/cosine rules, circle theorems,
  bearings, loci, vectors/matrices, pyramids/cones/spheres,
  histograms, standard deviation, dependent-event probability
  calculation. JSS 2 Term 3's "Statistics and Probability" theme
  contains no probability lessons. Exam preparation is thin: the
  BECE aim in the foreword plus 4 incidental mentions, no past
  papers or mocks. (Corrected by checking — see correction 1 below:
  set operations ARE taught.)
- **SSS — never taught:** matrices, calculus, complex numbers,
  binomial theorem, natural logs, 3-D trigonometry, formal
  proof-writing, distributions, sum to infinity. (Corrected by
  checking — see corrections 2–3 below: conditional probability and
  geometric-series sums ARE taught, in narrower form.) No mock exams
  or past-paper sets: SSS 3 ends with a 20-lesson whole-course
  review (L121–L140) as its main WASSCE-style preparation.

The only years in the text are in the foreword: the minister “since 2007” and the “Accelerated Teaching Syllabi in August 2015”. **Foreword (same text in all nine, printed p. unnumbered, PDF p. 3):** signed “Dr. Minkailu Bah, Minister of Education, Science and Technology”. It announces new lesson plans “across all primary and JSS school grades in Language Arts and Mathematics”, 2,700 plans in total, “written by experienced Sierra Leonean educators together with international experts” and reviewed by ministry officials, with a teacher training programme for the plans.

**Introduction (“Introduction to the Lesson Plan Manual”, one page, same wording in all nine):** the plans “are based on the National Curriculum and meet the requirements established by the Ministry of Education, Science and Technology”, “will not take the whole term, so use spare time to review material or prepare for exams”, and “Teachers can use other textbooks alongside or instead of these lesson plans.” Ten numbered teaching tips follow (read the plan beforehand, check learning outcomes and aids, review last lesson, follow time allocations, mix whole-class/pair/individual work, use the board and visual aids, involve quiet pupils, congratulate correct answers).

**Lesson anatomy (every lesson, 35 minutes):** a header block (lesson title, theme, lesson number, class, time) plus three columns — Learning Outcomes (“By the end of the lesson, pupils will be able to…”), Teaching Aids, Preparation — then fully scripted steps in timed blocks, usually Opening, Introduction to the New Material, Guided Practice, Independent Practice, Closing. The script is written as teacher speech (“Say: …”, “Ask: …”) with expected answers in brackets. Pupils answer orally, work on the board, work in pairs, and write in exercise books. **There is no pupil textbook in this record**: no pupil book is supplied, quoted, or paginated; pupil texts, if any exist for primary, live nowhere in these files. There are no homework sections, no formal assessment sections and no answer keys beyond the bracketed answers inside the teacher script. Checking happens inside the lesson (oral answers, board work, independent practice).

**Materials world:** low/no-cost classroom objects throughout — plastic bottles, counters, sticks, stones, leaves, bottle tops, hands, thumbs, footsteps, arm spans; the board, a large number line and a 100 chart as permanent displays; paper coins and bills made by the teacher for money lessons. Sierra Leonean settings recur: the market, palm oil, notebooks, buying and selling, designing fabric for a wrap skirt or shirt.

**Dating caution for course builders:** the money lessons use pre-redenomination Leone denominations (coins 10, 50, 100, 500; notes 1,000, 2,000, 5,000, 10,000). That currency scale no longer matches Leone in circulation after the 2022 redenomination, so money examples must be re-based before reuse — the maths (counting, change to 20) is unaffected.

**Page numbering:** printed page numbers restart at 2 for Lesson 1 in every file. Front matter is unnumbered: PDF p. 1 cover (image-only), PDF p. 2 blank, PDF p. 3 foreword, contents (3 pages in Terms 1–2, 2 pages in Term 3), then the introduction. After the last lesson each file has blank text-empty pages and then the image-only back cover (see trailing counts per section). Primary 1–2 lessons are 2 printed pages each; Primary 3 lessons are mostly 3 printed pages, including full-page visual-aid pages (blank number lines, 100 charts, place-value charts, part-part-whole models) that sit inside the lesson span.

## Primary 1, Term 1 — Lessons 1–60 (printed pp. 2–121)

**File:** `Lesson-Plans-for-Primary-1-Mathematics-Term-1.pdf` — Lesson numbers M_01_001–M_01_060 (underscores — the only file in the band using underscores; all others use hyphens). 60 lessons × 2 printed pages.

**Topic blocks.** L1–13 counting to 10 (objects, forwards/backwards, writing numerals, pictorial and real-object representation, counting on, counting outside the classroom). L14–27 addition to 10 (counters, real objects, fingers, number bonds to 10, writing sums with correct symbols, then a five-lesson word-problem run: identify, solve one-step, construct from pictures, represent by drawing, construct freely). L28–37 subtraction to 10 in the same shape (fingers, counters, real objects, symbol practice, word-problem run). L58–60 a first step to 100 (counting in ones, in 2s with counters, in 5s on the number line).

Word problems are taught as a skill in itself — pupils first spot the numbers and the operation in a spoken problem, then solve, then invent their own from pictures.

## Band A closing note
**Series pattern.** One continuous 150-lesson sequence per grade (60 + 60 + 30), every lesson 35 minutes, every lesson scripted in the same Say/Ask format with bracketed answers, every term opening with the same foreword and introduction. Number range grows by term: Class 1 to 10, then to 100, then to 20 for operations (multiplication and money only); Class 2 to 100, then to 1,000; Class 3 to 1,000, then to 10,000. Operations grow in the same order: counting → addition/subtraction → multiplication as repeated addition → tables → renaming → multi-digit visual multiplication/division → like-fraction arithmetic.

**Grade progression.** Class 1 is concrete and oral (objects, fingers, songs, position words). Class 2 adds structure (place value, number line, commutative law, mental strategies, word-problem construction as its own skill, the data project, odd/even). Class 3 adds formalism (expanded form, renaming, standard units, angles as turns, visual multi-digit multiplication/division, equivalent and like fractions, time to the minute). The measurement arc is the cleanest through-line in the band: everyday language → non-standard units (Class 1–2, all quantities) → standard units (Class 3 Term 2 length, Term 3 area, mass, volume, capacity), with conversion explicitly not taught.

## Band C closing note
### Series pattern

One series, one format, three classes × three terms × 150 lessons = **450 lessons**. Every lesson is a self-contained practice activity (Outcomes → Overview → Solved Examples → Practice) aligned to a Teachers' Guide lesson plan it never reproduces — the handbook assumes the teaching happened in class and supplies explanation plus practice for self-study. The Introduction says so explicitly, and the books make sense that way: overviews are short, worked examples carry the teaching weight, and practice sets are long. Sierra Leonean everyday contexts (markets, cassava, palm oil, leones, Freetown/Bo/Kenema, village chiefs, football) run through all three classes.

### Class progression

- **JSS 1 (Lessons 1–150):** foundations — factors/multiples/HCF/LCM, two index laws, fractions, decimals, rounding, percentages, ratio, integers, simple proportion and money (interest/discount/commission/tax), metric units, perimeter/area/volume of rectangles/squares/triangles/circles/cubes/cuboids, angles and constructions, first algebra (patterns, like terms, one-variable equations, Cartesian points), first statistics (tally → bar/line/pie, mean/median/mode/range) and first probability (one event, words → fractions → percents).
- **JSS 2 (Lessons 1–150):** consolidation plus depth — significant figures, all six index laws with negative powers, profit/loss, percentages over 100%, rates/unit prices, direct/indirect proportion, income/sales tax, time, full mensuration (parallelograms, trapeziums, triangular prisms, cylinders, composite solids, surface area), quadrilateral/polygon angle sums, transformations and scale, geometric patterns, algebra through expansion/factorisation/substitution/linear equations and first line graphing, statistics extended (frequency tables, pie-chart angles, stem diagrams).
- **JSS 3 (Lessons 1–150):** specialisation plus revision — sets (including union, intersection and complement in L13 [CHECKER'S CORRECTION]), Roman numerals, base 2, financial literacy, fractional powers, standard form, Pythagoras, congruence/similarity, right-triangle trigonometry with printed tables, quadratics (expand/factorise/complete the square, no formula), two-variable linear equations and slope (through y = mx + c), inequalities, grouped-data statistics, two-event probability with sample spaces and trees, and a final mensuration revision block.

### Spiral repeats (same topic, higher each year)

Indices (2 laws → 6 laws → fractional powers); percentages (basics → profit/loss/over-100% → revision); ratio/proportion (ratio → rates/proportion → revision); Cartesian plane (points → line graphing → slope); data handling (lists/charts → frequency/stem/pie angles → grouped data); mensuration (basic shapes → prisms/cylinders/surface area → revision); algebra (expressions → equations → quadratics/inequalities). A course builder should treat the JSS 3 version as the capstone and the earlier versions as its prerequisites, not as separate topics.

Set difference; three-set Venn problems; set laws; surds; logarithms; the quadratic formula and solving quadratics for roots; simultaneous equations; sine/cosine rules and non-right-angled trigonometry; angles of elevation/depression by name; circle theorems; bearings; loci; vectors and matrices; pyramids, cones and spheres (volume or surface area); histograms and cumulative frequency; standard deviation; dependent-event probability calculation; tessellations; compass constructions beyond JSS 1 Term 2. Band D holds 9 files: SSS 1–3 × Terms 1–3, all pupil handbooks, 1,474 PDF pages, 420 lessons (140 per class: 48 + 48 + 44; no L141–L144).
**About this band.** Lesson titles are as printed (contents page wording, checked against the lesson headings in the body); the short content line after each lesson is the reader's own paraphrase, not a transcription. Printed page numbers are used throughout. In every file the mapping is: cover = PDF p. 1, foreword = PDF p. 2, "do not write in the books" notice = PDF p. 3, contents = PDF pp. 4–5, introduction = printed p. 1 (PDF p. 6), WASSCE key-takeaways page = printed p. 2 (PDF p. 7), first lesson = printed p. 3 (PDF p. 8). So printed page = PDF page − 5 for all numbered pages.
**Series pattern (applies to all nine files).**

- Each file is a term's worth of numbered practice-activity lessons, not a
  conventional textbook: every lesson has the same shape — a header (lesson
  title, Theme, practice-activity code, class), one or more Learning
  Outcomes, an Overview that teaches the point with definitions and
  worked "Solved Examples", then a "Practice Activity" set of questions
  (usually 5–8 numbered items, many with lettered parts, mixing bare
  computation, diagrams to draw or read, and word problems). Answers are
  NOT in the lesson; they sit in an answer key at the end of the book
  (final answers only, no worked solutions; diagram answers are often
  given only as the word "Diagram:").
- Lesson numbering runs continuously across the three terms of each class
  (SSS 1: L1–L140; SSS 2: L1–L140; SSS 3: L1–L140), restarting at L1 for
  each class. Terms 1 and 2 hold 48 lessons each; Term 3 holds 44 lessons
  each — there is no L141–L144 in any class. Activity codes are
  PHM1-L001… (SSS 1), PHM2-L001… (SSS 2), PHM3-L001… (SSS 3),
  with two exceptions in SSS 3 that print a TGM3 prefix instead: SSS 3
  Term 2 L72 (TGM3-L072) and SSS 3 Term 3 L99 (TGM3-L099). [CHECK:
  added — the draft's blanket PHM-only claim missed these two.] The SSS 3
  headers vary between the labels "Practice Activity:" and "Lesson

- There are NO unit or chapter titles: the only grouping is the Theme
  label on each lesson (Numbers and Numeration, Algebraic Processes,
  Geometry, Mensuration, Logical Reasoning, Trigonometry, Bearings,
  Statistics and Probability, Vectors and Transformations, Review, and
  the SSS 2/SSS 3 opening "Review of SSS 1 / SSS 2" labels). Theme
  capitalisation is inconsistent in print ("Number and numeration" vs

- Common front matter in all nine files: cover (see below); a Foreword

## Band D closing note
Series pattern: SSS mathematics here is a three-year, 420-lesson
practice-activity course (140 lessons per class, 48 + 48 + 44 across
the terms), built to be used alongside a teacher's lesson plan for
each lesson. Every lesson teaches through a short overview plus
worked examples and then sets independent practice; answers are
collected in a key at the back of each term book, with no worked
solutions and no teacher's notes in the pupil book itself.

Class progression: SSS 1 lays number foundations (bases, modular
arithmetic, indices, base-10 logs with tables, sets, surds) and
then first algebra (linear equations, change of subject), functions,
quadratics and first geometry (angles, triangles, Pythagoras).
SSS 2 deepens algebra (simultaneous equations including
linear+quadratic, variation, algebraic fractions, formal logic),
then geometry/mensuration/construction with compasses, then
trigonometry (ratios, general angle, unit circle, sin/cos graphs,
sine/cosine rules, bearings) and first statistics (charts,
averages, histograms, cumulative frequency, quartiles). SSS 3 opens
with review, then circle theorems and solid mensuration, then
commercial maths (ratio/rate/interest/tax), vectors and
transformations, and finally probability, dispersion (standard and
mean deviation) and a 20-lesson whole-course review that doubles as
the band's only explicit WASSCE-style preparation.

Cross-checks between terms: numbering is continuous and titles do
not repeat across terms except for deliberate review lessons
(SSS 2 Term 1 L1–L4; SSS 3 Term 1 L1–L4; SSS 3 Term 3 L121–L140) and
the "Part 1 / Part 2" teaching pairs inside a topic. Two topics are
split across classes rather than repeated: logarithms are finished
in SSS 1 Term 2 and only revised inside SSS 3 Term 3 L122; statistics
runs SSS 2 Term 3 → SSS 3 Term 1 L4 (review) → SSS 3 Term 3 L109–L120
(dispersion). One progression quirk, not a gap: geometric-series sums are NOT
taught in SSS 2 Term 2 (arithmetic-series sums only), and are first
taught in the SSS 3 Term 3 review lesson L123 itself, which states
both GP sum formulae and works a GP-sum example. Sum to infinity is
still never taught — L123 says only that the sum of the first n terms
can be found, and remarks that 'it is impossible to find the sum of
an infinite sequence'. [CHECK: corrected — the draft claimed GP
sums were never taught anywhere.]

Gaps for the whole band (topics never taught): matrices; calculus
(differentiation/integration — curve gradients stay at hand-drawn
tangents); complex numbers; binomial theorem; natural logarithms;
three-dimensional trigonometry; formal geometric proof-writing;
formal conditional-probability notation, Bayes and distributions
(conditional probability itself is taught in SSS 3 Term 3 L106);
sampling and correlation;
any mock examination, timed test or past-paper set. What exam
preparation exists is: the front-matter WASSCE error notes in every
book, scattered "WASSCE-style" practice lessons (notably SSS 2 Term 1
L40 and SSS 3 Term 3 L120), and the SSS 3 Term 3 review block.
