### What the book teaches, chapter by chapter (printed pages)

- **Ch. 1 — Limits (pp. 1–40):** the limit concept through sequences, with an ε-δ definition and a worked ε-δ proof; one-sided limits; the limit laws; the squeeze theorem; rational-function limits and the 0/0 form; limits at infinity and degree rules; the indeterminate forms, with e built as lim(1 + 1/n)^n from a table; trigonometric limits, with lim sin θ/θ = 1 proved by areas; continuity (the three conditions, three types of discontinuity, continuity of combinations and composites). Proofs are given, not just rules.
- **Ch. 2 — The derivative (pp. 41–82):** slope of a curve and tangent slope as a limit of secants; average rate of change; the derivative from first principles and its geometric meaning, with tangent-line equations; the rules (constant, power, sum, difference, product, quotient), each proved; root functions; the chain rule; differentiability and one-sided derivatives; derivatives of sin, cos (from first principles) and tan; implicit differentiation, including a second implicit derivative; higher-order derivatives.
- **Ch. 3 — Applications of the derivative (pp. 83–132):** increasing/decreasing functions; critical points and local extrema; absolute extrema; a six-step curve-sketching procedure; concavity and inflection points; graphs of quadratics; asymptotes; homographic functions; cubic graphs and the centre of symmetry; Rolle's theorem; the mean value theorem with its proof; L'Hôpital's rule; optimisation and rate problems.
- **Ch. 4 — Integrals (pp. 133–172):** Riemann sums with lower and upper sums; the integral concept and the indefinite integral; indefinite-integral properties and the power rule, including the explicit non-rules for products and quotients; the definite integral and its properties, all proved; the fundamental theorem of calculus, parts 1 and 2, both proved; substitution; integration by parts.
- **Ch. 5 — Derivatives and integrals of logarithmic and exponential functions (pp. 173–198):** derivatives of aˣ, ln x, log_a x and log_a g(x), the first two proved; the derivative of an inverse function; derivatives of arcsin, arccos, arctan, arccot, each derived; partial fractions in three cases (distinct linear, repeated, irreducible quadratic factors); integrals of exponential functions; integrals of logarithmic functions by parts; evaluating integrals by partial fractions.
- **Ch. 6 — Applications of the integral (pp. 199–222):** area bounded by one curve, with sign handled by splitting at the roots; area between two curves; volumes of solids of revolution, deriving the sphere, the cone and the ellipsoid volumes; arc length in Cartesian and parametric form, including the circumference of the circle.
- **Ch. 7 — Statistics (pp. 223–260):** the probability distribution function — expectation and variance of a random variable, discrete and continuous; Bernoulli trials and the binomial distribution, with cumulative probabilities; the Poisson distribution as the binomial limit, checked numerically against the binomial; the normal distribution and its density; standardising, with TWO printed standard normal tables — a cumulative table (p. 241) and a mean-to-z table (p. 242); sampling and sample types; the sampling distribution of the mean, built by complete enumeration of samples of 2 from {1, 2, 3}; the central limit theorem; the sampling distribution of a proportion.
- **Ch. 8 — Probability (pp. 261–280 in hand):** discrete and continuous sample spaces; equally likely events; probability in continuous spaces by length, area and volume ratios (including a point chosen in a cube falling in the inscribed sphere, π/6); conditional probability with tree diagrams; the law of total probability and Bayes' theorem, for two and for n partitions, with worked examples; the multiplication principle; independent events; key points and general exercises (pp. 279–280).
