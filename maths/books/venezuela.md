### What the book teaches, chapter by chapter (printed pages)

- **¿Dónde estoy? (pp. 8–17):** position and direction words — delante/atrás, adentro/afuera, arriba/abajo, derecha/izquierda (right hand vs left hand marked with ribbons), al fondo, cerca/lejos, entre.
- **Colecciones (pp. 18–27):** collections: grouping objects by likeness, saying what a collection's elements have in common; comparing two collections by one-to-one matching with arrows — más, menos, tantos como; completing a collection until it has as many elements as another.
- **Los números (pp. 28–41):** numbers 1–10: counting by touching one finger per element — the count of a collection is its number; numeral symbols with a drawing rhyme for each digit; number words UNO–DIEZ; what numbers are for.
- **Contando de diez en diez (pp. 42–55):** grouping in tens — a decena is 10; reading and writing two-digit numbers (17 = 1 decena y 7 unidades); the centena = 10 decenas = 100; place decomposition up to 320 (3 centenas, 2 decenas, 0 unidades); a seed-grouping table recording tens and leftover units.
- **Orden y ordeno (pp. 56–61):** ordinal numbers primero, segundo, tercero…; ordering the months of the year with ordinals up to décimo primero / décimo segundo; ordering the steps of a task.
- **De ida y vuelta (pp. 62–67):** ascending and descending counting series (elephants arriving one by one to 10; dogs leaving one by one); adding 1 gives the successor, taking 1 away the predecessor.
- **¡A sumar! (pp. 68–79):** addition as joining collections; the + sign read "más"; sumandos and suma named; vertical layout; carrying introduced and worked (7 + 3 = 10; a money problem Bs. 10 + 8 + 14 = Bs. 32 with units 8 + 4 = 12, carry the ten; a book problem totalling 6 + 4 + 5 = 15 with carry). All worked answers recomputed correct.
- **¡A restar! (pp. 80–87):** subtraction as taking away; the − sign; minuendo, sustraendo, diferencia named; practice sets within 20; subtraction stated as the opposite operation to addition.
- **El número cero (pp. 88–91):** zero — the Maya zero, a blank cell read as cero aciertos; a card game of n + 0 / n + 1 / n + 2 sums showing that adding 0 leaves the number; what the 0 means in 40.
- **¿Problemas a mí? (pp. 92–101):** word-problem strategies — solving by drawing (a family diagram finds 3 children × 2 = 6 grandchildren, drawn not multiplied), draw-or-cross-out counts, organising conditions in a table; building an abacus and playing represent/add/subtract games with bead values 1, 10, 100.
- **¡Llegó el circo! (pp. 102–109):** 3D shapes — esfera (round, no points, only rolls), cilindro (two flat round faces, caras planas), paralelepípedo (box shape); geometric solids vs the plane figures obtained by tracing their outlines or flashlight shadows.
- **Con cubos, conos y pirámides (pp. 110–115):** the cube (6 flat faces, 8 esquinas/vértices, stated in dialogue), the cono (a point), the pirámide (flat triangular faces); tracing outlines; a faces/vertices counting table to complete.
- **Triángulo, rectángulo y cuadrado (pp. 116–123):** the plane figures from solids' faces; a song of properties (square: equal sides; triangle: three sides; rectangle: opposite sides equal); building a tangram from a square.
- **Círculo y circunferencia (pp. 124–129):** the coloured interior plus its border is the círculo, the border alone the circunferencia; the centre appears only as a find-it activity — no radius or diameter.
- **La cuarta y el paso (pp. 130–139):** non-standard length measures — la cuarta (thumb-to-little-finger span) and el paso (a pace); the book says explicitly that they are not exact because they vary from person to person.
- **¡Y sigo midiendo! (pp. 140–151):** capacity — the litro, symbol l, comparing capacities by pouring; time — a day defined by the Sun's return, the week calendar, 1 day = 24 hours, 1 hour = 60 minutes, analogue clock reading on the hour (10:00, 3:00, 1:00); the year's length posed as a question.
- **¡Con las manos en la masa! (pp. 152–161):** size ordering by height with más alto/bajo/grande/pequeño que; the centimetre ruler introduced; mass — masa vs the everyday word peso, the kilogramo named, balance equilibrium = equal mass, comparing masses by balancing classroom objects.
- **Datos, datos y más datos (pp. 162–171):** data — counting from a picture; bar graphs (bars as rectangles, horizontal or vertical); a pictograma defined and read (children per grade who like oranges); a coin-count table by denomination (1, 5, 10, 12½, 25, 50 céntimos).

### Honest negatives (what this book does NOT teach)

- No calculus: no derivatives and no integrals anywhere; Cavalieri's principle is used intuitively for volumes.
- Statistics stops at Pearson correlation and the least-squares line: no significance testing, no other correlation coefficients; probability stops at the binomial distribution (no normal distribution, no Bayes; expectation appears only as E(x) = np).
- No sequences or series and no trigonometric functions course (both are the 4to año book); complex numbers appear only as roots of polynomials.
- Matrices stop at 3×3 inverses by the adjugate: no eigenvalues, no diagonalisation. Linear programming is two-variable graphing only: no simplex method.
- Conics are never rotated and never put in polar form; the hyperbola's asymptotes are never given, even in the worked example. Quadric surfaces appear only at the level of definitions and the cylinder/sphere equations at the end of Lesson 12; no spherical or cylindrical coordinates.
- Vectors are never a topic in their own right in this book (they are assumed from earlier years); direction numbers stand in for them in Lesson 11.
