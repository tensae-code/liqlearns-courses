**The course, in outline:** Dimotiko (Grades 1-6) runs one
"Mathematics of Nature and Life" series per grade (pupil book +
workbook volumes + teacher guide), with Grade 5 as the newer series
against Grades 4 and 6's older-programme front matter. Gymnasio
(Grades 7-9) runs one pupil book per year, each in two parts
(Arithmetic-Algebra, then Geometry, with Trigonometry in Grade 9).
Lykeio (Grades 10-12) branches: common Algebra in Grade 10, Algebra
plus an orientation Mathematics book in Grade 11, and in Grade 12 the
Analysis book (Part B only, see gaps) for the positive/health/
economics-IT tracks versus Probability and Statistics for the
humanities track; Euclidean Geometry runs as one two-volume work
across Grades 10-11. EPAL runs a separate applied ladder: Financial
Mathematics and Business Statistics (with a worked-solutions companion)
for the Administration and Economics sector in Grade 11, and a compact
general-education Mathematics and Statistics book (with its own
solutions companion) in Grade 12. Publisher throughout is the Ministry
of Education / Institute of Educational Policy (IEP) /
ITYE-"Diofantos". Language is Greek throughout, in every file.

## How to read the page numbers in this draft

- In almost every Band A file the printed folio is one less than the PDF
  page (front matter is unnumbered, so printed p. 11 = PDF p. 12, etc.).
  This was verified against the printers' footers in each file.
- Exception: the Grade 3 pupil file binds four parts into one PDF
  (Part 1 = units 1–2, Part 2 = units 3–5, Part 3 = units 6–8,
  Part 4 = unit 9). Units 1–2 below are cited by the book's
  continuous printed folio (printed p. = PDF p. − 1). Units 3–9 are
  cited by part and by the part-page offset, counting from the
  part's first page (e.g. "Part 2, printed 3–4" in the draft's
  shorthand means Part 2 pages 3–4); the book's continuous folios
  for those units are: unit 3, 40–55; unit 4, 56–67; unit 5, 68–81;
  unit 6, 82–95; unit 7, 96–109; unit 8, 110–125; unit 9, 126–141,
  followed by two blank leaves. The draft's earlier wording, which
  said the part folios restart at 1, is corrected here: the held
  file's printed folios run continuously, and only the part-page
  counting restarts.
- "Chapter" (Κεφάλαιο) = one lesson, normally a two-page spread.
  "Unit" (Ενότητα) = a group of chapters ending in a revision lesson
  (Επαναληπτικό μάθημα) and, at period ends, an assessment criterion
  (Κριτήριο αξιολόγησης).
- The books are organised in three periods (Περίοδος) per year.
  Colour coding is used throughout Grades 1 and 3: numbers, operations,
  geometry, measurement and problems each have their own colour, and each
  activity carries a key-symbol showing the kind of work expected.
- No word-for-word transcription is used below: summaries and structure
  only, with very short quoted fragments where a wording matters.

## G1-W1 — Pupil book, Teuxos A (chapters 1–32) — 82 PDF pp.

File: grade-01_gov_greece_mathematics_teuxos-a.pdf. Book code 0-10-0007;
Teuxos A ISBN 978-960-06-2459-5. Front matter: how the book is built
(printed 4–5), the book's heroes (printed 6–7), contents (printed 8–9).
The heroes pages are short biographies of Pythagoras of Samos
(c. 600 BC) and Hypatia of Alexandria (370–415 AD), presented as the
first woman mathematician in history; the same two pages are repeated
in Teuxos B. Each chapter opens with a classroom game or dramatised
situation and works through numbered activities; the pupil is
constantly asked to show, draw, cut, build or act before writing
symbols.

Period A (units 1–3, ch. 1–24) and the start of Period B (unit 4,
ch. 25–32).

- Unit 1 (printed 11–28): Numbers to 5 — space and shapes. Ch. 1
  Orientation in space (above/below, right/left and similar expressions,
  taught through movement games); ch. 2 Geometric shapes (plane shapes
  and the solid bodies met in everyday surroundings); ch. 3 Comparison
  and estimation of quantities; ch. 4 The numbers 1 to 5; ch. 5
  Counting, reading and writing the numbers (I); ch. 6 the same (II);
  ch. 7 Addition and analysis of numbers to 5 (I); ch. 8 revision
  lesson.
- Unit 2 (printed 29–46): Addition and analysis of numbers to 5.
  Ch. 9 Comparison of magnitudes and vocabulary (tall/short, long/
  short, wide/narrow); ch. 10 The numbers 6 to 10 (I); ch. 11 the same
  (II); ch. 12 Comparison of numbers — the symbols =, > and < (taught
  with a whale's-mouth game); ch. 13 Addition and analysis of numbers
  to 5 (II); ch. 14 Writing addition with symbols (+ and =);
  ch. 15 Problems; ch. 16 revision lesson.
- Unit 3 (printed 47–62): Numbers to 20 — sums to 10 — coins.
  Ch. 17 The numbers 10 to 20 (counting, reading, writing); ch. 18
  Sums to 10 and the commutative property of addition; ch. 19 Ordinal
  numbers and doubles (taught with a block-of-flats lift game: first,
  second … tenth); ch. 20 Coins to 10 (a classroom supermarket for
  buying, selling and giving change); ch. 21 Additive analysis of
  numbers 6 to 10 (a kangaroo game); ch. 22 Problems; ch. 23 revision
  lesson; then the 1st assessment criterion.
- Unit 4 (printed 63–80): Subtraction — drawing lines — patterns.
  Ch. 25 The numbers to 50; ch. 26 Drawing lines (with ruler or free
  hand); ch. 27 Patterns (everyday examples, including the rhythmic,
  repeating sound of a train); ch. 28 Subtraction with a small
  subtrahend (through the tale of the goat and the seven kids);
  ch. 29 Distinguishing the symbols + and −; ch. 30 Subtraction with
  a large subtrahend; ch. 31 The complement (the harder subtraction
  form, e.g. finding what must be added to reach the total);
  ch. 32 revision lesson. Teuxos A ends here; the copyright notice is
  on printed p. 80 and the back cover follows.

What this book does NOT teach: no multiplication or division as
operations (only sharing/counting situations in play); no numbers
above 50; no formal written column addition or subtraction; no
fractions, no measurement with standard units (only comparison of
continuous magnitudes), no area, no clock time, no symmetry yet
(symmetry arrives in Teuxos B, ch. 56).

Held-copy defects: the copyright page (printed p. 80) extracts in a
broken font — the page itself renders normally. Otherwise the text
layer is clean.

# Band findings

## Progression across Grades 1–3

- Number range: to 100 (G1) → to 1,000 taught, 4-digit recognised
  (G2) → to 10,000 with decimals (G3).
- Operations: addition/subtraction to 10, then to 50/70/100 with
  crossing the ten, empirical grouping/sharing (G1) → tables to 11,
  column addition with carrying, column subtraction with borrowing,
  fair sharing written as division (G2) → four-digit operations,
  the multiplication algorithm, divisions (I)/(II), fractions,
  decimal fractions and decimals (G3).
- Geometry/measurement: orientation, shapes, solids, lines, patterns,
  symmetry, informal capacity/weight, coins, clock order (G1) →
  symmetry axes, solids, tangram, centimetres, metres, kilos/grams,
  banknotes, clock (o'clock/half past), perpendicular/parallel
  (G2) → cm/mm, nets, compass-and-ruler constructions, right angles,
  mass in grams, tilings/mosaics, symmetry, time durations, area by
  tile-counting (G3).
- Problems: picture problems and one-step situations (G1) →
  explicit problem-solving steps (understand, plan, solve, check),
  estimation, verification by a second strategy, correcting and
  completing faulty problems, compound problems (a)–(e) (G2) →
  multi-step problems with data in tables, diagrams and bills (G3).
- Assessment: revision lesson per unit everywhere; assessment
  criteria at period ends in G1 (after ch. 24, 44, 64) and G3
  (after units 3, 6, 9); Grade 2 adds individual assessment sheets
  per unit with a scale analysis and a peer-assessment sheet.
- Method, constant across the band: start from a game, story or
  everyday scene; manipulate objects before symbols; discuss
  strategies in class; verify by another method; keep the colour
  coding (numbers / operations / geometry / measurement / problems)
  so a pupil can see which strand a lesson belongs to.

## Band C as a whole — progression, overlaps and gaps

### The spine

- Gymnasio (W1→W2→W3) builds one continuous ladder: naturals → fractions → decimals →
  elementary equations → percentages → proportion → signed rationals (W1); first-degree
  equations and inequalities, real numbers, three function forms, first statistics, areas,
  Pythagoras, acute-angle trigonometry, vectors as arrows, circle measurement, solids (W2);
  polynomials and identities, quadratics, systems, the quadratic function, first probability,
  similarity and Thales, trigonometry to 180° with sine and cosine laws (W3).
- Lykeio general (W4→W6/W7→W12, plus W11 for humanities): W4 re-founds logic, sets,
  probability, reals, equations, inequalities, progressions and functions at Lykeio level;
  W6 adds systems, deeper function properties, full trigonometry, polynomials and
  exponentials/logarithms; W7 (positive-studies track) adds vectors with dot product,
  analytic geometry and conics, and elementary number theory; W12 (final year, positive /
  health / economics–IT tracks) adds limits, continuity, differential and integral calculus;
  W11 (final year, humanities track) is the alternative final-year book — probability with
  combinatorics plus descriptive statistics through normal distribution, contingency tables
  and correlation.
- Geometry (W5) runs alongside as its own deductive spine: Gymnasio intuition rebuilt as
  proof (Vol. A, Grade 10), then proportions, similarity, metric relations, areas, circle
  measurement, space geometry and solids (Vol. B, Grade 11).
- EPAL (W8/W9/W10, W13/W14) is a separate, applied ladder: commercial arithmetic (rule of
  three, percentages, simple and compound interest, discounting, bills of exchange, annuities,
  loans) and business statistics (descriptive measures, regression, correlation, time series)
  for the Administration and Economics sector; and a compact general-education book
  (differentiation, descriptive statistics, probability with combinatorics and conditional
  probability) for other EPAL pupils, each with a worked-solutions companion.

### Overlaps a merger should know about

- Probability is taught three times at three depths: W3 Ch. 5 (sets, sample space, classical
  definition), W4 Ch. 1 (same, Lykeio level), then W11 Units 1.1–1.4 (with combinatorics) for
  humanities and W13 Ch. 3 (with combinatorics and conditional probability) for EPAL.
- Statistics is taught in W2 Ch. 4 (mean, median), W9 (full descriptive + regression +
  time series, business-flavoured), W11 Part 2 (descriptive + normal + contingency +
  correlation, humanities-flavoured) and W13 Ch. 2 (descriptive + regression + correlation,
  compact). The four treatments agree on definitions; they differ in depth and examples.
- Trigonometry is taught in W2 (acute angles), W3 (to 180°, sine/cosine laws) and W6
  (functions, identities, equations, graphs) — a clean three-stage ladder with no
  contradictions found.
- The compound-interest formula appears in W4 Ch. 5 (as an application of geometric
  progressions) and is then developed commercially in W8 Ch. 7; the EPAL treatment is the
  fuller one.
