Areas: parallelogram/rhomboid A = b×h deduced by cutting and rearranging into a rectangle; rhombus A = D×d/2; trapezoid A = (B+b)×h/2 deduced by doubling into a parallelogram. Measuring right/acute/obtuse angles with a protractor. The sexagesimal system: 1° = 60′, 1′ = 60″, conversions both ways including decimal degrees/minutes. Probability: certain/random/impossible events, sample space, P = favourable/possible, tree diagrams, and two-stage draws with and without replacement (coin pairs 1/4; two hearts with replacement 1/16; red-then-white 1/9 with, 1/6 without replacement — all verified).

### (c) Unit-by-unit teaching record — Section 1 (Sections 2–4 as Grade 8, same pages)

- T1 (pp. 6–7): irrational numbers as reals that can be expressed neither exactly nor periodically; π as circumference/diameter; the golden ratio φ and Euler's constant e named; calculator approximations of roots to two decimals; a rectangle's diagonal (16 m × 9 m) as an irrational length via Pythagoras.
- T2 (pp. 8–9): rational numbers defined as Q = {p/q : p, q ∈ Z, q ≠ 0}; why integers, terminating decimals and fractions are rational, each with a worked justification; problems — study times written as rationals; Andrea's spending 1/4 + 3/8 + 1/8 leaving 1/4 of her savings.
- T3 (pp. 10–11): powers with natural exponents and the sign rules (equal signs positive, different signs negative); the laws of powers in a table — product and quotient of powers, power of a power, power of a product, power of a quotient — two of whose stated forms are wrong (see errors); budget and population word problems (7 buildings × 7 floors × 7 homes × 7 people = 7⁴).
- T4 (pp. 12–13): the real numbers R built from N, Z, Q and the irrationals, shown in a nesting diagram on the number line; classifying given numbers into each set; the order axiom (trichotomy: a > b, a = b or a < b); set-membership and subset statements judged true/false; placing numbers on the line — the list includes √−5, which is not a real number (see errors); comparing quantities stated in mixed units and radicals.
- T5 (pp. 14–15): roots — a student's wrong claim √−25 = −5 is posed and then corrected by the book itself: no real square root of a negative exists; parts of a radical (index, radicand, root); nth-root exercises (cube roots, fifth roots, roots of fractions); area problems built from radical side/diagonal lengths.
- T6 (pp. 16–17): intervals — open, closed, half-open, rays (semirrectas) and the whole line, each with notation, set form and graph; when an interval can be infinite; writing inequalities as intervals and graphing them; a budget problem leading to an inequality.
- T7 (pp. 18–19): functions — what a function is, rectangular coordinates, representations; a temperature–time relation written (with the variables' letters interchanged in the statement) as a table and graph; Cauchy's definition quoted ("one or several values" of y — the historical form); a stacking-weight function y = 3x tabulated and graphed.
- T8 (pp. 20–21): increasing vs decreasing functions defined by comparing f(x₁) and f(x₂) for x₁ < x₂, with diagrams; deciding growth intervals for y = x + 2 and for an inverse-type relation; a motorcycle fuel-consumption context (9 litres per 80 km).
- T9 (pp. 22–23): the linear function y = mx + b / f(x) = mx + b; reading slope m and intercept b from given functions; domain/codomain definition (a first-degree polynomial function on R); building tables and graphs for y = 2x + 2 (one worked cell is internally inconsistent — see errors), y = 3x − 2 and y = −(1/2)x + 2.
- T10 (pp. 24–25): propositions; simple (atomic) vs compound (molecular); connectives and their symbols (implication, negation, conjunction); writing examples of each and classifying given sentences.
- T11 (pp. 26–27): truth tables; tautology defined (true for all assignments); the tables for negation, conjunction, disjunction and the conditional with their verbal rules (conditional false only for true antecedent and false consequent); completing tables for "it is raining / it is cloudy".
- T12 (pp. 28–29): set theory and its operations — union, intersection, difference, complement — shown on Venn diagrams; exercises on A = {1,2,3,4,5,6}, B = {2,4,6,8} and on two shoppers' fruit sets; in both exercise lists item 2 repeats "A U B" instead of asking the intersection (see errors).
- T13 (pp. 30–31): similar triangles (same shape, equal angles, proportional sides); Thales' theorem (a line parallel to one side cuts off a similar triangle) with the proportion written out; finding a building's height from a tree's shadow; a tiling problem with multiple-choice distances.
- T14 (pp. 32–33): the triangle inequality (sum of the two smaller sides must exceed the largest) with a 3-5-10 counter-example; choosing which triples can form a triangle; compass construction steps; construction problems — one of which, sides 5 cm, 6 cm, 11 cm, cannot form a (non-degenerate) triangle since 5 + 6 = 11 (see errors).
- T15 (pp. 34–35): congruent figures (same shape and size); corresponding vertices, sides and angles; the criteria LLL, LAL, ALA to be justified on two pairs of land plots.
- T16 (pp. 36–37): right triangles and their elements (legs, hypotenuse); Pythagoras' formula applied step by step (a ladder against a 7.00 m / 1.80 m wall); distance and ramp-length problems (a 75.9 cm rise with a 303.6 cm run; a 198 m longest side).
- T17 (pp. 38–39): trigonometric ratios for an acute angle — sine, cosine, tangent defined as side ratios on a labelled triangle; solving a sail problem (opposite side 10 m, angle 55.8°: finding hypotenuse and adjacent by sine and tangent — set up correctly); a climber-and-shadow problem with two angles.
- T18 (pp. 40–41): mean, median and mode defined side by side, plus range; the worked example on a runner's laps is internally wrong (see errors); an ages data set for practice; a grouped table of hours worked by 130 employees for mean/median/mode.
- T19 (pp. 42–43): operations on events — union, intersection and difference of events, and symmetric difference — defined through dice events A (even), B (prime), C (greater than 3), with the combined event sets left to complete; sample points for "even or less than 4" and "prime and at most 5" exercises.
- T20 (pp. 44–45): De Morgan's laws for sets stated in words (complement of intersection = union of complements; complement of union = intersection of complements); a verification exercise with U = {1,…,12} whose given values (A, A′, A ∪ B) recompute correctly; two further verification exercises whose universe omits an element used by both sets (see errors).

### (d) Confirmed book errors

1. **Printed p. 10 (Section 1, T3), laws-of-powers table.** The power-of-a-product law is stated in print as "(ab)ᵐ = aᵐ × bⁿ" — the second exponent must be **m**, not n (the table's own example, (ab)⁻² = a⁻² × b⁻², contradicts the stated law). The last row states a "law" (a)ᵐ/aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ — a duplicate of the quotient row above it — while its example is the power-of-a-quotient, (a/b)⁻³ = a⁻³/b⁻³; the law that example illustrates, **(a/b)ᵐ = aᵐ/bᵐ**, is never correctly stated. Taught corrected in both forms.
2. **Printed p. 13 (Section 1, T4).** The exercise "place the following numbers on the number line" includes **√−5**. Taught corrected: √−5 is not a real number and has no place on the real number line — as this same book states two pages later (p. 14, on √−25).
3. **Printed p. 22 (Section 1, T9).** The first worked cell for y = 2x + 2 reads in print "2(−2) + 2 = 0" while the same row gives the point A = (−2; −2). Recomputed: 2(−2) + 2 = **−2**; the point is right and the "= 0" is wrong — the cell contradicts itself.
4. **Printed p. 29 (Section 1, T12).** Both set-operation exercise lists ask "1. A U B = " and then "2. A U B = " again; item 2 should ask the **intersection, A ∩ B** (the operation the tema teaches and the Venn diagram on the page shows, with intersection {2, 4, 6}). As printed, the intersection is never asked for. (The symbol printed is a plain "U" in both items — the same U-for-∩ confusion confirmed in the Grade 9 book, p. 32.)
5. **Printed p. 40 (Section 1, T18).** The worked mean/median/mode example gives the data as "13, 8, 14, 10, 6, 9" but then works throughout with the 8 replaced by a second 9: the mean is computed as (13 + 9 + 14 + 10 + 6 + 9)/6 = 61/6 and the median/mode lists read "6, 9, 9, 10, 13, 14", yielding a mode of 9. Recomputed on the data as stated: sum = 60, **mean = 10**; ordered data 6, 8, 9, 10, 13, 14, median = 9.5 (the book's median arithmetic, (9 + 10)/2, happens to survive); **there is no mode**, since every value occurs once.
6. **Printed p. 45 (Section 1, T20).** Both De Morgan verification exercises give the universe as U = {a, b, c, d, f, g, h, I, j, k} — there is **no e in U** — while both A = {a, d, e, h, j, k} and B = {a, b, d, e, g, I, k} contain e. A set cannot contain an element outside the universe, so the exercises as printed are inconsistent (the letter e was evidently dropped from U, between d and f). Taught corrected with e restored to U.
7. **Printed p. 33 (Section 1, T14), flagged with a caveat.** Students are told to construct a mirror with sides 5 cm, 6 cm and 11 cm; 5 + 6 = 11, so by the triangle inequality the book itself states on p. 32 the construction gives only a degenerate (flat) triangle. If the impossibility was intended as a discovery, the text gives no hint of it; as stated, the task cannot be completed.
8. **Heading/index defects (not mathematical errors):** Tema 6 is headed "Radicación" in the body (p. 16) although its content is intervals; the Section 1 index lists 14 temas against the body's 20, with names that stop matching the body from Tema 6 onward.
9. **Shared-core errors (also in Grades 8 and 9):** light-year "9,4608 × 10²⁷ km" at printed **p. 75** (correct ≈ 9.4608 × 10¹² km); light "1,08 × 10⁵ km en una hora" at **p. 80**, repeated in the evaluation at **p. 83** (correct ≈ 1.08 × 10⁹ km per hour); circumcentre defined as the intersection of the altitudes at **p. 118** (correct: intersection of the perpendicular bisectors; altitudes meet at the orthocentre).

### (e) Honest negatives (Grade 10)

- Despite being the last EGB year, there is no new algebra beyond the shared core: no quadratic functions theory, no exponential or logarithmic functions, no systems beyond 2×2, no inequalities theory beyond intervals and the shared-core exercise sets.
- No analytic/coordinate geometry beyond plotting functions: no distance or midpoint formulas, no equation of a line derived from two points or from slope and a point (slope is only read off given equations), no circle equation.
- No trigonometry beyond the three acute-angle ratios in a right triangle: no unit circle, no angles of elevation/depression theory (one evaluation item uses an elevation angle), no sine/cosine rules, no trigonometric identities.
- No circle geometry, no polygon area formulas in its own Section 1, no surface area (volume appears only in the shared core).
- Statistics in Section 1 stops at ungrouped mean/median/mode and event operations: no dispersion measures, no charts, no quartiles of its own (all shared-core); probability never computes a numerical probability in Section 1 — it only lists event sets.
- No sequences, no proportion/percentage theory, no financial mathematics, no vectors or transformations in its own right.

### (c) Unit-by-unit teaching record (all pages are printed pages)

**Sección 1, Tema 1 — Factorisation and simplification (p. 7).** Opening question on arithmetic vs algebra. Exercises only: factorise given expressions; simplify given polynomials; solve given equations. Concept box defines an algebraic expression (numbers, variables, operations) with an example. No worked factorisation method is shown.

**Sección 1, Tema 2 — Systems of two equations in two unknowns (p. 8).** Concept box defines a 2×2 system with a worked-shape example (2x + 3y = 10, 4x − y = 5) and names substitution, elimination and matrices as solution routes. Exercises: solve given systems by two different methods as a check; solve linear inequalities and express solutions as intervals and on a number line; simplify given expressions.

**Sección 1, Tema 3 — First-degree equations and inequalities (pp. 9–10).** The book's fullest prose explanation in Sección 1: step-by-step prose on solving a first-degree equation ax + b = c (isolate the unknown, apply inverse operations, simplify, verify). Exercises: isolate the indicated unknown in given formulas; solve inequalities and give interval solutions (p. 9); a powers/nested-radicals evaluation problem and a nested-radical simplification under an integer condition; word problems that reduce to 2×2 systems, including finding m so that y is less than x by 7 units (p. 10).

**Sección 1, Tema 4 — Systems of three equations in three unknowns (p. 11).** Concept box explains Cramer's rule (coefficient matrix, determinants formed by replacing each column with the independent terms; applies only when equations = unknowns; zero determinant means no solution or infinitely many). Exercises: solve given 3×3 systems by substitution, and given systems by Gaussian elimination. (Cramer is explained in the box but the set exercises name substitution and Gauss.)

**Sección 1, Tema 5 — Matrices (pp. 12–14; reading pp. 15–16).** Concept box: matrices as rectangular arrays of elements identified by row and column; a 2×3 example; uses across mathematics, computing and physics. Exercises: find x, y, z (and a, b, c) so matrix equalities hold (p. 12); solve exercises by the determinant method; matrix powers and combined matrix operations, including a lower-triangular matrix condition (find m + p + q), N⁵ for a 2×2 matrix N, and a combined (A⁸ + B⁶)(A⁴ + B³)(A¹² + B⁹) computation; word problems solved as systems (bus/Metrovía fares — 20 trips, $7.40; three-person work rates; gift prices with a 10% discount, by Gaussian elimination; stadium members of two teams, 72,000 capacity); simplify fractions; exercises with three given 3D vectors u, v, w; find z in the solution of a given 3×3 system; a café overcharge problem solved as a system; inverse matrices via augmented matrices (pp. 13–14). Closes with a long mathematical poem (Juan Manuel Sánchez Panta) and a note on its author (pp. 15–16).

**Sección 1, Tema 6 — Functions and their characteristics (pp. 17–19).** Concept box: how to identify a function — domain/codomain relation and unique image (the box's second point is wrongly stated; see Errors). Exercises: complete characteristics tables (domain, range/range recorrido, maximum or minimum, monotonicity increasing/decreasing, graph) for a linear function, a rational-plus-linear function, a radical function (pp. 17–18) and an absolute-value function (p. 19); classify given functions as injective, bijective or surjective (p. 19).

**Sección 1, Tema 7 — Operations between functions (p. 20).** The concept box is off-topic for the tema: it teaches four ways to solve quadratic equations (factorisation, the quadratic formula — misprinted, see Errors — completing the square, graphing). ### (d) Confirmed book errors (each recomputed/verified in this read; taught corrected)

1. **Function definition, printed p. 17 (Sección 1, Tema 6, concept box).** The box teaches that uniqueness of image means, in its own words, that there cannot be two different domain elements related to the same codomain element. That is injectivity, not the definition of a function. Correct: every domain element has exactly one image; two different domain elements may share the same image (e.g. f(x) = x² sends both −2 and 2 to 4).
2. **Quadratic formula, printed p. 20 (Sección 1, Tema 7, concept box).** As typeset, the radical's vinculum runs over the whole fraction: the formula reads x = (−b ± √((b² − 4ac) / (2a))) — the division by (2a) sits inside the square root. Correct: x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) / (2a).
3. **Sum of odd squares identity, printed p. 39 (Sección 2, Tema 1, ex. 7a).** The book asks students to prove 1² + 3² + 5² + … + (2n + 1)² = n(2n − 1)(2n + 1)/3. At n = 1 the left side is 1 + 9 = 10 and the right side is 1, so the identity as printed is false. Correct: the printed right side is the sum through (2n − 1)²; the sum through (2n + 1)² is (n + 1)(2n + 1)(2n + 3)/3.
4. **Reciprocal-sum identity, printed p. 39 (Sección 2, Tema 1, ex. 7b).** Under its own printed general term 1/(n(n + 1)(n + 2)), the printed right side n(3n + 5)/(4n(n + 1)(n + 2)) gives 1/3 at n = 1, while the sum's single term is 1/(1·2·3) = 1/6. Correct right side: n(n + 3)/(4(n + 1)(n + 2)).
5. **Ferris-wheel data, printed p. 30 (Sección 1, Tema 9, ex. 33).** The height table gives 0, 6.75, 13.5, 67.5, 0 m at t = 0, 7.5, 15, 22.5, 30 s. The 67.5 m entry exceeds the ride's own maximum in the same table (13.5 m at t = 15) and breaks the symmetry of one revolution (height at 22.5 s must equal height at 7.5 s). Taught corrected: the entry should read 6.75 m, giving the sinusoidal model h(t) = 6.75(1 − cos(πt/15)).
6. **Satellite orbit data, printed p. 50 (Sección 2, Tema 4, ex. 15c).** The problem states the maximum height above Earth's surface as 1,255 km and the minimum height as 1,542 km — the minimum exceeds the maximum as printed. Taught corrected (values swapped): with heights 1,255 km and 1,542 km and Earth's mean radius 6,371 km, the focal distances are 7,626 km and 7,913 km and the eccentricity is (7,913 − 7,626)/(7,913 + 7,626) ≈ 0.0185.
7. **Bayes problem that cannot be answered, printed p. 75 (Evaluación de Sección III, ex. 9).** Only the three machines' defect rates are given (A 5%, B 8%, C 3%); the machines' shares of production are never given, so neither the overall probability that a random part is defective nor the probability that a defective part came from machine A can be computed from the printed information. Both questions are unanswerable as printed; production shares are required.
8. **Bacteria worked solution, printed p. 77 (Desafíos, 2do BGU).** For bacteria that double every hour starting from 10, the printed solution defines f(x) = aˣ with a = "number of bacteria at the start (10)" and concludes f(5) = 10⁵ = 100,000 bacteria. Correct: the doubling model is N(t) = 10·2ᵗ, so after 5 hours there are 10·2⁵ = 320 bacteria. The initial count is a multiplier, not the base.
9. **Carpenter worked solution, printed p. 76 (Desafíos, 1ro BGU).** The question asks how many square metres of wood the carpenter can buy (he has $50; wood costs $10 per m²). The printed resolution computes the wood needed (0.375 m², costing $3.75) and then concludes that he "can buy 0.375 square metres". Correct: 0.375 m² is the amount needed (for which $3.75 of his $50 suffices); with $50 at $10 per m² he can buy 5 m².

10. **Wrong linear-programming "optimum", printed pp. 78–79 (Desafíos, 3ro BGU) — found at citation check.** The two-machine production problem (constraints 40x + 20y ≥ 200, 30x + 20y ≥ 150, minimise f(x, y) = x + y) is "solved" in print as x = 4 hours, y = 3 hours. That point actually produces 220 units of A and 180 of B, not the 200/150 the solution implies, and it is not the optimum: x = 5, y = 0 meets both requirements exactly (200 of A, 150 of B) with a total of 5 hours, beating the printed 7. Taught corrected: the optimum is x = 5, y = 0, f = 5.

Not counted as confirmed errors (noted for the checker): Evaluación de Sección II, ex. 2 (p. 55) repeats the Tema 1 exercise "three consecutive numbers in an arithmetic progression summing to 39" with the sum changed to 49; three consecutive integers in AP sum to three times the middle term, so no integer triple sums to 49, whereas the tema's 39 works (12, 13, 14) — a probable misprint, but real-valued readings keep it from being a provable falsehood. Sección 3, Tema 2, ex. 2b (p. 61) asks for the maximum cost of a school trip where the parallel problems ask for optima in the natural direction; treated here as a wording oddity, not a verified error. The Sección 3, Tema 1 repetition of Sección 2, Tema 5 (p. 59 vs pp. 53–54) is duplication, not error.

### (e) Honest negatives — notable BGU topics this book does NOT teach

- **No worked theory for calculus:** no differentiation rules (power, product, quotient, chain rule), no integration techniques (substitution, parts, partial fractions), no statement of the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, no formal limit definition or limit laws, no continuity — derivatives, integrals, sequence limits and sequence convergence are exercised (largely in figure-only form) but never developed. Rolle's theorem is never mentioned; the Mean Value Theorem is invoked once (p. 30) without statement.
- **No trigonometric identities or equations:** trigonometric functions are only tabulated and graphed; there are no identities, no equations, no inverse trigonometric functions, no sine/cosine rules, no radian-measure development.
- **No complex numbers, no polar form, no De Moivre** (the letter i appears once inside a matrix exercise, unexplained).
- **No analytic geometry of space beyond planes:** no distance formulas in space, no sphere, no line–plane angle work beyond the parallel/perpendicular tests; conic sections appear only as isolated exercises (circle, ellipse, parabola, hyperbola equations and one locus set) with no theory, no eccentricity definition (though one exercise asks for an orbit's eccentricity).
- **No inferential statistics:** despite criterion CE.M.5.11 naming the binomial distribution and inference, there are no confidence intervals, no hypothesis tests, no sampling theory; the Poisson distribution appears only as a simulation instruction with no formula; no Bayes' theorem is ever stated (the one Bayes-style problem lacks the data to solve it).
- **No sequences-and-series theory beyond AP/GP nth terms:** no series sums for GP (only a finite 10-term sum exercise), no convergence criteria, no Taylor/Maclaurin ideas; no mathematical induction is named even though four prove-for-all-n exercises are set.
- **No financial-mathematics formulas stated:** compound interest, continuous compounding and annuities are exercised without any formula being given.
- **No vectors in depth:** no cross product computation (named once in a "Dato curioso" box), no scalar triple product, no vector equations of planes as a topic; no eigenvalues, no matrix rank, no Gauss–Jordan named as such (Gaussian elimination is used), no 3×3 determinant formula stated.
- **No functions theory beyond characteristics tables:** no domain/range theory for composite or inverse functions (one exercise asks for an inverse), no piecewise-function theory (one piecewise graph is set), no exponential/logarithmic equation methods (equations are set without methods).
