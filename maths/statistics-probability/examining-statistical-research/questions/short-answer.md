# Short answer — Examining Statistical Research

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Layer 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — The Audit · (Layer 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Question, Population and Sample · (Layer 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Tracing the Source · (Layer 4) -> Sub lesson 4 — Personal Data and Privacy · (Layer 5) -> Sub lesson 5 — Consent and the Law. Two matching/ordering items span two sub lessons and keep a span marker, e.g. (Sub lessons 1–2).

1. (Sub lesson 2) A survey samples one class, asks that class its favourite lesson, then announces the result as the whole school's favourite lesson. Give the audit's verdict and the reason.
   **Answer: The study fails the audit: its stated population (the whole school), its sample (one class) and its research question (about that class) do not match, so it can speak only about the class it asked.** Turkey, *Matematik 7. Sınıf Ders Kitabı, 1. Kitap* (Grade 7), printed p. 190.

2. (Sub lesson 3) Why does the book accept the ice-sheet figures but interrogate the social-media health claim?
   **Answer: The ice-sheet data can be traced to a named reliable source (the NSIDC), and reliable information is reached from named sources and edu/gov/org domains; the health claim names no source at all, so there is nothing to examine and it cannot be believed as research.** Turkey, Grade 7, printed pp. 191–192.

3. (Sub lesson 4) Name the five kinds of personal-data question the book's warning box lists.
   **Answer: Health, identity, bank, address and income.** Turkey, Grade 7, printed p. 170.

4. (Sub lesson 4) In a survey, many respondents refuse the personal questions. State what the audit records about this, on both sides.
   **Answer: Ethically, the refusal is the respondents' right — the questions ask for personal data. For the results, the study now describes only those who answered, so its conclusions shrink to that group.** Turkey, Grade 7, printed pp. 170, 195–196.

5. (Sub lesson 5) What does the book require of consent, and which law does it name as the frame?
   **Answer: Consent must be informed — boxes ticked without reading ("I accept all permissions") do not pass; the book states Turkey's personal-data law, the KVKK, as the research-ethics frame.** Turkey, Grade 7, printed p. 193.
