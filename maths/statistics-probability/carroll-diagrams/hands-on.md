# Hands-on — Carroll Diagrams

The lesson sorted real things into the four boxes, the way the books teach it.

## Button sort, two ways
Take 12 buttons or shapes of two colours and two kinds (round / not round). First sort them into two overlapping string loops. Then clear the table, rule a big four-box Carroll diagram — red / not red across, round / not round down — and sort the same 12 things again. Count each box. Which drawing makes it easier to find the things that are NOT red and NOT round? Say why.

## Fish cards (North Macedonia's example)
Make picture cards: fish and other animals, some coloured red, some blue or green. Rule the diagram fish / not a fish × red / not red, as in North Macedonia's Grade 1 book (printed pp. 157–158). Place every card, then answer count-and-compare questions on your own diagram: how many fish are not red? Are there more red things or more fish?

## Classroom census
Pick two yes/no properties of your class or family — wears glasses / does not, walked here today / did not — and sort everyone's names into a Carroll diagram on a big sheet. Check the four box counts add up to the whole group: in a Carroll diagram nothing is counted twice and nothing is missed.
