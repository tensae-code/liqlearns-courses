# Lesson plan — Collecting and Showing Data

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 11 — Statistics and Probability · **Lesson:** Lesson 43 of 52
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 11, the second lesson of the unit (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** collecting data and showing it — tables, pictograms, bar charts, pie charts and histograms, and choosing the right display

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the sets lesson — data is counted in groups, and the groups are sets. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Collect data well: know what a population and a sample are, and where data comes from.
2. Build a tally and a frequency table, including grouped frequency tables.
3. Draw and read pictograms, bar charts (single and double), line graphs and pie charts.
4. Draw and read histograms, and say how a histogram differs from a bar chart.
5. Choose the display that fits the data — and say why.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil, a ruler, a protractor for pie charts. Something to count — classmates' answers to one question, or things in your home.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner asks one question of everyone in the room and tallies the answers (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — collecting data; tables; pictograms and bar charts; pie charts; histograms; choosing a display (lesson/lesson.md).
3. **Say it to your companion** — show your companion your chart and let them read three facts from it, then you read three from theirs (lesson/lesson.md).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — run a real survey at home, show it two ways, and say which way is better (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Draws a bar chart for continuous numbers → bars that touch are a histogram; bars with gaps are for separate categories. Ask: are the groups separate things, or slices of one line?
- Forgets the pie must add to 360° → the sectors are shares of one whole: each sector angle is its fraction of the whole times 360°.
- Reads a chart without its key → a pictogram's key says what one picture stands for; without the key the picture counts nothing.
- This lesson's next door: averages and spread (same unit) — the tables you build here are what the averages are taken from.
