# Hands-on — Collecting and Showing Data

## The room survey
Ask everyone in the room one question: favourite fruit, favourite colour, or how they came here today. Tally the answers in fives (four strokes and a cross). Build the frequency table, then the bar chart. Read it back to the room: which answer won, and by how many?

## Pictogram key
Draw a pictogram of the same survey where one picture stands for two people. Write the key big and clear. Swap with a partner and read each other's pictograms — if the key is missing, the picture says nothing.

## Pie by protractor
Take a survey with four answers. Work out each share as a fraction of the whole, times 360°, and draw the pie with a protractor, the way Sierra Leone's book teaches. Check your sectors add to 360°.

## Histogram or bar chart?
Take the heights of five people in your home, in centimetres. Group them into equal intervals (for example 150–159, 160–169) and draw the histogram with touching bars. Then draw the same data as a bar chart of separate people. Put them side by side and say which one shows the shape of the group.
