**Acceptance count: taught in 15 countries (Libya, Yemen, Bangladesh, Kuwait, Laos, Malaysia, Rwanda, Sudan, India, North Macedonia, Afghanistan, Ecuador, Bolivia, Armenia, Sierra Leone).** Libya carries the full binary-relations treatment — Cartesian product, equality of relations, domain and range, inverse relation — as the closing part of its Grade 10 sets chapter (printed pp. 9–43), the ground from which it then defines functions. Yemen carries the topic twice: in Grade 7 as the staircase ordered pair → Cartesian product (with S × T ≠ T × S) → relations (printed pp. 32, 34, 37), and in Grade 9 with transitive relations and equivalence relations as named lessons (printed pp. 17, 25) ahead of its function lesson. Bangladesh, Kuwait and Laos each carry the Cartesian product and relations material as a minor row — Bangladesh in one section (§1.9, printed p. 24), Kuwait inside its Grade 8 sets-and-relations unit (printed pp. 15–67), Laos inside its Grade 10 sets lessons (printed pp. 30–52) — and they are counted here as minor, exactly as filed. North Macedonia enters from its main set — Grade 7 (2025).

Additional Mathematics Form 4 opens with relations met in everyday situations, on the way to functions. (The classification of relations into their four types is Malaysia's Form 2 work and is filed under functions.)

Rwanda teaches relations in Senior 1, closing its sets unit: relations and their properties (reflexive, symmetric, transitive; equivalence relations), ordered pairs and the Cartesian product, mappings with domain, codomain and range and their four types, and graphs of relations on the Cartesian plane.

Sudan teaches relations three times over: Grade 7 gives ordered pairs, the Cartesian product, relations, image and range and the inverse relation; Grade 9 relates sets; and Grade 10 formalises the theory — special relations, reflexive, symmetric and transitive, up to equivalence relations proved in the book's logic language.

India enters relations from Mathematics Class 11–12: cartesian product; special relations.

Afghanistan enters through its Ministry of Education course — the Grade 1 course in two language editions (Pashto and Dari, one work with identical page maps) and Dari student books for Grades 4, 6, 7, 8, 10, 11 and 12, all printing year 1398 HS except the Grade 8 book, which prints 1399 HS on its cover and title page and 1398 HS on its specifications page. From its books: from Grade 8: relation; linear relation and its graph; from Grade 10: relations (as subsets of a Cartesian product); number of relations; domain and range of a relation; inverse of a relation; equivalence relations (reflexive, symmetric, transitive).

Bolivia enters through the Área Matemática sections of its Ministerio de Educación (Estado Plurinacional de Bolivia) Textos de Aprendizaje — the student books for Grades 7, 8, 10 and 12, each the maths section of that year's integrated year book, keeping the whole book's printed foliation, with no imprint or ISBN printed in any of them — and through the Compendio para Maestras y Maestros 2023–2024, the ministry's teacher compilation of the official student texts for all six Secundaria years (Grades 7–12), cited as teacher-compilation evidence only, never as or in place of a pupil text; its Secundaria 3 and Secundaria 5 sections are the only in-hand source for Grades 9 and 11, whose student texts are not in the record. From its books: from Grade 7: Producto cartesiano y pares ordenados; from the Compendium's Secundaria 6 section (Grade 12): Introducción al análisis matemático.

Armenia enters through its Algebra 7; the Algebra and Elements of Mathematical Analysis 12 teacher guide, Semester 1 (teacher evidence; the student book is not held). From its books: the histogram and ordered pairs; cartesian product of two sets and the multiplication rule.
