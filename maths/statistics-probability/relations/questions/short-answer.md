# Relations — short answer

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Ordered Pairs and the Cartesian Product · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Relations, Domain, Range and Inverse · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Transitive and Equivalence Relations.

Answer in a word or a short line.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Write out {1, 2} × {a, b, c}.
**Answer:** {(1, a), (1, b), (1, c), (2, a), (2, b), (2, c)} — 2 × 3 = 6 pairs.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** Is (3, 7) = (7, 3)? Say why in one line.
**Answer:** No — the first places differ (3 ≠ 7), and order inside a pair matters.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** A relation R on {2, 3, 4} to {4, 6, 8, 9} is "is a factor of". Write R.
**Answer:** {(2, 4), (2, 6), (2, 8), (3, 6), (3, 9), (4, 4), (4, 8)} — every pair where the first number divides the second exactly.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** For the relation in question 3, give the range.
**Answer:** {4, 6, 8, 9} — all four members of the second set appear.

**5. (Sub lesson 2)** Write the inverse of {(4, 2), (6, 3)}.
**Answer:** {(2, 4), (3, 6)} — swap the two places of every pair.

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** On {1, 2, 3}, the relation "has the same parity as" — list its pairs.
**Answer:** {(1, 1), (1, 3), (3, 1), (3, 3), (2, 2)} — odd with odd, even with even, each with itself.

**7. (Sub lesson 3)** Give the equivalence classes of the relation in question 6.
**Answer:** {1, 3} and {2} — the odds in one class, the evens in the other.

**8. (Sub lesson 3)** Is "is a brother or sister of" (on a family, counting nobody as their own sibling) an equivalence relation? Which test fails first?
**Answer:** No — it fails reflexivity: nobody is their own brother or sister.
