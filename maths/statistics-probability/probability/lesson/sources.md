**Acceptance count: taught in 16 countries (Egypt, Jordan, Greece, Sierra Leone, Libya, Vietnam, Mauritania, Armenia, Ghana, Laos, Yemen, Indonesia, Nicaragua, Ethiopia, South Sudan, Iraq).** Egypt carries the full first course (sample spaces, theoretical and experimental probability, complements) and the distributions course (conditional probability, random variables, binomial, geometric and normal); Jordan carries counting outcomes, compound events, Venn and geometric probability, permutations, combinations, random variables and distributions; Greece carries probability from Grade 5's dice game through Grade 12's operations with events and conditional probability; Sierra Leone carries the addition and multiplication laws, outcome tables, tree diagrams and conditional probability without replacement; Libya carries probability theory with conditional probability and Bayes-style reasoning and the distributions; Vietnam carries the probability rules, conditional probability and Bayes; Mauritania carries counting and probability through three streamed books; Armenia carries experiment, event and equally likely events; Ghana's evidence is teacher evidence only. Indonesia carries probability from the qualitative probability scale in Grade 6, through theoretical and empirical probability with sampling in Grade 9, to distributions and the addition and multiplication rules in Grade 10, counting rules with Pascal's triangle and compound-event probability in Grade 12, and uniform, binomial and normal distributions in Grade 12 Tingkat Lanjut; Bayes' formula is not a named topic in the held books. Nicaragua carries probability from counting groundwork — arrangements and combinations in Grade 6 — through the full first course in Grade 10 (experiments, sample spaces, events, tree diagrams, counting, classical and frequentist probability) to counting re-taught with circular permutations and the full conditional-probability and independence teaching in Grade 11; its Grade 10 book states no addition rule as a formula and teaches no conditional probability or independence, and its printed p. 21 sample space is corrected to 16 quadruples in this lesson. Iraq carries probability from the certain/possible/impossible vocabulary in Grade 4 — no P(E) as a number at that grade — through experimental outcomes and comparing probabilities in Grade 7, to formal probability P(E) = m/n with complementary events and experimental versus theoretical probability in Grade 8, permutations, combinations and compound events in Grade 9, and the counting, probability-ratio and probability-laws course with the binomial theorem in Grade 11 Scientific. The Literary track never meets probability, and Iraq teaches no probability distributions anywhere — elementary probability only.

**What this lesson does not claim:** no read country teaches continuous distributions as a lesson of its own in the primary/secondary ladder — the normal distribution is taught descriptively and for applications (Egypt Grade 12, Jordan Grade 12, Greece Grade 12, Libya Grade 12), and the curriculum records continuous distributions as a topic with no lesson yet. This lesson names the normal distribution as the books teach it and goes no further. Laos carries probability whole in Grade 11 — random experiments and sample spaces, then random variables and their distributions with a binomial table. Yemen carries probability from the vocabulary — certain, impossible, more likely, less likely — in Grade 2, through a taught probability lesson in Grade 3, to sample spaces in Grade 11 science and the full probability unit in Grade 12 science. Ethiopia carries probability from predicting the outcomes of simple experiments in Grade 5, through the first probability course — sample space, events, equally likely outcomes and P = favourable ÷ total — in Grade 8 and experimental vs theoretical probability with tree diagrams for three coins in Grade 9, to counting, permutations and combinations, the binomial theorem and probability of an event in Grade 11; it teaches no probability distributions anywhere (no normal or binomial distribution as a topic — the binomial theorem is taught, not the binomial distribution), no combined events beyond three coins, and no expected value. South Sudan carries probability from the 0–1 scale with certain and impossible in Primary 7, through chance vocabulary with card and dice examples and probabilities as fractions in Primary 8, to experimental and theoretical probability, sample spaces, complementary events, grids and tree diagrams for two-stage events in Secondary 1 — with four corrections: Primary 7 lists "tomorrow is Friday if today is Monday" as uncertain, but its probability is 0; Primary 7 sets no probability calculations (no P(event) = favourable/total); Primary 8 pp. 89–90 end their examples without stating the probability — not a spade is 39/52 = ¾ and an odd sum of two dice is 18/36 = ½; Secondary 1 p. 120 Exercise 3 Q3(a) asks for the probability of "no first name" when the data accounts for all 32 students, so the answer is 0 and is treated with care. No probability distributions are taught anywhere in South Sudan's set.
