**Acceptance count: taught in 13 countries (Egypt, Jordan, Greece, Sierra Leone, Libya, Vietnam, Mauritania, Armenia, Ghana, Laos, Yemen, Indonesia, Nicaragua).** Egypt carries the full first course (sample spaces, theoretical and experimental probability, complements) and the distributions course (conditional probability, random variables, binomial, geometric and normal); Jordan carries counting outcomes, compound events, Venn and geometric probability, permutations, combinations, random variables and distributions; Greece carries probability from Grade 5's dice game through Grade 12's operations with events and conditional probability; Sierra Leone carries the addition and multiplication laws, outcome tables, tree diagrams and conditional probability without replacement; Libya carries probability theory with conditional probability and Bayes-style reasoning and the distributions; Vietnam carries the probability rules, conditional probability and Bayes; Mauritania carries counting and probability through three streamed books; Armenia carries experiment, event and equally likely events; Ghana's evidence is teacher evidence only. Indonesia carries probability from the qualitative probability scale in Grade 6, through theoretical and empirical probability with sampling in Grade 9, to distributions and the addition and multiplication rules in Grade 10, counting rules with Pascal's triangle and compound-event probability in Grade 12, and uniform, binomial and normal distributions in Grade 12 Tingkat Lanjut; Bayes' formula is not a named topic in the held books. Nicaragua carries probability from counting groundwork — arrangements and combinations in Grade 6 — through the full first course in Grade 10 (experiments, sample spaces, events, tree diagrams, counting, classical and frequentist probability) to counting re-taught with circular permutations and the full conditional-probability and independence teaching in Grade 11; its Grade 10 book states no addition rule as a formula and teaches no conditional probability or independence, and its printed p. 21 sample space is corrected to 16 quadruples in this lesson.

**What this lesson does not claim:** no read country teaches continuous distributions as a lesson of its own in the primary/secondary ladder — the normal distribution is taught descriptively and for applications (Egypt Grade 12, Jordan Grade 12, Greece Grade 12, Libya Grade 12), and the curriculum records continuous distributions as a topic with no lesson yet. This lesson names the normal distribution as the books teach it and goes no further. Laos carries probability whole in Grade 11 — random experiments and sample spaces, then random variables and their distributions with a binomial table. Yemen carries probability from the vocabulary — certain, impossible, more likely, less likely — in Grade 2, through a taught probability lesson in Grade 3, to sample spaces in Grade 11 science and the full probability unit in Grade 12 science.
