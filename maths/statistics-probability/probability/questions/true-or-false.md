# Probability — true or false

Say true or false. If it is false, say why.

**1. (Level 1)** A probability can be bigger than 1.
**False** — a probability is always between 0 and 1.

**2. (Level 1)** The theoretical probability of heads on a fair coin is one half.
**True** — one favourable outcome out of two.

**3. (Level 2)** The addition law adds probabilities of events that cannot happen together.
**True** — mutually exclusive events (Sierra Leone, SSS 3 Term 3, L99–L100).

**4. (Level 2)** Independent events change each other's probabilities.
**False** — independent events do not change each other; that is what independent means.

**5. (Level 3)** Drawing without replacement leaves the probabilities unchanged.
**False** — the bag has changed, so the second probability is conditional (Sierra Leone, SSS 3 Term 3, L106).

**6. (Level 3)** Bayes' formula turns a probability round: it finds P(A given B) from P(B given A).
**True** — Vietnam, Toán 12 Tập Hai, Bài 19.
