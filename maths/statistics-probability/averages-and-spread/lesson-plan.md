# Lesson plan — Averages and Spread

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 11 — Statistics and Probability · **Lesson:** Lesson 44 of 52
**Place in the curriculum:** Stage 11, the third lesson of the stage (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** averages and spread — the mean, median and mode, and how spread out the numbers are

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before

1. **Mean, Median, Mode** — find the mean, median and mode of a list, and say which one fits. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Quartiles and the Box Plot** — find the quartiles and the interquartile range, and read a box-and-whisker plot. (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **Spread: Deviation, Variance, Standard Deviation** — find the range, mean deviation, variance and standard deviation, for grouped and ungrouped data; the closing part — choosing the average — is Sub lesson 1's "say which one fits" judgement, kept whole here at the end and not split. (Was depth Level 3.)

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the collecting-and-showing-data lesson — averages are taken from the tables you build there. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Find the mean of a list, of a frequency table, and (as an estimate) of grouped data.
2. Find the median and the mode, and say which average fits the data.
3. Find the range, the quartiles and the interquartile range, and draw a box-and-whisker plot.
4. Find the mean deviation, the variance and the standard deviation, for grouped and ungrouped data.
5. Say when the mean misleads and the median tells the truth.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A set of numbers to work on — heights, ages, or test scores, real ones from your home.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner takes five real numbers and finds the middle one and the fair-share one (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the mean; the median; the mode; quartiles and the box plot; spread: range, mean deviation, variance and standard deviation; choosing the average (lesson/lesson.md).
3. **Say it to your companion** — give your companion a set of numbers with one wild outlier and ask which average they trust (lesson/lesson.md).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — take a real set of numbers at home and report its mean, median, mode and range (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Adds the numbers but forgets to divide → the mean is a fair share: total first, then share it out by how many there are.
- Picks the median from an unordered list → order the numbers first, smallest to biggest; the middle only exists in order.
- Thinks the mode must exist → some sets have no mode at all, and some have two; both answers are honest.
- This lesson's next door: probability (same stage) — averages describe the data, probability says what happens next.
