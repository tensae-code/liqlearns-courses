# Hands-on — Averages and Spread

## Fair share with counters
Take 30 counters and share them into 5 piles any way you like — 2, 4, 6, 8, 10. Now level the piles out until all five are equal. Each pile is 6. That is the mean, done with your hands — Egypt's balance-point picture, made real.

## Median walk
Line up five people in height order. The person in the middle is the median. Add a sixth person: now the median sits between the two middle people — take the mean of those two. Feel why the median needs order first.

## Outlier test
Take your family's ages and find the mean and the median. Now add one wild number — a 95-year-old grandparent who does not live with you. The mean jumps; the median barely moves. That is why the books ask which average to trust when an outlier is in the room.

## Box plot on the wall
Take a set of ten numbers, order them, and mark the smallest, the first quartile, the median, the third quartile and the largest on a strip of paper. Draw the box and the whiskers. Read it back: the box holds the middle half of the numbers.
