# Lesson plan — Sets

**Course:** Maths · **Stage:** Stage 11 — Statistics and Probability · **Lesson:** Lesson 42 of 52
**Place in the curriculum:** Stage 11, the first lesson of the stage (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** sets — what a set is, and how sets join, meet and complement each other, shown on Venn diagrams

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the numbers lessons (counting, and the number line) — sets are groups of things you can already count. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a set is, name its elements, and write it by listing or by a rule.
2. Tell finite from infinite from empty, and say whether one set sits inside another.
3. Find a union and an intersection, and draw both on a Venn diagram.
4. Find a complement and a difference, and use De Morgan's laws.
5. Count a union with n(A ∪ B) = n(A) + n(B) − n(A ∩ B).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A handful of small different things — buttons, coins, stones — to sort into sets.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner sorts their things into groups on the table, and names each group (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — what a set is; kinds of sets; union and intersection; complement and difference; counting sets; sets of numbers (lesson/lesson.md).
3. **Say it to your companion** — describe your groups in set words, and let your companion draw the Venn diagram (lesson/lesson.md).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — build three sets from your own home and work out their union, intersection and complement (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE at the union/intersection part: a picture of two overlapping circles, one holding the odd numbers 1, 3, 5, 7, 9 and one holding 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, with 1, 3, 5 in the overlap — the student points at the overlap and says what lives there, then points outside both circles and says what lives there.
   - ADD GIF at the complement part: a picture of a big rectangle (the universal set) with a circle inside it, the circle lighting up, then the part outside the circle lighting up — the student says which lighting is the set and which is its complement.

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- IMAGE — a picture of two overlapping circles with the odd numbers and 1–5 → the student reads the overlap and the outside.
- GIF — a picture of a rectangle and a circle lighting up in turn → the student names the set and its complement.
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later) → the student sorts along with it.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes union and intersection → the books' own words: union is EITHER (everything in one or the other), intersection is BOTH (only what sits in the two at once). Say "either" and "both" aloud every time.
- Forgets the empty set → the empty set is a real set with nothing in it, and it sits inside every set; it is not "no set at all".
- Counts the overlap twice in a union → count the overlap once: add both sets, then take the overlap away once.
- This lesson's next door: collecting and showing data (same stage) — sets become the groups the data is counted in.
