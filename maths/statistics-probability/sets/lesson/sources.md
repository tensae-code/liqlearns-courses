**Acceptance count: taught in 11 countries (Egypt, Jordan, Libya, Armenia, Sierra Leone, Greece, Kazakhstan, Laos, Yemen, Indonesia, Nicaragua).** Egypt carries the first full set lesson (listing, set-builder, kinds of sets, union and intersection on Venn diagrams); Jordan carries sets as intervals; Libya carries the laws of set operations, De Morgan and the Cartesian product; Armenia carries sets, intersection and union, and subsets; Sierra Leone carries the worked intersection/union/complement lesson and the union-counting formula; Greece carries sets beside sample spaces and events; Kazakhstan carries sets at their youngest, on pictures. Laos carries sets from listing, Venn diagrams, subsets, Carroll diagrams, intersection, union and power sets in Grade 6 to full set operations with inclusion–exclusion and Cartesian products in Grade 10. Yemen carries sets from sets and relations in Grade 7, through partial sets and the universal set in Grade 8, to defining-property form, difference and complement sets and equivalence relations in Grade 9. Indonesia carries sets whole in Grade 7 K13 — presenting sets, Venn diagrams, subsets, power sets and all four operations with De Morgan — with sets again in the Grade 8 Merdeka guide; the Cartesian product is not a named topic in the held Indonesian sets chapter. Nicaragua carries sets only in part — a brief revision (unit set, empty set, extension vs comprehension, ordered pairs, Cartesian product) inside Grade 10 probability, union and intersection of events in Grade 11, and the number sets ℤ and ℝ named as sets in the Grade 7 and Grade 8 modules; it has no standalone sets chapter, no subsets, no De Morgan and no power set, and Grade 1's zero-as-empty-set is counting, not sets.

**Flagged, not taught:** the Grade 7 K13 book's Sabulan livestock problem (printed pp. 166–169) double-counts the triple region and its data are inconsistent, and its sports survey (p. 170) gives badminton-only as −2 — this lesson teaches neither; correct counting is used throughout.
