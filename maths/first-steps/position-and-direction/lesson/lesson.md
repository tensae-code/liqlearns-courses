# Position and Direction — the lesson

Course: Maths · Unit: Unit 18 — First Steps · Lesson: Position and Direction
Built from: Kuwait, Grade 1 Term 1 and Grade 2 Term 1 — Bangladesh, Class 2 — Laos, Grade 1 — North Macedonia, Grade 1 — Pakistan, Primer and Grade 1 — Yemen, Grade 1 Part 1 — Indonesia, Grade 2 — China, Grade 3 Volume 2 — Ghana, JICA B5 and B6 teacher manuals (teacher evidence)

## Do it first
Hide a toy. Do not point. Only talk: "It is **in** something. It is **under** something in the box's room." Can your companion find it from your words alone? You already use these words every day — this lesson names them all, one pair at a time, so you can say exactly where anything is. The books all start here: Kuwait's Grade 1 book opens its very first unit — numbers to 5 **and position** — with the position words (Unit 1, printed p. 17), before the numbers themselves.

## Sub lesson 1 — Position The Pairs for One Thing
**In and out, inside and outside.** A thing can be **in** a box or **out** of it; **inside** the room or **outside** it. Kuwait's Grade 1 book teaches inside, **between** and outside as its second lesson (lesson 1-2, printed p. 20) — between is the middle word: the ball between the two shoes is inside the pair, with one shoe on each side. Pakistan's Grade 1 book teaches inside/outside in its geometry unit (printed pp. 116–118), and its Primer shows in and out with a dog and its kennel (printed pp. 134–135): the dog in the kennel, the dog out of the kennel.

**Above and below, over and under.** **Above** is higher, **below** is lower — the bird above the tree, the roots below it. **Over** and **under** are their close cousins: the bridge is over the river, the fish swim under it. Kuwait teaches over/under and above/below side by side in its first position lesson (Grade 1, lesson 1-1, printed p. 17); Pakistan's Grade 1 book gives each pair its own pages (above/below, printed pp. 119–120; over/under, printed pp. 121–122); Yemen's Grade 1 book teaches above/below in its relative-positions lesson (lesson 1-4, printed p. 18). North Macedonia's Grade 1 book prints the pairs on one playground picture: under/above, up/down, beside/on/in (printed p. 10).

## Sub lesson 2 — Position Front, Back, Left, Right and Places in a Line
**In front and behind, front and back.** The chair **in front of** you is the one your face looks at; the chair **behind** you is at your back. Pakistan's Primer shows front and back with the same dog and kennel (printed pp. 134–135), and North Macedonia teaches front–back as its own lesson (printed p. 14), then movement forwards and backwards (printed p. 15). Indonesia's Grade 2 book teaches right/left, front/back and above/below together in its position chapter (Chapter 4, printed pp. 101–124).

**Left and right.** Hold up your hands. The hand you write with is the one most people name first — this is my **left** hand, this is my **right** hand. Kuwait puts right/left in its first position lesson (printed p. 17); North Macedonia spends three pages on it: left of–right of (printed p. 16), "what is left and what is right?" (printed p. 17), and moving to the left and to the right (printed p. 18). Watch out: when a friend faces you, their left hand is on your right side. Left and right belong to the person, not to the room.

**Far and near.** A thing close to you is **near**; a thing a long walk away is **far**. Pakistan's Grade 1 book teaches far/near as a pair (printed pp. 123–124), and North Macedonia pairs them on the playground page (printed p. 10) and in their own lesson (printed p. 13).

**Before and after.** In a line, the child **before** you is in front of you; the child **after** you comes behind you. Pakistan's Grade 1 book teaches before/after as the last of its position pairs (printed pp. 125–126). Laos's Grade 1 book makes **order** a whole chapter (Chapter 3, printed p. 22): order is the line idea — who comes first, who comes next, who comes last.

**Places in a line: first, second, third …** When we count places, the counting words change their coats: 1st **first**, 2nd **second**, 3rd **third**, 4th **fourth**, 5th **fifth**, 6th **sixth**, 7th **seventh**, 8th **eighth**, 9th **ninth**, 10th **tenth**. Bangladesh's Class 2 book teaches them in two steps — first to fifth, then first to tenth (printed pp. 34–39) — with lines of children, and a hard question the book loves: a child can be "third from the front and fifth from the back" in the same line. The place depends on the end you count from — always name the end.

## Sub lesson 3 — Position The Compass Points and Routes
**The eight compass points.** Outside, directions have names that never move. **North** is the point the compass needle finds. On maps, north is put at the top — China's Grade 3 book teaches the eight directions this way, on north-up maps (Unit 1, printed p. 2): north, south, east, west, and the four points halfway between them — **north-east, south-east, south-west, north-west**. The same book follows routes on a zoo map and tells the history of the compass in a reading box. Ghana's teacher manuals walk the same ground in steps, as teacher evidence: from home, the school is 3 steps east, the post office 2 steps south (JICA B5, Lesson 103), and Ghana's B6 manual gives position and direction from home to the factory and the market in steps north and east (JICA B6, Lesson 93).

> **Tip — from us, not from the books:** learn every word with its twin. If you can say "above", always say "and below is the twin" in the same breath. A pair learned together is never half forgotten.

## Say it to your companion
Take turns. One of you puts a toy somewhere and says it whole: "The toy is **under** the book, **between** the cup and the box." The other must repeat the sentence, then find the toy and check: was the sentence true? Five places each. Then the line game: stand in a line of friends or chairs and answer whole: "I am **third from the front**." Last, the route game: "From the door, go **2 steps north, then 3 steps east**" — your companion walks it. Did they land where you meant?

## Do It Yourself
1. Put a toy in five places, one after the other: in, out, over, under, between. Say each place in a whole sentence and write the five sentences.
2. Line up ten books. Write who is first, third and tenth from the left; then turn around and write who is first, third and tenth from the right.
3. Draw a little map of your room with north at the top. Write a route of three steps with compass words from your bed to the door.
4. Hide something for someone at home using only position words — no pointing allowed.
