# Hands-on — Position and Direction

The lesson played with real things in real places, the way every book starts it.

## Hide the toy
One player hides a small toy in the room. They may only help with position words: "It is under something." "It is in something." "It is behind you — near the door." The seeker follows the words only. Three hides each, then swap. This is Pakistan's kennel game grown up: the words must do all the work (Primer, printed pp. 134–135).

## The pairs walk
A caller calls a pair word and the learners move: "Hands above your heads!" "Hands below your knees!" "Stand behind your chair!" "Stand in front of your chair!" "One step forwards!" "One step backwards!" — North Macedonia's movement lessons turned into a game (Grade 1, printed pp. 15, 18). Wrong movers sit out one round; last mover standing wins.

## Race line
Line up ten children, chairs or soft toys. Give each a place card: 1st, 2nd, 3rd … 10th. A caller asks: "Who is third from the front?" "Who is fifth from the back?" "Who stands between the second and the fourth?" — the line work of Bangladesh's Class 2 book (printed pp. 34–39). Then shuffle the line and play again.

## Steps map
Draw a little map of the room or the yard on squared paper, north at the top. Mark home. A partner reads a route in Ghana's steps style — "from home: 3 steps east, 2 steps south" (teacher evidence: JICA B5, Lesson 103) — and the walker walks it on the real floor or traces it on the map. Swap: the walker now writes the route they just walked, in direction words.
