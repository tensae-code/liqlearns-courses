# Lesson plan — Repeating Patterns

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 18 — First Steps · **Lesson:** Repeating Patterns
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 136 of 136, the last lesson of Unit 18 — First Steps (maths/CURRICULUM.md). Unit 18 stands last by numbering, but its lessons are for the youngest learners: this one teaches the first pattern work in the course.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** repeating patterns — of shapes, colours, sounds and movements — how to find the part that repeats (the pattern rule), continue a pattern, fix a broken one, and meet growing and shrinking patterns.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on sorting (Level 135, same unit) — a pattern is a sorted row with a rule. It needs no counting beyond small numbers the learner can say. Pencil and Lines (Level 1, the shared course) is its companion for drawing pattern rows.

1. **Patterns Continuing a Repeating Pattern** — continue a repeating pattern of shapes or colours and say what comes next, for repeats of two and of three. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Patterns The Rule, Sounds and Movements, and Fixing Errors** — name the smallest part that repeats, continue a sound or movement pattern, and find and fix the wrong piece in a broken pattern. (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **Patterns Growing and Shrinking** — continue a growing or shrinking pattern, say how much it changes by each step, and follow a halving chain. (Was depth Level 3.)

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Continue a repeating pattern of shapes and colours and say what comes next, the way Kuwait's Grade 1 book teaches repeating patterns in its first unit (lesson 1-4, printed p. 24), and again at Grade 2 (lesson 1-2, printed p. 20).
2. Discover patterns — spot them, continue them — the way Yemen's Grade 1 book teaches discovering patterns (lesson 1-5, printed p. 22) and returns to it inside later units (lessons 3-8, printed p. 84; 4-7, printed p. 110; 5-3, printed p. 126).
3. Make and continue patterns of shapes, colours, movements and sounds, the way Indonesia's Grade 2 book teaches them in its position-and-patterns chapter (printed pp. 101–124).
4. Say the pattern rule — the small part that repeats — and fix a pattern with a wrong piece in it, the way Ghana's teacher manuals teach extending patterns, finding rules, correcting pattern errors and creating a pattern for a given rule (teacher evidence: JICA B3, Lessons 77–79).
5. Continue a growing pattern and a shrinking pattern and say how it grows or shrinks, the way Indonesia's Grade 4 book teaches growing/shrinking picture and number patterns (Bab 3, printed pp. 85–102).
6. Follow a shrinking chain by always halving — 560, 280, 140, 70, 35 — the divide-by-2 pattern of Ghana's B5 manual (teacher evidence: JICA B5, Lesson 78).
7. Recognise the older children's pattern world waiting ahead: tile patterns, lamp cycles and unit-digit patterns, the way Indonesia's Grade 8 book opens its number-patterns chapter (K13 Bab 1, printed pp. 1–40).

## What you need
Things in two or three kinds: red and blue bottle tops, big and small buttons, sticks and stones. Your body for movement patterns (clap, stomp, jump). Paper and a pencil or crayons in two colours.

1. **Do it first** — the learner hears a clapped pattern — clap, clap, stomp — and must clap what comes next, before a single word of teaching (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — what repeats: the repeating part; patterns of shapes and colours; patterns of sounds and movements; fixing a broken pattern; growing and shrinking patterns (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the repeating-part part: a picture of a row of tops being laid — red, blue, red, blue — with the repeating part lighting up as a bracket each time it completes → the student says the next colour before each new top lands.
   - ADD IMAGE under the sounds-and-movements part: a picture of a child doing clap, clap, stomp in three panels, with the three panels grouped in a ring marked "this part repeats" → the student does the movement pattern along with the panels, twice round.

## If the learner is stuck
- Continues the row but cannot say why → Kuwait's start: read the row aloud in the things' names — "red, blue, red, blue" — until the voice itself hears the repeat (Grade 1, printed p. 24).
- Loses the place in a long pattern → Ghana's rule-finding way (teacher evidence): bracket the smallest part that repeats, say it as one word — "red-blue is the rule" — and rebuild the row from copies of that part (JICA B3, Lessons 77–79).
- Cannot spot the wrong piece → walk the row in repeats, one bracket at a time; the bracket that does not match the rule is where the error hides — Ghana's manuals practise exactly this as correcting pattern errors (teacher evidence: JICA B3, Lessons 77–79).
- Growing patterns jump too fast → build only the next step, with real things, and count the change out loud (+2 each time) before predicting further (Indonesia, Grade 4, printed pp. 85–102).
- This lesson is the last of Unit 18: its next door is the whole course — counting (Unit 1) counts the repeats, and sequences (Unit 10) is where growing patterns grow up.
