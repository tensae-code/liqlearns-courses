# Hands-on — Repeating Patterns

The lesson built patterns with real things, sounds and bodies, the way the books teach it.

## Top rows
Lay a row of bottle tops: red, blue, red, blue. Your partner closes their eyes; lay three more and ask "what did I lay?" Then harder rows: red, red, blue — or big, small, small. Swap roles. Kuwait's pattern lesson done on the table (Grade 1, printed p. 24).

## Body patterns
Invent a movement pattern of three moves — clap, clap, stomp; jump, turn, clap — and perform it three times round without stopping. Your partner must join in on the second round: joining in proves they found the repeating part. Indonesia's Grade 2 book teaches movement and sound patterns beside shape and colour ones (printed pp. 101–124) — this is that lesson with no paper at all.

## Break it!
Build a correct pattern row of at least nine pieces. While your partner looks away, change ONE piece. They walk the row in repeats and must find the wrong piece and say the rule it breaks — Ghana's correcting-pattern-errors practice (teacher evidence: JICA B3, Lessons 77–79). Three breaks each; a found error earns the next break.

## Growing stairs
Build stairs of sticks or squares: a step of 1, then 3, then 5. Count each step aloud. Build the next step before you say its number, then check by counting: did it grow by 2 again? Now a shrinking stair: start with a row of 12 tops, take away 3, then 3 again — build each row and say the chain: 12, 9, 6, 3. Indonesia's Grade 4 growing/shrinking work, built by hand (Bab 3, printed pp. 85–102).
