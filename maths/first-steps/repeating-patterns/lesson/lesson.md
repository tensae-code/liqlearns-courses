# Repeating Patterns — the lesson

Course: Maths · Unit: Unit 18 — First Steps · Lesson: Repeating Patterns
Built from: Kuwait, Grade 1 Term 1 and Grade 2 Term 1 — Yemen, Grade 1 Part 1 — Indonesia, Grade 2, Grade 4 and Grade 8 — Ghana, JICA B3 and B5 teacher manuals (teacher evidence)

## Do it first
Listen: clap, clap, STOMP. Clap, clap, STOMP. What comes next? You knew — clap. You heard a **pattern**: a small part that repeats, again and again, so your ear knows the future. Patterns are not only heard. They are seen in rows of shapes and colours, and felt in dances. Kuwait's Grade 1 book teaches **repeating patterns** in its very first unit (lesson 1-4, printed p. 24) — right after sorting, because a pattern is a sort laid out in a row with a rule.

## Sub lesson 1 — Patterns Continuing a Repeating Pattern
**The repeating part.** Every repeating pattern has a small part that comes back, again and again: red, blue — red, blue — red, blue. The small part here is "red, blue", and the whole row is that part, copied. To continue any pattern, first find its repeating part. Ghana's teacher manuals teach this as **finding the pattern rule** (teacher evidence: JICA B3, Lessons 77–79 — extending patterns and finding rules): name the repeating part, and the rest of the row is just copies of it.

**Patterns of shapes and colours.** A row can repeat shapes — circle, square, circle, square — or colours, or both at once: red circle, blue square, red circle, blue square. The repeating part there is two pieces long: "red circle, blue square". Kuwait returns to patterns in Grade 2 as the second lesson of its first unit (lesson 1-2, printed p. 20), and Yemen's Grade 1 book names its lesson **Discovering patterns** (lesson 1-5, printed p. 22) — and then keeps discovering them, inside its addition unit (lesson 3-8, printed p. 84), its subtraction unit (lesson 4-7, printed p. 110) and its shapes unit (lesson 5-3, printed p. 126): patterns hide inside every topic.

## Sub lesson 2 — Patterns The Rule, Sounds and Movements, and Fixing Errors
**Patterns of sounds and movements.** A pattern does not need things at all. Indonesia's Grade 2 book teaches patterns in four kinds in one chapter (printed pp. 101–124): **shape patterns, colour patterns, movement patterns and sound patterns**. Clap, clap, stomp is a sound-and-movement pattern whose repeating part is three beats long. A dance step, a drum rhythm, a song's chorus — all patterns your body can continue before your mouth can explain them.

**Fixing a broken pattern.** Sometimes a row has a wrong piece: red, blue, red, blue, BLUE, blue. Walk the row in repeats — red-blue, red-blue — and the bracket that breaks the rule shows itself: the fifth piece should be red. Ghana's manuals practise exactly this as **correcting pattern errors**, and the step after: **creating a pattern for a given rule** (teacher evidence: JICA B3, Lessons 77–79). If you can fix a pattern and invent one from a rule, the rule is yours.

## Sub lesson 3 — Patterns Growing and Shrinking
**Growing and shrinking patterns.** Not every pattern repeats the same part. Some **grow**: 1 square, 3 squares, 5 squares — each step adds 2. Some **shrink**: 12, 9, 6, 3 — each step takes away 3. Indonesia's Grade 4 book teaches growing and shrinking picture and number patterns as their own chapter (Bab 3, printed pp. 85–102), with adding and subtracting up to 100. Ghana's B5 manual walks a famous shrinking chain (teacher evidence: JICA B5, Lesson 78): **560, 280, 140, 70, 35** — each number is half the one before, a divide-by-2 pattern. And the road keeps going: Indonesia's Grade 8 book opens with a whole chapter of number patterns (K13 Bab 1, printed pp. 1–40) — tile patterns that grow by a rule, lamp cycles that repeat in time, and the patterns hiding in unit digits. Today's red-blue row is the seed of all of it.

> **Tip — from us, not from the books:** say the row out loud in names, never in silence. "Big, small, small — big, small, small." The ear catches the repeating part a full copy before the eye does.

## Say it to your companion
Build a pattern where your companion can see it. They must say the repeating part whole — "the rule is **red, red, blue**" — then continue the row for two more copies while saying each piece aloud. Then the sound round: clap a pattern; your companion repeats it and adds one more copy. Last, the trap: take turns planting ONE wrong piece in each other's rows. The finder must say both the rule and the fix: "The rule is clap-clap-stomp; this stomp should be a clap."

## Do It Yourself
1. Build a pattern row of at least nine pieces with things at home. Write its rule: "the repeating part is ___."
2. Make a sound pattern and a movement pattern. Teach both to someone; they must perform two full copies without your help.
3. Copy this row and fix its wrong piece: square, circle, square, circle, circle, circle. Write the rule and the fix.
4. Build a growing pattern of things — start with 2, grow by 3 each step, four steps. Write the four counts. Then a shrinking one: start at 20, shrink by 5, four steps.
