**Acceptance count: taught in 5 countries (Kuwait, Yemen, Indonesia, Ghana, Syria).** Kuwait teaches repeating patterns in its first Grade 1 unit (lesson 1-4, printed p. 24) and returns to them at Grade 2 (lesson 1-2, printed p. 20). Yemen teaches discovering patterns as a named Grade 1 lesson (printed p. 22) and repeats the lesson inside its addition, subtraction and shapes units (printed pp. 84, 110, 126). Indonesia carries the widest run: patterns of shapes, colours, movements and sounds at Grade 2 (printed pp. 101–124), growing and shrinking patterns at Grade 4 (Bab 3, printed pp. 85–102), and a full number-patterns chapter at Grade 8 — tile patterns, lamp cycles, unit digits (K13 Bab 1, printed pp. 1–40). Ghana carries patterns as teacher evidence only — extending patterns, finding rules, correcting errors and creating patterns for a rule in the JICA B3 manual (Lessons 77–79), and the multiply/divide patterns with the divide-by-2 chain 560, 280, 140, 70, 35 in the JICA B5 manual (Lessons 73–78); Ghana's set holds no pupil textbook, so those rows are worded as teacher evidence wherever they are used.

Syria's repeating patterns open the Grade 1 Semester 1 book: AB and ABC patterns in colours, shapes and movements, completed, repaired and built by the pupil.
