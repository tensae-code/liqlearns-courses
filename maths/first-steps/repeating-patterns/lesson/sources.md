**Acceptance count: taught in 12 countries (Kuwait, Yemen, Indonesia, Ghana, Syria, Malaysia, India, Mongolia, Venezuela, Ecuador, Palestine, Turkey).** Kuwait teaches repeating patterns in its first Grade 1 unit (lesson 1-4, printed p. 24) and returns to them at Grade 2 (lesson 1-2, printed p. 20). Yemen teaches discovering patterns as a named Grade 1 lesson (printed p. 22) and repeats the lesson inside its addition, subtraction and shapes units (printed pp. 84, 110, 126). Indonesia carries the widest run: patterns of shapes, colours, movements and sounds at Grade 2 (printed pp. 101–124), growing and shrinking patterns at Grade 4 (Bab 3, printed pp. 85–102), and a full number-patterns chapter at Grade 8 — tile patterns, lamp cycles, unit digits (K13 Bab 1, printed pp. 1–40). Ghana carries patterns as teacher evidence only — extending patterns, finding rules, correcting errors and creating patterns for a rule in the JICA B3 manual (Lessons 77–79), and the multiply/divide patterns with the divide-by-2 chain 560, 280, 140, 70, 35 in the JICA B5 manual (Lessons 73–78); Ghana's set holds no pupil textbook, so those rows are worded as teacher evidence wherever they are used.

Syria's repeating patterns open the Grade 1 Semester 1 book: AB and ABC patterns in colours, shapes and movements, completed, repaired and built by the pupil.

Malaysia's first pattern work sits at the start of Form 2 — patterns spotted in number sets and in everyday objects, with Pascal's triangle built row by row. It is Malaysia's earliest pattern teaching (its set holds no primary books); the first-steps climb itself stands on the primary books of the other countries.

India carries repeating patterns through its NCERT ladder — Math-Magic Class 1–5 and Mathematics Class 6 — including extending a sequence; patterns around us; rangoli dot patterns; brick floor patterns.


Mongolia enters through its ministry textbooks Математик I, II (Grades 1, 2): sequences and neighbours; patterns and result changes.

Venezuela enters through the Colección Bicentenario, the MPPE's one-book-per-grade course: *La Patria Buena* (Grade 5). From its books: from Grade 5: patterns — Two repeating bead patterns (1 peonía, 2 pepas…; blue–brown–green…) — finding a term (zamuro seeds for 22 peonías; colour of the 20th stone).

Palestine enters through the pupil's books of its Ministry of Education (Curriculum Centre, Ramallah), all in Arabic — Mathematics Grade 1 in two parts (both fourth edition, 2020), Grade 10 in two parts (Part One third edition, 2020; Part Two second edition, 2019 — the held parts are different editions and are cited as such), and the Grade 12 Technological-stream book (التكنولوجي, first edition, 2019 — the book's own name for its stream, never "Information Technology"). No ISBN is printed in any of the five books. From its books: from Grade 1 Part Two: continue a colour pattern: colour the remaining beads of a necklace in the same pattern; unit review: continue a number pattern (balls in cups — the fourth cup) and a shape pattern.

Turkey enters through the pupil's textbooks of its Millî Eğitim Bakanlığı (MEB — Ministry of National Education), all in Turkish, first volumes (1. Kitap) only — Grade 3 (ISBN 978-975-11-9897-6, TTKB approval 13/07/2026), Grade 6 (ISBN 978-975-11-8773-4, TTKB approval 30.06.2025) and Grade 7 (ISBN 978-975-11-9899-0, TTKB approval 1.07.2026); no edition, printing or year line is printed in any of the three books, and the grades' second volumes (2. Kitap) are not in hand and are cited nowhere. From its books: from Grade 3: shape patterns: draw the missing steps of pictured patterns and write how many shapes each step has (recall); create your own shape pattern by colouring, record its counts, state its rule; express a shape pattern in numbers and compare patterns respectfully with classmates.
