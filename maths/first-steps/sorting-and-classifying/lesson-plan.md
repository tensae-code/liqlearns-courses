# Lesson plan — Sorting and Classifying

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 18 — First Steps · **Lesson:** Sorting and Classifying
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 135 of 136, inside Unit 18 — First Steps (maths/CURRICULUM.md). Unit 18 stands last by numbering, but its lessons are for the youngest learners: this one teaches the first grouping in all of mathematics.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** sorting real things into groups — first by one likeness (all the red ones together), then by two likenesses at once — and saying the rule of each group out loud.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on looking and touching only. It needs no numbers and no reading. Pencil and Lines (Level 1, the shared course) is its companion for drawing the groups and their circles.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an

shown changing.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Sorting By One Attribute** — sort a mixed pile by one named attribute (size, shape, colour or use) and say the rule of each group. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Sorting Re-Sorting and the Not Group** — sort the same set a second way, explain why the groups change, and use a "not" group so nothing is left homeless. (In the lesson text the "not" group passage is placed with this sub lesson, ahead of the two-attributes passage it was written after.) (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **Sorting By Two Attributes and Sorting for Data** — sort by two attributes at once in two passes, name each group's two-part rule, and say why sorting opens data work. (Was depth Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson (no hardness ranking is written in this lesson's question files).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Pencil and Lines (Level 1, the shared course) — sorting is drawn as circles and groups on paper. Recommended, never forced.
- Recommended first: Position and Direction (Level 134, same stage) — a sorted group is first gathered into one place. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Sort a mixed pile by one attribute — small things to the small bag, big things to the big bag — the way Yemen's Grade 1 book opens its whole course (lesson 1-1, printed p. 8).
2. Sort the same kind of pile by two attributes at once, the way Yemen's Grade 1 book teaches next (lesson 1-2, printed p. 12).
3. Group pictured shapes by shape, by colour and by size, the way Indonesia's Grade 1 book teaches grouping inside its shapes chapter (printed pp. 115–144).
4. Sort a set by one rule, record the sort, then re-sort the same set by a different rule and see the groups change, the way China's Grade 1 book teaches classification and organising (Unit 3, printed p. 27).
5. Put things into sets by a shared feature and say the feature, the way Kazakhstan's Grade 2 book teaches composing and classifying sets (Ospanov Part 1, Lessons 56–57).
6. Answer a teacher's "why can we group these?" with the likeness — by their shape, by their use, by their colours — the grouping talk of Nicaragua's Grade 1 teacher guide (teacher evidence: Unit 1, printed GM pp. 2–51).
7. Say why sorting comes first in data work: Yemen's Grade 2 statistics unit opens with sorting, before any picture or table is drawn (Part 2, Unit 11, lesson 1st — Sorting, printed p. 140).

## What you need
A mixed pile of real things: buttons, bottle tops, stones, leaves, spoons, socks. Two bags or boxes. String or chalk for group circles. Paper and a pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner tips out a mixed pile and puts it "in order" any way they like, then must say what rule they used; the companion tries to guess the rule before being told (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — sorting by one attribute; saying the rule; sorting the same set again by a new rule; sorting by two attributes; sorting as the door into data work (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the one-attribute part: a picture of a mixed pile of buttons sliding into two bags, big ones to the big bag, small ones to the small bag → the student calls out which bag each button must go to before it lands.
   - ADD IMAGE under the re-sort part: a picture of the same twelve shapes sorted twice — once circled by colour, once circled by shape, side by side → the student finds one shape and names its group in each picture, and sees the groups are different.
   - ADD IMAGE in the two-attribute part: a picture of four group circles holding big red, big blue, small red and small blue buttons → the student places one new button and says both parts of its rule.
3. **Say it to your companion** — sort in secret, and your companion must guess the rule from the groups; then defend a hard placement in a whole sentence (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the sorting game in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — sort one real drawer or shelf three ways and record the rules (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Groups by "the ones I like" → Yemen's first-lesson fix: the rule must be a likeness anyone can see — size, shape, colour — not a feeling; ask "could your friend sort it the same way without asking you?" (Grade 1 Part 1, printed p. 8).
- One thing fits no group → every sort needs a home for leftovers: make the "not" group (not red, not round) — a group is still a group when its rule is a *not*.
- Two-attribute sorting tangles → do it in two passes, China's way with one rule at a time: first split by the first attribute, then split each pile by the second (China, Grade 1, Unit 3, printed p. 27; Yemen, Grade 1 Part 1, printed p. 12).
- Counts a thing twice → Kazakhstan's set rule: a thing belongs to its set once; when it moves to a new sort, it leaves the old group (Grade 2 Part 1, Lessons 56–57).
- This lesson's next door: Repeating Patterns (Level 136, same stage) — a sorted row with a rule becomes a pattern.
