# Hands-on — Sorting and Classifying

The lesson sorted real piles, the way the books teach it.

## Big bag, small bag
Tip out a mixed pile of stones, buttons or bottle tops. Two bags: sort by size only — small things to the small bag, big things to the big bag — Yemen's first lesson of Grade 1 (Part 1, printed p. 8). Empty both bags back into one pile. Now sort by colour. Same pile, new groups: say both rules out loud so the change is heard, not just seen.

## Guess my rule
One player sorts a pile in secret — by shape, by colour, by size, by use — and shows only the finished groups. The other player studies the groups and must guess the rule, the way Nicaragua's teacher guide asks it: "Why can we group these?" — "By their shape, by their use, by their colours" (teacher evidence: Grade 1 guide, Unit 1). Three secret sorts each.

## Two likenesses
Take buttons of two colours and two sizes. Chalk four circles. First pass: split the whole pile by colour. Second pass: split each colour pile by size, moving each half into its own circle. Every button now has a two-part rule — big and red, small and blue. This is Yemen's two-attribute lesson done with your hands (Grade 1 Part 1, printed p. 12).

## Sort, then show
Sort the class's or family's shoes, books or pencils one way. Count each group. Draw the groups as a picture table — one row per group, one picture per thing — and feel how the sort became data: Yemen's Grade 2 statistics unit opens exactly here, with sorting, before pictorial representation (Part 2, Unit 11, printed pp. 140, 142).
