# Lesson plan — Business Mathematics

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 15 — Money, Time and Everyday Mathematics · **Lesson:** Business Mathematics
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 114 of 136, Unit 15 — Money, Time and Everyday Mathematics (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the mathematics of buying, selling, borrowing, lending and sharing money — profit and loss, discount, simple and compound interest, tax and VAT, commission, hire purchase, depreciation, partnership, insurance, wages, annuities, present value, amortisation, bills of exchange, currency conversion, zakat, ushr and inheritance shares, and reading bills and budgets.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on percentages, ratio and proportion — the learner can already find a percentage of an amount and share an amount in a ratio. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown changing.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Business Mathematics — the Shop** — profit, loss and their percentages on the cost price; take a discount off a marked price; find a commission; total a bill correctly, checking each line. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Business Mathematics — Borrowing and Lending** — use I = PRT/100 in all four directions; grow compound interest year by year and by formula; add VAT; price a hire purchase against its cash price; convert currency at a given rate; depreciate an amount. (Was depth Level 2.)
   In the lesson text, the commission part (old Level 1 depth, the shop) sits inside Sub lesson 2's text run, between tax/VAT and hire purchase; its question items sit in Sub lesson 1.
3. **Business Mathematics — Long Money** — find an annuity's present and final values and a discounted present value; read an amortisation; share partnership profit by capital × time; work an insurance premium; find zakat and ushr; share an inheritance in the books' shares; find a bill of exchange's discounted value and mean maturity. (Was depth Level 3.)
   In the lesson text, the wages-and-household-money and bills-and-budgets parts (old Level 1 depth) and the currency-conversion part (old Level 2 depth) sit inside this sub lesson's text run; their question items sit in Sub lessons 1 and 2. The question markers follow the old levels, item by item.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson (no hardness ranking is written in this lesson's question files).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Find profit, loss and their percentages from cost price and selling price, the way South Sudan's Primary 4 book starts (Pupil's Book 4, §2.4, pp.51–56) and Sierra Leone's JSS 2 handbook teaches percentage profit and loss (JSS 2 Term 1, L36–L38, pp.93–99).
2. Work a discount off a marked price, and a percent change, the way Cambodia's Grade 8 book orders them (L3, discount pp.33–34, percent change pp.34–35).
3. Find simple interest with I = PRT/100 and rearrange it for P, R or T, as Laos's Grade 9 book states it (Ch.1, L7, p.32) and Bangladesh's Class 8 book drills it (Exp.4, pp.71–92 — with the English p.76 principal taught corrected as 15,000 tk).
4. Grow an amount by compound interest year by year and by A = P(1+r)ⁿ, as Cambodia's Grade 8 book works it (L3, pp.37–38: 8,000,000 riel at 15% for 2 years grows by 2,580,000 riel).
5. Add VAT and work a tax chain, the way Nepal's Grade 9 book does (Unit 2, pp.25–49: a refrigerator at Rs 27,876.10 plus 13% VAT of Rs 3,623.90 makes Rs 31,500).
6. Find a commission as a percentage of a sale, as South Sudan's Primary 8 book does (§6.1, pp.93–94: 15% of SSP 25,000 is SSP 3,750).
7. Price a hire purchase — deposit plus instalments against the cash price — as South Sudan's Primary 7 book teaches it (Unit 6, pp.88–90, with its interest slip taught corrected as SSP 2,000).
8. Depreciate an amount by compound depreciation, as Nepal's Grade 10 book teaches it beside population growth (Unit 4, §4.2, Ex.4.2 p.42).
9. Share profit in a partnership by capital, and by capital × time for a compound partnership, as Pakistan's Grade 8 book teaches it (pp.74–76).
10. Price insurance in the books' own simple form — premiums building a fund, and a premium with a policy fee — as Pakistan's Grade 8 book teaches it (pp.71–73) and Iraq's Grade 11 Literary book works it (pp.62–63, log value taught corrected as 0.0253).
11. Find wages and rates of pay, and split pay into income and expenditure, as Sierra Leone's SSS 3 block opens (L59, p.33) and its JSS 2 handbook teaches personal expenditure (JSS 2 Term 2, L56, p.2).
12. Find the present value and the final value of an annuity, and read an amortisation of a loan, as Greece's EPAL Financial Mathematics book teaches them (Ch.8, pp.171–195; Ch.9, pp.197–218) and Indonesia's Grade 12 Revisi book teaches annuities for investment and loans (Bab 2, pp.35–80).
13. Discount a bill of exchange and find a replacement bill's common or mean maturity, as Greece's EPAL book teaches them (Ch.5, pp.111–136; Ch.6, pp.137–149).
14. Convert currencies at buying and selling rates, as Pakistan's Grade 8 book teaches it (pp.62–63) and Nepal's Grade 10 book teaches money exchange (Unit 2, §2.2, pp.11–22).
15. Find zakat at 2.5% above the nisab and ushr at 10% or 5%, as Pakistan's Grade 7 book teaches them (pp.62–63), and share an inheritance with the widow's 1/8 and sons : daughters = 2 : 1, as Pakistan's Grade 8 book teaches it (pp.76–78).
16. Read and total a bill, and keep a budget, the way Nepal's Grade 3 book teaches "Bill and Budget" (L28, pp.112–114 — its fruit-shop bill taught corrected at Rs 90) and South Sudan's Primary 7 book makes out bills (Unit 6, pp.90–92 — Example 7 taught corrected at SSP 1,750).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A calculator for the compound-interest, annuity and amortisation work. Real or play bills, price tags and receipts for the hands-on work.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner prices a small trade of their own: buy at one price, sell at another, and say profit or loss before any formula is given (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — profit and loss; discount and percent change; simple interest; compound interest; tax and VAT; commission; hire purchase; depreciation; partnership; insurance; wages; annuities and present value; amortisation; bills of exchange; currency conversion; zakat, ushr and inheritance; bills and budgets (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the compound-interest part: a picture of a money pile growing year by year, each year's growth bigger than the last because interest lands on interest — the student says why year 2's interest is larger than year 1's.
   - ADD IMAGE at the hire-purchase part: a picture of two price tags for the same bicycle, one the cash price and one the hire-purchase total split into deposit plus monthly payments → the student finds how much extra the hire purchase costs.
   - ADD IMAGE at the VAT-chain part: a picture of Nepal's four-step chain — producer, dealer, wholesaler, retailer — with the VAT added at each step → the student adds the four VAT amounts to Rs 2,340.
   - ADD GIF at the amortisation part: a picture of a loan balance stepping down payment by payment, the interest slice shrinking and the capital slice growing — the student points to the payment where the balance halves.
3. **Say it to your companion** — explain in your own words why compound interest beats simple interest over many years, and why a hire purchase costs more than the cash price (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — business mathematics in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — run one full money day of your own: a trade, a discount, a loan, a bill and a budget (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes up cost price and selling price → South Sudan's Primary 4 rule in words first: profit = selling price − buying price; loss = buying price − selling price (Pupil's Book 4, §2.4, pp.51–56). Name both prices before any subtraction.
- Finds a percentage of the wrong amount → profit and loss percentages are always on the COST price, as Sierra Leone's JSS 2 handbook states them (L37, p.96); VAT is on the price before VAT, as Nepal's Grade 8 book works it (Unit 16, pp.129–135).
- Grows compound interest like simple interest → work Cambodia's Grade 8 year-by-year table first (pp.37–38): each new year starts from the grown amount, never from the first principal alone.
- Cannot rearrange I = PRT/100 → Laos's Grade 9 book and Bangladesh's Class 8 book both drill one unknown at a time; cover the unknown with a finger and read the triangle off.
- This lesson's next door: Time and Calendars (Level 115, same stage) — the other half of everyday mathematics.
