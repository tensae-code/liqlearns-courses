# Hands-on — Business Mathematics

The lesson worked money the way the books work it: on real trades, bills and loans.

## Run a stall for an hour
Price five things you own. Write a cost price and a selling price for each, find each profit or loss and its percentage on the cost price — the South Sudan Primary 4 way (Pupil's Book 4, §2.4, pp.51–56) and the Sierra Leone JSS 2 way (L36–L38, pp.93–99). Then give one item a 10% discount, Nepal's Grade 8 way (Unit 16, pp.129–135), and reprice it.

## Grow the same loan two ways
Take 10,000 of any currency at 10% for 3 years. Grow it by simple interest, then by compound interest year by year in a table, Cambodia's Grade 8 way (L3, pp.37–38). Write the gap between the two totals in a full sentence.

## Total a real bill — then check it like a checker
Take a real shop bill or write one in Nepal's Grade 3 "Bill and Budget" form (L28, pp.112–114): rate × quantity on every line, then the total. Swap bills with a partner and check each other's totals line by line. Remember the lesson of the books themselves: Nepal's fruit bill prints 70 where the true total is Rs 90, and South Sudan's Primary 7 bill prints SSP 10,500 where the true total is SSP 1,750 — a printed total is a claim, not a fact, until you have added it yourself.

## Hire purchase versus cash
Find one real thing sold both ways (or invent the two tags, South Africa Grade 8 Workbook 1, WS9 way). Write the deposit, the monthly payment and the number of months; total the hire purchase; subtract the cash price. That difference is the price of paying slowly.

## Share an inheritance on paper
Share 96,000 in the shares Pakistan's Grade 8 book teaches (pp.76–78): the widow takes 1/8 first; the rest goes to sons and daughters with a son taking twice a daughter's share. Try it with 2 sons and 1 daughter, then with 1 son and 3 daughters. Check that your shares add back to 96,000.
