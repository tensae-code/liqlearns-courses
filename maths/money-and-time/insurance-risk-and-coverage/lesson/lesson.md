# Insurance: Risk and Coverage

## You do it first

Your neighbour's house burns down. The rebuild costs RM180,000 — money almost nobody keeps in a drawer. Yet houses that burn DO get rebuilt, paid for by companies that never met the family. Where does the money come from? Hold your answer; by the end of this lesson you will be able to give it in one sentence. (Malaysia, Mathematics Form 5, Ch. 3 §3.1, printed pp. 74–83.)

## Risk and the pool

**Insurance is protection against risk, bought by sharing it.** A loss like a fire is unlikely for any one household this year — but across a million households it is a certainty, and roughly how many fires there will be is predictable. So each household pays a small, regular **premium** into one pool, and the pool pays the **sum insured** to the few who suffer the loss. Insurance is not an investment and not a gamble: you are buying the promise that a disaster will not ruin you. (Malaysia, Mathematics Form 5, Ch. 3 §3.1, printed pp. 74–76.)

## Life insurance

- **Whole life insurance** covers you for your whole life and builds a savings value inside the policy — higher premiums.
- **Term life insurance** covers you for a fixed period only. If the period ends and you are alive, nothing is paid and nothing is returned — but the premiums are far cheaper for the same sum insured.

Same risk, two shapes: cover with savings, or pure cover for a term. (Malaysia, Mathematics Form 5, Ch. 3 §3.1, printed pp. 76–78.)

## Health insurance

- A **medical card** policy pays hospitalisation and surgery bills when you are treated — the card is presented at the hospital and the insurer settles the covered bills.
- **Critical-illness cover** pays a lump sum when a listed serious illness is diagnosed, to spend as the family needs.

One pays the bills; the other pays cash on diagnosis. (Malaysia, Mathematics Form 5, Ch. 3 §3.1, printed pp. 78–79.)

## Motor insurance — three sizes

Malaysia's book sets out the three motor policies, smallest to largest:

1. **The Act policy** — the legal minimum. It covers injury to third parties (other people) only. It pays nothing for vehicles or property.
2. **Third party, fire and theft** — adds damage to other people's property, and your own vehicle if it is burnt or stolen.
3. **Comprehensive** — adds damage to your own vehicle in an accident.

Each step up covers more, and costs more. (Malaysia, Mathematics Form 5, Ch. 3 §3.1, printed pp. 79–80.)

## Fire and house insurance

- **Houseowner insurance** covers the building itself — the structure against fire and similar disasters.
- **Householder insurance** covers the contents — the things inside the building.

A renter needs the second; an owner usually needs both. (Malaysia, Mathematics Form 5, Ch. 3 §3.1, printed pp. 80–81.)

## The premium — the price of the risk

The premium is set by how risky you are to insure and how much cover you buy. Malaysia's chapter reads premiums from **rate tables** (so much per RM1,000 of sum insured) and names the factors that move them: **age, health and smoking status, the length of a trip for travel cover, and the sum insured itself**. Older, sicker, smoking, longer, larger — the premium rises, because the expected claim rises. (Malaysia, Mathematics Form 5, Ch. 3 §3.1, printed pp. 80–83.)

What happens at claim time — the part you pay yourself (the deductible) and the part you share (co-insurance) — is the next lesson: insurance-deductibles-and-co-insurance.

> **Tip — from us, not from the books:** compare any two policies by asking the same three questions: What exactly does it pay for? What does it refuse? And what is the biggest sum it will pay? A cheap premium with small answers to the first and third questions is not cheap insurance.

## Say it to your companion

Explain to your companion where the money comes from when an insured house burns — the one-sentence answer this lesson promised. Then have them explain the difference between houseowner and householder cover back to you.

## Do It Yourself

1. A term-life premium table charges RM1.80 per RM1,000 per year for a non-smoker aged 30 and RM3.40 for a smoker aged 30. Price RM150,000 of cover for each, and state the two factors at work.
2. Sort these under the smallest motor policy that covers them: (a) you injure a pedestrian; (b) your car is stolen; (c) you dent your own car; (d) you break a shop window with your car.
3. A family owns their house and everything in it. Which two fire policies do they need, and what does each one cover?
