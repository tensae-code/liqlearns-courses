# Taxation

## You do it first

A worker earns RM48,000 in a year and hears: "your tax rate is 8%, so your tax is RM3,840." Her actual bill is far smaller. By the end of this lesson you will be able to compute her real bill — and to say exactly where the RM3,840 went wrong, twice over. (Malaysia, Mathematics Form 5, Ch. 4 §4.1, printed pp. 96–117.)

## Sub lesson 1 — Taxation — What Is Taxed: Reliefs and Chargeable Income

## What is being taxed

**Income tax** is the government's share of a person's yearly income, computed once a year on the year just earned. Malaysia's Form 5 book works the whole computation; this lesson follows its road: **reliefs first, then bands, then rebate, then balance.** (Governments set the relief amounts, the bands and the rates; they change from year to year and country to country. The road does not change.)

## Step 1 — Chargeable income: reliefs shrink the income

Not all of your income is taxed. **Tax reliefs** — amounts the law lets you set aside for the costs of living — are subtracted from your total income first:

    chargeable income = total income − total reliefs

Malaysia's relief list includes: personal relief RM9,000; spouse relief RM4,000; RM2,000 for each child; EPF contributions and life-insurance premiums together up to RM7,000; a lifestyle relief (books, devices, internet) up to RM2,500; and more for medical costs, education and savings schemes. Hold the key distinction: **a relief reduces the income that is taxed — it is not subtracted from the tax itself.** (Malaysia, Mathematics Form 5, Ch. 4 §4.1, printed pp. 96–103.)

## Sub lesson 2 — Taxation — Band by Band

## Step 2 — The bands: a staircase, not a wall

The rate table is **progressive**: the income is sliced into bands, and each slice is taxed at its own band's rate. The first slice is taxed at the lowest rate (in Malaysia's table, the first RM5,000 at 0%); the next slice a little higher; and only the slice that reaches the top band pays the top rate.

So the worker from the top of the lesson: chargeable income RM32,000 (after reliefs) is taxed — using the book's band structure — as 0% on the first 5,000, 1% on the next 15,000 (= RM150), and 3% on the remaining 12,000 (= RM360): **tax = RM510**. The "8% of everything" claim failed twice: it ignored the reliefs, and it charged the top rate on slices that never reached it. A pay rise can push a *slice* into a higher band; it can never tax the slices below any harder — that is why a rise always leaves you better off. (Malaysia, Mathematics Form 5, Ch. 4 §4.1, printed pp. 104–107. The worked figures here follow the book's band method; the book's printed rate table is the authority for its year's exact bands and rates.)

## Sub lesson 3 — Taxation — the Full Computation: Rebate and PCB

## Step 3 — Rebate, then the balance with PCB

A **rebate** comes straight off the computed tax: Malaysia gives a rebate of RM400 where the chargeable income does not exceed RM35,000 (with a further RM400 for a spouse in the matching case). Our worker: RM510 − RM400 = **RM110** of tax finally due.

Most workers never pay that bill in one lump: the employer has already deducted **PCB (Monthly Tax Deduction)** from each month's pay and sent it to the tax office. The year's end is a balancing:

- PCB paid **more** than the final tax → the difference is **refunded**.
- PCB paid **less** → the worker pays the **balance**.

Our worker paid RM40 × 12 = RM480 of PCB against RM110 due: she is refunded RM370. A refund is not a bonus — it is her own overpaid tax coming home. (Malaysia, Mathematics Form 5, Ch. 4 §4.1, printed pp. 107–111.)

> **Tip — from us, not from the books:** when someone says "I don't want a raise, it will push me into a higher tax bracket," do the staircase sum with them. The higher rate only ever touches the new slice — the slices below are taxed exactly as before.

## Say it to your companion

Explain to your companion the difference between a relief and a rebate — one shrinks the income before the bands, the other shrinks the tax after them. Then have them explain what PCB is and why a refund happens.

## Do It Yourself

1. A worker's total income is RM72,000. Reliefs: self RM9,000, spouse RM4,000, three children, EPF/life RM7,000. Find the chargeable income.
2. Using bands of 0% (first 5,000), 1% (next 15,000), 3% (next 15,000), 8% (next 15,000), compute the tax on a chargeable income of RM47,000 — band by band, showing each slice.
3. The worker in Question 2 qualifies for the RM400 rebate and paid RM95 a month in PCB. Find the final position: refund or balance, and how much.
