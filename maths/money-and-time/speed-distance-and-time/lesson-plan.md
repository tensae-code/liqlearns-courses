# Lesson plan — Speed, Distance and Time

**Course:** Maths · **Stage:** Stage 15 — Money, Time and Everyday Mathematics · **Lesson:** Speed, Distance and Time
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 116 of 136, Stage 15 — Money, Time and Everyday Mathematics (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** speed as distance ÷ time and its two rearrangements, average speed, distance–time and travel graphs, and meeting problems — with density and other compound units, and work rates, as named parts, because the books teach all three as the same kind of rate.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on division, ratio and proportion, and reading a graph — the learner can already divide, share in a ratio and read values off axes. It needs nothing else.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the ratio-and-proportion lesson and the graphs lesson (a speed is a ratio, and a travel graph is read off axes). Recommended, never forced.
- Recommended first: Time and Calendars (Level 115, same stage), for hours, minutes and seconds and changing between them. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Find speed from v = d ÷ t, the way Cambodia's Grade 6 book opens (L14 Speed, pp.95–98: a ship's 120 km in 3 h is 40 km/h).
2. Rearrange the triangle to find distance (d = v × t) and time (t = d ÷ v), as South Sudan's Primary 8 student book states the full triangle (§2.6, pp.44–46 — student book only; its guide key is wrong in 6 of 10 and is never used).
3. Change between km/h and m/s, as South Sudan's Primary 7 book teaches (×5/18 and ×18/5; 54 km/h = 15 m/s, §2.4, pp.20–23) and Cambodia's Grade 7 book checks (18 km/h = 5 m/s, L11, p.110).
4. Find average speed as total distance ÷ total time — never the average of the speeds — as Kazakhstan's Grade 6 books insist (Aldamuratova §8.1, p.127; Abylkasymova §47, p.118).
5. Read a distance–time graph: slope is speed, a flat part is a stop, and a returning slope is the way back, as Laos's Grade 5 book teaches drawing and reading them (L41–L42, pp.100–111) and Egypt's Grade 8 book reads a return speed off one (Unit 3, 3-7, pp.85–90).
6. Solve a meeting problem by adding the two speeds, the way Kazakhstan's Grade 4 books teach meeting and opposite-direction motion as their own lessons (Part 2, pp.110–128) and Laos's Grade 9 book sets meeting problems (Ch.1, L8, p.39).
7. Use density and other compound units the same triangle way — density = mass ÷ volume, population density, mass per unit area — as Cambodia's Grade 8 book teaches them (L4, pp.43–50, its d² line taught corrected) and Nicaragua's Grade 7 module teaches density inside SI units.
8. Use work rates the same way again — work done per hour, two workers' rates added for joint work — as Kazakhstan's Grade 4–5 books teach productivity and joint work (Grade 4 Pt2, pp.77–88; Grade 5 §3.15, p.10).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A ruler and squared paper for travel graphs. A watch or phone timer and a measured walk for the hands-on work.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner times a real walk over a known distance and guesses their speed before any formula is given (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the speed triangle; changing units; average speed; distance–time and travel graphs; meeting, opposite and catch-up problems; density and other compound units (named part); work rates (named part) (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the triangle part: a picture of the d–v–t triangle with each corner covered in turn, the uncovered formula appearing — the student says each of the three formulas before it appears.
   - ADD IMAGE at the graph part: a picture of a distance–time graph with an up slope, a flat top and a down slope → the student tells the journey's story in three sentences and reads the fastest speed off the steepest slope.
   - ADD GIF at the meeting part: a picture of two walkers starting from opposite ends of a road and walking towards each other, the gap shrinking — the student says why the gap closes at the SUM of the two speeds.
   - ADD IMAGE at the density part: a picture of three same-sized cubes — wood, iron, gold — on a balance one at a time → the student orders the three densities and says what stays the same (the volume).
3. **Say it to your companion** — explain in your own words why average speed is not the average of two speeds, and why two workers' rates add (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — speed, distance and time in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — time and graph one real journey of your own, then one density and one work-rate problem (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes up the three formulas → cover one corner of the triangle, South Sudan Primary 8's way (§2.6, pp.44–46): what you cover is what you divide or multiply for. Student book only — never practise from that book's guide key, which is wrong in 6 of its 10 answers.
- Averages two speeds → Kazakhstan's rule: add the distances, add the times, then divide once (Grade 6, §47, p.118). The average of the speeds is right only when the two times are equal.
- Loses the units → write the unit into every line: a speed is never just "40", it is 40 km/h — Cambodia's Grade 8 book treats the compound unit itself as the teaching point (L4, pp.48–49).
- Reads a flat graph part as "going slowly" → flat means the distance is not changing at all: the traveller is stopped. Laos's Grade 5 graphs (L41–L42) drill exactly this reading.
- This lesson's next door: Further Statistics (Stage 16) — rates return there as rates of change in data.
