**Acceptance count: taught in 19 countries (Laos, Kazakhstan, Vietnam, Libya, Yemen, Cambodia, South Sudan, Sierra Leone, Egypt, Mauritania, China, South Africa, Iran, Nicaragua, Syria, Malaysia, Rwanda, Sudan, India).** Laos carries distance–time graphs and mixed-unit speed work in Grade 5 and v = S/t with meeting problems in Grade 9. Kazakhstan carries the fullest motion course — the triangle, meeting, opposite, catch-up and lagging motion and motion graphs in Grade 4, average speed as total distance ÷ total time in both its Grade 6 books, and work rates from Grade 4 productivity through Grade 5 joint work to an Algebra 9 joint-work problem. Vietnam teaches uniform motion in words in Grade 5. Libya teaches speed and the Tripoli–Ghadames average-speed trip in Grade 6 and restates average speed in Grade 7. Yemen works one speed formula across a cyclist, a car and a plane in Grade 6. Cambodia opens speed in Grade 6, converts its units in Grade 7, and teaches average speed, real-world speeds and density in Grade 8 — its density d² line taught corrected, and its population-density row teacher evidence from the guide only. South Sudan teaches unit conversion in Primary 7 and the full triangle in Primary 8 — student books only, its P8 guide key being wrong in 6 of 10 and cited nowhere. Sierra Leone teaches rate, speed, travel graphs and density in SSS 3. Egypt reads speed off a distance–time slope, including a speed-limit judgement, in Grade 8. Mauritania introduces uniform motion in Grade 5 and meets it again in proportionality in Grade 6. China builds distance-per-pedal-turn from a gear ratio in its Grade 6 bicycle project. South Africa teaches speed as a rate inside Grade 9 ratio and proportion. Iran compares speeds inside Grade 5 proportion. Nicaragua teaches density inside SI units in its Grade 7 module.

Syria defines average speed as the rate distance ÷ time in Grade 7's uniform motion, and works v = d/t in all three directions — with unit conversions and distance–time graphs — in Grade 8 Algebra.

Malaysia teaches speed and acceleration in Form 2 with unit conversions worked both ways, then motion as graphs in Form 4 — gradient is speed on one graph and acceleration on the other, and the area under a speed–time graph is the distance.

Rwanda teaches speed, distance and time in Primary 6 — speed as distance ÷ time with unit conversions both ways, meeting and catching-up problems, and average speed over whole and return journeys — with interval-and-distance work in Primary 5 as groundwork.

Sudan teaches speed as a rate in Grade 8, with distance–time tables and a full relative-speed course: approach, pursuit and separation speeds.

India enters speed, distance and time from Math-Magic Class 5: speed.
