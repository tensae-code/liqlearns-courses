# Hands-on — Money

The lesson used real or play money, the way every book starts it.

## The value line
Lay out every coin and note you have (or play pieces marked with values) in one line, smallest value to biggest. Read the line aloud. Then a partner points at any piece: you say its value and name one thing in the room that costs about that much. Shuffle and do it again until the line can be rebuilt from memory.

## One amount, many ways
Pick an amount — 100 in your own money. Make it with pieces. Now make exactly the same amount a second way, and a third way. Each time, total the pieces aloud, piece by piece, the way Cambodia's books total note groups (3 × 1000 + 4 × 500 = 5000 riel) and Ghana's teacher-evidence workbook totals a set of cedi notes to GH¢465. If two ways give different totals, one way is wrong — find it.

## The two-person shop
One person is the shopkeeper: price five real things and write the prices. The other is the buyer: choose two things, say the total, and pay with a piece bigger than the total. The shopkeeper counts out the change aloud, counting up from the price to the payment. The buyer checks: price + change = payment. Swap roles and play again, once with exact money (no change) and once where the buyer does not have enough — the buyer must say so before paying.

## The saving jar
Pick a priced thing you want. Draw a jar and mark the price at the top. Each day for a week, "save" a fixed small amount into the jar on paper, the way Egypt's Grade 2 money chapter saves over days and weeks towards a target. After each day, write two numbers: saved so far, and still missing. At the end of the week, say whether the target is reached and what one more week would give.
