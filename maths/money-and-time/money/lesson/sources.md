Nicaragua teaches córdoba banknotes, equivalence and córdoba–centavo conversion in Grade 2 and the national currency, with 1 córdoba = 100 centavos and decimal amounts, in Grade 3. Jordan teaches its coins and equal-value coins in Grade 1 and the dinar and paper money in Grade 2. Ethiopia counts santim coins in Grade 1, adds 100 santim = 1 birr and birr notes in Grade 2, and lists, compares, adds and subtracts money in Grade 3. Nepal teaches coins and notes from Re 1 to Rs 1000, the paisa join, Mohar and Suka, and rupees-and-paisa arithmetic with change from Rs 100. South Sudan shows SSP notes 1 to 100 and a shop price list in Primary 1, gives change in Primary 2, and makes change from larger amounts in Primary 4. Mauritania runs coins to notes to small purchases across Grades 1–2 and buying and selling in Grade 4. Greece teaches euro coins in a classroom supermarket in Grade 1, orders the euro banknotes in Grade 2, and returns to banknotes, coins and decimals as money in Grade 4. Iraq is not counted: its filing states money is never a topic there — dinars appear only inside word problems — so Iraq is an explicit negative, not a source.

Syria teaches money in Syrian lira from Grade 1 Semester 2 — coins, then notes, then banknotes to 1,000 — always by components (what makes what), totalling mixed groups, and picture-data problems about enough money, change and saving towards a price.

Saudi Arabia teaches money in riyals from Grade 1 Part Two — recognising the 1, 2, 5 and 10, counting a set from the biggest denomination, paying the same amount in different ways and judging enough-or-not-enough — and extends it to the 50 and the 100-riyal note in Grade 2, with the older 1445 Term-3 volumes witnessing and adding test review items.

Malaysia's money chapter is about managing money rather than counting it: a five-step financial management process built on SMART goals. (Its worked family financial plans are filed under business-mathematics, and its insurance and taxation chapters are lessons of their own.)

Rwanda teaches money from Rwandan franc coins and notes in Year 2, through currency, saving, budgeting and four-operation word problems to 5 000 Frw in Year 3, profit and loss in Primary 4, money transfer, borrowing and foreign-exchange conversion in Primary 5, bank saving and investment in Primary 6, and discount, commission, profit and loss and dividends in Senior 1 — with the Year 3 teacher's guide adding cashless payment (cards and mobile money) as a discussion point.

Sudan meets money inside Grade 1's addition work, adding toy prices in Sudanese pounds.

India carries money through its NCERT ladder — Math-Magic Class 1–5 — including coins of India; paying with tens and ones; paying with notes and coins; price per litre from a pump display.


Mongolia enters through its ministry textbooks Математик I, II, III, IV, IX (Grades 1, 2, 3, 4, 9): money to 10; money chains / totals; money word problems to 100.

Venezuela enters through the Colección Bicentenario, the MPPE's one-book-per-grade course: *Contemos... 1, 2, 3 y 4* (Grade 1), *Triángulos, rectángulos y algo más...* (Grade 2), *Contando con los recursos* (Grade 4), *La Patria Buena* (Grade 5) and *Hecho en Venezuela* (Grade 6). From its books: from Grade 1: coins — counting coins of each denomination in a purse (1, 5, 10, 12½, 25, 50 céntimos) in a table; Karibay's given: 4 coins of 1 céntimo; from Grade 4: banknote problems — greatest number of Bs. 100 notes inside Bs. 3.245.786; Bs. 10 notes plus 12 Bs. 1 coins totalling Bs. 398.052 → 39.804 notes; 370×100 + 59×10 + 1 = 37.591 (all posed); from Grade 6: ledger — Community currency accounting: the guaicaipuro exchange ledger (see negative-numbers).

Afghanistan enters through its Ministry of Education course — the Grade 1 course in two language editions (Pashto and Dari, one work with identical page maps) and Dari student books for Grades 4, 6, 7, 8, 10, 11 and 12, all printing year 1398 HS except the Grade 8 book, which prints 1399 HS on its cover and title page and 1398 HS on its specifications page. From its books: from Grade 1: coins and banknotes: 1, 2, 5 afghani coins; 1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50 afghani notes (Lesson 35); afghani amounts as tens and ones: making amounts and comparing amounts.
