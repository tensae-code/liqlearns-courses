# Lesson plan — Money

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 15 — Money, Time and Everyday Mathematics · **Lesson:** Money
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 113 of 136, the first lesson of Unit 15 — Money, Time and Everyday Mathematics (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** money — knowing the notes and coins of a country, making an amount in more than one way, paying for a thing, and giving and checking change.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on counting and on adding and subtracting — the learner can already count things, add two amounts and take one amount away from another. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown changing.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Money — Knowing the Pieces** — name the coins and notes of your own country, order them by value, and say how the small unit makes the big unit (for example 100 paisa = 1 taka, 100 santim = 1 birr). (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Money — Making and Exchanging Amounts** — make one amount in at least two different ways, exchange a big piece for smaller pieces of exactly the same value, total a set of notes piece by piece, and judge whether money in hand is enough for a price. (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **Money — Paying, Change and Money Arithmetic** — pay for one or two items, work out the change and check it (price + change = payment), and add and subtract money amounts including small-unit amounts written as decimals. (Was depth Level 3.)
   The lesson text's closing part, saving towards a price, sits in this sub lesson.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson (no hardness ranking is written in this lesson's question files).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Counting (Unit 1) — money is counting with values on the pieces. Recommended, never forced.
- Recommended first: Adding and subtracting (Unit 2) — change is a taking-away, and a total is an adding. Recommended, never forced.
- This lesson does not do business mathematics — interest, profit as a system, tax and partnership are the next lesson, Business mathematics (Level 114). A single buying-and-selling price appears here only as paying and change.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the coins and notes of their own country and order them by value, the way Bangladesh's Class 1 book starts with coins of 1, 2 and 5 taka and notes to 100 taka (Class 1, Ch. 17, pp.105–108) and North Macedonia's Grade 1 book starts with denar coins 1, 2, 5 and 10 and the 10-denar note (Grade 1, p.91).
2. Say how the small unit makes the big unit in their own money — 100 paisa = 1 taka (Bangladesh, Class 3, Ch. 10, pp.99–109); 100 santim = 1 birr (Ethiopia, Grade 2, pp.157–166); 1 córdoba = 100 centavos (Nicaragua, Grade 3, pp.155–168) — and in a money built on tens, 1 yuan = 10 jiao and 1 jiao = 10 fen (China, Grade 1, U5, pp.52–60).
3. Make one amount in more than one way and exchange big pieces for small ones of the same value — two 500-riel notes = one 1000-riel note (Cambodia, Grade 2, Ch. 13, pp.85–88); making Rs 50 and Rs 100 from smaller pieces (Pakistan, Grade 1, Unit 4, pp.84–87).
4. Judge whether the money in hand is enough for a price (Pakistan, Grade 1, Unit 4, p.88; Egypt, Grade 2 new series, Ch. 6 word problems — 120 is more than 50 + 60, so Sara can buy).
5. Pay for things and work out the change, and check change given back — paying 5000 riel for things costing 2000 and 1500 riel leaves 1500 riel change (Cambodia, Grade 4, L7, pp.45–50); bread at R8 paid with R10 leaves R2 (South Africa, Grade 1 Workbook 2, units 75–77).
6. Add and subtract money amounts, rupees and paisa together — Rs 52.25 + Rs 50.50 = Rs 102.75 (Nepal, Grade 3, Lesson 16, pp.70–72).
7. Total a set of notes by valuing each piece first, the way Ghana's teacher-evidence workbook works a total of GH¢465 from 1 × GH¢5, 3 × GH¢20, 2 × GH¢50, 1 × GH¢100 and 1 × GH¢200 (Ghana, DL Workbook Level 1, Module 3 Lesson 1 — teacher evidence).

## What you need
Real or play coins and notes of your own country — or bottle tops and paper pieces marked with values.
Paper and a pencil.
A price list from a real shop, market stall or school bazaar.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner lays out the money they have, says what each piece is worth, and tries to pay an exact price with it (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — know your money; the small unit makes the big unit; making an amount and exchanging; is the money enough?; paying and giving change; adding and subtracting money; saving towards a price (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the denominations part: a picture of coins and notes of one country landing in a row from smallest value to biggest — the student names each piece and its value as it lands.
   - ADD IMAGE in the exchanging part: a picture of one big note beside the smaller notes of exactly the same value — the student says the two totals and checks they match.
   - ADD GIF in the change part: a picture of a hand paying a big note for a priced item, then smaller pieces coming back as change, counted one by one — the student counts the change aloud and checks it against price and payment.
   - ADD IMAGE in the saving part: a picture of a jar filling coin by coin towards a priced toy — the student says how much is still missing after each coin.
3. **Say it to your companion** — run a two-person shop: one prices, one pays, one gives change, then swap (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — paying and change in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — price five real things, pay for two of them on paper, and work out the change from the biggest note you have (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes up two pieces that look alike → lay all the pieces in a value line, smallest to biggest, and read the line before every task; the books all start this way (Bangladesh, Class 1, pp.105–108).
- Makes an amount one way only → exchange one piece: break a big note into smaller pieces of the same value, as Cambodia's Grade 2 book breaks 1000 riel into two 500s (pp.85–88), then make the amount again.
- Change comes out wrong → count up from the price to the payment, piece by piece, instead of subtracting at once; then check: price + change must equal what was paid.
- Adds rupees and paisa in separate piles and loses them → write the amount the Nepal way, Rs 52.25, and add the decimal form (Nepal, Grade 3, pp.70–72).
- This lesson's next door: Business mathematics (Level 114, same unit) — the paying and change learned here grow into interest, profit, tax and bills.

## Teacher note
The full text is lesson/lesson.md; every part traces to a book and page in lesson/sources.md. Two flags a teacher must keep: Ghana's money rows are all teacher evidence (the JICA teacher manuals and the DL workbook — there is no Ghana pupil textbook in the set), and Sierra Leone's rows are teacher evidence from lesson plans written before the Leone was redenominated, so the lesson re-bases its Sierra Leone-style examples to small present-day amounts and words them as teacher evidence as filed. One book error is taught corrected and never as a valid question: Kuwait's Grade 3 Term 2 item on printed p.147 offers coin/note groups totalling 8, 11 and 11 dinars for a 10-dinar note — no group matches, so the lesson teaches the totalling skill and never sets that item. Iraq is an explicit negative: money is never a topic in Iraq's books, so Iraq is not a source for this lesson. A teacher sending this lesson sends the lesson and its question list as they are — choosing, not remaking.
