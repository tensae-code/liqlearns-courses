China teaches month lengths, the leap-year rule and the 24-hour clock in its Grade 3 year-month-day unit. Vietnam tells the time and the days of the week in Grade 1 and reaches adding, subtracting, multiplying and dividing time measures in Grade 5. North Macedonia teaches the months, the days of the week, the parts of the day and full-hour clocks in Grade 1. Greece orders daily events, reads clocks and runs the days of the week in Grade 1, and reaches mixed-time addition, calendar arithmetic, century and millennium by Grades 4–6. Egypt teaches o'clock and half past in Grade 1, a.m./p.m. and quarter forms in Grade 2, and times before and after with durations in Grade 3. Kazakhstan teaches the hour in Grade 1, time and calendar across Grades 1–2, and the second, the century and the units of time in Grade 3. Nicaragua teaches analogue clocks, calendar work and elapsed time in Grade 2 — a 2:05 start lasting 1 h 15 min ends at 3:20 — and hours, minutes, seconds with elapsed-time calculation in Grade 3. Jordan teaches the days of the week and time by the hour and half hour in Grade 1, months and time to five minutes and the quarter hour in Grade 2, and elapsed time on a timeline in Grade 5. Sierra Leone's Primary time rows are teacher evidence — clock faces, quarters, time to the nearest minute, 12- to 24-hour conversion and time word problems across Primary 1–6 — and its JSS 2 pupils' handbook teaches time and duration, 12-hour and 24-hour, as pupil-book work. South Africa builds time through its workbooks from yesterday / today / tomorrow and months and days in Grade 1 to a.m./p.m., elapsed time and decades, centuries and millennia in Grade 5. Indonesia teaches whole-hour time on digital and analogue clocks in its Grade 2 measurement chapter, as filed, with no minutes, half-hours or calendar in that book.

Syria reads the clock from Grade 1 Semester 2 — whole hours, then half and quarter hours, then to the exact minute — works durations from Grade 2, adds and subtracts times in hours, minutes and seconds in Grades 4–5, converts time units with the metric ladders in Grade 6, and finishes with time-zone arithmetic in Grade 7.

Saudi Arabia times the school day from Grade 2 — o'clock, half and quarter hours, five-minute reading, and 1 hour = 60 minutes — computes elapsed minutes in Grade 3, and elapsed time with end-time problems in Grade 4, with the older 1445 Term-3 volumes witnessing and the partial exercises book adding practice evidence.

Rwanda teaches time from hours, half hours and the calendar in Year 2, through quarter hours, time conversions and timetables in Year 3 — whose Kinyarwanda edition also names thirty-minutes-to, a naming its English twin loses — to a.m./p.m. and the units of time in Primary 4, duration and time addition and subtraction in Primary 5, and 24-hour time and time zones in Primary 6.

Sudan teaches time from Grade 1's parts of the day and days of the week (listed from Friday), through Grade 2's day, week, months and year — where the book's “12 hours night + 12 hours day” stands corrected in the table above — to Grade 3's clock, minutes and seconds, and parts of the hour read with “and” (past) and “except” (to) phrasing.
