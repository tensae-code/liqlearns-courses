**Acceptance count: taught in 26 countries (Pakistan, Cambodia, Nepal, Ethiopia, Laos, Kuwait, Bangladesh, Yemen, Ghana, China, Libya, Vietnam, North Macedonia, Mauritania, Iraq, Greece, Egypt, Kazakhstan, Nicaragua, Jordan, Sierra Leone, South Africa, Iran, South Sudan, Indonesia, Syria).** Pakistan climbs from day and night and a January calendar in its Primer to solar and Islamic months in Grade 1, a.m./p.m. and the 2021 calendars in Grade 2, adding and subtracting hours in Grade 3, and 12-hour ↔ 24-hour conversion in Grade 4. Cambodia climbs from day parts and o'clock in Grade 1 to clocks to the minute in Grade 3, the 24-hour day in Grade 5 — taught corrected, its end of day is 24:00, not the printed 12:00 — and full time arithmetic in hours, minutes and seconds in Grade 6, with the Khmer-month 2015 calendar in Grade 2. Nepal teaches o'clock in Grade 1, quarter and half forms in Grade 2, time addition in Grade 3 — taught corrected, 6h 15m + 3h 10m = 9h 25m — and its books read printed Bikram Sambat calendars from the twelve month names in Grade 1 onwards. Ethiopia teaches whole and half hours in its Grade 1 student book, the Ethiopian clock names and twelve Ethiopian months in Grade 2, and the simple Ethiopian calendar in Grade 3; its Ethiopian time reckoning is teacher evidence from the Grade 1 teacher guide. Laos climbs from the hour hand in Grade 1 through a clock-and-calendar chapter in Grade 2 to adding, subtracting, multiplying and dividing compound times in Grade 5. Kuwait teaches whole hours, the minute and durations in Grades 1–2, reading time to 12 and to 24 hours in Grade 3, and Gregorian and Hijri months in Grade 1 — with the correction that its printed November calendar on p.76 is the 2025 layout, while November 2026 starts on a Sunday. Bangladesh reads clocks in Class 2, teaches the Bengali and English months together, and reaches 1 day = 86,400 seconds, the Bangla calendar, leap year, decade, era and century by Class 5. Yemen teaches the parts of the day and days of the week in Grade 1, clock reading and the Hijri months in Grade 2, and the Hijri and Gregorian calendars side by side in Grade 4. Ghana's time teaching is teacher evidence from the JICA Basic 3 and Basic 4 teacher manuals — personal referents, number-line and column-method durations, the knuckle method for months, and 12- and 24-hour reading. China teaches month lengths, the leap-year rule and the 24-hour clock in its Grade 3 year-month-day unit. Libya reads time on the hour and at half past in Grade 1, minutes and a.m./p.m. with elapsed time in Grade 2, time addition and subtraction in Grade 3, and 12- and 24-hour time again in Grade 7. Vietnam tells the time and the days of the week in Grade 1 and reaches adding, subtracting, multiplying and dividing time measures in Grade 5. North Macedonia teaches the months, the days of the week, the parts of the day and full-hour clocks in Grade 1. Mauritania climbs the time ladder in Band A, teaches time units, the clock, the minute, day, week, month and year in Grade 4, and sexagesimal time arithmetic in Grades 5–6. Iraq reads time to the hour, half hour and quarter hour in Grades 1–2, time in minutes in Grade 3, and names its two calendars directly — the Gregorian months in Grade 2 and the Hijri year in Grade 3. Greece orders daily events, reads clocks and runs the days of the week in Grade 1, and reaches mixed-time addition, calendar arithmetic, century and millennium by Grades 4–6. Egypt teaches o'clock and half past in Grade 1, a.m./p.m. and quarter forms in Grade 2, and times before and after with durations in Grade 3. Kazakhstan teaches the hour in Grade 1, time and calendar across Grades 1–2, and the second, the century and the units of time in Grade 3. Nicaragua teaches analogue clocks, calendar work and elapsed time in Grade 2 — a 2:05 start lasting 1 h 15 min ends at 3:20 — and hours, minutes, seconds with elapsed-time calculation in Grade 3. Jordan teaches the days of the week and time by the hour and half hour in Grade 1, months and time to five minutes and the quarter hour in Grade 2, and elapsed time on a timeline in Grade 5. Sierra Leone's Primary time rows are teacher evidence — clock faces, quarters, time to the nearest minute, 12- to 24-hour conversion and time word problems across Primary 1–6 — and its JSS 2 pupils' handbook teaches time and duration, 12-hour and 24-hour, as pupil-book work. South Africa builds time through its workbooks from yesterday / today / tomorrow and months and days in Grade 1 to a.m./p.m., elapsed time and decades, centuries and millennia in Grade 5. Iran starts deliberately at whole-hour faces in Grade 1, writes clock times two ways in Grade 2, reads 24-hour afternoon times like 19:25 in Grade 3, and shows the 60-minute carry in Grade 5. South Sudan teaches o'clock and the unit chain in Primary 1–3, converts 12-hour and 24-hour time both ways in Primary 4 — 4:45 p.m. = 16:45, 12:35 a.m. = 0:35 — and converts hours, minutes and seconds in Primary 5. Indonesia teaches whole-hour time on digital and analogue clocks in its Grade 2 measurement chapter, as filed, with no minutes, half-hours or calendar in that book.

Syria reads the clock from Grade 1 Semester 2 — whole hours, then half and quarter hours, then to the exact minute — works durations from Grade 2, adds and subtracts times in hours, minutes and seconds in Grades 4–5, converts time units with the metric ladders in Grade 6, and finishes with time-zone arithmetic in Grade 7.
