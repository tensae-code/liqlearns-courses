Syria reads the clock from Grade 1 Semester 2 — whole hours, then half and quarter hours, then to the exact minute — works durations from Grade 2, adds and subtracts times in hours, minutes and seconds in Grades 4–5, converts time units with the metric ladders in Grade 6, and finishes with time-zone arithmetic in Grade 7.

Saudi Arabia times the school day from Grade 2 — o'clock, half and quarter hours, five-minute reading, and 1 hour = 60 minutes — computes elapsed minutes in Grade 3, and elapsed time with end-time problems in Grade 4, with the older 1445 Term-3 volumes witnessing and the partial exercises book adding practice evidence.

Rwanda teaches time from hours, half hours and the calendar in Year 2, through quarter hours, time conversions and timetables in Year 3 — whose Kinyarwanda edition also names thirty-minutes-to, a naming its English twin loses — to a.m./p.m. and the units of time in Primary 4, duration and time addition and subtraction in Primary 5, and 24-hour time and time zones in Primary 6.

Sudan teaches time from Grade 1's parts of the day and days of the week (listed from Friday), through Grade 2's day, week, months and year — where the book's “12 hours night + 12 hours day” stands corrected in the table above — to Grade 3's clock, minutes and seconds, and parts of the hour read with “and” (past) and “except” (to) phrasing.

India carries time and calendars through its NCERT ladder — Math-Magic Class 1–5 — including order of the day; days of the week; how long things take; estimating arrival time.


Mongolia enters through its ministry textbooks Математик I, II, III, IV, V, VI, IX (Grades 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 9): days of the week; seasons and months; reading o'clock times.

Venezuela enters through the Colección Bicentenario, the MPPE's one-book-per-grade course: *Contemos... 1, 2, 3 y 4* (Grade 1), *Triángulos, rectángulos y algo más...* (Grade 2), *Aventuras de Patacalientes* (Grade 3) and *La Patria Buena* (Grade 5). Afghanistan enters through its Ministry of Education course — the Grade 1 course in two language editions (Pashto and Dari, one work with identical page maps) and Dari student books for Grades 4, 6, 7, 8, 10, 11 and 12, all printing year 1398 HS except the Grade 8 book, which prints 1399 HS on its cover and title page and 1398 HS on its specifications page. Barbados enters as CURRICULUM EVIDENCE only, through the *Primary Mathematics Syllabus* (Classes 1–4) of its Ministry of Education and Human Resource Development — its Class 1 syllabus requires naming the days of the week and the months of the year, reading the date from a calendar, using a.m. and p.m., and telling time on the hour, half hour and quarter hour; Class 2 adds determining dates at weekly intervals, stating and recording time in five-minute intervals, and calculating elapsed time; Classes 3 and 4 carry the full time programme — times of the day, periods of time, the instruments for measuring time and choosing between them, elapsed time, and the relationship between units of time. A syllabus states what is to be taught, in what order and to what depth; it teaches nothing itself. Barbados publishes no government pupil textbooks — primary mathematics books are commercial titles parents buy, and the syllabi's Suggested Texts appendices name them (*Caribbean Primary Mathematics* Levels 1–6; *Nelson Primary Maths for Caribbean Schools* 1–4; *Steps To Common Entrance Mathematics* 1–3) as named by the syllabus only: none is in hand and none is cited. Barbados is therefore named in this lesson's countries line and its syllabus requirements are cited in the table as curriculum evidence, but Barbados is not counted in the taught-in figure.
