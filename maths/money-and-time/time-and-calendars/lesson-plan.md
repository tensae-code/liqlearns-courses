# Lesson plan — Time and calendars

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 15 — Money, Time and Everyday Mathematics · **Lesson:** Time and calendars
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 115 of 136, the third lesson of Unit 15 — Money, Time and Everyday Mathematics (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** time — reading clocks, changing between 12-hour and 24-hour time, working out how long things last and adding and subtracting times, and reading the calendars of the world.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on counting and on adding and subtracting — the learner can already count in 5s around a clock, add two amounts and borrow in a subtraction. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown changing.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Time and Calendars — Reading Clocks and the Parts of the Day** — read analogue and digital clocks to the minute, say times as quarter past / half past / quarter to, use a.m. and p.m. correctly, and convert the time units (hour, minute, second, day, week). (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Time and Calendars — 12 Hours, 24 Hours and Durations** — change between 12-hour and 24-hour time both ways (including 0:35 for 12:35 a.m. and 24:00 for the end of the day), and work out durations from start and end times, including across noon on a timeline. (Was depth Level 2.)
   The lesson text's units-of-time part sits in this sub lesson; its depth is old Level 1 (the Level 1 pass mark carries the time-unit conversions), and its question items sit in Sub lesson 1. The question markers follow the old levels, item by item.
3. **Time and Calendars — Time Arithmetic and the Calendars of the World** — add and subtract compound times with the 60-carry and 60-borrow, and read a calendar page — naming its system (Gregorian, Hijri, Ethiopian, Bikram Sambat, Bangla, Khmer), its months, and whether its year is a leap year. (Was depth Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson (no hardness ranking is written in this lesson's question files).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Counting (Unit 1) — the clock is a counting circle, in 5s for the minutes. Recommended, never forced.
- Recommended first: Adding and subtracting (Unit 2) — durations and time arithmetic are adding and subtracting with a carry of 60, not 10. Recommended, never forced.
- Recommended first: Money (Level 113, same unit) — the other everyday-measures lesson; the two share the "join between units" idea. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Read analogue and digital clocks — o'clock and half past first (Egypt, Grade 1, Ch. 8, pp.86–87), then to the minute (Cambodia, Grade 3, Ch. 8, pp.55–60 — 6:10, 11:45, 10:38, 8:07).
2. Say a time as quarter past, half past and quarter to, and use a.m. and p.m. correctly (Egypt, Grade 2 old series, pp.82–91; Pakistan, Grade 2, Unit 5, pp.125–126).
3. Change between 12-hour and 24-hour time both ways — 4:45 p.m. = 16:45, 12:35 a.m. = 0:35, 14:55 = 2:55 p.m. (South Sudan, Primary 4, §2.5, pp.57–59); 8:15 at night = 20:15, and the end of the day is 24:00, taught corrected (Cambodia, Grade 5, L16, pp.103–106).
4. Convert time units — 1 hour = 60 minutes, 1 minute = 60 seconds, 1 day = 24 hours, 1 week = 7 days (South Sudan, Primary 3, §2.8, pp.41–44).
5. Work out a duration — a lesson starting at 2:05 lasting 1 h 15 min ends at 3:20 (Nicaragua, Grade 2, Unit 1, pp.13–24); an interval built on a timeline (Jordan, Grade 5 Term 2, Unit 9, p.108).
6. Add and subtract compound times with the 60-carry — 6h 15m + 3h 10m = 9h 25m, taught corrected (Nepal, Grade 3, Lesson 15, p.69); 4h 47m 32s + 3h 50m 45s = 8h 38m 17s (Cambodia, Grade 6, L19, pp.127–130).
7. Read a calendar and name its system — Gregorian months (Iraq, Grade 2, p.112), Hijri months with Ramadan the 9th (Pakistan, Grade 1, Unit 5, pp.102–103), the Ethiopian months (Ethiopia, Grade 2, Ch. 11, pp.167–181), the twelve Bikram Sambat months (Nepal, Grade 1, Lesson 45, p.101), the Bangla calendar (Bangladesh, Class 5, Ch. 12, p.133) and the Khmer months on a full-year calendar (Cambodia, Grade 2, Ch. 15, p.101) — and say what a leap year is (China, Grade 3, U6, pp.76–90).

## What you need
A clock with hands you can move — real, drawn, or two paper hands pinned to a cardboard circle.
A calendar of the current year, and if you can get one, a calendar of a second system.
Paper and a pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner reads three clocks, says what they are doing at those times today, and guesses how long a familiar activity lasts (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — reading the clock; a.m./p.m. and the parts of the day; 12 hours and 24 hours; the units of time; durations; adding and subtracting time; the calendars of the world (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the clock part: a picture of a clock's minute hand sweeping one full turn while the hour hand creeps from 3 to 4 — the student calls out the minutes in 5s as the hand passes each number.
   - ADD IMAGE in the 24-hour part: a picture of one straight day-line from 00:00 to 24:00 with the two 12-hour halves marked on it — the student points to 13:00, 20:15 and 24:00 and says each in 12-hour words.
   - ADD GIF in the duration part: a picture of a timeline bar filling from a start clock to an end clock — the student says the length of the bar in hours and minutes.
   - ADD IMAGE in the calendars part: a picture of one month page shown in three calendar systems side by side — the student finds today's date shape: week rows, day numbers, month name, in each one.
3. **Say it to your companion** — read clocks to each other, convert times both ways, and quiz each other on calendar facts (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — clocks and 24-hour time in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — write your day in both clock systems, work two durations, and read a real calendar page (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Reads the two hands the wrong way round → short hand first, always: the short hand names the hour, the long hand counts the minutes in 5s; Egypt's Grade 1 books separate the hands before mixing them (pp.86–87, then pp.50–55).
- Loses a.m./p.m. → anchor to the day: before noon is a.m., after noon is p.m., the way Egypt's Grade 2 new series reads it off a day timeline (Ch. 4, pp.48–55).
- Adds times with a carry of 100 → write the carry rule on the work: 60 minutes make the hour, so 75 minutes is 1 hour 15; Nepal's corrected example (6h 15m + 3h 10m = 9h 25m) is the model.
- Cannot find a duration across noon → build it in parts on a timeline, the Jordan way: to noon first, then the rest (Grade 5, p.108).
- Mixes the calendars up → one fact per system, said whole: "The Hijri year is a lunar year; Ramadan is its 9th month." "Ethiopia's calendar is taught from its own month names." Never merge the systems into one list.
- This lesson's next door: Speed, distance and time (Level 116, same unit) — durations learned here become the time in v = distance ÷ time.

## Teacher note
The full text is lesson/lesson.md; every part traces to a book and page in lesson/sources.md. Three book errors are taught corrected and never as fact: Cambodia's Grade 5 end-of-day clock is taught as 24:00, not the printed 12:00; Nepal's Grade 3 addition is taught as 6h 15m + 3h 10m = 9h 25m, not the printed "3h 5m"; Kuwait's Grade 1 November calendar on p.76 is the 2025 layout — for November 2026, 1 November is a Sunday and 30 November a Monday. Two stocks are teacher evidence and are worded as such: Ghana's time rows (JICA teacher manuals B3 and B4) and Ethiopia's Ethiopian-time-reckoning row, which comes from its Grade 1 teacher guide (Ch. 11, pp.78–81) — Ethiopia's student books themselves carry the Ethiopian months and the simple calendar. Sierra Leone's Primary time rows are likewise teacher evidence; its JSS 2 pupils' handbook rows are pupil-book rows. A teacher sending this lesson sends the lesson and its question list as they are — choosing, not remaking.
