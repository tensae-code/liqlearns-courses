# Hands-on — Time and calendars

The lesson used real clocks and real calendars, the way every book starts it.

## Make a clock, move the hands
Pin two paper hands — one short, one long — to a cardboard circle marked 1–12. A partner calls a time: you set the hands and say it three ways ("three fifteen", "quarter past three", "3:15"). Then your partner sets a time and you read it to the minute, counting the long hand in 5s, the way Cambodia's Grade 3 book reads 6:10, 11:45 and 10:38. Ten times each, then swap.

## The day-line
Draw one straight line for a day, 00:00 at the left and 24:00 at the right, with 12:00 (noon) in the middle — Cambodia's Grade 5 timeline shape. Mark your real day on it: waking, school or work, meals, sleep. Under each mark write the 12-hour form with a.m. or p.m. Read the whole line aloud both ways until 13:00, 20:15 and 24:00 feel as ordinary as 1 p.m. and 8:15 p.m.

## Duration detective
Time three real activities with a clock: boiling water, walking to a known place, a song. Write start, end and duration for each, building the duration in timeline parts the Jordan Grade 5 way (to the next whole hour first, then the rest). Then check one by adding: start + duration must give the end.

## Two calendars, one today
Take a calendar page from your own system and, if you can find one, a page from a second system taught in the lesson — Hijri, Ethiopian, Bikram Sambat, Bangla or Khmer. For each page, find and point to: the month name, the days of the week, today's number, and how many days the month has. Say one whole fact per system ("Ramadan is the 9th Hijri month"; "a common year has 365 days and a leap year 366"). If the printed page disagrees with the real year — as Kuwait's printed November page is the 2025 layout, not 2026's — say so and write the correct first day.
