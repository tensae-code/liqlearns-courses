# Time and calendars — true or false

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Time and Calendars — Reading Clocks and the Parts of the Day · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Time and Calendars — 12 Hours, 24 Hours and Durations · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Time and Calendars — Time Arithmetic and the Calendars of the World.

Say true or false. If it is false, say why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** The short hand names the hour and the long hand counts the minutes.
**Answer:** True — the hand rule every book in the lesson starts with.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** Iran's Grade 1 book teaches minutes from the start.
**Answer:** False — as filed, Iran's Grade 1 book stops at whole-hour faces; minutes are expressly not taught there.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** 12:35 a.m. is 12:35 in 24-hour time.
**Answer:** False — it is 0:35, because 12 a.m. is the start of the day (South Sudan, Primary 4).

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** A printed calendar page can be read for any year.
**Answer:** False — a page belongs to its own year: Kuwait's printed November page is the 2025 layout, not 2026's, taught corrected.

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** 6h 15m + 3h 10m = 3h 5m.
**Answer:** False — that is Nepal's printed error; the correct sum is 9h 25m, taught corrected.

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** Ethiopia's Ethiopian time reckoning is taught in its Grade 1 student book.
**Answer:** False — as filed, it comes from the Grade 1 teacher guide and is teacher evidence; the student books carry the Ethiopian months and the simple calendar.
