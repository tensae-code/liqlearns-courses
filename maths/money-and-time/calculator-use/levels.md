# Calculator Use — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, expressed as its SUB
LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure — README, "Units, Lessons and
Sub Lessons"). One lesson, one level (Level 164). The lesson
was built new in the expansion era (Sri Lanka expansion merge,
2026-11-11), so there are no old depth layers to map: the sub
lessons below are its depths as built, cut at the founding
books' own shape — the Grade 9 "Calculator" chapter's keys, then
the calculator's checking use, which the same chapter and the
Grade 9 guides' terms carry. The questions live in the type
files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view.

Pass marks: the founding chapter names its outcomes (the keys =,
%, x² and √x and their use) and states no pass mark, so no mark
is invented — each sub lesson's pass mark is stated as the
observable thing the learner can do.

## Sub lesson 1 — Meet the Calculator
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: name the display, the number keys, the four working keys, the = key and the clear key, and enter a two-number calculation in order, clearing and re-entering after a deliberate mis-press.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Everyday Keys: %, x² and √x
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: find a percentage of a quantity with the % key, square a number with x², take a square root with √x, and close the loop by squaring a root's answer back to the starting number.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Calculator as a Checking Tool
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: estimate before calculating, compare the display against the estimate, and say — from the estimate alone — why a given display (249.6 or 24,960 for 48 × 52) must be a mis-press.

## What "maxed" means here

The learner handles the machine the way Sri Lanka's Grade 9
books teach it: the keys named and used (=, %, x², √x — *Mathematics
Part II*, Grade 9 pupil's book, "The Calculator", printed p. 12,
and its Sinhala twin's chapter 11), and every machine answer
guarded by an estimate made first.
