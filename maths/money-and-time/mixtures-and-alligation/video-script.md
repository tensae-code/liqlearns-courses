# Video script — Mixtures and Alligation

Super-simple voice. The tutor talks; the two sacks and the balance change as they are named.

---

Two sacks of beans. One costs 300 a kilo. The other costs 400 a kilo. Mix them, and the mixed sack has its own price — between the two. Always between the two.

To find it: add up what everything cost, and divide by how much there is. Two kilos at 300, three kilos at 400. Total cost, 1,800. Total mass, 5 kilos. Mixture price: 360 a kilo.

Now run it backwards. You know the mixture price. Look at the gaps. The mixture sits 150 above the cheap price, and 50 below the dear one. The gaps are 3 to 1 — so the amounts are 1 to 3. The bigger gap takes the smaller amount. The gains and the losses balance, like a scale.

Unknown price? Total value of the mixture, minus what the known sack is worth, divided by the unknown sack's mass.

Gaps, balance, totals. That is mixtures.
