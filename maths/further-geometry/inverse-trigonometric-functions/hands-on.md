# Hands-on — Inverse trigonometric functions

## Fold the wave
Draw y = sin x from −360° to 360° on graph paper. Fold the paper so only the piece from −90° to 90° shows. That one rising piece is the only part that can be undone one-to-one — the restricted sine Nicaragua's and Armenia's books build arcsine from.

## The triangle inside arcsin
Pick x = 3/5. Draw a right triangle with hypotenuse 5 and opposite 3. Its sharp angle is arcsin(3/5). Now read cos and tan off the same triangle: you have just worked cos(arcsin(3/5)) and tan(arcsin(3/5)) with no formula — the books' composition method, in your hands.

## Calculator angle hunt
With a partner: one of you secretly picks an angle inside the arcsine range and says only its sine. The other recovers the angle with the calculator's inverse key. Swap roles for cosine and tangent, staying inside each range. Ten rounds.

## Solve it with a picture
Draw y = tan x and y = sin x on the same axes from 0° to 360°. Every crossing is a solution of tan x = sin x — the graphical solving Nicaragua's book teaches. Mark each crossing and read off its angle.
