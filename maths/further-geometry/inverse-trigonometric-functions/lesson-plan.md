# Lesson plan — Inverse trigonometric functions

**Course:** Maths · **Stage:** Stage 9 — Further Geometry · **Lesson:** Inverse trigonometric functions
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 74 of 136, in Stage 9 — Further Geometry (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** undoing sine, cosine and tangent — arcsine, arccosine and arctangent, why their domains must be restricted first, what values they give back, and how to use them to find angles and simplify expressions.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on right-triangle trigonometry and on trigonometric functions and their graphs — the learner can already work sin, cos and tan forwards. It needs nothing else.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain why sine, cosine and tangent must be restricted before they can be undone, and state the three restricted domains, the way Nicaragua's Grade 10 book teaches it (Unit 2, printed pp. 133–141).
2. Define arcsine, arccosine and arctangent and state the range each one returns, the way Armenia's Grade 10 book sets them out (Chapter 4, §10, printed p. 119; §12, printed p. 145).
3. Evaluate inverse values exactly for the special angles and by calculator for other values, including a very large tangent value, as Nicaragua's book does (printed pp. 133–141).
4. Simplify compositions such as cos(arcsin x) and sin(2 arcsin u), and solve a trigonometric equation graphically, as Nicaragua's book does (printed pp. 133–141).
5. Name where Laos's Grade 12 book places the inverses of the trigonometric functions — inside its chapter on inverse functions (Chapter 8, Lesson 24, printed p. 227) — and where Cambodia's Grade 12 Advanced book places them, filed from its contents and guide (§3.2, printed p. 54; see the Teacher note for the exact wording).

## If the learner is stuck
- Gives an angle outside the range → the inverse always answers with ONE angle, inside its range. Check the answer against the range first, before any working.
- Thinks arcsin(sin x) is always x → it is x only when x already sits inside the restricted domain; outside it, the answer folds back into the range. Nicaragua's book stresses exactly this point (printed pp. 133–141).
- Cannot start cos(arcsin x) → draw the triangle: hypotenuse 1, opposite x, third side by Pythagoras. The picture does the work.
- This lesson's next door: Trigonometric equations (same stage) — the inverses are how its answers are written down.
