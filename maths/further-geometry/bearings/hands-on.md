# Hands-on — Bearings

## Classroom compass walk
Stand in an open space with a compass. A partner calls a three-figure bearing and a number of steps ("060°, six steps"). Walk it. Then find your own reverse bearing and walk back to the start — if your reverse is wrong, you will not land home.

## The schoolyard map
Pick three landmarks you can see (a gate, a tree, a tap). From one standing point, take each landmark's bearing with a compass and pace its distance. Draw the three positions to scale on paper, each in distance-bearing form (r, θ) — Sierra Leone's Lesson 121 work, done for real.

## Nepal's ship, drawn
Draw Nepal's Grade 8 journey to scale: a ship sails on bearing 045° for 80 miles, then on bearing 135° for 90 miles, at 1 cm = 20 miles. Measure the start-to-finish line: the book's answer is 5 cm, so 100 miles. Your ruler checks the book.

## Two-leg treasure
Hide a small object with a two-leg clue you write yourself: a bearing and distance, then a second bearing and distance from a named start. Swap clues with a partner and hunt. A clue that cannot be followed has a wrong bearing — fix the clue, not the hunter.
