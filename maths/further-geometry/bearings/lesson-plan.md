# Lesson plan — Bearings

**Course:** Maths · **Stage:** Stage 9 — Further Geometry · **Lesson:** Bearings
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 76 of 136, in Stage 9 — Further Geometry (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** bearings — direction measured clockwise from north and always written in three figures — reading them, drawing them, reversing them, and solving travel problems with them using Pythagoras, the trig ratios and the sine and cosine rules.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on right-triangle trigonometry and on the sine and cosine rules — the learner can already solve a flat triangle. It needs nothing else.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Right-triangle trigonometry, and Sine and cosine rules. Recommended, never forced.
- A protractor, a ruler and a calculator are needed.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Give a direction as a compass bearing and as a three-figure bearing, the way Sierra Leone's SSS 2 course opens the topic (L117, printed p. 63; L118, printed p. 66).
2. Find a reverse (back) bearing by adding or subtracting 180°, as Sierra Leone (L119, printed p. 69) and Nepal's Grade 8 student book (Unit 9, printed pp. 77–82) both teach it.
3. Draw a journey from bearing statements and write a point's position in distance-bearing form (r, θ), as Sierra Leone does (L120, printed p. 72; L121, printed p. 75).
4. Solve bearing problems with right triangles, as Sierra Leone (L122, printed p. 78) and South Sudan's Secondary 1 book (Unit 3, printed pp. 74–77) do.
5. Solve bearing problems with non-right triangles using the sine and cosine rules, as Sierra Leone (L123, printed p. 81; L124, printed p. 84), Libya's Grade 11 Science book (Chapter 2, printed pp. 42–68) and Jordan's Grade 10 book (Unit 4, Lesson 1, printed p. 100) do.

## What you need
A protractor, a ruler, paper and a calculator. A compass face (or a phone compass) for the hands-on work.

## If the learner is stuck
- Measures from east, or anti-clockwise → bearings have ONE start and ONE direction: from north, clockwise. Redraw the north arrow at the FROM point first, every time.
- Writes 45° instead of 045° → bearings are always three figures; pad with zeros at the front.
- Adds 180° and passes 360° → wrap around: subtract 360° (equivalently, subtract 180° when the bearing is already past 180°).
- Cannot start a travel problem → draw it before calculating: north arrow at each point, legs in order, closing line last. The drawing chooses the rule.
