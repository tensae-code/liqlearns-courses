# Bearings — multiple choice

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Reading, Writing and Drawing Bearings · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Reverse Bearings and Drawn Journeys · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Solving Bearing Problems.

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** A bearing is always measured:
a) Clockwise from east  b) Clockwise from north  c) Anti-clockwise from north
**Answer:** b) — one start (north) and one direction (clockwise), as Sierra Leone's L118 and South Sudan's S1 book both state it.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** The bearing of due west is:
a) 090°  b) 180°  c) 270°
**Answer:** c) 270° — a three-quarter clockwise turn from north.

**3. (Sub lesson 1)** North-east written as a bearing is:
a) 45°  b) 045°  c) 450°
**Answer:** b) 045° — bearings are always written in three figures.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** The bearing from A to B is 075°. The reverse bearing from B to A is:
a) 075°  b) 105°  c) 255°
**Answer:** c) 255° — 075° + 180°, Nepal's own worked pair (Grade 8 student book, Unit 9).

**5. (Sub lesson 2)** The reverse bearing of 310° is:
a) 130°  b) 050°  c) 490°
**Answer:** a) 130° — past 180°, so subtract 180°: 310° − 180° = 130°.

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** A ship sails on bearing 045° for 80 miles, then on bearing 135° for 90 miles, drawn at 1 cm = 20 miles (Nepal, Grade 8). The start-to-finish distance is:
a) 170 miles  b) 100 miles  c) 85 miles
**Answer:** b) 100 miles — the closing line measures 5 cm on the drawing, and 5 × 20 = 100.

**7. (Sub lesson 3)** A bearing problem's drawn closing triangle has no right angle. The tools to finish it are:
a) Pythagoras only  b) The sine and cosine rules  c) Bearings cannot be solved then
**Answer:** b) — Sierra Leone's Part 3 (L123) and Libya's Chapter 2 both finish such problems with the sine and cosine rules.
