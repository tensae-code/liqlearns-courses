# Trigonometric equations — the lesson

Course: Maths · Unit: Unit 9 — Further Geometry · Lesson: Trigonometric equations
Built from: Nicaragua, Grade 10 student book and module — Laos, Grade 11, Ch. 8 — Armenia, Grade 10, Ch. 4 — Libya, Grades 11 and 12 Science — Yemen, Grade 11 Science Part 2 — Kuwait, Grades 10 and 11 Term 2 — Cambodia, Grade 11 Basic and Advanced — Vietnam, Grade 11, Bài 4 — South Sudan, Secondary 4 Additional Mathematics — China, Compulsory Volume 3 (B edition), §7.3.5

## Do it first
Solve sin x = 1/2 — with your finger, not a formula. Draw a
unit circle in your head: sine is the height of the point.
Where is the height exactly 1/2? At 30°, yes — but the point
also reaches height 1/2 coming down the other side, at 150°.
In radians: x = π/6 and x = 5π/6. Two answers in one turn.
Almost every equation in this lesson behaves like that, and
stopping at one answer is the classic way to lose half the
marks.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Basic Forms on the Unit Circle

**Equation, not identity.** The last lesson's identities are
true for every angle. A trigonometric **equation** holds only
for some angles, and solving it means finding all of them.
Armenia's Grade 10 book builds the topic in two steps: the
simplest equations first (§10, printed p. 119), then the main
solving methods (§11, printed p. 133). Yemen gives equations
their own lesson inside its trigonometry unit (Grade 11
Science Part 2, lesson 9-5, printed p. 108), and Libya meets
simple equations beside the simple identities in Grade 11
(Grade 11 Science, Ch. 3, printed pp. 71–92).

**The three basic forms.** Cambodia's Grade 11 Basic book
solves the forms one at a time: cos x = a, then sin x = a,
then tan x = a, then cot x = m (Ch. 3 Lesson 3, printed
pp. 98–112). Vietnam teaches the same basic equations as its
Bài 4 (Grade 11 Volume 1, printed p. 31). The unit circle
settles each one:

- **sin x = a** — a horizontal line at height a cuts the circle twice (unless |a| > 1: no solution; or |a| = 1: one solution per turn). sin x = 1/2 gives π/6 and 5π/6.
- **cos x = a** — a vertical line cuts twice. cos x = −√3/2 gives 5π/6 and 7π/6.
- **tan x = a** — tangent repeats every half turn, so tan x = 1 gives π/4 and 5π/4 in one full turn.

Nicaragua's module works the same ground with deliberately
easy examples: sen x = 1 on [0, 2π] gives the single solution
x = π/2, read off with arcsine, and a cosine equation is
solved by its two quadrants (Grade 10 module, Unit IV,
printed pp. 177–180). Kuwait's Grade 10 book solves equations
inside its relations-between-functions work (Term 2, printed
pp. 103–106) and returns to them in Grade 11 (Term 2, printed
pp. 92–99).

## Sub lesson 2 — The General Solution and Identity Equations

**The general solution.** One turn is rarely the whole
answer. Nicaragua's student book states the method: find the
particular solutions within one period, then write the
general solution with n ∈ ℤ — each solution plus whole turns
of 2π (Unit 3, printed pp. 156–165). So sin x = 1/2 solves
completely as x = π/6 + 2πn or x = 5π/6 + 2πn, n ∈ ℤ. Given
any interval, you then pick out the values of n that land
inside it — the book even asks for the solutions at n = −10,
to make the picking-out a habit.

**Equations that need identities first.** The module's rule
comes first: express everything in terms of a single ratio
(Grade 10 module, Unit IV, printed pp. 177–180). Then the
methods Armenia names (§11, printed p. 133): **factorise** —
2sin x cos x = sin x becomes sin x(2cos x − 1) = 0, so sin x
= 0 or cos x = 1/2 (never divide by sin x: that throws away
the sin x = 0 family); **substitute** — an equation like
2cos²x − 3cos x + 1 = 0 is a quadratic in cos x, solved by
the quadratic formula Nicaragua recalls for exactly this use
(Unit 3, printed pp. 156–165) and by the substitution method
South Sudan practises on its quadratic trigonometric
equations, solved by formula and by graph (Secondary 4
Additional Mathematics, Unit 2, printed pp. 76–79). South
Sudan solves the simple equations both graphically and
arithmetically (printed pp. 70–75), and China's B-edition
book solves equations and inequalities from the trig lines
and graphs in its §7.3.5 (Compulsory Volume 3, printed
p. 57) — three countries, one agreement: check the algebra
against the picture.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Single-Term Form, Graphs and Inequalities

**The single-term form.** The last lesson rewrote a·cosθ ±
b·sinθ as one wave. Libya's Grade 12 book solves equations
of exactly that form with it (Ch. 2, printed pp. 37–54): to
solve 3cos θ + 4sin θ = 5, write the left side as
5cos(θ − φ) with cos φ = 3/5 and sin φ = 4/5; then
cos(θ − φ) = 1, so θ = φ ≈ 53.1° plus whole turns.

**Inequalities on the unit circle.** Laos is the lesson's
inequality source, in two steps: equations in L31, then
inequalities worked on the unit circle in L32 (Grade 11,
Ch. 8, printed pp. 237, 244). Its worked example: solve
cos(2x − π/4) ≥ √3/2 for 0 ≤ x < 2π. Put t = 2x − π/4. On
the circle, cos t ≥ √3/2 holds within π/6 of the point
(1, 0): −π/6 ≤ t ≤ π/6, plus whole turns. Feeding the turns
through t = 2x − π/4 and keeping 0 ≤ x < 2π gives **x ∈
[π/24, 5π/24] or x ∈ [25π/24, 29π/24]**. Cambodia's Advanced
book carries inequalities of the form a·cos x + b·sin x > c
in the same spirit (Grade 11 Advanced, Ch. 3, printed
pp. 57–72).

> Tip — from us, not from the books. End every solution by
> asking two questions: "Did I get both angles in the turn?"
> and "Did the question ask for one turn, or for all turns?"
> Those two questions catch nearly every lost mark in this
> lesson — a missing second solution, or a general solution
> given where an interval was asked for.

## Say it to your companion
Solve cos x = −√3/2 aloud: name the line on the circle, both
angles (5π/6 and 7π/6), and the general solution. Then ask
your companion to solve tan x = 1 the same way, finishing
with the whole sentence: "In one turn, x is π/4 or 5π/4;
in general, x is π/4 plus nπ."

## Do It Yourself
1. Solve sin x = √3/2 on [0, 2π). (Answer: π/3 and 2π/3.)
2. Solve cos x = −1/2 on [0, 2π). (Answer: 2π/3 and 4π/3.)
3. Write the general solution of sin x = 1. (Answer: x = π/2 + 2πn, n ∈ ℤ — Nicaragua's module example.)
4. Solve 2cos²x − 1 = 0 on [0, 2π). (Answer: cos x = ±√2/2, so x = π/4, 3π/4, 5π/4, 7π/4.)
5. Solve sin x(2cos x − 1) = 0 on [0, 2π). (Answer: sin x = 0 gives 0 and π; cos x = 1/2 gives π/3 and 5π/3 — four solutions; dividing by sin x would have lost two of them.)
6. Solve cos x ≥ √3/2 on [0, 2π). (Answer: x ∈ [0, π/6] or x ∈ [11π/6, 2π) — the boundary angles come from the matching equation, the arcs from the circle, as in Laos's L32.)
