# Lesson plan — Trigonometric equations

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 9 — Further Geometry · **Lesson:** Trigonometric equations
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 73 of 136, Unit 9 — Further Geometry, after trigonometric identities (Level 72) and before inverse trigonometric functions (Level 74) (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** solving trigonometric equations — the basic forms sin x = a, cos x = a, tan x = a on the unit circle, the general solution, equations that need identities or factorising, and trigonometric inequalities.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on trigonometric identities (Level 72) and on radian measure (Level 71) — the learner can rewrite with the chain and read angles in radians. It needs nothing else.

2. **The General Solution and Identity Equations** — the general solution with n ∈ ℤ, and equations solved by rewriting in one ratio, factorising or substituting. (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **The Single-Term Form, Graphs and Inequalities** — a·cosθ ± b·sinθ = c by the single-term form, solutions read off a graph, and inequalities on the unit circle. (Was depth Level 3.)

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Trigonometric identities (Level 72, same stage) — most equations are opened up with an identity before they can be solved. Recommended, never forced.
- Recommended first: Radian measure (Level 71, same stage) — solutions are given in radians, on an interval like 0 ≤ x < 2π. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Solve sin x = a, cos x = a and tan x = a on [0, 2π) by reading the unit circle, and say when there is no solution (Cambodia, Grade 11 Basic, Ch. 3 Lesson 3, printed pp. 98–112; Vietnam, Grade 11 Volume 1, Bài 4, printed p. 31).
2. Write the general solution with n ∈ ℤ and pick out the solutions inside any given interval (Nicaragua, Grade 10 student book, Unit 3, printed pp. 156–165).
3. Solve equations that first need an identity, one ratio throughout, factorising or a substitution — including quadratic-shaped equations (Armenia, Grade 10, Ch. 4 §§10–11, printed pp. 119, 133; South Sudan, Secondary 4 Additional Mathematics, Unit 2, printed pp. 70–79).
4. Solve an equation of the form a·cosθ ± b·sinθ = c by the single-term form (Libya, Grade 12 Science, Ch. 2, printed pp. 37–54).
5. Solve a trigonometric inequality on the unit circle (Laos, Grade 11, Ch. 8 L32, printed p. 244).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A unit-circle drawing with the standard angles marked. A calculator for checking, not for solving.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner solves sin x = 1/2 by pointing at the unit circle and naming both angles before any method is given (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — equation or identity; the three basic forms on the unit circle; the general solution; using identities, factorising and substitution; the single-term form; solving from graphs; inequalities on the unit circle (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the basic-forms part: a picture of a point travelling round a unit circle, a horizontal line at height 1/2 cutting the circle twice, the two angles lighting up as the point reaches them — the student names both solutions of sin x = 1/2 as they light.
   - ADD IMAGE at the general-solution part: a picture of the same two lit angles with "+ 2π, + 4π …" written as extra full turns beside them → the student writes the general solution with n ∈ ℤ.
   - ADD IMAGE at the inequality part: a picture of a unit circle with the arc where cos(2x − π/4) ≥ √3/2 shaded, following Laos's worked example → the student shades the arc where cos t ≥ √3/2 on their own circle.
3. **Say it to your companion** — solve an equation aloud for your companion, naming each step and both solutions (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the unit circle, the two solutions and the general solution in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — solve the three basic forms, one identity equation, one quadratic-shaped equation and one inequality (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Gives only one solution → on one turn of the circle, sine and cosine almost always give two angles; draw the line cutting the circle and count the cuts (Cambodia, Grade 11 Basic, printed pp. 98–112).
- Loses solutions after using an identity → write everything in one ratio first, the way Nicaragua's module teaches (Unit IV, printed pp. 177–180), and never divide by a ratio that could be zero — factor instead.
- Cannot start an inequality → solve the matching equation first to find the boundary angles, then read the arc off the circle, the order Laos uses (Grade 11, Ch. 8 L31–L32, printed pp. 237, 244).
- This lesson's next door: Inverse trigonometric functions (Level 74, same stage) — the arcsine, arccosine and arctangent this lesson already meets as notation.
