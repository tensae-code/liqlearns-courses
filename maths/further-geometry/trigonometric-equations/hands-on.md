# Hands-on — Trigonometric equations

The lesson solved on the unit circle; here the hands draw it.

## The cutting line
Draw a big unit circle and mark the standard angles. Draw the horizontal line at height 1/2: it cuts the circle at π/6 and 5π/6 — the two solutions of sin x = 1/2. Repeat with the vertical line x = −√3/2 for cos x = −√3/2 (cuts at 5π/6 and 7π/6) and with the line y = x for tan x = 1. The picture is the method Cambodia teaches for cos x = a, sin x = a, tan x = a (Grade 11 Basic, printed pp. 98–112).

## Round and round
Take the two solutions of sin x = 1/2. Walk them forward: add 2π, then 4π, writing each new pair. Then pick out only the solutions between 0 and 4π. That picking-out is Nicaragua's method: particular solutions in one period first, then the general solution with n ∈ ℤ (Grade 10 student book, Unit 3, printed pp. 156–165).

## Graph crossing
Sketch y = sin x and y = 1/2 on the same axes from 0 to 2π. Mark the two crossings and read the angles off. South Sudan's Additional book solves simple equations graphically and arithmetically side by side (Unit 2, printed pp. 70–75) — do both and check they agree.

## Shade the arc
For cos t ≥ √3/2, shade the arc of the unit circle within π/6 of the point (1, 0) on either side. Then follow Laos: with t = 2x − π/4, turn the shaded t-arcs back into x-intervals for 0 ≤ x < 2π (Grade 11, Ch. 8 L32, printed p. 244). Compare your intervals with the lesson's: [π/24, 5π/24] and [25π/24, 29π/24].
