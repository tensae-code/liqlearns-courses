# The special lines and centres of a triangle — fill in the blank

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — The Four Special Lines and the Mid-Segment · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — The Four Centres and Their Facts · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Divisions, the 30° Side and Excircles.

Fill each blank with the one right word or value.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** The segment joining the midpoints of two sides is ______ to the third side.
**Answer:** parallel — and half its length: the mid-segment theorem (Kuwait 7-1; Sierra Leone L61).

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** An angle bisector splits a vertex angle into ______ equal angles.
**Answer:** two — bisector means a halving of the angle.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** The centroid divides every median in the ratio ______ : 1, measured from the vertex.
**Answer:** 2 — the vertex part is twice the side part (Kuwait 7-6; Iraq, Grade 9, p. 116).

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** The circumcentre is the centre of the circle through the three ______, called the circumcircle.
**Answer:** vertices — because OA = OB = OC (Cambodia, Lesson 16 §2, printed p. 207).

**5. (Sub lesson 2)** The incentre is the same perpendicular distance from all three ______ of the triangle.
**Answer:** sides — which is why its circle, the incircle, touches every side.

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** Every triangle has ______ excircles.
**Answer:** three — one touching each side from outside (Kazakhstan, Geometry 9 §20).
