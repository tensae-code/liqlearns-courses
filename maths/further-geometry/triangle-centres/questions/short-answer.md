# The special lines and centres of a triangle — short answer

Answer in a word or a short line.

**1. (Level 1)** Name the line that joins a vertex of a triangle to the midpoint of the opposite side.
**Answer:** A median.

**2. (Level 1)** The third side of a triangle is 9 cm. How long is the segment joining the midpoints of the other two sides?
**Answer:** 4.5 cm — the mid-segment is half the third side (Kuwait 7-1, printed pp. 140–152).

**3. (Level 1)** Name the line drawn from a vertex perpendicular to the opposite side.
**Answer:** An altitude.

**4. (Level 2)** What is the meeting point of the three altitudes called?
**Answer:** The orthocentre (Kuwait 7-5; Yemen 7-6).

**5. (Level 2)** A median AA′ is 21 cm. Using Cambodia's statement AG = (2/3)AA′, find AG and GA′.
**Answer:** AG = 14 cm and GA′ = 7 cm — the 2:1 split, long part at the vertex (Lesson 16 §1, printed p. 205).

**6. (Level 2)** In a right-angled triangle, where exactly does the circumcentre sit?
**Answer:** At the midpoint of the hypotenuse — it is half the hypotenuse from all three vertices (Kuwait 7-2 and 7-3).

**7. (Level 3)** The sides around angle A of a triangle are 6 cm and 9 cm. In what ratio does the bisector of A divide the opposite side? (Sierra Leone's angle bisector theorem, L62.)
**Answer:** 6:9, that is 2:3 — the bisector divides the opposite side in the ratio of the neighbouring sides.

**8. (Level 3)** In Cambodia's worked incentre example, the bisector at B makes ∠ABI = 34°. What is the whole angle B of the triangle?
**Answer:** 68° — the bisector halves the angle, so ∠B = 2 × 34° (Lesson 16 §4, printed p. 210).

**9. (Level 3)** A triangle has area S = 24 cm². For one excircle, b + c − a = 8 cm. Find that excircle's radius using r_a = 2S/(b + c − a).
**Answer:** r_a = 48/8 = 6 cm (Kazakhstan, Geometry 9 §20).
