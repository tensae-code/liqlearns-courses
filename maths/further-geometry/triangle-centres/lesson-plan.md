# Lesson plan — The special lines and centres of a triangle

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 9 — Further Geometry · **Lesson:** The special lines and centres of a triangle
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 77 of 136, in Unit 9 — Further Geometry (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the four special lines of a triangle — medians, angle bisectors, perpendicular bisectors and altitudes — each family meeting at one centre (centroid, incentre, circumcentre, orthocentre), plus the mid-segment theorem, the median to the hypotenuse, and the excircles that sit outside the triangle.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on constructing triangles and on the circle work — the learner can already draw a triangle, bisect a segment and an angle, and knows what a circle through three points means. It needs nothing else.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Draw the four special lines of a triangle and name each one, the way Kuwait's Grade 9 Term 2 book builds them one lesson at a time (Unit 7, printed pp. 140–190) and Laos's Grade 7 book gathers them as the "notable lines" (Part 5, Lesson 33).
2. Use the mid-segment theorem — the segment joining two midpoints is parallel to the third side and half its length (Kuwait, 7-1, printed pp. 140–152; Yemen, Grade 8 Part 2, 7-2, printed p. 70).
3. Find the centroid and split a median 2:1 from the vertex (Kuwait, 7-6, printed pp. 183–190; Cambodia, Grade 8, Lesson 16 §1, printed p. 205; Iraq, Grade 9, printed p. 116).
4. Find the circumcentre as the meeting of the perpendicular bisectors, with OA = OB = OC (Kuwait, 7-3, printed pp. 161–167; Laos, Part 5, Lesson 33), and the incentre as the meeting of the angle bisectors — taught corrected: Kuwait's Grade 10 Term 2 book, printed p. 23, prints this point's name wrong (see the lesson).
5. Find the orthocentre as the meeting of the altitudes (Kuwait, 7-5, printed pp. 178–182; Yemen, 7-6, printed p. 83; Cambodia, Lesson 16 §3, printed p. 208).
6. Use the median to the hypotenuse of a right triangle — it equals half the hypotenuse (Kuwait, 7-2, printed pp. 153–160; Egypt, Grade 8 Term 1, 3-4, printed pp. 83–88; Yemen, 7-3, printed p. 74) — and say what an excircle is and where its centre sits (Kazakhstan, Geometry 8 §1.7; Geometry 9 §20).

## What you need
Paper, a pencil, a ruler, compasses and a protractor. A large paper triangle and a pin for the balancing activity. Scissors help.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes up incentre and circumcentre → go by the lines, not the names: bisectors of ANGLES meet at the incentre (it is INside, equal distance from the SIDES); perpendicular bisectors of SIDES meet at the circumcentre (equal distance from the VERTICES, OA = OB = OC). One printed book swaps this label — the lesson teaches it corrected.
- Splits the median the wrong way → the long part is at the VERTEX: the centroid is twice as far from the vertex as from the side, 2:1.
- Draws an altitude outside the triangle and thinks it is wrong → in an obtuse triangle the altitudes' lines must be extended; the orthocentre then sits outside, and that is correct.
- Cannot see the mid-segment → mark the two midpoints first, then join them; measure: parallel to the third side and half as long, every time.
- This lesson's next door: Loci (same stage) — every centre here is found again there as the meeting of two loci.
