# Sine and cosine rules — true or false

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — The Sine Rule · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — The Cosine Rule · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Area and Choosing the Rule.

Say true or false, and why in one line.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** The sine rule works on any triangle, not only right-angled ones. — **True.** It needs no right angle.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** In the sine rule, side b is matched with sin A. — **False.** Side b goes with sin B, the angle across from it.

**3. (Sub lesson 1)** a / sin A = b / sin B = c / sin C, all three equal. — **True.** That is the sine rule.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** The cosine rule needs the angle between the two known sides. — **True.** The formula uses the included angle.

**5. (Sub lesson 2)** Pythagoras is a special case of the sine rule. — **False.** Pythagoras is the cosine rule with a 90° angle (cos 90° = 0).

**6. (Sub lesson 2)** If a² > b² + c², the angle at A is obtuse. — **True.** Comparing a² with b² + c² decides sharp, right or wide.

**7. (Sub lesson 3)** The area formula ½ab sin C needs the angle between sides a and b. — **True.** C is the included angle.

**8. (Sub lesson 3)** The sine rule can never give two answers for an angle. — **False.** That is the ambiguous case: a sharp angle and its supplement share a sine.
