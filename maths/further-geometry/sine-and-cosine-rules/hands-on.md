# Hands-on — Sine and cosine rules

## The naming game
Draw five triangles of different shapes. Label every side a,
b, c and every angle A, B, C — side across from angle, every
time. A partner checks: any letter in the wrong place means
the whole triangle is re-labelled.

## The river problem
Mark two points A and B on the edge of a field (or a room),
a known distance apart. Pick a tree or a post C across the
field. Measure the angles at A and B to C. Use the sine rule
to find the distances AC and BC without crossing the field.
That is Kazakhstan's river-width problem, in your field.

## The 3-4-5 check
Take sides 5, 7, 8. Use the cosine rule to find the angle
across from the 7. You should get 60° — a clean answer that
tells you the rule is working. Then change one side by 1 and
watch the angle move.

## Bearings walk
Walk a two-leg path in a field: walk 20 steps north, turn to
a bearing of 120°, walk 15 steps. Use the cosine rule to work
out how far you are from where you started, and the sine rule
to find the bearing back home. That is Jordan's and Libya's
bearings work, on foot.
