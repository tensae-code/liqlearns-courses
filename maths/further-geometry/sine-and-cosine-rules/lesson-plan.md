# Lesson plan — Sine and cosine rules

**Course:** Maths · **Stage:** 9, Further Geometry · **Lesson:** Sine and cosine rules
**Place in the curriculum:** Stage 9, the fourth lesson of the
stage (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** solving triangles that
are not right-angled — the sine rule, the cosine rule, the
area formula ½ab·sin C, and choosing between the rules.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on right-triangle
trigonometry (Stage 9, third lesson) and on Pythagoras
(Stage 8). It needs nothing else.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: right-triangle trigonometry — the sine
  and cosine of an angle are used here, not re-taught.
  Recommended, never forced.
- The naming rule (side a across from angle A) is taught
  inside this lesson, not assumed.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Write the sine rule and the cosine rule, and say when each
   one is used.
2. Find a missing side or angle of any triangle with the right
   rule.
3. Find the area of a triangle from two sides and the angle
   between them.
4. Spot the ambiguous case and check both answers.

## What you need
A ruler, a protractor, a calculator.

1. **Do it first** — the learner draws a non-right triangle and
   finds the old ratios do not fit (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the naming rule; the sine rule; the
   ambiguous case; the cosine rule; the area formula; solving
   a whole triangle (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE at the sine-rule part: a picture of a triangle

## If the learner is stuck
- Picks the wrong rule → count what you know: side + angle
  across from it means sine rule; two sides + the angle
  between them, or three sides, means cosine rule.
- Forgets the naming → side a sits across from angle A, every
  time, in every book.
- Gets two answers from the sine rule → that is the ambiguous
  case, not a mistake: check both against the triangle.
- This lesson's next door: circle theorems (the 2R in the sine
  rule is a circle's radius — the next lesson opens it).
