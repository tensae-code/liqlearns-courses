# Hands-on — Circle theorems

## The doubling angle
Draw a circle with compasses. Pick an arc and measure the
central angle and the inscribed angle on it. Move the point
on the circle to three new places and measure again. The
inscribed angle never changes, and the central angle stays
double — three measurements prove it better than one.

## The semicircle right angle
Draw a circle and a diameter. Pick five different points on
the circle and join each to both ends of the diameter. Measure
all five angles: every one is 90°. A set square will confirm
each one without a protractor.

## The cyclic quadrilateral
Draw a circle and mark four points on it. Join them into a
quadrilateral and measure opposite angles. Add each pair:
both pairs add to 180°. Then move one point off the circle
and measure again — the sums break, which is how you know
the circle was doing the work.

## The chord product
Draw two chords crossing inside a circle. Measure the four
parts and multiply the pairs: the products match. Change the
chords, measure again. Then draw a tangent and a secant from
a point outside, measure, and check tangent² against
whole secant × outside part.
