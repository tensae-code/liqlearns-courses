# Lesson plan — Circle theorems

**Course:** Maths · **Stage:** 9, Further Geometry · **Lesson:** Circle theorems
**Place in the curriculum:** Stage 9, the fifth lesson of the
stage (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the angles, chords and
tangents of a circle — the inscribed-angle theorem, the angle
in a semicircle, the tangent–chord angle, the chord and
secant products, and cyclic quadrilaterals.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Stage 7 circles
lesson (radius, diameter, circumference, area) and on
similarity (Stage 9, first lesson: the segment products are
proved from similar triangles). It needs nothing else.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: circles (Stage 7), similarity, and the
  sine and cosine rules (the 2R in the sine rule is a circle's
  radius). Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Use the inscribed-angle theorem: the angle at the centre
   is twice the angle at the circumference on the same arc.
2. Use the angle in a semicircle (90°) and the tangent–chord
   angle.
3. Use the chord, secant and tangent–secant products.
4. Use the cyclic quadrilateral rule: opposite angles add to
   180°.

## What you need
A pair of compasses, a ruler, a protractor, paper.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner draws a circle, measures a
   central angle and an inscribed angle on the same arc, and
   finds the doubling (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the inscribed-angle theorem and its
   three corollaries; chords and tangents; the segment
   products; cyclic quadrilaterals (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE at the inscribed-angle part: a picture of a
     circle with a central angle and an inscribed angle on the
     same arc, both marked → the student measures both and
     compares them.
   - ADD IMAGE at the semicircle part: a picture of a circle
     with a diameter and a point on the circle joined to both
     ends → the student measures the angle at the point.
   - ADD IMAGE at the chords part: a picture of two chords
     crossing inside a circle, the four parts labelled → the
     student writes the product rule on it.
3. **Say it to your companion** — draw a diameter, ask for the
   angle at the circle and the theorem (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the
     doubling angle and the cyclic quadrilateral in the
     super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served
   at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the four tasks at the end of
   lesson/lesson.md.

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- IMAGE — a central and an inscribed angle on one arc → the
  student measures both.
- IMAGE — a diameter with a point on the circle → the student
  measures the angle.
- IMAGE — two crossing chords → the student writes the product
  rule.
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later) → the student
  finds the doubling angle along with it.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes centre and circumference → the centre angle is always
  double; ask "where is the angle's corner — at the centre or
  on the circle?" first.
- Forgets the semicircle → a diameter makes a central angle of
  180°, so half of it is 90°, always.
- Mixes the segment products → inside the circle, chord ×
  chord; outside, whole secant × outside part; tangent
  squared is the tangent's own product.
- This lesson's next door: coordinates and graphs (Stage 10 —
  the circle gets an equation there in later books, but this
  lesson does not need it).
