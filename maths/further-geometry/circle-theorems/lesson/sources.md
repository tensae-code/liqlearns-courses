Yemen carries circle theorems whole in Grade 9 — chords, central and inscribed angles, the cyclic quadrilateral, tangents and the relative positions of two circles. Indonesia carries circle theorems whole in Grade 11 — arcs, tangents, chords and cyclic quadrilaterals in both the 2021 Merdeka and 2024 Revisi regular books, with the van Schooten and Pitot theorems in the 2021 book — plus analytic tangent-line equations in the Grade 12 Tingkat Lanjut student books (2022, and the 2025 Revisi edition read directly in the gap batch). Honest negative: Grade 9 teaches circle circumference, area, arc and sector but not circle theorems, tangents or chords — those come in Grade 11. Nicaragua touches this lesson only in part — the notable angles of the circumference in the Grade 8 module and the inscribed-angle theorem with the semi-inscribed, interior and exterior angles in Grade 9. No cyclic quadrilaterals, no tangent-length or power-of-a-point theorems, and no arc-length or sector-area formulas are taught as circle theorems. Ethiopia carries circle theorems in Grades 8 and 10 only — central and inscribed angles, the angle in a semicircle and intersecting chords in Grade 8, and Theorem 5.1 proved, chord symmetry, arcs, cyclic quadrilaterals, arc length and segment and sector area in Grade 10, with the Grade 10 guide's two-column proofs as teacher evidence. South Sudan touches this lesson only in part — construction only: inscribing and circumscribing circles of triangles (incentre by angle bisectors, circumcentre by perpendicular bisectors) in Primary 8. No circle theorems as such are taught — no angle at the centre, no angles in the same segment, no cyclic quadrilaterals, no tangent theorems — and the Primary 8 book's p. 48 claim that the radius is the segment from the incentre to where the bisector meets the side is corrected: the radius is the perpendicular distance from the incentre to a side. Iraq carries circle theorems in Grade 9 — arcs, chords, central angles and tangents, circumscribed and inscribed circles with the intersecting-chords theorem, and the inscribed angle, tangent angle and cyclic quadrilateral — with the angle in a semicircle (Thales) in Grade 11 Scientific. Bangladesh touches this lesson only in part — inscribed and central angles, the angle on a semicircle, cyclic quadrilaterals and secants and tangents in Grade 8. It teaches no alternate segment (tangent–chord) theorem, no intersecting-chords/secant/tangent–secant product rules, and no radius-through-a-chord's-midpoint rule, and Grade 9 teaches no circle theorems at all — those parts rest on the other countries. Nepal touches this lesson only in part — chord theorems in Grade 9 and the central-angle theorem (the angle at the centre is double the angle at the circumference, with angles in the same segment) in Grade 10; no cyclic-quadrilateral theorem and no tangent theorems are taught. Cambodia carries circle theorems only in part — the diameter–chord theorems and central and inscribed angles in Grades 8–9, and the tangent with a bare list of circle angle types in Grade 10; no circle theorem is stated or proved anywhere in the set, inscribed quadrilaterals and the angle in a semicircle are not pinned, and intersecting-chords and secant relations are not present. Kuwait carries circle theorems whole in Grade 10 Term 2 — the tangent theorems, chords and arcs, central and inscribed angles with cyclic quadrilaterals, and the intersecting-chords and tangent–secant relations, with the book's 'Circum Center' label for the incenter taught corrected.

China carries circle theorems whole in Grade 9 Volume 1 — the perpendicular-diameter theorem, the central- and inscribed-angle theorems, cyclic quadrilaterals and the tangent theorems.

Pakistan (Punjab) carries the circle theorems in Class 10's four full geometry units — chords, tangents, arcs and the angle in a segment — and proves the central ones again analytically in Class 12.

Syria's circle theorems are all in Grade 9 Geometry: inscribed and central angles with the half-angle theorem, equal chords and equal arcs, the tangent-chord angle, and the cyclic quadrilateral — opposite angles supplementary, proved with the reflex central angle — with both converses stated.
