# Circle theorems — the lesson

Course: Maths · Unit 9, Further Geometry · Lesson: Circle theorems
Built from: Kazakhstan, Jordan, Armenia, Greece, Vietnam, Egypt
— see lesson/sources.md for every part.

## Do it first
Draw a circle. Mark its centre O and two points A and B on
the circle. Join O to A and O to B: the angle AOB at the
centre is the **central angle** standing on the arc AB. Now
pick a third point C on the circle, on the far side from the
arc, and join C to A and C to B: the angle ACB at the circle
is the **inscribed angle** standing on the same arc AB.
Measure both angles with a protractor. The angle at the
centre is twice the angle at the circle. Draw C somewhere
else on the same arc and measure again — the inscribed angle
is the same every time. That is the first circle theorem,
and you have just found it yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Inscribed Angle

**The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the
circumference.** Kazakhstan's Grade 9 book proves the
inscribed-angle theorem for all three positions of the
centre, and Armenia's Grade 9 book teaches central and
inscribed angles in the same chapter: an inscribed angle
equals half its arc (or half the central angle on the same
arc). Greece's Lykeio Geometry book states it the same way
in its chapter on inscribed figures: the relation between an
inscribed angle and the corresponding central angle. Vietnam's
Grade 9 book teaches the inscribed angle as its own lesson.

Three things follow at once, and the books all teach them:

- **Angles in the same segment are equal.** Two inscribed
  angles standing on the same arc are the same angle — move
  C anywhere on the arc and the angle does not change.
- **The angle in a semicircle is a right angle.** If AB is a
  diameter, the central angle is 180°, so the inscribed
  angle is half of that: 90°. Kazakhstan's book states this
  as the corollary, and Armenia's book uses the
  diameter-subtended angle in its applications.
- **The angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle
  in the alternate segment.** Greece's book teaches the angle
  of a chord and a tangent, and Kazakhstan's other Grade 9
  book teaches the tangent–chord angle theorem: the angle
  between a tangent and a chord through the point of contact
  equals half the intercepted arc — the same size as the
  angle in the opposite segment.

## Sub lesson 3 — Cyclic Quadrilaterals and Segment Products

- **Intersecting chords:** if two chords cross inside the
  circle, the products of the parts are equal:
  AE × BE = CE × DE.
- **Two secants from an outside point:** the products are
  equal again: AP × BP = CP × DP.
- **Tangent and secant:** the tangent squared equals the
  whole secant times its outside part: KP² = BP × AP.

**Cyclic quadrilaterals — four corners on one circle.**
Kazakhstan's Grade 9 book proves the theorem and its
converse as separate theorems, and Vietnam's Grade 9 book
teaches the cyclic quadrilateral as its own lesson: in a
cyclic quadrilateral, **opposite angles add to 180°**.
And the converse is true too: if a quadrilateral's opposite
angles add to 180°, a circle can be drawn through all four
corners. Armenia's book teaches the circumscribed circle of
a quadrilateral in the same chapter. Jordan's Grade 10 book
teaches cyclic quadrilaterals inside its angles-in-a-circle
lesson. Kazakhstan's book adds the matching fact for a
quadrilateral with an incircle: in a circumscribed
quadrilateral, the sums of the opposite sides are equal.

> **Tip — from us, not from the books.** When a problem
> gives you a circle and an angle, first ask: is this angle
> at the centre or at the circle? Everything in this lesson
> hangs on that one question — the centre angle is always
> double.

## Say it to your companion
Draw a circle with a diameter AB and a point C on the circle.
Ask your companion: "What is angle ACB — and which theorem
tells you?" Then swap: they draw two chords crossing inside
a circle, and you write the product rule that links the four
parts.

## Do It Yourself
1. A central angle is 80°. What is the inscribed angle on
   the same arc? (Answer: 40°.)
2. AB is a diameter of a circle and C is a point on the
   circle. Angle ACB is what? (Answer: 90° — the angle in
   a semicircle.)
3. A quadrilateral ABCD has its corners on a circle. Angle A
   is 70°. What is angle C? (Answer: 110°, because opposite
   angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to 180°.)
4. Two chords cross inside a circle. One is cut into parts
   3 cm and 8 cm; the other is cut into parts 4 cm and x cm.
   Find x. (Answer: 3 × 8 = 4 × x, so x = 6 cm.)
