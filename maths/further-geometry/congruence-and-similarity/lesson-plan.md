# Lesson plan — Congruence and similarity

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** 9, Further Geometry · **Lesson:** Congruence and similarity
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 9, the first lesson of the
unit (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** telling whether two shapes
are the same shape and size (congruent) or the same shape in a
different size (similar), proving it with the triangle checks,
and using the scale factor — including what it does to area
(k²) and volume (k³).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on shapes and angles
(Unit 7: triangles, angles, measuring) and on ratio and
proportion (Unit 4). It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Congruent Shapes** — congruent means an exact fit, and the SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS and RHS checks for triangles. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Similar Shapes** — similar means the same shape, a different size — the AA, SSS and SAS checks and the scale factor. (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **The Scale Factor on Area and Volume** — what similarity does to area and volume: k for lengths, k² for areas and k³ for volumes. (Was depth Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson (no hardness ranking is written in this lesson's question files).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the Unit 7 shapes and angles lessons
  (triangles and their angles), and ratio and proportion.
  Recommended, never forced.
- Pythagoras is NOT needed here — Libya teaches this whole
  lesson without it.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say whether two shapes are congruent, similar, or neither,
   and say why.
2. Use SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS and RHS to prove triangles
   congruent, and AA, SSS and SAS to prove triangles similar.
3. Find a missing length in a similar figure using the scale
   factor.
4. Use k² for areas and k³ for volumes of similar shapes, and
   measure a tall thing from its shadow with similar triangles.

## What you need
Paper, scissors, a ruler, a pencil. A stick and a sunny day
for the shadow work.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner cuts two matching triangles
   and one double-size triangle (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — congruent and the five checks;
   similar and the three checks; scale factor; area and
   volume rules (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE at the congruent part: a picture of two paper
     triangles laid exactly on top of each other, one turned
     over → the student checks every side and corner matches.
   - ADD GIF at the similar part: a picture of a small triangle
     growing into a big one, every side stretching by the same
     factor, angles staying put → the student watches the
     angles not change.
   - ADD IMAGE at the shadow part: a picture of a stick and its
     shadow beside a tree and its shadow → the student marks
     the two similar triangles on it.
3. **Say it to your companion** — show two triangles and ask
   for the verdict and the check (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the
     cut-out test and the shadow measure in the super-simple
     voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served
   at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the four tasks at the end of
   lesson/lesson.md.

## If the learner is stuck
- Matches the wrong corners → write the matching pairs in
  order first (A matches A′, B matches B′, C matches C′),
  then check sides and angles.
- Thinks similar means congruent → similar keeps the shape
  and the angles, but the size changes; congruent changes
  nothing at all.
- Uses k for area → lengths take k, areas take k², volumes
  take k³; say it as a chant until it sticks.
- This lesson's next door: transformations (the moves that
  make congruent copies and similar enlargements).
