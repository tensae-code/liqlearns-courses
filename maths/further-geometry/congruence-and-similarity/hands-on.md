# Hands-on — Congruence and similarity

## The cut-out test
Cut two triangles from paper so they match exactly. Flip one,
turn one, slide one. Every fit is a congruence check you can
feel. Then cut a third triangle with every side doubled. It
looks the same and will not fit — that feeling is similarity.

## The matching game
Draw six triangles on cards: two congruent pairs and two
similar pairs mixed in. Shuffle them. A partner picks two
cards and must name the verdict — congruent, similar, or
neither — and the check (SSS, SAS, ASA, AA) that proves it.
Wrong check, the cards go back in.

## Shadow measuring
On a sunny day, stand a stick of known height upright and
measure its shadow. Measure a tree's shadow at the same
moment. Work out the tree's height from the similar
triangles, the way Libya's scout does. Then measure the tree
another way (counting floors, asking someone) and compare.

## The k, k², k³ boxes
Build a small cube from paper, 2 cm on a side. Build a second
one 4 cm on a side — the scale factor is 2. Count how many
small cubes fit inside the big one: 8. That is k³, in your
hands. Paint the faces: the big cube's faces take 4 times the
paint of the small one's — that is k².
