# Lesson plan — Right-triangle trigonometry

**Course:** Maths · **Stage:** 9, Further Geometry · **Lesson:** Right-triangle trigonometry
**Place in the curriculum:** Stage 9, the third lesson of the
stage (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** sine, cosine and tangent
in a right triangle — naming the sides, using the three ratios
to find missing sides and angles, the special angles, and
angles of elevation and depression.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Pythagoras (Stage 8)
and on similarity (Stage 9, first lesson: the ratios are fixed
because the triangles are similar). It needs nothing else.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Pythagoras, and congruence and
  similarity. Recommended, never forced.
- A calculator (or the printed tables Sierra Leone's book
  teaches) is needed from Level 2 on.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the hypotenuse, opposite and adjacent for any sharp
   angle of a right triangle.
2. Write sin, cos and tan as ratios and use them to find a
   missing side.
3. Use the inverse ratios to find a missing angle.
4. Use sin, cos and tan of 30°, 45° and 60° from memory, and
   solve an elevation or depression problem.

## What you need
A ruler, a protractor, a calculator (or printed trig tables),
a long tape for the outdoor work.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner names the three sides of a
   right triangle and sees the ratios stay the same as the
   triangle grows (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the three ratios; finding a side;
   finding an angle; the special-angle table; elevation and
   depression (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE at the naming part: a picture of a right
     triangle with hypotenuse, opposite and adjacent labelled
     for a marked angle → the student points at each side and
     names it.
   - ADD IMAGE at the special-angles part: a picture of the
     30°–60°–90° and 45°–45°–90° triangles with their side
     ratios marked → the student reads the table off the
     triangles.
   - ADD IMAGE at the applications part: a picture of a person
     looking up at the top of a building, the angle of
     elevation marked → the student marks the right triangle
     on it.
3. **Say it to your companion** — draw a 30° triangle, ask for
   the opposite side and the ratio used (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the
     three ratios and the ladder problem in the super-simple
     voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served
   at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the four tasks at the end of
   lesson/lesson.md.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes opposite and adjacent → the opposite is across from
  the angle; the adjacent touches the angle. Point, then name.
- Picks the wrong ratio → write which side you know and which
  side you want first; the two sides pick the ratio.
- Forgets sin 30° → it is 1/2: the shortest side of a
  30°–60°–90° triangle is half the hypotenuse.
- This lesson's next door: sine and cosine rules (the same
  ideas for triangles that are not right-angled).
