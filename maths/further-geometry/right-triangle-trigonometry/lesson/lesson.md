# Right-triangle trigonometry — the lesson

Course: Maths · Stage 9, Further Geometry · Lesson: Right-triangle trigonometry
Built from: Jordan, Egypt, Sierra Leone, Kazakhstan, Vietnam,
Armenia, Mauritania, Greece — see lesson/sources.md for every part.

## Do it first
Draw a right-angled triangle. Pick one of the two sharp
corners and call its angle θ (say "theta"). The side across
from the right angle is the **hypotenuse** — always the longest
side. The side across from your angle θ is the **opposite**.
The side touching your angle θ (that is not the hypotenuse) is
the **adjacent**. Say the three names out loud, pointing at
each side. Now draw a bigger triangle with the same angle θ.
The sides are longer — but the ratios between them are the
same. That sameness is the whole lesson.

## Then the explanation
**Three ratios, one for each pair of sides.** Jordan's Grade 9
book introduces trigonometry from similar right triangles:
because all right triangles with the same sharp angle are
similar, the ratios of their sides are fixed by the angle
alone. The three ratios are named sine, cosine and tangent,
and Sierra Leone's JSS 3 book states them exactly as the books
everywhere do:

- **sin θ = opposite / hypotenuse**
- **cos θ = adjacent / hypotenuse**
- **tan θ = opposite / adjacent**

Say them as SOH-CAH-TOA, the way Sierra Leone's book teaches
it: Sine is Opposite over Hypotenuse, Cosine is Adjacent over
Hypotenuse, Tangent is Opposite over Adjacent. Egypt's
Preparatory 3 book adds the link between them: tan θ =
sin θ / cos θ. And there is one identity Jordan's Grade 9
book names: the Pythagorean identity, sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 —
the squares of sine and cosine always add to 1, for any angle.

**Finding a missing side.** Pick the ratio that uses the side
you know and the side you want. If you know the hypotenuse
(10 cm) and the angle (30°), and you want the opposite side,
use sine: opposite = 10 × sin 30° = 10 × 0.5 = 5 cm. If you
know the adjacent and want the hypotenuse, use cosine. If you
know the opposite and the adjacent, use tangent. Sierra
Leone's book spends a whole lesson on choosing the ratio
yourself — that choice is the skill, not the button-pressing.

**Finding a missing angle.** If you know two sides, work the
ratio backwards. If opposite = 3 and hypotenuse = 5, then
sin θ = 3/5 = 0.6, so θ = sin⁻¹(0.6) ≈ 37°. Egypt's Secondary
1 book teaches the inverse notation (sin⁻¹ and the like) and
the calculator key sequences for it.

**Angles you should know by heart.** Egypt's Preparatory 3
book teaches the table for 30°, 45° and 60°, worked out from
the 30°–60°–90° and 45°–45°–90° triangles:

| angle | sin | cos | tan |
|---|---|---|---|
| 30° | 1/2 | √3/2 | 1/√3 |
| 45° | 1/√2 | 1/√2 | 1 |
| 60° | √3/2 | 1/2 | √3 |

Sierra Leone's book teaches the same common angles from a
table, and then teaches how to read the printed sine, cosine
and tangent tables for other angles (its Appendices I–III),
including decimal angles like 10.52°. A calculator does the
same job today; the tables are the reason the books print
values to four figures.

**Using it outside.** Jordan's Grade 9 book ends the topic
with applications: angles of **elevation** (looking up) and
**depression** (looking down), and finding unknown
lengths and heights. A ladder against a wall, a flagpole's
shadow, the height of a building from the ground — all of
them are one right triangle, one known side, one known angle.
Vietnam's Grade 9 book teaches the same side–angle relations
for right triangles, and Armenia's Grade 9 book uses them to
compute areas of triangles and parallelograms. Mauritania's
4AS book teaches trigonometry as its own chapter, and
Greece's Grade 9 book carries trigonometry to 180°.

> **Tip — from us, not from the books.** Before you touch the
> calculator, write down which ratio you need and why. The
> commonest mistake is using sine when the side you want is
> the adjacent — the name of the side tells you the ratio.

## Say it to your companion
Draw a right triangle with a 30° angle and a hypotenuse of
10 cm. Ask your companion: "What is the opposite side — and
which ratio did you use?" Then swap: they draw one, and you
answer with the ratio named first.

## Do It Yourself
1. A right triangle has hypotenuse 13 cm and angle 30° at one
   corner. Find the side opposite the 30° angle.
   (Answer: 13 × sin 30° = 13 × 0.5 = 6.5 cm.)
2. A ladder 5 m long leans against a wall and makes 60° with
   the ground. How high up the wall does it reach?
   (Answer: 5 × sin 60° = 5 × √3/2 ≈ 4.33 m.)
3. A right triangle has opposite 4 and adjacent 3 for angle θ.
   Find tan θ, then find θ. (Answer: tan θ = 4/3 ≈ 1.333,
   θ ≈ 53.1°.)
4. Stand a known distance from a tree (say 20 m). Measure the
   angle of elevation to its top (say 35°). Work out the tree's
   height above your eye level. (Answer: 20 × tan 35° ≈
   20 × 0.700 = 14.0 m.)
