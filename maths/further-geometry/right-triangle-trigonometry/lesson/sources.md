**Acceptance count: taught in 13 countries (Jordan, Egypt, Sierra
Leone, Kazakhstan, Vietnam, Armenia, Mauritania, Greece, Laos, Yemen, Indonesia, Nicaragua, Ethiopia).**
Jordan carries the cleanest introduction — ratios from similar
right triangles, then elevation and depression. Egypt carries
the special-angle table and tan = sin / cos in Preparatory 3,
and the inverse notation in Secondary 1. Sierra Leone carries
the fullest practice run — naming the sides, the three ratios
one by one, SOHCAHTOA, and the printed tables. Kazakhstan
carries right-triangle solving in Grade 9 Geometry. Vietnam
carries right-triangle trigonometry in Grade 9. Armenia
carries sine, cosine and tangent with area applications in
Grade 9 Geometry. Mauritania carries a trigonometry chapter in
4AS. Greece carries trigonometry to 180° in Grade 9. Honest
negatives: Libya Grades 5–9 teach no trigonometry at all, and
South Africa's Grades 7–9 books never teach trigonometry —
neither country is claimed for this lesson. Sierra Leone's JSS
never teaches angles of elevation and depression by name; the
lesson takes that vocabulary from Jordan. Laos carries right-triangle trigonometry whole in Grade 9 — sin, cos and tan with the special-angle table, the complementary-angle identity and the Pythagorean identity. Yemen carries right-triangle trigonometry from the side opposite 30° in Grade 8, through trig ratios for acute angles and for 30°, 45° and 60°, to solving the right triangle in Grade 10. Indonesia carries right-triangle ratios only, in both Grade 10 editions — tan first with the special angles 0°/30°/45°/60°/90° in the 2021 Merdeka book, and ratios with heights, distances and the clinometer project in the 2023 Revisi book; neither edition teaches sine/cosine rules or trig functions or graphs. Nicaragua carries right-triangle trigonometry whole in Grade 10 — the six ratios from similar right triangles, identities, special angles, functions of any angle with graphs and inverses, and solving right triangles with angles of elevation in the student book, with the module's shorter parallel (ratios, identities, sum/difference and double/half-angle formulas, easy right-triangle problems, no trig graphs and no inverse trig functions). Book correction carried here: the student book's p. 141 exercise "y = 2 sen⁻¹(x + 4)" is impossible as printed — arcsine needs an argument in [−1, 1] — and is almost certainly a misprint for y = 2 sen⁻¹ x + 4; the corrected form is taught here. Ethiopia carries right-triangle trigonometry whole — sine, cosine and tangent as ratios with exact values for 30°, 45° and 60° and the printed trig table in Grade 9, and trigonometric functions for any angle with reciprocal functions, identities, graphs and equations in Grade 10. Only sin/cos/tan in Grade 9; no secant/cosecant/cotangent, no identities and no graphs there.
