# Hands-on — Right-triangle trigonometry

## The growing triangle
Draw right triangles with a 30° angle, one small, one big.
Measure the opposite and the hypotenuse of each. Divide
opposite by hypotenuse both times: both give about 0.5. The
ratio belongs to the angle, not to the size — that is why
trigonometry works.

## The ladder
Lean a broom or a ladder against a wall. Measure its length
and the angle it makes with the ground (a protractor and a
piece of string with a weight will do). Work out how high it
reaches with sine, then measure the height and compare.

## The clinometer
Make a simple clinometer: a protractor, a straw along its
straight edge, a string with a small weight hanging from the
centre. Look through the straw at the top of a tree, read the
angle, measure how far you stand from the tree, and work out
its height with tangent. That is Jordan's angle of elevation,
in your hands.

## The table race
With a partner, race to write sin, cos and tan of 30°, 45°
and 60° from memory. The loser checks the winner's table
against the two special triangles. Play until both of you
can write all nine values without looking.
