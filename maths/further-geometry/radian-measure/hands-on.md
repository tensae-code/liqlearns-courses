# Hands-on — Radian measure

The lesson measured angles with string, the way the radian is defined.

## The string radian
Take a round plate or lid. Measure its radius with a piece of string (centre to rim) and cut the string there. Lay that string along the rim and mark where it ends; the angle at the centre between the two marks is 1 radian. Keep laying: count how many radius-strings fit in a half turn (just over 3 — that is π) and in a whole turn (just over 6 — that is 2π).

## The three dials
Draw three circles. On the first, mark the quarter, half and full turn in degrees (90°, 180°, 360°). On the second, mark the same turns in radians (π/2, π, 2π). On the third, mark them in gradians (100, 200, 400) — the centesimal system Nicaragua's module and Nepal's Optional book teach. Read each turn aloud in all three systems.

## DMS on the clock
A clock's minute hand moves 360° in 60 minutes — 6° a minute, and 0.5° a second-hand minute step is not the point: time the hand for 2 minutes 30 seconds of dial time and work out its turn in degrees, then in DMS and in radians. Then do Pakistan's conversion both ways on paper: 45.36° = 45°21′36″ (Class 10, Unit 7, printed pp. 145–152).

## Arc walk
Chalk a circle of radius 2 m on the ground. Walk an arc of 1 radian (2 m of string along the chalk), then an arc of π/2 (about 3.14 m). Measure each arc with the string and check s = rθ before you trust it.
