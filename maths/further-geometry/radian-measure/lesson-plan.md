# Lesson plan — Radian measure

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 9 — Further Geometry · **Lesson:** Radian measure
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 71 of 136, Unit 9 — Further Geometry, after right-triangle trigonometry and before trigonometric identities (Level 72) (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** measuring angles three ways — degrees with minutes and seconds (DMS), radians, and gradians — changing between them, and using s = rθ to find an arc length.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on right-triangle trigonometry (Stage 9) — the learner can already name sine, cosine and tangent in a right triangle — and on circles (circumference 2πr). It needs nothing else.

2. **Radians and Gradians** — the radian defined by the arc, 180° = π to convert both ways, and the third system, gradians. (Was depth Level 2.)

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Right-triangle trigonometry (Stage 9) — radians measure the same angles whose ratios you already know. Recommended, never forced.
- Recommended first: Circles (Stage 7) — the radian is defined by an arc of a circle, and s = rθ grows out of the circumference. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a degree, a minute and a second are, and change a DMS angle to decimal degrees and back (Pakistan, Class 10, Unit 7, printed pp. 145–152; Bangladesh, Class Nine, Ch. 7, printed pp. 158–160).
2. Say what a radian is — the angle whose arc equals the radius — and use 180° = π rad to change between degrees and radians both ways (Bangladesh, Class Nine, Ch. 7 §11, printed pp. 175–177; Laos, Grade 10, Ch. IX L22, printed pp. 233–250).
3. Use the centesimal (gradian) system: 400 gradians in a full turn, 100 gradians in a right angle (Nicaragua, Grade 10 module, Unit IV, printed pp. 130–133; Nepal, Optional Mathematics Grade 9, §2.1, printed pp. 89–145).
4. Find coterminal angles and say which quadrant an angle lies in, for angles past 360° and for negative angles (Bangladesh, Class Nine, Ch. 7, printed pp. 162–163; Laos, Grade 10, Ch. IX L22, printed pp. 233–250).
5. Use s = rθ (θ in radians) for arc length, and A = ½r²θ for a sector's area (Bangladesh, Class Nine, Ch. 7 §11.1, printed pp. 175–177; Pakistan, Class 10, Unit 7, printed pp. 145–152).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A piece of string and a round plate or lid (for the radian). A protractor. A calculator for the last part.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner wraps a string once round a plate, marks one radius length along the rim, and sees the angle it makes at the centre (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — degrees, minutes and seconds; the radian; changing between degrees and radians; gradians; directed and coterminal angles; arc length s = rθ and sector area (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the radian part: a picture of a string being laid along a circle's rim, one radius at a time, the centre angle growing mark by mark — 1 rad, 2 rad, 3 rad — until just past 3 marks make a half turn → the student counts the radius lengths aloud and says how many radians make a half turn.
   - ADD IMAGE at the DMS part: a picture of one degree split into a zoomed wedge showing 60 minutes, and one minute split again into 60 seconds → the student reads 1° = 60′ and 1′ = 60″ off the picture and converts 2°30′ aloud.
   - ADD IMAGE at the systems table: a picture of three dials side by side — a 360° dial, a 2π radian dial and a 400-gradian dial — each with the quarter turn marked → the student reads the same quarter turn in all three systems: 90°, π/2, 100 gradians.
3. **Say it to your companion** — change angles for your companion in both directions and answer their arc-length question in a whole sentence (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the string, the radian and s = rθ in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — convert five angles each way, find two coterminal angles, and work two arc lengths (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Converts the wrong way → remember the anchor: 180° = π. Degrees to radians multiply by π/180; radians to degrees multiply by 180/π. Check against the anchor every time.
- Uses s = rθ with θ in degrees → s = rθ only works in radians. Change the angle to radians first, the way Bangladesh's door problem does (Class Nine, Ch. 7, printed pp. 175–177).
- Loses minutes and seconds → 1° = 60′ and 1′ = 60″, so 1° = 3600″ — the same ladder Nicaragua's module prints (Unit IV, printed pp. 130–133).
- This lesson's next door: Trigonometric identities (Level 72, same stage) — the formulas there are written in the angles this lesson measures.
