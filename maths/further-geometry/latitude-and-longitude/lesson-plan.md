A lesson that teaches the earth as a sphere with a grid drawn
on it — latitude and longitude — and how to turn angle
differences on that grid into real distances on the earth's
surface. Dominica's Grade 11 course book (Toolsie,
*Mathematics: A Complete Course with CXC Questions and SBA
Guidelines, Volume 2*) teaches it as the closing section of
its Trigonometry 4 chapter — "The earth: latitude and
longitude, and distances on the earth's surface", printed
p. 1127. The course already reads latitude and longitude on
maps inside scale-drawing-and-maps (a scale lesson), and
meets a latitude-circle exercise inside geometry-in-space,
but no lesson taught the earth grid and its distances as
their own thing; the expansion read flagged it NEW LESSON
NEEDED and this lesson is built from that flag. Prerequisite
(soft hint only — every lesson stands alone): circles (arc
length) and right-triangle-trigonometry (cosine). A learner
who opens this lesson directly still gets the whole thing:
the grid is built here from the sphere up, and every distance
is worked from the circumference.
