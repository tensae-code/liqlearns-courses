# Hands-on — Latitude and Longitude

## Activity 1 — Draw the grid (10 minutes)

Take a ball (or an orange) and a marker. Draw the equator.
Mark the two poles and draw two meridians at right angles
to each other through them. Draw parallels at about 30°
and 60° in each hemisphere. Put a dot somewhere and read
its position aloud as "latitude … N/S, longitude … E/W"
from your prime meridian. This is Sub lesson 1 done with
your own hands.

## Activity 2 — String distances (15 minutes)

With the same ball and a piece of string, measure the
circumference round the equator. Then measure the string
length for a quarter of a meridian (equator to pole).
Check the ratio: the meridian quarter should be a quarter
of the equator's circumference. Now stretch the string
along your 60° parallel and compare it with the equator's
circumference — it should come out near half. This is Sub
lessons 2 and 3 measured, not just calculated.

## Activity 3 — The shrinking degree (15 minutes)

Make a table. For latitudes 0°, 30°, 45°, 60° and 90°,
write cos φ beside each, then the parallel's circumference
(40,192 × cos φ km), then the surface length of 1° of
longitude on that parallel (circumference ÷ 360). Finish
with 1° of latitude — the same 112 km on every row. Read
the table aloud and say, in your own words, why the two
degrees behave differently. (cos 30° ≈ 0.87 and cos 45° ≈
0.71 are enough.)
