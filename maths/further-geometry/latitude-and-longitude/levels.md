# Latitude and Longitude — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, expressed as its SUB
LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure — README, "Units, Lessons and
Sub Lessons"). One lesson, one level (Level 169). The lesson
was built new in the expansion era (Dominica expansion merge,
2026-11-11), so there are no old depth layers to map: the sub
lessons below are its depths as built, cut at the founding
section's own shape — the grid first (latitude and longitude
defined on the sphere), then distances along great circles,
then distances along the parallels, whose circles shrink by
cosine of the latitude. The questions live in the type files;
this file is the by-sub-lesson view.

Pass marks: the founding section names its topic (the earth:
latitude and longitude, and distances on the earth's surface)
and states no pass mark, so no mark is invented — each sub
lesson's pass mark is stated as the observable thing the
learner can do.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Earth's Grid: Latitude and Longitude
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: name the poles, the equator, meridians and parallels; say what latitude and longitude each measure and from where; and give a place's position as a latitude and a longitude with their N/S and E/W letters.

## Sub lesson 2 — Distances along Great Circles
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: take the earth's circumference from its radius (40,192 km for R = 6,400 km, π = 3.14) and find a distance along a meridian or along the equator as the angle difference over 360 of that circumference.

## Sub lesson 3 — Distances along Parallels of Latitude
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: find a parallel's radius as R cos φ and its circumference, and find an east–west distance along a parallel as the longitude difference over 360 of that smaller circumference — including why a degree of longitude shrinks towards the poles while a degree of latitude does not.

## What "maxed" means here

The learner handles the earth the way Dominica's Grade 11
course book teaches it in its Trigonometry 4 chapter: the
grid of latitude and longitude laid on the sphere, distances
along great circles from the angle over 360 of the whole
circumference, and distances along parallels from the same
fraction of the parallel's own, cosine-shrunk circumference
(*Mathematics: A Complete Course with CXC Questions and SBA
Guidelines, Volume 2*, Ch. 23, printed p. 1127).
