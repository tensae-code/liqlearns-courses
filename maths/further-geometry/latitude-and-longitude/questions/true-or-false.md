# True or false — Latitude and Longitude

Sub-lesson markers: every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to — Sub lesson 1 — The Earth's Grid: Latitude and Longitude · Sub lesson 2 — Distances along Great Circles · Sub lesson 3 — Distances along Parallels of Latitude. This lesson was built new with its sub lessons (Dominica expansion merge, 2026-11-11), so its markers are original — there are no older depth layers to map from.

1. (Sub lesson 1) Every meridian passes through both poles.
   **Answer: True.** Item constructed for this lesson from its founding teaching. Dominica, *Mathematics: A Complete Course with CXC Questions and SBA Guidelines, Volume 2* (student book), Grade 11, Ch. 23 — Trigonometry 4, printed p. 1127.

2. (Sub lesson 1) Every parallel of latitude is a great circle.
   **Answer: False** — only the equator is a great circle; the other parallels are smaller circles. Item constructed for this lesson from its founding teaching. Dominica, *Mathematics: A Complete Course with CXC Questions and SBA Guidelines, Volume 2* (student book), Grade 11, Ch. 23 — Trigonometry 4, printed p. 1127.

3. (Sub lesson 2) The distance between two places on the same meridian depends only on their difference of latitude.
   **Answer: True.** Item constructed for this lesson from its founding teaching. Dominica, *Mathematics: A Complete Course with CXC Questions and SBA Guidelines, Volume 2* (student book), Grade 11, Ch. 23 — Trigonometry 4, printed p. 1127.

4. (Sub lesson 2) One degree of latitude is about 112 km of surface distance.
   **Answer: True** — 40,192 ÷ 360 ≈ 112. Item constructed for this lesson from its founding teaching. Dominica, *Mathematics: A Complete Course with CXC Questions and SBA Guidelines, Volume 2* (student book), Grade 11, Ch. 23 — Trigonometry 4, printed p. 1127.

5. (Sub lesson 3) A degree of longitude covers the same surface distance at every latitude.
   **Answer: False** — it shrinks with cos φ towards the poles. Item constructed for this lesson from its founding teaching. Dominica, *Mathematics: A Complete Course with CXC Questions and SBA Guidelines, Volume 2* (student book), Grade 11, Ch. 23 — Trigonometry 4, printed p. 1127.

6. (Sub lesson 3) At latitude 60°, the parallel's circumference is half the equator's circumference.
   **Answer: True** — cos 60° = 0.5. Item constructed for this lesson from its founding teaching. Dominica, *Mathematics: A Complete Course with CXC Questions and SBA Guidelines, Volume 2* (student book), Grade 11, Ch. 23 — Trigonometry 4, printed p. 1127.
