# Video script — Latitude and Longitude

Super-simple voice. The tutor talks; a globe turns slowly on
screen, and each named line lights up as it is drawn.

---

This is the earth. A ball. It spins. The top end is the
North Pole. The bottom end is the South Pole.

Round the middle runs the widest circle: the equator.

Now the grid. Lines from pole to pole are meridians. One
special meridian runs through Greenwich. Lines round the
earth, flat like belts, are parallels.

Latitude tells you how far north or south of the equator.
Longitude tells you how far east or west of Greenwich.
Two angles, and every place on earth has its own pair.

How far is it from here to there? On the same meridian,
count the latitude difference. Ninety degrees is a quarter
of the whole way round — about ten thousand kilometres.

East and west is trickier. Away from the equator, the
circles shrink. At sixty degrees north, the circle is only
half as big. The cosine does the shrinking. Same angle,
shorter road.

Latitude from the equator. Longitude from Greenwich. And
the cosine shrinks the east–west circles. That is the
earth's grid.
