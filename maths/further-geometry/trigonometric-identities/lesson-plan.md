# Lesson plan — Trigonometric identities

**Course:** Maths · **Stage:** Stage 9 — Further Geometry · **Lesson:** Trigonometric identities
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 72 of 136, Stage 9 — Further Geometry, after radian measure (Level 71) and before trigonometric equations (Level 73) (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the identity chain — the basic identities, the addition formulas proved from cos(α − β), and the double, half and triple formulas that follow — and how to prove an identity.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on right-triangle trigonometry (Stage 9) and on radian measure (Level 71) — the learner knows the six ratios and can measure an angle either way. It needs nothing else.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Right-triangle trigonometry (Stage 9) — the identities are statements about the ratios you already know. Recommended, never forced.
- Recommended first: Radian measure (Level 71, same stage) — half-angle and allied-angle work is cleaner in radians. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Use the basic identities — sin²θ + cos²θ = 1, tan θ = sin θ/cos θ, and the reciprocal and Pythagorean partners — to find one ratio from another (Kuwait, Grade 10 Term 2, printed pp. 107–113; Nicaragua, Grade 10 student book, Unit 2, printed pp. 68–71; Egypt, Grade 10 Term 2, Unit 5 lesson 5-1, English contents printed p. 106; Iran, Mathematics (1), Grade 10, printed pp. 42–46; Kazakhstan, Soltan Algebra 9, §24, printed p. 181).
2. Follow and use the addition formulas, with cos(α − β) proved first and the rest deduced (Pakistan, Class 11, Unit 10, printed pp. 177–189; Laos, Grade 10, Ch. IX L22, printed pp. 233–250; Kazakhstan, Abylkasymova Algebra 9 Part 2, §24, printed p. 55; Egypt, Pure Mathematics Grade 11 Term 2, lesson 4-2, printed p. 121).
3. Use the double, half and triple formulas, and change sums to products and products to sums (Yemen, Grade 11 Science Part 2, Unit 9, printed pp. 91–104; China, Compulsory Volume 3 (B edition), §8.2, printed pp. 87–110; Iran, Mathematics (3), Grade 12, printed p. 43; Egypt, Pure Mathematics Grade 11 Term 2, lesson 4-3, printed p. 128 — double angle only).
4. Write a·cosθ ± b·sinθ as a single sine or cosine term (Libya, Grade 12 Science, Ch. 2, printed pp. 37–54).
5. Prove an identity by working one side into the other, and show a claimed identity is false with one counter-value (Nicaragua, Grade 10 student book, Unit 3, printed pp. 148–155; Jordan, Grade 12 Term 1, Unit 2, printed pp. 38, 50).
6. Rewrite sin 2α and cos 2α through tan α alone (the universal substitution), and read a greatest value off an auxiliary-angle single term (Kazakhstan, Soltan Algebra 9, §27, printed p. 201; Shynybekov Algebra 9, §4.5, printed p. 146).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A unit-circle drawing. A calculator only for checking counter-values.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner checks sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 for θ = 30°, 45° and an allied angle, and evaluates cot(−225°) carefully (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — what an identity is; the basic identities; the fundamental law cos(α − β) and the addition chain; double, half and triple angles; sum↔product; the single-term form; proving identities (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the fundamental-law part: a picture of two unit vectors on a unit circle, the angle between them closing from α − β wide to narrow, their dot product appearing beside them — the student watches the dot product become cos(α − β), the way Laos proves it.
   - ADD IMAGE at the chain part: a picture of a family tree starting at cos(α − β) at the top, with arrows down to cos(α + β) and sin(α ± β), then to the double-angle formulas, then to the half-angle formulas → the student traces one path with a finger and says which formula each arrow uses.
   - ADD IMAGE at the proving part: a picture of two columns, left side and right side of an identity, with the left column's steps rewriting it line by line until it matches the right → the student points at the line where a basic identity was used.
3. **Say it to your companion** — prove a short identity aloud for your companion, naming each formula as you use it (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the chain from cos(α − β) to sin 18° in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — evaluate with the chain, rewrite in a single term, and prove two identities (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Treats an identity like an equation to solve → an identity is true for every allowed angle; you prove it by rewriting, you never "find θ" (Nicaragua, Grade 10 student book, Unit 3, printed pp. 148–155).
- Forgets which addition formula is which → go back to the one proved formula, cos(α − β) = cos α cos β + sin α sin β, and rebuild the others from it, the way Pakistan and Laos do.
- Sign errors on allied angles → draw the unit circle and check the quadrant first; that is also how the corrected value cot(−225°) = −1 is found (see the lesson).
- This lesson's next door: Trigonometric equations (Level 73, same stage) — there the same formulas become tools for finding angles.
