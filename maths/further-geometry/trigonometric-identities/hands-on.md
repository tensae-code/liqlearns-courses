# Hands-on — Trigonometric identities

The lesson proved the chain on paper; here the hands check it.

## Check the mother formula
With a calculator, pick α = 50° and β = 20°. Work out cos(α − β) = cos 30° directly, then work out cos α cos β + sin α sin β. The two numbers agree — that agreement is the fundamental law Pakistan proves on the unit circle (Class 11, Unit 10, printed pp. 177–189).

## The family tree on one page
Write cos(α − β) at the top of a big sheet. Draw the tree yourself: replace β by −β for cos(α + β); use sin x = cos(90° − x) for the sine formulas; set β = α for the double-angle formulas; halve for the half-angle formulas. Beside each arrow, write the one move you used. If an arrow has no move, that branch is memorised, not understood — redo it.

## The counter-value test
A partner claims sin(α + β) = sin α + sin β for all angles. Test α = β = 30°: the left side is sin 60° ≈ 0.866, the right side is 0.5 + 0.5 = 1. One failing value kills a claimed identity — Nicaragua's book teaches exactly this test (Grade 10 student book, Unit 3, printed pp. 148–155).

## The 18° prize
Follow Laos's exercise (Grade 10, Ch. IX L22, printed pp. 233–250): with θ = 18°, sin 2θ = cos 3θ because 2θ + 3θ = 90°. Write sin 2θ = 2 sin θ cos θ and cos 3θ = 4cos³θ − 3cos θ, cancel cos θ, and solve 4sin²θ + 2 sin θ − 1 = 0 for sin 18° = (√5 − 1)/4. Check it on the calculator: about 0.309.
