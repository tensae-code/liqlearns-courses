# Three-dimensional trigonometry — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** The first job in any 3-D trigonometry problem is:
a) Pick a trig ratio  b) Find and draw the hidden flat triangle  c) Measure the solid's volume
**Answer:** b) — Jordan's method starts by finding the flat triangle inside the solid (Grade 10, Unit 4, Lesson 5, p. 124).

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** A cuboid is 6 cm by 8 cm on its base. Its base diagonal is:
a) 14 cm  b) 10 cm  c) 48 cm
**Answer:** b) 10 cm — √(6² + 8²) = √100, flat Pythagoras on the base.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** The cuboid above is 5 cm tall. Its space diagonal is about:
a) 11.2 cm  b) 15 cm  c) 13 cm
**Answer:** a) 11.2 cm — √(10² + 5²) = √125 ≈ 11.2.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** The angle between that space diagonal and the base satisfies tan θ = 5/10, so θ is about:
a) 63.4°  b) 26.6°  c) 45°
**Answer:** b) 26.6° — opposite (height 5) over adjacent (shadow 10) in the standing triangle.

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** The angle between a line in space and a face is measured between the line and:
a) The nearest edge of the solid  b) Its shadow on the face  c) The face's diagonal
**Answer:** b) — the shadow is the line dropped straight onto the face.

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** A hidden triangle inside a solid has no right angle. The finishing tools come from Jordan's same unit:
a) The sine rule and the cosine rule  b) Pythagoras only  c) The area formula only
**Answer:** a) — Unit 4 teaches the sine rule (p. 106) and cosine rule (p. 113) before the 3-D lesson (p. 124).
