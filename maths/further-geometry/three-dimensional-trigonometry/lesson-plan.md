# Lesson plan — Three-dimensional trigonometry

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 9 — Further Geometry · **Lesson:** Three-dimensional trigonometry
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 75 of 136, in Unit 9 — Further Geometry (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** solving trigonometry problems in three dimensions — finding the flat right triangle hidden inside a solid, then using ordinary sine, cosine, tangent and Pythagoras on it, step by step.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on right-triangle trigonometry and on the sine and cosine rules — the learner can already solve a flat triangle. It needs nothing else. This lesson has ONE full source: Jordan's Grade 10 book (Unit 4, Lesson 5, printed p. 124). It is built honestly at the size that stock supports.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Finding the Hidden Triangle** — the one method: every 3-D problem is a flat right triangle wearing a solid — name it, with its three vertices. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **The Two Flat Steps** — step one, a length by Pythagoras; step two, a ratio in the standing triangle. (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **Angles to Faces and Mixed Problems** — the shadow angle of a line against a face, non-right hidden triangles, and the same hunt in a pyramid. (Was depth Level 3.)

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Right-triangle trigonometry, and Sine and cosine rules. Recommended, never forced.
- A calculator is needed throughout.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Find the hidden flat right triangle inside a cuboid or pyramid problem, the way Jordan's Grade 10 book teaches 3-D problems (Unit 4, Lesson 5, printed p. 124).
2. Work a 3-D problem in two flat steps: first a diagonal or slant edge with Pythagoras, then an angle or length with a trig ratio (Jordan, Grade 10, Unit 4, Lesson 5, printed p. 124).
3. Find the angle between a line in space and a flat face (its shadow angle) by dropping the line to its shadow on the face (Jordan, Grade 10, Unit 4, Lesson 5, printed p. 124).
4. Use the unit's neighbours as Jordan sets them: bearings (Lesson 1, printed p. 100), the sine rule (Lesson 2, printed p. 106), the cosine rule (Lesson 3, printed p. 113) and triangle area by ½ab·sin C (Lesson 4, printed p. 119) inside a 3-D problem when the hidden triangle is not right-angled.

## What you need
A box (a cuboid) and a stick or ruler to be the diagonal. Paper, a pencil, a calculator.

1. **Do it first** — the learner holds a stick as the diagonal of a box corner and is asked for its length and its angle to the table, with no flat triangle given (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Cannot see any triangle → put a real stick inside a real box corner. The stick, its shadow on the base, and the vertical edge ARE the triangle. Draw exactly those three.
- Mixes the two steps → always the same order: length first (Pythagoras on a face), angle second (a ratio in the new triangle). Never reach for a ratio before the triangle exists on paper.
- Finds an angle to the wrong face → the angle to a face is measured to the line's SHADOW on that face, never to an edge of the solid.
- This lesson's next door: Bearings (same stage) — Jordan teaches the two side by side, bearings first in the same unit.
