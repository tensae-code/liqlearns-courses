# Transformations — the lesson

Course: Maths · Unit 9, Further Geometry · Lesson: Transformations
Built from: Egypt, Jordan, Kazakhstan, Sierra Leone, South Africa,
Greece, Mauritania, Ghana (teacher evidence) — see lesson/sources.md
for every part.

## Do it first
Draw a small triangle on squared paper. Put your finger on it
and slide it three squares to the right and two squares up,
without turning it. Draw it again where it landed. That slide
is a **translation**.

Now hold a mirror along one side of your triangle and draw what
you see in the mirror. That is a **reflection**. Now turn your
paper round a point — a quarter-turn, a half-turn. That is a
**rotation**. Now draw the triangle twice as big from one corner.
That is an **enlargement**.

Four moves. Four ways to make a new shape from an old one.

## Sub lesson 1 — Reflection and Translation

**A transformation moves a shape.** The shape you start with is
the object; the shape you get is the image. Egypt's Grade 7 book
defines all four moves in one lesson, and Jordan's Grade 6 book
teaches them on the coordinate plane, so every move can be written
down with numbers.

**Reflection — the mirror move.** Flip the shape over a line,
the mirror line. Every point jumps to the other side, the same
distance from the line. The image is the same size and shape —
congruent to the object — but back to front, like the word
"ambulance" written backwards on the front of an ambulance so
drivers read it right in their mirrors (Egypt's own example).
On the coordinate plane the rules are short, and Egypt's book
states them exactly:

- Reflect in the x-axis: (x, y) → (x, −y).
- Reflect in the y-axis: (x, y) → (−x, y).

South Africa's Grade 9 book practises reflecting over axes, over
other lines, and over any line. Ghana's teacher manual introduces
reflection in its Grade 6 geometry block — teacher evidence, not
a pupil book.

**Translation — the slide.** Move every point the same distance
in the same direction. No turning, no flipping, no change of
size. Jordan's Grade 6 book teaches it on the coordinate plane,
and Egypt's Grade 7 book writes the rule: a translation by
(a, b) sends (x, y) → (x + a, y + b). A translation of 3 right
and 2 up sends (1, 1) to (4, 3). Kazakhstan's Grade 9 book
proves what you can feel: a translation moves points along
parallel lines by equal distances.

## Sub lesson 2 — Rotation and Enlargement

**Rotation — the turn.** Turn the shape round a fixed point,
the centre of rotation, by an angle, in a direction — clockwise
or anticlockwise. The shape keeps its size and shape. Jordan's
Grade 7 book teaches rotation as its one transformation lesson,
on the coordinate plane with a GeoGebra lab. Egypt's Grade 7
book writes the rules for turns about the origin:

- Quarter-turn anticlockwise, 90°: (x, y) → (−y, x).
- Quarter-turn clockwise, 90°: (x, y) → (y, −x).
- Half-turn, 180°: (x, y) → (−x, −y).

A half-turn is the same as reflecting in the x-axis and then in
the y-axis — Egypt's book proves that in its next lesson.
Egypt's book also notes a starfish: turn it 72° and it looks
the same again — that is rotational symmetry, and a 20-seat
Ferris wheel turns 18° from one seat to the next.

**Enlargement — the growing move.** Make the shape bigger or
smaller from a fixed point, the centre of enlargement, by a
scale factor. Every length is multiplied by the scale factor;
every angle stays the same. The image is similar to the object.
Jordan's Grade 7 book teaches enlargement with a GeoGebra lab,
and its Grade 8 book teaches the same move under the name
**dilation**, drawing a polygon's image in the coordinate plane.
Kazakhstan's Grade 9 book calls it **homothety** and proves it
is a similarity transformation: a line goes to a parallel line,
a circle goes to a circle with its radius multiplied by k.
A scale factor bigger than 1 enlarges; a scale factor between
0 and 1 shrinks (Egypt's dilation lesson says the same).

## Sub lesson 3 — Combining Moves

**Doing two moves — composition.** Egypt's Grade 7 book gives
composition its own lesson: do one transformation, then
another, and ask what single move would do the same job.
Two translations add: slide (2, 1) then (1, 3) is the same as
one slide (3, 4). Reflect in the x-axis then in the y-axis is
the same as a half-turn. Sierra Leone's JSS 3 book practises
exactly this: combine transformations, then say whether the
result is congruent or similar to the start. The rule that
never breaks: reflection, translation and rotation keep the
shape congruent; only enlargement changes the size.

> **Tip — from us, not from the books.** If you lose track of
> which corner went where, label the corners A, B, C before
> you move anything, and label the image A′, B′, C′ after.
> The letters travel with the corners.

## Say it to your companion
Draw a triangle on squared paper. Tell your companion a move
("reflect in the y-axis", "turn 90° anticlockwise about the
origin", "enlarge by scale factor 2"). They draw the image.
Then swap, and check each other's images corner by corner.

## Do It Yourself
1. Draw triangle A(1, 1), B(3, 1), C(1, 4). Translate it by
   (2, −1). Write the new corners. (Answer: (3, 0), (5, 0),
   (3, 3).)
2. Reflect the same triangle in the x-axis. Write the new
   corners. (Answer: (1, −1), (3, −1), (1, −4).)
3. Rotate the same triangle 180° about the origin. Write the
   new corners. (Answer: (−1, −1), (−3, −1), (−1, −4).)
4. Enlarge the same triangle by scale factor 2 from the origin.
   Write the new corners and say whether the image is
   congruent or similar to the first triangle. (Answer: (2, 2),
   (6, 2), (2, 8) — similar.)
