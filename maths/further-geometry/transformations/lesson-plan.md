# Lesson plan — Transformations

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** 9, Further Geometry · **Lesson:** Transformations
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 9, the second lesson of the
stage (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the four moves that make a
new shape from an old one — reflection, translation, rotation
and enlargement (dilation) — written with coordinates, and what
two moves in a row are worth.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Stage 7 shapes
lesson and on coordinates (Stage 10's first lesson is its
near neighbour, but this lesson teaches every coordinate rule
it needs). It needs nothing else.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Reflection and Translation** — a transformation moves a shape from object to image; reflection, the mirror move, and translation, the slide. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Rotation and Enlargement** — rotation, the turn about a centre, and enlargement, the growing move from a centre by a scale factor. (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **Combining Moves** — doing two moves — composition — and the single move a pair of moves amounts to. (Was depth Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson (no hardness ranking is written in this lesson's question files).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: shapes and angles (Stage 7), and
  congruence and similarity (Stage 9, first lesson) — the
  moves in this lesson are what make congruent copies and
  similar enlargements. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name and draw a reflection, a translation, a rotation and
   an enlargement.
2. Write the image of a point under each move in coordinates.
3. Say whether an image is congruent or similar to its object.
4. Combine two moves and name the single move that does the
   same job.

## What you need
Squared paper, a ruler, a pencil, a small mirror (or a phone
screen, face down, as a mirror).

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner slides, mirrors, turns and
   grows a triangle on squared paper (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — reflection, translation, rotation,
   enlargement, then composition (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE at the reflection part: a picture of a butterfly

   - ADD GIF at the rotation part: a picture of a triangle

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses track of corners → label A, B, C before the move and
  A′, B′, C′ after; the letters travel with the corners.
- Mixes reflection and rotation → a reflection flips the
  shape over (back to front); a rotation turns it round (the
  front stays the front).
- Forgets which way (x, y) changes → write the rule down
  first, then put the numbers in; never do it in your head.
- This lesson's next door: right-triangle trigonometry (the
  angles the moves keep are the angles trig measures).
