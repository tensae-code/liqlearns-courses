# Hands-on — Loci: paths of moving points

## The rope circle
Push a pin into a board or the ground. Tie a string to it, hold a pencil at the string's full stretch, and walk the pencil around, keeping the string tight. The pencil draws the locus of points at a fixed distance from a fixed point — a circle. This is Sierra Leone's first loci construction (SSS 2, L92, printed p. 126), done with garden tools.

## The fair line between two pins
Push in two pins, A and B. Hunt for points that are the same distance from both: stretch the string to A, then to B, and shuffle until both reaches match. Mark five such points. They line up — on the perpendicular bisector of AB. Draw the line through them and fold the paper so A lands on B: the fold IS your line. Sierra Leone's L93 (printed p. 129), checked by folding.

## The two-sided path
Draw a long straight line. With compasses set to 2 cm, prick pairs of points 2 cm above and 2 cm below the line, all along it. Join each set: two parallel lines. Measure the gap in three places — it never changes. That pair is the locus of points at a given distance from a given straight line (L95, printed p. 135). One parallel only is half an answer.

## Corner bisectors in string
Draw two crossing lines. A point that is the same perpendicular distance from both lines must sit on a bisector. Use the string to test a point on each of the two bisectors, then a point off them (the distances disagree). The locus is BOTH bisectors (L94, printed p. 132).

## Pin the meeting point
A treasure is 3 cm from pin A and the same distance from pins B and C. Draw the circle of radius 3 cm around A, and the perpendicular bisector of BC. Where they cross are the only possible spots — two loci meeting pin the point. Dig (check) both. This is the mixed practice of L96 (printed p. 138).
