# Lesson plan — Loci: paths of moving points

**Course:** Maths · **Stage:** Stage 9 — Further Geometry · **Lesson:** Loci: paths of moving points
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 78 of 136, in Stage 9 — Further Geometry (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a locus — the set of all points that obey one rule — naming the standard loci (the circle, the perpendicular bisector, the angle bisectors, the parallel pair), proving the two bisector loci both ways, and constructing loci with ruler and compasses.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the construction lessons and on the triangle-centres lesson just before it — the learner can already bisect a segment and an angle, and has met the perpendicular bisector as the circumcentre's line. It needs nothing else.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Constructions with ruler and compass (Level 60), and The special lines and centres of a triangle (Level 77) — its perpendicular bisectors and angle bisectors return here as loci. Recommended, never forced.
- Compasses, a ruler and a long piece of string are needed for the drawing work.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a locus is — the set of all points satisfying a condition — as Kazakhstan's Turapbekov Grade 7 book defines the topic (Ch. 4 §1, printed p. 142) and Smirnov's Geometry 7 teaches it as its own section (§21, Chapter 4, printed pp. 101–134 as filed).
2. Construct the locus of points at a given distance from a given point — a circle (Sierra Leone, SSS 2, L92, printed p. 126).
3. Construct the locus of points equidistant from two given points — the perpendicular bisector (Sierra Leone, L93, printed p. 129).
4. Construct the locus of points equidistant from two straight lines — the angle bisectors (Sierra Leone, L94, printed p. 132).
5. Construct the locus of points at a given distance from a given straight line — two parallel lines (Sierra Leone, L95, printed p. 135), and combine loci in mixed construction practice (L96, printed p. 138).
6. State and use Iran's catalogue way: the perpendicular bisector and the angle bisector proved as loci, the circle proved as a locus in both directions, and further standard loci from the class-work catalogue (Geometry (3), Ch. 2, Lesson 1, printed pp. 34–39).

## If the learner is stuck
- Draws only a few points and stops → a locus is ALL the points that obey the rule, joined: test points on your curve (do they obey?) and points off it (do they fail?). Both checks belong to the locus, as Iran's book insists.
- Gives one parallel line for the fixed-distance locus → the rule does not choose a side: points on BOTH sides of the line qualify, so the locus is a pair of parallels.
- Gives one bisector for the equidistant-from-two-lines locus → two crossing lines make two bisectors, at right angles to each other; both are the locus.
- Confuses "equidistant from two points" with "equidistant from two lines" → points give the perpendicular bisector of the JOIN; lines give the bisectors of the ANGLES between them. Say which object the rule names before drawing.
- This lesson's next door: the conic sections in the Advanced stage are loci too — Iran's catalogue in this lesson is their doorway (the ellipse and parabola are built as loci there).
