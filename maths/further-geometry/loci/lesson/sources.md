Maths content hiding under an all-subjects label was checked for across the whole in-hand set; this package is the only such volume. The seven pupil books (Primary Four, the Primary Five booklet, Primary Six, Senior One, Senior Two, Senior Three, Senior Four) are real pupil teaching books — lessons, worked examples and exercises addressed to the learner — so Uganda's pupil rows **count** in a lesson's taught-in figure. The one teacher book, the Senior One *Teacher's Guide* (prototype, 76 pp.), is **teacher evidence only** and counts nowhere; it covers all 12 Senior One prototype topics, including five topics (1 Number Bases, 2 Working with Integers, 8 Angle Properties, 9 Data Collection and Presentation, 12 Time and Time Tables) for which no Senior One pupil text is in hand, and its evidence for those topics carries that “no pupil book in hand” caveat. NOT in hand, and cited nowhere: the Senior One prototype pupil Mathematics textbook (NCDC posted only the Teacher's Guide) and the Scribd combined S1&S2 Science Package (no download route). The placements number **324 items: 278 pupil items and 46 teacher-evidence items (TG1–TG46)** — one item per distinct lesson in the home-study books, one per distinct topic treatment in the guide. Page citations are PDF pages for the pupil books (in the primary home-study books the printed folios run 6 behind: printed = PDF − 6; Primary Five pages are the merged package's PDF pages) and the guide's printed pages for the TG items. The books' own errors are taught corrected: the Senior One home-study book heads the conversion example at PDF p. 8 “Convert 13/4 into an improper fraction” — 13/4 is already improper, and the worked example in fact converts it to the mixed number 3¼; the Primary Six book's triangle-angles exercise (PDF p. 85) prints two interior angles as “360 and 540” — the degree signs set as zeros, read as 36° and 54°; the Senior Three book heads its mensuration topic “TRIGONOMETRY” while its own stated outcomes and lessons are units, surface area and volume — taught as mensuration. Uganda's own ways are kept: equations written with a shape for the unknown before letters (Primary Four); integers walked on a ground number line, face forward for positive and back for backward (Primary Five and Six); base five taught in primary school (Primary Five); fractions taught by slicing chapattis (Senior One); income tax worked from Uganda's own tax bands and allowances (Senior Three). Uganda's class for Maths is **COURSES-CLOSED** — every Maths book in hand has been read fresh and placed; what remains outside the set (the Senior One prototype pupil textbook, the Senior Two–Four prototype books and guides) is not in hand and is a library hunting matter, not a course hold.
