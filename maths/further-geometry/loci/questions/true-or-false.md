# Loci: paths of moving points — true or false

Items that match or order the whole catalogue of loci stay with the sub lesson of their old level.

Say true or false. If it is false, say why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** The locus of points at a given distance from a given point is a circle. True or false?
**Answer:** True — centre the point, radius the distance (Sierra Leone, L92, printed p. 126).

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** A locus may contain just some of the points that satisfy the condition. True or false?
**Answer:** False — a locus is ALL the points satisfying the condition, and no others.

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** The locus of points at a given distance from a straight line is a single parallel line. True or false?
**Answer:** False — it is two parallel lines, one on each side; the rule names no side (L95).

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** Every point on the perpendicular bisector of AB is equidistant from A and B. True or false?
**Answer:** True — that equality is exactly the rule the bisector is the locus of (L93).

**5. (Sub lesson 4)** To prove a path is a locus, it is enough to check that points on the path obey the rule. True or false?
**Answer:** False — Iran's book proves it both directions: points obeying the rule must also all lie on the path (Geometry (3), Ch. 2 L1, printed pp. 36–38).

**6. (Sub lesson 4)** In Greece's book, a point that keeps seeing a fixed chord under the same known angle moves along a straight line. True or false?
**Answer:** False — it moves along an arc of a circle, the arc subtending the known angle (Euclidean Geometry Volume A, Ch. 6, chapter opens printed p. 127).
