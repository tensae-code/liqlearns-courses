# Loci: paths of moving points — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Level 1)** A locus is:
a) One special point  b) The set of all points that satisfy a given condition  c) Any curve you can draw
**Answer:** b) — all the points obeying the rule, and no others (Turapbekov, Grade 7, Ch. 4 §1, p. 142).

**2. (Level 1)** The locus of points at a given distance from a given point is:
a) A straight line  b) A pair of parallel lines  c) A circle
**Answer:** c) — centre the given point, radius the given distance (Sierra Leone, L92, printed p. 126).

**3. (Level 1)** The locus of points at a given distance from a given straight line is:
a) One parallel line  b) Two parallel lines, one on each side  c) A circle
**Answer:** b) — the rule names no side, so both sides answer (Sierra Leone, L95, printed p. 135).

**4. (Level 2)** The locus of points equidistant from two given points A and B is:
a) The perpendicular bisector of AB  b) The line AB itself  c) A circle centred on AB
**Answer:** a) — the perpendicular bisector (Sierra Leone, L93, printed p. 129).

**5. (Level 2)** The locus of points equidistant from two intersecting straight lines is:
a) One line only  b) The two bisectors of the angles between the lines  c) A circle
**Answer:** b) — both bisectors belong to the locus (Sierra Leone, L94, printed p. 132).

**6. (Level 2)** To construct the perpendicular bisector of AB with compasses, the arcs from A and B must be drawn with:
a) Different openings  b) The same opening, wider than half of AB  c) An opening of exactly AB
**Answer:** b) — equal radii, wide enough for the arcs to cross above and below AB.

**7. (Level 3)** Iran's Geometry (3) book proves the circle is a locus "in both directions". That means proving:
a) The circle is round  b) Every point on the circle is at the fixed distance, AND every point at the fixed distance is on the circle  c) The centre exists
**Answer:** b) — both halves belong to any locus claim (Geometry (3), Ch. 2 L1, printed pp. 36–38).

**8. (Level 3)** A point must obey TWO locus rules at once. The possible positions of the point are found where:
a) The two loci cross  b) The two loci are parallel  c) One locus is erased
**Answer:** a) — mixed construction practice works by intersecting loci (Sierra Leone, L96, printed p. 138).

**9. (Level 3)** A point P moves on one side of a fixed chord AB so that angle APB stays the same known angle. Greece's book names P's locus as:
a) A straight line  b) An arc of a circle over AB  c) The chord AB itself
**Answer:** b) — the arc of a circle subtending a known angle is Greece's basic locus in the circle (Euclidean Geometry Volume A, Ch. 6, chapter opens printed p. 127).
