# Hands-on — Numerals of the World

The lesson read the world's numeral systems on paper. Here your hands write them.

## Clock hunt
Find every Roman numeral you can in one day: clock faces, book chapters, film dates, building stones. Write each one down with where you saw it and what number it stands for — Sierra Leone's JSS 3 book starts Roman numerals from clocks and watches for exactly this reason (L16, p.43).

## Sign cards
Make cards for I, V, X, L, C, D, M. A partner calls a number to 100; you lay the cards. Rule check before you lay: never more than three of the same sign in a row, and a smaller card before a bigger one takes away. Swap after five numbers.

## Six scripts, one number
Pick the number 12. Write it six ways: Hindu-Arabic (12), Roman (XII), Devanagari (१२), and — copying slowly from the lesson's picture row — Lao, Khmer and Ge'ez. Nepal's Grade 2 book sets the first three side by side to XII for this exact exercise (Lesson 8, pp.22–26).

## Lakh ladder
Write 1,000, 10,000, 100,000 and 10,000,000 twice: once with Western grouping, once with South Asian grouping (commas every two digits after the first three). Read the South Asian row aloud with its names: thousand, ten thousand, one lakh, one crore.
