# Numerals of the World — short answer

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Numerals of the World Roman Numerals · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Numerals of the World The Digit Sets of the World · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Numerals of the World Lakh, Crore and the Big-Number Names.

Answer in a word or a short line.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Write 9 in Roman signs.
**Answer:** IX — one before ten, the subtraction rule (Laos, Grade 6, L1).

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** Write 50 and 100 in Roman signs.
**Answer:** L, C — the two signs Sierra Leone adds for conversions to 100 (JSS 3, L17, p.47).

**3. (Sub lesson 1)** Convert MMMCMXCIX to ordinary digits.
**Answer:** 3999 — MMM + CM + XC + IX = 3000 + 900 + 90 + 9 (Laos, Grade 6, L1 exercises).

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** Which country's Grade 1 book teaches the digits ໐–໙?
**Answer:** Laos — Grade 1 (new series), Ch. 18, p.154.

**5. (Sub lesson 2)** Ethiopia's Grade 4 book carries Ge'ez counting numerals up to what number?
**Answer:** 100 — Grade 4, Ch. 1, pp.1–28.

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** Read 40,72,802 the Nepali lakh way, as the book reads it.
**Answer:** "40 lakhs 72 thousands 8 hundreds 2" — the book's reading is "40 lakhs 72 thousands…" (Grade 3, Lesson 14, pp.61–63).

**7. (Sub lesson 3)** Bangladesh's Class 3 book names 10 thousand with what word?
**Answer:** Ayut — Class 3, Ch. 1.

**8. (Sub lesson 3)** Write one lakh with South Asian comma grouping.
**Answer:** 1,00,000 — Bangladesh, Class 4, §§1.1–1.2.
