# Numerals of the World — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Level 1)** The Roman sign X stands for ______.
a) 5  b) 10  c) 100
**Answer:** b) 10 — I, V, X, L, C, D, M stand for 1, 5, 10, 50, 100, 500, 1000 (Laos, Grade 6, L1).

**2. (Level 1)** IV in ordinary digits is ______.
a) 6  b) 4  c) 15
**Answer:** b) 4 — a smaller sign before a bigger one takes away: one before five.

**3. (Level 1)** 34 in Roman signs is ______.
a) XXXIV  b) XXXIIII  c) IVXXX
**Answer:** a) XXXIV — tens first (XXX), then ones (IV), the South Sudan way (§1.4, pp.14–17).

**4. (Level 2)** Nepal's Grade 2 matching exercise sets १२ beside ______.
a) 12 = XII  b) 21 = XXI  c) 2 = II
**Answer:** a) 12 = XII — Devanagari, Hindu-Arabic and Roman run side by side to XII (Lesson 8, pp.22–26).

**5. (Level 2)** Ge'ez numerals are taught in Ethiopia's student books starting in ______.
a) Grade 1  b) Grade 2  c) Grade 3
**Answer:** c) Grade 3 — §1.5, p.15; the Grade 1–2 books do not teach them, as filed.

**6. (Level 2)** In Cambodia's Grade 4 book, 100,000 is read ______.
a) one saen  b) one lakh  c) one crore
**Answer:** a) one saen — the Khmer place word for that landing point (L1, p.1).

**7. (Level 3)** 1 lakh is ______.
a) 10,000  b) 100,000  c) 10,000,000
**Answer:** b) 100,000 — Bangladesh, Class 3, Ch. 1.

**8. (Level 3)** One crore written with South Asian grouping is ______.
a) 10,000,000 with commas 1,00,00,000  b) 100,00,00  c) 1,000,00
**Answer:** a) 1,00,00,000 — first three digits from the right, then groups of two (Bangladesh, Class 4, §§1.1–1.2).
