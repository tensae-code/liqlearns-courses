# Lesson plan — Numerals of the World

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 1 — Numbers · **Lesson:** Numerals of the World
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 54 of 136, inside Unit 1 — Numbers (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the digits 0–9 are only one way of writing numbers — Roman, Ge'ez, Devanagari, Lao, Khmer, Hindi and ancient Egyptian numerals each write the same counts in their own signs, and South Asian naming groups large numbers in lakhs and crores.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Counting and Place Value — the learner can already count and read ordinary Hindu-Arabic numbers. It needs nothing else.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Read and write Roman numerals using I, V, X, L, C, D, M with the repetition and subtraction rules, converting both ways up to MMMCMXCIX in the exercises (Laos, Grade 6 student book (2010), L1, printed pp.1–9).
2. Read Roman numerals on clocks and in everyday use, and convert between base 10 and Roman numerals to 100 (Sierra Leone, JSS 3, Term 1, L16–L17, printed pp.43–47).
3. Read and write Ethiopian (Ge'ez) numerals 1–10 and counting numerals to 100 (Ethiopia, Grade 3, §1.5, printed p.15; Grade 4, Ch. 1, printed pp.1–28).
4. Match Devanagari, Hindu-Arabic and Roman numerals side by side to XII (Nepal, Grade 2 (English), Lesson 8, printed pp.22–26).
5. Read large numbers in the South Asian system — lakh and crore, ayut/ajut and nijut — with South Asian comma grouping such as 1,00,000 (Bangladesh, Class 4, §§1.1–1.2, printed pp.2–18; Nepal, Grade 3, Lesson 14, printed pp.61–63).
6. Write Khmer numerals with their stroke order and read the Lao digits (Cambodia, Grade 1, L3–L4, printed pp.6–15; Laos, Grade 1 (new series), Ch. 18, printed p.154).
7. Read 100,000 in Khmer place words as "one saen" (Cambodia, Grade 4, L1, printed p.1).
8. Build numbers with ancient Egyptian numeral signs, the way South Africa's workbooks use them for 5-digit numbers (South Africa, Grade 5 Workbook 1, WS2–3, printed pp.2–13 block).
9. Name the Hindi/Indian digit system as a taught alternative digit set (Jordan, Grade 3, Term 1, Unit 1 L7, printed p.20).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A clock face with Roman numerals, if one is near.
Cards with the seven Roman signs: I, V, X, L, C, D, M.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner writes their age three ways: in ordinary digits, as tally strokes, and — if they have ever seen a clock with letters — in Roman signs, then compares the three writings (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — Roman numerals and their two rules; Roman in daily life; Ge'ez numerals; the digit sets of Nepal, Laos, Cambodia and Jordan; Egyptian signs; lakh and crore naming (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the Roman part: a picture of Roman signs being built one by one — I, II, III, then V replacing IIII, then IV for one-before-five — the student reads each stage aloud.
   - ADD IMAGE under the digit sets: a picture of the same number 7 written in Hindu-Arabic, Devanagari, Lao, Khmer, Ge'ez and Hindi digits in a row → the student copies the row and says which script each sign belongs to.
   - ADD IMAGE in the lakh part: a picture of 100000 written twice, grouped the Western way (100,000) and the South Asian way (1,00,000) → the student reads both aloud and says the lakh name.
3. **Say it to your companion** — dictate a number up to 100, your companion writes it in Roman signs and reads it back, then swap scripts (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the numerals chant in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — write today's date and your birth year in Roman numerals, and write 250,000 in South Asian grouping with its lakh name (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Writes IIII for 4 → the subtraction rule: a smaller sign before a bigger one means take away — IV, one before five (Laos, Grade 6, L1, pp.1–9).
- Puts a sign after a bigger one and subtracts → a smaller sign AFTER a bigger one adds: VI is five and one, six — South Sudan's Primary 5 table works VI = 6 and IX = 9 as the pair to remember (§1.4, pp.14–17).
- Reads a crore as "ten million" but groups the commas the Western way → Bangladesh's grouping: after the first three digits from the right, commas come every TWO digits: 1,00,00,000 (Class 4, §§1.1–1.2, pp.2–18).
- Mixes up the scripts → go back to one number, 7, in every script, copied slowly; the scripts are shapes to recognise, not new numbers.
- This lesson's next door: Place Value once more — lakh and crore are place value with different group sizes, exactly as a base is place value with a different bundle.
