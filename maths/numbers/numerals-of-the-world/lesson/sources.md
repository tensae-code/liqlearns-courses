**Acceptance count: taught in 26 countries (Bangladesh, Cambodia, Ethiopia, Jordan, Kazakhstan, Laos, Nepal, Sierra Leone, South Africa, South Sudan, Vietnam, Rwanda, Sudan, India, Mongolia, Venezuela, North Macedonia, Afghanistan, Uzbekistan, Ecuador, Albania, Tajikistan, Turkey, Tanzania, Armenia, El Salvador).** Laos carries Roman numerals at full strength in Grade 6 — all seven signs, both rules, conversions to MMMCMXCIX — and its own Lao digits in Grade 1. Ethiopia carries its own Ge'ez numerals, from 1–10 in Grade 3 to counting numerals to 100 in Grade 4. Nepal carries the three-script matching (Devanagari, Hindu-Arabic, Roman) in Grade 2 and the lakh reading of Devanagari-digit numbers in Grade 3. Bangladesh carries the South Asian naming ladder whole: ayut and lakh in Class 3, ajut, lac, nijut and crore with the 1,00,000 grouping in Class 4, and Roman rules in Class 6. Cambodia carries Khmer numeral writing with stroke order in Grade 1 and the "saen" name for 100,000 in Grade 4. South Africa carries ancient Egyptian numerals as a working system in its Grade 5 and Grade 6 workbooks. Jordan carries the Hindi/Indian digits as a named Grade 3 lesson. Sierra Leone carries Roman numerals from clocks to conversions to 100 in JSS 3. South Sudan carries the Roman 1–50 table in Primary 5. Vietnam carries Roman numerals as a "getting to know" introduction only, as filed. Kazakhstan carries Roman, Egyptian and old Slavic numerals as extra reading only, optional as filed. North Macedonia enters from its main set — Grade 6 (2023).
