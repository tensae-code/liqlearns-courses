**Acceptance count: taught in 23 countries (Bangladesh, Cambodia, Ethiopia, Jordan, Kazakhstan, Laos, Nepal, Sierra Leone, South Africa, South Sudan, Vietnam, Rwanda, Sudan, India, Mongolia, Venezuela, North Macedonia, Afghanistan, Uzbekistan, Ecuador, Albania, Tajikistan, Turkey).** Laos carries Roman numerals at full strength in Grade 6 — all seven signs, both rules, conversions to MMMCMXCIX — and its own Lao digits in Grade 1. Ethiopia carries its own Ge'ez numerals, from 1–10 in Grade 3 to counting numerals to 100 in Grade 4. Nepal carries the three-script matching (Devanagari, Hindu-Arabic, Roman) in Grade 2 and the lakh reading of Devanagari-digit numbers in Grade 3. Bangladesh carries the South Asian naming ladder whole: ayut and lakh in Class 3, ajut, lac, nijut and crore with the 1,00,000 grouping in Class 4, and Roman rules in Class 6. Cambodia carries Khmer numeral writing with stroke order in Grade 1 and the "saen" name for 100,000 in Grade 4. South Africa carries ancient Egyptian numerals as a working system in its Grade 5 and Grade 6 workbooks. Jordan carries the Hindi/Indian digits as a named Grade 3 lesson. Sierra Leone carries Roman numerals from clocks to conversions to 100 in JSS 3. South Sudan carries the Roman 1–50 table in Primary 5. Vietnam carries Roman numerals as a "getting to know" introduction only, as filed. Kazakhstan carries Roman, Egyptian and old Slavic numerals as extra reading only, optional as filed. North Macedonia enters from its main set — Grade 6 (2023).

Rwanda names the Hindu-Arabic, Roman and Chinese numeral systems as three common systems, with the ten Hindu-Arabic digits 0–9, in P4.

Sudan opens Grade 7's number-systems unit with a history of numerals: tally strokes, Roman numerals, Indian and Arabic digits, the positional decimal system and the octal system named.

India enters numerals of the world from Mathematics Class 6: the Indian and international systems of numeration — lakh and crore set beside million and billion.


Mongolia enters through its ministry textbooks Математик V (Grade 5): Roman numerals I–M: values, additive/subtractive writing, reading years and clock faces.

Venezuela enters through the Colección Bicentenario, the MPPE's one-book-per-grade course: *Hecho en Venezuela* (Grade 6). From its books: from Grade 6: Roman — Roman numerals: a non-positional system of seven symbols (I V X L C D M), full table 1–1000 with subtractive forms (IV, XL, CD, XC), and its surviving uses.

Afghanistan enters through its Ministry of Education course — the Grade 1 course in two language editions (Pashto and Dari, one work with identical page maps) and Dari student books for Grades 4, 6, 7, 8, 10, 11 and 12, all printing year 1398 HS except the Grade 8 book, which prints 1399 HS on its cover and title page and 1398 HS on its specifications page. From its books: from Grade 6: Roman numerals 1-20 and the rules for writing them.

Albania enters through its Mediaprint teacher's books (Libër Mësuesi, in Albanian) — TEACHER EVIDENCE only: no pupil's book is in hand for any grade, so Albania is never cited as a pupil-textbook source; what the teacher's books evidence is what is taught and in what order, through their lesson plans, worked answers and answer keys. From its books: from Grade 6 (teacher's book): Opener + 1A: counting systems of other cultures; symbols and number systems (unit 1A, plans 1–3).

Tajikistan enters through its Ministry-approved pupil's textbooks in Tajik (Cyrillic), all published in Dushanbe — Математика for Grade 5 (Маориф, 2022) and Grade 6 (5th edition, Маориф, 2021), Алгебра for Grades 7 (4th improved edition, 2023) and 8 (4th edition, 2023), and Геометрия — the continuation of stereometry — for Grade 11 (3rd edition, Лавҳ, 2020). From its books: from Grade 5: Large-number names in powers of ten (million to the largest named powers).

Barbados enters as CURRICULUM EVIDENCE only, through the *Primary Mathematics Syllabus* (Classes 1–4) of its Ministry of Education and Human Resource Development — its Class 1 syllabus requires Roman numerals up to 12, Class 2 Roman numerals up to 50, and Class 3 (in the body only) Roman numerals up to 1000. A syllabus states what is to be taught, in what order and to what depth; it teaches nothing itself. Barbados publishes no government pupil textbooks — primary mathematics books are commercial titles parents buy, and the syllabi's Suggested Texts appendices name them (*Caribbean Primary Mathematics* Levels 1–6; *Nelson Primary Maths for Caribbean Schools* 1–4; *Steps To Common Entrance Mathematics* 1–3) as named by the syllabus only: none is in hand and none is cited. Barbados is therefore named in this lesson's countries line and its syllabus requirements are cited in the table as curriculum evidence, but Barbados is not counted in the taught-in figure.

Turkey enters through the pupil's textbooks of its Millî Eğitim Bakanlığı (MEB — Ministry of National Education), all in Turkish, first volumes (1. Kitap) only — Grade 3 (ISBN 978-975-11-9897-6, TTKB approval 13/07/2026), Grade 6 (ISBN 978-975-11-8773-4, TTKB approval 30.06.2025) and Grade 7 (ISBN 978-975-11-9899-0, TTKB approval 1.07.2026); no edition, printing or year line is printed in any of the three books, and the grades' second volumes (2. Kitap) are not in hand and are cited nowhere. From its books: from Grade 3: Roman numerals researched, then the full table 1–24 (I, II, III, IV, V … IX, X … XIX, XX … XXIV); where Roman numerals are used (centuries, rulers' names, book volumes, lists) with paired examples (XX. century = 20th, III.
