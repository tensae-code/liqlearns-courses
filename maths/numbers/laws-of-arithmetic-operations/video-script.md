# Video script — The Laws of Arithmetic Operations

Super-simple voice. The tutor talks; the screen shows each sum being rearranged as it is named.

---

Some sums look long. But numbers have rules. The rules let you move the numbers. Then the sum turns short.

Rule one. Add zero, and nothing changes. Times by one, and nothing changes. Zero and one leave a number alone.

Rule two. You may swap. Three plus five is five plus three. Two times six is six times two. Swapping works when you add. Swapping works when you multiply. It does not work when you take away. It does not work when you share.

Rule three. You may move the groups. Add the last two first, if that helps. 87 plus 29 plus 71: add 29 and 71 first — that makes 100! Then 87 plus 100 is easy.

Rule four. Spread it out. Seven times 99 looks hard. But 99 is 100 take away 1. Seven hundreds is 700. Take away 7. 693. Done.

Careful. One book prints rule four the wrong way round. It is times that spreads over plus and take-away. Not the other way. The sums on the same page prove it.

Now you try. Move the numbers. Make the sum short.
