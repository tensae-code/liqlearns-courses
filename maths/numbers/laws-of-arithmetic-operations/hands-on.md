# Hands-on — The Laws of Arithmetic Operations

## Activity 1 — The receipt game (10 minutes)

Write three prices on paper slips (for example 47, 25, 53). The learner must find the pair that makes a round number, group them, and only then add the third. Repeat with prices that make 1000. The rule they are using is named out loud every time: associative first, then commutative if a swap was needed.

## Activity 2 — Four in a packet (10 minutes)

With four number cards (for example 25, 8, 4, 125), race to make 1000 by multiplying, choosing the grouping: (25 × 4) × (8 × 125) = 100 × 1000... no — find the grouping that works, then check by multiplying in the written order. The point: the laws say both ways give the same answer; the clever way is just faster.

## Activity 3 — Spot the illegal move (5 minutes)

Show four rearrangements, one of them illegal: (a) 9 − 4 = 4 − 9? (b) 12 ÷ 3 = 3 ÷ 12? (c) 6 + 14 = 14 + 6 ✓ (d) 3 × 7 = 7 × 3 ✓. The learner finds the two illegal ones and says which law was wrongly borrowed.
