# Multiple choice — The Laws of Arithmetic Operations

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth layers ran across the four laws together, so the items are split item by item, by the law each item actually asks about: identity -> Sub lesson 1 — Arithmetic Laws The Identity Law · commutative -> Sub lesson 2 — Arithmetic Laws The Commutative Law · associative -> Sub lesson 3 — Arithmetic Laws The Associative Law · distributive -> Sub lesson 4 — Arithmetic Laws The Distributive Law · calculating cleverly (old Layer 3) -> Sub lesson 5 — Arithmetic Laws Calculating Cleverly.

1. (Sub lesson 2) Which law says 13 + 27 = 27 + 13?
   A. Identity B. Commutative C. Associative D. Distributive
   **Answer: B** — Malaysia, Mathematics Form 1, Ch. 1 §1.2, printed p. 11.

2. (Sub lesson 3) (4 + 9) + 1 = 4 + (9 + 1) is an example of which law?
   A. Associative B. Distributive C. Identity D. Commutative
   **Answer: A** — Malaysia, Mathematics Form 1, Ch. 1 §1.2, printed p. 11.

3. (Sub lesson 2) Which of these is FALSE?
   A. 8 × 5 = 5 × 8 B. 20 − 6 = 6 − 20 C. 3 + 9 = 9 + 3 D. 7 × 1 = 7
   **Answer: B** — the commutative law does not hold for subtraction. Malaysia, Mathematics Form 1, Ch. 1 §1.2, printed p. 11.

4. (Sub lesson 5) The clever way to do 25 × 13 × 4 starts with:
   A. 25 × 13 B. 13 × 4 C. 25 × 4 D. 25 + 4
   **Answer: C** — 25 × 4 = 100. Malaysia, Mathematics Form 1, Ch. 1 §1.2, printed pp. 11–12.
