**Acceptance count: taught in 1 country (Ecuador).**

This lesson exists because of the 2026-11-08 re-verification sweep of Ecuador's books. The sweep re-read all ten in-hand Ecuador maths books page by page and rebuilt their placements from the PDFs themselves — 908 fresh teaching items; 907 were already in the course, and this closing anthology was the single missing item (908 = 907 + 1). No existing lesson could home it: Counting (same unit) stocks the count to 100 itself — its Ecuador rows from this same book are the anthology's neighbours (reading and writing natural numbers to 999, printed pp. 16–17; quantities from 0 to 999, printed pp. 52–53), and the sweep marks the anthology's counting-to-100 and Gauss parts reconcile-with-repair against it; Numerals of the World (same unit) stocks the full Roman numeral system, which Ecuador's Grade 7 book teaches at printed pp. 30–34; Mathematical Investigations stocks investigations, not reading. A designed closing anthology that teaches mathematics through stories and poems is a teaching topic of its own, so under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06) it gets its own lesson — the same call the chess-and-sudoku-reasoning lesson was built on. Searchable tag: "Yo Leo" — the anthology's own name in the book.

Copyright: the anthology's stories and poems are never transcribed in this lesson. The lesson teaches their mathematics in its own words, refers to the stories by what they teach, and gives the Gauss story as a plot summary only; the worked pairings (1 to 10 = 55, 1 to 20 = 210, 1 to 50 = 1,275, 1 to 100 = 5,050) are the lesson's own workings of the pairing idea the anthology presents, verified correct.

Ecuador enters through its Matemática, Grade 3 student book (Talento matemático 3, Serie Talentos) — an EDINUN book for the Ministerio de Educación, an earlier edition (© Edinun 2016 / © Ministerio 2018, primera impresión agosto 2016, cuarta impresión febrero 2018, no ISBN printed), not one of the 2024 curricular-transition series the country's other in-hand books belong to, and never presented as one. From its books: the closing "Yo Leo" reading anthology of number-themed stories and poems, printed pp. 210–224.
