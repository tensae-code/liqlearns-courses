# Multiple choice — Numbers in Stories

Sub-lesson markers: every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to — Sub lesson 1 — Counting to 100 in a Story · Sub lesson 2 — The Young Gauss: Adding 1 to 100 · Sub lesson 3 — Times Tables in Verse · Sub lesson 4 — Roman Numerals in Books. This lesson was built new with its sub lessons (Ecuador sweep merge, 2026-11-08), so its markers are original — there are no older depth layers to map from.

1. (Sub lesson 1) The counting stories of the "Yo Leo" anthology carry the count all the way to:
   (a) 20
   (b) 50
   (c) 100
   (d) 1,000

   **Answer: (c)** — the anthology's number stories count to 100. Ecuador, *Matemática (Talento matemático 3)*, Grade 3 student book, closing "Yo Leo" anthology, printed pp. 210–224.

2. (Sub lesson 1) Counting by tens — the stepping stones of the story count — the stone after 40 is:
   (a) 41
   (b) 45
   (c) 50
   (d) 60

   **Answer: (c)** — the tens run 10, 20, 30, 40, 50 … Item constructed for this lesson from the anthology's counting-to-100 stories. Ecuador, *Matemática (Talento matemático 3)*, Grade 3 student book, closing "Yo Leo" anthology, printed pp. 210–224.

3. (Sub lesson 2) The young Gauss paired 1 with 100, and 2 with 99. Every pair adds to:
   (a) 100
   (b) 101
   (c) 110
   (d) 200

   **Answer: (b)** — as one partner climbs by 1 the other falls by 1, so every pair sums to 101. Ecuador, *Matemática (Talento matemático 3)*, Grade 3 student book, closing "Yo Leo" anthology, printed pp. 210–224.

4. (Sub lesson 2) The numbers 1 to 100, taken two at a time, make how many pairs?
   (a) 25
   (b) 50
   (c) 99
   (d) 100

   **Answer: (b)** — 100 numbers ÷ 2 = 50 pairs, so the sum is 50 × 101 = 5,050. Item constructed for this lesson from the anthology's Gauss story. Ecuador, *Matemática (Talento matemático 3)*, Grade 3 student book, closing "Yo Leo" anthology, printed pp. 210–224.

5. (Sub lesson 3) Why do the books carry times tables in verse?
   (a) the verse changes the answers
   (b) rhythm and rhyme help memory hold the facts
   (c) verses are shorter than tables
   (d) poems are easier to print

   **Answer: (b)** — the verse is a carrier for memory; the facts themselves do not change. Ecuador, *Matemática (Talento matemático 3)*, Grade 3 student book, closing "Yo Leo" anthology, printed pp. 210–224.

6. (Sub lesson 3) The fact the verse carries: 3 × 4 =
   (a) 7
   (b) 9
   (c) 12
   (d) 34

   **Answer: (c)** — three fours are twelve, in prose, in verse and in song.

7. (Sub lesson 4) The Roman numeral X stands for:
   (a) 5
   (b) 10
   (c) 50
   (d) 100

   **Answer: (b)** — I = 1, V = 5, X = 10, L = 50, C = 100. Item constructed for this lesson from the anthology's Roman-numeral reading. Ecuador, *Matemática (Talento matemático 3)*, Grade 3 student book, closing "Yo Leo" anthology, printed pp. 210–224.

8. (Sub lesson 4) A book's chapter headed VIII is chapter:
   (a) 6
   (b) 7
   (c) 8
   (d) 13

   **Answer: (c)** — VIII is V + III, five and three, eight.
