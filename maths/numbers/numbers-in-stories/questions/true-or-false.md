# True or false — Numbers in Stories

Sub-lesson markers: every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to — Sub lesson 1 — Counting to 100 in a Story · Sub lesson 2 — The Young Gauss: Adding 1 to 100 · Sub lesson 3 — Times Tables in Verse · Sub lesson 4 — Roman Numerals in Books. This lesson was built new with its sub lessons (Ecuador sweep merge, 2026-11-08), so its markers are original — there are no older depth layers to map from.

1. (Sub lesson 1) A counting story can carry the count all the way to 100.
   **Answer: True** — the anthology's number stories count to 100 inside the reading. Ecuador, *Matemática (Talento matemático 3)*, Grade 3 student book, closing "Yo Leo" anthology, printed pp. 210–224.

2. (Sub lesson 2) The young Gauss added 1 to 100 one number at a time, in order, just faster than his classmates.
   **Answer: False** — he paired first with last: fifty pairs of 101, giving 5,050 without the long adding. Ecuador, *Matemática (Talento matemático 3)*, Grade 3 student book, closing "Yo Leo" anthology, printed pp. 210–224.

3. (Sub lesson 2) 49 and 52 are a Gauss pair in 1 to 100.
   **Answer: True** — 49 + 52 = 101, the sum every pair must make.

4. (Sub lesson 3) Putting a times table into verse changes its answers.
   **Answer: False** — 4 × 5 is 20 in prose and in verse; the verse only carries the fact.

5. (Sub lesson 4) In Roman numerals, IV means 4.
   **Answer: True** — one smaller letter before a bigger one takes away: 5 − 1 = 4.

6. (Sub lesson 4) The Roman letter L stands for 10.
   **Answer: False** — L is 50; X is 10. Item constructed for this lesson from the anthology's Roman-numeral reading. Ecuador, *Matemática (Talento matemático 3)*, Grade 3 student book, closing "Yo Leo" anthology, printed pp. 210–224.
