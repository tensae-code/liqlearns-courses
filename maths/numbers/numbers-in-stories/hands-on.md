# Hands-on — Numbers in Stories

## Activity 1 — The story-walk count (10 minutes)

Walk a short route you know — to the gate, the shop, the next room — and count your steps the way the anthology's stories count: whisper the ones, say the tens aloud; the tens are your stepping stones. If you pass 100, keep going, and note which ten you land on last. Afterwards write the count whole: "The walk is 143 steps — one hundred, four tens and three ones." This is the counting of Sub lesson 1 done on your own road.

## Activity 2 — Pair-and-add races (15 minutes)

Race the pairing against plain adding. Partner A adds 1 + 2 + … + 20 one by one; Partner B pairs — (1, 20), (2, 19), … — ten pairs of 21 — and writes 210. Swap jobs for 1 to 10 (55). Then both run 1 to 100 by pairing together: fifty pairs of 101, answer 5,050, and check three pairs at random by plain adding (1 + 100, 7 + 94, 25 + 76 — each must make 101). Finish by testing the edge: does the trick work on 1 to 50? (Yes — 25 pairs of 51, giving 1,275.)

## Activity 3 — Make the verse, read the book (15 minutes)

Pick the table fact you drop most (say 7 × 8 = 56) and make a two-line verse that lands on it — rhyme it with anything; the verse is a handle, not a poem for prizes. Chant the 2, 5 and 10 tables to a steady clap, then your own verse three times. Last, find a book with Roman chapter numbers, or a clock face with letters, and read every chapter number aloud to XII, writing each in figures beside it — the anthology's own closing exercise, done on a real book (Ecuador, *Matemática (Talento matemático 3)*, Grade 3 student book, closing "Yo Leo" anthology, printed pp. 210–224).
