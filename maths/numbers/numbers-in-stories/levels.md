# Numbers in Stories — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, expressed as its SUB
LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure — README, "Units, Lessons and
Sub Lessons"). One lesson, one level (Level 163) — that level
line is unchanged. The lesson was built new in the restructure
era (Ecuador sweep merge, 2026-11-08), so there are no old
depth layers to map: the sub lessons below are its depths as
built, cut at the anthology's own four contents — the count,
the Gauss sum, the verse, the Roman numerals. The questions
live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view.

Pass marks: the book offers its closing anthology as enrichment
reading and states no pass mark for it, so no mark is invented —
each sub lesson's pass mark is stated as the observable thing
the learner can do, the same form the counting lesson uses for
its book-unmarked bands.

## Sub lesson 1 — Counting to 100 in a Story
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: count to 100 in ones without losing the line, run the tens to 100 as stepping stones (10, 20, …, 100), and recover the count from the last ten when interrupted.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Young Gauss: Adding 1 to 100
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: pair 1 to 100 into fifty pairs of 101 and give the sum 5,050, name any number's partner (101 minus the number), and run the same pairing unaided on 1 to 10 (55).

## Sub lesson 3 — Times Tables in Verse
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: chant one full table (2s, 5s or 10s) in rhythm without a wrong landing, and state any three facts from the tables the verse carries.

## Sub lesson 4 — Roman Numerals in Books
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: read I, V, X, L and C at sight, read chapter-style numbers to XII, and write the numbers 1–10 in Roman letters.

## What "maxed" means here

The learner reads mathematics the way Ecuador's Grade 3 book closes its year: following a count to 100 inside a story, reproducing the young Gauss pairing and its 5,050, holding times tables by their verse, and reading the Roman numerals books and clocks actually use — the four contents of the closing "Yo Leo" anthology (*Talento matemático 3*, Grade 3 student book, printed pp. 210–224).
