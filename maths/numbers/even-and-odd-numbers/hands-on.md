# Hands-on — Even and Odd Numbers

## Activity 1 — Last-digit hunt (10 minutes)

Open any book, newspaper or bus timetable and find ten numbers. For each one, cover everything except the last digit, say "even" or "odd" aloud, then uncover and check with the full number: even numbers end in 0, 2, 4, 6, 8; odd numbers end in 1, 3, 5, 7, 9 — the test India's Class 3 book teaches on the 100 chart.

## Activity 2 — The ranges game (10 minutes)

Play the book's own hunts with a partner. One of you calls a range — "odd numbers between 400 and 410" — and the other lists them (401, 403, 405, 407, 409). Swap with "even numbers between 155 and 165" (156, 158, 160, 162, 164), then make up ranges of your own, some wide, some so narrow that only one number fits.

## Activity 3 — Predict the sum (10 minutes)

Write down six pairs of numbers — some even + odd, some odd + odd, some even + even. Before adding any pair, predict: will the sum be even or odd? Use the rules (odd + 1 = even; even + odd = odd; odd + odd = even). Then add and check your predictions. The game is won on the predictions, not the adding.
