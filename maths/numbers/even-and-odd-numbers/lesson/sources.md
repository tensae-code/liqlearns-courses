**Acceptance count: taught in 1 country (India).** India's Class 3 Math-Magic book is the one read book that gives even and odd numbers a named treatment of their own — defined by the last digit (even ends 0, 2, 4, 6, 8; odd ends 1, 3, 5, 7, 9), hunted in ranges (odds between 400 and 410, evens between 155 and 165), and finished with the adding rules (odd + 1 = even, even + 1 = odd, and the question even + odd left for the learner to settle) — so under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06) the topic gets its own lesson, built at the size of its source. The pairing way of meeting even and odd (pairing pictured objects, classifying sums) already lives inside the counting lesson from Egypt, North Macedonia and Pakistan (Punjab), and India's own Class 10 book returns to even and odd integers in proved form (every even integer is 2q, odd integers are 4q + 1 or 4q + 3, from Euclid's division lemma), stocked in factors-multiples-and-primes. Nothing here is cited from the Class 3 book's planted find-the-mistake pages (printed pp. 83, 196) — those are subtraction and money exercises, filed in their own lessons as the deliberate-error teaching they are.
