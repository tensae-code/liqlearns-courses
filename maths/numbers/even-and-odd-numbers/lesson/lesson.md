# Even and Odd Numbers

## You do it first

Look at these three numbers: 156, 263, 410. Which of them are even, and which are odd? Before you read on, decide what you would look at to tell.

Try it with this hint: you do not need the whole number. One digit is enough.

## The last-digit test

Every whole number is one of two kinds: **even** or **odd**. And you can tell which kind by looking at **only the last digit**:

- A number is **even** when its last digit is **0, 2, 4, 6 or 8**.
- A number is **odd** when its last digit is **1, 3, 5, 7 or 9**.

India's Class 3 book teaches it on the 100 chart: run your eye down the chart and the even numbers are exactly the ones ending in 0, 2, 4, 6, 8. The hundreds and tens in front do not matter at all — 410 is even because it ends in 0, and 1,263 is odd because it ends in 3. (India, Math-Magic Class 3, Ch. 10, printed p. 151.)

## Hunting in a range

Once the test is yours, you can hunt. The book asks for the **odd numbers between 400 and 410**: work through the last digits — 401, 403, 405, 407, 409. And the **even numbers between 155 and 165**: 156, 158, 160, 162, 164.

Evens and odds also come in runs, stepping by 2. The book leaves a gap to fill: 96, 98, __, 102 — the missing even number is **100**. (India, Math-Magic Class 3, Ch. 10, printed pp. 151–152.)

## What happens when you add

Adding 1 always flips the kind:

- **odd + 1 = even** (7 + 1 = 8)
- **even + 1 = odd** (10 + 1 = 11)

The book then asks its own question: what is **even + odd**? Try 4 + 7 = 11 — odd. It always lands odd: the even number pairs off completely, the odd number has one left over, and that one makes the sum odd. Its partner rule: **odd + odd = even** (7 + 5 = 12) — the two left-over ones pair up with each other. (India, Math-Magic Class 3, Ch. 10, printed p. 152.)

> **Tip — from us, not from the books:** if pairing is how you first met even and odd — objects in pairs with none left over are even, one left over is odd — the last-digit test is the same fact in shortcut form. India proves the shortcut on the 100 chart; both ways agree on every number.

## Where this leads

Even and odd come back at the top of the school course in proved dress: India's Class 10 book shows from Euclid's division lemma that every even integer is 2q and odd integers are 4q + 1 or 4q + 3 — that treatment lives in factors-multiples-and-primes. It is a soft hint only: you can start there directly whenever you want it.

## Say it to your companion

Explain to your companion, in your own words: how the last digit tells even from odd, how you listed the odds between 400 and 410, and why even + odd must come out odd. Then let them test you with the biggest number they can write down.

## Do It Yourself

1. Say whether each is even or odd, naming the last digit that decides it: 89, 240, 517, 1,006, 73,452.
2. List all the even numbers between 231 and 245, and all the odd numbers between 998 and 1,010.
3. Fill the gaps: (a) 45, 47, __, 51, __; (b) 300, __, 304, __, 308. Say before you start whether each run is the evens or the odds.
4. Predict first, then check by adding: (a) 26 + 39; (b) 81 + 47; (c) 150 + 152. Which sums came out even, and did your predictions match the rules?
