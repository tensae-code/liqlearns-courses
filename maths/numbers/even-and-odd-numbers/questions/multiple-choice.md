# Multiple choice — Even and Odd Numbers

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. 1. (Sub lesson 1) Which of these numbers is odd?
   A. 248 B. 390 C. 517 D. 1,006
   **Answer: C** — its last digit is 7, and odd numbers end in 1, 3, 5, 7 or 9. India, Math-Magic Class 3, printed p. 151.

2. (Sub lesson 1) The number 73,452 is:
   A. Odd, because it is big B. Even, because its last digit is 2 C. Odd, because its first digit is 7 D. Even, because it has 5 digits
   **Answer: B** — only the last digit decides: 2 is one of 0, 2, 4, 6, 8. India, Math-Magic Class 3, printed p. 151.

3. (Sub lesson 2) The odd numbers between 400 and 410 are:
   A. 402, 404, 406, 408 B. 401, 403, 405, 407, 409 C. 401, 404, 407, 410 D. 400, 403, 406, 409
   **Answer: B** — the numbers strictly between 400 and 410 whose last digit is 1, 3, 5, 7 or 9. India, Math-Magic Class 3, printed p. 152.

4. (Sub lesson 2) In the run 96, 98, __, 102, the missing number is:
   A. 99 B. 100 C. 101 D. 104
   **Answer: B** — the run steps by 2 through the even numbers, and 100 is the even number between 98 and 102. India, Math-Magic Class 3, printed p. 151.

5. (Sub lesson 3) Even + odd always gives:
   A. Even B. Odd C. Zero D. It depends on the numbers
   **Answer: B** — the book's own question on the page, settled: the odd number's one left over makes the sum odd. India, Math-Magic Class 3, printed p. 152.
