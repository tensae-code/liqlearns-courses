# Lesson plan — Even and Odd Numbers

**One thing:** telling even numbers from odd numbers by their last digit — even numbers end in 0, 2, 4, 6, 8 and odd numbers end in 1, 3, 5, 7, 9 — finding them inside a range, and knowing what happens when you add them.

**Stage:** Numbers (Stage 1). **Level 151** (India merge, 2026-11-07).

**Why this lesson exists:** India's Class 3 Math-Magic book (Ch. 10, printed pp. 151–152) is the one read book that teaches even and odd as a named topic with its own definition and rules. Everywhere else in the library the pair appears as an aside — pairing pictured objects inside counting (Egypt, North Macedonia, Pakistan), or in proved form at Class 10 inside the real-numbers work. A learner who looks for "even and odd numbers" should find the whole thing in one place, so under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06) it gets its own lesson. It stands alone: the counting lesson is a soft hint before it, never a gate.

## The climb

1. **The last-digit test** — look only at the last digit. Even numbers end in 0, 2, 4, 6, 8; odd numbers end in 1, 3, 5, 7, 9. On the 100 chart the even numbers are the ones ending in those five digits.
2. **Hunting in a range** — list the odd numbers between 400 and 410 (401, 403, 405, 407, 409) and the even numbers between 155 and 165 (156, 158, 160, 162, 164); continue a run: 96, 98, __, 102 (100).
3. **Adding rules** — odd + 1 = even and even + 1 = odd (one step always flips the kind); the book's question — even + odd = ? — settled: odd. And odd + odd = even.
