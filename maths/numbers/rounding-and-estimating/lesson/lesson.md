# Rounding-and-estimating — the lesson

Course: Maths · Stage: Stage 1 — Numbers · Lesson: Rounding-and-estimating
Built from: Jordan, Grade 2 Student Book Term 1 — Vietnam, Toán 3 and Toán 4 — Armenia, Mathematics 3 Part 1 — Egypt, Grade 2 Term 1 (old Discover series) — South Africa, Grade 6 Book 1 — Greece, Grade 4

## Do it first
A jar of stones on the table. How many stones are in it? Guess
— write the number down. Now count them. How close was your
guess? A guess you check is an estimate. This lesson is about
good guesses: numbers rounded to the nearest ten or hundred, and
sums estimated before they are worked out.

## Then the explanation
**Nearest ten.** Jordan's Grade 2 book teaches rounding numbers
as its own lesson (Student Book Term 1, L7, p.34). The rule: look
at the ones digit. Five or more rounds up; four or less rounds
down. On the number line, rounding is asking which ten the number
is nearest to.

47 rounds to 50 — the ones digit is 7, and 47 stands nearer 50
than 40. 43 rounds to 40 — the ones digit is 3, and 43 stands
nearer 40. 45 sits halfway, and halfway rounds up: 45 rounds to
50.

**Nearest hundred.** Vietnam's Grade 3 book teaches rounding to
tens and hundreds (Toán 3, Bài 48), and Armenia's Grade 3 book
teaches rounding a natural number to a given place (§7, p.28).
The rule is the same, one place up: look at the tens digit.

382 rounds to 400 — the tens digit is 8, and 382 stands nearer
400 than 300. 341 rounds to 300 — the tens digit is 4. 350 sits
halfway, and halfway rounds up: 350 rounds to 400.

**Nearest thousand.** Vietnam's Grade 4 book carries rounding to
thousands and ten-thousands (Toán 4, Bài 13), and South Africa's
Grade 6 Book 1 revises rounding off to the nearest 5, 10 and 100.
The rule never changes — only the place does:

4,736 rounds to 5,000 — the hundreds digit is 7. 4,281 rounds to
4,000 — the hundreds digit is 2.

**Estimating.** Egypt's Grade 2 book teaches estimating before
solving: estimate by place value, explicitly "Do not solve the
problems" (Chapter 3, p.52), then estimate from tens digits,
decompose, find the sum, and judge whether estimate and sum are
close (p.53). Jordan's Grade 2 book prints the same move inside
regrouping: first estimate 247 + 45 ≈ 250 + 50 = 300. Greece's
Grade 4 book teaches numbers to 20,000 with strategies and
estimation (printed pp.88–89).

Estimate first, then work it out, then check: if the exact answer
is far from the estimate, something went wrong — work it again.

## Say it to your companion
Your companion calls a number: "63." Round it to the nearest
ten: "60." They call "487": "500, to the nearest hundred."
Trade five times. Then the estimate round: they give you a sum,
"38 + 52" — estimate first: "about 90" — then work it out:
90. Exact.

## Do It Yourself
1. Round to the nearest ten: 23, 47, 68, 85, 94.
2. Round to the nearest hundred: 141, 382, 659, 704, 950.
3. Round to the nearest thousand: 2,481; 7,350; 9,620.
4. Estimate, then work out: 48 + 36; 73 − 29; 247 + 45.
