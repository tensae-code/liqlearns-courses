Vietnam carries rounding from tens and hundreds in Grade 3 to thousands and ten-thousands in Grade 4. Armenia carries rounding a natural number to a given place in Grade 3. Egypt carries estimating by place value before solving in Grade 2 Term 1, and the named rounding-to-nearest-10 rule with its number line in Grade 2 Term 2. South Africa revises rounding to the nearest 5, 10 and 100 in Grade 6. Greece carries estimation strategies for numbers to 20,000 in Grade 4. Other read countries teach rounding inside their own Grade 3–6 books and join by merge.** Laos carries rounding and estimating as its own Grade 4 chapter — approximate numbers and estimating sums — and continues it with estimating quotients and rounding decimal quotients in Grade 5. Yemen carries rounding from estimating sums and differences within 1000 in Grade 2, through rounding to the nearest ten, hundred and thousand within 10,000, to rounding within 999,999 and estimating all four operations. Indonesia carries estimating — in Grade 1 (numbers 11–20) and Grade 2 (to 100), and estimating to place values for rational-number operations in Grade 7 (Merdeka). No named rounding-to-a-place topic is filed for Indonesia: estimation for integer operations in Grade 7 Merdeka is a keyword only, so this lesson is partial for Indonesia. Nicaragua carries rounding to a named place — to the nearest thousand in the old Grade 3 guide, whole numbers and decimals in Grade 4 — and division rounding the quotient in Grades 5–6; estimation is taught as estimate-before-calculating in the Grade 2 guide and estimate-the-product in the old Grade 3 guide, while the 2025 Grade 2 and Grade 3 books teach no rounding or estimation as a taught skill, so Nicaragua's rounding sits in Grades 3–4, not in the 2025 Grade 2–3 books. Ethiopia carries rounding from estimating in tens in Grade 1, through approximate value in Grade 3 and rounding to the 10, 100 and 1000 places in Grade 4, to rounding decimals in Grade 6 (7.366 → 7.4; 5.633 → 5.63, round-half-up school convention). South Sudan carries rounding from the nearest ten and hundred in Grade 2 (324 → 320; 361 → 400), through estimation defined in Grade 3 ("a rough idea… without measuring") and rounding to the nearest thousands in Grade 4 (7421 → 7000; 8697 → 9000), to area estimation in Secondary 4 — grid counting, the mid-ordinate rule and the trapezium rule. One book slip is never taught as fact: the Grade 4 book's p. 11 wording "add a zero into the digit" is wrong phrasing — state the rule correctly (leave the thousands digit unchanged when the next digit is 0–4). Iraq carries rounding from the nearest ten in Grade 2 with estimate-or-exact, through rounding to the nearest ten and hundred in Grade 3 (150 → 200, half-up), to rounding to the nearest thousand with estimating sums and differences in Grade 4, and rounding to the nearest million with estimating sums and differences in Grade 5. Bangladesh carries this lesson only in part — estimating the quotient in Class 4 and approximate value with square roots in Class 8, plus estimating-then-measuring in Class 3. It teaches no rounding of a whole number to a named place (nearest ten, hundred or thousand) and no estimating of sums before solving, so the rounding rule itself is not covered by Bangladesh. Nepal carries rounding only in part — rounding to the nearest 10 in Grade 3, to the nearest 10 and 100 in Grade 4 (teach the 720 → 700 correction) and to the nearest ten, hundred and thousand in Grade 5, with rounding and truncation of decimals in Grade 6 — but it files no estimating of sums before solving, so the estimating half of this lesson is not covered by Nepal. Cambodia carries rounding and estimating only in part — rounding to the nearest 100, 1000 and million, and estimating decimal sums, products, differences and quotients; rounding to the nearest 10 and rounding to tens and hundreds as a general taught rule is not pinned anywhere in the set, and no rounding of decimals to a stated place beyond the Grade 7 estimating use. Kuwait carries rounding and estimating from estimating numbers to 100 in Grades 1–2, through rounding to the nearest ten and hundred in Grades 2–3 and estimating sums and differences in Grade 3, to rounding to millions, estimating division results and rounding decimals in Grade 4, with whole-number and decimal rounding and estimation revisited in Grades 5, 6 and 7.
