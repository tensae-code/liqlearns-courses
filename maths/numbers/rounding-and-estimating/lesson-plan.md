# Lesson plan — Rounding-and-estimating

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 1 — Numbers · **Lesson:** Rounding-and-estimating
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 5, the fourth lesson of Unit 1 (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** rounding numbers to the nearest ten, hundred and thousand, and making sensible estimates before calculating.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Comparing-and-ordering (Level 4) — the learner knows near and far on the number line. It needs nothing else.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Comparing-and-ordering (same stage) — rounding is saying which ten or hundred a number is nearest to. Recommended, never forced.
- A soft hint only: estimating is not guessing — it is a rough answer you can check against.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Round a number to the nearest ten (47 → 50) using the ones digit.
2. Round a number to the nearest hundred (382 → 400) and to the nearest thousand.
3. Estimate a sum or difference before working it out, and judge whether the exact answer is close (Egypt's Grade 2 estimation lessons).
4. Use rounding to check an answer: if the estimate and the answer are far apart, something is wrong.

## What you need
A number line 0–100 with the tens marked; number cards.
Paper and a pencil.

## If the learner is stuck
- Rounds 45 to 40 → five or more rounds up: 45 sits halfway, and the rule the books teach is that halfway rounds up — 45 rounds to 50.
- Rounds 382 to 300 → look at the tens digit, not the ones: 382 is nearer 400 than 300, because 82 is more than 50.
- Estimates by working out the exact answer first → estimate first, on rounded numbers: 247 + 45 is about 250 + 50 = 300, Jordan's own printed estimate.
