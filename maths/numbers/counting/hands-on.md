# Hands-on — Counting

The lesson counted on real things, the way every book starts it.

## Touch and count
Line up things — pencils, spoons, stones. Touch each one as you count it and slide it aside. Touched means counted; a thing is never counted twice. Count the line forward, then check it backward from the end.

## How-many hunt
Walk a room with a partner. They point and ask: "How many windows are there?" You count aloud, touching or ticking, and answer whole: "There are 2 windows." Ten questions each, then swap. Windows, doors, chairs, pictures, shoes — the room is the worksheet.

## Bundles of ten
Make ten bundles of ten sticks, each bundle tied with string. Count the bundles: ten, twenty, thirty … one hundred. Then untie one bundle and count its ten sticks one by one. Ten tens make one hundred — you can see it.

## Tens jumps
Stand. Jump and land on each ten: 10, 20, 30 … 100 — ten jumps, arms up at 100. Back down the line in jumps: 100, 90, 80 … 10. The body learns the tens line as landings.
