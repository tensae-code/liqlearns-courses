**Acceptance count: taught in 14 countries (Libya, Greece, Sierra Leone, Ghana, Kazakhstan, South Africa, Laos, Yemen, Indonesia, Nicaragua, Ethiopia, South Sudan, Iraq, Bangladesh). Libya carries the whole primary climb in one book — 1–10, then to 20, then to 100. Greece carries numbers to 20 with sums to 10 and coins. Sierra Leone carries counting to 100 sung and played, grouped in 10s — teacher evidence, since its Primary set is lesson plans, not a pupil book. Ghana carries the same climb as teacher evidence (counting 1–50, 1–100, in bundles) — its set holds no pupil textbook at all. Kazakhstan carries counting in tens inside forming two-digit numbers. South Africa carries counting checked against real things on beads. Other read countries teach counting inside their own Grade 1 books and join by merge.** Laos carries the whole primary climb in one series — 1–10 and 0, teens as 10 + n, then counting in tens to 100 with place value in Grade 1, and hundreds in Grade 2. Yemen carries the whole primary climb in one series — 0–10 with zero as the empty set, to 20, to 100, to 1000 in Grade 2, to 10,000 in Grade 3, to 999,999 in Grade 4, to hundreds of millions in Grade 5 and billions in Grade 6. Indonesia carries the whole primary climb in one series — counting to 10 and to 20 in Grade 1, to 50 and to 100 in Grade 2, whole numbers to 1,000 in Grade 3 and to 10,000 in Grade 4, then to 100,000 and to 1,000,000 in Grade 5 (reading and writing only at 1,000,000; operations are not extended there). Nicaragua carries the whole primary climb in two parallel series — numbers to 100 in Grade 1, to 1,000 in Grade 2 (ordinals to 20° in the old book only), to 10,000 in Grade 3, and beyond 10,000 in Grade 4. Ethiopia carries the whole primary climb in one bureau's series — counting to 20 in Grade 1, to 100 in Grade 1, to 1000 in Grade 2, to 10,000 in Grade 3 with Ethiopian (Ge'ez) numerals 1–10, and to 1,000,000 in Grade 4 with Ethiopian counting numerals to 100. South Sudan carries the whole primary climb in one series — counting 1–99 and in tens to 100 in Grade 1, 3-digit numbers in Grade 2, 4-digit numbers in Grade 3, 5-digit numbers in Grade 4, six-digit numbers in Grade 5 and numbers to a million in Grade 6, with natural numbers and place value again in Secondary 1. Iraq carries the whole primary climb in one series — numbers to 20 and to 99 in Grade 1, to 999 in Grade 2, to 9999 in Grade 3, to 9,999,999 in Grade 4, and within billions in Grade 5. Bangladesh carries the whole primary climb in one series — counting and numbers 1–10 with zero in Class 1, to 100 in Class 1 and to 1000 in Class 2, to 10,000 with Ayut and lakh in Class 3, to crore in Class 4, and tally marks, Roman numbers and the number line in Class 6.
