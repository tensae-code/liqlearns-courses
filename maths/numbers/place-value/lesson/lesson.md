# Place-value — the lesson

Course: Maths · Unit: Unit 1 — Numbers · Lesson: Place-value
Built from: Egypt, Grade 2 Term 1 (old Discover series) and Grade 3 Term 1 (new SPRIX series) — Armenia, Grades 2–3 — Jordan, Grades 1–2 and 4 — Kazakhstan, Grade 2 (Ospanov set) — South Africa, Grades 2–3 Workbooks

## Do it first
Count out 47 sticks. Now bundle them in tens — tie ten sticks
together, again and again. How many bundles did you make? Four.
How many sticks are left over? Seven. You have just done place
value: 47 is 4 tens and 7 ones. The bundles are the tens; the
leftovers are the ones.

## Sub lesson 1 — Place Value Tens and Ones
**Tens and ones.** Every two-digit number is tens and ones.
Armenia's Grade 2 book starts exactly here: two-digit numbers as
tens + ones — 18 = 1 ten + 8 (Revision, pp.4–7) — then place
analysis of numbers (p.16). Jordan's Grade 1 book builds the same
thing: ones and tens (p.30), representing two-digit numbers
(p.32), place value (p.34). Kazakhstan's Grade 2 book teaches
place-value components as its third lesson on two-digit numbers
(Ospanov set, L3), with graphic models of tens and ones (L4).

47 = 4 tens + 7 ones. 26 = 2 tens + 6 ones. 83 = 8 tens + 3 ones.

## Sub lesson 2 — Place Value Hundreds
**Hundreds.** Ten tens make a new place: the hundred. Egypt's
Grade 2 book teaches ones, tens and hundreds as a whole chapter
(old Discover series, Chapter 14, pp.26–43), and its Grade 3 book
works the example every learner meets first: 385 = 3 hundreds,
8 tens, 5 ones (new SPRIX series, Chapter 3, L1, p.30). Jordan's
Grade 2 book does the same climb: hundreds (p.8), numbers within
three places (p.10), place value (p.12). South Africa's Grade 2
and 3 Workbooks build hundreds the same way, in hundreds, tens
and units boxes — 100 + 40 + 9.

234 = 2 hundreds, 3 tens, 4 ones. 500 = 5 hundreds, 0 tens,
0 ones.

A zero in a place means that place is empty — but the place is
still there. Egypt's Grade 2 book teaches zero in the tens place
with 204 = two hundreds, 0 tens, 4 ones (Chapter 6, Part 2,
pp.72–75). 204 is not 24: the zero holds the tens place open.

## Sub lesson 3 — Place Value Thousands and Beyond
**Thousands and beyond.** Ten hundreds make a thousand, and the
places keep going in threes. Armenia's Grade 3 book teaches
four-digit numbers (§1, p.7), five- and six-digit numbers (§2,
p.10), writing a multi-digit number (§3, p.14) and number classes
(§5, p.21) — the classes are the groups of three: ones, thousands,
millions. Jordan's Grade 4 book teaches place value within
thousands (p.10) and within hundred-thousands, and Egypt's Grade 4
book carries place value to the milliards (Unit 1, folios 3–21).

3,456 = 3 thousands, 4 hundreds, 5 tens, 6 ones.

**Expanded form** is the number written as the sum of its places:

723 = 700 + 20 + 3.  5,048 = 5,000 + 40 + 8.

Tip — from us, not from the books: read a big number from the
left, but count its places from the right — the rightmost digit
is always the ones.

## Say it to your companion
Your companion shows you a number on digit cards. Read it
place by place: "3 hundreds, 8 tens, 5 ones — three hundred and
eighty-five." Then they show you 2,047. Read it: "2 thousands,
0 hundreds, 4 tens, 7 ones." Trade five times. Then build a
number they call — "four thousand, six hundred and nine" — with
the cards: 4, 6, 0, 9.

## Do It Yourself
1. Split these into tens and ones: 35, 68, 91, 24, 57.
2. Split these into hundreds, tens and ones: 472, 305, 690, 128.
3. Read these big numbers aloud, place by place: 4,321; 56,078;
   902,415.
4. Write these in expanded form: 846; 3,507; 62,019.
