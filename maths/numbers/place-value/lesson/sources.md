**Acceptance count: taught in 24 countries (Egypt, Armenia, Jordan, Kazakhstan, South Africa, Laos, Yemen, Indonesia, Nicaragua, Ethiopia, South Sudan, Iraq, Bangladesh, Nepal, Cambodia, Kuwait, North Macedonia, Iran, China, Pakistan, Syria, Saudi Arabia, Rwanda, Sudan).**. Egypt carries the full climb — ones, tens and hundreds in Grade 2, three-digit place value in Grade 3, big numbers to the milliards in Grade 4. Armenia carries tens and ones in Grade 2 and number classes to millions in Grade 3. Jordan carries place value from Grade 1 ones and tens to Grade 4 hundred-thousands. Kazakhstan carries place-value components with graphic models in Grade 2. South Africa carries hundreds, tens and units boxes in Grades 2–3. Other read countries teach place value inside their own Grade 2–3 books and join by merge.** Laos carries place value from tens-and-ones to 100 in Grade 1, through hundreds, expanded forms and numbers to 100000 in Grades 2–3, to millions and billions in Grade 5. Yemen carries place value from ones and tens in Grade 1 through expanded form to 1000, 10,000 and 999,999, then to hundreds of millions and billions. Indonesia carries place value from tens and ones in Grades 1–2, through three-digit place value in Grade 3 and place value to 10,000 in Grade 4, to ten-thousands and then to 1,000,000 in Grade 5. Nicaragua carries place value from tens and ones to 100 in Grade 1, through the hundreds table and expanded form to 1,000 in Grade 2 and to 10,000 in Grade 3, to the hundred-thousands table in Grade 4. Ethiopia carries place value from tens and ones in Grade 1, through 249 as 2 hundreds + 4 tens + 9 ones in Grade 2 and place value to 10,000 in Grade 3, to place value of 6-digit numbers in Grade 4. South Sudan carries place value from tens and ones in Grade 1 (12 = 1 ten, 2 ones), through hundreds, tens and ones with 734 in Grade 2 and the place-value table to thousands with expanded form 319 = 300 + 10 + 9 in Grade 3, to ten-thousands in Grade 4, hundred-thousands with 583,216 worked in Grade 5, and place value in groups (millions/thousands/ones) to a million in Grade 6, with place value again in Secondary 1. One book slip is never taught as fact: the Grade 4 book asks for 3647 in words but answers 3467 — teach the 3467 naming only. Iraq carries place value from tens and ones to 99 in Grade 1, through place value to 999 in Grade 2 and to 9999 in Grade 3, to place value within millions in Grade 4, and to place value within billions in Grade 5. Bangladesh carries place value from tens and ones in Class 1, through hundreds, tens and ones with thousands in Class 2 and place value up to Ayuts in Class 3, to lac, nijut and crore with South Asian commas in Class 4, and the decimal number system and place value in Class 6. Nepal carries place value from tens and ones in Grade 1, through place value to 1000 with blocks, abacus and expanded form in Grade 2 and to 9,99,999 with lakh in Grade 3, to crore in Grade 4 and 10 crore in Grade 5, and whole-number place value again in Grade 6. Cambodia carries place value from tens and ones in Grade 1 to 10 digits in Grade 6, with decimal place value in Grade 6. Kuwait carries place value up the whole ladder — tens and ones to 100 in Grade 1, expanded form of 3-digit numbers in Grade 2, 6-digit numbers in Grade 3, millions and decimal tenths and hundredths in Grade 4, and whole-number and decimal place value again in Grades 5 and 6. North Macedonia carries place value at Grade 1 (2022) — numbers to 20 and 30 taken apart into tens and ones, and counting by 10s to 100. Iran carries place value through its whole primary series — tens bundles and the tens-and-ones table in Grade 1, building through Grades 2 and 3, to millions in Grade 4 and billions in Grade 5.

China carries place value through the primary books — composition, reading and writing to 100 in Grade 1, and to 10,000 with the abacus and expanded form in Grade 2.

Pakistan (Punjab) carries place value through the primary climb — tens and ones in Grade 1, hundreds in Grade 2, to 100,000 in Grades 3–4 and to a billion in the Urdu-medium Class 5 book — with decimal place value in Class 7 and binary, octal and hexadecimal place values in the Class 12 statistics book's computers chapter.

Syria carries place value grade by grade — ten-rods and ones-cubes and the place-value table in Grade 1, hundreds and the expanded form in Grade 2, four places in Grade 3, five places in Grade 4, periods of three to the millions in Grade 5 and to the billions in Grade 6, and digit-value work into the billions in Grade 7.

Saudi Arabia builds place value one place per grade — tens in Grade 1 Part Two, hundreds and the expanded form in Grade 2 Part Two, digit-value in a table and the periods in Grade 3, and numbers within millions in Grade 4 — with the older 1445 Term-3 volumes witnessing the Part Two chapters. Kuwait gains further practice evidence from the teacher-made Grade 3 booklet misfiled in Saudi Arabia's record: block, abacus, word-form and expanded-form exercises on numbers to 1000 (PDF pp. 3–12, 35–42), cited as a Kuwaiti school booklet, not as a textbook.

Rwanda teaches place value up the whole primary ladder — hundreds, tens and ones to 999 in Year 2, on to 10,000 in Year 3, to 100,000 in P4, to 1,000,000 in P5 and beyond 1,000,000 in P6 — and returns in Senior 1 with large natural numbers and decimal place value in expanded form.

Sudan builds place value in Grade 1 — tens and ones on the abacus and place chart, in blocks to 100 — then the hundreds and numbers to 1000 in Grade 2, four-digit numbers in Grade 3, and numbers to hundreds of thousands and to millions in Grade 4.
