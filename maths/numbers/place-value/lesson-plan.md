# Lesson plan — Place-value

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 1 — Numbers · **Lesson:** Place-value
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3, the second lesson of Unit 1 (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** place value — what each digit in a number is worth: ones, tens, hundreds, thousands, and reading big numbers.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Counting (Level 2) — the learner can count to 100 and count in tens. It needs nothing else.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before

1. **Place Value Tens and Ones** — splitting a two-digit number into tens and ones, and building one back. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Place Value Hundreds** — reading and writing three-digit numbers as hundreds, tens and ones, including a zero in a place. (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **Place Value Thousands and Beyond** — reading big numbers by their places to millions, and writing a number in expanded form. (Was depth Level 3.)

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Counting (same unit) — place value is what the counted tens and ones are called. Recommended, never forced.
- A soft hint only: nothing here is adding or taking away — that is Unit 2's home.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Split any two-digit number into tens and ones (47 = 4 tens + 7 ones), the way Armenia's Grade 2 book teaches it (18 = 1 ten + 8).
2. Read and write three-digit numbers as hundreds, tens and ones (385 = 3 hundreds, 8 tens, 5 ones — Egypt's worked example).
3. Read big numbers by their places — thousands, ten-thousands, hundred-thousands, millions (Armenia's number classes; Jordan's place value within thousands).
4. Write a number in expanded form and build it back (723 = 700 + 20 + 3).

## What you need
Bundles of ten sticks (or straws) and single sticks; digit cards 0–9.
Paper and a pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner counts 47 sticks, bundles them in tens, and sees what is left (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — tens and ones; hundreds; thousands and beyond; expanded form (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE at the tens-and-ones part: a picture of 4 bundles of ten sticks and 7 single sticks, labelled 47 → the student counts the bundles, then the singles, and says "4 tens and 7 ones".
   - ADD IMAGE under hundreds: a picture of a hundreds tray, a tens tray and a ones tray holding 3, 8 and 5 counters, labelled 385 → the student reads each tray's worth aloud.

3. **Say it to your companion** — read numbers aloud by their places and build numbers your companion calls (lesson/lesson.md).

4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — split five numbers into places, read three big numbers, and build numbers from digit cards (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Reads 47 as "four seven" → the bundles fix it: 4 bundles of ten is forty, then 7 ones — "forty-seven".
- Mixes up 305 and 350 → the zero holds a place: 305 is 3 hundreds, 0 tens, 5 ones; 350 is 3 hundreds, 5 tens, 0 ones. Egypt's Grade 2 book teaches zero in the tens place with 204.
- Loses the place in big numbers → read in groups of three from the right: thousands, then the rest. Armenia's number classes do exactly this.
- This lesson's next door: Comparing-and-ordering (same unit) — the biggest place decides which number is bigger.
