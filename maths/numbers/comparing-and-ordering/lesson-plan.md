# Lesson plan — Comparing-and-ordering

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 1 — Numbers · **Lesson:** Comparing-and-ordering
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4, the third lesson of Unit 1 (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** comparing numbers — bigger, smaller, equal — with the signs > < =, and ordering numbers on the number line.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Place-value (Level 3) — the learner reads numbers by their places. It needs nothing else.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Place-value (same stage) — the biggest place decides which number is bigger. Recommended, never forced.
- A soft hint only: the number line itself is a counting tool from the Counting lesson.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Compare two numbers with > , < and = , and say the comparison in words ("47 is greater than 39").
2. Compare by the biggest place first — tens before ones, hundreds before tens (Armenia's "stronger digit" rule).
3. Order three or more numbers from smallest to biggest and biggest to smallest.
4. Place numbers on the number line and use it to compare.

## What you need
A number line 0–100 (drawn on paper or on the floor); number cards.
Paper and a pencil.

## If the learner is stuck
- Writes the sign backwards → the mouth always eats the bigger number: the open end faces the bigger number, the sharp end points to the smaller. Armenia's Grade 2 book says the same: the sign's sharp end points to the smaller number.
- Compares 98 and 102 by the first digit only → count the places first: 102 has three places, 98 has two — the longer number is bigger when the places differ.
- Orders 29, 31, 27 by the ones digit → compare the tens first: 27, 29, 31 — the tens are 2, 2, 3, then the ones split the tie.
- This lesson's next door: Rounding-and-estimating (same stage) — a rounded number is a near number, and near needs comparing.
