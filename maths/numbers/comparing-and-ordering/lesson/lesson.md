# Comparing-and-ordering — the lesson

Course: Maths · Stage: Stage 1 — Numbers · Lesson: Comparing-and-ordering
Built from: Armenia, Grades 2–4 — Libya, Grade 1 — Egypt, Grade 2 Term 1 (old Discover series) — Kazakhstan, Grade 2 (Ospanov set) — Greece, Grade 4 — Jordan, Grade 4

## Do it first
Two piles of stones on the table. Which pile is bigger? Do not
count — just look. You can tell: the bigger pile takes up more
room. Comparing starts with looking. Counting comes second, to
check what your eyes already know.

## Then the explanation
**Bigger, smaller, equal.** Libya's Grade 1 book teaches
comparing right after counting to 20: bigger than and smaller
than (Unit 8, p.85), then biggest and smallest (p.87) — and again
after counting to 100: comparing numbers (Unit 16, p.64). Two
numbers are either equal, or one is bigger:

47 > 39 means "47 is greater than 39". 39 < 47 means "39 is less
than 47". 52 = 52 means "52 is equal to 52".

Tip — from us, not from the books: the sign is a mouth. The open
end eats the bigger number; the sharp end points to the smaller
number.

**The biggest place decides.** Armenia's Grade 2 book teaches the
rule plainly: compare tens first — "the stronger digit" — then
ones (p.8). The sign's sharp end points to the smaller number.
Armenia's Grade 3 book carries the same rule to multi-digit
numbers: comparing natural numbers (§6, p.24), and Grade 4
compares multi-digit numbers again (§2, p.13). Kazakhstan's Grade
2 book teaches comparing two-digit numbers as its fifth lesson on
two-digit numbers (Ospanov set, L5). Egypt's Grade 2 book compares
by the largest place value (Chapter 6, Part 9, pp.94–95).

Compare 47 and 39: the tens are 4 and 3 — 4 beats 3, so 47 is
bigger. The ones never get a vote. Compare 128 and 182: the
hundreds tie at 1 — so the tens decide: 2 against 8, so 128 is
smaller. Compare 98 and 102: different lengths — 102 has three
places and 98 has two, so 102 is bigger before any digit is read.

**Ordering** is comparing more than two numbers at once: smallest
to biggest, or biggest to smallest.

27, 29, 31 — smallest to biggest. 31, 29, 27 — biggest to
smallest. Jordan's Grade 4 book teaches comparing numbers inside
place value within thousands (p.10), and Greece's Grade 4 book
teaches names, comparison and ordering for numbers to 200,000
(printed pp.92–93).

**The number line** shows comparing as position: bigger numbers
live to the right, smaller numbers to the left. Armenia's Grade 2
book teaches series of two-digit numbers on the number line, with
unit steps and locating numbers on a line (p.38). If 47 stands to
the right of 39 on the line, 47 is bigger — and the line never
lies.

## Say it to your companion
Your companion shows you two number cards. Say the comparison
whole: "56 is greater than 49." Then they show you four cards:
order them aloud, smallest to biggest. Trade five times. Then
the line round: they call a number, you point to where it lives
on the number line — left or right of the number already there?

## Do It Yourself
1. Compare with > , < or = : 34 __ 43; 87 __ 78; 105 __ 99;
   246 __ 264; 1,005 __ 999.
2. Order smallest to biggest: 58, 23, 91, 47, 36.
3. Order biggest to smallest: 312, 128, 405, 296.
4. Draw a number line 0–100 and mark 17, 42, 68, 85 on it.
