**Acceptance count: taught in 14 countries (Armenia, Libya, Egypt, Kazakhstan, Greece, Jordan, Laos, Yemen, Indonesia, Nicaragua, Ethiopia, South Sudan, Iraq, Bangladesh). Libya carries comparing from the first count — bigger and smaller at 20, comparing at 100. Armenia carries the rule itself — compare the stronger digit first — from two-digit numbers in Grade 2 to multi-digit numbers in Grade 4. Egypt carries comparing by the largest place value in Grade 2. Kazakhstan carries comparing as its fifth two-digit lesson in Grade 2. Greece carries comparison and ordering to 200,000 in Grade 4. Jordan carries comparing numbers within thousands in Grade 4. Other read countries teach comparing inside their own Grade 1–4 books and join by merge.** Laos carries comparing from the first count — one-to-one matching, then <, > and = in Grade 1 — through ordering whole numbers, decimals and fractions in Grade 5 to comparing integers on the number line in Grade 6. Yemen carries comparing and ordering from <, > and = within 10 in Grade 1 through 1000, 10,000 and 999,999 to hundreds of millions in Grade 5. Indonesia carries comparing and ordering from comparing groups and numbers to 20 in Grade 1, through three numbers and to 100 in Grade 2, to 1,000 in Grade 3, 10,000 in Grade 4 and 1,000,000 in Grade 5, then comparing integers and rationals in Grade 7 (Merdeka) and comparing integers and fractions in Grade 7 (K13 Semester 1). Nicaragua carries comparing and ordering from to 10 in Grade 1, through 3-digit numbers in Grade 2 and to 10,000 in Grade 3, beyond 10,000 and to decimals in Grade 4, to fractions in Grade 5, and to integers and rationals in Grade 7. Ethiopia carries comparing and ordering from numbers to 20 in Grade 1, through comparing to 1000 by Ethiopian city distances in Grade 2 and to 10,000 in Grade 3, to comparing 54,367 with 54,369 in Grade 4. South Sudan carries comparing and ordering from ascending and descending order of 1–10 in Grade 1, through ordering 3-digit numbers in Grade 2 and comparing and ordering to 4 digits in Grade 3, to 5 digits in Grade 4 (4263 > 4193), six-digit numbers in Grade 5, and ordering to a million in Grade 6 (Unity 645,465 vs Eastern Equatoria 962,716). Two things are not cited here: the Grade 1 printed answer rows omit 10 from both orders — include 10 — and the Grade 6 Exercise 2 area question mixes square metres with square miles (both should be square kilometres), so it is not used; the Grade 3 guide's ordering keys for Activities 15–16 contain errors, so only the student book is cited. Iraq carries comparing and ordering from to 9 and to 20 in Grade 1 — ordinal numbers taught before comparing to 20 — through comparing and ordering to 999 in Grade 2 and to 9999 in Grade 3, to comparing and ordering within millions in Grade 4. Bangladesh carries comparing from sight and touch before numbers in Class 1, through comparison by blocks and by place value with ordering in Class 2 and < and > in Class 3, to comparison, ordering, largest/smallest from digit cards and successor/predecessor in Class 4, and ordering integers in Class 6. One English-only misprint is never taught as fact: the Class 2 comparison lesson says "3 blocks of tens more in 325" (correct: in 354, printed p. 16) — the Bangla original is correct; teach corrected.
