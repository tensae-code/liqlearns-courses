**Acceptance count: taught in 11 countries (Armenia, Libya, Egypt, Kazakhstan, Greece, Jordan, Laos, Yemen, Indonesia, Nicaragua, Ethiopia). Libya carries comparing from the first count — bigger and smaller at 20, comparing at 100. Armenia carries the rule itself — compare the stronger digit first — from two-digit numbers in Grade 2 to multi-digit numbers in Grade 4. Egypt carries comparing by the largest place value in Grade 2. Kazakhstan carries comparing as its fifth two-digit lesson in Grade 2. Greece carries comparison and ordering to 200,000 in Grade 4. Jordan carries comparing numbers within thousands in Grade 4. Other read countries teach comparing inside their own Grade 1–4 books and join by merge.** Laos carries comparing from the first count — one-to-one matching, then <, > and = in Grade 1 — through ordering whole numbers, decimals and fractions in Grade 5 to comparing integers on the number line in Grade 6. Yemen carries comparing and ordering from <, > and = within 10 in Grade 1 through 1000, 10,000 and 999,999 to hundreds of millions in Grade 5. Indonesia carries comparing and ordering from comparing groups and numbers to 20 in Grade 1, through three numbers and to 100 in Grade 2, to 1,000 in Grade 3, 10,000 in Grade 4 and 1,000,000 in Grade 5, then comparing integers and rationals in Grade 7 (Merdeka) and comparing integers and fractions in Grade 7 (K13 Semester 1). Nicaragua carries comparing and ordering from to 10 in Grade 1, through 3-digit numbers in Grade 2 and to 10,000 in Grade 3, beyond 10,000 and to decimals in Grade 4, to fractions in Grade 5, and to integers and rationals in Grade 7. Ethiopia carries comparing and ordering from numbers to 20 in Grade 1, through comparing to 1000 by Ethiopian city distances in Grade 2 and to 10,000 in Grade 3, to comparing 54,367 with 54,369 in Grade 4.
