# Lesson plan — Factors-multiples-and-primes

**Course:** Maths · **Stage:** Stage 1 — Numbers · **Lesson:** Factors-multiples-and-primes
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 6, the fifth lesson of Stage 1 — the last lesson of the stage (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** factors, multiples and prime numbers — what divides a number, what a number divides into, and the numbers with only two factors — with HCF and LCM.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Counting (Level 2) and Multiplication is not needed yet — factors are found by dividing, and dividing here is sharing, which the learner already does with real things. It needs nothing else.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Counting (same stage) — factors are found by counting in steps. Recommended, never forced.
- A soft hint only: the multiplication tables (Stage 3) make factors faster, but this lesson finds them by sharing and listing.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. List the factors of a number (the factors of 12 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12).
2. List multiples of a number (multiples of 3 are 3, 6, 9, 12 …).
3. Tell prime numbers from composite numbers (7 is prime; 12 is composite; 1 is neither).
4. Find the HCF of two numbers and the LCM of two numbers, and know which one a problem needs.

## What you need
Counters or stones; a 1–100 grid (drawn on paper).
Paper and a pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner shares 12 stones into equal piles in every way that works (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — factors; multiples; prime and composite; HCF; LCM (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE at the factors part: a picture of 12 stones shared into equal piles — 1 pile of 12, 2 piles of 6, 3 piles of 4 — and the piles that do not share evenly crossed out → the student lists the factors from the piles that work.
   - ADD IMAGE under primes: a picture of a 1–100 grid with the prime numbers coloured → the student reads the coloured numbers aloud.
3. **Say it to your companion** — list factors and multiples your companion calls, and test numbers for prime (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the factors chant in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — list factors and multiples for five numbers, test five numbers for prime, and find one HCF and one LCM (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Misses a factor of 12 → list factor pairs, not single factors: 1 × 12, 2 × 6, 3 × 4 — each pair gives two factors, and the pairs stop when they meet.
- Calls 1 prime → 1 has only one factor (itself), so it is neither prime nor composite. Egypt's Grade 5 book says it plainly: 1 is neither.
- Mixes up HCF and LCM → HCF is the biggest factor they share (it divides both); LCM is the smallest multiple they share (both divide it). Egypt's Grade 5 book teaches choosing between them in word problems.
- This lesson's next door: Addition (Stage 2) — the next stage starts with adding, and factors return whenever fractions need a common denominator.
