**Acceptance count: taught in 27 countries (Egypt, Armenia, Jordan, Libya, Kazakhstan, Greece, Vietnam, South Africa, Laos, Yemen, Indonesia, Nicaragua, Ethiopia, South Sudan, Iraq, Bangladesh, Nepal, Cambodia, Kuwait, Iran, China, Pakistan, Syria, Saudi Arabia, Malaysia, Rwanda, Sudan). Egypt carries the whole topic twice — prime and composite with GCF in Grade 4, prime factorisation with GCF and LCM in Grade 5, including choosing between them in word problems. Armenia carries divisibility whole in Grade 5 — divisor and multiple, GCD, LCM, primes, factorisation. Jordan carries factors and multiples with GCF and LCM in Grade 5. Libya carries the topic in Grade 7 — prime factors, HCF, multiples, LCM. Kazakhstan carries GCD and LCM in Grade 5. Greece generalises powers, GCD/LCM and primes in Grade 6. Vietnam carries divisors, multiples, primes, GCD and LCM in Grade 6. South Africa carries primes first as a coloured 1–100 grid in Grade 5 and prime/composite with factors of 1,000,000 in Grade 6. Other read countries teach factors inside their own Grade 5–7 books and join by merge.** Laos carries multiples and factors in Grade 4, primes to 19 with divisibility rules in Grade 5, and the full GCD/LCM course — listing, Euclidean algorithm and factorisation — in Grade 6. Yemen carries divisibility rules for 2, 5, 10 and 3 in Grade 4, then factors, primes, prime factorisation, GCD and LCM in Grade 5. Indonesia carries factors and multiples first in Grade 4, the full LCM/GCD course — multiples, factors, primes, the Sieve of Eratosthenes, prime factorisation and LCM/GCD by listing, factorisation and repeated division — in Grade 5, and FPB/KPK again with prime factorisation in Grade 7 (Merdeka and K13 Semester 1). Two book errors are never taught as fact: the Grade 5 student book's worked FPB(75, 150) = 6 on printed p. 59 (the true value is 75), and both Grade 5 books' glossaries define KPK as the greatest common multiple (KPK is the smallest common multiple; the chapter teaching on student p. 40 is correct). The Grade 7 K13 successive-division ladder for FPB(24, 48, 72) on printed p. 97 gives 12; the true value is 24. Nicaragua carries multiples, divisors, LCM, GCD and primes in Grade 5, prime factorisation with GCD and LCM by the prime-factor method in Grade 6, and divisibility criteria with LCM/GCD again in the Grade 7 module. Ethiopia carries the whole topic in Grade 6 — divisibility rules for 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9 and 10, even and odd numbers, multiples and factors, prime and composite numbers with 1 neither prime nor composite, prime factorisation with factor trees, and HCF and LCM by listing and by prime factorisation; Grade 9 revises the whole topic — divisibility tests, primes, prime factorisation, GCF and LCM — in its number-system unit. South Sudan carries divisibility tests for 2, 5 and 10 in Grade 3, multiples and factors in Grade 4, divisibility tests for 3, 4, 6 and 9 with primes, factors and HCF/LCM in Grade 5, divisibility tests of 8 and 11 in Grade 6, factors and multiples including negatives in Grade 8, and factors with prime/composite numbers and HCF/LCM by prime factors in Secondary 1. One book slip is never taught as fact: the Grade 6 book's p. 5 divisibility-of-8 example tests by 4 on the wrong digits mid-working — cite the rule, not that working. Iraq carries divisibility by 2, 3, 5 and 10 with factors and multiples in Grade 4, then primes, factorisation in exponential form, perfect squares and cubes, and GCD and LCM in Grade 5. Bangladesh carries the whole topic from Class 4 — multiples, factors, LCM, HCF, primes with the sieve to 100 and prime factorisation with divisibility by 2, 3 and 5 — through LCM, GCF and factor trees in Class 5, divisibility rules, prime factorisation, the HCF game and the Euclidean method in Class 6, to GCD and LCM of fractions and of algebraic expressions in Class 7. Nepal carries the whole topic from prime and composite numbers with prime factors in Grade 4 — teach the p. 23 list corrected, 1 is not prime — through prime factorization in Grade 5, to factors, multiples, prime factorization, HCF and LCM in Grade 6 (teach the common prime factors of 450 and 240 corrected: 2, 3 and 5), and HCF and LCM again in Grade 7. Grade 3 teaches no prime numbers. Cambodia carries factors, multiples, GCD and LCM by prime factorisation in Grades 6–7 and again in Grade 12 Advanced, with the prime definition there; an explicit prime-versus-composite classification and any prime-listing or sieve is not pinned in the set, and the Grade 12 chapter does not teach prime factorisation as a method. Kuwait carries factors first inside its Grade 4 division unit (exploring factors; divisibility by 2, 5 and 10) and then as its own Grade 6 number-theory unit — factors, multiples, prime and composite numbers, and GCF and LCM in simple form. Iran carries the topic from divisibility in Grade 6 through divisors, highest common factor and least common multiple in Grade 7 and the sieve of Eratosthenes in Grade 8, up to divisibility and congruences in Grade 12 Discrete Mathematics.

China carries factors, multiples and primes whole in Grade 5 Volume 2 — definitions, the divisibility rules for 2, 5 and 3, primes and the sieve, the highest common factor and the lowest common multiple.

Pakistan (Punjab) carries factors, multiples and primes in Grades 4–6 — divisibility rules and prime factorisation in Grade 4, the HCF and LCM methods in the Class 5 book, and the full treatment with the sieve of Eratosthenes in Class 6.

Syria builds factors in two passes — multiples, fact families and the least common multiple by listing in Grades 4–5, then the full prime-factorisation machinery in Grade 6: divisibility rules, ladder factorisation, GCD and LCM from factorisations, coprime numbers — and returns in Grade 9 Algebra with divisors of integers, the GCD's properties, and the successive-subtraction and Euclid algorithms.

Saudi Arabia introduces factors and multiples inside its Grade 4 multiplication chapter — every factor pair of a number listed, multiples read off the table, and the two-way fact that a number is a multiple of each of its factors stated plainly.

Malaysia carries factors and multiples whole in Form 1 — factors, prime factorisation and HCF first, then multiples and LCM — with all three methods (listing, prime factorisation, repeated division) taught for both HCF and LCM.

Rwanda teaches factors, multiples and primes from P4 — odd and even numbers, multiples, factors, prime and composite numbers, and LCM by listing and by the prime-factor ladder — through prime factorisation and the divisibility tests for 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10, 11 and 12 in P5, to LCM and GCF by listing and by prime factorisation, with word problems and the product rule, in P6.

Sudan meets even and odd numbers in Grade 1 (a set shared into two equal groups is even), multiples in Grade 3, and gives Grade 4 a full divisibility suite — primes, tests for 2, 3, 5 and 10, prime factorisation, HCF and LCM — extended in Grade 5 by discovery lessons for 9, 4, 8 and 6, and returns to prime factorisation in Grade 9.
