**Acceptance count: taught in 11 countries (Egypt, Armenia, Jordan, Libya, Kazakhstan, Greece, Vietnam, South Africa, Laos, Yemen, Indonesia). Egypt carries the whole topic twice — prime and composite with GCF in Grade 4, prime factorisation with GCF and LCM in Grade 5, including choosing between them in word problems. Armenia carries divisibility whole in Grade 5 — divisor and multiple, GCD, LCM, primes, factorisation. Jordan carries factors and multiples with GCF and LCM in Grade 5. Libya carries the topic in Grade 7 — prime factors, HCF, multiples, LCM. Kazakhstan carries GCD and LCM in Grade 5. Greece generalises powers, GCD/LCM and primes in Grade 6. Vietnam carries divisors, multiples, primes, GCD and LCM in Grade 6. South Africa carries primes first as a coloured 1–100 grid in Grade 5 and prime/composite with factors of 1,000,000 in Grade 6. Other read countries teach factors inside their own Grade 5–7 books and join by merge.** Laos carries multiples and factors in Grade 4, primes to 19 with divisibility rules in Grade 5, and the full GCD/LCM course — listing, Euclidean algorithm and factorisation — in Grade 6. Yemen carries divisibility rules for 2, 5, 10 and 3 in Grade 4, then factors, primes, prime factorisation, GCD and LCM in Grade 5. Indonesia carries factors and multiples first in Grade 4, the full LCM/GCD course — multiples, factors, primes, the Sieve of Eratosthenes, prime factorisation and LCM/GCD by listing, factorisation and repeated division — in Grade 5, and FPB/KPK again with prime factorisation in Grade 7 (Merdeka and K13 Semester 1). Two book errors are never taught as fact: the Grade 5 student book's worked FPB(75, 150) = 6 on printed p. 59 (the true value is 75), and both Grade 5 books' glossaries define KPK as the greatest common multiple (KPK is the smallest common multiple; the chapter teaching on student p. 40 is correct). The Grade 7 K13 successive-division ladder for FPB(24, 48, 72) on printed p. 97 gives 12; the true value is 24.
