# Hands-on — Factors-multiples-and-primes

The lesson shared stones, coloured a grid, and marched multiples.

## Share the stones
12 stones. Share them into equal piles in every way that works: 1 pile of 12, 2 piles of 6, 3 piles of 4, 4 piles of 3, 6 piles of 2, 12 piles of 1. The pile sizes are the factors: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12. Try it with 18 and with 20.

## Colour the primes
A 1–100 grid. Colour the prime numbers — South Africa's Grade 5 book does exactly this. Cross out 1 (neither). Cross out the even numbers past 2, the multiples of 3 past 3, the multiples of 5 past 5. What is left coloured is prime.

## Multiples march
Stand in a line. Count by threes aloud, stepping forward on each multiple: 3, 6, 9, 12 … Then by fours. The numbers where both marches land are the common multiples of 3 and 4 — the first is 12, the LCM.

## HCF hunt
Your partner gives you two numbers — 12 and 18. List both factor lists. Circle the factors they share: 1, 2, 3, 6. The biggest circled one is the HCF: 6.
