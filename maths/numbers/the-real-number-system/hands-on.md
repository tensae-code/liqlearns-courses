# Hands-on — The Real Number System

The lesson drew the number families as rings on paper. Here your body and your cards draw them.

## Rings on the floor
Draw five chalk rings, one inside the next, or lay five hoops the same way. Label them N, W, Z, Q, R from the inside out. A partner reads a number card aloud; you stand in the SMALLEST ring that holds it — 7 stands in N, 0 in W, −3 in Z, 1/2 in Q, and √2 must step all the way out to R. Ten cards, then swap.

## Law-checking bench
Take the distributive law to the bench with real things: three bags, each holding 4 red stones and 5 blue stones. Count all the stones two ways — bags first (3 × 9), then colours first (3 × 4 + 3 × 5). Both counts give 27. The law is the two counts agreeing; Pakistan's Grade 5 book verifies it exactly this way with whole numbers (pp.13–15).

## Inverse hunt
On a long number line on the floor, a partner stands on a number. You must stand on its additive inverse — the same distance from 0 on the other side — then on a second call find where its multiplicative inverse sits (between 0 and 1 for a whole number bigger than 1). Zero is the trap card: hunt for its multiplicative inverse and report why the hunt must fail.

## Broken-law detective
Cards carry claims: "7 − 3 = 3 − 7", "(2 + 3) + 4 = 2 + (3 + 4)", "5 ÷ 2 = 2 ÷ 5", "a × 1 = a". Test each with numbers, the way Pakistan's Grade 6 book tests the integer laws (pp.32–33, 38–39). Stamp each card HOLDS or BROKEN, with the test written on the back.
