# Lesson plan — The Real Number System

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 1 — Numbers · **Lesson:** The Real Number System
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 55 of 136, inside Unit 1 — Numbers (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** all the numbers you know live in one system — naturals inside whole numbers inside integers inside rationals inside reals, with irrationals filling the rest of the line — and the real numbers obey named laws: closure, commutative, associative, distributive, identities and inverses.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Unit 1 number lessons (Counting, Place Value, Negative Numbers, Fractions, Decimals) — the learner has met every family before seeing the whole map. It needs nothing else.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Negative Numbers (Unit 1) — the integers family is one ring of this lesson's map. Recommended, never forced.
- Recommended first: Fractions and Decimals (Unit 1 / Unit 4) — rationals are the family built from fractions. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Draw the nesting N ⊂ W ⊂ Z ⊂ Q ⊂ R and place any given number in its smallest family (Pakistan, Grade 8, Unit 1, printed pp.1–3).
2. Say what makes a number irrational — a non-terminating, non-recurring decimal — and give π and √2 as examples, knowing π ≠ 22/7 (Pakistan, Grade 8, Unit 1, printed pp.1–3).
3. State the laws of operations for real numbers — closure, commutative, associative, distributive, additive and multiplicative identities, additive and multiplicative inverses — and check each one on numbers (Pakistan, Grade 8, Unit 1, printed pp.6–9).
4. Verify the distributive property over addition and subtraction with whole numbers (Pakistan, Grade 5, printed pp.13–15).
5. Verify the commutative and associative properties for fraction addition and fraction multiplication (Pakistan, Grade 5, printed pp.31–38).
6. Verify the commutative and associative laws for integer addition and multiplication, name the additive identity 0 and multiplicative identity 1, and show where subtraction breaks the pattern (Pakistan, Grade 6, printed pp.32–33, 38–39).
7. Verify the commutative, associative and distributive properties for rational numbers (Pakistan, Grade 7, printed pp.16–19).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Five hoops or chalk rings, one inside the next, for the family map.
Number cards: 7, 0, −3, 1/2, 0.75, √2, π, −0.5, 100.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner sorts the number cards into piles by kind, names each pile, then tries to draw the piles as rings inside rings (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the family map N ⊂ W ⊂ Z ⊂ Q ⊂ R; the irrationals; π ≠ 22/7; then the laws, family by family: whole numbers, fractions, integers, rationals, reals (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the map part: a picture of rings growing one inside the next on a number line — naturals first, then whole numbers with zero joining, then integers spreading left, then rationals filling between, then irrationals lighting the remaining points — the student names each new family as its ring appears.
   - ADD IMAGE under the laws: a picture of a balance with 3 × (4 + 5) on one side and 3 × 4 + 3 × 5 on the other, both pans level at 27 → the student says the distributive law in words from the picture.
   - ADD IMAGE in the inverses part: a picture of a number line with 5 and −5 standing the same distance from 0, joined by an arc → the student points to the additive inverse of three more numbers.
3. **Say it to your companion** — name a number, your companion names its smallest family and one law it obeys, then swap (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the number-families chant in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — draw the family map from memory, place ten numbers of your own choosing, and verify one law on rationals with working shown (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks every decimal is rational → Pakistan's Grade 8 test: a rational's decimal terminates or recurs; an irrational's never ends and never repeats (Unit 1, pp.1–3). Check the decimal, not the look of the number.
- Thinks π = 22/7 exactly → the book stresses π ≈ 22/7 only: 22/7 is a rational close to π, it is not π (Grade 8, Unit 1, pp.1–3).
- Mixes up the identities → additive identity 0 (adding it changes nothing), multiplicative identity 1 (multiplying changes nothing) — Pakistan's Grade 6 books verify them separately for integers (pp.32–33, 38–39).
- Assumes every law holds for subtraction too → test it the Grade 6 way: 7 − 3 ≠ 3 − 7, so subtraction is not commutative (Grade 6, pp.32–33).
- This lesson's next door: the laws become the working tools of Expressions and Equations in Unit 6 — every rearrangement there is one of these laws, named here first.
