# Computation Short Cuts — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, expressed as its SUB
LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure — README, "Units, Lessons and
Sub Lessons"). One lesson, one level (Level 168). The lesson
was built new in the expansion era (Dominica expansion merge,
2026-11-11), so there are no old depth layers to map: the sub
lessons below are its depths as built, cut at the founding
section's own shape — short cuts built on tens first, then the
near-ten numbers 9, 99 and 11, then the restructuring cuts
(breaking apart, doubling and halving, compensation). The
questions live in the type files; this file is the
by-sub-lesson view.

Pass marks: the founding section names its topic (short cuts
in computation — rapid calculation strategies) and states no
pass mark, so no mark is invented — each sub lesson's pass
mark is stated as the observable thing the learner can do.

## Sub lesson 1 — Short Cuts with Tens and Hundreds
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: multiply and divide by 10, 100 and 1,000 by shifting places, and work ×5 and ×50 as ×10 or ×100 followed by halving.

## Sub lesson 2 — Near-Ten Short Cuts: 9, 99 and 11
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: multiply by 9 and by 99 as ×10 or ×100 minus the number itself, multiply a two-figure number by 11 by splitting and adding the middle, and add or subtract 9 or 99 by compensation.

## Sub lesson 3 — Breaking Apart, Doubling and Halving
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: double one factor and halve the other without changing the product, break a factor into parts and multiply part by part, and compensate in addition and subtraction (98 + 47 as 100 + 45).

## What "maxed" means here

The learner calculates the way Dominica's Grade 10 course
book teaches in its Computation chapter: the short cuts named
there as rapid calculation strategies (*Mathematics: A Complete
Course with CXC Questions, Volume One*, 2nd ed., Ch. 3,
printed p. 75) — tens first, near-tens second, restructuring
third — each one chosen to fit the numbers in front of them,
and each answer still checkable by the slow written way.
