# True or false — Computation Short Cuts

Sub-lesson markers: every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to — Sub lesson 1 — Short Cuts with Tens and Hundreds · Sub lesson 2 — Near-Ten Short Cuts: 9, 99 and 11 · Sub lesson 3 — Breaking Apart, Doubling and Halving. This lesson was built new with its sub lessons (Dominica expansion merge, 2026-11-11), so its markers are original — there are no older depth layers to map from.

1. (Sub lesson 1) Multiplying a whole number by 10 moves every figure one place to the left.
   **Answer: True.** Item constructed for this lesson from its founding teaching. Dominica, *Mathematics: A Complete Course with CXC Questions, Volume One*, 2nd ed. (student book), Grade 10, Ch. 3 — Computation, printed p. 75.

2. (Sub lesson 1) 5,800 ÷ 100 = 580.
   **Answer: False** — dividing by 100 moves the figures two places to the right: 5,800 ÷ 100 = 58. Item constructed for this lesson from its founding teaching. Dominica, *Mathematics: A Complete Course with CXC Questions, Volume One*, 2nd ed. (student book), Grade 10, Ch. 3 — Computation, printed p. 75.

3. (Sub lesson 2) 56 × 9 = 560 − 56 = 504.
   **Answer: True.** Item constructed for this lesson from its founding teaching. Dominica, *Mathematics: A Complete Course with CXC Questions, Volume One*, 2nd ed. (student book), Grade 10, Ch. 3 — Computation, printed p. 75.

4. (Sub lesson 2) To multiply by 11 you just write the number twice, so 23 × 11 = 2323.
   **Answer: False** — split and add the middle: 23 × 11 = 253. Item constructed for this lesson from its founding teaching. Dominica, *Mathematics: A Complete Course with CXC Questions, Volume One*, 2nd ed. (student book), Grade 10, Ch. 3 — Computation, printed p. 75.

5. (Sub lesson 3) Doubling one factor and halving the other never changes the product.
   **Answer: True** — the doubling and the halving cancel. Item constructed for this lesson from its founding teaching. Dominica, *Mathematics: A Complete Course with CXC Questions, Volume One*, 2nd ed. (student book), Grade 10, Ch. 3 — Computation, printed p. 75.

6. (Sub lesson 3) 18 × 15 = 9 × 30 = 270.
   **Answer: True.** Item constructed for this lesson from its founding teaching. Dominica, *Mathematics: A Complete Course with CXC Questions, Volume One*, 2nd ed. (student book), Grade 10, Ch. 3 — Computation, printed p. 75.
