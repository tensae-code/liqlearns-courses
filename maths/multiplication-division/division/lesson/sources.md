**Acceptance count: taught in 25 countries (Egypt, Jordan, Vietnam, Kazakhstan, Armenia, Greece, Laos, Yemen, Indonesia, Nicaragua, Ethiopia, South Sudan, Iraq, Bangladesh, Nepal, Cambodia, Kuwait, Iran, China, Pakistan, Syria, Saudi Arabia, Rwanda, Sudan, India). Egypt carries division whole — sharing equally, remainders as a named topic, and long division layout. Jordan carries division with remainder in Grade 3 and long division with zeros in the quotient in Grade 4. Vietnam carries exact division and division with remainder in Grade 3. Kazakhstan carries division as equal sharing in Grade 2 and non-table division with remainders in Grade 3. Armenia carries unknown divisors and dividends in Grade 4 and division with remainder in Grade 5. Greece carries sharing and the tables only — it never teaches formal long division, stated honestly here. Other read countries teach dividing inside their own Grade 2–4 books and join by merge.** Laos carries division from sharing equally in the old Grade 2 guide, through the Grade 3 long-division model and Grade 4 division with remainder, to decimal division in Grade 5 — with the Grade 3 book's misprinted divisor (3 for 22) corrected to 462 ÷ 22 = 21. Yemen carries division from sharing and grouping with ÷2, ÷5, ÷3 and ÷4 in Grade 2, through the ÷6–÷9 facts and the long-division bracket, to six-digit division by two-digit divisors with estimating by rounding. Indonesia carries division from repeated subtraction and sharing to 100 in Grade 3 and column division to 100 in Grade 4, through fraction division by and of natural numbers in Grade 6, to integer, fraction and decimal division in Grade 7 (Merdeka and K13 Semester 1). Grades 1–2 teach no division, as the filing states. Nicaragua teaches no division in Grades 1–2 — division starts in Grade 3 with sharing, remainders and vertical long division — then division by two- and three-digit divisors in Grade 4, decimal division in Grades 5–6, fraction division in Grade 6, and signed-number division in Grade 7. Ethiopia carries division from equal sharing and dividing to 100 in Grade 3, through dividing to 1,000 by partial quotients, to dividing to 1,000,000 by 1-digit, 2-digit and larger numbers with remainders in Grade 4. South Sudan carries division only in part — division as equal sharing with division facts to 50 ÷ 10, long-division layout drills and word problems in Grade 2, and division of fractions by reciprocals in Secondary 1. Missing: division with remainders; no 2-digit ÷ 1-digit is taught anywhere in the set. One book slip is never taught as fact: the Grade 2 book's p. 34 line "18 divide by 6 is 3" sits under the item "18÷3 =" — correct is 18 ÷ 3 = 6; teach corrected. Iraq carries division from its meaning and the division/multiplication link in Grade 3, through dividing by a 1-digit number with remainders in Grade 4, to division by 2-digit numbers and by multiples of 10, 100 and 1000 in Grade 5, dividing fractions and decimals in Grade 6, and dividing an expression by a term in Grade 8. Bangladesh carries division from first division with sharing vs grouping and remainders in Class 3, through long division with 3-digit ÷ 2-digit and 4-digit ÷ 2-digit and estimating the quotient in Class 4, to long division with 2- and 3-digit divisors in Class 5, division on the number line with division by zero undefined in Class 6, and division of exponents in Class 7. Two book errors are never taught as fact: the Class 3 remainder rule is printed "smaller than the dividend" (correct: smaller than the divisor, printed p. 70) and the unit-price problem says "price of 32 pens" (correct: 12 pens, printed p. 76) — both English-only, the Bangla original is correct; teach corrected. Nepal carries division from sharing only in Grade 1 — no ÷ symbol there — through grouping and the long-division form in Grade 2 (both books; the Nepali book names no remainders, and its teacher guide adds that division is the short form of subtraction, taught from the multiplication table) and division with remainder to 3-digit ÷ 2-digit in Grade 3 (teach the p. 59 example corrected: 595 ÷ 27 = 22 r1), to 5-digit ÷ 2- and 3-digit division in Grade 4, and division of fractions and decimals in Grades 5–6. Cambodia carries division whole from sharing into equal groups in Grade 2 through dividing by decimals and rationals in Grades 6–8. Kuwait carries division from equal sharing and the ÷2–÷9 facts in Grade 3, through division with remainder in Grade 3 and ÷1-digit work with zeros in the quotient in Grade 4, to dividing by one- and two-digit numbers in Grade 5. Iran carries division from sharing, grouping and remainders in Grade 3 to long division with two-digit divisors in Grade 4.

China carries division whole in the primary books — both sharing meanings and remainders in Grade 2, written division to three-digit numbers in Grade 3, and the zero rules in Grade 4.

Pakistan (Punjab) carries division from Grade 2's sharing and successive subtraction through the written long-division climb of Grades 3–5 to integer division in Class 6.

Syria teaches division as dealing-out sharing in Grade 2 Semester 2, names its terms and its link with multiplication in Grade 3, builds written long division in Grade 4 — regrouping, zeros in the quotient, remainders and the dividend check — continues it into decimal places in Grade 5, checks it in Grade 6, and divides rationals in Grade 7, where division also produces rates and unit rates.

Saudi Arabia teaches division in Grade 3 as sharing, grouping and repeated subtraction tied to the multiplication facts, states the zero and one rules explicitly, then moves to written division with remainders in Grade 4 — modelled with base-ten pieces, checked by multiplying back, and extended to three-digit quotients with zeros.

Rwanda teaches division divisor by divisor — ÷2 to ÷6 with the standard written method and word problems in Year 2, 4-digit ÷ 1-digit division with no remainder in Year 3, long division with and without remainder in P4, 3-digit ÷ 2-digit and decimal division in P5, long division of numbers beyond 1,000,000 in P6, and division of natural numbers and of integers in Senior 1.

Sudan teaches division divisor by divisor: by 2 to 6 in Grade 2, then by 7, 8 and 9 in Grade 3 — each with and without remainder, with written long division and word problems — and by two-digit and three-digit divisors in Grade 4.

India carries division through its NCERT ladder — Math-Magic Class 2–5 and Mathematics Class 6–7 — including grouping into tens; sharing into equal groups; halving a measurement; loan repayment.
