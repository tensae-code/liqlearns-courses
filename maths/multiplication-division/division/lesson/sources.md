**Acceptance count: taught in 13 countries (Egypt, Jordan, Vietnam, Kazakhstan, Armenia, Greece, Laos, Yemen, Indonesia, Nicaragua, Ethiopia, South Sudan, Iraq). Egypt carries division whole — sharing equally, remainders as a named topic, and long division layout. Jordan carries division with remainder in Grade 3 and long division with zeros in the quotient in Grade 4. Vietnam carries exact division and division with remainder in Grade 3. Kazakhstan carries division as equal sharing in Grade 2 and non-table division with remainders in Grade 3. Armenia carries unknown divisors and dividends in Grade 4 and division with remainder in Grade 5. Greece carries sharing and the tables only — it never teaches formal long division, stated honestly here. Other read countries teach dividing inside their own Grade 2–4 books and join by merge.** Laos carries division from sharing equally in the old Grade 2 guide, through the Grade 3 long-division model and Grade 4 division with remainder, to decimal division in Grade 5 — with the Grade 3 book's misprinted divisor (3 for 22) corrected to 462 ÷ 22 = 21. Yemen carries division from sharing and grouping with ÷2, ÷5, ÷3 and ÷4 in Grade 2, through the ÷6–÷9 facts and the long-division bracket, to six-digit division by two-digit divisors with estimating by rounding. Indonesia carries division from repeated subtraction and sharing to 100 in Grade 3 and column division to 100 in Grade 4, through fraction division by and of natural numbers in Grade 6, to integer, fraction and decimal division in Grade 7 (Merdeka and K13 Semester 1). Grades 1–2 teach no division, as the filing states. Nicaragua teaches no division in Grades 1–2 — division starts in Grade 3 with sharing, remainders and vertical long division — then division by two- and three-digit divisors in Grade 4, decimal division in Grades 5–6, fraction division in Grade 6, and signed-number division in Grade 7. Ethiopia carries division from equal sharing and dividing to 100 in Grade 3, through dividing to 1,000 by partial quotients, to dividing to 1,000,000 by 1-digit, 2-digit and larger numbers with remainders in Grade 4. South Sudan carries division only in part — division as equal sharing with division facts to 50 ÷ 10, long-division layout drills and word problems in Grade 2, and division of fractions by reciprocals in Secondary 1. Missing: division with remainders; no 2-digit ÷ 1-digit is taught anywhere in the set. One book slip is never taught as fact: the Grade 2 book's p. 34 line "18 divide by 6 is 3" sits under the item "18÷3 =" — correct is 18 ÷ 3 = 6; teach corrected. Iraq carries division from its meaning and the division/multiplication link in Grade 3, through dividing by a 1-digit number with remainders in Grade 4, to division by 2-digit numbers and by multiples of 10, 100 and 1000 in Grade 5, dividing fractions and decimals in Grade 6, and dividing an expression by a term in Grade 8.
