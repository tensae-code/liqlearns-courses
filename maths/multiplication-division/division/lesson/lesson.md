# Division — the lesson

Course: Maths · Unit: Unit 3 — Multiplication and Division · Lesson: Division
Built from: Egypt, Grade 3 Term 1 (old Discovery edition) and Grade 3 Term 2 — Jordan, Grades 3–4 — Vietnam, Toán 3 — Kazakhstan, Grades 2–3 — Armenia, Grades 4–5 — Greece, Grades 2–3

## Do it first
12 stones on the table, 3 friends waiting. Share the stones
equally — deal them out one at a time, round and round, until
none are left. Each friend gets 4. That is division: 12 ÷ 3 = 4.
The ÷ sign means "shared by"; the answer is called the
quotient.

## Sub lesson 1 — Division Sharing and the Tables
**Sharing.** Kazakhstan's Grade 2 book introduces division as
dividing a set into equal parts (Ospanov set, L73, p.21) — the
sign ":" is introduced with its readings: "6 : 3 is read as '6
divided by 3' … 'in 6 there are 2 threes'". Egypt's Grade 3 book
teaches division as sharing equally (Chapter 3, folios 32–43).
Jordan's Grade 3 book teaches division with remainder (p.64).

12 ÷ 3 = 4: twelve shared by three is four each. 20 ÷ 4 = 5:
twenty shared by four is five each.

**The tables backwards.** Division is multiplication undone:
56 ÷ 7 asks "7 times what is 56?" — and the 7s table answers 8.
Kazakhstan's Grade 2 book teaches multiplication and division as
inverse operations (L77) and uses the table for division by 2–5;
its Grade 3 book teaches non-table division of the type 96 : 6
(L86, p.14) and 75 : 15 (L87, p.17). Armenia's Grade 4 book
teaches finding an unknown divisor from dividend and quotient
(§6, p.30) and an unknown dividend from divisor and quotient
(§7, p.35).

42 ÷ 6 = 7, because 6 × 7 = 42. 81 ÷ 9 = 9, because 9 × 9 = 81.

## Sub lesson 2 — Division Remainders
**Remainders.** Sometimes the sharing does not come out even.
Egypt's Grade 3 book teaches division with remainders as a named
topic (Chapter 8, folios 82–91), and Vietnam's Grade 3 book
teaches exact division and division with remainder (Bài 26,
p.72). Armenia's Grade 5 book teaches division with remainder
(§2.10, p.65), and Kazakhstan's Grade 3 book teaches division
with remainder by a one-digit number (L83–84, pp.8–10).

17 ÷ 5 = 3 remainder 2: five threes are 15, and 2 are left over.
The remainder is always smaller than the divisor — if it were
not, one more whole share would fit. Jordan's Grade 4 book
teaches long division including zeros in the quotient.

## Sub lesson 3 — Division In Columns and Checking
**In columns.** Egypt's Grade 4 book teaches long division
layout, and Jordan's Grade 4 book teaches long division including
zeros in the quotient. The chant is divide, multiply, subtract,
bring down:

96 ÷ 4: 9 ÷ 4 = 2 (write 2), 2 × 4 = 8, 9 − 8 = 1, bring down the
6 — 16 ÷ 4 = 4. Answer: 24.

84 ÷ 4 = 21: 8 ÷ 4 = 2, 4 ÷ 4 = 1. 96 ÷ 6 = 16: 9 ÷ 6 = 1
remainder 3, bring down the 6 — 36 ÷ 6 = 6.

**Checking.** Multiply the answer back: 24 × 4 = 96 proves
96 ÷ 4 = 24. With a remainder, multiply and add the remainder:
17 ÷ 5 = 3 remainder 2 is checked by 3 × 5 + 2 = 17.

Honest negative, stated plainly: Greece never teaches formal
long division with a written algorithm beyond sharing — its
division stays at sharing and the tables — so this lesson's
column work stands on Egypt, Jordan, Vietnam, Kazakhstan and
Armenia, not on Greece.

## Sub lesson 4 — Long Division: Big Numbers, Two- and Three-Digit Divisors
**The same chant, longer numbers.** Long division does not
change when the dividend grows — divide, multiply, subtract,
bring down, digit by digit. Ethiopia's Grade 4 book divides
to 1,000,000 by 1-digit, 2-digit and larger numbers, with
remainders (ch. 4, printed pp.83–106). Its biggest worked
division runs the whole chant:

564,923 ÷ 8: 56 ÷ 8 = 7; bring down the 4 — 4 ÷ 8 = 0 (the
zero in the quotient is a real digit — never skip it); bring
down the 9 — 49 ÷ 8 = 6, remainder 1; bring down the 2 —
12 ÷ 8 = 1, remainder 4; bring down the 3 — 43 ÷ 8 = 5,
remainder 3. Answer: **70,615 remainder 3**. The same book
divides 300,000 ÷ 6 and 800,000 ÷ 9 = 88,888 remainder 8.

**Two- and three-digit divisors.** Nepal's Grade 4 book
divides 5-digit numbers by 2- and 3-digit divisors (printed
pp.26–42), and Bangladesh's Class 5 book teaches long
division with 2- and 3-digit divisors (Ch. 2, printed
pp.7–11). The one new skill is judging the first step: how
many times does the divisor fit? Work it from the tables you
know, and take enough digits first — Rwanda's Primary 6
teacher's guide shows a 7-digit number divided by the
3-digit divisor 130, taking four digits first because 130 is
bigger than the first three (Teacher's Guide, Unit 1,
printed p.20):

4,876 ÷ 23: 48 ÷ 23 = 2 (2 × 23 = 46, remainder 2); bring
down the 7 — 27 ÷ 23 = 1 (remainder 4); bring down the 6 —
46 ÷ 23 = 2. Answer: 212.

Rwanda's Primary 6 book takes long division beyond
1,000,000, by 2- and 3-digit divisors (Unit 1, printed
pp.22–25), and Tanzania's Standard Five book works long
division with dividends to 1,000,000 and divisors to three
digits (printed p.69). **Checking** grows with the work, in
Nepal's own form: quotient × divisor (+ remainder) =
dividend — 70,615 × 8 + 3 = 564,923, the number you started
with.

## Say it to your companion
Your companion calls a sharing: "12 stones, 3 friends." Deal
them in your head: "4 each." They call "17 ÷ 5": "3 remainder 2."
They call "96 ÷ 4": work it in columns and say the chant —
"divide, multiply, subtract, bring down — 24." Trade five times,
then check each answer by multiplying.

## Do It Yourself
1. Share equally: 12 ÷ 3; 20 ÷ 4; 18 ÷ 3; 24 ÷ 6; 30 ÷ 5.
2. From the tables: 56 ÷ 7; 42 ÷ 6; 81 ÷ 9; 63 ÷ 7; 72 ÷ 8.
3. With remainders: 17 ÷ 5; 23 ÷ 4; 31 ÷ 6; 29 ÷ 3.
4. In columns: 96 ÷ 4; 84 ÷ 4; 96 ÷ 6; 132 ÷ 3.
5. Check each column answer by multiplying.
6. Long division: 564,923 ÷ 8; 800,000 ÷ 9; 4,876 ÷ 23.
   Check each by quotient × divisor (+ remainder).
