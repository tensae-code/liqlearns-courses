# Lesson plan — Division

**Course:** Maths · **Stage:** Stage 3 — Multiplication and Division · **Lesson:** Division
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 10, the second lesson of Stage 3 (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** dividing — sharing into equal parts, remainders, and dividing in columns.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Multiplication (Level 9) — the learner knows the tables, and division is their inverse. It needs nothing else.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Multiplication (same stage) — every division is checked by its times table. Recommended, never forced.
- A soft hint only: the division sign is ÷ or : ; the answer is called the quotient, and what is left over is the remainder.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Share a number into equal parts and say the division (12 ÷ 3 = 4).
2. Divide using the times tables (56 ÷ 7 = 8).
3. Divide with a remainder and say what the remainder means (17 ÷ 5 = 3 remainder 2).
4. Divide two- and three-digit numbers by one digit in columns, and check by multiplying.

## What you need
Counters or stones; a multiplication table grid.
Paper and a pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner shares 12 stones equally among 3 friends (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — sharing; the tables backwards; remainders; in columns; checking by multiplying (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE at the sharing part: a picture of 12 stones shared into 3 equal piles of 4 → the student counts each pile and says "12 shared by 3 is 4".
   - ADD IMAGE under remainders: a picture of 17 stones shared into 5 piles, with 2 stones left over beside the piles → the student says "3 each, remainder 2".

4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — share five numbers equally, divide five from the tables, divide three with remainders, and divide three in columns (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Says 12 ÷ 3 = 3 → share it out: deal the stones one at a time into 3 piles — each pile gets 4. Dealing beats guessing.
- Forgets the remainder must be smaller than the divisor → if the remainder is 5 and you divided by 5, one more whole share fits: 17 ÷ 5 is 3 remainder 2, never 2 remainder 7.
- Loses the place in long division → the chant is divide, multiply, subtract, bring down — say it at every step, and check the final answer by multiplying.
- This lesson's next door: The-order-of-operations (same stage) — the last lesson of the stage, where division meets the other operations.
