# The-order-of-operations — the lesson

Course: Maths · Stage: Stage 3 — Multiplication and Division · Lesson: The-order-of-operations
Built from: Egypt, Grade 4 Term 1 (Discovery Techbook) — Jordan, Grade 4 Term 1 and Grade 8 — Kazakhstan, Grades 1–4 — Armenia, Mathematics 4 — Vietnam, Toán 6 — Greece, Grade 5 — South Africa, Grade 6 Book 1

## Do it first
Work out 2 + 3 × 4. Left to right: 2 + 3 = 5, then 5 × 4 =
20. But the books say 14. Why? Because multiplication comes
before addition: 3 × 4 = 12 first, then 2 + 12 = 14. The order
of operations is the rule that decides which operation goes
first — and it changes the answer.

## Then the explanation
**Multiply and divide first.** Kazakhstan's Grade 2 book teaches
order of operations in expressions as its own lesson (Ospanov
set, L84), and its Grade 2 Zhakupova set teaches order of
operations in two lessons: (1) without brackets — × and ÷ before
+ and −, left to right — and (2) with brackets, each with fully
worked numbered-step examples. Armenia's Grade 4 book teaches
expressions with operations of different levels (§3, p.17).

2 + 3 × 4 = 2 + 12 = 14. The multiplication goes first, even
though it stands second in the line. 10 − 6 ÷ 2 = 10 − 3 = 7.
8 + 12 ÷ 4 = 8 + 3 = 11.

**Brackets first.** Kazakhstan's Grade 1 book teaches order of
operations in expressions with brackets (p.13) — the youngest
teaching of the rule in the set — and Armenia's Grade 4 book
teaches order of operations with brackets (§4, p.20). Egypt's
Grade 4 book teaches the order of operations as its own unit:
brackets first, then multiplication and division, then addition
and subtraction (Unit 8, folios 124–127). Jordan's Grade 4 book
teaches order of operations (p.63), and its Grade 8 book teaches
it again with exponents and roots beside it (p.20).

(2 + 3) × 4 = 5 × 4 = 20. The brackets are a box: do what is
inside the box first, then the rest. 10 − (4 + 2) = 10 − 6 = 4.
(10 − 4) ÷ 2 = 6 ÷ 2 = 3 — but 10 − 4 ÷ 2 = 10 − 2 = 8. The
brackets change the answer.

**Left to right.** When two operations are on the same level,
work left to right. 12 ÷ 3 × 2 = 4 × 2 = 8 — never 12 ÷ 6 = 2.
20 − 8 + 3 = 12 + 3 = 15 — never 20 − 11 = 9. Vietnam's Grade 6
book teaches order of operations with powers, and Greece's Grade
5 book teaches mixed numerical expressions — order of operations
— as its own lesson (pp.23–24).

**The full order, said whole.** South Africa's Grade 6 Book 1
spells it out in full — BODMAS: Brackets first, Order, Division
and Multiplication, Addition and Subtraction (worksheet 47).
The books here say the same order in words:

1. Brackets first — and the same order inside the brackets.
2. Multiply and divide, left to right.
3. Add and subtract, left to right.

Work it step by step, one operation at a time, saying each step
aloud: (6 + 2) × 3 − 10 ÷ 2 = 8 × 3 − 10 ÷ 2 = 24 − 5 = 19.

## Say it to your companion
Your companion builds an expression with operation cards:
"6 + 2 × 5." Work it out aloud: "2 × 5 = 10 first, then
6 + 10 = 16." They build "(6 + 2) × 5": "Brackets first: 8.
8 × 5 = 40." Trade five times. Then the chain round: they call
"12 ÷ 3 × 2" — work it left to right, aloud: "4, then 8." 

## Do It Yourself
1. No brackets: 2 + 3 × 4; 10 − 6 ÷ 2; 8 + 12 ÷ 4; 5 × 3 + 7;
   20 − 4 × 3.
2. With brackets: (2 + 3) × 4; (10 − 4) ÷ 2; 10 − (4 + 2);
   (6 + 2) × 3; 18 ÷ (3 + 3).
3. Left to right: 12 ÷ 3 × 2; 20 − 8 + 3; 2 × 3 ÷ 2; 100 ÷ 10 ÷ 2.
4. Full order: (6 + 2) × 3 − 10 ÷ 2; 4 + 12 ÷ (2 + 2).
