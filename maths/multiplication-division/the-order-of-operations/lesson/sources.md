**Acceptance count: taught in 13 countries (Egypt, Jordan, Kazakhstan, Armenia, Vietnam, Greece, South Africa, Laos, Indonesia, Nicaragua, Iraq, Bangladesh). Kazakhstan carries the rule from Grade 1 (brackets) through Grade 4 (first- and second-level operations, brackets first). Armenia carries expressions of different levels and brackets in Grade 4. Egypt carries the order of operations as its own Grade 4 unit. Jordan carries it in Grade 4 and again in Grade 8 with exponents and roots. Vietnam carries it with powers in Grade 6. Greece carries mixed numerical expressions in Grade 5. South Africa spells it out in full as BODMAS in Grade 6. Other read countries teach the order of operations inside their own Grade 2–8 books and join by merge.** Laos carries the order of operations from its own Grade 4 chapter on calculation principles, through bracketed calculations in Grade 5, to bracket rules with calculation trees in Grade 6. Indonesia carries the order of operations in the Grade 7 K13 Semester 1 student book, Ch. 1, printed pp. 1–108. The Grade 7 Merdeka book teaches the four operations on integers and rationals but files no order-of-operations teaching, so only the K13 edition is cited. Nicaragua carries the order of operations from the old Grade 2 guide (parentheses first, then left-to-right, ×/÷ before +/−) through the old Grade 3 book, where the rules are stated explicitly, and the 2025 Grade 3 book, where they are built up through worked examples, to order of operations with fractions in Grade 6 and with signed numbers and grouping signs in Grade 7. One book error is never taught as fact: the old Grade 3 student book's Unit 11 problem '(40 − 25) ÷ 5 − 9' is worked beside it as '(40 − 25) ÷ 5 + 9' = 12 — the intended problem is the '+ 9' one (with '− 9' the answer would be −6, which this grade never teaches) — and the old Grade 3 guide repeats the same error. Iraq carries the order of operations as its own lesson in Grade 6, then again on integers in Grade 7, on rational numbers in Grade 8, and in real numbers in Grade 9. Bangladesh carries the order of operations from multiply/divide first in Class 3, through the full calculation-order rules with brackets and the distributive idea in Class 4, to the bracket rules and order of operations on a calculator in Class 5. Note honestly: the Class 5 Rule 2 ("division first then multiplication") is a level-appropriate simplification for that age, not an error — carry it as the book states it. One English-only misprint is never taught as fact: the Class 3 mango problem prints "550 − 46 = 50" (correct 504, printed p. 82) — the Bangla original is correct; teach corrected. Nepal carries the order of operations as named precedence teaching — brackets, then multiplication, then addition/subtraction in Grade 4, BODMAS with brackets in Grade 5 (teach the p. 36 example corrected: 5 + 8×2 − (5−2) = 18, not 8), simplification with brackets in Grade 6, and BODMAS rules again in Grade 7.
