**Acceptance count: taught in 23 countries (Egypt, Jordan, Kazakhstan, Armenia, Vietnam, Greece, South Africa, Laos, Indonesia, Nicaragua, Iraq, Bangladesh, Nepal, Cambodia, Kuwait, Iran, China, Pakistan, Syria, Malaysia, Rwanda, Sudan, India). Kazakhstan carries the rule from Grade 1 (brackets) through Grade 4 (first- and second-level operations, brackets first). Armenia carries expressions of different levels and brackets in Grade 4. Egypt carries the order of operations as its own Grade 4 unit. Jordan carries it in Grade 4 and again in Grade 8 with exponents and roots. Vietnam carries it with powers in Grade 6. Greece carries mixed numerical expressions in Grade 5. South Africa spells it out in full as BODMAS in Grade 6. Other read countries teach the order of operations inside their own Grade 2–8 books and join by merge.** Laos carries the order of operations from its own Grade 4 chapter on calculation principles, through bracketed calculations in Grade 5, to bracket rules with calculation trees in Grade 6. Indonesia carries the order of operations in the Grade 7 K13 Semester 1 student book, Ch. 1, printed pp. 1–108. The Grade 7 Merdeka book teaches the four operations on integers and rationals but files no order-of-operations teaching, so only the K13 edition is cited. Nicaragua carries the order of operations from the old Grade 2 guide (parentheses first, then left-to-right, ×/÷ before +/−) through the old Grade 3 book, where the rules are stated explicitly, and the 2025 Grade 3 book, where they are built up through worked examples, to order of operations with fractions in Grade 6 and with signed numbers and grouping signs in Grade 7. One book error is never taught as fact: the old Grade 3 student book's Unit 11 problem '(40 − 25) ÷ 5 − 9' is worked beside it as '(40 − 25) ÷ 5 + 9' = 12 — the intended problem is the '+ 9' one (with '− 9' the answer would be −6, which this grade never teaches) — and the old Grade 3 guide repeats the same error. Iraq carries the order of operations as its own lesson in Grade 6, then again on integers in Grade 7, on rational numbers in Grade 8, and in real numbers in Grade 9. Bangladesh carries the order of operations from multiply/divide first in Class 3, through the full calculation-order rules with brackets and the distributive idea in Class 4, to the bracket rules and order of operations on a calculator in Class 5. Note honestly: the Class 5 Rule 2 ("division first then multiplication") is a level-appropriate simplification for that age, not an error — carry it as the book states it. One English-only misprint is never taught as fact: the Class 3 mango problem prints "550 − 46 = 50" (correct 504, printed p. 82) — the Bangla original is correct; teach corrected. Nepal carries the order of operations as named precedence teaching — brackets, then multiplication, then addition/subtraction in Grade 4, BODMAS with brackets in Grade 5 (teach the p. 36 example corrected: 5 + 8×2 − (5−2) = 18, not 8), simplification with brackets in Grade 6, and BODMAS rules again in Grade 7. Cambodia carries the order of operations whole — brackets first in Grade 5, the full precedence rules with a wrong-grouping warning in Grade 7. Kuwait carries the order of operations as a named lesson twice in primary school — lesson 3-12 in Grade 3 Term 1 and lesson 5-10 in Grade 4 Term 2 — and again with rational numbers, powers and roots in Grade 8. Iran restates the full order of operations with powers inserted in Grade 7 and applies it in fully stepped workings, including a substitution example where the wrong order is shown and corrected.

China carries the order of operations from brackets-first in Grade 1, through the ×/÷-before-+/− rule in Grade 2, to square brackets and two-step problems in Grade 4.

Pakistan (Punjab) carries the order of operations in the Class 5 book's left-to-right rules and named BODMAS, then Class 6's four bracket kinds and Class 7's algebraic BODMAS.

Syria states the full order once, in Grade 6 — brackets, then powers, then multiplication and division left to right, then addition and subtraction — with fraction-bar expressions and bracket-insertion puzzles, and then keeps it in use: brackets-first evaluations of rational expressions in Grade 7, restatements for rational expressions and for powers in Grade 8 Algebra, and a diagnostic check in Grade 9 Algebra.

Malaysia states the order of operations explicitly in Form 1, inside its integer chapter — brackets, then multiply and divide, then add and subtract — and then holds every later combined calculation (fractions, decimals, rationals) to the same ladder.

Rwanda touches the order of operations in P5, where mixed decimal multiplication-and-division expressions are evaluated as a single fraction, and in Senior 1, where the order of operations (BODMAS) is applied to fractions and to decimals.

Sudan states the order of operations in Grade 6 and works mixed operations in Grade 7.

India enters the order of operations from Mathematics Class 6: brackets — the order in which operations are worked when brackets are present.
