**Acceptance count: taught in 27 countries (Kazakhstan, Egypt, Greece, Libya, Jordan, Armenia, Vietnam, Laos, Yemen, Indonesia, Nicaragua, Ethiopia, South Sudan, Iraq, Bangladesh, Nepal, Cambodia, Kuwait, North Macedonia, Iran, China, Pakistan, Syria, Saudi Arabia, Rwanda, Sudan, India).**. Kazakhstan carries the tables in order — 2 to 5 in Grade 2, 6 to 9 in Grade 3 — and multiplication by 10 and 100. Egypt carries multiplication from arrays as repeated addition in Grade 2 Term 2 (old Discover series — rows and columns counted, no × sign yet), through vertical multiplication, 2-digit and 3-digit by 1-digit, with multiplying by tens and hundreds, in Grade 3. Greece carries the tables from Grade 2 and multiplication with division in Grade 3. Libya carries column multiplication with regrouping to hundreds in Grade 3. Jordan carries the tables for 2 to 5 in Grade 3. Armenia carries finding the unknown factor in Grade 4. Vietnam carries multiplication within 1000 in Grade 3. Other read countries teach multiplying inside their own Grade 2–4 books and join by merge.** Laos carries multiplication as repeated addition with tables ×2–×9 in the new Grade 2 book (to ×10 in the old guide) and multiplication with carrying, including by 10 and 100, in Grade 3. Yemen carries multiplication from repeated addition and the ×2, ×5, ×3, ×4 facts in Grade 2, through the ×6–×9 facts and multiplying by 10, 100 and 1000, to three-digit by three-digit multiplication with estimating by rounding. Indonesia carries multiplication from repeated addition to 100 in Grade 3 and column multiplication to 100 in Grade 4, through fraction multiplication by natural numbers in Grade 6, to integer, fraction and decimal multiplication in Grade 7 (Merdeka and K13 Semester 1). Grades 1–2 teach no multiplication, as the filing states. Nicaragua carries multiplication from the tables in Grade 2 (2, 5, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9 in the old book; 5, 2, 3, 4, then 6, 7, 8, 9 in the 2025 book), through vertical multiplication in Grade 3 and 12 × 23 with the named properties in Grade 4, to decimal and fraction multiplication in Grades 5–6 and signed-number multiplication in Grade 7. One book error is never taught as fact: the old Grade 3 teacher guide's multiplication-tables page misprints '1 x 10 = 80' — the true value is 8 x 10 = 80. Ethiopia carries multiplication from the tables ×2, ×10, ×5, ×0/×1, ×3, ×4, ×6, ×7 and ×8 in Grade 2 — there is no ×9 section, ×9 appearing only in the full 0–10 table — through multiplying to 1,000 in Grade 3 and to 10,000, to multiplying to 1,000,000 with 2-digit and larger multipliers in Grade 4. South Sudan carries multiplication only in part — multiplication as repeated addition and groups with the 1–10 table and word problems in Grade 2, and multiplication of fractions in Secondary 1. Missing: column and multi-digit multiplication; no 2-digit × 1-digit is taught anywhere in the set. Iraq carries multiplication from repeated addition to 5×5 in Grade 2, through ×0–×10 and 2-digit × 1-digit without carrying in Grade 3, to 2-digit × 2-digit in Grade 4, 3-digit × 2-digit in Grade 5, fraction and decimal multiplication in Grade 6, and multiplying algebraic terms and expressions in Grades 7–8. Bangladesh carries multiplication from the tables of 1–10 with commutativity in Class 2, through tables of 13, 14 and 15 with 2-digit × 2-digit and 3-digit × 2-digit in Class 3, to 3-digit × 3-digit in Class 4, short methods in Class 5, multiplication on the number line in Class 6, and multiplication of exponents in Class 7. Two book errors are never taught as fact: the Class 3 tables print 6 × 9 as 55 (correct 54, printed p. 39) and 2 × 9 as 19 (correct 18, printed p. 45) — teach the correct values. Nepal carries multiplication from repeated addition with the ×2–×5 tables in Grade 1 (teach the p. 82 butterflies corrected: 4 × 4 = 16), through the ×6–×10 tables in Grade 2 (both books) and ×1–×12 with 2-digit × 2-digit and 3-digit × 2-digit in Grade 3, to 3–4 digit by up to 3-digit multiplication in Grade 4, and multiplication of fractions and decimals in Grades 5–6. Cambodia carries multiplication whole from repeated addition in Grade 2 through decimals, fractions and rationals in Grades 5–8. Kuwait carries multiplication from the concept itself in Grade 2 (repeated addition, arrays, ×2–×5), through the full ×1–×9 tables and 1-digit × 2-/3-digit work in Grade 3 and 2-digit × 2-or-more-digit work in Grade 4, to multiplying by one- and two-digit numbers in Grade 5. North Macedonia touches multiplication only at its very start — doubling to 10 at Grade 1 (2022), as readiness for multiplying by 2. Iran seeds the multiplication tables with skip-counting charts in Grade 1, builds multiplication in Grade 3, and reaches two-digit by two-digit multiplication in Grade 4.

China carries multiplication from the table facts in Grade 2 to written two-digit × two-digit in Grade 3 and the laws and part-relations in Grade 4.

Pakistan (Punjab) carries multiplication from Grade 2's repeated addition and the 2, 3, 4, 5 and 10 tables through Classes 3–5's written methods to integer multiplication and its sign rules in Class 6.

Syria introduces multiplication in Grade 2 Semester 2 as joining equal groups and reads its commutativity off arrays read by rows and by columns; builds the tables of 2–9 one at a time in Grade 3 with their patterns; carries in stages through Grade 4 with partial products; and uses the commutative, associative and distributive properties as mental-calculation tools in Grades 5–6, before rational multiplication in Grade 7. Two review slips in the Grade 2 book are taught corrected where they occur: the printed 8 × 3 = 2 (the product is 24) and the apple labelled 16 whose three offered multiplications give 8, 12 and 18.

Saudi Arabia teaches the whole multiplication table inside Grade 3 — meaning, arrays and commutativity, the facts by pattern, the zero/one and associative properties — then builds multi-digit multiplication in Grade 4 on partial products and the area model, estimating first at every step.

Rwanda teaches multiplication table by table — ×2 to ×6 with two-digit multiplication and word problems in Year 2, the ×7, ×8 and ×9 tables with 3-digit × 2-digit multiplication and multiplying by 100 and 1,000 in Year 3, the table to 12 × 12 and quick multiplication by 5 in P4, quick methods and multiplication by a 3-digit number in P5, multiplication beyond 1,000,000 in P6, and multiplication of natural numbers and of integers in Senior 1.

Sudan builds the tables one at a time — 2, 10, 3, 4, 5, 6 in Grade 2; 7, 8, 9 in Grade 3, with multiplying by 0 and 1, by 10, 100 and multiples of ten, and two-digit and three-digit products — and returns to mental multiplication in Grade 9.

India carries multiplication through its NCERT ladder — Math-Magic Class 3–5 and Mathematics Class 6–7 — including skip counting on a number line; multiplying by thousands; cost tables; word problems.
