# Hands-on — Inclusion–Exclusion and Venn Counting

The lesson counted overlapping groups with real hoops and counters.

## Two hoops on the floor
Lay two overlapping hoops (or chalk circles). Take 20 counters for classmates who play football (12) and basketball (9), with 4 playing both. Put the 4 in the overlap first, then the only-football and only-basketball counters. Count the floor: 17 counters, not 21. The missing 4 are the double count — this is the region-first method of Laos's Grade 10 survey problems (Ch.II L5, pp.38–48).

## Class survey
Survey your own class or family on two questions (for example: drinks tea? drinks coffee?). Tally into the four regions — both, tea only, coffee only, neither — and check: the four regions add to the whole group, and n(A) + n(B) − n(A∩B) matches your in-either count. Nepal's books run exactly this survey shape (Grade 8 §10.3; Grades 9–10).

## Eight regions
Draw three overlapping circles and label all eight regions in words before writing any number: all three; each pair only (three regions); each one only (three regions); none. Then place the Sierra Leone SSS1 three-set numbers (L64–70 style) into the regions, middle first.

## Onto arrows
Draw 4 dots on the left and 3 on the right. Count all arrow-diagrams where every right dot is hit: first count all 3⁴ = 81 functions, then take away the ones missing a named dot, then fix the double-subtraction — inclusion–exclusion with your own hands, the method Iran's Discrete Mathematics book uses for onto functions (pp.73–78). The answer is 36.
