# Lesson plan — Inclusion–Exclusion and Venn Counting

**Course:** Maths · **Stage:** Stage 14 — Counting and Discrete Mathematics · **Lesson:** Inclusion–Exclusion and Venn Counting
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 107 of 136, Stage 14 — Counting and Discrete Mathematics (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** counting things that belong to overlapping groups without counting anything twice — the Venn diagram method, n(A∪B) = n(A) + n(B) − n(A∩B), the three-set formula, "neither" and "only" counts, and counting onto functions by inclusion–exclusion.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the sets lesson and on Counting Principles (Level 105) — the learner can already read a Venn diagram and use ⁿCᵣ. It needs nothing else.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the sets lesson — union, intersection and the Venn diagram itself. Recommended, never forced.
- Recommended first: Counting Principles (Level 105) — the onto-function count at the end is inclusion–exclusion over the counting principle. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Read and fill a two-set Venn diagram region by region, starting from the overlap, the way Laos's Grade 10 book works its survey problems (Ch.II L5, pp.38–48).
2. Use n(A∪B) = n(A) + n(B) − n(A∩B) and explain why the overlap is subtracted once, as Laos's Grade 10 book states the formula in those survey problems (pp.38–48).
3. Find "only A", "only B" and "neither" counts, with Nepal's filed correction honoured: in the Nepal survey example the "neither" count is 18 (Nepal Grade 8 §10.3; Grades 9–10).
4. Use the three-set formula, adding the three singles, subtracting the three pair overlaps, and adding the triple overlap back, the way Sierra Leone's SSS1 course teaches three sets (L64–70) and its SSS3 course states the formulae (L121).
5. Solve survey problems forwards and backwards — given the regions, find a missing total; given the totals, find a missing region (Kazakhstan Grade 5 §5.4; Grade 9 books).
6. Count onto (surjective) functions by inclusion–exclusion, the closing part, the way Iran's Discrete Mathematics book counts them (pp.73–78).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Two or three overlapping hoops or drawn circles. Counters in two colours.

1. **Do it first** — the learner counts how many classmates in a described group play football or basketball by adding the two lists, then finds the double-counted names by hand (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the Venn regions; the two-set formula; neither and only; the three-set formula; onto functions by inclusion–exclusion (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the two-set part: a picture of two overlapping circles filling with counters, the overlap counters flashing as they are counted twice and then once removed — the student says why one copy of the overlap must go.
   - ADD IMAGE at the three-set part: a picture of three overlapping circles with all eight regions labelled — the student names the region "in A and B but not C".

## If the learner is stuck
- Adds the groups and gets too big an answer → put real counters in two hoops on the floor; count the overlap counters twice out loud, then take one copy away. The formula is that action, written down.
- Puts numbers into the wrong region → always fill the Venn from the middle out: overlap first, then the "only" crescents, then the outside — the order Laos's Grade 10 survey problems model (pp.38–48).
- Three sets overwhelm → label the eight regions with words before any numbers (in all three; in A and B only; in A only; in none).
- This lesson's next door: Graph Theory (Level 108) — counting gives way to connections: paths and routes through a network.
