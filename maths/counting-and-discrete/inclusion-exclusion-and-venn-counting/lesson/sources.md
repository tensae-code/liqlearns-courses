**Acceptance count: taught in 10 countries (Iran, Laos, Nepal, Sierra Leone, Kazakhstan, South Sudan, Malaysia, Sudan, India, Mongolia).** Iran carries the deepest treatment — inclusion–exclusion in the Discrete Mathematics book (pp.73–78), carried all the way to counting onto functions, which is this lesson's closing part. Laos carries the working method: the two-set and three-set formulas inside Grade 10 survey problems (Ch.II L5, pp.38–48). Nepal carries the survey shape across Grades 8 (§10.3), 9 and 10, with its filed "neither" count taught corrected at 18. Sierra Leone carries three sets taught region by region at SSS1 (L64–70) and the formulae stated at SSS3 (L121). Kazakhstan carries the overlap idea at Grade 5 (§5.4) and the formula in its Grade 9 books. South Sudan carries Venn counting in the Secondary 2 sets unit.

Malaysia's Form 4 set chapter counts with its Venn diagrams throughout — the inclusion–exclusion count n(A ∪ B) = n(A) + n(B) − n(A ∩ B) worked on survey data, up to three sets combined.

Sudan counts with Venn diagrams in Grade 10, inside its sets course.

India enters inclusion–exclusion and venn counting from Math-Magic Class 5 and Mathematics Class 11: common multiples; counting with two sets.


Mongolia enters through its ministry textbook Математик VIII (Grade 8): the inclusion–exclusion rule for two sets, |A∪B| = |A| + |B| − |A∩B|, boxed in the sets chapter.
