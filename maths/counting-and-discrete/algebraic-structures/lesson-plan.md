# Lesson plan — Algebraic structures

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 14 — Counting and Discrete Mathematics · **Lesson:** Algebraic structures
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 111 of 136, Unit 14 — Counting and Discrete Mathematics (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** algebraic structures — what a binary operation is, the properties it can have (closure, associativity, identity, inverses, commutativity), and the named structures built from them: groups, rings and fields.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the sets and mappings lessons — the learner already knows what a set and a rule are. It needs nothing else.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Binary Operations, Tables and Properties** — what a binary operation is, reading and building its operation table, and checking closure, commutativity, associativity, identity and inverses. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Systems and Groups** — the mathematical system, and the group: its axioms, identities and inverses, and deciding whether a small system is a group (and whether it is abelian). (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **Rings and Fields** — what a ring is, (ℤ, +, ×) checked against the definition, and what a field adds — why ℝ is a field and ℤ is not. (Was depth Level 3.)

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a binary operation on a set is and read or build its operation table, the way Yemen's Grade 10 book opens the unit (Unit 5, 5-1, p.96).
2. Check a binary operation for the properties Yemen lists — the operation's behaviour under the book's property checks (5-2, p.100).
3. Say what a mathematical system is, in Yemen's sense of a set with an operation and its properties (5-3, p.108).
4. State the group axioms — associative operation, identity element, an inverse for every element, with commutativity giving an abelian group — and check small systems against them, as Yemen's Grade 10 book states them (5-4, p.110).
5. State what a ring is in Yemen's Grade 11 Science definition — (S, *, ∘) with (S, *) an abelian group, ∘ associative, and ∘ distributing over * — and verify that (ℤ, +, ×) is a ring (Unit 1, 1-2, p.14).
6. Say what a field adds to a ring, and why the real numbers ℝ form a field, as Yemen's book closes the unit (1-3, p.21; 1-4, p.24).

## If the learner is stuck
- Closure fails and it feels like a trick → that IS the check: one answer landing outside the set ends the matter — the system fails at the first axiom.
- Identity vs inverse confused → Yemen's order helps: find the identity e first (e * a = a for all a); only then ask, for each a, which element undoes it back to e (5-4, p.110).
- Rings feel like "groups with extra" → use Yemen's own statement: a ring needs TWO operations; the first must be an abelian group, the second only associative, and the second must distribute over the first (1-2, p.14).
- Why is ℤ not a field → 2 has no multiplicative inverse inside ℤ; a field demands one for every non-zero element (1-3, p.21).
- This lesson's next door: Mathematical investigations (Level 112, same stage).
