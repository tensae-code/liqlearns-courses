# Hands-on — Graph theory

The lesson drew graphs by hand, the way every book starts them.

## The bridge walk
Draw four dots for the two banks and two islands of a river town, and seven lines for seven bridges, as Iran's book retells Euler's Königsberg problem (Discrete Mathematics, pp.32–33). Try to trace every line once without lifting your pencil. Then count the odd-degree dots — and say why the walk had to fail.

## Degree check
Draw any graph with 6 vertices and 7 edges. Write each degree beside its vertex. Add the degrees: the total must be 14, twice the edges — the handshaking lemma Iran's book proves (p.40). If your total is odd, find your counting slip.

## First-fit packing
Take ten small boxes or books of different widths and three big boxes ("bins") of width 10 on paper. Pack in the order given by first-fit, Jordan's way (Business Mathematics, Grade 12 Term 1, pp.68–82): each item goes into the first bin with room. Write which bin each item entered.

## Guard the graph
Draw a path of 6 vertices and a cycle of 6 vertices. Place counters ("guards") so every vertex is a guard or next to one, using as few counters as you can — Iran's domination pictures (pp.43–48). Write your count for each graph, then check it against the bound ⌈n/(Δ+1)⌉ (p.49).
