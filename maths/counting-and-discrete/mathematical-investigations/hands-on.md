# Hands-on — Mathematical investigations

The lesson investigated with real objects, the way China's books set these tasks.

## The nine-coin hunt
Take 9 coins or bottle tops. A partner secretly marks one as "light" (a dot underneath). Using a drawn balance — or two hands as pans — find it in 2 weighings by China's split-in-three method (Grade 5, second volume, p.111). Then try 27 counters and do it in 3.

## The phone tree on paper
Draw the tree for the first four minutes of China's phone-call lesson (Grade 5, second volume, p.102): 1 caller, then 2, 4, 8, 16 knowing. Write the running totals. Then answer backwards: how many minutes until at least 100 people know?

## Paint and count
Build a 3×3×3 cube from 27 small cubes, dice or sugar lumps. "Paint" it by marking every outside face with a dot of colour. Pull it apart and sort the small cubes into 3-face, 2-face, 1-face and 0-face piles — China's Exploring figures activity (Grade 5, second volume, p.44). Your piles should read 8, 12, 6, 1.

## Segment ladders
Draw 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6 points on a line and count the segments each time, China's review way (Grade 6, second volume, pp.100–104). Write the counts 1, 3, 6, 10, 15 and find the rule: each new point adds as many segments as there were points before it.
