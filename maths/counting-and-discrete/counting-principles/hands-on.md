# Hands-on — Counting Principles

The lesson counted choices with trees, lines and circles of real things.

## Outfit tree
Take 3 tops and 2 bottoms from your own clothes. Draw the tree: 3 branches, then 2 from each. Count the leaves: 6 outfits. Now add a third step — 2 hats. The tree gives 3 × 2 × 2 = 12. This is the fundamental principle Pakistan's Class 11 book proves with tree diagrams (Unit 7, pp.123–132).

## Race orders
Three runners, three finishing places. Act out or write all the orders: there are 3! = 6. Add a fourth runner and predict before listing: 4! = 24. List only the orders starting with one fixed runner (6 of them) and see the pattern — each fixed first place holds (n−1)! orders.

## The four-team check
Four teams, every pair plays once. List the pairs on paper the way China's Grade 3 Volume 2 book lists them (Unit 8, pp.101–105): you get 6 matches. Then compute C(4,2) = 6 and watch the formula and the list agree.

## Round-table seats
Put five chairs in a circle. Seat five people, then rotate everyone one chair clockwise — nothing has changed, the neighbours are the same. That is why the count is (5−1)! = 24, not 5!. Now flip the test: a necklace that can be turned over — the count halves to (5−1)!/2 = 12, the case Kuwait's Grade 11 Term 2 book teaches (11-1, pp.152–161).
