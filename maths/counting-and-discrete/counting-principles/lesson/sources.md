**Acceptance count: taught in 21 countries (Pakistan, Kuwait, Yemen, Vietnam, Indonesia, China, Laos, Armenia, Mauritania, Iran, Egypt, Cambodia, Kazakhstan, Nicaragua, Iraq, Jordan, Ethiopia, South Sudan, Syria, Malaysia, Sudan).** Pakistan carries the whole lesson in one unit — the principle proved by trees, factorials, permutations with restrictions and repeats, both circular forms, and combinations proved from permutations — with its p.134 example taught corrected at 6,160. Kuwait carries the climb across three grades: small choice situations in Grade 8 Term 2, repeats in Grade 10 Term 2 and again in Grade 11 Statistics Term 2, and circular permutation as its own Grade 11 Term 2 lesson. Yemen carries the unit twice, in full in the Grade 12 Science book and in small opening counts (3 × 2 = 6, P(4,4) = 24, C(4,2) = 6) in the Grade 12 Literary book. Vietnam carries the multiply/add counting rules in Grade 10 Volume 2. Indonesia carries a full counting chapter in both its Grade 12 editions. China carries the listing check — 4 teams, 6 matches — in Grade 3 Volume 2. Laos carries combinations in its Grade 11 counting chapter. Armenia carries the same ground as teacher evidence only: its Grade 12 guide plans arrangements, permutations and combinations in Chapter 2, but the Grade 12 student book is not held. Mauritania carries a counting chapter in eight books — first in 3AS (Ch.13, p.255), then in every streamed line (5AS-M, 5AS-D, 6AS-M, 6AS-C, 6AS-D, 6AS-LM, 7AS-LM). Iran carries it in three books: the full Grade 10 treatment (Ch.6, pp.118–140), the Grade 12 Humanities restart from everyday choices (Ch.1 L1, pp.2–11), and the Grade 12 Discrete extension to permutations with repetition and distributing identical objects (Ch.3, pp.56–61). Egypt carries it in both Grade 11 Term 2 tracks, Pure (Unit 2, pp.49–63) and the abridged General (Unit 2, lessons at pp.38, 41 and 46). Cambodia carries it in Grade 10 Part 2 (Ch.8, pp.61–90) and again in the Grade 12 Probability booklet (PRIVATE — a private Khem Putthi booklet, not MoEYS), whose p.23 repeated-items formula is taught corrected. Kazakhstan carries a full combinatorics chapter in each of its four Grade 9 algebra books, and counting by option trees in its Grade 6 books. Nicaragua carries it in Grade 10 (Unit 1, pp.23–37) and re-teaches it in Grade 11 (Unit 6, pp.118–129), adding circular permutations as a taught lesson. Iraq carries it at three levels: Grade 9 (6-3, p.147), Grade 11 Scientific (Ch.8, pp.190–201) and Grade 12 Literary (Ch.1, pp.6–23). Jordan carries permutations and combinations in Grade 11 Term 1 (Unit 3 L1, p.120) and counting outcomes in Grade 8 Term 2 (Unit 9 L3, p.161). Ethiopia carries the fundamental principle, permutations and combinations in Grade 11 Unit 8 (pp.413–479, filed at unit range). South Sudan carries the permutation and combination formulae with worked examples and 30 multiple-choice questions in Secondary 4 (Unit 3, pp.56–59).

Syria's combinatorics unit in Grade 12 Part Two is a complete counting-principles course: lists, trees, the slot method, factorials, permutations, arrangements and combinations.

Malaysia teaches counting in Additional Mathematics Form 5 as one chapter — the multiplication rule from tree diagrams, permutations in all their school forms, and combinations — the same chapter whose distributions follow it.

Sudan counts in Grade 12 Specialized Book One — the fundamental principle, factorial, permutations and combinations, with equations and identities — and meets the power set in Grade 10's set course.
