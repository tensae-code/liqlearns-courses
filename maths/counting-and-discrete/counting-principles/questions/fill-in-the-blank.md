# Counting Principles — fill in the blank

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — The Fundamental Principle and Factorials · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Permutations, Repeats and Restrictions · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Combinations and Circular Permutations.

Fill each blank with the missing number or word.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** If a choice is made in 4 ways and then another in 3 ways, the two together can be made in ______ ways.
**Answer:** 12 — the fundamental counting principle multiplies step counts.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** 1! = ______ and 0! = ______.
**Answer:** 1; 1 — both are fixed by the factorial definition.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** ⁿPᵣ = n! / ______.
**Answer:** (n−r)! — the places not filled cancel from the factorial.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** When repeats are allowed, a 4-place code from 10 symbols has ______ codes.
**Answer:** 10,000 — 10⁴, each place free (Kuwait, Grade 10 Term 2, pp.183–191).

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** ⁿCᵣ = ⁿC______ — choosing what you take is choosing what you leave.
**Answer:** ₙ₋ᵣ — the complementary combinations fact (Pakistan, Class 11, pp.133–139).

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** Six beads on a necklace that can be flipped over can be arranged in ______ ways.
**Answer:** 60 — (6−1)!/2 = 120/2 (Kuwait, Grade 11 Term 2, pp.152–161).

**7. (Sub lesson 2)** Mauritania's 6AS-D counting chapter (Ch.VIII, p.147) opens with set language and an ______-draw model.
**Answer:** urn — the chapter's counting model is drawing from an urn.

**8. (Sub lesson 3)** The non-negative whole-number solutions of x₁ + x₂ + x₃ = 7 number ______. (Iran, Discrete Mathematics, Grade 12, Ch.3.)
**Answer:** 36 — distributing 7 identical objects into 3 boxes: C(9,2) = 36.

**9. (Sub lesson 2)** C(10,7) = C(10, ______). (Egypt, Pure Mathematics, Grade 11 Term 2, Unit 2.)
**Answer:** 3 — choosing the 7 taken is choosing the 3 left.

**10. (Sub lesson 1)** The duty order of three pupils, listed on an option tree, has ______ variants. (Kazakhstan, Aldamuratova Grade 6, §8.3, p.141.)
**Answer:** 6 — 3 × 2 × 1 = 6, found by listing the tree.

**11. (Sub lesson 2)** Four people sit around a round table in ______ ways. (Nicaragua, Grade 11, Unit 6, p.124.)
**Answer:** 6 — (4−1)! = 3! = 6; rotations are the same seating.
