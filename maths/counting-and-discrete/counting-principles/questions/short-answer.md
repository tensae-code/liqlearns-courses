# Counting Principles — short answer

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — The Fundamental Principle and Factorials · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Permutations, Repeats and Restrictions · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Combinations and Circular Permutations.

Answer in a word or a short line.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** 2 shirts, 3 trousers, 2 hats — how many outfits? (Yemen's Grade 12 Literary book opens with this kind of count, pp.6–16.)
**Answer:** 12 — 2 × 3 × 2, steps multiply.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** Compute 6!.
**Answer:** 720 — 6 × 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1.

**3. (Sub lesson 1)** A tree diagram for a two-step choice has 5 branches at the first step and 3 at the second. How many leaves has it?
**Answer:** 15 — the leaves are the outcomes, and outcomes multiply (Pakistan, Class 11, Unit 7).

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** Compute ⁷P₂.
**Answer:** 42 — 7 × 6.

**5. (Sub lesson 2)** Five different books stand on a shelf, but one named book must stand at the left end. How many arrangements are there?
**Answer:** 24 — the restricted place is filled first (1 way), then 4! = 24 for the rest.

**6. (Sub lesson 2)** How many 2-digit numbers can be made from the digits 1–9 with no repeat?
**Answer:** 72 — 9 × 8; the first digit cannot be 0 and digits here run 1–9.

**7. (Sub lesson 3)** Compute C(8,3).
**Answer:** 56 — 8! / (3! × 5!) = (8 × 7 × 6) / 6.

**8. (Sub lesson 3)** A hexagon is drawn. How many diagonals has it?
**Answer:** 9 — C(6,2) = 15 segments between vertices, minus the 6 sides (Pakistan, Class 11, pp.133–139).

**9. (Sub lesson 2)** In how many ways can 5 pupils line up in a row? (Mauritania's Dénombrement chapters count line arrangements first.)
**Answer:** 120 — 5! = 120.

**10. (Sub lesson 2)** From 6 books, 4 are chosen (order does not matter). How many choices are there? (Iran, Mathematics (1), Grade 10, Ch.6.)
**Answer:** 15 — C(6,4) = 15; arranging the same 4 would give 360, which is the contrast the book teaches.

**11. (Sub lesson 2)** Compute C(8,2). (Egypt, General Mathematics, Grade 11 Term 2, Unit 2.)
**Answer:** 28 — (8 × 7) / 2 = 28.

**12. (Sub lesson 3)** In how many ways can 8 students be chosen from 20? (Cambodia, Grade 12 Probability booklet [PRIVATE], p.28.)
**Answer:** 125,970 — C(20,8) = 125,970.

**13. (Sub lesson 1)** Five points stand on a line. How many segments join pairs of them? (Kazakhstan, Aldamuratova Grade 6, §8.3, p.141.)
**Answer:** 10 — listed as 4 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 10 from the option tree.

**14. (Sub lesson 2)** Six trees of different species are planted around a roundabout. In how many orders can they stand? (Nicaragua, Grade 11, Unit 6, p.124.)
**Answer:** 120 — circular arrangements: (6−1)! = 5! = 120.

**15. (Sub lesson 2)** Compute P(8,3). (Iraq, Grade 11 Scientific, Ch.8.)
**Answer:** 336 — 8 × 7 × 6 = 336.

**16. (Sub lesson 2)** Compute C(7,3). (Jordan, Grade 11 Term 1, Unit 3 L1, p.120.)
**Answer:** 35 — (7 × 6 × 5) / (3 × 2 × 1) = 35.

**17. (Sub lesson 2)** A president and a vice-president are chosen from 8 pupils. How many pairs are there? (Ethiopia, Grade 11, Unit 8.)
**Answer:** 56 — 8 × 7 = 56; the two posts differ, so order matters.

**18. (Sub lesson 1)** Compute P(6,2). (South Sudan, Secondary 4, Unit 3, pp.56–59.)
**Answer:** 30 — 6 × 5 = 30.
