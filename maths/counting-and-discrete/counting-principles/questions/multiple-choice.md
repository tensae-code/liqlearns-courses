# Counting Principles — multiple choice

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — The Fundamental Principle and Factorials · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Permutations, Repeats and Restrictions · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Combinations and Circular Permutations.

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** A road trip has 3 possible first stops and 4 possible second stops. How many two-stop routes are there?
a) 7  b) 12  c) 34
**Answer:** b) 12 — steps in a row multiply: 3 × 4 = 12.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** What is 5! ?
a) 15  b) 25  c) 120
**Answer:** c) 120 — 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 120.

**3. (Sub lesson 1)** What is 0! ?
a) 0  b) 1  c) undefined
**Answer:** b) 1 — 0! = 1 by definition (Pakistan, Class 11, Unit 7).

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** In how many ways can 4 runners take 1st, 2nd and 3rd place (no ties)?
a) 4  b) 12  c) 24
**Answer:** c) 24 — ⁴P₃ = 4 × 3 × 2 = 24; order matters.

**5. (Sub lesson 2)** A 3-digit code uses digits 0–9 with repeats allowed. How many codes are there?
a) 30  b) 720  c) 1,000
**Answer:** c) 1,000 — 10 choices for each of the 3 places: 10³ (Kuwait, Grade 10 Term 2).

**6. (Sub lesson 2)** How many arrangements are there of the letters of LEVEL, where the two L's are identical and the two E's are identical?
a) 120  b) 30  c) 60
**Answer:** b) 30 — 5! / (2! × 2!) = 120 / 4 = 30.

**7. (Sub lesson 3)** From 10 students, a committee of 3 is chosen. How many committees are there?
a) 120  b) 720  c) 30
**Answer:** a) 120 — C(10,3) = 10! / (3! × 7!) = 120; order does not matter.

**8. (Sub lesson 3)** Eight people sit around a round table. Rotations count as the same. How many seatings are there?
a) 40,320  b) 5,040  c) 720
**Answer:** b) 5,040 — (8−1)! = 7! = 5,040; one seat is fixed first.

**9. (Sub lesson 2)** A journey is chosen in 3 ways to a town and then in 5 ways onward. How many complete journeys are there? (Mauritania's streamed books each open a Dénombrement chapter with this kind of staged count.)
a) 8  b) 15  c) 35
**Answer:** b) 15 — steps in a row multiply: 3 × 5 = 15.

**10. (Sub lesson 2)** The 3-subsets of {1, 2, 4, 6} are listed one by one. How many are there? (Iran, Mathematics and Statistics (3), Grade 12, p.9.)
a) 3  b) 4  c) 12
**Answer:** b) 4 — the list has 4 entries, and C(4,3) = 4.

**11. (Sub lesson 2)** What is P(5,2)? (Egypt, Pure Mathematics, Grade 11 Term 2, Unit 2.)
a) 10  b) 20  c) 25
**Answer:** b) 20 — 5 × 4 = 20; order matters.

**12. (Sub lesson 2)** Eight students stand in a row for a photograph. How many orders are possible? (Cambodia, Grade 12 Probability booklet [PRIVATE], pp.22–27.)
a) 40,320  b) 5,040  c) 64
**Answer:** a) 40,320 — 8! = 40,320.

**13. (Sub lesson 3)** A team is picked as 3 from a group of 4 and 4 from a group of 6. How many teams are there? (Kazakhstan, Yelemessov Grade 9, Ch.2.)
a) 24  b) 60  c) 120
**Answer:** b) 60 — C(4,3) × C(6,4) = 4 × 15 = 60.

**14. (Sub lesson 1)** A book cover is chosen by title colour (2 choices) and background colour (4 choices). How many covers are there? (Nicaragua, Grade 11, p.118.)
a) 6  b) 8  c) 24
**Answer:** b) 8 — 2 × 4 = 8 combinations on the tree diagram.

**15. (Sub lesson 1)** A three-step choice can be made in 3, then 4, then 6 ways. How many outcomes are there? (Iraq, Grade 11 Scientific, Ch.8.)
a) 13  b) 24  c) 72
**Answer:** c) 72 — 3 × 4 × 6 = 72.

**16. (Sub lesson 1)** A coin is tossed twice. How many outcomes are in the sample space? (Jordan, Grade 8 Term 2, Unit 9 L3, p.161.)
a) 2  b) 3  c) 4
**Answer:** c) 4 — HH, HT, TH, TT: the outcomes are counted first, before any probability.

**17. (Sub lesson 2)** In how many ways can 5 different flags be arranged in a line? (Ethiopia, Grade 11, Unit 8.)
a) 25  b) 120  c) 720
**Answer:** b) 120 — 5! = 120 arrangements.

**18. (Sub lesson 3)** The letters of LOGARITHMS are all different. In how many ways can they be arranged? (South Sudan, Secondary 4, Unit 3, pp.56–59.)
a) 362,880  b) 3,628,800  c) 100
**Answer:** b) 3,628,800 — 10 distinct letters give 10! = 3,628,800.
