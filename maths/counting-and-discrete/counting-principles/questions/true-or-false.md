# Counting Principles — true or false

Say true or false, and why.

**1. (Level 1)** True or false: 4! = 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 24.
**Answer:** True — that is the factorial product (Yemen's Grade 12 Literary book checks it as P(4,4) = 24).

**2. (Level 1)** True or false: choices offered as alternatives — this one or that one — are counted by multiplying.
**Answer:** False — alternatives add; steps in a row multiply (Vietnam, Grade 10 Volume 2, Ch.VIII).

**3. (Level 2)** True or false: a permutation changes if two of its items swap places.
**Answer:** True — order is the whole content of a permutation.

**4. (Level 2)** True or false: with repeats allowed, more codes are possible than without repeats.
**Answer:** True — every place keeps all its choices, so nothing is used up.

**5. (Level 3)** True or false: C(4,2) = 6, and listing the pairs of 4 teams gives the same 6.
**Answer:** True — formula and list agree (China, Grade 3 Volume 2, pp.101–105).

**6. (Level 3)** True or false: n people around a round table can be seated in n! ways.
**Answer:** False — rotations are the same seating, so the count is (n−1)!.

**7. (Level 3)** True or false: in the repeated-items formula, the denominator takes a factorial of the whole product n₁! × n₂! × … × n_p!.
**Answer:** False — Cambodia's Grade 12 Probability booklet [PRIVATE] prints it that way on p.23, but that is a book error: the denominator is the product itself, with no extra factorial.

**8. (Level 2)** True or false: a bicycle offered in 5 types, 3 sizes and 6 colours gives 90 bicycles. (Iraq, Grade 12 Literary, Ch.1, p.6.)
**Answer:** True — 5 × 3 × 6 = 90 by the fundamental counting principle.

**9. (Level 2)** True or false: in Jordan's Grade 8 Term 2 book, counting outcomes (Unit 9 L3, p.161) comes before the probability of compound events (L4, p.166).
**Answer:** True — outcomes and the sample space are counted first, then compound events are priced from them.

**10. (Level 2)** True or false: swapping two members of a chosen team gives a new team. (Ethiopia, Grade 11, Unit 8.)
**Answer:** False — a team is a combination; swapping its members changes nothing.

**11. (Level 1)** True or false: South Sudan's Secondary 4 book follows its permutation and combination formulae with 30 multiple-choice questions.
**Answer:** True — the formulae and worked examples at pp.56–59 are tested by 30 multiple-choice questions, as filed.
