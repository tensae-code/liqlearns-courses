# Congruences and Diophantine equations — short answer

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Remainders and a Modulus · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Congruences, Tests and Euclid's Algorithm · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Linear Congruences and Diophantine Equations.

Answer in a word or a short line.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Give the simplest form of −3 in mod 7.
**Answer:** 4 — add 7: −3 + 7 = 4 (Sierra Leone, L22, p.62).

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** Compute (5 × 4) mod 6.
**Answer:** 2 — 20 = 3×6 + 2 (Sierra Leone, L23, p.65).

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Today is Wednesday. What day is it in 50 days?
**Answer:** Thursday — 50 = 7×7 + 1, so the remainder 1 moves one day on (Iran, p.24).

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** Find gcd(252, 198) by Euclid's algorithm.
**Answer:** 18 — 252 = 198 + 54; 198 = 3×54 + 36; 54 = 36 + 18; 36 = 2×18 + 0.

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Solve 2x ≡ 4 (mod 6), giving all solutions mod 6.
**Answer:** x ≡ 2 or x ≡ 5 (mod 6) — gcd(2, 6) = 2 divides 4, so there are two solutions mod 6 (Iran, p.25).

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** Find all non-negative whole-number solutions of 3x + 5y = 22.
**Answer:** (x, y) = (4, 2) only — the general solution steps by (5, −3) from any one solution, and only (4, 2) stays non-negative (Iran, pp.26–29).
