**Acceptance count: taught in 2 countries (Bolivia, Armenia).**

This lesson exists because Bolivia's books carry a topic no other read country teaches as a strand: a mathematics laboratory of chess and sudoku set as logic training, designed progressively across four Secundaria years — Chess I with Sudoku (Secundaria 1), Chess II with Sudoku (Secundaria 2), Chess V (Secundaria 5) and Chess VI with advanced rules and mate-in-two puzzles (Secundaria 6). The course's investigations lesson absorbs one-off enrichment items inside other units, and its latin-squares lesson is Iran's theory lesson with no Sudoku in it; neither is a genuine home for a named four-year reasoning strand, so — as with the four-means lesson (North Macedonia) and the Euler's totient lesson (North Macedonia) before it — the topic gets its own lesson under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06). The worked positions and the 4×4 training sudoku in the lesson text are constructed for this lesson in the style of the compendium's exercises and are verified correct; what is cited to the book is the strand itself — the laboratory, its year-by-year progression, and its exercise types (mate-in-one and mate-in-two reasoning, sudoku as logic training).

Bolivia enters through the Área Matemática sections of its Ministerio de Educación (Estado Plurinacional de Bolivia) Textos de Aprendizaje — the student books for Grades 7, 8, 10 and 12, each the maths section of that year's integrated year book, keeping the whole book's printed foliation, with no imprint or ISBN printed in any of them — and through the Compendio para Maestras y Maestros 2023–2024, the ministry's teacher compilation of the official student texts for all six Secundaria years (Grades 7–12), cited as teacher-compilation evidence only, never as or in place of a pupil text; its Secundaria 3 and Secundaria 5 sections are the only in-hand source for Grades 9 and 11, whose student texts are not in the record.
