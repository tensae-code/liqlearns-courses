# Levels — Euler's Totient and Multiplicative Functions

One lesson, one level (Level 157). The depth layers inside it are ranks.

## Depth layers

- **Layer 1 — The count.** Say what φ(m) counts — the numbers from 1 to m coprime to m, the elements of a reduced residue system — and find φ(m) for small m by listing. Pass mark: 4 of 5.
- **Layer 2 — The table and the primes.** Read values off the book's table φ(1)…φ(12), and use φ(p) = p − 1 at primes. Pass mark: 4 of 5.
- **Layer 3 — Multiplicative, with the condition.** State multiplicativity correctly — f(mn) = f(m)·f(n) for coprime m, n — and compute φ of a composite from a coprime split. Pass mark: 4 of 5.
- **Layer 4 — Catch the slip.** Show, from the table, why φ does NOT split over non-coprime factors: φ(4) = 2 against φ(2)·φ(2) = 1; name the hypothesis the printed Theorem 1 dropped. Pass mark: 4 of 5.
- **Layer 5 — Bigger values.** Compute φ at numbers beyond the table by splitting into coprime factors whose values are known, e.g. φ(36) = φ(4)·φ(9) = 12. Pass mark: 4 of 5.

## What "maxed" means here

The learner can define a multiplicative function with its coprimality condition, can say what Euler's φ counts, can read and extend its table, can use φ(p) = p − 1 and the coprime split to compute values without listing, and can catch the book's own printed overstatement using the book's own table — the exact skills North Macedonia's Grade 12 Algebra book teaches in §§2.2 and 2.5 (printed pp. 28 and 37), with Theorem 1 taught corrected.
