# Hands-on — Algorithmic Thinking

## Activity 1 — Draw it and trace it (15 minutes)

Draw the flowchart for "is this number divisible by 3?" on
paper: a Start oval, an input box for the number, a process
box ("add the digits"), a decision diamond ("is the digit-sum
divisible by 3?") with Yes and No exits, an output box, and an
End oval. Trace three numbers through it, following the arrows
with your finger and saying each answer out loud: 342
(digit-sum 9 → Yes), 341 (digit-sum 8 → No), 345 (digit-sum 12
→ Yes). Check each one by real division. This is Sub lesson 1
done on paper.

## Activity 2 — Debug a lying chart (10 minutes)

A partner draws a flowchart for "is this number divisible by
3?" but its decision diamond wrongly asks "does the number end
in 3, 6 or 9?" Trace the number 342 through it — it ends in 2,
so the chart says No, but 342 ÷ 3 = 114 exactly, so the chart
is wrong. Say exactly which box holds the mistake, fix the
diamond to the digit-sum test, and trace again until three
known numbers come out right. This is the debugging half of
Sub lesson 1.

## Activity 3 — Sort, search, generate (15 minutes)

Write seven numbers on cards in a messy order. Sort them by
comparing and swapping neighbouring pairs, moving the cards
with your hands, until a full pass makes no swap. Then search
the sorted row for a number your partner names — check one
card at a time, counting the checks — and search for a number
that is not there, stopping as soon as the cards prove it
cannot come later. Last, roll a die ten times, writing each
result in order: that list is generated by simulation. Sort it
too, and search it for a 6. This is Sub lesson 2 with your own
hands.
