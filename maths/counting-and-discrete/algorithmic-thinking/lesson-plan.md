A lesson that teaches thinking like a machine that cannot guess:
say exactly what goes in, exactly what comes out, and the exact
steps between. Australia's Book A3 — *Essential Mathematics for
the Victorian Curriculum Year 8, Third Edition* (Greenwood et
al., Cambridge University Press, ©2024) — carries a whole
chapter on it, Chapter 11, Algorithmic thinking (printed
pp. 926–941, bound after the Answers in the held file although
its contents lists the chapter as "(online only)"). The
chapter's Introduction and Activity 1 (printed pp. 926–927)
teach algorithms and flowcharts — divisibility tests written
and debugged as flowcharts, with inputs and outputs defined —
and its Activity 2 (printed p. ~929) teaches working with lists
of data: sorting, searching, and generating lists by
simulation. No lesson in this course taught either before; the
expansion read flagged both NEW LESSON NEEDED and this lesson
is built from that flag, founded on a pupil textbook, so
Australia counts in it. Jacaranda's Maths Quest 10+10A (Book
A7) lists a parallel algorithmic-thinking chapter in its
contents, but that chapter is NOT in the held file — if its
file is ever secured, its items belong to this lesson too.
Prerequisite (soft hint only — every lesson stands alone):
none; the divisibility the flowcharts test is met inside the
lesson itself.
