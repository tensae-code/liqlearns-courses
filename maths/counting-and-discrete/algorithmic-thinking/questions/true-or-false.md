# True or false — Algorithmic Thinking

Sub-lesson markers: every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to — Sub lesson 1 — Algorithms and Flowcharts · Sub lesson 2 — Working with Lists of Data. This lesson was built new with its sub lessons (Australia expansion merge, 2026-11-11), founded on Book A3's Chapter 11, so its markers are original — there are no older depth layers to map from.

1. (Sub lesson 1) An algorithm's steps may leave room for guessing and still be an algorithm.
   **Answer: False** — every step must say exactly what to do; guessing is what an algorithm leaves out. Item constructed for this lesson from its founding chapter. Australia, *Essential Mathematics for the Victorian Curriculum Year 8, Third Edition* (Greenwood et al., Cambridge University Press, ©2024), Chapter 11 (Algorithmic thinking), Introduction + Activity 1 — algorithms and flowcharts, printed pp. 926–927.

2. (Sub lesson 1) A decision diamond in a flowchart has two exits, labelled Yes and No.
   **Answer: True.** Item constructed for this lesson from its founding chapter. Australia, *Essential Mathematics for the Victorian Curriculum Year 8, Third Edition* (Greenwood et al., Cambridge University Press, ©2024), Chapter 11 (Algorithmic thinking), Introduction + Activity 1 — algorithms and flowcharts, printed pp. 926–927.

3. (Sub lesson 1) A flowchart that gives the right answer for one test number is proved correct.
   **Answer: False** — it must be traced with several numbers whose answers are already known; one lucky number proves nothing, and debugging is the tracing that finds a wrong step. Item constructed for this lesson from its founding chapter. Australia, *Essential Mathematics for the Victorian Curriculum Year 8, Third Edition* (Greenwood et al., Cambridge University Press, ©2024), Chapter 11 (Algorithmic thinking), Introduction + Activity 1 — algorithms and flowcharts, printed pp. 926–927.

4. (Sub lesson 2) Sorting a list changes which items are in the list.
   **Answer: False** — sorting changes only the order; the same items go in and come out. Item constructed for this lesson from its founding chapter. Australia, *Essential Mathematics for the Victorian Curriculum Year 8, Third Edition* (Greenwood et al., Cambridge University Press, ©2024), Chapter 11 (Algorithmic thinking), Activity 2 — working with lists of data, printed p. ~929.

5. (Sub lesson 2) In a sorted list, a search can stop early once the items have passed the value being searched for.
   **Answer: True** — everything further along is bigger still, so the value cannot appear later. Item constructed for this lesson from its founding chapter. Australia, *Essential Mathematics for the Victorian Curriculum Year 8, Third Edition* (Greenwood et al., Cambridge University Press, ©2024), Chapter 11 (Algorithmic thinking), Activity 2 — working with lists of data, printed p. ~929.

6. (Sub lesson 2) A list generated by simulation is not data, because chance made the numbers.
   **Answer: False** — it is a list of data like any other: it can be sorted, searched and counted. Item constructed for this lesson from its founding chapter. Australia, *Essential Mathematics for the Victorian Curriculum Year 8, Third Edition* (Greenwood et al., Cambridge University Press, ©2024), Chapter 11 (Algorithmic thinking), Activity 2 — working with lists of data, printed p. ~929.
