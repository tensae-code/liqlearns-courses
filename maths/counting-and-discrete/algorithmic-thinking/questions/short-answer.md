1. (Sub lesson 1) A flowchart for the test "divisible by 3" has a decision diamond that asks "does the number end in 3, 6 or 9?" Trace the number 342 through it. What does the chart say, what is the truth, and what is the fix?
   **Answer: the chart says No, because 342 ends in 2. The truth is Yes — 342 ÷ 3 = 114 exactly. The fix is to replace the decision with the digit-sum test: add the digits (3 + 4 + 2 = 9) and ask whether the sum is divisible by 3.** Item constructed for this lesson from its founding chapter. Australia, *Essential Mathematics for the Victorian Curriculum Year 8, Third Edition* (Greenwood et al., Cambridge University Press, ©2024), Chapter 11 (Algorithmic thinking), Introduction + Activity 1 — algorithms and flowcharts, printed pp. 926–927.

2. (Sub lesson 1) State the input and the output of the algorithm "is this number divisible by 5?"
   **Answer: the input is the number; the output is Yes or No — whether the number is divisible by 5.** Item constructed for this lesson from its founding chapter. 3. (Sub lesson 2) Search the list 5, 3, 8, 1 for the value 8 with a linear search. How many checks does it take, and where is 8 found?
   **Answer: three checks — 5 (no), 3 (no), 8 (yes) — 8 is found at the third place in the list.** Item constructed for this lesson from its founding chapter. Australia, *Essential Mathematics for the Victorian Curriculum Year 8, Third Edition* (Greenwood et al., Cambridge University Press, ©2024), Chapter 11 (Algorithmic thinking), Activity 2 — working with lists of data, printed p. ~929.

4. (Sub lesson 2) A learner rolls a die ten times and records: 2, 6, 1, 6, 3, 6, 2, 4, 6, 5. How many 6s are in the list, and what is this way of building a list called?
   **Answer: four 6s. Building a list by recording the results of a repeated chance action is generating a list by simulation.** Item constructed for this lesson from its founding chapter. Australia, *Essential Mathematics for the Victorian Curriculum Year 8, Third Edition* (Greenwood et al., Cambridge University Press, ©2024), Chapter 11 (Algorithmic thinking), Activity 2 — working with lists of data, printed p. ~929.
