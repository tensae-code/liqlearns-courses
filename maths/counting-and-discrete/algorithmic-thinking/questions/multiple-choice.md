1. (Sub lesson 1) An algorithm is:
   (a) a lucky guess at the answer
   (b) a set of steps so exact that following them always gives the answer ✔
   (c) a drawing of a shape
   (d) the answer itself, written first
   **Answer: (b).** Item constructed for this lesson from its founding chapter. 3. (Sub lesson 1) In a flowchart, a decision is drawn as:
   (a) a rectangle
   (b) a rounded oval
   (c) a diamond ✔
   (d) a triangle
   **Answer: (c)** — the diamond holds a question, and its two exits are labelled Yes and No. Item constructed for this lesson from its founding chapter. Australia, *Essential Mathematics for the Victorian Curriculum Year 8, Third Edition* (Greenwood et al., Cambridge University Press, ©2024), Chapter 11 (Algorithmic thinking), Introduction + Activity 1 — algorithms and flowcharts, printed pp. 926–927.

4. (Sub lesson 1) Tracing 342 through the digit-sum flowchart for "divisible by 3" gives:
   (a) No, because 342 ends in 2
   (b) Yes, because 3 + 4 + 2 = 9, and 9 is divisible by 3 ✔
   (c) Yes, because 342 ends in 3
   (d) No, because 342 is even
   **Answer: (b)** — and 342 ÷ 3 = 114 exactly, so the chart told the truth. Item constructed for this lesson from its founding chapter. Australia, *Essential Mathematics for the Victorian Curriculum Year 8, Third Edition* (Greenwood et al., Cambridge University Press, ©2024), Chapter 11 (Algorithmic thinking), Introduction + Activity 1 — algorithms and flowcharts, printed pp. 926–927.

6. (Sub lesson 2) A list of data is generated by simulation when:
   (a) the teacher writes numbers from memory
   (b) the results of a repeated chance action are recorded in order ✔
   (c) the list is copied from another book
   (d) the numbers are arranged in alphabetical order
   **Answer: (b)** — for example, rolling a die twenty times and writing down every result. Item constructed for this lesson from its founding chapter.
