# Hands-on — Platonic solids and Euler's formula

Building the solids and testing the law on them.

## Build from nets (Nicaragua Grade 10)
Build the five regular polyhedra from coloured card nets, the group activity of Nicaragua's Unit 6 (pp.304–308): tetrahedron, cube, octahedron, dodecahedron, icosahedron. If time is short, build the tetrahedron, cube and octahedron first.

## Count and check (South Africa Grade 8, WS130)
For each built solid, count faces, vertices and edges. Mark each edge with a pencil dot as you count it so none is counted twice. Fill the table: Solid | F | V | E | V + F − E.

## Break the law — try to
Repeat the count on a triangular prism, a square pyramid and a frustum (a pyramid with its top cut off), the solids of China's A-edition table exercise. Write down what V + F − E gives each time.

## The sixth-solid hunt
Try to design a sixth regular polyhedron on paper: pick a regular face shape and a number meeting at each vertex, and add the face angles at one vertex. Show, with the sum, why every candidate fails — it reaches 360° or passes it, so the vertex lies flat or will not close.
