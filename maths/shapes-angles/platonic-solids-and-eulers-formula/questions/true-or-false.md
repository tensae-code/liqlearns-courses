# Platonic solids and Euler's formula — true or false

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Platonic Solids The Five Solids · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Platonic Solids Counting Faces, Vertices and Edges · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Platonic Solids Euler's Formula.

Say true or false, and say why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** There are six regular polyhedra.
**Answer:** False — there are exactly five (Nicaragua, Grade 10, pp.304–308).

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** Every face of a regular polyhedron is the same regular polygon.
**Answer:** True — that is half of the definition; the same number must also meet at every vertex.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** The octahedron and the cube have the same number of edges.
**Answer:** True — both have E 12.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** The icosahedron has 20 vertices.
**Answer:** False — it has 20 faces and 12 vertices.

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Euler's formula works only for the five Platonic solids.
**Answer:** False — it holds for prisms, pyramids and frustums too — China's A edition sets it as a table exercise over exactly those.
