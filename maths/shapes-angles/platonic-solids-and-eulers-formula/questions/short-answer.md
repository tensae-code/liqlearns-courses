# Platonic solids and Euler's formula — short answer

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Platonic Solids The Five Solids · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Platonic Solids Counting Faces, Vertices and Edges · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Platonic Solids Euler's Formula.

Answer in a word or a short line.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Name the five regular polyhedra.
**Answer:** Tetrahedron, hexahedron (cube), octahedron, dodecahedron, icosahedron.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** Which Platonic solid is a common die shape, and what are its faces?
**Answer:** The cube (hexahedron); its faces are squares.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Give F, V and E for the tetrahedron.
**Answer:** F 4, V 4, E 6.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** Which two Platonic solids swap their F and V counts with each other? Give one pair.
**Answer:** Cube (F 6, V 8) and octahedron (F 8, V 6) — or dodecahedron (F 12, V 20) and icosahedron (F 20, V 12).

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Check Euler's formula for a triangular prism (F 5, V 6, E 9).
**Answer:** V + F − E = 6 + 5 − 9 = 2. It holds.

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** At a vertex, three squares meet in a cube. What is the angle sum, and why does it allow a solid?
**Answer:** 3 × 90° = 270°, which is less than 360°, so the vertex closes into a corner instead of lying flat.
