# Lesson plan — Constructing triangles and quadrilaterals

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 7 — Shapes and Angles · **Lesson:** Constructing triangles and quadrilaterals
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 61 of 136, Unit 7 — Shapes and Angles (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** building an exact triangle or quadrilateral from given sides and angles with ruler and compasses — SSS, SAS, ASA and RHS triangles, and the whole quadrilateral family.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Constructions with ruler and compass (Level 60, same stage) — bisection and the special angles. It needs nothing else.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Construct a triangle from three sides (SSS) (Sierra Leone, SSS 2, L84, p.103).
2. Construct a triangle from two sides and the included angle (SAS), following a five-step build (Yemen, Grade 5 Part 2, p.145; Sierra Leone, SSS 2, L85, p.106).
3. Construct a triangle from two angles and a side (ASA) (Sierra Leone, SSS 2, L86, p.109).
4. Construct a right-angled triangle from its hypotenuse and one side (RHS) (Pakistan, Grade 8, pp.161–170).
5. Construct a square and a rectangle from given sides (Yemen, Grade 4 Part 2, pp.100, 103; Sierra Leone, SSS 2, L87, p.112).
6. Construct a rhombus and a parallelogram from two sides and an angle (Sierra Leone, SSS 2, L88, p.115).
7. Construct a trapezium, a kite and the remaining quadrilaterals (Sierra Leone, SSS 2, L89, p.118; Bangladesh, Class 8, Exp.5, pp.93–124).
8. Construct the incentre and circumcentre of a triangle (Pakistan, Grade 8, pp.161–170).

## What you need
A pair of compasses, a ruler, a sharp pencil, a protractor for the given angles, and paper.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Take three sticks of different lengths. Can you always make a triangle from them? Lay the two short ones end to end against the long one — if together they do not reach past it, no triangle exists. When they do reach, the triangle they make is fixed: there is only one shape they can form. That fixedness is this lesson — a small set of givens builds exactly one figure. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — SSS, SAS, ASA and RHS triangles first, then squares and rectangles, rhombi and parallelograms, trapeziums and kites, and the two triangle centres (lesson/lesson.md).
3. **Say it to your companion** — Give your companion a set of givens — "sides 5, 6, 7" or "two sides 6 and 4, angle between them 50°" — and name the construction (SSS or SAS) before they start. They build it; you measure the finished figure against the givens. Trade five times, mixing triangles and quadrilaterals. (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — constructing triangles and quadrilaterals in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching, ordering — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — 1. Construct an SSS triangle with sides 7 cm, 6 cm and 5 cm, and check all three sides by measuring.
2. Construct a rhombus with sides 5 cm and one angle of 60° (construct the angle, do not measure it).
3. Construct a trapezium and a kite from givens you choose; write the givens beside each figure.
4. Construct a triangle, then find its incentre and its circumcentre on the same drawing, each labelled. (lesson/lesson.md).

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- GIF — a picture of an SSS triangle growing: the base drawn, then one arc from each end swelling until they cross at the third corner → the student draws each arc as it appears and joins the crossing point.
- GIF — a picture of a parallelogram sliding from a rectangle as its top side is pushed sideways, its sides and angle staying marked → the student says what stayed equal and what changed.
- IMAGE — a picture of a triangle with its three angle bisectors meeting at one inside point and its three perpendicular bisectors meeting at one point → the student labels which point is the incentre and which the circumcentre.

## If the learner is stuck
- The two arcs for the third corner of an SSS triangle do not meet → the two given sides together are shorter than the base: no triangle exists with those lengths. Check the three lengths first.
- An SAS triangle comes out the wrong shape → the angle must be the INCLUDED angle, the one between the two given sides (Yemen's five steps put it there, Grade 5 Part 2, p.145).
- A rhombus construction gives a square instead → a square is the special case: if the given angle is 90° the rhombus is a square. Use a non-right angle for a slanted rhombus.
- This lesson's next door: Tessellations (same stage, Level 62) — constructed regular shapes are the tiles that fit without gaps.
