# Constructing triangles and quadrilaterals — the lesson

Course: Maths · Stage: Stage 7 — Shapes and Angles · Lesson: Constructing triangles and quadrilaterals
Built from: Pakistan, Grades 7 and 8 — Bangladesh, Class 8 — Yemen, Grades 4 and 5 — Sierra Leone, SSS 2 Handbook

## Do it first
Take three sticks of different lengths. Can you always make a triangle from them? Lay the two short ones end to end against the long one — if together they do not reach past it, no triangle exists. When they do reach, the triangle they make is fixed: there is only one shape they can form. That fixedness is this lesson — a small set of givens builds exactly one figure.

## Then the explanation
**Triangles from three sides — SSS.** Draw the longest side as the base. Open the compasses to the second side and arc from one end of the base; open them to the third side and arc from the other end. Where the arcs cross is the third corner (Sierra Leone, SSS 2, L84, p.103; Pakistan, Grade 8, pp.161–170). One set of three lengths gives one triangle — that is why SSS is a congruence test as well as a construction.

**Triangles from sides and angles — SAS and ASA.** For SAS, draw one side, construct the given angle at its end — the angle must be the one **between** the two given sides — mark the second side along the angle's arm, and join. Yemen's Grade 5 book steps this build in five steps (Part 2, 8-3, p.145), and Sierra Leone gives it its own lesson (SSS 2, L85, p.106). For ASA, draw the given side, construct the two given angles at its two ends, and the arms meet at the third corner (Sierra Leone, SSS 2, L86, p.109).

**Right-angled triangles — RHS.** A right-angled triangle is fixed by its hypotenuse and one other side: Pakistan's Grade 7 practical geometry builds right-angled triangles from their legs and from hypotenuse plus leg (pp.141–148), and Grade 8 carries RHS among its construction cases (pp.161–170).

**Quadrilaterals.** A square needs one side and its right angles; a rectangle needs its length and width — Yemen's Grade 4 book constructs both, the rectangle from its length and width (Part 2, pp.100, 103), and Sierra Leone pairs them in one lesson (SSS 2, L87, p.112). A rhombus and a parallelogram are built from two sides and the angle between them (Sierra Leone, SSS 2, L88, p.115): construct the angle, mark the two sides on its arms, then complete the parallel sides. A trapezium is built from three sides and an angle, and the kite from its two pairs of adjacent equal sides (Sierra Leone, SSS 2, L89, p.118; Pakistan, Grade 8, pp.161–170). Bangladesh's Class 8 Exp.5 (pp.93–124) is a whole chapter of quadrilateral constructions worked this way.

**The two centres.** Bisect a triangle's three angles and the bisectors meet at one point inside — the **incentre**. Bisect its three sides at right angles and those perpendicular bisectors meet at the **circumcentre** (Pakistan, Grade 8, pp.161–170). Sierra Leone finishes the course by turning word problems into constructions (SSS 2, L90, p.121; L91, p.124): read the words, list the givens, then build.

> Tip — from us, not from the books. Check every construction, tiling or count a second way — measure it, fold it, or count it again in a different order — before you trust it. A drawing that only works once is not finished yet.

## Say it to your companion
Give your companion a set of givens — "sides 5, 6, 7" or "two sides 6 and 4, angle between them 50°" — and name the construction (SSS or SAS) before they start. They build it; you measure the finished figure against the givens. Trade five times, mixing triangles and quadrilaterals.

## Do It Yourself
1. Construct an SSS triangle with sides 7 cm, 6 cm and 5 cm, and check all three sides by measuring.
2. Construct a rhombus with sides 5 cm and one angle of 60° (construct the angle, do not measure it).
3. Construct a trapezium and a kite from givens you choose; write the givens beside each figure.
4. Construct a triangle, then find its incentre and its circumcentre on the same drawing, each labelled.
