disc area in Grade 6. Jordan carries the parts in Grade 6 and
then circumference and area in Grade 7. Sierra Leone carries
the parts with d = 2r in JSS 1 and both formulas in JSS 2.
Mauritania carries radius and diameter in Grade 5 and
circumference and disc area in Grade 6. Libya carries radius,
diameter, circumference and area in Grade 6, with pi as 3.14 and
as 22/7. Egypt carries the circle's definitions in Grade 9.

carries circle, arc and degree in Grade 5 and the circle chapter
in Geometry 9. Vietnam carries centre, radius and diameter in
Grade 3. South Africa carries circle area from the radius in
Grade 8. Ghana is counted separately and honestly: its Basic 8
curriculum states the diameter–circumference relationship as a
standard, but no pupil textbook in Ghana's set teaches circles,
so Ghana is curriculum evidence only, not a teaching country. Laos carries circles from circumference and area with π ≈ 3.14 in Grade 5 (with the book's 87,5 cm² slip corrected to 78.5 cm²), through circle parts and inscribed and central angles in Grades 7–8, to circle equations in Grade 10. Yemen carries circles from the circle and its circumference and area in Grade 6, through the full circle unit in Grade 9, to the equation of the circle and tangents in Grade 11 science. Indonesia carries circles from the circle named among plane shapes in Grade 2, through circumference, area, arc length and sector area in Grade 9, to arcs, tangents, chords and cyclic quadrilaterals in Grade 11 (both the 2021 Merdeka and 2024 Revisi regular books) and arcs and sectors in Grade 12. Honest negatives: Grade 1 teaches no circle as a shape, and Grade 5 teaches no circles at all. Nicaragua carries circles from drawing circles in Grade 3, through circumference (but not area) in Grade 5 and circle area with sectors in Grade 6, to circumference, circle area, arc length and sector area in Grade 7 and the circle's notable angles in the Grade 8 module and Grade 9 — with the Grade 10 sector-area exercise's impossible numbers (25 m² in a 1.5 cm circle) corrected, not taught. The Grade 11 analytic-geometry circle (equation with centre at the origin and at (h, k)) is not stocked here: it belongs in coordinates-and-graphs, not in this lesson. Ethiopia carries circles from parts and drawing only in Grade 6 (no circumference, no area there), through circumference and area in Grade 7, to central and inscribed angles, tangents and secants in Grade 8 and the full circle chapter — chord symmetry, arcs, cyclic quadrilaterals, arc length and segment and sector area — in Grade 10; no circle equation is taught (that belongs in coordinates-and-graphs). South Sudan carries circles from parts, π and area in Grade 6, through circumference in Grade 7 and circumference and area in Grade 8, to circumference, area and spheres in Secondary 1, the unit circle in Secondary 2, and the equation of a circle in Secondary 4 — with the Primary 8 book's semicircle-area formula corrected (A = ½πr², mislabelled "Circumference" and printed "A = ½ π2" on p. 32). Iraq carries circles from the circle's elements in Grade 6 with circumference and area using π = 22/7, through arcs, chords, central angles, tangents, circumscribed and inscribed circles and the cyclic quadrilateral in Grade 9, to the circle as a conic section with its standard and general equations in Grade 11 Scientific. Bangladesh carries circles from drawing a circle with centre, radius and diameter in Grade 5, through measuring circumference by rolling and the area by cutting and rearranging in Grade 7, to parts of a circle, inscribed and central angles, the angle on a semicircle and cyclic quadrilaterals in Grade 8, and arc length and sector area in Grade 9 — with the Grade 9 book's arc/segment wording corrected (the straight segment AB is a chord, and the area is a sector, not a segment). Nepal carries circles from the circle as a round shape traced from bangles and coins in Grade 1, through circles made by tracing round objects in Grade 2, to circle parts with diameter = 2 × radius in Grade 6, circle parts in Grade 7, circumference and area in Grade 8, chord theorems in Grade 9, and the central-angle theorem with angles in the same segment in Grade 10 — with the honest limit that no circle equation is taught anywhere in Nepal's set (the compulsory books teach no coordinate geometry, and the Optional books only introduce conics without developing them). Cambodia carries circles only in part — parts, circumference and area from Grade 1, chord properties and central and inscribed angles in Grades 8–9, the circle equation, tangent and sector area in Grade 10, and the circle as a parametric curve in Grade 12; cyclic quadrilaterals and intersecting-chords relations are objectives only, with no pinned worked example of sector area in the student books.
