# Hands-on — Circles

The lesson drew circles on paper; here the hands measure real
round things.

## Thread round a tin
Take a tin or a plate. Wrap a piece of string once around it and
mark the string. Lay the string straight and measure it — that is
the circumference. Now measure across the tin — the diameter.
Divide the circumference by the diameter. However big or small
the tin is, the answer comes out near 3.14. That is pi, found by
your own hands.

## Draw with compasses
Sierra Leone's own method: a pair of compasses draws the circle,
and a string or a paper strip stands in when there are no
compasses. Draw a circle of radius 4 cm. Measure its diameter —
it must be 8 cm. Draw a second circle of radius 6 cm. Its
diameter must be 12 cm. If it is not, your compasses slipped.

## Roll the wheel
Mark a spot on a wheel, a bottle cap or a coin. Roll it once along
a ruler and mark where the spot touches down again. That distance
is one circumference. Roll it three times and check: three rolls
are three circumferences.

## Lids and plates
Collect five round things: a coin, a lid, a plate, a tin, a bowl.
For each one, measure the diameter, work out the radius, then
find the circumference and the area. Write them in a table.
Check: does the bigger lid always have the bigger area? Yes —
area grows with the square of the radius.
