# Hands-on — Symmetry

The lesson folded shapes on paper; here the hands fold, cut and
turn real things.

## Fold and cut
Ghana's own method, from its teacher manual: fold a piece of paper
and cut a shape out of the folded edge. Open it. The fold line is
a line of symmetry, and the two halves match exactly. Cut a heart,
a star, a leaf. Each one opens symmetrical.

## Mirror completion
Greece's own method: draw half a shape against a mirror line, hold
a mirror on the line, and see the whole shape in the mirror. Then
draw the missing half yourself. A butterfly, a house, a face —
half drawn, half mirrored, whole when finished.

## The turn test
Cut out a square, a rectangle, an equilateral triangle and a plus
sign. Pin each one through its centre with a pencil point and turn
it slowly. Count how many times it matches itself before it comes
all the way round. The square matches four times, the rectangle
twice, the triangle three times, the plus sign four times. Write
each order down.

## Symmetry hunt
Walk your home. Find: something with many lines of symmetry (a
plate, a round clock face), something with exactly one (a mug with
one handle, a spoon), something with rotational symmetry but no
line symmetry (a pinwheel, a fan), and something with no symmetry
at all (a shoe, a key). Photograph or draw each one and write its
counts.
