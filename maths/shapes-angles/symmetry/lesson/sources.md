**Acceptance count: taught in 20 countries (Kazakhstan, Jordan, South Africa, Egypt, Greece, Armenia, Sierra Leone, Libya, Vietnam, Ghana, Laos, Yemen, Nicaragua, Ethiopia, South Sudan, Iraq, Bangladesh, Nepal, Cambodia, Kuwait).** Greece carries symmetry from the very start
— folding and mirror completion in Grade 1, the fold-along-the-
red-line test in Grade 3, axial symmetry in Grade 6, and the
full axis-and-point symmetry chapter in Grade 7. Kazakhstan
carries symmetric figures in Grades 3 and 4 and the full axial
and central symmetry sections in Grade 6. Libya carries
identifying and making symmetrical shapes in Grade 4 and the
three-part line/rotational/polygon symmetry chapter in Grade 9.
Sierra Leone carries reflection, the folding test and rotational
symmetry with its order in JSS 2. Armenia carries symmetry of
figures in Grade 3 and central symmetry in Geometry 8. Jordan
carries symmetry and reflection in Grade 4, the axis of symmetry
in Grade 7 and planes of symmetry in Grade 8. Egypt carries
symmetry inside its Grade 5 coordinate unit. Vietnam carries
recognition and construction of symmetry in Grade 6. South
Africa carries symmetry in its Grade 4 pattern work. Ghana's
evidence is teacher-manual only (JICA B4 Lessons 89–107,
fold-and-cut paper): no pupil textbook in Ghana's set teaches
symmetry, and the lesson words Ghana that way throughout. The
ladder's fold rule was not needed: the stock proved rich enough
for symmetry to stand as its own lesson. Laos carries symmetry from grid symmetry in Grade 3, through translation, reflection, rotation and axes of symmetry in Grade 5, to line and point symmetry with bisectors in Grades 6–7 and full isometries in Grade 9. Yemen touches this lesson only in part — line symmetry by folding in Grade 3 only: the fold line is the axis of symmetry and a shape may have more than one axis; no rotational or central symmetry. Nicaragua touches this lesson only in part — line symmetry in Grade 6 only: the concept, the characteristics of symmetric figures and constructing symmetric figures. No rotational symmetry and no central symmetry is taught anywhere in Nicaragua's set. Ethiopia touches this lesson only in part — line symmetry only: symmetrical and non-symmetrical shapes in Grade 3, axis-symmetry inside the lines-and-angles chapter in Grade 5 (not its own topic), lines of symmetry of polygons in Grade 9, and chord symmetry in Grade 10. No rotational or central symmetry is taught anywhere in Ethiopia's set. South Sudan touches this lesson only in part — line symmetry only: lines of symmetry by paper folding in Grade 3 and lines of symmetry of triangles in Grade 5. No rotational symmetry is taught anywhere in South Sudan's set (the Primary 7 guide promises it in its outcomes, but the student book never teaches it). Iraq touches this lesson only in part — reflection symmetry within transformations: translation, reflection and symmetry in Grade 7 and reflection and rotation in the coordinate plane in Grade 8. No standalone symmetry lesson and no rotational or central symmetry of shapes is taught anywhere in Iraq's set. Bangladesh touches this lesson only in part — line symmetry and rotational symmetry with the mirror test in Grade 8 (Exp 8, 8 pages), applied to measurement and garden arrangement. It teaches no central symmetry, and no per-shape line counts or rotation orders (square 4/order 4, rectangle 2/order 2, and the rest), so those parts rest on the other countries. Nepal carries symmetry from line symmetry with mirror completion and tessellation in Grade 6, to line and point symmetry with rotational order and regular, semi-regular and irregular tessellation in Grade 7. Cambodia carries symmetry only in part — symmetry about a point and about a line in Grade 7, with a point symmetric in a diameter in Grade 8; rotational symmetry is not pinned anywhere in the set. Kuwait carries symmetry from lesson 7-1 in Grade 3 and lesson 3-11 in Grade 4, through the Grade 6 geometry unit, to the Grade 7 transformation geometry unit.
