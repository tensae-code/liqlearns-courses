figures in Grade 3 and central symmetry in Geometry 8. Jordan
carries symmetry and reflection in Grade 4, the axis of symmetry
in Grade 7 and planes of symmetry in Grade 8. Egypt carries
symmetry inside its Grade 5 coordinate unit. Vietnam carries
recognition and construction of symmetry in Grade 6. South
Africa carries symmetry in its Grade 4 pattern work. Ghana's
evidence is teacher-manual only (JICA B4 Lessons 89–107,
fold-and-cut paper): no pupil textbook in Ghana's set teaches
symmetry, and the lesson words Ghana that way throughout. The
ladder's fold rule was not needed: the stock proved rich enough
for symmetry to stand as its own lesson. Laos carries symmetry from grid symmetry in Grade 3, through translation, reflection, rotation and axes of symmetry in Grade 5, to line and point symmetry with bisectors in Grades 6–7 and full isometries in Grade 9. Yemen touches this lesson only in part — line symmetry by folding in Grade 3 only: the fold line is the axis of symmetry and a shape may have more than one axis; no rotational or central symmetry. Nicaragua touches this lesson only in part — line symmetry in Grade 6 only: the concept, the characteristics of symmetric figures and constructing symmetric figures. No rotational symmetry and no central symmetry is taught anywhere in Nicaragua's set. Ethiopia touches this lesson only in part — line symmetry only: symmetrical and non-symmetrical shapes in Grade 3, axis-symmetry inside the lines-and-angles chapter in Grade 5 (not its own topic), lines of symmetry of polygons in Grade 9, and chord symmetry in Grade 10. No rotational or central symmetry is taught anywhere in Ethiopia's set. South Sudan touches this lesson only in part — line symmetry only: lines of symmetry by paper folding in Grade 3 and lines of symmetry of triangles in Grade 5. No rotational symmetry is taught anywhere in South Sudan's set (the Primary 7 guide promises it in its outcomes, but the student book never teaches it). Iraq touches this lesson only in part — reflection symmetry within transformations: translation, reflection and symmetry in Grade 7 and reflection and rotation in the coordinate plane in Grade 8. No standalone symmetry lesson and no rotational or central symmetry of shapes is taught anywhere in Iraq's set. Bangladesh touches this lesson only in part — line symmetry and rotational symmetry with the mirror test in Grade 8 (Exp 8, 8 pages), applied to measurement and garden arrangement. It teaches no central symmetry, and no per-shape line counts or rotation orders (square 4/order 4, rectangle 2/order 2, and the rest), so those parts rest on the other countries. Nepal carries symmetry from line symmetry with mirror completion and tessellation in Grade 6, to line and point symmetry with rotational order and regular, semi-regular and irregular tessellation in Grade 7. Cambodia carries symmetry only in part — symmetry about a point and about a line in Grade 7, with a point symmetric in a diameter in Grade 8; rotational symmetry is not pinned anywhere in the set. Kuwait carries symmetry from lesson 7-1 in Grade 3 and lesson 3-11 in Grade 4, through the Grade 6 geometry unit, to the Grade 7 transformation geometry unit. North Macedonia carries symmetry from Grade 1 (2022), where the line of symmetry is met in the second geometry block.

China carries symmetry as axial symmetry in Grades 2 and 4, and as central and rotational symmetry in Grade 9 Volume 1.

Pakistan (Punjab) carries symmetry from the Primer's complete-the-half pictures through reflective symmetry in Grades 3–4 to Class 7's counted lines and rotational orders.

Syria folds shapes from Grade 2, builds axial symmetry through Grades 3–6 — axes counted, images drawn on grids — and in Grade 7 adds central symmetry as a half-turn with its preservation properties and the axes-and-centres census of the familiar figures; Grade 8 Geometry then uses central symmetry as a proof tool for the midpoint theorems.

Saudi Arabia teaches symmetry twice: line symmetry by folding in Grade 3, with lines counted and half-drawings completed, then rotational symmetry with its angle of rotation in Grade 4, with the older 1445 Term-3 volume witnessing the Grade 3 chapter.

Malaysia closes its Form 2 transformations chapter with rotational symmetry and its order — symmetry treated as something a shape does under a turn, not only under a fold.

Rwanda teaches symmetry in Year 4, counting lines of symmetry per shape inside its shapes unit and meeting rotational symmetry with its order.

India stocks symmetry from Math-Magic Class 3–5 and Mathematics Class 6–7: mirror halves in a story; mirror halves in patterns; mirror halves; line symmetry: folding so halves match exactly; the mirror/fold line is the line of symmetry.


Mongolia enters through its ministry textbooks Математик II, III, V, VI, VII (Grades 2, 3, 5, 6, 7): recognising symmetric shapes; completing symmetric shapes; consolidation: pyramids and symmetry.

Venezuela enters through the Colección Bicentenario, the MPPE's one-book-per-grade course: *Hecho en Venezuela* (Grade 6), *Conciencia Matemática* (Grade 8) and *Naturaleza Matemática* (Grade 10). From its books: from Grade 6: axes — Symmetry defined; axis of symmetry divides a figure into two identical parts; from Grade 8: definition — symmetry about a point, an axis (line) or a plane; exact correspondence of size, shape and position; from Grade 10: symmetry of a graph: the second half of the track repeats the first.

Afghanistan enters through its Ministry of Education course — the Grade 1 course in two language editions (Pashto and Dari, one work with identical page maps) and Dari student books for Grades 4, 6, 7, 8, 10, 11 and 12, all printing year 1398 HS except the Grade 8 book, which prints 1399 HS on its cover and title page and 1398 HS on its specifications page. From its books: from Grade 8: concept of symmetry; axis of symmetry (axial symmetry); central (point) symmetry; chapter key points and exercises (symmetry).

Albania enters through its Mediaprint teacher's books (Libër Mësuesi, in Albanian) — TEACHER EVIDENCE only: no pupil's book is in hand for any grade, so Albania is never cited as a pupil-textbook source; what the teacher's books evidence is what is taught and in what order, through their lesson plans, worked answers and answer keys. From its books: from Grade 1 (teacher's book): Symmetry: recognise lines of symmetry with mirrors; the line the mirror sits on is the line of symmetry;…. from Grade 5 (teacher's book): Symmetry and the axis of symmetry; symmetry on the coordinate grid.
