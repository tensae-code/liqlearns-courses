# Lesson plan — Symmetry

**Course:** Maths · **Stage:** 7, Shapes and Angles · **Lesson:** Symmetry (lesson 4 of 4 in the stage)
**Place in the curriculum:** Stage 7, "Shapes and Angles" — the fourth lesson of the stage (maths/CURRICULUM.md). The ladder allows this lesson to be folded into Shapes if its stock proves too thin; the stock proved rich (ten countries), so it stands on its own.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** when a shape matches itself — folded over a line, or turned about a point.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the shapes lesson (you cannot count a shape's symmetries until you can name it) and on the angles lesson (a turn is measured in degrees). It needs nothing else.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Shapes, then Angles — symmetry is a shape
  matching itself by folding or turning. Recommended, never forced.
- Reflection and rotation as transformations belong to Stage 9;
  this lesson teaches symmetry itself — the fold test and the turn
  test — and no transformation rules.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Test a shape for line symmetry by folding, find its lines of
   symmetry, and say how many a square, a rectangle, an equilateral
   triangle and a circle have.
2. Test a shape for rotational symmetry by turning it, and say its
   order — how many times it matches itself in one full turn.
3. Tell line symmetry from rotational symmetry, and say which shapes
   have one, the other, both, or neither.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Scissors. A small mirror (or a phone screen
held upright — any shiny flat surface).

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner folds a paper shape in half and
   watches the two halves land on each other (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — line symmetry and the axis; rotational
   symmetry and the order; symmetry in polygons and in the circle
   (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the line-symmetry part: a picture of a paper
     shape folding along a line, the two halves landing exactly
     on each other — the student folds a paper shape the same way.
   - ADD IMAGE at the rotational part: a picture of a plus sign
     turning by quarter-turns, matching itself at 90°, 180°,

   - ADD IMAGE at the polygons part: a picture of a square, a
     rectangle, an equilateral triangle and a circle, each with

3. **Say it to your companion** — test shapes for your companion

4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,

5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — find symmetrical things in your home and

## If the learner is stuck
- Counts a line of symmetry that does not fold exactly → the
  books' own test: fold the paper (or hold the mirror) and look —
  the two halves must land exactly on each other, edge for edge.
  If they do not, it is not a line of symmetry.
- Counts the starting position in the order → the order counts the
  matches in one full turn, and the starting position is one of
  them: a square matches at 90°, 180°, 270° and 360° — four
  matches, order 4, and the 360° match is the start come round
  again.
- Says a shape has no symmetry because it has no line of symmetry
  → a shape can have rotational symmetry without line symmetry:
  a parallelogram has rotational symmetry of order 2 and no lines
  of symmetry at all.
- This lesson's next door: Measuring (Stage 8) — the fold test
  needs no measuring, but the turn test is measured in degrees.
