# Tessellations — short answer

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Tessellations Tiling by Moves · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Tessellations Which Shapes Tile Alone · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Tessellations Kinds of Tessellation.

Answer in a word or a short line.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** What are the three moves Laos's Grade 5 lessons use to build tilings?
**Answer:** Translation (slide), reflection (flip) and rotation (turn) — L49 and L50, pp.112–123.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** In one line: what two faults must a tessellation never have?
**Answer:** No gaps and no overlaps.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** How many equilateral triangles meet at a point in their tessellation, and why?
**Answer:** Six — 6 × 60° = 360°.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** Why do rectangles tessellate even though they are not regular?
**Answer:** Their corners are 90°, and 4 × 90° = 360°, so they meet exactly (Nepal, Grade 6, pp.215–223).

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Name Nepal Grade 7's three kinds of tessellation.
**Answer:** Regular, semi-regular and irregular (Unit 9, pp.75–83).

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** Give one real place a hexagon tessellation is found.
**Answer:** A honeycomb (also tiled floors in the hexagon pattern of Laos, Grade 5, L50).
