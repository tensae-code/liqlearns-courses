# Constructions with ruler and compass — the lesson

Course: Maths · Unit: Unit 7 — Shapes and Angles · Lesson: Constructions with ruler and compass
Built from: Sierra Leone, SSS 2 Handbook — Laos, Grade 6 — Iran, Geometry (1) — Armenia, Geometry 7 — Pakistan, Grade 6 — Bangladesh, Class 7 — Kuwait, Grade 7 Term 2

## Do it first
Draw a line segment on paper. Now — with no ruler markings, no measuring — can you cut it exactly in half? Try folding the paper first. Folding works because the two ends meet. A pair of compasses does the same meeting job on a line that cannot be folded, and that is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Constructions Bisections
**Bisecting a segment.** Sierra Leone's SSS 2 book starts the construction course here (L79, p.90): open the compasses to more than half the segment, draw arcs above and below from one end, keep the same opening and draw arcs from the other end, then join the two crossing points. That joining line cuts the segment in half and stands at a right angle to it — it is the **perpendicular bisector**. Pakistan's Grade 6 unit teaches the same construction and names the difference plainly: a bisector cuts a thing in half; a perpendicular bisector cuts it in half at 90° (SD10, pp.140–141).

**Bisecting an angle.** Put the compass point on the vertex and draw one arc cutting both sides (Sierra Leone, SSS 2, L80, p.93). From each cut point, draw a small arc inside the angle, using one equal opening. Join the vertex to the crossing of those arcs. Sierra Leone's book then checks the result with a protractor — the protractor checks, the compasses construct.

## Sub lesson 2 — Constructions The Special Angles
**The special angles.** A compass opened to exactly the length of a segment, stepped around its own arc, makes **60°**; the perpendicular bisector method makes **90°**; 60° joined to its neighbour on a straight line makes **120°** (Sierra Leone, SSS 2, L81, p.96; Pakistan, Grade 6, pp.147–152). Then bisection does the rest: halve 90° for **45°**, halve 60° for **30°**, halve 30° for **15°** (Sierra Leone, SSS 2, L82, p.99). Joining does the rest the other way: 45° + 30° = **75°**, 60° + 45° = **105°**, 90° + 60° = **150°** (Sierra Leone, SSS 2, L83, p.101).

## Sub lesson 3 — Constructions Parallels, Copying and Reasons
**Parallels, perpendiculars and copying.** Laos's Grade 6 book (L12–L14, L18, L20, pp.87–107, 136–166) and Kuwait's Grade 7 Term 2 Unit 6 (pp.63–128) teach the working set: a perpendicular through a point on a line and from a point outside it, a parallel through a point, copying a segment by carrying its length in the compasses, and copying an angle by carrying its opening. Kuwait returns to the same work in Grade 8 (Term 2, L5-2, p.26).

**Why the arcs work.** Iran's Geometry (1) presents constructions **with justifications** (pp.10–16): the crossing arcs work because every point on the perpendicular bisector is the same distance from both ends of the segment — the compasses guarantee those equal distances. Armenia's Geometry 7 treats construction as a method with stages (Ch.II §10, §17, pp.34–76, 107–140): what is given, what is to be made, the steps, and the proof that the steps work. Bangladesh's Class 7 experiments (Exp.5, pp.104–124) set the same constructions as hands-on experiments, after Class 4 first meets the compass (Ch.14, pp.155–160).

> Tip — from us, not from the books. Check every construction, tiling or count a second way — measure it, fold it, or count it again in a different order — before you trust it. A drawing that only works once is not finished yet.

## Say it to your companion
Describe each construction to your companion while they draw it — no pointing, words only: "Open the compasses to more than half. Arc above, arc below." If their drawing comes out right, your words were exact. Trade after each construction: segment, angle, 60°, a parallel through a point.

## Do It Yourself
1. Bisect a 12 cm segment with compasses only; measure the halves to check.
2. Construct 60°, bisect it to 30°, and bisect again to 15°. Label each step.
3. Draw a line and a point outside it; construct the perpendicular from the point and a parallel through the point.
4. Copy a segment and an angle your companion draws, using compasses only, and write the reason each arc works.
