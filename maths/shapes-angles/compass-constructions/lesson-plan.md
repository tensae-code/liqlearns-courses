# Lesson plan — Constructions with ruler and compass

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 7 — Shapes and Angles · **Lesson:** Constructions with ruler and compass
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 60 of 136, Unit 7 — Shapes and Angles (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** drawing exact figures — bisected segments and angles, perpendiculars, parallels and the special angles — with only a ruler and a pair of compasses, and being able to say why each arc is drawn.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Shapes and Angles (same stage) — the learner can name angles and lines. It needs nothing else.

1. **Constructions Bisections** — bisecting a segment and an angle with compasses, the compass openings kept fixed, and checking the halves equal. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Constructions The Special Angles** — constructing 90°, 60° and 120°, then making 45°, 30° and 15° by bisecting, and 75°, 105° and 150° by joining. (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **Constructions Parallels, Copying and Reasons** — parallels and perpendiculars through a point, copying a segment and an angle, and the reason each arc is drawn. (Was depth Level 3.)

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Shapes and Angles (same stage) — a construction names the angle or line it makes. Recommended, never forced.
- A protractor may check a finished construction, the way Sierra Leone's book checks a bisected angle — it never replaces the compass steps.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Bisect a line segment and draw its perpendicular bisector, the way Sierra Leone's SSS 2 book teaches it (L79, p.90).
2. Bisect an angle and check the two halves with a protractor (Sierra Leone, SSS 2, L80, p.93).
3. Construct 90°, 60° and 120° with compasses alone (Sierra Leone, SSS 2, L81, p.96).
4. Make 45°, 30° and 15° by bisecting a construction already made (Sierra Leone, SSS 2, L82, p.99).
5. Make 75°, 105° and 150° by joining two constructed angles (Sierra Leone, SSS 2, L83, p.101).
6. Draw a parallel and a perpendicular through a given point (Laos, Grade 6, pp.87–107; Kuwait, Grade 7 Term 2, Unit 6, pp.63–128).
7. Copy a segment and copy an angle onto a new line (Laos, Grade 6, pp.87–107; Pakistan, Grade 6, pp.146–147).
8. Say, for one construction, why the arcs work — the justification step Iran's Geometry (1) requires (pp.10–16).

## What you need
A pair of compasses, a ruler, a sharp pencil and paper. If there are no compasses, string or a strip of paper does the same job — Sierra Leone's book teaches that substitute itself (SSS 2, L79, p.90). A protractor, only for checking.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Draw a line segment on paper. Now — with no ruler markings, no measuring — can you cut it exactly in half? Try folding the paper first. Folding works because the two ends meet. A pair of compasses does the same meeting job on a line that cannot be folded, and that is this lesson. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — bisecting a segment and an angle, the special angles, parallels and perpendiculars, copying, and the reasons the arcs work (lesson/lesson.md).
3. **Say it to your companion** — Describe each construction to your companion while they draw it — no pointing, words only: "Open the compasses to more than half. Arc above, arc below." If their drawing comes out right, your words were exact. Trade after each construction: segment, angle, 60°, a parallel through a point. (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — constructions with ruler and compass in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching, ordering — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — 1. Bisect a 12 cm segment with compasses only; measure the halves to check.
2. Construct 60°, bisect it to 30°, and bisect again to 15°. Label each step.
3. Draw a line and a point outside it; construct the perpendicular from the point and a parallel through the point.
4. Copy a segment and an angle your companion draws, using compasses only, and write the reason each arc works. (lesson/lesson.md).

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- GIF — a picture of a segment being bisected, the two compass arcs growing step by step until they cross above and below the line → the student pauses the GIF at each arc and draws that arc on paper.
- GIF — a picture of an angle being bisected, the compass point walking from the vertex to the two sides to the crossing arcs inside → the student copies each step as it appears.
- GIF — a picture of a 60° angle growing into 120°, then being halved to 30° → the student names each new angle as it lands.
- IMAGE — a picture of a parallel line and a perpendicular line drawn through one marked point outside a line → the student points to which is which and says why.
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later) → the student constructs along with it.

## If the learner is stuck
- The arcs do not cross → the compass opening was smaller than half the segment. Sierra Leone's rule in practice: open the compass to MORE than half, keep that opening for both arcs.
- The two halves of a bisected angle measure differently → the first arc must cut BOTH sides at the same opening, and the two inner arcs must use one equal opening. Start again and keep the openings fixed.
- The construction looks right but the reason is missing → go back to Armenia's stages (Geometry 7, Ch.II §10, §17): say what is given, what is to be made, then the steps, then why it works.
- This lesson's next door: Constructing triangles and quadrilaterals (same stage, Level 61) — these same arcs build whole figures.
