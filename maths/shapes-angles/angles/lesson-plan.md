# Lesson plan — Angles

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** 7, Shapes and Angles · **Lesson:** Angles (lesson 2 of 4 in the unit)
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7, "Shapes and Angles" — the second lesson of the unit (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** what an angle is, how to measure it, and the angle facts that never change — on a straight line, around a point, in a triangle, and between parallel lines.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the shapes lesson (a corner is where an angle lives). It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Angles Kinds and Measuring** — what an angle is, the kinds of angle in order of size, and measuring with a protractor. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Angles on a Straight Line and Around a Point** — angles on a straight line (180°) and around a point (360°), complementary and supplementary pairs, and vertically opposite angles. (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **Angles in Shapes and Between Parallel Lines** — angle sums in a triangle (180°) and a quadrilateral (360°), and the angles where a transversal crosses parallel lines. (Was depth Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson (no hardness ranking is written in this lesson's question files).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Shapes — the angle lives at a shape's corner.
  Recommended, never forced.
- Measuring length is used only to place the protractor; the
  protractor itself is taught here.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name an angle's kinds — acute, right, obtuse, straight, reflex —
   and measure one with a protractor.
2. Use the two straight facts: angles on a straight line add to
   180°, angles around a point add to 360°; complementary angles
   add to 90°, supplementary to 180°, and vertically opposite angles
   are equal.
3. Find a missing angle in a triangle (angles add to 180°) and in a
   quadrilateral (angles add to 360°), and find angles where a
   transversal crosses parallel lines.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A protractor. A ruler.

## If the learner is stuck
- Reads the wrong protractor scale → the books' own check: the
  angle you measure must match the kind you see — an acute angle
  cannot read 140°. If the number and the kind disagree, read the
  other scale.
- Adds the angles in a triangle and gets more than 180° → one of
  the three was misread; measure again, then subtract from 180°
  to find the missing one.
- Mixes complementary and supplementary → complementary makes a
  CORNER (90°, like the corner of a page); supplementary makes a
  STRAIGHT line (180°). Sierra Leone's own words: complementary
  is the corner, supplementary is the straight.
- This lesson's next door: Circles (same unit) — the full turn,
  360°, is the circle's own number.
