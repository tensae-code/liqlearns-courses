# Hands-on — Angles

The lesson measured angles on paper; here the hands and the room
do the measuring.

## The door angles
Open a door a little, then to a square corner, then wide, then
flat. At each stop, name the kind of angle. Your companion checks
with a protractor held against the floor edge. The door is the
protractor's big brother.

## Paper protractor
Sierra Leone's own trick: if you have no protractor, trace one
from a friend's, or fold a paper circle in half again and again —
half is 180°, a quarter is 90°. Measure the corners of your books
and papers with it. A book corner must read 90°; if it does not,
your folding is off, not the book.

## Tear the triangle
Draw a big triangle on paper. Tear off its three corners. Put the
three torn corners together on a straight edge. They make a
straight line — 180°. Do it again with a different triangle: long
and thin, short and fat. The corners always make the same straight
line.

## Angle hunt
Find in your home: a right angle (a door frame), an acute angle
(an open book, half open), an obtuse angle (a leaning ladder),
angles on a straight line (a shelf edge with a book standing on
it), angles around a point (where four tiles meet). Measure each
one and write it down.
