# Shapes — true or false

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Shapes Polygons by Their Sides · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Shapes Triangles Two Ways · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Shapes The Quadrilateral Family.

Say true or false. If it is false, say why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** A hexagon has 6 sides.
**True** — named by its side count (Egypt, Grade 3, Chapter 4).

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** A polygon can have curved sides.
**False** — a polygon has straight sides, closed all the way round.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** An isosceles triangle has all three sides equal.
**False** — that is equilateral; isosceles has two sides equal (Jordan, Grade 5, p.70).

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** A triangle can be both right-angled and isosceles.
**True** — the two sortings answer different questions.

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Every square is a rectangle.
**True** — a square is a parallelogram with four right angles, which is what a rectangle is (Jordan, Grade 8, p.91).

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** Every rectangle is a square.
**False** — a rectangle's sides need not be equal; a square needs four equal sides.
