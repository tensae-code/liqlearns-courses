# Hands-on — Shapes

The lesson named shapes on paper; here the hands do the sorting.

## Shape hunt
Walk a room with a partner. Find and touch: a triangle, a
quadrilateral, a pentagon, a hexagon. A book cover is a rectangle;
a tile is a square; a roof edge makes a triangle. Say each name
aloud as you touch it, and count its sides with your finger.

## Sides with sticks
Take sticks, straws or pencils. Make a triangle with three equal
sticks — equilateral. Swap one stick for a shorter one — isosceles.
Make all three different — scalene. Then make a square, and push
one corner sideways until it is a rhombus: the sides stay equal,
the corners stop being right angles.

## The family tree
Draw the parallelogram family tree on paper: parallelogram at the
top, rectangle and rhombus below it, square at the bottom. Cut out
paper quadrilaterals — a long thin rectangle, a slanted rhombus, a
square, a trapezium, a kite — and stick each one on the tree where
it belongs. A shape that fits two branches sits on both.

## Draw and defend
Draw one of each kind: equilateral, isosceles and scalene triangles;
a parallelogram, a rectangle, a rhombus, a square, a trapezium and
a kite. Your companion points at one and asks "why is it that
kind?" Answer with sides and angles, not with "it looks like it".
