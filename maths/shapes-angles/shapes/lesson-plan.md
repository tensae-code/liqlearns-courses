# Lesson plan — Shapes

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** 7, Shapes and Angles · **Lesson:** Shapes (lesson 1 of 4 in the stage)
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7, "Shapes and Angles" — the first lesson of the stage (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** naming flat shapes by their sides and corners, and sorting triangles and quadrilaterals into their kinds.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on counting and on measuring length (a side is a length). It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown changing.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Shapes Polygons by Their Sides** — name a triangle, a quadrilateral, a pentagon and a hexagon by counting sides; say what a side, a vertex and a regular polygon are. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Shapes Triangles Two Ways** — sort a triangle by its sides (equilateral, isosceles, scalene) and by its angles (acute, right, obtuse) without mixing the two sortings. (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **Shapes The Quadrilateral Family** — place any quadrilateral in the parallelogram family — parallelogram, rectangle, rhombus, square, trapezium, kite — and say why a square is both a rectangle and a rhombus. (Was depth Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson (no hardness ranking is written in this lesson's question files).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: measuring length — a side is a length, and
  "equal sides" means sides of equal length. Recommended, never forced.
- Angles are the next lesson; this lesson names a right angle only
  where a shape needs it (a square has four right angles), and the
  angle lesson teaches what a right angle is in full.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name a polygon by its number of sides: triangle 3, quadrilateral 4,
   pentagon 5, hexagon 6 — and say what a vertex, a side and a
   regular polygon are.
2. Sort a triangle by its sides (equilateral, isosceles, scalene) and
   by its angles (acute, right, obtuse).
3. Sort a quadrilateral into the parallelogram family — parallelogram,
   rectangle, rhombus, square, trapezium, kite — and say what makes
   each one what it is.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A ruler. Things around you with flat faces
(books, boxes, tiles, coins are round — they are for the circles
lesson, not this one).

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner looks around the room and names the
   flat shapes they see, then draws a triangle, a square and a
   rectangle (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — polygons and their names; triangles by sides
   and by angles; quadrilaterals and the parallelogram family
   (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE at the polygons part: a picture of a triangle, a
     quadrilateral, a pentagon and a hexagon in a row, each with its
     sides counted in dots — the student counts the sides of each
     and says its name.
   - ADD GIF at the triangles part: a picture of one triangle
     changing — its sides stretching and shrinking until it is
     equilateral, then isosceles, then scalene — the student names
     the kind at each stop.
   - ADD IMAGE at the quadrilaterals part: a picture of the
     parallelogram family as a tree — parallelogram at the top,
     rectangle and rhombus below it, square at the bottom belonging
     to both — the student places a new quadrilateral on the tree.
3. **Say it to your companion** — name and sort shapes for your
   companion, and defend each sorting (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the shape
     names in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are
     made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served
   at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — sort the shapes in your home, and draw and
   name one of each kind (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Counts the corners instead of the sides → the books' own rule:
  a polygon has as many sides as corners, so count either — but
  name it by the sides. Count both and check they match.
- Calls every four-sided shape a square → a square needs four
  EQUAL sides AND four right angles; a rectangle has the right
  angles but not the equal sides. Measure the sides.
- Mixes rhombus and square → a rhombus has four equal sides but
  its angles are not right angles; a square is a rhombus with
  right angles. The square sits in both families.
- This lesson's next door: Angles (same stage) — the corner
  measure every shape name leans on.
