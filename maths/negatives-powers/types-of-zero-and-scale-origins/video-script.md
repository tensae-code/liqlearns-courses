# Video script — Types of Zero and Scale Origins

Super-simple voice. The tutor talks; a scale, a ruler, a thermometer and a timeline stay on screen. Every fact spoken is one of the lesson's own book facts.

---

Zero. It looks like nothing. But look closer — zero is not always the same nothing.

A scale says 0 kilograms. That means: no mass at all. Nothing is there. A ruler starts at 0: no length. Your phone says 0 MB left: no data. These zeros are natural. They mean "none of it." And nothing — nothing — can be smaller. You cannot have less than no mass.

Now the thermometer. It says 0 degrees. Is there no temperature? No! It is just cold — the cold of freezing water. People *chose* that mark and called it zero. And the scale keeps going down: minus 5, minus 9 — real temperatures, colder than the chosen zero. A timeline is the same: its zero is a year people picked, and years run before it too.

And one zero is different from all the chosen ones. Kelvin's zero. That one nobody chose — it is where heat itself runs out completely. No temperature can go below it. The book's question was: does every 0 show the same start? Now you know. It does not.
