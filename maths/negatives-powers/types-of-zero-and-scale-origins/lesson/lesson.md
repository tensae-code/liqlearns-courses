# Types of Zero and Scale Origins

## Sub lesson 1 — Zeros The Question of the Four Zeros

### You do it first

Here are four readings, all of them zero: a history timeline standing at its year 0; a phone showing 0 MB of data left; a kitchen scale reading 0 kg; a thermometer reading 0 °C. Turkey's Grade 7 textbook opens its integers theme with one question about them: **does every 0 show the same start?** Before you read on, sort the four: for which of them does zero mean "there is none of it at all" — and for which is zero just a mark somewhere in the middle of the scale?

## Sub lesson 2 — Zeros Natural — Zero Means None of It

### Natural zeros — zero means none of it

Some quantities have a **natural starting point**. A scale reading 0 kg means there is **no mass** on it. A length of 0 m is **no length**. 0 MB left is **no data left**. For these quantities, zero is the true beginning: nothing of the thing exists, and nothing can lie below — there is no negative mass and no negative length waiting under the floor. (Turkey, *Matematik 7. Sınıf Ders Kitabı, 1. Kitap*, printed pp. 15–16.)

## Sub lesson 3 — Zeros Chosen — A Mark People Picked

### Chosen zeros — zero is a mark people picked

Other quantities carry a zero that **people chose**. 0 °C does not mean "no temperature": it is the temperature at which water freezes, picked as the scale's mark. Below it the temperature goes on being real: the book's city temperatures run from +12 °C down to **−9 °C** (printed p. 19). The history timeline's zero is likewise a year people picked, with years running before it, and sea level is a chosen zero for heights and depths. A chosen zero sits in the middle of its scale's life — and **a scale with a chosen zero is exactly where negative numbers are needed**: values below the mark must be written with a "−" sign, because they are not "none of it," only "below where we started counting." (Turkey, *Matematik 7. Sınıf Ders Kitabı, 1. Kitap*, printed pp. 15–16.)

The book has pupils classify quantities into the two families — those with a natural starting point and those without one — before a single negative number is computed. The classification is the lesson: **look at a scale's zero and ask what it means** — none of the quantity, or a chosen mark — and you know whether the numbers below it can exist.

## Sub lesson 4 — Zeros Absolute Against Relative

### Absolute zero against relative zero

The theme's performance task sharpens the contrast into a poster: **absolute versus relative zero**. Celsius's zero is **relative** — chosen, with a whole world of temperatures beneath it. Kelvin's zero is **absolute**: it is the complete absence of heat, the coldest temperature there is, and no temperature lies below 0 K. The same quantity — temperature — carries both kinds of zero on its two scales: the everyday scale's zero is a human choice, the scientific scale's zero is nature's floor. (Turkey, *Matematik 7. Sınıf Ders Kitabı, 1. Kitap*, printed p. 34.)

## Do it yourself

1. Sort these by their zero: a jug holding 0 litres; a temperature of 0 °C; 0 pages left in a book; a city at sea level, 0 m. (Natural zero — none of it: the jug and the pages. Chosen zero: 0 °C and sea level. Turkey, Grade 7, printed pp. 15–16.)
2. Why is there no temperature of −5 K, when −5 °C is an ordinary winter reading? (Kelvin's zero is absolute — the absence of heat itself — so nothing lies below it; Celsius's zero is chosen, so its scale continues below. Turkey, Grade 7, printed p. 34.)
3. A timeline shows an event 200 years before its year 0, written as −200. Which kind of zero does the timeline have, and how do you know? (A chosen zero: values run on both sides of it, which only a picked mark allows. Turkey, Grade 7, printed pp. 15–16.)
4. A pupil says "0 kg and 0 °C are both just nothing." Correct the statement in one sentence. (0 kg is nothing — no mass at all, a natural zero; 0 °C is a real, chosen mark — the freezing point of water — with colder temperatures below it. Turkey, Grade 7, printed pp. 15–16.)
