# Levels — Types of Zero and Scale Origins

One lesson, one level (Level 162). The depth layers inside it are ranks.

## Depth layers

- **Layer 1 — The question.** Say why the four zeros of the book's opener — timeline, data left, scale, thermometer — are not the same starting point. Pass mark: 4 of 5.
- **Layer 2 — Natural zeros.** Identify quantities whose zero means "none of it" (mass, length, a count of what is left) and say why nothing lies below them. Pass mark: 4 of 5.
- **Layer 3 — Chosen zeros.** Identify quantities whose zero people chose (Celsius degrees, the timeline, sea level) and explain why negative values live below a chosen zero. Pass mark: 4 of 5.
- **Layer 4 — Absolute versus relative zero.** State the Celsius/Kelvin contrast: Celsius's zero is relative and chosen; Kelvin's zero is absolute, and no temperature lies below it. Pass mark: 4 of 5.

## What "maxed" means here

The learner can sort any everyday quantity by the kind of zero its scale carries — natural (absolute) or chosen (relative) — the way Turkey's Grade 7 book teaches it at the head of its integers theme (printed pp. 15–16) and in its performance task (printed p. 34), and can state why negative numbers are needed exactly on chosen-zero scales, and why Kelvin's zero, alone among the temperature zeros, is absolute.
