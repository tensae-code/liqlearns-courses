1. (Sub lesson 1) The book opens by asking whether every 0 shows the same start. Its answer is:
   (a) yes — zero is zero on every scale
   (b) no — some zeros are natural starting points and others are marks people chose
   (c) yes — except on thermometers
   (d) no — because zero has not been invented for some scales

   **Answer: (b)** — Turkey, *Matematik 7. Sınıf Ders Kitabı, 1. Kitap* (Grade 7), printed pp. 15–16.

2. (Sub lesson 2) Which reading has a natural zero — zero meaning "none of it"?
   (a) 0 °C on a thermometer
   (b) the year 0 on a history timeline
   (c) 0 kg on a scale
   (d) sea level, 0 m

   **Answer: (c)** — no mass at all; the others are chosen marks with values below them. Turkey, Grade 7, printed pp. 15–16.

3. (Sub lesson 3) A temperature of −9 °C is possible because:
   (a) Celsius zero is a chosen mark — water's freezing point — and temperatures continue below it
   (b) thermometers are allowed to be wrong
   (c) zero means no temperature, and −9 is less than nothing
   (d) the book prints it as a deliberate mistake

   **Answer: (a)** — Turkey, Grade 7, printed pp. 15–16 (the −9 °C reading itself is the book's, printed p. 19).

4. (Sub lesson 3) Which scale's zero is chosen by people?
   (a) a measuring jug's 0 litres
   (b) a countdown's 0 objects left
   (c) a history timeline's year 0
   (d) a balance's 0 g

   **Answer: (c)** — years run before the picked year, so the zero sits mid-scale. Turkey, Grade 7, printed pp. 15–16.

5. (Sub lesson 4) Kelvin's zero differs from Celsius's zero because:
   (a) Kelvin's zero is colder than any possible temperature on paper only
   (b) Kelvin's zero is absolute — the complete absence of heat — and no temperature lies below it, while Celsius's zero is a chosen mark
   (c) Kelvin's zero was chosen later
   (d) the two zeros are the same temperature

   **Answer: (b)** — the book's performance task: absolute versus relative zero. Turkey, Grade 7, printed p. 34.
