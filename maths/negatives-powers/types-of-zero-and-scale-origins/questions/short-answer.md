# Short answer — Types of Zero and Scale Origins

Two items carried range markers: the matching item marked (Layers 1–3) is marked Sub lesson 3 — Zeros Chosen — A Mark People Picked, the part where the natural/chosen sorting is completed; the ordering item marked (Layers 2–4) is marked Sub lesson 4 — Zeros Absolute Against Relative, the part it culminates in.

1. (Sub lesson 1) State the book's opening question and its answer in one sentence each.
   **Answer: Question: "Does every 0 show the same start?" Answer: No — some quantities have a natural starting point whose zero means none of the quantity, and others carry a zero people chose, with values running below it.** Turkey, *Matematik 7. Sınıf Ders Kitabı, 1. Kitap* (Grade 7), printed pp. 15–16.

2. (Sub lesson 2) Give two quantities with a natural zero and say what their zero means.
   **Answer: Mass (0 kg = no mass) and length (0 m = no length); for each, zero is the true beginning — none of the quantity exists, and nothing lies below.** Turkey, Grade 7, printed pp. 15–16.

3. (Sub lesson 3) Why does a scale with a chosen zero need negative numbers, while a scale with a natural zero does not?
   **Answer: Below a chosen zero the values are still real (colder temperatures, earlier years), so they must be written somehow — with a minus sign, as "below the mark." Below a natural zero there is nothing to write: none of the quantity is already the bottom.** Turkey, Grade 7, printed pp. 15–16.

4. (Sub lesson 4) A pupil says: "Kelvin just chose a different zero, like Celsius did." Correct the pupil using the book's contrast.
   **Answer: Celsius's zero is relative — a mark people picked (water's freezing point) with real temperatures below it. Kelvin's zero is absolute — the complete absence of heat; it is not a picked mark, and no temperature can lie below it.** Turkey, Grade 7, printed p. 34.
