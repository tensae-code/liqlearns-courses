# Hands-on — Types of Zero and Scale Origins

## Activity 1 — Sort the zeros (10 minutes)

Write these readings on cards, as in Turkey's Grade 7 book (printed p. 15): 0 on a history timeline, 0 MB of data left, a scale reading 0 kg, 0 °C. Add four of your own (0 m of ribbon, sea level 0 m, 0 books left, a year 0 on a timeline you draw). Sort all the cards into the book's two families (printed p. 16): quantities with a natural starting point — zero means none of it — and quantities whose zero people chose. For each card, say what a value *below* its zero would mean, or why there cannot be one.

## Activity 2 — The two temperature ladders (10 minutes)

Draw two ladders side by side, as the book's performance task sets up (printed p. 34): Celsius from −10 to 30, Kelvin from 0 to 310. Mark water's freezing point on both (0 °C, 273 K) and ring each scale's zero. Write one sentence under each ring: "chosen — temperatures continue below" and "absolute — nothing is colder." Then place −9 °C (a temperature from the book's integers work) on the Celsius ladder and find where it lands on the Kelvin ladder (264 K).

## Activity 3 — Hunt for chosen zeros (10 minutes)

Find three more scales in your home or school whose zero was chosen by people (a calendar's year count, a gauge, a game score that can go negative, floors below ground in a building) and one more whose zero is natural (a measuring jug, a countdown of objects). Present them the way the book's performance task asks for its poster (printed p. 34): absolute zero versus relative zero, one example of each, explained in your own words.
