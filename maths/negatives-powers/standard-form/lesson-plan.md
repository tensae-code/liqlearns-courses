# Lesson plan — Standard Form

**Course:** Maths · **Stage:** Stage 5 — Negative Numbers, Powers and Roots · **Lesson:** Standard Form
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 20 of the maths course — the fourth lesson of Stage 5 (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** standard form — writing very big and very small numbers as a number between 1 and 10 times a power of ten.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on powers-and-indices (Level 18) and on place value (Stage 1). It needs nothing else.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: powers-and-indices — powers of ten. Recommended, never forced.
- Two names for one thing: Egypt and South Africa call it scientific notation; Kazakhstan, Sierra Leone, Libya and Greece call it standard form. The rule is the same everywhere: a × 10ⁿ with 1 ≤ a < 10.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Write a big number in standard form and read a standard-form number back as an ordinary number.
2. Write a small number in standard form, using negative powers of ten.
3. Convert between standard form and ordinary form in both directions.
4. Compare and order numbers in standard form, and judge when standard form is worth using.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A list of real big and small numbers (planet distances, atom masses) — the books supply them.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner writes the Earth–Sun distance in full and feels why a short way is wanted (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — big numbers; small numbers; converting both ways; comparing and choosing (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE at the big-numbers part: a picture of the Earth–Sun distance written two ways, 149,600,000 km and 1.496 × 10⁸ km → the student counts the zeros that vanished.
   - ADD GIF at the small-numbers part: a picture of a decimal point sliding left past zeros as the power drops: 0.001, 10⁻³ → the student calls the power at each stop.

3. **Say it to your companion** — one calls an ordinary number, the other answers in standard form, and back (lesson/lesson.md).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — planet masses in order, a bee's wingbeats, and ten conversions (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Writes 25,000 as 25 × 10³ → the first number must sit between 1 and 10: 2.5 × 10⁴. Count the places the point moved: four.
- Writes 0.004 as 4 × 10³ → small numbers take negative powers: 4 × 10⁻³. The point moved three places the other way.
- Writes 10 × 10⁵ as standard form → 10 is not allowed as the first number (it must be under 10): 10 × 10⁵ = 1 × 10⁶.
- Compares 3 × 10⁵ and 2 × 10⁶ by the first numbers only → the power decides first: 10⁶ beats 10⁵ whatever the first number is. Compare powers, then first numbers.
- This lesson's next door: expressions (Stage 6) — letters take the place of numbers, and the same short writing starts to carry algebra.
