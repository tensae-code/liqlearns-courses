# Hands-on — Standard Form

The lesson writes real big and small numbers short, the way Egypt's Grade 7 book does.

## The vanishing zeros
Write 149,600,000 on paper. Cross out the zeros in groups of three
as you move the point left: 149,600,000 → 1.496 × 10⁸. Count the
places moved: eight. Do the same for 15,000,000 (the Sun's core
temperature, 15 million degrees): 1.5 × 10⁷. Then for your own big
number — the population of a country, the distance to the Moon.

## The sliding point
Write 0.001, 0.0001, 0.00001 in a column. Beside each, write the
standard form: 1 × 10⁻³, 1 × 10⁻⁴, 1 × 10⁻⁵. Slide your finger
along as the point moves left and the power drops. Then take a real
small number — a hydrogen atom's mass, Egypt's book's own example —
and say why nobody writes it out in full.

## Power-first ordering
Take five planet masses written in standard form (Egypt's book
orders Earth, Venus, Mercury and Mars this way). Order them without
converting any back: biggest power first, then biggest first number.
Check by converting only the winner and the loser.

## When is it worth it?
Sierra Leone's JSS 3 book gives a rule of thumb: standard form for
numbers under 0.001 or over 10,000, ordinary form otherwise. Test
it: write 500, 9,500, 12,000 and 0.0008 both ways and decide, for
each, which form you would actually use.
