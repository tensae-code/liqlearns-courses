**Acceptance count: taught in 16 countries (Kazakhstan, Egypt, Greece, South Africa, Sierra Leone, Libya, Laos, Indonesia, Nicaragua, Ethiopia, South Sudan, Iraq, Nepal, Cambodia, Kuwait, Iran).** Nepal carries standard form from powers-of-10 form (9000 = 9×10³) inside the Grade 7 exponents unit (Unit 23, pp. 197–202), to scientific notation proper in Grade 8 (Unit 13, pp. 104–109: 0.7 = 7×10⁻¹, 0.00071 = 7.1×10⁻⁴, Earth's mass 5.972×10²¹ kg, the hydrogen atom 1.673×10⁻²⁷ kg, with multiplication and division in scientific notation). The Grade 8 rocket problem is taught corrected: the total is 2.7×10⁹ km, not 2.7×10⁸ km. Iraq carries standard form only in part — scientific notation inside the powers lessons of Grade 7 (Ch. 1 L1-1, p. 8) and Grade 8 (Ch. 1 L1-2, p. 10; L1-4, p. 18, recurring decimals and scientific notation using a calculator), with a correct-the-error exercise on standard form in Grade 9 (Ch. 1, Q23, p. 9 — the printed wrong sum is intentional, not a book error); there is no ordering or operating in standard form. Egypt carries the fullest lesson — scientific notation for large and small numbers, conversion, ordering and operations, all on real data (planet masses, the Earth–Sun distance, the hydrogen atom, the bee). Kazakhstan carries standard form inside its powers chapter in two Grade 7 books, with word problems and sums in standard form. Greece carries it twice in Grade 7 — large numbers, then large and small together. South Africa carries scientific notation as one worksheet in Grade 8. Sierra Leone carries it as three JSS 3 lessons with an explicit rule of thumb for when to use it. Libya carries it as one section in Grade 8. Jordan is not in this lesson: its Grade 8 Term 1 book is not held, so no standard-form page can be pinned from Jordan. Armenia, Vietnam, Ghana and Mauritania are not in this lesson on the filed stock. Laos carries standard form through powers of 10 and scientific-notation forms in Grade 7, used again as the stated prerequisite form A × 10ⁿ in Grade 8. Indonesia carries standard form in the Grade 8 Merdeka student book (Bab 1, printed pp. 37–40) — conversions in both directions and calculating in standard form, with the book's stated range taught corrected as 1 ≤ a < 10 and its Earth's-mass exponent taught corrected; the companion guide's nanometre and Earth–Sun-distance slips in that chapter are never taught as fact. Nicaragua carries standard form only as module evidence — scientific notation in the Grade 7 module (Tema 3 of Unit II), the only place in its Grade 5–8 set where it is taught; no Nicaraguan student book teaches it. Ethiopia carries scientific notation in Grade 6 (754.25 = 7.5425 × 10²; 92,000,000 = 9.2 × 10⁷) and again in Grade 9 with rounding, significant figures, upper/lower bounds and error. South Sudan carries standard form only in part — a single page in Secondary 1 Unit 1 (p. 20), standard form for very large and small numbers, with no conversion, ordering or operations in standard form. Secondary 1 is the sole carrier. Cambodia carries standard form only in part — a single section giving the notation A × 10ⁿ with 1 ≤ A < 10 in Grade 8, with no pinned worked examples, no conversions to or from standard form, no operations in standard form and no small-number form. Kuwait carries standard form as scientific notation with integer exponents in Grade 9 Term 1 — conversion and operations on real data (the Jupiter–Earth diameter difference, 1.302×10⁵ km), with a scientific-notation project. Iran teaches scientific notation in Grade 9 (a × 10ⁿ, 1 ≤ a < 10); its Grade 8 book shows 10¹³ in a chapter opener's prose only, so Grade 8 is not cited here.
