**Acceptance count: taught in 11 countries (Kazakhstan, Jordan, Armenia, Egypt, Greece, South Africa, Libya, Sierra Leone, Laos, Yemen, Indonesia).** Kazakhstan carries the fullest ladder — natural exponent, same-base laws, zero and negative exponents, and standard form in one chapter. Jordan carries the integer-exponent laws in Grade 7. Armenia carries powers from Grade 7, including writing large numbers in exponent form. Egypt carries the powers lesson with factor trees and the 2³⁰-bytes example. Greece carries powers of rationals with natural and then integer exponents. South Africa carries the laws across Grades 8 and 9, stating the negative-exponent case as a law of its own. Libya carries all six index laws in Grade 10. Sierra Leone carries fractional powers in JSS 3. Vietnam is not in this lesson: its Grade 6 teaches no powers or roots, and its powers work sits in grades whose filed summaries do not pin this lesson's pages. Mauritania is not in this lesson: its Grade 6 teaches no powers or roots. Laos carries powers from repeated multiplication and powers of 10 in Grade 7, through the full rules table with negative powers in Grade 8, to rational exponents in Grade 9. Yemen carries powers from squaring and cubing in Grade 5, through powers and roots with named base and exponent in Grade 6, to rational exponents and power and root equations in Grade 10. Indonesia carries powers from positive-integer powers only in Grade 7 K13, through integer exponents in the Grade 8 Merdeka guide (guide-only evidence), to the laws of exponents, exponential functions and surds in Grade 10 (Merdeka and Revisi editions); Grade 7 Merdeka teaches no integer powers or roots.
