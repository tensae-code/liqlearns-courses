**Acceptance count: taught in 26 countries (Kazakhstan, Jordan, Libya, South Africa, Greece, Armenia, Vietnam, Ghana, Laos, Yemen, Indonesia, Nicaragua, Ethiopia, South Sudan, Iraq, Bangladesh, Nepal, Cambodia, Kuwait, Iran, China, Pakistan, Syria, Malaysia, Rwanda, Sudan).** Nepal carries negative numbers from integers on the number line with ordering only in Grade 6 (पाठ 3, pp. 50–52 — no integer arithmetic operations), through full integer arithmetic — addition, subtraction, multiplication and division with sign rules, opposites and absolute value — in Grade 7 (Unit 13, pp. 121–132), to integer simplification with the sign laws in Grade 8 (Unit 12, pp. 100–103). Grade 4 teaches no negative numbers. Bangladesh carries negative numbers only in part — integers as directed numbers, ordering integers, and addition and subtraction of integers on the number line in Class 6 (Ch. 2, pp. 15–35). Multiplication and division of negative numbers are not taught: the filing states explicitly that the Class 6 integers chapter covers addition and subtraction only, and no later class takes up ×/÷ of negatives. Iraq carries negative numbers from integers in Grade 6 — positives, negatives, zero neither, comparing and ordering and all four operations (Ch. 1, pp. 8–31) — through integers deepened in Grade 7 and rational numbers in Grade 8, to absolute value in Grade 10 Scientific (Ch. 2). Kazakhstan carries the fullest treatment — the agreed zero, the line, the bracket table, and all four operations with the Euler justification for two negatives. Jordan carries the first introduction (Grade 5, temperatures on the line) and the full integer unit a year later, taught with counting chips. Libya carries integers as a ten-lesson chapter in Grade 7, each sign case its own lesson. South Africa carries integers late, in Grade 7 Term 4, motivated by temperature and money. Greece carries positives and negatives as a whole chapter in Grade 7. Armenia carries negatives in Grade 6, including negative fractions. Vietnam carries integers in Grade 6, with negative fractions in Bài 23. Ghana carries only teacher-manual evidence (JICA Basic 5, Lesson 79), including one flagged wrong printed answer that this lesson teaches against. Mauritania is not in this lesson: its Grade 6 teaches no negatives and its 1AS teaches none either — the honest limit is stated, not filled. Laos carries negative numbers whole in Grade 6 — integers and the number line, addition and subtraction, sign rules for multiplication and division, and distance and absolute value with equations and inequalities. Yemen carries negative numbers from integers in Grade 7 — comparing and all four operations — to rational numbers in Grade 8, on the number line and in decimal form. Indonesia carries negative numbers whole in Grade 7 — integers on the number line with all four operations in both the Merdeka and the K13 Semester-1 editions — with powers of negative bases and their signs in the Grade 8 Merdeka student book. Nicaragua carries negative numbers whole in Grade 7 — the full signed-number unit in the student book (concept, line, absolute value, ordering, all four operations with decimals and fractions, powers of signed numbers) and integers as a set with divisibility, LCM and GCD in the module (module evidence). Ethiopia carries negative numbers from integers in Grade 7 — comparing, ordering and all four operations by counters and the number line — to rational numbers in Grade 8, with absolute value and the relationships among ℕ, 𝕎, ℤ and ℚ; there are no negative numbers before Grade 7 in Ethiopia. South Sudan carries negative numbers only in part — negative multiples and factors in Grade 8, and substituting negative numbers in Secondary 1. There is no standalone negative-number arithmetic topic and no integer-operations chapter; negatives in Grade 6 appear only inside algebra answers and are not cited. Cambodia carries negative numbers only in part — ordering and all four operations with sign rules in Grade 7, with negatives inside rational arithmetic in Grade 8; absolute value is not pinned anywhere in the set, with no definition, notation |x| or worked example. Kuwait carries negative numbers as its own Grade 6 integers unit — the number line, comparing and ordering, adding and subtracting — and again inside Grade 8 rational numbers with absolute value. Iran introduces integers through signed readings in Grade 6, teaches full integer arithmetic in Grade 7, and extends to rationals in Grade 8 and real numbers with absolute value in Grade 9.

China carries negative numbers only in part: Grade 6 Volume 2 teaches their meaning and their place on the number line, and Grade 7 extends opposites and absolute value to all real numbers — but no arithmetic with negative numbers is taught anywhere in the held set, so China enters this lesson as a partial.

Pakistan (Punjab) carries negative numbers through the integer work of Class 6, the rational-number line of Class 7 and the number-set treatment of Class 9.

Syria builds the integers in Grade 7 — opposites on the number line, operations with explicit sign rules, and the sign of a product decided by counting negative factors — and reuses that counting rule for products of several numbers in Grade 8 Algebra.

Malaysia builds negative numbers in Form 1 from real opposite changes, then works the whole integer arithmetic — number line, coloured chips, and sign rules generalised from a pattern — before repeating the same treatment for negative fractions and negative decimals.

Rwanda teaches negative numbers from Year 4 — integers in real-life signs, the number line from −10 to +10, opposites and distance as absolute value — through Year 5's comparing, ordering, adding and subtracting integers by the number line and the sign rules, Year 6's multiplying and dividing integers by the same sign rules, to the set of integers Z in Senior 1.

Sudan builds the integers in Grade 6 from opposite situations — floors above and below ground, sea level, temperatures — constructs their number line, and gives absolute value, addition, subtraction as adding the opposite, multiplication (negative × negative found from a rising pattern table) and division; its printed “−6 + 10 = −4” is corrected in the table above. Grade 7 plots integers on the number line again.
