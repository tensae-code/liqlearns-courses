# Lesson plan — Negative Numbers

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 5 — Negative Numbers, Powers and Roots · **Lesson:** Negative Numbers
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 17 of the maths course — the first lesson of Unit 5 (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** numbers below zero — reading them, placing them on the number line, and adding, subtracting, multiplying and dividing them.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on comparing-and-ordering (Unit 1) and on addition, subtraction, multiplication and division (Units 2–3). It needs nothing else.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: comparing-and-ordering — the number line, bigger and smaller. Recommended, never forced.
- Negative numbers are not new numbers invented for school: every book starts them from real things going below an agreed zero — temperature below freezing, depth below sea level, money owed. The zero is agreed, not natural.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Read a negative number and say where it sits on the number line — left of zero, and which of two negatives is bigger.
2. Add and subtract negative numbers, including subtracting a negative, by the books' rules.
3. Multiply and divide negative numbers by the sign rules — sign first, then size.
4. Use negatives in real readings: temperatures, heights and depths, money in and money out.

## What you need
A thermometer picture (or a real thermometer). Paper and a pencil. A number line drawn on paper, from −10 to 10.

## If the learner is stuck
- Says −5 is bigger than −2 → back to the line: the larger number lies to the right. On a thermometer, −2 °C is warmer than −5 °C; the nearer to zero, the bigger the negative.
- Adds −3 + (−4) and gets 1 → the books' picture: two debts make a bigger debt. Add the sizes, keep the minus: −3 + (−4) = −7.
- Turns subtracting a negative into subtracting more → say it in words first: taking away a debt of 4 is the same as gaining 4. 5 − (−4) = 5 + 4 = 9.
- Forgets the sign in multiplication → sign first, then size. Count the minus signs: an odd count of negatives gives a minus answer, an even count gives a plus.
- This lesson's next door: powers-and-indices (same unit) — powers of negative numbers, where the sign rule decides (−2)² against −2².
