# Hands-on — Negative Numbers

The lesson starts from real readings below zero, the way every book starts it.

## The thermometer walk
Draw a tall thermometer on paper, from −10 to +10. A partner calls a
temperature — "minus 5", "plus 3", "zero" — and you mark it and say
whether it is above or below zero. Then the partner calls two
temperatures and you say which is warmer. Warmer means nearer the top
of the thermometer, and that is the bigger number, even when both are
below zero.

## The debt table
Make a table with two columns: money in, money out. Write five money-in
amounts and five money-out amounts as signed numbers (+12, −7, …). Add
each column, then add the two totals. The books do this as accounting
tables of profit and loss; yours is the same table with your own numbers.

## The line walk
Draw a number line from −10 to 10 on the floor with chalk or tape.
Stand on a number. A partner calls an addition — "add minus 4" — and
you step left four places. "Subtract minus 3" — step right three
places. Say the landing number aloud before you look down. Ten moves
each, then swap.

## Sign-first race
A partner calls a multiplication, for example "(−3) × (−2) × (−5)".
You answer the sign first — odd count of minuses, so minus — and only
then the size: 30. First to ten correct signs wins; the size is the
check, the sign is the race.
