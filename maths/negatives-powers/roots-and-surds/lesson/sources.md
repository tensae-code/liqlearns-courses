**Acceptance count: taught in 17 countries (Kazakhstan, Jordan, Egypt, Greece, South Africa, Sierra Leone, Libya, Armenia, Vietnam, Laos, Yemen, Indonesia, Nicaragua, Ethiopia, South Sudan, Iraq, Bangladesh).** Bangladesh carries roots and surds only in part — square roots (√2 by long division to 1.4142), cube roots, rationalising denominators and irrational numbers on the number line in Class 8 (Exp. 2, pp. 25–46). Surds as such are not taught: there is no simplifying to basic form and no surd arithmetic, and the filing states explicitly that Class 9–10 teaches no surds at all. Iraq carries roots and surds from perfect squares and square roots with perfect cubes and cube roots in Grade 5 (Ch. 7 L3–L4), through square and cube roots in Grade 7 — the square garden of area 1,296 cm² with side 36 cm (Ch. 1 L1-5, p. 24) and estimating square and cube roots (Ch. 2 L2-6, p. 53) — and simplifying number sentences containing square roots in Grade 8 (Ch. 2 L2-3, p. 37), to roots and operations on them with conjugates a ± √b in Grade 10 Scientific (Ch. 3 L3-3–L3-4, pp. 40–57). Kazakhstan carries the deepest treatment — the inverse-operation definition, the irrationality proof, and surd work with sign conditions. Egypt carries the array-built introduction (144 squares, 729 cubes) and the root laws in two grades. Jordan carries surd operations and radical equations in Grade 9. Greece carries the square root and the irrationals in Grade 8. South Africa carries roots as worksheet practice across Grades 8 and 9, with estimation. Sierra Leone carries surds only in SSS 1 (Lessons 87–96) — its JSS never teaches surds as a topic, and the lesson says so. Libya carries the laws of surds in Grade 10. Armenia carries nth roots and rational exponents in Grade 9. Vietnam carries radicals in Grade 9. Ghana is not in this lesson: its JHS curriculum lists surds as a standard, but no taught surd material is filed from its teacher manuals. Laos carries roots from square roots and their rules in Grade 8, through nth roots, radicals and radical equations in Grade 9, to square-root and cube-root functions in Grade 10. Yemen carries roots from whole-number square and cube roots in Grade 5, through roots of powers and of rational numbers, to simplifying roots and operating on them in Grade 10. Indonesia carries roots from radicals in the Grade 8 Merdeka guide (guide-only evidence), through surds with rationalising denominators in Grade 10 (Merdeka and Revisi editions); Grade 7 teaches no roots in either edition. Nicaragua carries roots from square roots with simplifying and rationalisation in Grade 8, through irrationals on the number line in the Grade 8 module (module evidence), to nth roots and rational exponents in Grade 11. Ethiopia carries roots from squares, square roots, cubes and cube roots approximated by table or calculator in Grade 8 (surds are not kept exact there), to irrational numbers, operations on surds, like/unlike radicals and rationalising denominators in Grade 9. South Sudan carries roots and surds from the square root as the reverse of squaring in Grade 6 (√9 = 3), through square roots of fractions and decimals in Grade 7, square roots by the prime-factor method (√81) and the Newton/averaging method for √484 with roots of decimals and fractions in Grade 8, to surds — simplest form, adding and subtracting, simplifying, multiplication and division, and rationalising denominators — in Secondary 2. Honest limits: Grade 7 Activity 2b asks for √125 among perfect squares — 125 is not a perfect square and is not cited; Grade 8 Activity 2 Q5 "√195 + 17²" is not cited, since √195 is not whole (almost certainly meant √196).
