**Acceptance count: taught in 13 countries (Kazakhstan, Jordan, Egypt, Greece, South Africa, Sierra Leone, Libya, Armenia, Vietnam, Laos, Yemen, Indonesia, Nicaragua).** Kazakhstan carries the deepest treatment — the inverse-operation definition, the irrationality proof, and surd work with sign conditions. Egypt carries the array-built introduction (144 squares, 729 cubes) and the root laws in two grades. Jordan carries surd operations and radical equations in Grade 9. Greece carries the square root and the irrationals in Grade 8. South Africa carries roots as worksheet practice across Grades 8 and 9, with estimation. Sierra Leone carries surds only in SSS 1 (Lessons 87–96) — its JSS never teaches surds as a topic, and the lesson says so. Libya carries the laws of surds in Grade 10. Armenia carries nth roots and rational exponents in Grade 9. Vietnam carries radicals in Grade 9. Ghana is not in this lesson: its JHS curriculum lists surds as a standard, but no taught surd material is filed from its teacher manuals. Laos carries roots from square roots and their rules in Grade 8, through nth roots, radicals and radical equations in Grade 9, to square-root and cube-root functions in Grade 10. Yemen carries roots from whole-number square and cube roots in Grade 5, through roots of powers and of rational numbers, to simplifying roots and operating on them in Grade 10. Indonesia carries roots from radicals in the Grade 8 Merdeka guide (guide-only evidence), through surds with rationalising denominators in Grade 10 (Merdeka and Revisi editions); Grade 7 teaches no roots in either edition. Nicaragua carries roots from square roots with simplifying and rationalisation in Grade 8, through irrationals on the number line in the Grade 8 module (module evidence), to nth roots and rational exponents in Grade 11.
