# Hands-on — Roots and Surds

The lesson builds roots from arrays of counters, the way Egypt's Grade 7 book builds them.

## Square arrays
Lay out counters in squares: 9 counters (3 by 3), 16 (4 by 4), 25
(5 by 5), 36, 49, 64, 81, 100, 121, 144. Read each side length aloud
as the square root: √144 = 12. Then try 50 counters: no square fits
exactly — √50 sits between 7 and 8, nearer 7. That is an estimate,
and the book's tables do exactly this.

## Cube arrays
Build cubes from small cubes or sugar lumps: 8 (2 by 2 by 2), 27,
64, 125, 216, 512. Read the edge as the cube root: ∛512 = 8. Egypt's
book works a cube of volume 512 with edge 8, one face 64 and total
area 384 — check each number on your own cube.

## The pull-out game
A partner calls a surd — √48, √75, √98, √200. You pull out the
biggest perfect square inside and say the basic form: √48 = 4√3,
√75 = 5√3, √98 = 7√2, √200 = 10√2. Check by squaring back: 4² × 3
must be 48. First to five clean pull-outs wins.

## Rationalise race
Write 1/√2, 1/√3, 2/√5, 3/√7 on cards. A partner picks one; you
multiply top and bottom by the root and simplify: √2/2, √3/3,
2√5/5, 3√7/7. No root may stay downstairs.
