**Acceptance count: taught in 23 countries (Egypt, Jordan, Libya, Kazakhstan, Greece, Armenia, Vietnam, Ghana, Mauritania, Laos, Yemen, Indonesia, Nicaragua, Ethiopia, South Sudan, Iraq, Bangladesh, Nepal, Cambodia, Kuwait, North Macedonia, Iran, China).**. Egypt carries the column algorithm whole — borrowing from hundreds to tens, borrowing in both places, borrowing across zero. Jordan carries subtracting without regrouping in Grade 1 and with regrouping in Grade 2. Libya carries subtracting without regrouping to 100 in Grade 1, regrouping to 1000 in Grade 2, and to 10,000 in Grade 3. Kazakhstan carries the written column algorithm on named cases in Grade 2. Greece carries vertical subtraction with borrowing in Grade 1 Teuxos B. Armenia carries subtracting with place transitions in Grade 2. Vietnam carries subtracting without regrouping in Grade 1 and with regrouping in Grade 2. Ghana carries subtracting to 20 as teacher evidence — and its workbook's printed '15 − 3 = 18' is flagged wrong in the filing and is never taught. Mauritania carries subtraction with and without carrying in Grade 2. Other read countries teach subtracting inside their own Grade 1–3 books and join by merge.** Laos carries subtraction from take-away stories within 10 and crossing 10 by splitting to 10 in Grade 1, through 2-digit borrowing and 3-digit place-value subtraction in Grade 2, to whole-number and decimal subtraction in Grade 5. Yemen carries subtraction from take-away within 10 in Grade 1 through borrowing within 1000, 10,000 and 999,999, with estimating and checking by addition, to hundreds of millions in Grade 5. Indonesia carries subtraction from taking away to 10 and to 20 in Grade 1, through subtraction under 20 in Grade 2, column subtraction to 100 with borrowing in Grade 3 and to 1,000 in Grade 4, to column subtraction to 100,000 in Grade 5, then integer, fraction and decimal subtraction in Grade 7 (Merdeka and K13 Semester 1). Nicaragua carries subtraction from taking away within 10 in Grade 1, through borrowing to 100 in Grade 2 and to 1,000 in Grade 3, to a 4–5-digit worked box in Grade 4 (its only subtraction teaching there), and signed-number subtraction in Grade 7. Ethiopia carries subtraction from subtracting to 10 with the addition–subtraction relationship in Grade 1, through subtracting to 1000 without borrowing and with borrowing in Grade 2, to subtracting to 10,000 in Grade 3 and to 1,000,000 with borrowing in Grade 4. South Sudan carries subtraction from taking away without and with regrouping in Grade 1 (90 − 10 = 80; 188 − 69 = 119), through number-line subtraction and subtraction without borrowing in Grade 2, to subtraction to 4 digits in Grade 3 (6981 − 2221), and subtraction of fractions in Secondary 1. Iraq carries subtraction from to 10 and to 20 in Grade 1 — no regrouping — through regrouping to 999 in Grade 2 and to 9999 in Grade 3, to subtraction within millions in Grade 4, and to subtracting large numbers within billions in Grade 5. Bangladesh carries subtraction from the concept within 10 including subtraction of zero in Class 1, through subtraction with borrowing to 3 digits in Class 2 and borrowing across zeros in Class 3, to 5-digit subtraction in Class 4, and subtraction of integers in Class 6. Nepal carries subtraction from take-away to 5, 9, 10 and 19 and two-digit subtraction without borrowing in Grade 1, through subtraction with borrowing in Grade 2 (both books, including borrowing across zero in the Nepali book) and to 4 digits in Grade 3, to 5–6 digit subtraction with borrowing checked by adding back in Grade 4, and subtraction of fractions and decimals in Grades 5–6. Cambodia carries subtraction whole from single digits in Grade 1 through decimals in Grades 4–6, with subtracting negatives as the motive for negative numbers in Grade 7. Kuwait carries subtraction up the same ladder as addition — from 10 in Grade 1, to 100 across Grades 1–2, 3-digit numbers in Grade 2, to 10,000 in Grade 3, to millions in Grade 4, integers in Grade 6 and rational numbers in Grade 8. North Macedonia carries subtraction at Grade 1 (2022) — differences to 5, to 10 and to 20, built on the link between addition and subtraction from the start. Iran carries subtraction from the first written expressions in Grade 1 (§14) through borrowing in Grade 2 to larger numbers in Grade 3, and keeps it in use through the primary years.

China carries subtraction from Grade 1 borrowing work and the subtraction table to the Grade 4 part-relations and the chained-subtraction simplification.
