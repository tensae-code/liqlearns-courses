# Subtraction — the lesson

Course: Maths · Unit: Unit 2 — Addition and Subtraction · Lesson: Subtraction
Built from: Egypt, Grade 2 Term 1 (old Discover series) — Jordan, Grades 1–2 — Libya, Grades 1–3 — Kazakhstan, Grade 2 (Zhakupova set) — Greece, Grade 1 Teuxos B — Armenia, Grade 2 — Vietnam, Grades 1–2 — Ghana, DL workbook Level 2 (teacher evidence) — Mauritania, Grades 1–2

## Do it first
13 stones in a pile. Take 5 away. Count what is left: 8.
That is subtracting — taking a number away and counting what
is left. 13 − 5 = 8. The − sign means "take away"; the answer
is called the difference.

## Sub lesson 1 — Subtraction In the Head
**In the head.** Two ways to take away small numbers. Count
back: 13 − 5 is 12, 11, 10, 9, 8. Or count up: from 5 to 13 is
5 jumps to 10 and 3 more to 13 — 8 jumps. Counting up is often
faster when the numbers are close: 52 − 48 is 4 jumps up.
Vietnam's Grade 1 book teaches subtraction without regrouping
within 100 (p.44), and Ghana's teacher guides teach subtracting
1-digit numbers, then 1- and 2-digit numbers to 20, as teacher
evidence (DL teacher guides, Module 2).

## Sub lesson 2 — Subtraction In Columns with Borrowing
**In columns, without borrowing.** Line the numbers up by place
value and subtract each column, starting from the ones. Libya's
Grade 1 book teaches subtracting ones, subtracting tens, and
subtracting tens and ones (Unit 16, pp.74–79), all without
regrouping. Jordan's Grade 1 book subtracts two-digit numbers
without regrouping (p.26).

  78
− 43
----
  35

Ones: 8 − 3 = 5. Tens: 7 − 4 = 3. Answer 35.

**Borrowing.** When the top digit is smaller than the bottom
digit, borrow one ten from the next place — it becomes ten ones.
Egypt's Grade 2 book teaches vertical subtraction with borrowing
from hundreds to tens — 137 − 54 = 83 (Chapter 8, Part 3,
pp.108–109) — and borrowing in both ones and tens places,
137 − 59 = 78 (Part 4). Jordan's Grade 2 book teaches subtraction
with regrouping. Kazakhstan's Grade 2 book teaches the written
column algorithm on the cases 77 − 32 and 60 − 14, 100 − 82
(Zhakupova set, pp.18–20). Greece's Grade 1 book teaches vertical
subtraction with borrowing (Teuxos B, ch.35). Libya's Grade 2
book teaches subtraction with regrouping ones, tens, and numbers
containing zeros (Unit 2, pp.36–43). Armenia's Grade 2 book
teaches subtracting two-digit numbers with place transitions
(p.34). Mauritania's Grade 2 book teaches subtraction with
carrying.

 137
− 54
----
  83

Ones: 7 − 4 = 3. Tens: 3 − 5 will not go — borrow one hundred,
which is ten tens: 13 tens − 5 tens = 8 tens. Answer 83.

## Sub lesson 3 — Subtraction Borrowing Across Zero and Checking
**Borrowing across zero.** When the next place is 0, borrow from
the place after that. Egypt's Grade 2 book works it: borrow 1
from hundreds to tens, then 1 from tens to ones — 107 − 59 = 48
(Chapter 8, Part 5). Libya's Grade 2 book teaches subtracting
numbers containing zeros (p.43), and its Grade 3 book teaches
subtraction to 10,000 with regrouping hundreds and thousands and
subtracting numbers containing zeros (Unit 3, pp.31–45).

 107
− 59
----
  48

107 is 0 hundreds, 10 tens, 7 ones; then 9 tens, 17 ones.
Ones: 17 − 9 = 8. Tens: 9 − 5 = 4. Answer 48.

**Checking.** Subtraction is undone by adding: the difference
plus what was taken away must give the starting number.
137 − 54 = 83 is checked by 83 + 54 = 137. If the check
disagrees, the work is wrong, not the check.

Note, honestly: one worked answer in Ghana's Lower workbook
prints "15 − 3 = 18". That is wrong — 15 − 3 = 12 — and it is
never taught here.

## Say it to your companion
Your companion calls two numbers: "13 take 5." Answer in your
head: "8." They call "137 take 54": write it in columns and say
each step aloud: "Ones: 3. Tens: borrow — 13 tens take 5 is 8
tens. 83." Trade five times, then check each answer by adding.

## Do It Yourself
1. Subtract in your head: 13 − 5; 16 − 9; 52 − 48; 31 − 7.
2. Subtract in columns: 78 − 43; 54 − 37; 137 − 54; 92 − 46.
3. Subtract in columns: 107 − 59; 503 − 268; 1,000 − 437.
4. Check each column answer by adding.
