# Lesson plan — Subtraction

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 2 — Addition and Subtraction · **Lesson:** Subtraction
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 8, the second lesson of Unit 2 (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** taking away — in the head and in columns, with borrowing — and checking the answer by adding.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Addition (Level 7) — the learner adds in columns with carrying, and subtraction is its inverse. It needs nothing else.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Subtraction In the Head** — subtract small numbers in the head by counting back and by counting up. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Subtraction In Columns with Borrowing** — subtract two-digit numbers in columns — the plain columns first, then borrowing from tens to ones — and say each step aloud. (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **Subtraction Borrowing Across Zero and Checking** — subtract three-digit numbers in columns including borrowing across zero, and check every subtraction by adding. (Was depth Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson (no hardness ranking is written in this lesson's question files).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Addition (same unit) — subtraction undoes addition, and adding checks every subtraction. Recommended, never forced.
- A soft hint only: the minus sign means "take away"; the answer is called the difference.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Subtract small numbers in the head by counting back and by counting up (13 − 5 = 8).
2. Subtract two-digit numbers in columns without borrowing, then with borrowing (137 − 54 = 83 — Egypt's worked example).
3. Borrow across a zero (107 − 59 = 48) and subtract three-digit numbers in columns.
4. Check a subtraction by adding: the difference plus what was taken away must give the starting number.

## What you need
Bundles of ten sticks and single sticks; squared paper.
Paper and a pencil.

## If the learner is stuck
- Takes the smaller digit from the bigger one in each column (54 − 37 = 23) → never swap digits in a column: borrow from the next place instead. 54 − 37 = 17.
- Forgets the borrowed ten → cross out the digit you borrowed from and write its new value small above it, before you subtract.
- Borrowing across zero feels impossible → borrow from the hundreds first: 107 becomes 0 hundreds, 10 tens, 7 ones — then borrow one ten for the ones: 9 tens, 17 ones. Egypt's worked example: 107 − 59 = 48.
- This lesson's next door: Multiplication (Unit 3) — the next unit starts with repeated adding.
