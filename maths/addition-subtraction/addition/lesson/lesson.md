# Addition — the lesson

Course: Maths · Unit: Unit 2 — Addition and Subtraction · Lesson: Addition
Built from: Egypt, Grade 2 Term 1 (old Discover series) — Jordan, Grades 1–2 — Libya, Grades 1–3 — Kazakhstan, Grade 2 (Zhakupova set) — Greece, Grade 1 Teuxos B — Armenia, Grade 2 — Vietnam, Grades 1–2 — Mauritania, Grade 1

## Do it first
8 stones in one pile, 5 stones in another. Push the piles
together. Count the pile: 13. That is adding — putting two
numbers together and counting what you get. 8 + 5 = 13. The +
sign means "put together"; the = sign means "is".

## Sub lesson 1 — Addition In the Head
**In the head.** The fastest way to add small numbers is to make
ten. 8 + 5: 8 needs 2 to make 10, and 5 gives 2 and leaves 3 —
10 + 3 = 13. Jordan's Grade 1 book teaches adding by doubles and
make-ten, and Vietnam's Grade 1 book teaches addition and
subtraction without regrouping within 100 (p.44) — the numbers
stay small enough to hold in the head. Kazakhstan's Grade 2 book
teaches adding one-digit numbers across the ten (L12) and the
addition table across the ten (L14).

27 + 5: 27 needs 3 to make 30, and 5 gives 3 and leaves 2 —
30 + 2 = 32.

## Sub lesson 2 — Addition In Columns with Carrying
**In columns, without carrying.** Line the numbers up by place
value — ones under ones, tens under tens — and add each column,
starting from the ones. Libya's Grade 1 book teaches adding ones,
adding tens, and adding tens and ones (Unit 16, pp.67–72), all
without regrouping. Jordan's Grade 1 book adds two-digit numbers
without regrouping (p.26), and its Grade 2 book adds with
regrouping only after the plain columns are firm.

  32
+ 45
----
  77

Ones: 2 + 5 = 7. Tens: 3 + 4 = 7. Answer 77.

**Carrying.** When a column adds to ten or more, ten of them
become one of the next place. Egypt's Grade 2 book teaches adding
by regrouping ones (Chapter 3, L8, pp.54–55) and vertical addition
with carrying from tens to hundreds — 74 + 63 = 137 (Chapter 8,
Part 1, pp.104–105) — and carrying in both ones and tens places,
74 + 68 = 142 (Part 2). Jordan's Grade 2 book teaches addition
with regrouping in two lessons (pp.48–50). Kazakhstan's Grade 2
book teaches the written column algorithm on the cases 32 + 45
and 46 + 14, 82 + 18 (Zhakupova set, pp.18–20). Greece's Grade 1
book teaches vertical addition with carrying in its last unit
(Teuxos B, ch.34). Libya's Grade 2 book teaches addition with
regrouping ones, tens, and tens and ones to 1000 (Unit 2,
pp.29–32). Armenia's Grade 2 book teaches adding with place
transitions — carrying — on p.24. Mauritania's Grade 1 book
teaches addition with carrying (p.23).

  74
+ 63
----
 137

Ones: 4 + 3 = 7. Tens: 7 + 6 = 13 tens — write 3 in the tens
place and carry 1 into the hundreds. Answer 137.

## Sub lesson 3 — Addition Three Digits and Checking
**Three digits.** The same columns, one more place. Egypt's Grade
2 book works 658 + 24 = 682 (Chapter 8, Part 6), aligning place
values and starting from the ones place. Libya's Grade 3 book
teaches addition to 10,000 with regrouping hundreds (Unit 2,
pp.22–30).

**Checking.** Add the numbers the other way round — 63 + 74 must
give the same 137. Estimate first — 247 + 45 is about 250 + 50 =
300, Jordan's printed estimate — and the exact answer, 292, sits
close to it. If the check disagrees, the work is wrong, not the
check.

## Say it to your companion
Your companion calls two numbers: "27 and 5." Add in your head:
"32." They call "74 and 63": write it in columns and say each
step aloud: "Ones: 7. Tens: 13 tens — write 3, carry 1. 137."
Trade five times, then check each answer by adding the other way
round.

## Do It Yourself
1. Add in your head: 8 + 6; 9 + 7; 27 + 5; 46 + 8; 58 + 7.
2. Add in columns: 32 + 45; 47 + 35; 74 + 63; 56 + 28.
3. Add in columns: 658 + 24; 347 + 185; 926 + 138.
4. Check each column answer by adding the other way round, and
   estimate 247 + 45 before working it out.
