# Lesson plan — Trigonometric Functions

**Course:** Maths · **Stage:** Stage 10 — Functions, Graphs and Sequences · **Lesson:** Trigonometric Functions
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 82 of 136, in Stage 10 — Functions, Graphs and Sequences (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the trigonometric functions of any angle — sine, cosine, tangent and their partners cotangent, secant and cosecant as functions: their signs by quadrant, their graphs, period, amplitude and phase, and sinusoidal models of things that rise and fall.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on right-triangle trigonometry (Stage 9) — sine, cosine and tangent of an acute angle — and on functions (Stage 10) and radian measure. It needs nothing else.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: right-triangle trigonometry (Stage 9) — the ratios in a right-angled triangle become functions of any angle here. Recommended, never forced.
- Recommended first: radian measure (Stage 9) — the books graph these functions in radians. Recommended, never forced.
- Recommended first: functions (Stage 10) — domain, range and reading a graph are used here without re-teaching. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Give the sine, cosine and tangent of an angle of any size, and name the sign of each function in each quadrant, the way Iraq's Grade 11 Scientific book organises the circular functions (Chapter 4, printed pp.54–103).
2. State the domain and range of all six trigonometric functions, as Pakistan's Class 11 book lists them (Unit 11, printed pp.200–217).
3. Draw the graphs of y = sin x, y = cos x and y = tan x, marking the period of each and the asymptotes of the tangent, as Ethiopia's Grade 10 book draws them (Unit 4, printed pp.199–250).
4. Read the amplitude, period, phase shift and middle line of a sinusoidal function a + b sin(cθ + d) (Pakistan, Class 11, Unit 11, printed pp.200–213).
5. Say how the graph of y = A sin(ωx + φ) is built from y = sin x by amplitude, period and phase, as China's B-edition book builds it (Compulsory Volume 3, §7.3, printed pp.36–56).
6. Fit a simple sinusoidal model to a rising-and-falling pattern — a tide table or a Ferris wheel (China, §7.4, printed pp.64–65; Pakistan, Unit 11, printed pp.200–217).

## What you need
Paper, a pencil, squared paper. A circle drawn large helps — the functions are read off it.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner walks a point around a circle, watching its height rise, fall below the middle, and come back — before the height is named sine (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — trig functions of any angle and their signs; the six functions, domains and ranges; the graphs; amplitude, period and phase; sinusoidal models (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the any-angle part: a picture of a point travelling around a unit circle, its height traced out beside it becoming the sine wave, one full turn drawing one full wave → the student watches the height go negative on the bottom half of the turn.
   - ADD IMAGE at the graphs part: a picture of the three graphs y = sin x, y = cos x and y = tan x side by side over 0 to 2π, the tangent's asymptotes drawn as dashed lines → the student names each graph and points to the asymptotes.
   - ADD IMAGE at the models part: a picture of a harbour tide table beside its fitted sine curve, the high and low tides touching the curve's top and bottom → the student reads the tide's period off the curve.
3. **Say it to your companion** — name an angle; your companion says the quadrant and the sign of sine, cosine and tangent there (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the walk around the circle and the wave it draws, in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — quadrant signs; domains and ranges; a graph's amplitude and period; a tide model (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks sine cannot be negative → walk the circle: on the bottom half the point's height is below the centre line, and sine IS that height (Iraq, Grade 11, Chapter 4).
- Mixes up which functions are positive in a quadrant → one picture holds it: in each quadrant name the one family that is positive there and let the other two be negative (Iraq's signs-by-quadrant table).
- Reads the period of sin(2x) as 2π → the c inside squeezes the wave: the period is 2π/c, so sin(2x) finishes its wave in π (Pakistan, Unit 11).
- Looks for a value of tan(π/2) → there is none: the tangent's graph runs off to its asymptote there — Ethiopia's book marks the asymptotes at odd multiples of π/2 (Unit 4).
- This lesson's next door: trigonometric identities and trigonometric equations (Stage 9's later lessons, built as catch-up) — the functions here get their formulas and their solving there.
