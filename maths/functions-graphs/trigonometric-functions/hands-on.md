# Hands-on — Trigonometric Functions

The lesson's functions come from a walk around a circle; these activities keep the walk physical.

## Walk the circle, draw the wave
Chalk a big circle on the floor with its centre and axes. One learner walks slowly around it while a partner, at a long strip of paper, plots the walker's height (distance above the centre line) against the angle walked. One full walk draws one full sine wave. Walk a second lap on the same strip: the wave repeats exactly — that is the period, felt in the feet.

## The quadrant sign game
Deal angle cards: 100°, 190°, 300°, −40°, 450°. For each card, stand in the right quadrant of the floor circle and call the signs: "sine — plus! cosine — minus! tangent — minus!" A wrong sign sends the card back into the deck. The deck is beaten when every card is called right first time.

## Stretch and squeeze the wave
Take the wave strip from the first activity. Stretch a copy tall on squared paper (every height doubled) and squeeze a copy short (every angle halved). Name what changed on each copy: amplitude on the tall one, period on the squeezed one. Then slide a copy sideways and name the phase shift.

## Fit the tide
Take a simple tide table (high tide, low tide, high tide, with times). Plot the heights against time, draw the smooth wave that touches the highs and lows, and read off the middle line, the amplitude and the time from high tide to high tide — the period. Write the model in the form a + b sin(cθ + d) with your own a, b and c.
