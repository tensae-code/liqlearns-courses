A lesson that teaches functions that come in a family: one
formula containing a parameter t, where each value of t gives
one graph of the family — and how a family is investigated
(zeros and extreme points computed in terms of t, with case
splits on t) and how the extreme points of the whole family
trace a single fixed curve, the locus curve (Ortskurve),
found by eliminating the parameter. Germany's Grade 11 book
(Lambacher Schweizer *Mathematik 11*, Ausgabe Bayern) teaches
it as the Exkursion "Funktionenscharen und Ortskurven" in
Kapitel V (printed p. 181), and its Grade 12 book opens with
the same topic as a full chapter unit (Kapitel I, printed
p. 6): investigating families with the tools of differential
calculus. No lesson in the course treated parameter families
— the course's loci lesson (further-geometry/loci) teaches
geometric loci under distance conditions, a different thing
from the locus curve of a family's extreme points — so the
expansion read flagged it NEW LESSON NEEDED and this lesson
is built from that flag, with the families and their loci
stocked together exactly as the placements place them.
Prerequisite (soft hint only — every lesson stands alone):
derivatives for finding extreme points. A learner who opens
this lesson directly still gets the whole thing: the family
idea, the investigation in terms of t, and the elimination
that produces the locus are all worked here.
