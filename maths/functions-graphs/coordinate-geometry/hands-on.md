# Hands-on — Coordinate Geometry

The lesson worked on squared paper, the way the books set it.

## Walk the segment
On big squared paper on the floor, mark A(0, 0) and B(10, 5). A partner calls a ratio — 1:1, 3:2, 4:1. Walk that fraction of the way from A to B, stand on the landing point, and read its coordinates off the floor grid. Then check with the section formula. Your feet should land where the formula says.

## The shortest rope
Draw a line across a page. Mark a point off the line. Cut strings of different lengths and lay each from the point to the line at a different slant. Measure them all. The shortest string is the one that stands perpendicular to the line — that string's length is the distance from the point to the line.

## Prove it with coordinates
Draw any four-sided shape on squared paper and read off its four corners. Prove what it is with coordinates only: midpoints of the diagonals for a parallelogram, lengths of the diagonals for a rectangle, lengths of the sides for a rhombus. No ruler on the drawing, no protractor — numbers only.

## A circle from a string
Pin a string at a centre point on squared paper, tie a pencil at the other end, and draw the circle the string forces. Read the centre and the string length, write the equation, then test it: pick three points on your drawn circle and check each one in the equation.
