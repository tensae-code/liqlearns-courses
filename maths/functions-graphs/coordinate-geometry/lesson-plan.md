# Lesson plan — Coordinate Geometry

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 10 — Functions, Graphs and Sequences · **Lesson:** Coordinate Geometry
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 80 of 136, in Unit 10 — Functions, Graphs and Sequences (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** coordinate geometry past plotting — the section formula for a point dividing a segment inside or outside, the shortest distance from a point to a line, proving shapes with coordinates, and the equation of a circle.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on coordinates and graphs (Unit 10) — plotting points, distance between two points, midpoint and slope — and on the circle work of the geometry units. It needs nothing else.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Dividing a Segment Inside** — the section formula for a point dividing a segment internally in a given ratio, and saying in words which end it sits closer to and why. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Dividing a Segment Outside** — dividing a segment externally in a given ratio — the point on the line past one end — and why equal external division has no point at all. (Was the external-division part of depth Level 3, which the lesson text teaches second.)
3. **Distance to a Line and Coordinate Proof** — the shortest distance from a point to a line, and choosing and carrying out the right coordinate test to prove a quadrilateral is a parallelogram, rectangle, rhombus or square. (Was depth Level 2.)
4. **The Equation of a Circle** — writing a circle's equation from its centre and radius or from a diameter, reading centre and radius back by completing the square, and saying when an equation names no real circle. (Was the circle part of depth Level 3.)

Old depth Level 3 splits across the text: its external-division part is Sub lesson 2 (taught second, straight after internal division) and its circle part is Sub lesson 4; old Level 2 (distance to a line and coordinate proof) is Sub lesson 3.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: coordinates and graphs (Unit 10) — distance, midpoint and slope are used here without re-teaching. Recommended, never forced.
- Recommended first: Pythagoras (Unit 8) — the distance formula and the point-to-line distance both rest on it. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Find the point that divides a segment internally in a given ratio, the way Kuwait's Grade 10 Term 2 book teaches internal division (Unit 9, section 9-2, printed pp.124–130).
2. Find the point that divides a segment externally in a given ratio (Kuwait, Grade 10 Term 2, section 9-2, printed pp.124–130).
3. Find the shortest distance from a point to a line, the way Jordan's Grade 9 book teaches it (Unit 4, Lesson 2, printed p.168).
4. Use coordinates to prove a quadrilateral is a parallelogram, a rectangle, a rhombus or a square (Jordan, Grade 9, Unit 4, Lesson 3, printed p.177).
5. Write the equation of a circle from its centre and radius, and from the ends of a diameter (Jordan, Grade 10, printed p.54; Ethiopia, Grade 10 student book, §7.5, printed pp.345–380).
6. Read the centre and radius back from a circle's equation by completing the square, and say when an equation names no real circle at all (South Sudan, Secondary 4, printed pp.30–40; Iran, Geometry (3), printed pp.40–46).

## What you need
Paper, a pencil, squared paper, a ruler. A compass helps for the circle work.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner marks two points on squared paper, walks three-fifths of the way from one to the other, and guesses the landing point's coordinates before any formula is given (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the section formula inside; the section formula outside; distance from a point to a line; coordinate proof; the circle's equation (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE at the section-formula part: a picture of a segment AB on a grid with a point P sitting three-fifths of the way from A to B, the two part-lengths labelled 3 and 2 → the student points to which end P sits closer to, and says why.
   - ADD GIF at the point-to-line part: a picture of a point above a line, with a rope dropped from the point to the line swinging until it stands straight up and down, the shortest landing marked → the student watches the rope shorten as it straightens.
   - ADD IMAGE at the circle part: a picture of a circle on a grid with centre (3, 4) and a radius line drawn to the point (5, 6) → the student measures the radius line with the grid and checks it against the equation.
3. **Say it to your companion** — give your companion two points and a ratio; they find the dividing point and say the method back to you (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the dividing point, the shortest rope and the circle in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — divide a segment, drop a distance to a line, prove a quadrilateral, and write two circle equations (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Swaps the ratio weights in the section formula → Kuwait's way: the weight on an end is the length of the part at the OTHER end; a point sitting near A takes mostly A's coordinates.
- Gets a negative length for a distance → a distance is the size of the answer, never its sign: take the value without the minus sign, as Jordan's lesson does.
- Tries to prove a rectangle with slopes alone → a coordinate proof needs the right test for the right claim: equal diagonals for a rectangle, equal sides for a rhombus, both for a square (Jordan, Grade 9, printed p.177).
- Cannot read a centre from x² + y² + 6x − 4y + 4 = 0 → complete the square on x and on y separately, the way South Sudan's book does (printed pp.30–40); the centre hides in the two brackets.
- This lesson's next door: conic sections (Unit 12) — the circle here is the first conic; the parabola, ellipse and hyperbola wait there.
