# Coordinate Geometry — short answer

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. Answer in a word or a short sentence.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Find the point dividing A(1, 2) to B(11, 7) internally in the ratio 3:2.
**Answer:** (7, 5) — ((2·1 + 3·11)/5, (2·2 + 3·7)/5).

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** A point divides the segment from A to B in the ratio 4:1. Which end does it sit close to?
**Answer:** Close to B — it has walked 4 of the 5 parts from A, so only 1 part remains; in the formula the weight on B's coordinates is 4.

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** Find the distance from (3, 1) to the line 3x + 4y − 10 = 0.
**Answer:** 3/5 — |9 + 4 − 10| / √(9 + 16) = 3/5 (Jordan, Grade 9, p.168).

**4. (Sub lesson 3)** A parallelogram has corners (0, 0), (4, 1), (5, 4), (1, 3). What single midpoint check proves it?
**Answer:** Both diagonals have midpoint (2.5, 2) — (0, 0)–(5, 4) and (4, 1)–(1, 3) share it.

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** What extra fact turns a proven parallelogram into a proven rectangle?
**Answer:** Equal diagonals — the coordinate-proof test in Jordan's Grade 9 book (p.177).

**6. (Sub lesson 4)** Write the equation of the circle with centre (3, 4) passing through (5, 6).
**Answer:** (x − 3)² + (y − 4)² = 8 — r² = (5 − 3)² + (6 − 4)² = 8 (Ethiopia, Grade 10, §7.5).

**7. (Sub lesson 4)** Find the centre and radius of x² + y² − 6x + 2y + 6 = 0.
**Answer:** Centre (3, −1), radius 2 — completing the square gives (x − 3)² + (y + 1)² = 4 (South Sudan, Secondary 4, pp.30–40).

**8. (Sub lesson 4)** A circle has diameter ends (0, 0) and (6, 8). Write its equation.
**Answer:** (x − 3)² + (y − 4)² = 25 — centre is the midpoint (3, 4), radius is half the diameter, 5.
