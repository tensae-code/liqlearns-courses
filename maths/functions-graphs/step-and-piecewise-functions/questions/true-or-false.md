# Step and Piecewise Functions — true or false

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — The Floor Function and Its Staircase · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Fractional Part, Sign and Stepped Charges · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Piecewise Rules and the Absolute-Value Graph.

Say true or false, and why in one line.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** [7] = 7. — **True.** A whole number is its own floor.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** [−3.1] = −3. — **False.** −3 is bigger than −3.1; the floor is −4.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** {x} can equal 1 for some x. — **False.** {x} < 1 always; at the moment it would reach 1, [x] jumps and {x} drops to 0.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** sgn(x) is a step function. — **True.** Its graph is three flat pieces with jumps at 0 (Laos, Grade 10, p.83).

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** A piecewise function is really two different functions, not one. — **False.** It is one function with different rules on different parts of its domain (Iran, Hesaban (1), pp.44–53).

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** The graph of f(x) = |ax + b| + c opens downward. — **False.** It is a V opening upward, with its vertex where the inside is zero (Kuwait, Grade 10 Term 1, pp.16–32).
