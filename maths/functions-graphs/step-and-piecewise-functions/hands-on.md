# Hands-on — Step and Piecewise Functions

The lesson's functions jump; these activities make the jumps physical.

## The staircase walk
Draw the floor function's staircase big on the floor with chalk: flat treads from each whole number to the next, a jump at each whole number. Walk slowly along a tread calling out "[x] is 2… still 2… still 2…" and shout the jump as you step up: "3!" Then walk the negative side and feel why [−1.2] drops to −2 — you walk DOWN to the whole number below.

## Price the parcels
Make a stepped postage table (up to 1 kg: one price; over 1 kg to 2 kg: the next price; and so on — the shape of Iran's tariff examples). Weigh or invent ten parcels — 0.4 kg, 1 kg exactly, 1.01 kg, 2.7 kg — and price each one from the table. The parcel at exactly 1 kg and the parcel at 1.01 kg are the argument: which step holds each one?

## Fold the V
Graph paper: plot y = x, a straight climbing line. Now fold the paper along the x-axis and lift the part below the axis up. Open it: the V of y = |x|. Repeat with the fold line shifted — fold y = x + 1 along y = 0 — and read the vertex of y = |x + 1| off the fold.

## Two rules, one machine
One partner is a piecewise machine: "below 0, double it; 0 and above, add 10." The other feeds numbers: −3, −0.5, 0, 0.5, 4. The machine must say the rule out loud before answering each time. Swap roles with a new pair of rules.
