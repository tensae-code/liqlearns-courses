# Coordinates and graphs — the lesson

Course: Maths · Unit 10, Functions, Graphs and Sequences · Lesson: Coordinates and graphs
Built from: Egypt, Jordan, Kazakhstan, Vietnam, South Africa,
Mauritania, Ghana (teacher evidence) — see lesson/sources.md
for every part.

## Do it first
Draw two lines crossing at right angles on squared paper.
The flat line is the **x-axis**, the upright line is the
**y-axis**, and where they cross is the **origin**, (0, 0).
Now pick a point: walk 3 squares right along the x-axis, then
2 squares up. That point is (3, 2) — x first, then y, always.
Plot (3, 2), (−2, 1), (1, −3) and (−4, −2). You have just
used the coordinate plane, and the four quarters you plotted
in are the four **quadrants**. Jordan's Grade 6 book starts
exactly here: the coordinate plane in all four quadrants,
with negative coordinates.

## Sub lesson 1 — Plotting, Distance and Midpoint
**Distance and midpoint.** Two points on the plane have a
distance between them and a point halfway between them.
Kazakhstan's Grade 9 book proves both from coordinates: the
distance between (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂) is
√((x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²) — Pythagoras on the plane, with
the differences as the two short sides. The midpoint is the
average of the ends: ((x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2). The distance
from (1, 2) to (4, 6) is √(3² + 4²) = 5; the midpoint is
(2.5, 4). Egypt's Preparatory 3 book uses the same distance
idea when it works with coordinates, and Kazakhstan's book
uses it to find the centroid of a triangle: the average of
its three corners.

## Sub lesson 2 — Gradient and Intercepts

**Straight-line graphs.** A straight line is a set of points
whose coordinates fit one equation. South Africa's Grade 9
book builds graphs from tables of ordered pairs: pick x
values, work out y, plot the points, join them. For y = 2x + 1,
x = 0 gives y = 1, x = 1 gives y = 3, x = 2 gives y = 5 —
the points lie on a straight line. Vietnam's Grade 8 book
teaches the first-degree function and its graph the same way,
with a table first and the line second.

**Gradient — how steep, and which way.** South Africa's Grade
9 book defines it in words the lesson keeps: "Gradient =
change in y / change in x", read left to right — up to the
right is positive, down to the right is negative. Its worked
gradients are 5/2 = 2.5 and 2/4 = 0.5. The gradient is the
same between any two points on a straight line — that is
what makes it straight. A gradient of 2 means y rises 2 for
every 1 that x rises; a gradient of −3 means y falls 3 for
every 1 that x rises; a gradient of 0 means the line is flat.
Egypt's Preparatory 3 book adds the link to angles: the
gradient m is the tangent of the line's inclination angle,
m = tan θ. And it adds the parallel and perpendicular rules:
parallel lines have equal gradients, and perpendicular lines
have gradients whose product is −1.

**Intercepts — where the line crosses the axes.** South
Africa's Grade 9 book names them plainly: "The point at
which the line crosses the y-axis is called the y-intercept."
The x-intercept is where the line crosses the x-axis (where
y = 0). For y = 2x + 1, the y-intercept is 1 (put x = 0)
and the x-intercept is −1/2 (put y = 0 and solve).

## Sub lesson 3 — The Equation y = mx + c

**The equation of a straight line.** Egypt's Preparatory 3
book teaches it as y = mx + b from the gradient m and the
y-intercept b, and other forms including a line through a
point with a given gradient. Jordan's Grade 8 book assumes
writing a line's equation in slope–intercept form as prior
learning when it solves systems graphically, and Kazakhstan's
Grade 9 book derives the same forms from vectors: the
two-point form, the general form ax + by + c = 0, and the
slope form y = kx + r. Mauritania's 3AS book teaches linear
and affine functions — y = ax and y = ax + b — as its
function chapter, and Ghana's teacher manual teaches the
coordinate plane as pairs of numbers for a point's position
(teacher evidence, not a pupil book).

So the whole lesson is one line: **y = mx + c**. The m tells
you the steepness and the direction; the c tells you where
the line crosses the y-axis. Given any two points, you can
find m from the change in y over the change in x, then find
c by putting one point in. Given m and c, you can draw the
line: mark c on the y-axis, then step along with the gradient.

> **Tip — from us, not from the books.** If a gradient comes
> out as a fraction, keep it as a fraction (rise 2, run 3)
> while you draw — "up 2, across 3" is easier to step out on
> squared paper than 0.667.

## Say it to your companion
Plot the points (0, 1) and (2, 5). Ask your companion: "What
is the gradient, what is the equation of the line, and where
does it cross the x-axis?" Then swap: they give you a line,
and you read off its gradient and intercepts.

## Do It Yourself
1. Find the distance between (0, 0) and (6, 8), and the
   midpoint of the two points. (Answers: distance 10,
   midpoint (3, 4).)
2. Find the gradient of the line through (1, 2) and (4, 8).
   (Answer: (8 − 2)/(4 − 1) = 6/3 = 2.)
3. Write the equation of the line with gradient 3 and
   y-intercept −2. (Answer: y = 3x − 2.)
4. A line passes through (0, 4) and (2, 0). Find its equation
   and its gradient. (Answer: gradient = (0 − 4)/(2 − 0) =
   −2; equation y = −2x + 4.)
