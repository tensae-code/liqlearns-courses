# Coordinates and graphs — true or false

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Plotting, Distance and Midpoint · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Gradient and Intercepts · (Level 3) splits by what the item asks: items about parallel or perpendicular gradients, and reading an intercept off a given equation, -> Sub lesson 2, where the gradient part teaches those rules; items about writing a line's equation -> Sub lesson 3 — The Equation y = mx + c.

Say true or false, and why in one line.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** The point (2, 3) is the same as the point (3, 2). — **False.** Order matters: x first, then y.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** The distance between (0, 0) and (3, 4) is 5. — **True.** √(9 + 16) = 5.

**3. (Sub lesson 1)** The midpoint of (1, 1) and (5, 5) is (3, 3). — **True.** The average of the ends.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** A gradient of 0 means the line is flat. — **True.** No change in y.

**5. (Sub lesson 2)** The gradient between any two points on a straight line is the same. — **True.** That is what makes it straight.

**6. (Sub lesson 2)** The line y = 2x + 1 crosses the y-axis at 2. — **False.** It crosses at 1; 2 is the gradient.

**7. (Sub lesson 2)** The lines y = 3x + 1 and y = 3x − 4 are parallel. — **True.** Both have gradient 3.

**8. (Sub lesson 2)** The lines y = 2x + 1 and y = (1/2)x + 1 are perpendicular. — **False.** Perpendicular gradients multiply to −1; 2 × 1/2 = 1, not −1.
