# Sequences and series — true or false

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Sequences, Rules and the nth Term · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Series — Adding the Terms · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — The Infinite Geometric Sum.

Say true or false, and why in one line.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** In an arithmetic sequence, the difference between neighbours is always the same. — **True.** That is the definition.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** 2, 4, 8, 16 is an arithmetic sequence. — **False.** It is geometric (multiply by 2); the differences 2, 4, 8 are not steady.

**3. (Sub lesson 1)** The 5th term of 1, 3, 5, 7, … is 9. — **True.** a₅ = 1 + 4 × 2 = 9.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** The sum 1 + 2 + … + 10 is 55. — **True.** Ten halves of 11: 10/2 × 11 = 55.

**5. (Sub lesson 2)** A series is the list of terms; a sequence is their sum. — **False.** It is the other way round: the sequence is the list, the series is the sum.

**6. (Sub lesson 2)** The sum of 2 + 4 + 8 is 14. — **True.** 2 + 4 + 8 = 14.

**7. (Sub lesson 3)** 1 + 2 + 4 + 8 + … has a finite sum to infinity. — **False.** The ratio is 2, which is not less than 1, so the terms grow and the sum never settles.

**8. (Sub lesson 3)** 0.999… equals 1. — **True.** It is the infinite geometric sum 9/10 + 9/100 + … = (9/10)/(1 − 1/10) = 1.
