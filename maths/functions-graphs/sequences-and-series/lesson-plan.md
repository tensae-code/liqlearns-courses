# Lesson plan — Sequences and series

**Course:** Maths · **Stage:** 10, Functions, Graphs and Sequences · **Lesson:** Sequences and series
**Place in the curriculum:** Stage 10, the third lesson of the
stage (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** sequences — arithmetic
(add the same) and geometric (multiply by the same) — their
nth terms, and series: the sums, including the infinite
geometric sum.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on functions (Stage 10,
second lesson: a sequence is a function of position) and on
powers (Stage 5: the geometric term uses powers). It needs
nothing else.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: functions, and powers and indices.
  Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Find the rule of a sequence and write its next terms.
2. Use aₙ = a₁ + (n − 1)d for arithmetic sequences and
   bₙ = b₁rⁿ⁻¹ for geometric sequences.
3. Add up an arithmetic series and a geometric series.
4. Use S = b₁/(1 − r) for an infinite geometric series, and
   turn a repeating decimal into a fraction.

## What you need
Paper, a pencil, a calculator for the powers.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner extends 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 and
   3, 6, 12, 24 and feels the two different rules
   (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the sequence and its rule; arithmetic;
   geometric; series and the two sum formulas; the infinite
   geometric sum (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE at the arithmetic part: a picture of a staircase
     of blocks growing by 3 each step, with the terms labelled
     → the student counts the steps.
   - ADD IMAGE at the geometric part: a picture of a bouncing

     student measures the shrinking heights.
   - ADD IMAGE at the series part: a picture of Gauss's pairing
     (1 with 100, 2 with 99, every pair 101) → the student

3. **Say it to your companion** — give a sequence, ask for its

4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,

5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.

## If the learner is stuck
- Cannot tell the families apart → check the neighbours: a
  steady difference means arithmetic, a steady ratio means
  geometric.
- Mixes the two sum formulas → arithmetic sums pair up
  (first + last); geometric sums shift and subtract.
- Thinks an infinite sum must be infinite → if the terms
  shrink fast enough (|r| < 1), the sum settles; 1/2 + 1/4 +
  1/8 + … never passes 1.
- This lesson's next door: Stage 11, statistics and
  probability — sequences return there as patterns in data.
