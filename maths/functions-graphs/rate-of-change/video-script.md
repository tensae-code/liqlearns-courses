# Video script — Rate of Change

Super-simple voice. The tutor talks; each number appears on screen as it is worked.

---

A school counts its pupils every year. The number grows. How fast is it growing? That question has a name: the rate of change.

A child is measured at nine years old: 135 centimetres. At thirteen: 155 centimetres. The height changed by 20 centimetres. The age changed by 4 years. Divide: 20 divided by 4 is 5. The height is changing at 5 centimetres per year. Change divided by change — that is a rate of change.

Rates can go down, too. A temperature falls by 2 degrees every hour. Its rate of change is minus 2 degrees per hour. The minus sign says: going down.

And if nothing changes? The rate is zero. Nothing gained, nothing lost.

Now look at a table. A typist writes 12 lines in a minute, 24 in two minutes, 36 in three. Every step adds 12. Same step, every time — this rate is constant.

But this sugar table: the price jumps by different amounts. The steps are not the same. This rate is not constant.

Two readings give a rate. A table tells you if the rate stays the same. That is rate of change.
