# Lesson plan — Special Sequences

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 10 — Functions, Graphs and Sequences · **Lesson:** Special Sequences
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 84 of 136, inside Unit 10 — Functions, Graphs and Sequences (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the special families that sit beyond the plain arithmetic and geometric progressions — harmonic progressions and the harmonic mean, arithmetico-geometric progressions, and the square, cube and triangular numbers.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on sequences-and-series — the learner already knows arithmetic progressions (A.P.), geometric progressions (G.P.), nth terms and sums. It needs nothing else.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Square, Cube and Triangular Numbers** — continuing each pictured family, stating its rule, and finding a named term — for example the 6th triangular number. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Harmonic Progressions and the Harmonic Mean** — flipping a harmonic progression into its arithmetic progression, finding an nth term and a harmonic mean, and inserting a harmonic mean between two numbers. (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **Arithmetico-Geometric Progressions** — spotting the A.P. part and the G.P. part of an arithmetico-geometric progression, and finding its sum to infinity by the multiply-and-subtract method. (Was depth Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson (no hardness ranking is written in this lesson's question files).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: sequences-and-series — a harmonic progression is an A.P. turned upside down, and an arithmetico-geometric progression is an A.P. married to a G.P. Recommended, never forced.
- Recommended first: fractions — harmonic work is fraction work: the terms are reciprocals. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Recognise and build the square numbers, cube numbers and triangular numbers, and state their rules, the way Sierra Leone's Primary 6 lesson plans teach them as teacher evidence (Primary 6 Term 2, Lessons 116–120, printed pp. 143–151 — teacher evidence).
2. Turn a harmonic progression into the A.P. of its reciprocals, find its nth term, and find the harmonic mean of two numbers as 2ab/(a+b), as Pakistan's Class 11 book teaches in Unit 6 (printed pp. 88–122).
3. Insert harmonic means between two numbers, working through the reciprocal A.P. (Pakistan, Class 11, Unit 6, printed pp. 88–122).
4. Recognise an arithmetico-geometric progression — each term an A.P. term times a G.P. term — and use the book's method for its sum to n terms and to infinity (Pakistan, Class 11, Unit 6, printed pp. 88–122).

## What you need
Counters or stones for building squares, cubes and triangle stacks. Paper and a pencil. A calculator helps for the fraction work.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner builds 1, 3, 6, 10 with counters in triangle stacks and 1, 4, 9 in square arrays, and says what comes next before any name is given (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — square, cube and triangular numbers with their rules; harmonic progressions by way of the reciprocal A.P. and the harmonic mean; arithmetico-geometric progressions and their sums (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the triangular-numbers part: a picture of counters stacking row by row — 1, then 2 under it, then 3 — the running totals 1, 3, 6, 10 appearing beside the stacks — the student calls out each running total before it appears.
   - ADD IMAGE under the square/cube part: a picture of a 4 × 4 square array of dots beside a 3 × 3 × 3 cube of small blocks → the student counts both and says which family each belongs to.
   - ADD IMAGE in the harmonic part: a picture of the two ladders side by side — the A.P. 1, 2, 3, 4 … on top and its reciprocals 1, 1/2, 1/3, 1/4 … underneath, rungs joined — the student reads a rung pair aloud in both directions.
3. **Say it to your companion** — name the family of a sequence your companion calls out, and defend the name with the rule (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the counter stacks and the flipped ladder in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — continue each family, find a harmonic mean, and sum an arithmetico-geometric progression to infinity (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Cannot see the triangular rule → build the stacks and write the running totals under them; the totals ARE the sequence, and each new row adds one more than the row before (Sierra Leone Primary 6, Lesson 120, printed p. 151 — teacher evidence).
- Harmonic nth term goes wrong → stop working with the fractions directly: flip every term, do the A.P. work on the flipped list, flip back at the end. That three-step move is the whole method in Pakistan's Unit 6.
- Confuses the three means → for two positive numbers the order never changes: arithmetic mean is the biggest, then geometric, then harmonic. Check any answer against that order.
