# Lesson plan — Polar Coordinates

**Course:** Maths · **Stage:** Stage 10 — Functions, Graphs and Sequences · **Lesson:** Polar Coordinates
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 83 of 136, inside Stage 10 — Functions, Graphs and Sequences (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** naming a point by how far it is from the origin and by the angle of the turn to reach it — the polar coordinates (r, θ) — and changing between polar and Cartesian coordinates.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on coordinates-and-graphs and on trigonometry — the learner can already plot (x, y) and use sin, cos and tan on a right triangle. It needs nothing else.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: coordinates-and-graphs — a polar point is still a point on the same plane, only named a second way. Recommended, never forced.
- Recommended first: trigonometric ratios — cos θ and sin θ are the two legs of the right triangle whose hypotenuse is r. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name a point in polar form P(r, θ), saying what r (the distance from the origin) and θ (the angle from the positive x-axis) each mean, the way Bangladesh's Class 9 book sets it up in §10.2 (printed pp. 173–175).
2. Change polar coordinates into Cartesian coordinates with x = r cos θ and y = r sin θ, as in Bangladesh's worked example P(5, 240°) → (−5/2, −5√3/2) (§10.2, printed pp. 173–175).
3. Change Cartesian coordinates into polar coordinates with r = √(x² + y²) and the angle found from the point's quadrant — including the corrected example: for P(−√3, 1), r = 2 and the polar name is P(2, 150°), NOT the r = √5 printed in the book (Bangladesh, §10.2 Example 1, printed p. 174, taught corrected).
4. Read polar coordinates the Kuwait way, inside the trigonometric form of a complex number, with r ≥ 0 and the argument taken in [0, 2π) (Kuwait, Grade 11 Term 2, lesson 7-2, printed pp. 25–32).

## What you need
Squared paper, a ruler, a protractor. A piece of string helps: one end pinned at the origin, the free end swung to the point.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner pins a string at the corner of a squared page, swings it to a marked point, and says two things: how long the string is, and how far it has turned (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the polar name (r, θ); polar to Cartesian; Cartesian to polar, with the corrected Bangladesh example; the same point with many polar names, and Kuwait's r ≥ 0, argument in [0, 2π) rule (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the polar-name part: a picture of a ray starting along the positive x-axis and sweeping round to a point, the angle arc growing as it sweeps and the distance r marked along the ray — the student calls out the angle where the sweep stops.
   - ADD IMAGE under the conversion part: a picture of a right triangle with hypotenuse r, angle θ at the origin, horizontal leg x = r cos θ and vertical leg y = r sin θ → the student traces each leg and says which formula gives it.
   - ADD IMAGE in the corrected-example part: a picture of the point (−√3, 1) plotted in the second quadrant with its distance 2 and angle 150° marked → the student checks r with Pythagoras before reading the angle.
3. **Say it to your companion** — give your companion a point in one form and have them answer in the other form, saying r and θ (or x and y) whole (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the string-and-turn story in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — convert four points each way and plot one point from its polar name (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes up r and θ → go back to the string: the length of the string is r, the turn of the string is θ. Length first, turn second — that is the order in (r, θ).
- Finds r with √(x² + y²) but takes the acute angle in the wrong quadrant → plot the point first, look at which quadrant it sits in, then choose the angle that actually points there; Bangladesh's corrected example (−√3, 1) sits in the second quadrant, so its angle is 150°, not 30°.
- Gets a negative r → Kuwait's rule keeps r ≥ 0: turn the angle by 180° instead and keep the distance positive (Kuwait, Grade 11 Term 2, printed pp. 25–32).
