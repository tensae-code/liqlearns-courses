**Acceptance count: taught in 3 countries (Bangladesh, Kuwait, Syria).** Bangladesh carries polar coordinates as a named section of its Class 9 trigonometry-and-coordinates chapter — the definition of P(r, θ), both conversion formulas, and a full exercise set on printed p. 178 asking for points to be expressed in (r, θ) — with its Example 1 on printed p. 174 taught corrected in this lesson: r = 2 and P(2, 150°), not the printed r = √5. Kuwait carries polar coordinates inside its Grade 11 scientific complex-numbers unit, where lesson 7-2 (printed pp. 25–32) teaches polar coordinates together with the trigonometric form, under the conditions r ≥ 0 and argument in [0, 2π). Kuwait teaches no De Moivre's theorem and no nth roots in this book, and this lesson claims neither for it.

Syria meets polar coordinates in the Argand plane: every point is located by its modulus r and argument θ.
