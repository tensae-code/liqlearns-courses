**Acceptance count: taught in 2 countries (Pakistan, Greece).** Pakistan is the sole full source and carries the whole lesson in one chapter: attributes, dichotomy and the (A)/(α) notation with class and ultimate frequencies and consistency (pp.215–216); the independence criterion (AB) = (A)(B)/n (pp.217–219); positive, negative and complete association with Yule's Q (pp.219–220); r×c contingency tables (p.223); and the chi-square test of independence with df = (r − 1)(c − 1), the expected-frequency ≥ 5 rule with pooling, Yates' correction, and the contingency coefficient C (pp.224–232, C at p.226). Its recorded boundary is part of the count: the book teaches **no chi-square goodness-of-fit test** — χ² is used only for independence — and none is claimed anywhere in this lesson. Greece carries the descriptive ground only: contingency tables and bar charts (W11, Unit 2.5) and comparing characteristics across categories (Unit 2.6), with no inference of any kind, as filed. No other country's filing teaches association of attributes, so the lesson stands honestly on one full source and one descriptive companion — it is not padded.
