# Association of Attributes — multiple choice

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Attributes and the 2×2 Table · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Independence and Yule's Q · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — The Chi-Square Test and the Contingency Coefficient.

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** An attribute is:
a) a measurement on a scale  b) a quality that is present or absent  c) an average
**Answer:** b) a quality, present or absent — Pakistan Statistics Class 12, Ch. 15 (pp.215–216).

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** In the notation of Ch. 15, (αB) counts the people who:
a) have both A and B  b) lack A but have B  c) have A but lack B
**Answer:** b) lack A but have B — α is the absence of A (pp.215–216).

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** A and B are independent exactly when:
a) (AB) = 0  b) (AB) = (A)(B)/n  c) (AB) = (A) + (B)
**Answer:** b) (AB) = (A)(B)/n — the independence criterion (pp.217–219).

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** Yule's Q = 0 means:
a) complete association  b) independence  c) the table is inconsistent
**Answer:** b) independence — Q = ±1 is complete association, Q = 0 is no association (pp.219–220).

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** For a 3×4 contingency table, the chi-square test of independence has df =
a) 12  b) 6  c) 11
**Answer:** b) 6 — df = (r − 1)(c − 1) = 2 × 3 (pp.224–232).

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** An expected frequency in a cell comes out at 3. Before computing χ² you should:
a) proceed anyway  b) pool that row or column with a neighbour so expectations reach at least 5  c) replace it with the observed value
**Answer:** b) pool — the expected-frequency ≥ 5 rule (pp.224–232).

**7. (Sub lesson 3)** Pakistan's Statistics Class 12 teaches the chi-square test for:
a) independence only  b) goodness of fit only  c) both independence and goodness of fit
**Answer:** a) independence only — the book teaches no goodness-of-fit test, as filed; claiming one for it would be false.
