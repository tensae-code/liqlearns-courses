# Sampling and Sampling Distributions — ordering

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Populations, Censuses and Kinds of Data · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — The Sampling Methods, Bad Samples and Errors · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Sampling Distributions and the Central Limit Theorem.

Put the steps or values in the right order.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Order the stages of a sample survey: (a) draw the sample from the frame; (b) name the population and build the frame; (c) compute the statistic; (d) make the decision about the population.
**Answer:** (b), (a), (c), (d) — population and frame first, the statistic last but one (Pakistan, Ch. 11, pp.27–36).

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** Order a systematic sample's steps, interval 70: (a) take unit 108; (b) choose the random start 38; (c) take unit 38; (d) take unit 178.
**Answer:** (b), (c), (a), (d) — 38, 108, 178, …, 668 (Kuwait, Unit 2, p.50).

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Order by how much of the population is examined, least first: (a) a census; (b) a stratified sample of fraction 0.05; (c) a systematic sample of 1 in 60.
**Answer:** (c), (b), (a) — 1/60 ≈ 0.017 < 0.05 < the whole population.

**4. (Sub lesson 3)** Order the enumeration build: (a) list every possible sample; (b) compute the statistic for each; (c) collect the values into a distribution; (d) read off its mean and standard error.
**Answer:** (a), (b), (c), (d) — complete enumeration, Pakistan's method (Ch. 11, pp.36–51).
