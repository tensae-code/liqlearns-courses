# Sampling and Sampling Distributions — short answer

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Populations, Censuses and Kinds of Data · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — The Sampling Methods, Bad Samples and Errors · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Sampling Distributions and the Central Limit Theorem.

Answer in a word or a short line.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** In Nicaragua's Grade 9 case, a population of 50 pupils gives a sample of 12 interviewed. Which number is the population size?
**Answer:** 50 — the whole group; 12 is the sample (Nicaragua, Grade 9, Unit 8, pp.149–158).

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** Name the list from which a sample is actually drawn.
**Answer:** The sampling frame (Pakistan, Ch. 11, pp.28–29).

**3. (Sub lesson 1)** Give one discrete and one continuous example of data.
**Answer:** Discrete: goals in a match (counted). Continuous: a pupil's weight (measured) — Kazakhstan's chart, Ch. 7, pp.104–132; Ghana JICA B6, Lessons 99–108 (teacher evidence).

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** From a population of N, how many samples of size n are possible WITH replacement?
**Answer:** Nⁿ (Pakistan, Ch. 11, Theorem 11.1, pp.30–32).

**5. (Sub lesson 2)** A survey about a whole town's travel is taken only at a bus stop. In one line, what is wrong?
**Answer:** The sample is not representative — bus-stop people cannot speak for the whole town (North Macedonia, Grade 8, Theme 1, unit 13, pp.50–51).

**6. (Sub lesson 2)** A form is printed with a confusing question and hundreds answer it wrongly. Sampling error or non-sampling error?
**Answer:** Non-sampling error — it would poison a census too (Pakistan, Ch. 11, pp.32–33).

**7. (Sub lesson 3)** State the mean and the variance of the sampling distribution of a proportion P.
**Answer:** Mean π; variance π(1 − π)/n (Pakistan, Ch. 11, pp.57–62).

**8. (Sub lesson 3)** In randomised response, why does the collector accept a larger sampling error?
**Answer:** Because no one can tell which question any single answer belongs to, so people answer the sensitive question honestly (China, A edition, Ch. 9, reading, p.185).
