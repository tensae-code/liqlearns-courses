# Sampling and Sampling Distributions — true or false

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Populations, Censuses and Kinds of Data · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — The Sampling Methods, Bad Samples and Errors · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Sampling Distributions and the Central Limit Theorem.

Say true or false, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** A census has no errors at all.
**Answer:** False — a census can still carry non-sampling errors: missed houses, wrong records, refusals (Pakistan, Ch. 11, pp.32–33).

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** Monthly family income is discrete data.
**Answer:** False — income is measured on a scale and is continuous (Kuwait workbook, Unit 6, pp.85–86, workbook evidence).

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Interviewing ten times as many people at the same bus stop makes the survey representative.
**Answer:** False — a bigger bad sample repeats the same wrong crowd; the missing groups stay missing (North Macedonia, Grade 8, unit 13, pp.50–51).

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** In systematic sampling the first unit is chosen at random inside the first interval.
**Answer:** True — the start is random, then every k-th unit follows (Kuwait, Unit 2, pp.49–50).

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** The Central Limit Theorem says the population itself becomes normal as the sample grows.
**Answer:** False — the population keeps its shape; the distribution of the sample MEAN closes on normal (Pakistan, Ch. 11, pp.36–51).

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** E(S²) = ((n − 1)/n)σ², so S² with denominator n underestimates σ² on average.
**Answer:** True — shown by complete enumeration (Pakistan, Ch. 11, pp.66–67).
