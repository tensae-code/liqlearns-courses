# Time Series — ordering

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — The Series and Its Four Components · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — The Trend by Freehand, Semi-Averages and Moving Averages · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Least-Squares Trend and Seasonal Indices.

Put the steps in the right order.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** One turn of the business cycle: (a) peak (b) trough (c) recession (d) expansion.
**Answer:** b, d, a, c — trough, expansion, peak, recession (Pakistan, pp.242–243).

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** Semi-averages: (a) split the series into two equal halves (b) join the two plotted points with a straight line (c) mean each half (d) plot each mean at its half's middle time.
**Answer:** a, c, d, b — halves, means, plotting, then the line (Pakistan, pp.247–250).

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** A centred 4-term moving average: (a) average neighbouring moving averages (b) take means of windows of four (c) line the results up with time points.
**Answer:** b, a, c — the even period falls between points until it is centred (Pakistan, pp.250–253).

**4. (Sub lesson 3)** Coded least squares: (a) code time so Σx = 0 (b) compute b = Σxy/Σx² (c) write ŷ = a + bx with a = ȳ (d) compute Σxy and Σx².
**Answer:** a, d, b, c — coding first shrinks every sum that follows (Pakistan, pp.253–261).

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Seasonal indices by simple averages: (a) average each season over the years (b) find the general average of the season averages (c) divide each season's average by the general average and multiply by 100 (d) check the indices average to 100.
**Answer:** a, b, c, d — Libya, Ch. 3, pp.59–88.
