Laos carries the closing ANOVA part, taught beside correlation in Grade 12 L16. South Africa (scatter plots only) and Iran (none of this ground) are recorded negatives and are cited nowhere as sources.

Syria reads correlation off scatter diagrams in Grade 7 — positive, negative and none — with the explicit warning that a diagram with no correlation cannot be used for prediction.

Malaysia's 'linear law' chapter is its doorway into regression: scatter recognised as linear or not, a line of best fit drawn by eye and checked, and non-linear laws straightened into lines whose gradient and intercept carry the constants. (The equation of the line of best fit itself is filed under coordinates-and-graphs; correlation coefficients are not in Malaysia's books.)

Rwanda teaches correlation and regression in Advanced Senior Five — bivariate data and covariance, both least-squares regression lines, Pearson's r with its bounds and properties, and Spearman's rank correlation, with regression fitted to real pulse-rate recovery data.


Mongolia enters through its ministry textbooks Математик IX, X (Grades 9, 10): scatter (dot) diagrams with trend lines, correlation named as positive, negative or zero, and predictions read off the trend line — height against arm span one way, sport hours against TV hours the other.

Venezuela enters through the Colección Bicentenario, the MPPE's one-book-per-grade course: *La Matemática y el Vivir Bien* (Grade 11). From its books: from Grade 11: point cloud (nube de puntos): its concentration, and direct (positive) vs inverse (negative) sense; a circular cloud means no linear relation, not no relation.

Afghanistan enters through its Ministry of Education course — the Grade 1 course in two language editions (Pashto and Dari, one work with identical page maps) and Dari student books for Grades 4, 6, 7, 8, 10, 11 and 12, all printing year 1398 HS except the Grade 8 book, which prints 1399 HS on its cover and title page and 1398 HS on its specifications page. From its books: from Grade 11: multivariate (bivariate) populations: paired data; scatter graph (scatter diagram) and reading association from it; correlation and the correlation coefficient r; the regression line by least squares: slope a = r·Sy/Sx and intercept b = ȳ − a·x̄; chapter key points and exercises.

Albania enters through its Mediaprint teacher's books (Libër Mësuesi, in Albanian) — TEACHER EVIDENCE only: no pupil's book is in hand for any grade, so Albania is never cited as a pupil-textbook source; what the teacher's books evidence is what is taught and in what order, through their lesson plans, worked answers and answer keys. From its books: from Grade 12 (teacher's book): Korrelacioni (Interpreting correlation; correlation does not necessarily imply causation); Regresi linear (Linear regression).

Palestine enters through the pupil's books of its Ministry of Education (Curriculum Centre, Ramallah), all in Arabic — Mathematics Grade 1 in two parts (both fourth edition, 2020), Grade 10 in two parts (Part One third edition, 2020; Part Two second edition, 2019 — the held parts are different editions and are cited as such), and the Grade 12 Technological-stream book (التكنولوجي, first edition, 2019 — the book's own name for its stream, never "Information Technology"). No ISBN is printed in any of the five books. From its books: from Grade 10 Part One: scatter diagrams; linear correlation — reading scatter; linear correlation — exercises; pearson coefficient — definition; pearson coefficient — computing; spearman coefficient — definition; spearman coefficient — computing; spearman coefficient — exercises; simple linear regression.

China enters through its Grade 7 Volume 2 book — in introductory form only: the water-saving project at the end of Chapter 10, a scatter plot with a hand-fitted trend line and a prediction.
