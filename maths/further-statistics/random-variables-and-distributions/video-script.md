Toss two coins. Count the heads. Zero, one, or two.

That count is a number tied to chance. It is called a random variable.

Now list its chances. Zero heads: one quarter. One head: one half. Two heads: one quarter. Add them: one whole. Always one whole. If your list does not add to one, it is wrong.

Each family of lists has a name. Many tries, two outcomes, same chance every try: binomial. Drawing from a small box without putting things back: hypergeometric. Counts of rare happenings: Poisson. Every value in a stretch equally likely: uniform. And the bell — the normal curve — where most values sit near the middle.

Find the middle of the bell, step one spread to each side, and about 68 of every 100 values live there. Two steps: about 95. Three steps: about 99.7. That is the empirical rule.

A number tied to chance, its list of chances, and the list's name. That is this lesson.
