# Random Variables and Distributions — short answer

Answer in a word or a short line.

**1. (Level 1)** Pakistan's Statistics Class 11, p.206: what do that example's seven probabilities actually sum to, and what follows?
**Answer:** 1.07 — so as printed it is not a valid distribution; teach it corrected, never as valid.

**2. (Level 1)** For a continuous variable, what is P(X = a) for one exact value a?
**Answer:** 0 — probability is area under the density curve, and one point has no area (Kuwait Grade 12 Statistics, Ex. 23).

**3. (Level 2)** X takes 1, 2, 3, 4 with probabilities 0.1, 0.6, 0.2, 0.1. Find E(X) and Var(X).
**Answer:** E(X) = 2.3; Var(X) = 0.61 — Kuwait Grade 12 Statistics, Ex. 10 values.

**4. (Level 2)** Using the corrected discrete forms for X with F(3) = 0.5, F(4) = 0.8, P(X = 3) = 0.5, P(X = 4) = 0.3: find P(3 ≤ X ≤ 4).
**Answer:** 0.8 — F(4) − F(3) + P(X = 3) = 0.8 − 0.5 + 0.5; Kuwait's p.169 summary form, taught corrected.

**5. (Level 3)** A binomial variable has n = 200, p = 0.01 (Kuwait's car-factory shape). Find its mean and variance.
**Answer:** μ = np = 2; σ² = npq = 1.98.

**6. (Level 3)** Pakistan's p.245 example, taught corrected: what is P(X = 5), and how many tomato cans does the problem have?
**Answer:** P(X = 5) = 0.003968 (= 1/252); 5 tomato cans — not the printed 0.005968 and not 7.

**7. (Level 3)** A variable is uniform on [1, 5]. Find its mean and P(X > 3).
**Answer:** Mean (1 + 5)/2 = 3; P(X > 3) = 1/2 — Kuwait Grade 12 Statistics, Ex. 23 and the uniform formulas at pp.38–50.

**8. (Level 3)** By the empirical rule, about what share of a normal distribution lies within two standard deviations of the mean?
**Answer:** About 95% — the 68–95–99.7 rule, Kuwait Grade 11 Statistics, pp.27–32 (Pakistan's exact normal figure is 95.45%).
