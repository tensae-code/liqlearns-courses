# Hands-on — Odds

The lesson converted chances on the book's own die and pens. Now hold them in your hands.

## The pens box, for real
Put 7 red, 3 green and 4 blue pens (or counters) in a box, exactly Kuwait's box. Without looking, draw one pen 28 times, putting it back and shaking each time, and tally the colours. Blue should land near 28 × 2/7 = 8 times. Then state blue's odds from the box itself — 4 for, 10 against, simplified 2:5 — and say why the tally and the odds need not match exactly after only 28 draws.

## Die odds
Roll a die 30 times and tally the 2s. The odds of a 2 are 1:5 — one helpful face against five unhelpful. Compare your tally with the 5 successes the odds lead you to expect in 30 rolls, in one honest sentence.

## Build a fair game
With one die, Kuwait's book calls prime (2, 3, 5) against even (2, 4, 6) a fair game — three faces each, once the shared face 2 is seen to sit in both camps by the book's own counting. Now invent your own two camps of die faces with equal counts, state both sides' odds against each other, and play 20 rounds to watch the fairness hold roughly.
