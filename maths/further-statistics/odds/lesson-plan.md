# Lesson plan — Odds

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 16 — Further Statistics and Probability · **Lesson:** Odds
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 131 of 136, the fifteenth lesson of Unit 16 — Further Statistics and Probability (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** odds — probability said as a comparison, favourable against unfavourable — and how to turn odds into probability and probability back into odds, inside the weighting-and-fairness lesson where Kuwait's book teaches them.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Probability (Unit 11) alone — favourable outcomes over all outcomes — and on nothing else. It is a small lesson because its source is one lesson of one book, and it stays small on purpose.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees

(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Stating Odds** — state the odds of a simple event as favourable : unfavourable, simplified, from the book's own die case. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Odds ↔ Probability** — convert odds a : b to the probability a/(a + b) and a probability back to odds, landing on the book's own pairs; the pens box is worked whole here — its odds-of-blue case is also Sub lesson 1's stating ground. (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **Odds and Fairness** — judge a two-sided game fair or unfair from the outcome counts, and defend the verdict in a whole sentence. (Was depth Level 3.)

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Probability (Unit 11) — odds are a probability wearing a comparison's clothes; the conversion needs the probability first. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define the odds of an event as favourable outcomes : unfavourable outcomes (Kuwait, Grade 9 Term 1, lesson 4-6, pp.204–210 — this lesson's sole source).
2. Turn odds into probability: odds a : b give probability a/(a + b) — the book's own pair, odds 2:3 ↔ probability 2/5 (Kuwait, 4-6).
3. Turn probability into odds: probability 5/13 gives odds 5:8 (Kuwait, 4-6 assessment ground).
4. Work the book's own cases: the odds of rolling a 2 on a die are 1:5; a box of 7 red, 3 green and 4 blue pens gives P(blue) = 2/7 and odds of blue 2:5; the odds of a multiple of 3 on a die are 1:2 (Kuwait, 4-6).
5. Judge a simple game fair or unfair by comparing the two sides' odds — the book's two-dice game (doubles 6 outcomes against 30 different) and its prime-versus-even game, which is fair (Kuwait, 4-6).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil.
A die and a small box of pens or counters in three colours.

## If the learner is stuck
- Writes odds as a fraction of the whole → odds compare the two *sides*, not the side against the total: 2:3 means 2 for and 3 against, and the total, 5, appears only when you convert to a probability.
- Simplifies the wrong way → odds simplify like a ratio: the pens give 4:10 for blue, and the book's own answer is the simplified 2:5.
- Calls the two-dice game fair because both sides can win → fairness is about the counts: doubles have 6 outcomes, different numbers have 30, so Omar's side holds the better chance (Kuwait, 4-6).
- Hunts for more odds material in other books → there is none in this library, as filed: this lesson is Kuwait's, honestly and only.
