# Hands-on — Moments, Skewness and Kurtosis

The lesson built shape out of powers of distances. Now feel the powers with small numbers.

## The free check
Take any five numbers you like. Find their mean, write the five signed deviations, and add them. The total must be zero — m₁ = 0 in your hands. If it is not zero, hunt the slip: this check costs nothing and catches everything early.

## Cubes keep the sign
Use the set 1, 2, 3, 4, 10 (mean 4). The deviations are −3, −2, −1, 0, 6. Cube them: −27, −8, −1, 0, 216 — total 180, so m₃ = 36, positive. One far value on the right has shouted louder than three small values on the left. Say in one sentence why cubes hear a lean that squares cannot.

## Bowley on the box
Kuwait's bank-deposits set (27, 28, 30, 32, 34, 38, 41, 42, 43, 44, 46, 53, 56, 60) has Q1 = 32, median 41.5, Q3 = 46. Draw its box on paper, mark where the median sits inside the box, then compute Bowley's coefficient, (46 + 32 − 2 × 41.5)/(46 − 32) ≈ −0.36. The picture and the number must tell the same story — a left (negative) skew.

## Three bells
Fold three paper strips into three humps: one flat-topped, one ordinary, one sharp-peaked with heavy tails. Label them platykurtic (β₂ < 3), mesokurtic (β₂ = 3), leptokurtic (β₂ > 3). Swap with a partner and have them re-label yours from the shapes alone.
