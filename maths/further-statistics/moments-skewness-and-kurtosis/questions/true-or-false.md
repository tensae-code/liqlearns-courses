# Moments, Skewness and Kurtosis — true or false

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Moments About the Mean, an Origin and Zero · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Skewness by Eye, by Pearson and by Bowley · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Moment Skewness and Kurtosis.

Say true or false. If it is false, say why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Moments can be taken about the mean, about an arbitrary origin, or about zero.
**Answer:** True — Pakistan's Ch. 4 treats all three families, with conversions (pp.104–120).

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** If your m₁ about the mean is not zero, your mean or a deviation is wrong.
**Answer:** True — m₁ = 0 is an identity, so it works as a free check.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** In a negatively skewed distribution the mean is the largest of mean, median and mode.
**Answer:** False — negative (left) skew drags the mean to the left; the mode stays largest (Kuwait, lesson 4-2; the book's chains state the standard order).

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** Cambodia's −0.05 skewness value comes from its Grade 11 Basic student book's worked examples.
**Answer:** False — it is the teacher guide's answer (guide p.236): teacher evidence only.

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** β₂ = 3 describes a sharply peaked distribution.
**Answer:** False — β₂ = 3 is mesokurtic, the middle bell-like case; sharper is leptokurtic, β₂ > 3.

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** Pakistan's book uses Q1 = 40.15 consistently through Example 4.7.
**Answer:** False — it computes Q1 = 40.51 and then transposes the digits to 40.15 in the working; 40.51 is correct and is taught (p.91, corrected).
