# Moments, Skewness and Kurtosis — short answer

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Moments About the Mean, an Origin and Zero · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Skewness by Eye, by Pearson and by Bowley · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Moment Skewness and Kurtosis.

Answer in a word or a short line.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** What is the r-th moment about zero the average of?
**Answer:** xʳ — the raw values raised to the power r (Pakistan, Ch. 4, pp.104–120).

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** Why compute a moment about an arbitrary origin at all?
**Answer:** As a working device when the mean is untidy — compute cheaply about a handy origin, then convert back to the mean (Pakistan, Ch. 4).

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** State Pearson's two skewness coefficients.
**Answer:** First: (Mean − Mode)/S. Second: 3(Mean − Median)/S (Pakistan, Ch. 4).

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** In a symmetric distribution, how do mode, median and mean stand (Kuwait, lesson 4-2)?
**Answer:** All three coincide at one point (Kuwait, p.19).

**5. (Sub lesson 2)** Kuwait's deposits box plot has its median closer to Q3 than to Q1. What skew does the book call that?
**Answer:** A skew to the left — negative skewness (Kuwait, p.21).

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** Write β₂ in terms of the moments, and give the mesokurtic value.
**Answer:** β₂ = m₄/m₂²; mesokurtic is β₂ = 3 (Pakistan, Ch. 4).

**7. (Sub lesson 3)** Formula (4.22) in Pakistan's book prints the coefficient of quartile deviation with which wrong denominator, and what should it be?
**Answer:** It prints (Q3 − Q1); the denominator should be (Q3 + Q1), as the book itself uses on pp.89–91 (p.102, corrected).
