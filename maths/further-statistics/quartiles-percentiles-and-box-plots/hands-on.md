# Hands-on — Quartiles, Percentiles and Box Plots

## The class in a line
Measure the height of everyone in the room, or collect a set of test marks. Write them in order, smallest first. Find the median, then Q1 as the median of the lower half and Q3 as the median of the upper half — the method of Kuwait's Grade 11 Statistics book (lesson 4-1, pp.12–18) and Jordan's Grade 8 book (p.144). Write the five-number summary and draw the box-and-whisker plot on a number line.

## Kuwait's phone cards
Work Kuwait's own worked example by hand (Grade 11 Statistics, lesson 4-1, p.12): phone cards sold in a week — values 200, 300, 350, 400, 500 with frequencies 2, 2, 3, 2, 2 (n = 11). Check the book's answers yourself: median 350 (rank 6), Q1 = 300, Q3 = 400, and draw the box plot from 200 to 500.

## Build an ogive and read it back
Take grouped data — marks in classes 0–10, 10–20, … with frequencies you invent or collect. Build the cumulative frequency table, plot the ogive against the upper class boundaries, then read off the median (at cf = n/2), Q1 (at n/4) and Q3 (at 3n/4), the reading method of Iraq's Grade 10 Literary book (Ch. 5, pp.78–82) and South Sudan's S2 book (pp.76–82). Check the median against the interpolation formula.

## Two groups, two boxes
Split your height or marks data into two groups — two classes, two teams, two weeks — and draw both box plots on the *same* scale, one above the other, as Kuwait's Grade 10 book sets Europe against the Arab countries (pp.174–175). Write three sentences: which group has the higher middle, which is more spread out (compare the IQRs, box length first), and which has the longer whiskers.
