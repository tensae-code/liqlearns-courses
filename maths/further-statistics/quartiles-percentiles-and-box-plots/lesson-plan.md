# Lesson plan — Quartiles, Percentiles and Box Plots

**Course:** Maths · **Stage:** Stage 16 — Further Statistics and Probability · **Lesson:** Quartiles, Percentiles and Box Plots
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 126 of 136, the tenth lesson of Stage 16 — Further Statistics and Probability (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** describing a set of values by where its quarters, tenths and hundredths fall — quartiles, deciles and percentiles, in raw and grouped data — summed up in the five-number summary, drawn as a box-and-whisker plot, and read back off a cumulative frequency curve.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Averages and Spread (Stage 11), where the median, quartiles and cumulative frequency curves first appear — this lesson takes that ground in depth: deciles and percentiles, grouped interpolation, and reading percentiles from the ogive. It needs nothing else.

1. 2. State and draw the five-number summary — smallest, Q1, median, Q3, largest — as a box-and-whisker plot (Kuwait's Grade 9 book, lesson 4-5, pp.198–203 — its data 75, 76, 77, 80, 82, 84, 85 gives 75, 76, 80, 84, 85; Kuwait's Grade 10 book, lesson 10-2, pp.170–175).

4. Find quartiles in grouped data by interpolation (Kuwait, Grade 11 Statistics, lesson 4-1, pp.12–18 — its building-ages example gives median 28 by interpolation; its phone-card example gives median 350, Q1 = 300, Q3 = 400).
5. Define and find deciles D₁–D₉ and percentiles P₁–P₉₉ for individual and discrete data (Nepal's Grade 9 Optional book, §§5.1.1–5.1.4, pp.228–266; Cambodia's Grade 11 Basic book, Ch. 7 Lesson 1, pp.220–227).
6. Use Cambodia's grouped decile and quartile answers as teacher evidence — the answers are the teacher guide's (guide printed p.222: Q1 = 53.31 for the 460-investor table; second table Q1 = 42, Q3 = 59.23, L50 = 51.54) — worded as teacher evidence wherever used.
7. Use Ethiopia's Grade 11 guide values as teacher evidence, at their corrected values: D3 = 20.15 (not the printed 21.5), Q1 = 34 (not the printed 33), and the cumulative frequency 92 (not the printed "9") — Ethiopia Grade 11 teacher guide, Unit 7, printed pp.227–230, corrected as filed.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil, squared paper for the ogive and the box plots.
A ruler.
A set of real measurements to order — heights, marks, times, prices.

## If the learner is stuck
- Puts Q1 at a quarter of the *values' size* → quartiles split the *count* of ordered values, not the size of the numbers: order first, then split the line of values into four equal-count parts (Kuwait Grade 11 Statistics, p.12).
- Forgets to order the data → no position measure means anything unordered; every book's method starts by arranging the values (Jordan, Grade 8, p.144).
- Reads the whiskers as the quartiles → the box holds the middle half (Q1 to Q3); the whiskers run to the smallest and largest values. The five numbers, in order, settle it (Kuwait, Grade 9, pp.198–203).
- Interpolates grouped data with the raw-rank method → inside a class, spread the class's frequency evenly across the class width: value = lower limit + ((rank − cf before) ÷ class frequency) × class width (Kuwait Grade 11 Statistics, pp.15–18).
- Copies Ethiopia's guide D3 = 21.5 or Q1 = 33 → those are the guide's own arithmetic slips, taught corrected here: D3 = 20.15, Q1 = 34, and its table's cumulative frequency is 92, not "9" (guide evidence, corrected as filed).
- Cites Cambodia's decile answers as the student book's → they are the teacher guide's answers (guide printed p.222) — teacher evidence, worded as such.
