# Quartiles, Percentiles and Box Plots — the lesson

Course: Maths · Stage: Stage 16 — Further Statistics and Probability · Lesson: Quartiles, Percentiles and Box Plots
Built from: Kuwait, Grade 9 Term 1, Grade 10 Term 2 and Grade 11 Statistics Term 2 — Cambodia, Grade 11 Basic student book and teacher guide (teacher evidence) — Ethiopia, Grade 11 teacher guide (teacher evidence) — Jordan, Grade 8 Term 2 — Kazakhstan, NIS Grade 8 — Nepal, Grade 9 and Grade 10 Optional — Iraq, Grade 10 Literary — South Sudan, S2 — Sierra Leone, SSS3

## Do it first
Write these seven values in order: 75, 76, 77, 80, 82, 84, 85. Find the middle one — 80, with three values below it and three above. Now find the middle of the bottom three (76) and the middle of the top three (84). You have just split the data into four equal parts with three cuts. Those cuts have names, and the five landmark numbers you used — 75, 76, 80, 84, 85 — are a famous summary. This lesson names them, draws them, and then cuts finer: into tenths and hundredths.

## Then the explanation
**Quartiles in raw data.** Order the values. The **median** splits them in two; the **lower quartile Q1** is the median of the lower half, and the **upper quartile Q3** is the median of the upper half — exactly the definitions of Kuwait's Grade 11 Statistics book, which works them first on a frequency table of 37 workers' salaries (lesson 4-1, p.12). Jordan's Grade 8 book teaches the same lesson with the **interquartile range** IQR = Q3 − Q1 and **outliers** beside it (Unit 9, L1, p.144). The distance Q3 − Q1 holds the middle half of the data, and half of it has its own name: the **semi-interquartile range** = (Q3 − Q1)/2 (Kuwait Grade 11 Statistics, lesson 4-3, p.23). Work that lesson's own set: 2, 4, 5, 6, 6, 7, 7, 7, 8, 8 — median 6.5, Q1 = 5, Q3 = 7, so the IQR is 2 and the semi-interquartile range is **1**; the same page gives range 6 and mean 6 for the full picture.

**The five-number summary and the box plot.** Five numbers describe a set's whole shape: **smallest value, Q1, median, Q3, largest value**. Drawn on a number line — a box from Q1 to Q3, a line inside it at the median, and whiskers from the box out to the two ends — they make the **box-and-whisker plot**. Kuwait's Grade 9 book teaches it as its own lesson (4-5, pp.198–203) on the data you met at the start: 75, 76, 77, 80, 82, 84, 85 → five-number summary **75, 76, 80, 84, 85**. Its second worked set, advertisement prices, gives range 24, median 9.5, Q1 = 6, Q3 = 15. Kuwait's Grade 10 book returns to quartiles as a named lesson (10-2, pp.170–175): one worked summary is (14, 19.5, 24.5, 36.5, 51), and its closing example draws **two box plots to compare** — Europe (min 310, Q1 400, median 480, Q3 575, max 750) against the Arab countries (min 190, Q1 510, median 780, Q3 975, max 1190) (pp.174–175). Read comparisons off three things: where the median line sits (the middle), how long the box is (the IQR, the middle half's spread), and how far the whiskers reach. Kazakhstan's NIS Grade 8 book adds the box-and-whisker plot in its statistics chapter with Q1 and Q3 computed from a table (Ch. 7, §7.3, pp.104–132), and Nepal's Optional books draw box-and-whisker plots for discrete data in Grade 9 (Lesson 5, p.260) and for continuous data in Grade 10 (§11.6, p.268). Jordan's Grade 8 final test works with the plot, so it is examined ground there (pp.172–173).

**Quartiles in grouped data — interpolation.** When data come in classes, positions fall *inside* a class, and the books spread that class's frequency evenly across its width: **value = lower limit of the class + ((rank − cumulative frequency before the class) ÷ frequency of the class) × class width** — with rank n/2 for the median, n/4 for Q1 and 3n/4 for Q3 (Kuwait Grade 11 Statistics, lesson 4-1, pp.15–18). Its worked ages example: classes 0–10, …, 50–60 with frequencies 3, 3, 5, 2, 5, 2 (cumulative 3, 6, 11, 13, 18, 20); the median class is 20–30, and the median = 20 + ((10 − 6)/5) × 10 = **28**. Its phone-card table (values 200, 300, 350, 400, 500, frequencies 2, 2, 3, 2, 2; n = 11) gives median 350, Q1 = 300, Q3 = 400 and the box plot from 200 to 500 (p.12).

**Deciles and percentiles.** Cut finer. **Deciles** D₁, …, D₉ split ordered data into ten equal parts; **percentiles** P₁, …, P₉₉ split it into a hundred. P₅₀ is the median, P₂₅ is Q1, P₇₅ is Q3; D₅ is the median too. Nepal's Grade 9 Optional book teaches quartiles and percentiles of individual and discrete data as named sections (§§5.1.1–5.1.4, Lesson 5, pp.228–266). Cambodia's Grade 11 Basic book teaches deciles and percentiles inside its measures-of-position lesson, with position counted in terms — its quartile example takes Q3 as the 12.75th term of 16 values (Ch. 7, Lesson 1, pp.220–227). For grouped data the same interpolation shape serves every cut: for Dₖ use rank kn/10, for Pₖ use rank kn/100. On Cambodia, be exact about evidence: the student book teaches the methods, but the *answers* to its grouped quartile/decile exercises — for the 460-investor table (classes 30–40 to 80–90, cumulative frequencies 17, 72, 202, 342, 435, 460) the guide gives **Q1 = 53.31**, and for a second table **Q1 = 42, Q3 = 59.23, L50 = 51.54** — are **teacher-guide answers, cited as teacher evidence** (Cambodia Grade 11 Basic teacher guide, printed p.222), never as the student book's own printed answers.

**Ethiopia's guide — teacher evidence, corrected.** Ethiopia's Grade 11 *teacher guide* works deciles and percentiles of grouped data in its Unit 7 solutions (printed pp.227–236) — teacher evidence only, as filed, and three of its values are taught **corrected**, never as printed. For time spent on the internet (n = 41): D5 = 24.25 and D7 = 27.79 are correct, but the guide's printed D3 = 21.5 is an arithmetic slip — it drops the division by the class frequency; the correct value is **D3 = 20.15** (20 + ((12.3 − 12)/10) × 5) (p.228, corrected). For quartiles (n = 72): Q2 = 37.13 and Q3 = 42 are correct, but the printed Q1 = 33 is wrong by its own working line, which gives **Q1 = 34** (p.227, corrected). And in its Exercise 7.15 table the cumulative frequency for the class 81–90 is printed "9"; it should be **92** (68 + 24), as the final class's correct 100 confirms (p.230, corrected). Its correct percentile values stand as guide evidence too: P30 = 26.37 kg and P70 = 44.54 kg for children's weights, and P25 = 34.33, P61 = 74 for scores (n = 100).

**The cumulative frequency curve, and reading percentiles from it.** Plot cumulative frequency against the upper class boundaries and join the points in a rising curve — the **ogive**, or cumulative frequency curve. Iraq's Grade 10 Literary book builds it step by step: cumulative frequency tables, ascending and descending, then ogive curves (Ch. 5, pp.78–82). To read a position measure off the curve, run across from the needed cumulative frequency — n/2 for the median, n/4 and 3n/4 for the quartiles, kn/100 for the k-th percentile — meet the curve, and drop down to the value axis. South Sudan's S2 book teaches the full reading set from the ogive: medians, quartiles and percentiles (Statistics, pp.76–82). Sierra Leone's SSS3 book reviews the curve for exactly this: constructing a cumulative frequency curve and estimating quartiles and the interquartile range from it (L109, p.39). Kazakhstan's NIS Grade 8 chapter draws cumulative-frequency curves beside its box plots (Ch. 7, §§7.4–7.5). Nepal's school books draw the ogive too — its Grade 9 book's statistics unit works cumulative frequency tables and the curve among its measures (as filed). The curve and the formula are two routes to the same numbers: the ogive is the interpolation drawn.

> **Tip — from us, not from the books:** a percentile is a standing, not a score. "P70 = 44.54 kg" does not say a child weighs 44.54 kg is *good* or *bad* — it says 70 of every 100 children weigh less. Read every percentile aloud as a standing ("higher than … of every 100") and you will never confuse it with a percentage score.

## Say it to your companion
Hand your companion a box plot — use Kuwait's Europe plot: 310, 400, 480, 575, 750. They must read it back as five whole sentences: "The smallest value is 310. A quarter of the values lie below 400…" and so on. Swap with the Arab-countries plot and compare in sentences. Then the ogive round: your companion names a percentile — "the 30th" — and you say where you would run across the curve (at cf = 30n/100) and what dropping down gives you. Finish with Ethiopia's corrected values from memory: "D3 is 20.15, not 21.5; Q1 is 34, not 33; the cumulative frequency is 92, not 9 — teacher-guide values, taught corrected."

## Do It Yourself
1. For 75, 76, 77, 80, 82, 84, 85 (Kuwait Grade 9's data, pp.198–203): write the five-number summary and describe the box plot in words. (Answer: 75, 76, 80, 84, 85; box from 76 to 84, line at 80, whiskers to 75 and 85.)
2. For 2, 4, 5, 6, 6, 7, 7, 7, 8, 8 (Kuwait Grade 11 Statistics, p.23): find Q1, Q3, the IQR and the semi-interquartile range. (Answer: Q1 = 5, Q3 = 7, IQR = 2, semi-interquartile range = 1.)
3. Kuwait's ages table (classes 0–10 to 50–60; frequencies 3, 3, 5, 2, 5, 2): use interpolation to find the median. (Answer: median class 20–30; 20 + ((10 − 6)/5) × 10 = 28.)
4. State, as teacher evidence and corrected: Ethiopia's guide values for D3 (internet time, n = 41) and Q1 (n = 72), and what the guide printed for each. (Answer: D3 = 20.15, printed 21.5; Q1 = 34, printed 33 — guide, Unit 7, pp.227–228, corrected.)
