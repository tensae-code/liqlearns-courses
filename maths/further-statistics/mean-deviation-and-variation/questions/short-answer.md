# Mean Deviation and Variation — short answer

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Range, Mean Deviation and Quartile Deviation · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Variance, Standard Deviation and the Two Conventions · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — The Coefficient of Variation and Judging Consistency.

Answer in a word or a short line.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Bangladesh's Class 9 book often calls the mean deviation by another name. What name?
**Answer:** The "mean interval" (Ch. 9, pp.217–222) — this lesson uses the standard name, mean deviation.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** Why must the signs be dropped (or the deviations squared) before spread is averaged around the mean?
**Answer:** Because the signed deviations from the mean always add to zero — Bangladesh's family-ages example (5, 12, 36, 40, 67, mean 32) is built on this (pp.217–222).

**3. (Sub lesson 1)** Write the coefficient of quartile deviation for Q1 = 6, Q3 = 14.
**Answer:** (14 − 6)/(14 + 6) = 8/20 = 0.4 (Pakistan, Ch. 4).

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** Batsman B's variance is given by Bangladesh's book. What is it, and what does its small size say?
**Answer:** 17.4 (p.224) — a small variance: a steady player's scores stay close to his mean.

**5. (Sub lesson 2)** In Bangladesh's family-ages comparison, whose dispersion is larger — Aleya's or Thomas's? Give both variances.
**Answer:** Aleya's — 1750/6 ≈ 291.67 against Thomas's 2480/31 = 80; the English p.225 sentence saying the opposite is an error, taught corrected.

**6. (Sub lesson 2)** Laos's grouped rainfall example ends with s² = 147.37. Which denominator produced it?
**Answer:** n − 1 — the sample convention of Laos's Grade 9 book (L44, p.239).

**7. (Sub lesson 3)** For a symmetric distribution, Pakistan's chapter relates mean deviation to σ. How?
**Answer:** M.D. = (4/5)σ (Pakistan, Ch. 4).
