# Lesson plan — Mean Deviation and Variation

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 16 — Further Statistics and Probability · **Lesson:** Mean Deviation and Variation
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 127 of 136, the eleventh lesson of Unit 16 — Further Statistics and Probability (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** measuring spread around the centre — mean deviation, quartile deviation, variance and standard deviation — and comparing spreads fairly with the coefficient of variation, with the two variance conventions (divide by n, divide by n − 1) shown side by side, each with its country's name on it.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Averages and Spread (Unit 11) and on Quartiles, Percentiles and Box Plots (Level 126, same unit) — the learner can already find a mean, a median and quartiles. It needs nothing else.

1. **Range, Mean Deviation and Quartile Deviation** — say why the centre is not enough, and find a range, a mean deviation from the mean, and a quartile deviation with its coefficient. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Variance, Standard Deviation and the Two Conventions** — find variance and standard deviation by the direct method, and say which denominator — n or n − 1 — a calculation used and which country's book uses it. (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **The Coefficient of Variation and Judging Consistency** — compute a coefficient of variation, compare two sets with it, and defend the verdict — including when the raw standard deviations point the other way. (Was depth Level 3.)

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say why the same mean can hide very different spreads, the way Bangladesh's Class 9 book opens the topic: two batsmen both average 53, yet their scores spread very differently (Bangladesh, Class 9, Ch. 9, pp.212–214).
2. Find the range, ungrouped and grouped (Bangladesh, Class 9, Ch. 9, p.215).
3. Find the mean deviation from the mean and from the median — Bangladesh's book calls it the "mean interval" in places; this lesson uses the standard name (Bangladesh, Class 9, Ch. 9, pp.217–222).
4. Find the quartile deviation (Q3 − Q1)/2 and its coefficient (Q3 − Q1)/(Q3 + Q1) (Pakistan, Statistics Class 11, Ch. 4, pp.86–102). Bangladesh names quartile deviation on p.211 but never teaches it, so it is never cited for it here.
5. Find variance and standard deviation, and date Karl Pearson's standard deviation correctly to 1893 — Bangladesh's p.223 prints 1983, which is wrong and is taught corrected (Bangladesh, Class 9, Ch. 9, pp.223–232, p.223 corrected).
6. Use BOTH variance conventions honestly: Pakistan's Statistics Class 11 book divides by n for population and sample alike (Ch. 4), while Laos's Grade 9 book divides a sample by n − 1 (L44, p.239, rainfall s² = 147.37) — and say which convention a number used.
7. Compare two sets fairly with the coefficient of variation, CV = (σ/x̄) × 100% (Pakistan, Statistics Class 11, Ch. 4).

## If the learner is stuck
- Adds signed deviations and gets zero → that always happens around the mean; mean deviation uses the distances, ignoring the sign. Bangladesh's own family-ages example (5, 12, 36, 40, 67, mean 32) is built on exactly this point (pp.217–222).
- Divides a sample by the wrong number → ask first: "Whose rule am I following?" Pakistan's book divides by n both times; Laos's book divides a sample by n − 1. Neither is a mistake — they are two countries' conventions, and the lesson keeps both names attached.
- Compares standard deviations of sets with very different means → that is what the coefficient of variation is for: spread as a percentage of the mean.
- Repeats the book's 1983 → Pearson's year is 1893, taught corrected; the printed 1983 is a book error.
- This lesson's next door: Moments, Skewness and Kurtosis (Level 128, same unit) — spread measured to higher powers.
