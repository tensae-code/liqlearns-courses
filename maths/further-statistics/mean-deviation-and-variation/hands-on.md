# Hands-on — Mean Deviation and Variation

The lesson measured spread on the books' own data shapes. Now measure your own.

## Two rows, one mean
Write two lists of five numbers, both with mean 10 — one list tight (9, 10, 10, 10, 11), one list wild (0, 5, 10, 15, 20). Find the mean deviation of each list. The wild list's mean deviation must come out bigger; if it does not, find the slip before you move on.

## The absent-students check
Bangladesh's book works the absent students 3, 6, 6, 7, 8, 11, 15, 16 (mean 9, mean deviation 3.75). Redo it with counters or marks on paper: for each day, pace out its distance from 9, write the distance, then average the eight distances. Your hands should land on 3.75.

## Both doors
Take the rainfall-style job Laos's book sets: a small sample of five values — 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 (mean 8). Find the variance twice: once dividing by n, Pakistan's way (8), and once dividing by n − 1, Laos's sample way (10). Write both answers with their country's name beside them. Same data, two honest answers — the name is part of the answer.

## Fair comparison
Measure two real sets at home — for example, the lengths of five spoons and five pencils. Find each set's mean, standard deviation and coefficient of variation. Say which set is more consistent, and say it with the coefficient, not the raw standard deviation.
