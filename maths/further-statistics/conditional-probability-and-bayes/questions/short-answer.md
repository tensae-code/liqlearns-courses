# Conditional Probability and Bayes' Theorem — short answer

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Reading "Given" · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Independence, Dependence and Trees · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Total Probability and Bayes.

Answer in a word or a short line.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Write the formula for P(A|B) using P(A∩B) and P(B).
**Answer:** P(A|B) = P(A∩B) / P(B) — Nicaragua's Grade 11 formula box, p.142.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** P(A∩B) = 0.2 and P(B) = 0.5. Find P(A|B).
**Answer:** 0.4 — 0.2 / 0.5.

**3. (Sub lesson 1)** In Kazakhstan's Shynybekov Algebra 9 (§5.1.5, p.187), how is the conditional probability of A given B written?
**Answer:** P_B(A) — the given event is written as a subscript on P.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** A bag has 5 red and 5 blue balls. One red is drawn and not replaced. What is the chance the next draw is red?
**Answer:** 4/9 — 4 reds remain out of 9 balls, the Sierra Leone L106 changing-probability shape.

**5. (Sub lesson 2)** P(A) = 0.5, P(B) = 0.4 and P(A∩B) = 0.2. Are A and B independent? Say how you know.
**Answer:** Yes — P(A)·P(B) = 0.5 × 0.4 = 0.2 = P(A∩B).

**6. (Sub lesson 2)** Kuwait's Grade 10 book (Ex (9), p.200) draws two red balls without replacement. What probability does it get?
**Answer:** 5/14.

**7. (Sub lesson 3)** Write the total-probability formula for B arriving by two roads, A and not-A.
**Answer:** P(B) = P(A)·P(B|A) + P(not A)·P(B|not A) — each road weighted by its share, as in Cambodia's PRIVATE Probability booklet, pp.85–94.

**8. (Sub lesson 3)** P(A) = 0.01, P(B|A) = 0.9, P(B|not A) = 0.05. Find P(A|B) by Bayes' theorem.
**Answer:** P(B) = 0.01×0.9 + 0.99×0.05 = 0.0585, so P(A|B) = 0.009 / 0.0585 ≈ 0.154 — the given evidence is mostly produced by the common road.
