# Conditional Probability and Bayes' Theorem — multiple choice

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Reading "Given" · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Independence, Dependence and Trees · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Total Probability and Bayes.

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** P(A|B) is read as:
a) the probability of A divided by B  b) the probability of A given B  c) the probability of A or B
**Answer:** b) the probability of A given B — the letter after the bar is the event you are given.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** If P(A∩B) = 0.12 and P(B) = 0.4, then P(A|B) =
a) 0.3  b) 0.48  c) 0.052
**Answer:** a) 0.3 — P(A|B) = P(A∩B) / P(B) = 0.12 / 0.4.

**3. (Sub lesson 1)** Kuwait's Grade 10 book (p.202) works a pair of conditional probabilities. They are:
a) equal  b) P(B|A) = 2/3 and P(A|B) = 1/3  c) both 1/2
**Answer:** b) P(B|A) = 2/3 and P(A|B) = 1/3 — the two directions are different questions.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** Events A and B are independent exactly when:
a) P(A∩B) = P(A) + P(B)  b) P(A∩B) = P(A)·P(B)  c) P(A∩B) = 0
**Answer:** b) P(A∩B) = P(A)·P(B) — one event does not change the other's chance.

**5. (Sub lesson 2)** A bag has 4 red and 6 blue counters. Two are drawn without replacement. The chance both are red is:
a) 4/10 × 4/10  b) 4/10 × 3/9  c) 4/10 + 3/9
**Answer:** b) 4/10 × 3/9 — the bag changed: after one red is taken, 3 reds remain out of 9.

**6. (Sub lesson 2)** On a tree diagram, the probability at the end of one path is found by:
a) adding along the branch  b) multiplying along the branch  c) taking the largest branch fraction
**Answer:** b) multiplying along the branch — each step is conditional on the steps before it.

**7. (Sub lesson 3)** Bayes' theorem turns P(B|A) into:
a) P(A|B)  b) P(A∪B)  c) P(A∩B)
**Answer:** a) P(A|B) — Bayes swaps which event is given and which is asked for.

**8. (Sub lesson 3)** In Kuwait's quality-test example (pp.204–205), an item that was not cancelled by the test is defective with probability about:
a) 0.41  b) 0.041  c) 0.00041
**Answer:** c) 0.00041 — the test catches almost every defective item, so very few survive uncancelled.
