# Estimation and Confidence Intervals — short answer

Answer in a word or a short line.

**1. (Level 1)** Distinguish an estimator from an estimate in one line.
**Answer:** The estimator is the rule (the statistic); the estimate is the number it gives on your sample (Pakistan, Ch. 12, p.73).

**2. (Level 1)** Why does Ŝ² divide by n − 1 while S² divides by n?
**Answer:** Dividing by n biases the variance low — E(S²) = ((n − 1)/n)σ² — and n − 1 corrects it to unbiasedness (Pakistan, Ch. 12, pp.74–76).

**3. (Level 1)** A sample gives x̄ = 50 while μ = 47. What is the point estimation error?
**Answer:** |x̄ − μ| = 3 — the one realised miss; it is NOT the standard error (Kuwait, 4-1, p.171, taught corrected).

**4. (Level 2)** n = 49, x̄ = 30, σ = 7 known. Give the 95% interval for μ.
**Answer:** E = 1.96 × 7/7 = 1.96, so (28.04, 31.96) (Pakistan, Ch. 12, pp.82–90).

**5. (Level 2)** Kuwait's t case: n = 25, S = 10, x̄ = 15, t_{0.025} = 2.064. Give E and the interval.
**Answer:** E = 2.064 × 10/5 = 4.128; interval (10.872, 19.128) (Kuwait, 4-1, pp.168–176).

**6. (Level 3)** A sample of 400 has 236 successes. Give the point estimate of π and its standard error.
**Answer:** p = 0.59; SE = √(0.59 × 0.41/400) ≈ 0.0246 (Pakistan, Ch. 12, pp.92–95).

**7. (Level 3)** For the pooled-variance t interval for μ₁ − μ₂, what are the degrees of freedom?
**Answer:** ν = n₁ + n₂ − 2 (Pakistan, Ch. 12, pp.96–103).

**8. (Level 3)** Name the quantity that China's German tank reading estimates, and the data it uses.
**Answer:** The total production run N, estimated from the largest serial number seen and the count of parts, N ≈ x_max·(n + 1)/n (China, reading, pp.208–209).
