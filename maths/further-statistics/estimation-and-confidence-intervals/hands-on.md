# Hands-on — Estimation and Confidence Intervals

The lesson estimated from real samples, the way the books set it.

## Twenty intervals, one truth
A bag holds counters with a known mean your teacher keeps secret. In pairs, draw samples of 10, compute x̄, and build the 95% interval x̄ ± 1.96·s/√n (using s as the σ estimate, the large-sample way of Pakistan's Ch. 12, pp.82–90). Draw all twenty class intervals on one number line under the secret mean, revealed at the end. Count how many caught it — about nineteen should. That count IS the meaning of 95%.

## Ŝ² against S²
Take the tiny population 2, 4, 6, 8 (σ² = 5). Enumerate all samples of two WITH replacement, compute each sample's variance both ways — dividing by n and by n − 1 — and average each list. The n − 1 list averages to 5; the n list averages to 2.5. You have just proved by enumeration what Pakistan proves: Ŝ² is unbiased, S² is not (Ch. 12, pp.74–76).

## Pulse-rate rerun
Repeat Kuwait's worked study with your own class: rest a minute, count pulses for 15 seconds and multiply by 4. With the class σ estimate and n, build the 95% interval for the class mean the way Kuwait builds its n = 40, σ = 12.5, x̄ = 76.3 interval (72.4262, 80.1738) (4-1, pp.168–176). Compare widths: whose interval is narrower, and why?

## Capture the tanks
Number 60 slips 1–60 and hide them — the "production run". A partner draws 5 slips without looking and shows you only the five numbers. Estimate the total run by the German tank rule N ≈ x_max·(n + 1)/n from China's reading (Ch. 9, pp.208–209). Repeat ten times, average your estimates, and compare with a guess of "largest + a bit" made without the rule. Then reveal 60.
