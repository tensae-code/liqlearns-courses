# Geometric and Experimental Probability — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Level 1)** A point lands at random on a 20 cm segment; 5 cm of it is marked. The probability it lands on the marked part is ______ (Jordan, p.165).
a) 5  b) 1/4  c) 1/5
**Answer:** b) 1/4 — favourable length over whole length, 5/20.

**2. (Level 1)** A circle is inscribed in a square. A random dart hits the square. The probability it lands in the circle is about ______.
a) 0.5  b) 0.785  c) 1
**Answer:** b) 0.785 — the circle fills π/4 of the square (Kazakhstan's area-ratio form).

**3. (Level 1)** Geometric probability divides ______.
a) Favourable outcomes by all outcomes, always by counting  b) Favourable measure by whole measure  c) The whole by the part
**Answer:** b) Favourable measure by whole measure — length, area or volume (Abylkasymova, §32, p.113).

**4. (Level 2)** In the unit square, P(y < 2x) is ______ (Abylkasymova, §32).
a) 1/2  b) 1/4  c) 3/4
**Answer:** c) 3/4 — only the small triangle above the line, area 1/4, fails the condition.

**5. (Level 2)** Two random breaks in a stick form a triangle with probability ______ (Soltan, §33).
a) 1/2  b) 1/4  c) 1/3
**Answer:** b) 1/4 — the favourable region in the unit square measures a quarter of it (p.238).

**6. (Level 2)** In Shynybekov's meeting problem, the probability comes from ______ (§5.2).
a) Counting arrival times  b) The shaded region's area over the whole square's area  c) A frequency table
**Answer:** b) The shaded region's area over the whole square's area — the region is graphed from the waiting condition |x − y| (p.197).

**7. (Level 3)** Pearson's 24,000 coin tosses gave 12,012 heads (Shynybekov, §5.1.4). The relative frequency is ______.
a) 0.5005  b) 0.49  c) 0.512
**Answer:** a) 0.5005 — 12012/24000, the standing picture of a frequency settling on 1/2 (p.185).

**8. (Level 3)** A frequency of 0.48 from 240 girls in 500 newborns (Abylkasymova, §31) is best described as ______.
a) The exact probability of a girl  b) An estimate of the probability, from trials  c) A geometric probability
**Answer:** b) An estimate of the probability, from trials — statistical probability is an estimate that steadies as trials multiply (p.107).
