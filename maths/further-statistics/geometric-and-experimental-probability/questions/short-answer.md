# Geometric and Experimental Probability — short answer

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Probability from Length and Area · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Regions, Meetings and the Broken Stick · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Probability from Frequency.

Answer in a word or a short line.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Write Shynybekov's area form of geometric probability.
**Answer:** P = S_G/S — favourable area over whole area (§5.2, p.197).

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** Which three measures does Abylkasymova's §32 use for geometric probability?
**Answer:** Length, area and volume (p.113).

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** In the meeting problem, what two things are plotted on the square's axes?
**Answer:** The two workers' arrival times, x and y, within the hour (Shynybekov, §5.2, p.197).

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** Why can the broken-stick problem not be solved by counting outcomes?
**Answer:** The break points can fall anywhere on a continuum — there is no finite list to count, so the favourable region must be measured (Soltan, §33, p.238).

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Soltan's coin frequencies run 0.49, 0.505, 0.508 at 100, 200, 250 tosses. What is happening, and who is named for it?
**Answer:** The relative frequency is stabilising toward 1/2 as n grows; Jacob Bernoulli is named (Soltan, §32, p.234).

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** Nicaragua's book repeats its coin experiment how many times, and how often is the cumulative relative frequency written down (pp.45–48)?
**Answer:** 500 tosses, with the cumulative relative frequency taken every 100 tosses.

**7. (Sub lesson 3)** Which of Kazakhstan's four Grade 9 books has no statistical definition of probability?
**Answer:** Yelemessov's English Algebra 9 — geometric probability only, as filed.
