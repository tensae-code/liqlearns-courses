**Acceptance count: taught in 14 countries (Kazakhstan, Jordan, Nicaragua, Rwanda, India, Mongolia, Afghanistan, Albania, Armenia, China, South Africa, Vietnam, Iran, Kuwait).** Kazakhstan carries both halves across its four Grade 9 algebra books — geometric probability in all four (Yelemessov's dartboard, Shynybekov's meeting problem, Abylkasymova's three measures, Soltan's broken stick at 1/4) and the frequency definition in three of them, Yelemessov's book honestly recorded as having no statistical definition. Jordan carries geometric probability as a named closing lesson with examined segment items. Nicaragua carries the experimental half as a taught contrast — theoretical against frequentist — with its 500-toss coin experiment.

Vietnam carries experimental probability in Grade 5 (Bài 65: the ratio of an event's repetitions to the total number of trials) and Grade 6 (Bài 43). Stocked 2026-11-11 by the Vietnam re-verification sweep merge.

Kuwait carries geometric probability inside its Grade 7 Term 2 probability unit — probability computed from geometric models (lesson 8-5, printed p. 198). Stocked 2026-11-11 by the Kuwait re-verification sweep merge.

Rwanda stocks the experimental half of this lesson in Primary 5 — chance found from recorded coin and dice results in the pupil's book, with the teacher's guide contrasting experimental probability (6 heads in 10 tosses = 0.6) against the theoretical 1/2; Rwanda teaches no geometric probability in the held books.

India enters geometric and experimental probability from Mathematics Class 9: experimental probability.
