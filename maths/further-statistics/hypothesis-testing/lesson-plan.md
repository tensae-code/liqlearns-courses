# Lesson plan — Hypothesis Testing

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 16 — Further Statistics and Probability · **Lesson:** Hypothesis Testing
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 121 of 136, Unit 16 — Further Statistics and Probability (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** hypothesis testing — H₀ and H₁, Type I and Type II errors, test statistics, and the Z, t, paired-t and proportion tests, run as an ordered procedure.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Estimation and Confidence Intervals (Level 120, same unit) — the learner can already build an interval and name a standard error. It needs nothing else.

1. **Hypotheses, Tails and the Two Errors** — state H₀ and H₁ for a claim, tell one-tailed from two-tailed and simple from composite, and name the Type I and Type II errors with α and β. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **The Procedure and Tests of a Mean** — run the nine-step procedure, choose Z or t correctly, and decide from the test statistic and the critical region — including Kuwait's two-sided-only cases with their recorded limits. (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **Proportions, Differences and Paired Tests** — test a proportion, a difference of two means, a paired difference by the paired t, and a difference of two proportions; the closing part records who does NOT teach this. (Was depth Level 3.)

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State H₀ and H₁ for a claim, and tell simple from composite and one-tailed from two-tailed tests, the way Pakistan's Class 12 Statistics book builds the vocabulary (Ch. 13, pp.111–114).
2. Name the Type I and Type II errors, and the level of significance α, as Pakistan defines them (Ch. 13, pp.115–117) and Libya's Grade 12 Science book teaches them (Ch. 6, chapter start p.161).
3. Run a test as Pakistan's nine-step procedure — hypothesis, level, statistic, distribution, critical region, computation, decision (Ch. 13, pp.115–117).
4. Test a mean by Z (σ known), by t (σ unknown, small n) and by large-sample Z, as Pakistan works them (Ch. 13, pp.120–130).
5. Run Kuwait's two-sided-only tests exactly as that book allows — H₁: μ ≠ μ₀, Z or t by its table, decision by the acceptance region — including its salary case, Z = −2 against (−1.96, 1.96), reject H₀ (Grade 12 T1, 4-2, pp.177–181). Kuwait teaches NO p-values and NO χ² tests — recorded negatives, never supplied from elsewhere under Kuwait's name.
6. Test a proportion, a difference of two means, a paired difference (paired t) and a difference of two proportions with the proportion pooled under H₀, as Pakistan teaches them (Ch. 13, pp.135–174).
7. Say where testing sits in Jordan's inference unit (Grade 12 Business, Unit 6, L4 p.141) and in Laos's Grade 12 statistics chapter (Ch. 5, L15, p.156).
8. State the recorded negatives: Egypt, Cambodia, Greece and Iran do NOT teach hypothesis testing in their filings — and never cite any of them for a test.

Paper and a pencil. A calculator. The standard normal (z) table and the t table.

## If the learner is stuck
- Writes H₁ as the claim they hope for and H₀ as its opposite, then "proves" H₀ → H₀ is never proved; a test only rejects it or fails to reject it. Pakistan's steps end in a decision rule, not a proof (Ch. 13, pp.115–117).
- Mixes the two errors → Type I is rejecting a TRUE H₀ (its rate is α, chosen by you); Type II is keeping a FALSE H₀ (rate β). Anchor: the I is the false alarm.
- Reaches for a p-value or a χ² test "from Kuwait" → Kuwait's book has neither: two-sided tests only, decided by critical values and the acceptance region (4-2, pp.177–181). Those are recorded negatives, not gaps to fill under its name.
- Treats paired data as two independent samples → pair first: one sample of differences, one mean μ_D, one t test (Pakistan, Ch. 13, pp.158–165).
- This lesson's next door: Correlation and Regression (Level 122, same unit) — from deciding about a mean to describing how two variables move together.
