# Dynamics: motion, momentum and energy — true or false

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Rectilinear Motion and Free Fall · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Momentum and Newton's Laws, except impulse items, which are Sub lesson 4 — Impulse · (Level 3) splits item by item: motion-as-a-function-of-time and connected-bodies items are Sub lesson 3, impulse items are Sub lesson 4, and work, energy and power items are Sub lesson 5 — Work, Energy and Power. Items that match or order several of these quantities together are marked with the last sub lesson they cover.

Say true or false. If it is false, say why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** A body moving at a steady velocity along a straight line has acceleration zero.
**Answer:** True — acceleration is the rate of change of velocity, and the velocity is not changing (Egypt, Applied Grade 11 Term 2, lesson 1-1, printed p.15).

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** At the top of its rise, a body thrown straight up has velocity zero and acceleration zero.
**Answer:** False — its velocity is zero for an instant, but its acceleration is still g downwards (lesson 1-3, printed p.35).

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** A moving body needs a continuous resultant force to keep moving at a steady velocity.
**Answer:** False — Newton's first law: with no resultant force, a body keeps its velocity; force changes velocity, it does not maintain it (lesson 2-2, printed p.46).

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** Action and reaction act on the same body, so they cancel each other.
**Answer:** False — the pair act on different bodies — A on B, and B on A — so they never cancel on one body (lesson 2-4, printed p.61).

**5. (Sub lesson 5)** Doing the same work in half the time needs twice the power.
**Answer:** True — power is work ÷ time, so halving the time doubles the rate (Egypt, Applied Grade 12, lesson 3-3, printed p.150).

**6. (Sub lesson 5)** A force acting at right angles to a body's movement does work equal to the force times the distance moved.
**Answer:** False — work counts the force (or its component) along the movement; a force at right angles to the movement does no work (Grade 12, lesson 3-1, printed p.124).
