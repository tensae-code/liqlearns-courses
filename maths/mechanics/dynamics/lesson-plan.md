# Lesson plan — Dynamics: motion, momentum and energy

**Course:** Maths · **Stage:** Stage 17 — Mechanics · **Lesson:** Dynamics: motion, momentum and energy
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 133 of 136, the second lesson of Stage 17 — Mechanics (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** dynamics — rectilinear motion, free fall, momentum, Newton's laws, impulse, work, energy and power: what happens when forces do not balance and a body moves.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Statics (Level 132) for forces, and on the differentiation and integration lessons for the Grade 12 vector-function part — the learner can already name a force, and can meet velocity as a rate of change. It needs nothing else.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Statics (Level 132, same stage) — dynamics uses the same forces, unbalanced. Recommended, never forced.
- Recommended first: the differentiation and integration lessons — the Grade 12 part of this lesson differentiates and integrates position with respect to time. Recommended, never forced.
- Recommended first: Speed, distance and time (Level 116) — this lesson sharpens plain speed into velocity and acceleration along a straight line. Recommended, never forced.

1. Describe rectilinear motion by position, velocity and acceleration along a straight line, the way Egypt's Applied Grade 11 Term 2 book opens its dynamics (lesson 1-1, printed p.15).
2. Use the constant-acceleration equations for uniformly accelerated rectilinear motion, as that book teaches in lesson 1-2 (printed p.26).
3. Handle vertical motion under gravity — free fall — as that book teaches in lesson 1-3 (printed p.35).
4. Find the momentum of a moving body, as that book teaches in lesson 2-1 (printed p.42).
5. State and use Newton's first law, as that book teaches in lesson 2-2 (printed p.46).
6. Use Newton's second law, F = ma, including in friction problems, as that book teaches in lesson 2-3 (printed p.53).
7. Use Newton's third law and mixed applications of the laws, as that book teaches in lesson 2-4 (printed p.61).
8. Differentiate and integrate position, velocity and acceleration as vector functions of time, as Egypt's Applied Grade 12 book teaches (lesson 1-1, printed p.72).
9. Meet motion of bodies with variable mass or acceleration, and motion of connected bodies, as the Grade 12 book teaches (lessons 2-1, printed p.88, and 2-2, printed p.95).
10. Find impulse and use impulse as change of momentum, as the Grade 12 book teaches (lesson 2-3, printed p.112).
11. Find work done by a force, as the Grade 12 book teaches (lesson 3-1, printed p.124).
12. Find kinetic and potential energy and use the energy idea, as the Grade 12 book teaches (lesson 3-2, printed p.136).
13. Find power, including the power of a moving force as P = Fv, as the Grade 12 book teaches (lesson 3-3, printed p.150 — the body's own numbering; the English contents list it as "3-2" by a contents slip).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A calculator for the falling and energy numbers.
A ball or a stone you can let fall a short, safe distance onto soft
ground, a toy car or a ball for rolling on the floor, and a stopwatch
or phone timer, for the hands-on work. Nothing is thrown at anyone,
and nothing is dropped from a height or onto anyone.

2. **The explanation** — rectilinear motion; the constant-acceleration equations; free fall; momentum; Newton's three laws; differentiating and integrating motion; variable and connected bodies; impulse; work; energy; power (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the rectilinear-motion part: a picture of a dot moving along a straight line, leaving equal marks each second while steady, then marks that spread wider as it speeds up — the student says which stretch is steady speed and which is acceleration.
   - ADD GIF at the free-fall part: a picture of a ball falling past a ruler, its gaps between snapshots growing second by second — the student counts how the gaps grow.
   - ADD IMAGE at the momentum part: a picture of a light ball and a heavy ball rolling at the same speed towards two identical empty boxes → the student says which box gets knocked farther and why.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes velocity and acceleration → velocity is how fast position changes; acceleration is how fast velocity changes. A body can have big velocity and zero acceleration (steady speed) or zero velocity for an instant and big acceleration (the top of a throw) (Grade 11 Term 2, lesson 1-1, printed p.15).
- Picks the wrong constant-acceleration equation → list the three you know and the one you want from u, v, a, s, t first; the right equation is the one holding exactly those four (lesson 1-2, printed p.26).
- Gets the sign wrong in free fall → choose one direction as positive at the start — down is easiest for a falling body — and give u, a and s that same sign all the way through (lesson 1-3, printed p.35).
- Thinks a big force is needed to keep a body moving → Newton's first law says the opposite: with no resultant force a body keeps its velocity; force is needed to change velocity, not to keep it (lesson 2-2, printed p.46).
- Confuses impulse with momentum → momentum is what a moving body has, mv; impulse is what a force delivers over time, Ft, and it equals the change of momentum (Grade 12, lesson 2-3, printed p.112).
- Confuses work with power → work is force times distance, in joules; power is how fast the work is done, work divided by time, in watts (Grade 12, lessons 3-1 and 3-3, printed pp.124, 150).
