# Hands-on — Dynamics: motion, momentum and energy

The lesson worked moving bodies, on safe, floor-level setups only: rolling, a short fall onto soft ground, gentle pushes and small lifts. Nothing is thrown at anyone, nothing is dropped from a height, and nothing is lifted above knee height.

## Roll and slow
Roll a ball gently along a smooth floor and mark with bits of tape where it is after each clap of your hands — one clap per second, a partner clapping steadily. The gaps between the marks shrink: friction is slowing the ball, a resultant force changing its velocity, exactly Newton's first and second laws in one roll (Egypt, Applied Grade 11 Term 2, lessons 2-2 and 2-3, printed pp.46, 53). Repeat on a carpet and compare the gaps.

## A short, safe fall
Hold a ball just above a cushion or a folded towel on the floor — no higher than your knee — and let it fall. It lands almost at once. Now say why timing a short fall is hard: gravity accelerates the ball the whole way down, so it covers the small distance faster and faster (lesson 1-3, printed p.35). Do not drop anything from tables, stairs or windows, and never drop anything near feet or people.

## Light ball, heavy ball
Roll a light ball gently at an empty light cardboard box on the floor and watch how far the box slides. Roll a heavier ball at the same box at about the same speed and watch again. Same speed, more mass, more momentum — the box slides farther (lesson 2-1, printed p.42). Keep both rolls slow and along the floor, away from feet.

## Push, time it, feel impulse
Push a box of books a short way along the floor twice: once with a short, sharp push and once with a longer, gentler push that gets it moving at about the same speed. Say it whole: "The longer push gave the same change of momentum with a smaller force, because impulse is force times time" (Egypt, Applied Grade 12, lesson 2-3, printed p.112).

## Lift it fast, lift it slow
Lift a small stack of books from the floor onto a low stool — no higher than your knee. Do it once slowly, counting to five, and once in one count. The work is the same both times: the same weight lifted through the same height. The power is not the same: the fast lift did the work in less time (Grade 12, lessons 3-1 and 3-3, printed pp.124, 150). Bend your knees, keep the stack light, and stop if anything feels strained.

## Two trolleys, one string
If you have two toy cars, tie them loosely together with string and pull the front one gently along the floor so both roll together. They share one acceleration, and the string's pull is what accelerates the back car — connected bodies, moving as one system with an inside pull between them (Grade 12, lesson 2-2, printed p.95). Pull gently; if the string snaps or a car tips, reset and pull more slowly.
