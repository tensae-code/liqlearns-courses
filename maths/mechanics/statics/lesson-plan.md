# Lesson plan — Statics: forces in balance

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 17 — Mechanics · **Lesson:** Statics: forces in balance
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 132 of 136, the first lesson of Unit 17 — Mechanics (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** statics — forces and their resolution, resultants, equilibrium, friction and limiting equilibrium, moments, couples and parallel forces, all for bodies that stay in balance.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the trigonometry lessons (sine and cosine of an angle) and on solving simple equations — the learner can already split a slanted quantity into a sideways part and an up-down part in idea. It needs nothing else.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the trigonometry lessons — resolving a force uses sine and cosine at every step. Recommended, never forced.
- Recommended first: the vectors work in the earlier units — a force is treated in this lesson as a quantity with size and direction. Recommended, never forced.
- This lesson is the first half of mechanics as Egypt's books build it: statics is balance, dynamics (Level 133) is motion. Nothing here moves — that is the point of the lesson.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a force is, name its unit, and treat it as a vector with size and direction, the way Egypt's Applied Grade 11 Term 1 book opens its statics unit (lesson 1-1, printed p.12), after its introduction to mechanics (printed p.2).
2. Resolve a force into two components at right angles to each other, as that book teaches in lesson 1-2 (printed p.20).
3. Find the resultant of coplanar forces meeting at a point, as that book teaches in lesson 1-3 (printed p.25).
4. Test and use equilibrium at a point — the components balance in each direction — as that book teaches in lesson 1-4 (printed p.31).
5. Work with friction on a horizontal rough plane and find limiting equilibrium, as that book teaches in lesson 1-5 (printed p.43).
6. Work with a body in equilibrium on an inclined rough plane, as that book teaches in lesson 1-6 (printed p.50).
7. Find the moment of a force about a point and use the general theorem of moments, as Egypt's Applied Grade 12 book teaches in its Moments unit (lesson 1-1, printed p.10).
8. Find the resultant of parallel coplanar forces and use equilibrium of a system of parallel forces, as the Grade 12 book teaches (lessons 2-1, printed p.22, and 2-2, printed p.32).
9. Recognise a couple, find its moment, and find a resultant couple, as the Grade 12 book teaches (lessons 3-1, printed p.42, and 3-2, printed p.50).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A ruler and a protractor for force diagrams.
A book or a box, a flat table, and a board or a large book you can
tilt into a ramp, for the hands-on work. Nothing heavy, nothing
raised above the table.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner pushes a book gently across a table, first straight, then slanting, and says which way it moved each time; the lesson names what they just did (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — forces; resolution; resultants at a point; equilibrium at a point; friction and limiting equilibrium, flat and inclined; moments; parallel forces; couples (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the resolution part: a picture of one slanted arrow splitting into a sideways arrow and an up arrow that grow as the slanted arrow grows — the student reads the two component sizes off the picture.
   - ADD IMAGE at the resultant part: a picture of three arrows meeting at one point on a ring, with one single arrow drawn dashed beside them → the student says in one sentence what the dashed arrow does that the three do together.
   - ADD GIF at the friction part: a picture of a box on a board that tilts slowly until the box just begins to slide — the student says what "just begins" means for the friction force.
   - ADD IMAGE in the moments part: a picture of a door being pushed near its handle and then near its hinge, the turning effect marked bigger at the handle → the student explains why the handle push turns the door more.
   - ADD IMAGE in the couples part: a picture of two hands turning a steering wheel, one hand pushing up on one side and the other pulling down on the other side → the student names the two forces and says why the wheel turns without moving forward.
3. **Say it to your companion** — explain resultants, limiting friction and a couple in whole sentences, and answer your companion's questions (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — statics in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — resolve, balance and turn five real setups on paper (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes the sine and cosine components → always draw the angle at the force's tail first: the component next to the angle takes cosine, the component across from the angle takes sine (Grade 11 Term 1, lesson 1-2, printed p.20).
- Adds forces as plain numbers when they point different ways → forces are vectors: resolve every force first, add sideways parts together and up-down parts together, then rebuild the one resultant (lesson 1-3, printed p.25).
- Thinks friction is always at its biggest → friction only grows as big as it needs to be, up to its limit; it reaches that limit only at limiting equilibrium (lesson 1-5, printed p.43).
- Forgets the distance in a moment → a moment is a force times its perpendicular distance from the point; the same force turns more the farther out it acts (Grade 12, lesson 1-1, printed p.10).
- Thinks a couple moves a body forward → a couple's two forces cancel as pushes; what is left is pure turning (Grade 12, lesson 3-1, printed p.42).
- This lesson's next door: Dynamics (Level 133, same unit) — what happens when the forces do not balance.
