# Video script — Statics: forces in balance

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the
lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are
made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Push a book. It moves the way you push it.
A push has a size, and it has a way it points. That is a **force**.

Now push on a slant. Part of your push goes sideways.
Part of it goes up and down.
Splitting one force into two parts — that is **resolution**.

Many forces at one point can do the same job as ONE force.
That one force is the **resultant**.

If the parts balance — sideways zero, up and down zero —
the body stays still. That is **equilibrium**.

Rub two surfaces together. They grip. That grip is **friction**.
Push harder and harder. At one moment the grip is at its biggest,
and the body is just about to slide. That is **limiting equilibrium**.

Push a door far from its hinge — it turns easily.
Push near the hinge — it hardly turns.
Force times distance is a **moment**.

Two equal forces, opposite ways, not in one line?
They do not push the body anywhere. They only turn it.
That pair is a **couple**.

Now you. Resolve a force. Balance a point. Turn a door.
Say it whole. That is statics.
