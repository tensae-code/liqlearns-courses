# Hands-on — Hydrostatics

## Activity 1 — The three-hole bottle (10 minutes)

Take a tall plastic bottle and make three small holes down one
side, evenly spaced. Cover them with tape, fill the bottle,
stand it in a sink, and pull the tape off in one go. Watch:
the lowest jet travels furthest, the top jet shortest — Sub
lesson 1's depth rule made visible. Refill and hold the bottle
so two holes sit at the same depth: their jets match.

## Activity 2 — Load the boat (15 minutes)

Float a small plastic bowl in a basin. Add coins one at a time,
keeping them spread so the bowl stays level. After each coin,
mark the waterline on the bowl's side with a washable pen. The
marks sink steadily: each coin's weight is answered by exactly
its weight of pushed-aside water. Note the coin count at which
the bowl first ships water over its rim — that is its floating
limit, Sub lesson 3 with your own hands.

## Activity 3 — The two-syringe press (15 minutes)

Join two syringes of different sizes with a short tube, fill
the system with water, and press the small syringe's plunger:
the big plunger rises against your other thumb — and pushes
far harder than your pressing thumb does, while moving less.
Feel Pascal's trade directly: same pressure, bigger area,
bigger thrust, shorter travel. This is the hydraulic press of
Sub lesson 4, at table size.
