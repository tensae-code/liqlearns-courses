# Hydrostatics — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, expressed as its SUB
LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure — README, "Units, Lessons and
Sub Lessons"). One lesson, one level (Level 165). The lesson
was built new in the expansion era (Sri Lanka expansion merge,
2026-11-11), so there are no old depth layers to map: the sub
lessons below are its depths as built, cut at the founding
books' own contents — pressure, thrust on surfaces, flotation,
and the gases-and-machines chapters both A/L texts carry. The
questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson
view.

Pass marks: the founding A/L texts state no school pass mark
for the lesson as a whole, so no mark is invented — each sub
lesson's pass mark is stated as the observable thing the
learner can do.

## Sub lesson 1 — Pressure in a Liquid
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: state the three pressure facts (grows with depth; equal in all directions at a point; independent of the vessel's shape), write thrust = pressure × area, and predict which of three holes in a tank throws the longest jet.

## Sub lesson 2 — Thrust on Immersed Surfaces
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: work a thrust from a pressure and an area, and say why the centre of pressure on a vertical wall lies below its middle — and why dams are thickest at the base.

## Sub lesson 3 — Floating Bodies
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: state Archimedes' principle, decide sink-or-float for a body given its weight and the weight it displaces, and describe how a floating body settles — and what a hydrometer reads.

## Sub lesson 4 — Gases, Machines and Flow
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: explain the hydraulic press from "the same pressure on a bigger area," work the press's thrust multiplication from the pistons' areas, and say why a deeper hole drives a faster jet.

## What "maxed" means here

The learner reasons about still fluids the way Sri Lanka's A/L
candidates are taught to: pressure and thrust from Ramsey's
*Hydrostatics* (Cambridge University Press, 1967) and Priedel's
*Intermediate Hydrostatics* (Edward Arnold, 1969) — the whole of
both books' ground, from the pressure facts to flotation and the
hydraulic press.
