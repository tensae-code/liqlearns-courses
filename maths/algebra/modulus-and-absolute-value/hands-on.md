# Hands-on — Modulus and Absolute Value

## Activity 1 — Walk the distances (10 minutes)

Draw a number line from −10 to 10 on paper, or pace one out on the floor with a strip of tape marked 0 in the middle. Stand at 0 and walk to 6, then to −6, counting your steps: both walks are 6 — that is |6| = |−6| = 6, the definition Mongolia's Grade 7 book gives. Then measure the distance between −3 and 4 by walking it: 7 steps, the book's |a − b| with a = −3 and b = 4. Finish by finding, on the line, every point at distance 3 from 5 — you should land on exactly the solutions of |x − 5| = 3.

## Activity 2 — Solve it, then stand on it (15 minutes)

Solve |x + 2| = 5 by cases on paper (x + 2 = 5 or x + 2 = −5, giving x = 3 and x = −7). Now check both answers the distance way: |x + 2| is the distance from x to −2 — stand at −2 on your line and find the points 5 steps away. The two methods must give the same pair. Then write down |3x − 1| = −2 and explain, standing on the line, why no point can ever answer it.

## Activity 3 — The squaring discovery (10 minutes)

Make a table: x = −4, −1, 0, 3, 5. For each, work out |x|, then |x|², then x². Look down the last two columns — they match every time, which is the book's proved theorem |x|² = x². Now use it: |x| = |2x − 1| becomes x² = (2x − 1)²; expand, solve the quadratic (3x² − 4x + 1 = 0), and get x = 1/3 and x = 1. Check both in the original equation with the bars still on.
