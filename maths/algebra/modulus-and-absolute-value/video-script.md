# Video script — Modulus and Absolute Value

Super-simple voice. The tutor talks; a number line stays on screen, and each number lights up as it is named.

---

Stand at 0 on this line. Walk to 5. You walked 5 steps. Now walk to −5 instead. You also walked 5 steps. The walk does not care which way you went. That walk — the distance from 0 — is the modulus. We write it with two straight bars: |5| is 5, and |−5| is 5 too.

So the rule is small. If the number is 0 or more, the modulus is the number itself. If the number is below 0, the modulus is that number with its sign dropped. A modulus can be 0. A modulus can be big. A modulus can never be negative. Never.

Now a question. |x − 5| = 3. In words: which points are exactly 3 steps away from 5? Walk 3 steps one way: 8. Walk 3 steps the other way: 2. Two answers: 2 and 8. That is the whole trick of these equations — a modulus equation is a distance question.

And sometimes the answer is: nobody. |2x − 5| = −7. A distance of minus 7? There is no such walk. No solution. When you see a negative number on the other side of a modulus, stop. The book itself shows you this one.

Inequalities are walks too. |x| ≤ 4: stay within 4 steps of 0 — everything from −4 to 4. |x − 5| > 8: stand more than 8 steps away from 5 — below −3, or above 13. Draw the line, mark the point, count the steps. The picture gives the answer.

Last, one gift. Square a modulus and the bars vanish: |x|² = x². And |x| is the square root of x². The book proves these — they are not tricks, they are theorems. With them, even |f| > |g| becomes plain algebra: (f − g)(f + g) > 0.

Distance from 0. Two cases. Bands and rays on a line. Squares that remove the bars. That is modulus.
