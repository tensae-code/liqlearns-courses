## Sub lesson 1 — The Four Means of a Pair
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. State all four means for positive numbers — arithmetic, geometric, harmonic, quadratic (root-mean-square) — and compute each one for a pair of numbers. Pass mark: 4 of 5. Three or more numbers (old Layer 4): compute the four means for a triple of positive numbers and check the chain holds — the n-number forms are stated in this sub lesson's text. Pass mark: 4 of 5.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Chain of the Four Means
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. State the order QM ≥ AM ≥ GM ≥ HM and place a computed set of four means in it; spot a claimed order that breaks the chain. Pass mark: 4 of 5.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Equality Case
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Say when the four means are all equal, verify it on an equal set, and predict how the chain opens as a set spreads out. Pass mark: 4 of 5.

## Sub lesson 4 — Reading the Chain as a Tool
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Turn the chain into a bound: greatest product for a fixed sum (AM ≥ GM), and quick estimates that replace a hard mean by an easier one on the correct side. Pass mark: 4 of 5.

## What "maxed" means here

The learner can define and compute all four means, can recite and use the chain QM ≥ AM ≥ GM ≥ HM, knows it is proved in North Macedonia's Grade 12 Algebra book (§3.4) with QM ≥ AM proved first, and can call on the equality case — all means equal exactly when all the numbers are equal — the exact skills that book's mean-values unit teaches (printed p. 58).
