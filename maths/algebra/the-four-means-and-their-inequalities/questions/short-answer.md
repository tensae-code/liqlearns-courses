1. (Sub lesson 1) Compute all four means of 2 and 8 and write them in chain order.
   **Answer: QM = √((4 + 64)/2) = √34 ≈ 5.83; AM = 5; GM = √16 = 4; HM = 2·16/10 = 3.2 — so HM ≤ GM ≤ AM ≤ QM, i.e. 3.2 ≤ 4 ≤ 5 ≤ 5.83.** North Macedonia, АЛГЕБРА ЗА ТРЕТА ГОДИНА (Grade 12, elective), §3.4, printed p. 58.

2. (Sub lesson 1) Compute the four means of 1, 3, 9 and verify the chain.
   **Answer: AM = 13/3 ≈ 4.33; GM = 27^(1/3) = 3; HM = 3/(1 + 1/3 + 1/9) = 27/13 ≈ 2.08; QM = √(91/3) ≈ 5.51. Chain: 5.51 ≥ 4.33 ≥ 3 ≥ 2.08 — holds.** North Macedonia, АЛГЕБРА ЗА ТРЕТА ГОДИНА (Grade 12, elective), §3.4, printed p. 58.

3. (Sub lesson 3) Explain why all four means of 7, 7, 7 are 7, in one or two sentences.
   **Answer: every recipe — averaging, averaging squares, the root of the product, the flipped average — applied to copies of one number returns that number; the chain collapses because there is no spread.** North Macedonia, АЛГЕБРА ЗА ТРЕТА ГОДИНА (Grade 12, elective), §3.4, printed p. 58.

4. (Sub lesson 4) A rectangle has perimeter 40. What is its greatest possible area, and which link of the chain settles it?
   **Answer: the sides a and b have a + b = 20, so AM = 10 ≥ GM = √(ab) gives ab ≤ 100; the greatest area is 100, reached by the 10 × 10 square — the AM ≥ GM link.** North Macedonia, АЛГЕБРА ЗА ТРЕТА ГОДИНА (Grade 12, elective), §3.4, printed p. 58.
