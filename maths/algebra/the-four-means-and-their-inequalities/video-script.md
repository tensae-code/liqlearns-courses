# Video script — The Four Means and Their Inequalities

Super-simple voice. The tutor talks; the two numbers 2 and 8 stay on screen, and four markers for the four means appear one by one on a number line.

---

Average. You know one average: add the numbers, divide by how many. For 2 and 8, that gives 5. But that is only one of four averages — and a Grade 12 book from North Macedonia lines all four up side by side.

The second average multiplies instead of adding. The geometric mean of 2 and 8 is the square root of 2 times 8 — the square root of 16 — which is 4. Smaller than 5. Interesting.

The third average flips the numbers upside down first. The harmonic mean: flip 2 and 8 into 1/2 and 1/8, average those, then flip back. It gives 3.2. Smaller again.

The fourth squares first. The quadratic mean — square 2 and 8, average the squares, take the root. Root of 34, about 5.83. This one is bigger than 5.

Now look at the line: 3.2, 4, 5, 5.83. Harmonic, geometric, arithmetic, quadratic. That order is not luck. For any positive numbers at all, the quadratic mean is at least the arithmetic mean, which is at least the geometric mean, which is at least the harmonic mean. The book proves it — and it proves the first step, quadratic against arithmetic, before all the others.

When do they all agree? Only when there is nothing to average — when all the numbers are already the same. 3 and 3 give 3, 3, 3, 3.

Four averages. One chain. Quadratic, arithmetic, geometric, harmonic — biggest to smallest, every time.
