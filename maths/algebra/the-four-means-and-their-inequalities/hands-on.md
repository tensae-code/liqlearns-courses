# Hands-on — The Four Means and Their Inequalities

## Activity 1 — Four averages of one pair (10 minutes)

Take the pair 2 and 8 from North Macedonia's Grade 12 Algebra book's mean-values unit (printed p. 58). Compute all four means by hand: arithmetic (2 + 8)/2 = 5; geometric √(2·8) = √16 = 4; harmonic 2·2·8/(2 + 8) = 32/10 = 3.2; quadratic √((4 + 64)/2) = √34 ≈ 5.83. Mark all four on a number line in colour, smallest to largest, and write the chain underneath: HM ≤ GM ≤ AM ≤ QM. This is the definition-and-chain skill of the book's §3.4.

## Activity 2 — Slide them together (15 minutes)

Now take the pairs (1, 9), (3, 7), (4, 6) and (5, 5) — every pair has the same arithmetic mean, 5. Compute the geometric mean of each: √9 = 3, √21 ≈ 4.58, √24 ≈ 4.90, √25 = 5. Watch the geometric mean climb as the pair closes up, and reach the arithmetic mean exactly when the two numbers meet. Write the sentence the picture proves: for a fixed sum, the product — and so the geometric mean — is greatest when the numbers are equal. That is AM ≥ GM read as a machine, the middle link of the book's chain (printed p. 58).

## Activity 3 — The chain on three numbers (15 minutes)

Take the triple 1, 3, 9. Compute: AM = 13/3 ≈ 4.33; GM = (27)^(1/3) = 3; HM = 3/(1 + 1/3 + 1/9) = 27/13 ≈ 2.08; QM = √((1 + 9 + 81)/3) = √(91/3) ≈ 5.51. Check the chain: 5.51 ≥ 4.33 ≥ 3 ≥ 2.08 — it holds. Then do the same for 3, 3, 3 and watch all four means land on 3: the equality case of the book's §3.4, where the numbers do not spread at all.
