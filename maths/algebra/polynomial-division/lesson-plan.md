# Lesson plan — Polynomial Division

**Course:** Maths · **Stage:** Stage 6 — Algebra · **Lesson:** Polynomial Division
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 58 of 136, inside Stage 6 — Algebra (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** dividing one polynomial by another — long division and synthetic division — and the two theorems the division hands you for free: the remainder theorem and the factor theorem.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on expressions (factorising and expanding) and on polynomials and their values p(a). It needs nothing else.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: expressions — expanding brackets and factorising; a division is checked by multiplying back. Recommended, never forced.
- Recommended first: evaluating a polynomial p(a) by substitution — the remainder theorem is that value wearing a new name. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Divide a polynomial by a binomial by long division, writing p(x) = d(x)q(x) + R(x) (Indonesia, Grade 11 TL Revisi, Bab 2, pp.70–88; Bangladesh, Class 9, Ch. 4 §8.3, pp.96–99).
2. Divide by (x − a) quickly by synthetic division / Horner's method (Indonesia, Bab 2, pp.70–88; Laos, Grade 9, Ch. 1 L3, p.10).
3. State and use the remainder theorem: the remainder on dividing by (x − a) is p(a) — and by (ax + b) is p(−b/a) (Bangladesh, Class 9, §9, pp.99–101; Yemen, Grade 10 Part 1, §4-3, p.88).
4. State and use the factor theorem: (x − a) is a factor exactly when p(a) = 0, and use it to factorise a cubic fully (Ethiopia, Grade 10, Unit 2, pp.67–120 — the theorems proved there; Pakistan, Class 11, Unit 9, pp.169–176).
5. List the possible rational zeros p/q of a polynomial and test them (Indonesia, Bab 2 — the rational-zero theorem; Ethiopia, Grade 10, Unit 2).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil — long division is written work. A multiplication grid helps for checking by multiplying back.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner divides 47 by 5 the primary-school way (quotient 9, remainder 2, check 5 × 9 + 2), then is asked: can x³ + 2x² − 5x − 6 be divided by x + 1 the same way? (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the division identity; long division; synthetic division (Horner); the remainder theorem; the factor theorem and full factorisation; rational zeros (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the long-division part: a picture of a long division being built line by line — divide, multiply, subtract, bring down — the student calls out each next line before it appears.
   - ADD IMAGE in the synthetic part: a picture of a synthetic-division table for x³ − 4x² + x + 6 divided by (x − 2), coefficients in a row with the brought-down numbers under them → the student copies the table and completes the last two cells.
   - ADD IMAGE in the factor-theorem part: a picture of a cubic curve crossing the x-axis at −1, 2 and 3, with its three factors written under the crossings → the student matches each crossing to its factor.
3. **Say it to your companion** — state both theorems whole, and explain why p(a) = 0 makes (x − a) a factor (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the division chant in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — one long division, one synthetic division, one remainder without dividing, and one cubic factorised fully (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Long division loses the place → write every power, including 0x² terms for missing powers, and work one line at a time: divide, multiply, subtract, bring down (the layout Egypt's Grade 7 book drills, §2-5, printed pp.43–47).
- Synthetic division sign errors → the table uses a from (x − a); for (x + 2) the number is −2, not 2 (Laos, Grade 9, L3, p.10; Grade 11, Ch. 1, L3–L5).
- Remainder theorem feels like magic → it is the division identity at x = a: p(a) = (a − a)q(a) + R = R. Ethiopia's Grade 10 book proves it in full (Unit 2, pp.67–120) — read the proof there.
