**Acceptance count: taught in 13 countries (Ethiopia, Indonesia, Pakistan, Yemen, Bangladesh, Laos, Nicaragua, Iran, Cambodia, Egypt, Greece, Kuwait, Sudan).** Ethiopia carries the proof: its Grade 10 Unit 2 proves the division, remainder and factor theorems and adds rational-zero candidates and the location theorem. Indonesia carries the fullest school treatment — division algorithm, long division, Horner, both theorems and the rational-zero theorem in one chapter (Bab 2, pp.70–88). Pakistan carries long and synthetic division with both theorems proved and applied, including a quartic factorised completely (Class 11, Unit 9). Yemen carries the theorems in use, with its clean worked check p(3) = 3 (§4-3, p.88), and passes straight to zeros (§4-4, p.92). Bangladesh carries division, the identity, and both theorems with the (ax + b) remainder form (Ch. 4, pp.96–102). Laos carries long and synthetic division twice, in Grade 9 and again in Grade 11. Nicaragua carries the climb from monomial division in Grade 8 to synthetic division inside rational functions in Grade 10. Iran carries polynomial division inside rational expressions (pp.126–129). Cambodia pairs division with GCD and LCM of polynomials (pp.78–90). Egypt carries the written layout earliest, in Grade 7. Greece carries division of polynomials in its Grade 9 expressions chapter without naming the two theorems. Kuwait carries the topic as workbook evidence only — its Grade 11 Term 1 polynomials unit (pp.43–48) is exercises in an exercise workbook whose student book is not in hand, and it is counted as workbook evidence, never as teaching text.

Sudan divides polynomials in Grade 10 (by a monomial, long division and synthetic division) and proves the remainder and factor theorems in Grade 11, using them to factorise cubics and solve higher-degree equations. Its Grade 10 long-division example is the flawed one recorded in the table above — the divisor misprinted, the remainder misstated — and is taught corrected from the book's own correct example.
