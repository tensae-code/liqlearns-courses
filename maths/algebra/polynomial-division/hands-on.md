# Hands-on — Polynomial Division

The lesson divided on paper, the slow way and the fast way, and checked by multiplying back.

## Divide, then multiply back
Divide x³ + 2x² − 5x − 6 by (x + 1) by long division. Write the identity underneath: (x + 1)(quotient) + remainder. Now expand the right side with a partner checking each term, until it matches the starting polynomial exactly. The check is part of the method, not an extra.

## Race the two roads
Same division, two roads: one partner does (x³ − 4x² + x + 6) ÷ (x − 2) by long division; the other by the synthetic table. Compare quotient and remainder when both finish — they must agree (x² − 2x − 3, remainder 0). Swap roads and repeat with (2x³ − 3x² + 4x + 5) ÷ (x − 1)... first agree what each road should give, then race.

## Remainder without dividing
Cards with polynomials and divisors: p(x) = x³ − 2x² − x + 2 by (x − 1); p(x) = x² − 5x + 9 by (x − 3); p(x) = 3x³ − 2x + 1 by (2x + 1). No division allowed — evaluate p(a) only, write the remainder, then one card is checked by a full division to prove the shortcut honest.

## Factor hunt
For p(x) = x³ − 2x² − x + 2, test x = 1, −1, 2, −2 in turn, writing p of each. Circle the zeros. Each zero hands you a factor; divide once and finish the factorisation. Three zeros, three factors — check by expanding.
