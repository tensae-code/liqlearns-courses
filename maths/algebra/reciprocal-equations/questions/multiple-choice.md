# Multiple choice — Reciprocal Equations

1. (Layer 1) Which polynomial is reciprocal?
   A. 2x³ + 3x² + 3x + 2 B. x³ + 2x² + 3x + 4 C. 3x⁴ + 4x³ − 14x² + 4x + 5 D. x² + 3x + 2
   **Answer: A** — its coefficients 2, 3, 3, 2 read the same in both directions; each other list breaks the mirror (B: 1…4, C: 3…5, D: 1…2). Venezuela, La Matemática y el Vivir Bien (Grade 11), printed p. 75.

2. (Layer 2) A reciprocal equation has the root x = 4. Another root is guaranteed:
   A. −4 B. 1/4 C. −1/4 D. 16
   **Answer: B** — roots come in reciprocal pairs: p(1/r) = p(r)/rⁿ = 0. Venezuela, La Matemática y el Vivir Bien (Grade 11), printed p. 75.

3. (Layer 3) Every reciprocal equation of odd degree has the root:
   A. 1 B. 0 C. −1 D. 2
   **Answer: C** — −1 is the fixed point of 1/x that the odd-degree pairing leaves over; the book proves it and opens its cubic with it. Venezuela, La Matemática y el Vivir Bien (Grade 11), printed p. 76.

4. (Layer 4) In the fold of 3x⁴ + 4x³ − 14x² + 4x + 3 = 0, after dividing by x² and setting y = x + 1/x, the expression x² + 1/x² becomes:
   A. y² B. y² − 2 C. y² + 2 D. 2y
   **Answer: B** — x² + 1/x² = (x + 1/x)² − 2 = y² − 2. Venezuela, La Matemática y el Vivir Bien (Grade 11), printed p. 77.

5. (Layer 5) The roots of 3x⁴ + 4x³ − 14x² + 4x + 3 = 0 are:
   A. 1 (double), −1/3 and −3 B. 1, −1, 3 and −3 C. 2 and −10/3 D. −1 (double), 1/3 and 3
   **Answer: A** — the fold gives 3y² + 4y − 20 = 0, so y = 2 (→ x = 1, double) and y = −10/3 (→ x = −1/3, −3); option C names the y-values, not the roots. Venezuela, La Matemática y el Vivir Bien (Grade 11), printed p. 77.
