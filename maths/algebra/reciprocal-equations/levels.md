## Sub lesson 1 — What a Reciprocal Polynomial Is
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Recognise a reciprocal polynomial from its coefficients (they read the same in both directions), and state the definition both ways: symmetric coefficients, and p(1/x) = p(x)/xⁿ. Pass mark: 4 of 5.

## Sub lesson 2 — Roots in Reciprocal Pairs
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Use the pairing: if r ≠ 0 is a root of a reciprocal equation, so is 1/r; and say why x = 0 is never a root when the constant term is not 0. Pass mark: 4 of 5.

## Sub lesson 3 — Odd Degree — the Root −1
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Use the guaranteed root −1 of every odd-degree reciprocal equation: divide it out by Ruffini (synthetic division) and solve the smaller equation that remains. Pass mark: 4 of 5.

## Sub lesson 4 — Even Degree — Folding in Half
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. For even degree, divide by x², group the symmetric terms, and turn the equation into one in y = x + 1/x, using x² + 1/x² = y² − 2. Pass mark: 4 of 5. Full solves (old Layer 5): carry a reciprocal equation of degree 3 or 4 all the way to its roots, and check the answer with the pairing: roots must arrive as r with 1/r, with ±1 allowed to pair with themselves. Pass mark: 4 of 5.

## What "maxed" means here

The learner can look at any polynomial and say at once whether it is reciprocal; can solve reciprocal equations of odd degree through the root −1 and of even degree through the substitution y = x + 1/x; and can audit any claimed solution set by the pairing test — the exact skills Venezuela's Grade 11 book teaches in its polynomials lesson (printed pp. 75–77).
