A lesson that teaches the three relations between a cubic
equation's roots and its coefficients — the sum of the roots,
the sum of the products in pairs, and the product of the
roots — and how to run them both ways: reading facts about
the roots off an equation, and building an equation from
roots. Dominica's Grade 12 CAPE course book (Toolsie, *Pure
Mathematics: A Complete Course for CAPE, Unit 1*) teaches them
in its Cubic Functions and Equations chapter, printed p. 207,
immediately after the matching relations for a quadratic
(printed p. 205, stocked in quadratic-equations). The course
carried the quadratic relations and one generalised-Vieta
citation inside polynomial-division, but no lesson taught
the cubic relations as their own thing; the expansion read
flagged it NEW LESSON NEEDED and this lesson is built from
that flag. Prerequisite (soft hint only — every lesson
stands alone): quadratic-equations for the two-root case and
polynomial-division for what a root and a factor are. A
learner who opens this lesson directly still gets the whole
thing: the three relations are stated, checked on a factored
cubic and used here.
