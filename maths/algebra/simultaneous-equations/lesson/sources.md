**Acceptance count: taught in 14 countries (Kazakhstan, Jordan, Libya, Greece, Vietnam, Mauritania, Laos, Yemen, Indonesia, Nicaragua, Ethiopia, South Sudan, Iraq, Bangladesh).** Bangladesh carries simultaneous equations whole in Class 9 (Ch. 5, pp. 113–140) — formulating linear equations in two variables, graphical solution, consistency conditions, substitution, elimination and cross-multiplication (derivation verified correct), with the worked solutions (4, 4) and (2, −1). Two errors on the cited pages are taught corrected: the graphical-method English on p. 121 says “y = 4x + 5” (correct y = 4x − 5; the Bangla prints no operator at all, “y = 4x 5”, while its table gives the correct “y = 4x − 5”), and the p. 138 team project suggests pairs (p = 1, q = 3) and (p = 2, q = 4) that do not satisfy q = p² − 2p + 44 — the system has no real market-equilibrium point. Iraq carries simultaneous equations from systems of two linear equations in two variables — graphical, substitution, elimination — in Grade 9 (Ch. 3, 3-1, p. 56), through two simultaneous equations in two variables, analytic and graphical including second-degree systems, in Grade 10 Scientific (Ch. 2, 2-3) and simultaneous systems including circle/line systems in Grade 10 Literary (Ch. 2; ex. 12, p. 41), to solving systems by matrices, inverse and Cramer's rule in Grade 11 Scientific (Ch. 9, pp. 216–258) and Cramer's rule for 2×2 and 3×3 systems in Grade 11 Literary (Ch. 3, 3-10–3-12, pp. 87–100). Kazakhstan carries the fullest treatment — two-variable equations and both algebraic methods in Grade 6, the graphical method in Grade 7, and symmetric systems in Grade 9. Jordan carries the three methods in order in Grade 8: graphical, substitution, elimination. Libya carries the hens-and-roosters puzzle and all three algebraic methods plus the graphical interpretation in Grade 9. Greece carries systems in Grade 9, graphical first and algebraic second. Vietnam carries two-unknown linear systems in Grade 9. Mauritania carries equations, inequalities and systems in its 5AS-LO book, Chapter 3. South Africa is not in this lesson: its Grade 9 book states plainly that it teaches no simultaneous equations. Sierra Leone is not in this lesson: its JSS never teaches simultaneous equations. Egypt, Armenia and Ghana are not in this lesson on the filed stock. Laos carries simultaneous equations by elimination, substitution and graphically in Grade 8 (only the checker-verified systems cited — the filing's two unverified L14/L15 items are not used), through quadratic and symmetric systems in Grade 9, to linear systems in Grade 12. Yemen carries simultaneous equations from systems in two variables in Grade 9 to solving them by matrices in Grade 11, in both tracks. Indonesia carries simultaneous equations from SPLDV by graphing, substitution and elimination in Grade 8 K13, through systems in two variables by graphing, substitution, elimination and the combined method in Grade 9, to systems of linear equations and inequalities in Grade 10 (Merdeka and Revisi editions); the Grade 8 Merdeka guide has no SPLDV chapter. Nicaragua carries simultaneous equations from 2×2 systems by substitution and elimination in the Grade 8 student book, through the same three methods (substitution, equalisation, reduction) on fractional and decimal systems in the Grade 8 module (module evidence), to 3×3 systems with matrices, determinants and Cramer's rule in the Grade 10 student book and module (module evidence); the graphical reading of the 2×2 cases is in the Grade 10 module, but no book teaches solving a system by graphing. Ethiopia carries simultaneous equations from systems of linear equations in two variables by graphing, substitution and elimination in Grade 9, to systems defined and solved by row operations (matrix method) in Grade 11. South Sudan carries simultaneous equations from elimination and substitution in Secondary 1 to solving simultaneous equations using matrices in Secondary 2. Honest limit: the Secondary 1 teacher guide's simultaneous-equations answers (guide p. 87) do not match the student book's systems — cite the student book only.
