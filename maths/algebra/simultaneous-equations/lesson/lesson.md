# Simultaneous Equations — the lesson

Course: Maths · Stage 6 · Lesson: Simultaneous Equations
Built from: Kazakhstan, Grade 6 (Atamura 2018), Grade 7 Algebra (Mektep 2017) and Grade 9 Algebra (Astana-kitap 2023) — Jordan, Grade 8 Term 2 — Libya, Grade 9 — Greece, Grade 9 — Vietnam, Grade 9 — Mauritania, 5AS-LO

## Do it first
3 hens + 4 roosters cost 99 dinars. 2 hens + 3 roosters cost 71
dinars. What does one hen cost, and one rooster? Guess: try a
rooster at 15 dinars. Then 3 hens cost 99 − 60 = 39, so a hen is 13.
Check the second: 2 × 13 + 3 × 15 = 71. It fits! This is Libya's
Grade 9 opener — a price puzzle solved first by a bar model, and
only then algebraically. You have just solved a system by guessing
well. The methods below do the same thing without the guessing.

## Then the explanation
**What a system is.** A system is two (or more) equations that must
hold **at the same time**. A solution is a pair — an ordered pair
(x, y) — that makes **both** equations true at once. One equation
with two unknowns has infinitely many pairs: x + y = 5 is satisfied
by (0, 5), (1, 4), (2, 3) and every other pair that adds to 5.
Kazakhstan's Grade 6 book says this plainly: with b ≠ 0 there are
infinitely many solutions, and you choose x freely and compute y.
The second equation is what pins one pair down. Two equations are
**equivalent** when they have the same solution set, or when both
have none.

**The graphical method.** Each equation is a straight line. Draw
both, and where they cross is the answer. Jordan's Grade 8 book
teaches this first, with a GeoGebra lab for it, and Greece's Grade 9
book does the same: the idea of a linear system and its graphical
solution before any algebra. The crossing is a guess until you check
it: put the pair into **both** equations. If the lines are parallel,
they never cross — no solution. If they are the same line, every
point on it fits — infinitely many solutions.

**Substitution.** Express one variable from one equation and put it
into the other. Kazakhstan's Grade 6 example: from x = 7 − 5y,
substitute into the other equation and solve −7y + 42 = 0, giving
y = 6 and x = −23. Express the easy variable first — the one
standing alone, or with coefficient 1. Jordan's Grade 8 book teaches
substitution as its second method, after the graphical one, and
Kazakhstan's Grade 9 book adds symmetric systems: with p = x + y
and t = xy, a system like x + y = 5, xy = 6 is solved through the
quadratic z² − 5z + 6 = 0.

**Elimination.** Add or subtract the equations so that one variable
cancels. Kazakhstan's Grade 6 book gives the five-step algorithm:
add the equations memberwise when one variable cancels; if equal
terms appear, subtract; if neither cancels, multiply one or both
equations first. Worked: with a ×3 and a ×(−4) to force a
cancellation. Libya's Grade 9 book teaches the same three algebraic
methods in order — equating expressions, substitution, elimination —
and then the graphical interpretation, including infinite and no
solutions. Vietnam's Grade 9 book teaches two-unknown linear
systems, and its Grade 10 study topics extend them to three
unknowns. Mauritania's 5AS-LO book carries equations, inequalities
and systems in one chapter.

## Say it to your companion
One of you sets a system and names the method — graph, substitute,
or eliminate — and the other solves it aloud, move by move, and
checks the pair in both equations. Ten each, then swap. Any pair
that fails one equation is not an answer.

## Do It Yourself
1. Solve Libya's puzzle three ways — guess, substitution,
   elimination: 3 hens + 4 roosters = 99; 2 hens + 3 roosters = 71.
   (Answers: hen 13 dinars, rooster 15 dinars.)
2. Solve graphically, then check: x + y = 5 and x − y = 1.
   (Answer: (3, 2).)
3. Solve by substitution: x = 7 − 5y and the second equation that
   gives −7y + 42 = 0. (Answers: y = 6, x = −23.)
4. Solve by elimination: x + y = 7 and 2x − y = 5. (Answer: (4, 3).)
5. Solve these, and name each case: x + y = 5 with 2x + 2y = 10;
   x + y = 5 with x + y = 6; 2x + 3y = 12 with x − y = 1.
   (Answers: infinitely many; no solution; (3, 2).)
