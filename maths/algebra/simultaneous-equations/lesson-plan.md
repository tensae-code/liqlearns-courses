# Lesson plan — Simultaneous Equations

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 6 — Algebra · **Lesson:** Simultaneous Equations
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 24 of the maths course — the fourth lesson of Unit 6 (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** simultaneous equations — two equations, two unknowns, and the one pair that satisfies both at once.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on equations (Level 22) — each equation alone is already solvable — and on coordinates-and-graphs, which comes later in the course but is used here only as a picture. It needs nothing else.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Systems and the Graphical Method** — what a system's solution is, and drawing both lines and reading the crossing point, checked in both equations. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Solving by Substitution** — expressing one variable from one equation, substituting it into the other, and solving the one-variable equation that results. (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **Solving by Elimination** — adding or subtracting to cancel one variable (multiplying first where needed), and the no-solution and infinite-solutions cases. (Was depth Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson (no hardness ranking is written in this lesson's question files).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: equations — solving one equation in one unknown. Recommended, never forced.
- Two honest limits: South Africa's Grade 9 book states plainly that it teaches no simultaneous equations, and Sierra Leone's JSS never teaches them either. This lesson stands on the countries that do teach them.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a system is and what its solution means — one pair that fits both equations.
2. Solve a system by drawing both lines and reading where they cross.
3. Solve a system by substitution.
4. Solve a system by elimination, and recognise no-solution and infinitely-many-solutions cases.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Squared paper for drawing the lines.

## If the learner is stuck
- Solves one equation and stops → one equation with two unknowns has infinitely many pairs. The second equation is what pins the pair down. Check the pair in BOTH equations.
- Graphs the lines and reads the crossing wrong → check the pair by substitution in both equations. A crossing read off a sketch is a guess until the check confirms it.
- Substitutes and gets a mess → express the easy variable first — the one alone, or with coefficient 1. Kazakhstan's example expresses x = 7 − 5y for exactly this reason.
- Eliminates and loses a sign → when you subtract equations, every sign in the subtracted equation flips. Add when the terms are opposites; subtract when they are the same.
- Gets 0 = 5 → the system has no solution: the lines are parallel and never meet. Gets 0 = 0 → infinitely many solutions: the two equations are the same line.
- This lesson's next door: quadratic-equations (same stage) — where one of the equations can be a curve, and the pair still has to fit both.
