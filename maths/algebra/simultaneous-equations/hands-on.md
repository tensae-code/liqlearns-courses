# Hands-on — Simultaneous Equations

The lesson opens with a puzzle, the way Libya's Grade 9 book opens with one.

## The hens and roosters
3 hens + 4 roosters = 99 dinars; 2 hens + 3 roosters = 71 dinars.
Guess first: try rooster = 15. Then 3 hens = 99 − 60 = 39, so a hen
is 13. Check the second: 2 × 13 + 3 × 15 = 26 + 45 = 71. It fits!
Libya's book solves this puzzle first with a bar model and only then
algebraically. Your guess-and-check is the bar model in numbers.

## The crossing on squared paper
Draw x + y = 5 and x − y = 1 on squared paper. Make a table for
each: x = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5. Plot both lines. They cross at (3, 2).
Check: 3 + 2 = 5, and 3 − 2 = 1. Now draw x + y = 5 and 2x + 2y = 10
on the same paper: one line on top of the other — infinitely many
solutions. And x + y = 5 with x + y = 6: parallel lines, no crossing,
no solution.

## The cancellation race
A partner writes a system on a card. You have one minute to make
one variable cancel — multiply one equation if you must — and say
which variable cancelled and how. First to five cancellations wins;
the full solution can wait, the cancellation is the skill.

## Three ways, one system
Take x + y = 7 and 2x − y = 5. Solve it three ways: graphically,
by substitution, by elimination. All three must give (4, 3). If one
way disagrees, that way has the error — find it.
