# Lesson plan — Expressions

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 6 — Algebra · **Lesson:** Expressions
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 21 of the maths course — the first lesson of Unit 6 (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** algebraic expressions — letters standing for numbers, terms and coefficients, simplifying, expanding brackets, and factorising.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the four operations (Stages 2–3) and on negative-numbers (Level 17), which decides the signs when brackets open. It needs nothing else.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Terms and Like Terms** — naming an expression's terms, coefficients and variables, and collecting like terms without joining unlike ones. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Expanding Brackets** — expanding a number or letter times a bracket and a bracket with a minus in front, checked by substitution. (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **Factorising** — taking out a common factor, factorising by grouping, and the identities — always checking by expanding back. (Was depth Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson (no hardness ranking is written in this lesson's question files).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: negative-numbers — signs when brackets open. Recommended, never forced.
- Factorising is the reverse of expanding, and every book in this lesson says so in those words. South Africa's Grade 9 book puts it as a worksheet title: 'Factorising is the reverse of expanding.'

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Read an algebraic expression: name its terms, its coefficients and its variables.
2. Collect like terms and simplify an expression.
3. Expand brackets, including a minus before the brackets and a number times a bracket.
4. Factorise by taking out a common factor, by grouping, and with the notable identities.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Algebra tiles or paper rectangles if you have them — the books use blocks and card models.

## If the learner is stuck
- Adds 3x + 2y and writes 5xy → only like terms combine. x-terms join x-terms; y-terms join y-terms. 3x + 2y stays 3x + 2y.
- Expands 2(x + 3) as 2x + 3 → the 2 multiplies EVERY term inside: 2x + 6. Draw the rectangle and count both pieces.
- Opens a − (b − c) as a − b − c → a minus before the brackets flips every sign inside: a − b + c. Say it: minus a bracket, change every sign.
- Factorises x² + 5x + 6 as (x + 2)(x + 3) without checking → check by expanding back: (x + 2)(x + 3) = x² + 5x + 6. Expanding back is the proof.
- This lesson's next door: equations (same stage) — an expression with an equals sign, solved by balancing.
