# Hands-on — Expressions

The lesson works with blocks and rectangles, the way South Africa's and Egypt's books work with them.

## Sorting the pile
Write terms on cards: 3x, 2x², 5, −x, 4x, 7, x², −2x. Sort them into
piles — x² pile, x pile, number pile. Add each pile: the x² pile is
3x², the x pile is 6x, the number pile is 12. Unlike terms never join
a pile; that is the whole rule, in your hands.

## The rectangle split
Draw a rectangle 2 units high and (x + 3) wide. Split it into two
pieces: 2 by x, and 2 by 3. Read the area two ways: 2(x + 3), and
2x + 6. The two readings are the same area — that is what expanding
is. Do it again for 3(x − 4) and for x(x + 5).

## The minus flip
Write a − (b − c) on a card. Flip the card: on the back, a − b + c.
Practise with numbers: 10 − (4 − 1) = 7, and 10 − 4 + 1 = 7. The
minus before the bracket flips every sign inside. Ten cards, ten
flips, then swap with a partner.

## Factorise and check
Take (x + 2)(x + 3) = x² + 5x + 6. Cover the answer and factorise
x² + 5x + 6 from scratch: two numbers that multiply to 6 and add to
5 — 2 and 3. Expand back to prove it. Do the same for x² − 9
(difference of two squares: (x − 3)(x + 3)) and for 6x + 9 (common
factor 3: 3(2x + 3)).
