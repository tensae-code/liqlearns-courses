# Lesson plan — Partial Fractions

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 6 — Algebra · **Lesson:** Partial Fractions
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 59 of 136, inside Unit 6 — Algebra (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** splitting one rational fraction into a sum of simpler fractions — one piece per factor of the denominator — and putting them back into one fraction.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on polynomial division (Level 58) — an improper fraction is divided first — and on factorising denominators. It needs nothing else.

exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Distinct Linear Factors** — checking a fraction is proper before splitting, one piece per distinct linear factor, and the constants found by substitution (the "zero's method") or by comparing coefficients. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **A Repeated Linear Factor** — a factor that appears twice demands two pieces — one over the factor, one over its square — and solving for all their constants. (Was the repeated-factor half of depth Level 2.)
3. **A Quadratic Factor** — an irreducible quadratic factor gets a piece with a linear numerator Ax + B, including the repeated-quadratic shape (Pakistan's Cases III and IV). (Was the quadratic-factor part of depth Level 3.)
4. **Divide First When Improper** — an improper fraction is divided first — quotient plus a proper remainder-fraction — and only then split. (Was the divide-first half of depth Level 2.)
5. **Integration and Recombining** — why the split matters: a split fraction integrates piece by piece into logarithms, and the pieces recombine into a single fraction as the check. (Was the integration-and-recombining part of depth Level 3.)

The old depth layers do not stand in the lesson text's order: old Level 2 (repeated factors + divide first) and old Level 3 (quadratic factors + integration and recombining) each split at their natural jumps, and the sub lessons follow the text's order — distinct, repeated, quadratic, divide-first, integration.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson (no hardness ranking is written in this lesson's question files).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: polynomial division (Level 58) — partial fractions only work on a proper fraction, and Level 58 is how an improper one is made proper. Recommended, never forced.
- Recommended first: factorising quadratics and cubics — you cannot write one piece per factor until the denominator is in factors. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Tell a proper fraction from an improper one, and divide an improper one down first (Pakistan, Class 11, Unit 5, pp.78–87; Bangladesh, Class 9, Ch. 4 §10.8, pp.107–111).
2. Split a fraction whose denominator has distinct linear factors, by substituting the values that kill a factor and by comparing coefficients (South Sudan, Secondary 4 Additional, Unit 4, pp.128–135; Pakistan, Class 10, Unit 4, pp.74–84).
3. Split a fraction with a repeated linear factor, writing one piece for each power of the factor (Bangladesh, §10.8, pp.107–111 — its repeated-linear worked example).
4. Split a fraction with an irreducible quadratic factor, giving that piece a linear numerator Ax + B (Pakistan, Class 11, Unit 5 — Cases III–IV, pp.78–87; South Sudan, Unit 4, pp.136–142).
5. Use partial fractions inside integration, for distinct linear factors (Kuwait, Grade 12 Term 2, §5-6, pp.42–49; Jordan, Grade 12, p.23 and its integration use, p.45), and recombine pieces into a single fraction (South Sudan, Unit 4, pp.147–148).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. The factorising and division skills from Levels 58 and the algebra stage, ready to use.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner adds 1/(x + 1) + 2/(x − 1) into one fraction, then is asked the reverse question: can (3x + 1)/(x² − 1) be split back into pieces? (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — proper before you split; distinct linear factors (two ways to find the constants); repeated linear factors; a quadratic factor; divide first when improper; the use in integration; recombining (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the distinct-linear part: a picture of one fraction bar splitting like a branch into two smaller fraction bars, one over (x − 4) and one over (x + 2) — the student writes the two pieces with their unknown letters before the letters are filled in.
   - ADD IMAGE in the repeated-factor part: a picture of the factor (x − 1) wearing two hats, one labelled (x − 1) and one labelled (x − 1)², each with its own fraction piece → the student lists the pieces a repeated factor demands.
   - ADD IMAGE in the integration part: a picture of an integral sign turning into two small log terms, 2 ln|x + 3| + 3 ln|x − 5| → the student says why the split had to come before the integrating.
3. **Say it to your companion** — state the shape for each denominator kind, and why an improper fraction is divided first (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the splitting chant in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — one split of each kind, one divide-first split, and one recombination check (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Writes pieces before checking the fraction is proper → the degree test first: numerator degree must be below denominator degree; if not, divide (Pakistan, Class 11, Unit 5, pp.78–87).
- Gives a repeated factor only one piece → one piece per power: (x − 1)² in the denominator demands pieces over (x − 1) AND over (x − 1)² (Bangladesh, §10.8, pp.107–111).
- Gives a quadratic factor a bare constant on top → an irreducible quadratic piece carries a linear numerator, Ax + B (Pakistan, Cases III–IV).
- South Sudan caution: the South Sudan worked example on printed pp.130–131 is muddled as printed — its working tangles, though its final answers (A = 3/4, B = −1/4) are correct. This lesson teaches the method cleanly and keeps only the verified final answers from that example; never copy that page's working.
