**1. (Sub lesson 1)** (3x + 1)/(x² − 1) is a proper fraction because:
a) its top is smaller in degree than its bottom  b) it has no x² on top  c) it can be factorised
**Answer:** a) — numerator degree 1 is below denominator degree 2, so it may be split at once.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** In (3x + 1)/(x² − 1) = A/(x + 1) + B/(x − 1), the values are:
a) A = 2, B = 1  b) A = 1, B = 2  c) A = 3, B = 1
**Answer:** b) A = 1, B = 2 — x = −1 gives A = 1; x = 1 gives B = 2 (Bangladesh's worked example).

**3. (Sub lesson 1)** The substitution ("zero's") method finds A first by choosing x so that:
a) A becomes zero  b) the other piece becomes zero  c) x becomes zero
**Answer:** b) the other piece — choose x equal to the other factor's root and its piece vanishes.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** The denominator (x − 1)²(x + 1) needs how many pieces?
a) 2  b) 3  c) 4
**Answer:** b) 3 — one over (x − 1), one over (x − 1)², one over (x + 1).

**5. (Sub lesson 4)** (3x² + 1)/(x² − 1) must be divided first because:
a) it is improper — the degrees are equal  b) its denominator factorises  c) its numerator has no x term
**Answer:** a) improper — partial fractions are written for proper fractions; divide first.

**6. (Sub lesson 4)** After dividing, (3x² + 1)/(x² − 1) = 3 + 4/(x² − 1). The split of 4/(x² − 1) is:
a) 2/(x − 1) − 2/(x + 1)  b) 4/(x − 1) − 4/(x + 1)  c) 1/(x − 1) + 3/(x + 1)
**Answer:** a) — rejoining gives (2(x + 1) − 2(x − 1))/(x² − 1) = 4/(x² − 1).

**7. (Sub lesson 3)** The piece over an irreducible quadratic factor (x² + 1) carries on top:
a) a constant A  b) a linear form Ax + B  c) a quadratic Ax² + Bx + C
**Answer:** b) Ax + B — a quadratic piece always wears a numerator one degree below it.

**8. (Sub lesson 5)** ∫ (5x − 1)/((x + 3)(x − 5)) dx, after splitting as in Kuwait's §5-6, equals:
a) 2 ln|x + 3| + 3 ln|x − 5| + C  b) 5 ln|x² − 2x − 15| + C  c) ln|x + 3| + ln|x − 5| + C
**Answer:** a) — the split is 2/(x + 3) + 3/(x − 5), and each piece integrates to a logarithm.
