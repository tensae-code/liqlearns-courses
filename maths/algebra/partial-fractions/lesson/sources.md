**Acceptance count: taught in 14 countries (South Sudan, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Jordan, Kuwait, Syria, Sudan, India, Mongolia, Venezuela, North Macedonia, Afghanistan, Albania, Bolivia).** South Sudan carries the fullest single treatment: its Secondary 4 Additional book gives partial fractions a whole unit (Unit 4, pp.126–148) — linear factors by both constant-finding methods, quadratic and repeated factors, the integration application, and recombining — with one caution: the worked example on printed pp.130–131 is muddled as printed, so this lesson teaches the method cleanly and keeps only its verified final answers, A = 3/4 and B = −1/4. Pakistan carries the topic twice over: Class 10's Unit 4 with its four rules and the "zero's method", and Class 11's Unit 5 with the proper/improper opening and Cases I–IV up to the repeated irreducible quadratic, the technique presented as preparation for calculus. Bangladesh carries it inside its Class 9 polynomial chapter (§10.8, pp.107–111) with verified worked splits for distinct linear, repeated linear, and linear-plus-quadratic denominators, and division-first handling of improper fractions. Jordan carries partial fractions as a named Grade 12 lesson (p.23) and uses it inside integration (p.45, as filed). Kuwait carries the integration use only, and only for distinct linear factors (§5-6, pp.42–49) — a recorded negative: no repeated and no quadratic factors there. North Macedonia enters from its main set — the Grade 12 Algebra elective.

Syria integrates rational functions by partial fractions in Grade 12 Part One, inside its integrals unit.

Sudan teaches partial fractions in Grade 12 Specialized Book One, through distinct and repeated linear factors to the irreducible quadratic case.

India enters partial fractions from Mathematics Class 12: method.


Mongolia enters through its ministry textbooks Математик XII (Grade 12): proper fractions first; distinct linear factors; three linear factors.

Venezuela enters through the Colección Bicentenario, the MPPE's one-book-per-grade course: *La Matemática y el Vivir Bien* (Grade 11). From its books: from Grade 11: (x + 5)/(x² − 4) split as A/(x + 2) + B/(x − 2) with A = −3/4, B = 7/4 by undetermined coefficients.

Afghanistan enters through its Ministry of Education course — the Grade 1 course in two language editions (Pashto and Dari, one work with identical page maps) and Dari student books for Grades 4, 6, 7, 8, 10, 11 and 12, all printing year 1398 HS except the Grade 8 book, which prints 1399 HS on its cover and title page and 1398 HS on its specifications page. From its books: from Grade 12: partial fractions: distinct, repeated and quadratic factors.

Albania enters through its Mediaprint teacher's books (Libër Mësuesi, in Albanian) — TEACHER EVIDENCE only: no pupil's book is in hand for any grade, so Albania is never cited as a pupil-textbook source; what the teacher's books evidence is what is taught and in what order, through their lesson plans, worked answers and answer keys. From its books: from Grade 12 (teacher's book): Elective course: thyesat elementare (Partial fractions); Elective course: faktorët e përsëritur (Partial fractions with repeated linear factors).

Bolivia enters through the Área Matemática sections of its Ministerio de Educación (Estado Plurinacional de Bolivia) Textos de Aprendizaje — the student books for Grades 7, 8, 10 and 12, each the maths section of that year's integrated year book, keeping the whole book's printed foliation, with no imprint or ISBN printed in any of them — and through the Compendio para Maestras y Maestros 2023–2024, the ministry's teacher compilation of the official student texts for all six Secundaria years (Grades 7–12), cited as teacher-compilation evidence only, never as or in place of a pupil text; its Secundaria 3 and Secundaria 5 sections are the only in-hand source for Grades 9 and 11, whose student texts are not in the record. From its books: from Grade 12: Producción teórica (taller).
