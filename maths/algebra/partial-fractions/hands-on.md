# Hands-on — Partial Fractions

The lesson split fractions apart and put them back together, checking both directions.

## Split and rejoin
Take (3x + 1)/(x² − 1). Factorise the denominator, write the two pieces with letters, and find the letters by substitution. Now the partner's job: add your pieces back into one fraction and compare with the original, term by term. The split is only trusted when the rejoin matches.

## The killing-value game
Cards with split-setups: A/(x − 4) + B/(x + 2) with the numerator (5x + 4). One partner "kills" the first piece by naming x = 4 and reads off A; the other kills the second with x = −2 and reads off B. Say out loud why the killed piece vanishes — that is the whole trick.

## Repeated-factor pieces
Write the denominator (x − 1)²(x + 1) on a big sheet. Stick on it one piece-card for every power: a piece over (x − 1), a piece over (x − 1)², a piece over (x + 1). If a card is missing, the split cannot work — find Bangladesh's worked answer for this very denominator in the lesson and check your three constants against it.

## Divide first
Take the improper (3x² + 1)/(x² − 1). Do the division on one side of the page (answer: 3, remainder 4), then split only the proper remainder-fraction 4/(x² − 1) on the other side. Join the two halves into the full answer and check by recombining.
