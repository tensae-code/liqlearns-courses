# Lesson plan — Factorisation

**One thing:** writing an algebraic expression as a product of its factors — the exact reverse of expanding brackets — by common factors, by regrouping, by the standard identities, and by splitting the middle term, up to factorising cubics with the Factor Theorem.

**Unit:** Algebra (Unit 6). **Level 152** (India merge, 2026-11-07).

**Why this lesson exists:** India's Class 8 book teaches factorisation as a complete chapter of its own (Ch. 14, printed pp. 217–229) — definition, four methods in sequence, and a deliberate error-spotting page — and its Class 9 book continues it for quadratics and cubics (printed pp. 41–42). The readers of the India set flagged it as having no home: the course stocked other countries' factorisation as parts inside expressions, because those books treat it as one section of an expressions chapter, but India's treatment is a standalone topic with its own method ladder. Under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06) it gets its own lesson. It stands alone: expressions and quadratic-equations are soft hints around it, never gates.

## The climb

1. **What factorisation is** — the reverse of multiplication: 3(x + 2) expands to 3x + 6, so 3x + 6 factorises to 3(x + 2). Factors that cannot be split further are irreducible. First method: take out the common factor.
2. **Regrouping** — when no factor is common to every term, group the terms so each group has one, then take out the factor the groups now share.
3. **The identities backwards** — spot a² + 2ab + b² = (a + b)², a² − 2ab + b² = (a − b)² and a² − b² = (a + b)(a − b) inside an expression and write the product at once.
4. **Trinomials** — x² + bx + c = (x + a)(x + b) where a and b multiply to c and add to b; take a common factor out first when there is one.
5. **Splitting the middle term (Class 9)** — for ax² + bx + c, split bx into two terms whose coefficients multiply to ac, then regroup.
6. **Cubics with the Factor Theorem (Class 9)** — test values until p(a) = 0 gives a factor (x − a), divide it out, and factorise the quadratic quotient.
7. **Error-spotting woven through** — the Class 8 book's §14.5 page of wrong simplifications, expansions and substitutions, each exhibited to be found and corrected.
