**Acceptance count: taught in 8 countries (India, Venezuela, North Macedonia, Afghanistan, Uzbekistan, Ecuador, Albania, Bolivia).** India's Class 8 book gives factorisation a whole chapter of its own (Ch. 14, printed pp. 217–229) and its Class 9 book returns to it inside Polynomials — quadratics by splitting the middle term, cubics by the Factor Theorem — so under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06) the topic is gathered into its own lesson, at the size of that treatment. Other read countries teach factorisation as a part inside their expressions chapters, and their merges stocked it there; India's own Class 9 identity work ((x + y + z)² and x³ + y³ + z³ − 3xyz, applied to factorise) is likewise stocked in expressions, and its Class 8 division of algebraic expressions in polynomial-division. One slip in the Class 8 chapter is taught corrected: the chapter summary (printed p. 229) prints the trinomial form as "x² + (a + b) + ab", dropping the x — the correct form x² + (a + b)x + ab stands in the formula list on the same page and is the form this lesson teaches. The §14.5 "Can you find the error?" page (printed p. 226) is the book's deliberate wrong-work exercise, not a book error.
