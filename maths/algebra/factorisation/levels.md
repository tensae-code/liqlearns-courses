# Factorisation — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its SUB
LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure — README, "Units, Lessons and Sub
Lessons"). Practice is tied to the skill number, as before. The
questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson
view. The sub lessons are the depths inside this one thing —
factorisation — common factors first, then regrouping and
identities, then trinomials, then quadratics and cubics,
then the error-spotting game, the order the book teaches
it in.

One lesson, one level (Level 152).

## Sub lesson 1 — Taking Out a Common Factor
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Say what factorisation is (the reverse of expanding) and factorise by taking out the common factor, leaving irreducible factors. Pass mark: 4 of 5.

## Sub lesson 2 — Factorisation by Regrouping and Identities
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Factorise four-term expressions by regrouping, and recognise and use the identity forms a² ± 2ab + b² and a² − b². Pass mark: 4 of 5.

## Sub lesson 3 — Factorisation of Trinomials
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Factorise x² + bx + c into (x + a)(x + b), taking a common factor out first when there is one. Pass mark: 4 of 5. (The trinomial half of old Layer 3.)

## Sub lesson 4 — Factorisation of Quadratics and Cubics
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Factorise ax² + bx + c by splitting the middle term, and factorise a cubic by finding one zero with the Factor Theorem, dividing, and factorising the quotient. Pass mark: 4 of 5.

## Sub lesson 5 — Finding the Error
Sub lesson 5 questions from the type files. Spot the planted errors in worked lines set for correction, and correct them by expanding back. Pass mark: 4 of 5. (The error-spotting half of old Layer 3.)

## What "maxed" means here

The learner can take an algebraic expression and write it as a product of irreducible factors by whichever method fits — common factor, regrouping, identities, middle-term splitting, or the Factor Theorem — and can expand the answer back to check it, the exact ladder India's Class 8 factorisation chapter climbs (printed pp. 217–226) with its Class 9 continuation (printed pp. 41–42).
