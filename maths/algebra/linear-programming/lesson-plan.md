# Lesson plan — Linear Programming

**One thing:** making the best choice when several straight-line limits hold at once — write the limits as a system of linear inequalities, shade the feasible region where all of them hold together, and find the best value of an objective function, which always sits at a corner of the region.

**Unit:** Algebra (Unit 6). **Level 139** (Malaysia merge, 2026-11-07).

**Why this lesson exists:** Malaysia's Additional Mathematics Form 5 closes its inequalities chapter with linear programming as a named topic — constraints, the feasible region, the objective function, and maximum/minimum problems. Single inequalities are already a lesson (inequalities, Stage 6); the optimisation method built on a system of them had no home. Under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06), it gets its own lesson.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Writing the Limits** — turning a situation's limits into linear inequalities, including x ≥ 0, y ≥ 0 where they belong. (Was depth Layer 1.)
2. **Drawing the Feasible Region** — drawing every boundary line solid or dashed by its sign, testing a point, and shading the feasible region where every inequality holds at once. (Was depth Layer 2.)
3. **Finding the Optimum** — stating the objective function, evaluating it at each corner of the region, and giving the maximum or minimum in the words of the situation. (Was depth Layer 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson (no hardness ranking is written in this lesson's question files).

## The climb

1. **From a situation to constraints** — read a real situation (so many hours, so much material, at least, at most) and write each limit as a linear inequality, with x ≥ 0 and y ≥ 0 when the quantities cannot be negative.
2. **The system and the feasible region** — draw every boundary line (solid for ≤ and ≥, dashed for < and >), test a point — (0, 0) when it is not on a line — and shade the region that satisfies ALL the inequalities at once.
3. **The objective function** — the quantity to be made biggest or smallest, written ax + by (a profit, a cost, a total).
4. **The optimum is at a corner** — evaluate the objective function at each corner (vertex) of the feasible region; the largest (or smallest) value there is the optimum.
5. **Whole problems** — model, shade, evaluate, and answer in the words of the situation.

## Add-on slots

- slot-linprog-0 (IMAGE): a picture of a shaded polygon on a grid with its corners circled, next to an unshaded copy of the same lines. What the student does: says what the shaded region means — every point inside obeys every limit at once.
- slot-linprog-1 (IMAGE): a picture of a stall-keeper weighing two limits — cloth for shirts and trousers — drawn as two boundary lines crossing. What the student does: points to the corner where the two limits bite at the same time.
- slot-linprog-2 (GIF, 1–2 s): a strip showing a profit line sliding across the feasible region, stopping at the last corner it touches. What the student does: watches, then explains why the best point is always a corner and never the middle of the region.
