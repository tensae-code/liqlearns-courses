# Multiple choice — Linear Programming

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Layer 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Writing the Limits · (Layer 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Drawing the Feasible Region · (Layer 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Finding the Optimum.

1. (Sub lesson 1) "At most 30 kg" for x kg becomes:
   A. x > 30 B. x ≥ 30 C. x ≤ 30 D. x < 30
   **Answer: C** — Malaysia, Additional Mathematics Form 5, Ch. 7 §7.3, printed pp. 234–237.

2. (Sub lesson 2) The boundary line of x + y < 12 is drawn:
   A. Solid B. Dashed C. Doubled D. Not drawn
   **Answer: B** — strict inequalities take dashed boundaries. Malaysia, Additional Mathematics Form 5, Ch. 7 §7.3, printed pp. 237–240.

3. (Sub lesson 3) The maximum of an objective function over a feasible region is found:
   A. At the centre of the region B. At a corner of the region C. On the y-axis only D. By testing every point
   **Answer: B** — Malaysia, Additional Mathematics Form 5, Ch. 7 §7.3, printed pp. 242–244.

4. (Sub lesson 1) In a problem about numbers of items made, x ≥ 0 appears because:
   A. It is always required B. A count cannot be negative C. The book says so D. It makes the region bigger
   **Answer: B** — Malaysia, Additional Mathematics Form 5, Ch. 7 §7.3, printed pp. 234–237.
