**Acceptance count: taught in 14 countries (Kazakhstan, Jordan, Egypt, Greece, Armenia, Vietnam, Sierra Leone, Libya, Mauritania, Laos, Yemen, Indonesia, Nicaragua, Ethiopia).** Kazakhstan carries the fullest treatment — six properties in Grade 6, proved properties and quadratic inequalities in Grade 8. Jordan carries linear inequalities in Grade 8 and compound, absolute-value and two-variable inequalities in Grade 9 (systems of inequalities explicitly not taught). Egypt carries inequalities in Grades 6, 7 and 8, with the situations, the spot-the-mistake item and the compound work. Greece carries first-degree inequalities in Grades 8 and 9. Armenia carries inequalities with square roots in Grade 8. Vietnam carries first-degree inequalities in Grade 9. Sierra Leone carries inequalities in JSS 3, holding the sign-reversal rule to its last lesson. Libya carries inequalities in Grade 11, including second-degree inequalities. Mauritania carries equations, inequalities and systems in its 5AS-LO book, Chapter 3. South Africa is not in this lesson: no inequalities teaching is filed from its Grade 7–9 workbooks. Laos carries inequalities from first-degree and absolute-value inequalities in Grade 7, through systems of inequalities in Grade 8, to quadratic inequalities in Grade 10 and notable inequalities in Grade 11. Yemen carries inequalities from first-degree inequalities in one variable in Grade 7, through Grade 8, to intervals, second-degree inequalities, absolute value and systems of inequalities in Grade 10. Indonesia carries inequalities from linear inequalities in one variable in Grade 7 K13, through linear inequalities in the Grade 8 Merdeka guide (guide-only evidence), to systems of linear equations and inequalities in Grade 10 (Merdeka and Revisi editions); the Grade 7 K13 book's negative-multiplication tables on p. 284 are wrong — the sign DOES flip when multiplying or dividing by a negative — and are never taught as printed. Nicaragua carries inequalities whole in the Grade 10 module (module evidence) — linear, simultaneous, quadratic and absolute-value inequalities with interval notation and number-line graphs — the only book in its set that teaches inequalities; interval notation itself appears earlier, with restricted domains in the Grade 7 student book. Ethiopia carries inequalities from substitution and equivalent inequalities on the number line in Grade 6, through linear inequalities with domain-dependent solution sets and graphs in Grade 8, to linear, systems, absolute-value and quadratic inequalities with applications in Grade 9, quadratic inequalities graphically in Grade 10, and rational inequalities in Grade 11; Exercise 4.9 a–c in Grade 9 are unanswerable as printed and are never presented as solvable.
