Syria teaches inequalities once, in Grade 9 Algebra, with the reversal rule for negative multipliers and number-line solutions, and returns to them at the top level in Grade 12, where polynomial bounds for sin and cos are proved by comparing derivatives and then used with explicit error control.

Malaysia teaches inequalities twice: in one variable in Form 1, with the sign-reversal property stated plainly, and in two variables in Form 4, where systems of inequalities shade out a common region — the same regions its Additional Mathematics Form 5 then optimises over in linear programming.

Rwanda teaches inequalities in Primary 6 — solving one-unknown and compound inequalities with solution sets on a number line, with an extra worked example in its Teacher's Guide — and again in Senior 1, from the inequality symbols and number lines through the sign-reversal rule, double inequalities and word problems.

Sudan teaches inequalities in Grade 8 (the concept, solution sets, the properties, linear and compound inequalities), returns to intervals in Grade 10, and completes the course in Grade 11 with absolute-value inequalities, inequalities in two variables and systems.

India enters inequalities from Mathematics Class 11: meaning and rules.
