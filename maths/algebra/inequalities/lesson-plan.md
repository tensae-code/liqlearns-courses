# Lesson plan — Inequalities

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 6 — Algebra · **Lesson:** Inequalities
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 23 of the maths course — the third lesson of Unit 6 (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** inequalities — saying less than and greater than, solving them like equations, and showing the answer on a number line.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on equations (Level 22) — the same moves, with one new rule — and on comparing-and-ordering (Unit 1). It needs nothing else.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: equations — balancing both sides. Recommended, never forced.
- The one new rule is the whole lesson's hinge, and Sierra Leone's JSS 3 book holds it back to its last inequalities lesson on purpose: multiplying or dividing an inequality by a negative number reverses the symbol.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Read and write the four inequality symbols, and write an inequality from a real situation.
2. Show an inequality's answer on a number line, with open and closed circles in the right places.
3. Solve one-variable inequalities, including the sign flip when multiplying or dividing by a negative.
4. Solve compound inequalities and show a two-variable inequality on a graph.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A number line from −10 to 10, and a second one for graphing two-variable inequalities.

## If the learner is stuck
- Writes x < 2 for '2 or more' → read the words first: 'or more' means ≥. The symbol points at the smaller side; the line under it means 'or equal to'.
- Draws a closed circle for x > 2 → open means not included, closed means included. x > 2 leaves 2 out; x ≥ 2 keeps it in.
- Multiplies by −2 and keeps the sign → the flip is the rule: multiplying or dividing by a negative reverses the symbol. Test with numbers: 2 < 3, but −4 > −6.
- Solves 0x > 9 and writes x > 9 → 0x is 0, and 0 is never more than 9: no solutions. And 0x > −9 is true for every number.
- This lesson's next door: simultaneous-equations (same unit) — two equations at once, where inequalities become regions on a graph.
