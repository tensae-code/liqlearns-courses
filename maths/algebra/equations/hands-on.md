# Hands-on — Equations

The lesson balances both sides, the way South Africa's Grade 8 book balances them.

## The real balance
Put a bag of unknown weight and a 5-weight on one side of a balance
scale (or draw it), and a −4 weight on the other — use a picture
weight for the negative, or agree that a balloon pulls up. Add and
take away the same from both sides until the bag stands alone. Say
each move aloud: "take 5 from both sides."

## The move cards
Write the moves on cards: "add 3 to both sides", "subtract 5 from
both sides", "divide both sides by 2", "multiply both sides by 4".
A partner sets an equation; you pick the cards in order and play
them, writing each new line. The cards run out when x stands alone.

## The check habit
Solve five equations, then check all five by substitution before
anyone says "done". The check is part of the solving, not an extra:
Kazakhstan's book solves a word problem about two rectangles and
checks it with 288 = 288. Your checks should end in a line like
that — two equal numbers.

## The story shop
Write three word problems of your own — a gym membership, a phone
bill, a rectangle — turn each into an equation, solve it, and check
the answer against the story. Egypt's Grade 8 book does exactly
this with a gym membership (100 joining fee, 20 per session, a 300
budget — how many days?) and with two car-rental companies. Steal
their shapes; use your own numbers.
