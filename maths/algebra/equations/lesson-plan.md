# Lesson plan — Equations

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 6 — Algebra · **Lesson:** Equations
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 22 of the maths course — the second lesson of Unit 6 (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** solving equations in one variable — balancing both sides until the unknown stands alone.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on expressions (Level 21) and on the four operations (Units 2–3). It needs nothing else.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees

1. **One-Step Equations and Balancing** — what an equation and its root are, and solving one-step equations by balancing both sides, checked by substitution. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Two-Step and Multi-Step Equations** — collecting the x-terms on one side and the numbers on the other, and the no-root and every-number cases. (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **Brackets, Fractions and Word Problems** — clearing brackets and fractions before solving, and turning a word problem into an equation and checking it against the story. (Was depth Level 3.)

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: expressions — simplifying and expanding before you solve. Recommended, never forced.
- Every book in this lesson solves by the same idea — do the same thing to both sides — but the books name it differently: South Africa calls it balancing, Kazakhstan calls it transposition, Egypt calls it the equality properties. One idea, three names.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what an equation is, what its root is, and what it means to solve it.
2. Solve one-step and two-step equations by balancing, and check the answer by substitution.
3. Solve multi-step equations, including brackets and fractions, by clearing and collecting.
4. Turn a word problem into an equation, solve it, and check it against the story.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A balance picture (or a real balance scale) for the first steps.

## If the learner is stuck
- Adds to one side only → the scale tips. Whatever you do to one side, do to the other. Check by substitution: the two sides must give the same number.
- Moves 3x across and keeps it +3x → crossing the equals sign changes the sign. +3x on the left becomes −3x on the right. Kazakhstan calls this transposition; South Africa calls it balancing — same move.
- Solves 0x = 4 and writes x = 4 → 0 times anything is 0, never 4: no root, the solution set is empty. And 0x = 0 has every number as a root.
- Forgets to check a fraction equation → multiply through first (clear the fractions), then solve, then substitute back. South Africa's worked example: (2x − 1)/4 = 1 gives x = 2½.
- This lesson's next door: inequalities (same unit) — the same moves, but the sign flips when you multiply or divide by a negative.
