# Equations — the lesson

Course: Maths · Unit 6 · Lesson: Equations
Built from: Kazakhstan, Grade 6 (Atamura 2018) — Jordan, Grade 7 Term 1 — Greece, Grade 7 and Grade 8 — South Africa, Grade 8 Book 1 and Grade 9 Book 1 — Armenia, Grade 7 — Egypt, Grade 6 Term 1 and Grade 8 Term 2 — Libya, Grade 9 — Vietnam, Grade 8 — Sierra Leone, JSS 2 and JSS 3 — Ghana, JICA Basic 5 teacher manual (teacher evidence only)

## Do it first
A bag sits on a balance scale with a 5-weight beside it. The other
side holds a weight of −4 (imagine a balloon pulling up). The scale
is level. How heavy is the bag? Take 5 from both sides and the bag
stands alone: −9. You have solved x + 5 = −4 — South Africa's Grade
8 book works this exact equation as its balancing example.

## Sub lesson 1 — One-Step Equations and Balancing
**What an equation is.** An equation is a statement that two things
are equal, with an unknown inside it. In ax + b = 0, a is the
coefficient of the variable and b is the free term. The **root** is
the value that makes the two sides equal. To solve an equation is to
find all its roots, or to prove there are none. Greece's Grade 7
book starts smaller: six elementary forms only — α + x = β,
x − α = β, α − x = β, αx = β, α : x = β, x : α = β — and word
problems set up and solved with those forms. Egypt's Grade 6 book
starts smaller still: one-step equations only, 3 + x = 12 and
6x = 18.

**Balancing.** South Africa calls the method balancing, and it is
one rule: do the same thing to both sides. x + 5 = −4: subtract 5
from both sides, x = −9. 3x = 18: divide both sides by 3, x = 6.
Kazakhstan calls the same moves transposition and states them as
equivalence rules: add or subtract the same number or expression on
both sides, or multiply or divide both sides by the same non-zero
number — and warns that multiplying both sides by 0 destroys
equivalence. Check by substitution: put the root back in and the
two sides must give the same number.

## Sub lesson 2 — Two-Step and Multi-Step Equations

**Multi-step equations.** Collect the x-terms on one side and the
numbers on the other, then divide by the coefficient. Kazakhstan's
worked self-check: 8x − 9 = 3x + 8. Move 3x left (it becomes −3x)
and −9 right (it becomes +9): 5x = 17, so x = 17/5, which is 3.4.
Jordan's Grade 7 book teaches one-variable linear equations the
same way, one step first and then more. Sierra Leone's JSS 2 book
adds the case learners meet next: the variable on **both** sides,
which is exactly what 8x − 9 = 3x + 8 already is.

**The three cases.** A linear equation ax + b = 0 has three possible
endings, and Kazakhstan prints all three: if a ≠ 0, one root,
x = −b/a; if a = 0 and b ≠ 0, no root — the solution set is empty;
if a = 0 and b = 0, every number is a root. So 0x = 4 has no answer,
and 0x = 0 has every answer. Sorting equations into "no root" and
"every number is a root" is one of Kazakhstan's exercises.

## Sub lesson 3 — Brackets, Fractions and Word Problems

**Brackets and fractions.** Clear them first. Expand the brackets,
then collect. With fractions, multiply everything through by the
common denominator: South Africa's Grade 9 book works (2x − 1)/4 = 1
step by step — multiply through by 4, 2x − 1 = 4, x = 2½. Egypt's
Grade 8 book solves 6x + 2 = 4x − 4 the same way and gets x = −3,
and 5(√3x − 7) + 11 = 6, which gives x = 2√3. Libya's Grade 9 book
solves equations containing algebraic fractions, and its Grade 10
book solves equations containing powers and surds.

**Word problems.** Turn the story into an equation, solve it, check
it against the story. Kazakhstan solves a two-rectangle problem and
checks it with 288 = 288. Egypt's Grade 8 book asks: a gym charges
a 100 joining fee and 20 per session, with a 300 monthly budget —
how many sessions? (100 + 20n = 300, so n = 10.) And two car-rental
companies, 900 a day + 1.5 per km against 1100 a day + 1 per km —
how far before the first stops being cheaper? The equation is the
translation; the check is the story saying yes.

## Say it to your companion
One of you sets an equation — start one-step, then two-step, then
with a bracket or a fraction — and the other solves it aloud, move
by move, naming each move: "subtract 5 from both sides", "divide by
3". The setter checks the answer by substitution. Ten each, then
swap.

## Do It Yourself
1. Solve and check: x + 7 = 2; 4x = 20; x − 9 = −3; x/5 = 4.
   (Answers: −5; 5; 6; 20.)
2. Solve 8x − 9 = 3x + 8, and check by substitution. (Answer: 3.4.)
3. Solve (2x − 1)/4 = 1. (Answer: 2½.)
4. A gym charges a 100 joining fee and 20 per session. With a 300
   budget, how many sessions? (Answer: 10.)
5. Solve these, and say which case each is: 5x + 3 = 0; 0x + 4 = 0;
   0x + 0 = 0; 2(x − 3) = 8; 3x + 2 = 3x + 5.
   (Answers: −0.6; no root; every number; 7; no root — 2 = 5 is false.)
