# Mathematical Logic — short answer

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Propositions and Connectives · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Truth Tables, Equivalence and De Morgan · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Quantifiers, Open Sentences and Inference.

Answer in a word or a short line.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Is "Please pass the salt" a proposition? Say why in a few words.
**Answer:** No — it is a request; it is neither true nor false (Laos, Grade 10, Ch.I L1, p.1).

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** Give the truth value of "7 = 4 + 5".
**Answer:** False — 4 + 5 = 9, not 7 (Yemen, Grade 10 Part 1, Unit 1, p.7).

**3. (Sub lesson 1)** With p: "6 is even" and q: "6 is a multiple of 4", give the truth value of p∨q.
**Answer:** True — p is true, and "or" needs only one true side (Sierra Leone, SSS 2 Term 1, L45, p.115).

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** How many rows has the truth table of a compound of four propositions?
**Answer:** 16 — 2⁴ rows (Laos, Grade 10, Ch.I L1).

**5. (Sub lesson 2)** Rewrite ~(p∧q) using De Morgan.
**Answer:** ~p∨~q — "not (both)" is "at least one not" (Laos, Grade 10, Ch.I L2, p.14).

**6. (Sub lesson 2)** (p∧q)∧~q is true in no row at all. What is it called?
**Answer:** A contradiction — false in every row (Laos, Grade 10, Ch.I L2, pp.14–19).

**7. (Sub lesson 3)** Negate: "There exists a number whose square is negative."
**Answer:** For every number, its square is not negative — ~(∃x, P(x)) is ∀x, ~P(x) (Iran, Statistics and Probability Grade 11, Ch.1, p.13).

**8. (Sub lesson 3)** Give a counterexample to the converse of "If a number ends in 0, it is even."
**Answer:** 12 — it is even but does not end in 0, so the converse is false (Armenia, Grade 12 logic book, §6, p.36).
