# Mathematical Logic — ordering

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Propositions and Connectives · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Truth Tables, Equivalence and De Morgan · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Quantifiers, Open Sentences and Inference.

Put the items in the right order.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Put the steps of judging a compound proposition in order: (A) Judge the smallest joins first. (B) Read off the final truth value. (C) Write down the truth value of each simple proposition. (D) Work outward to the biggest join.
**Answer:** C, A, D, B — values first, then inside-out, as the tree method does (Laos, Grade 10, Ch.I L1).

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** Put the rows of the (p, q) truth table in the standard order, by p then q: (A) F, F (B) T, F (C) T, T (D) F, T
**Answer:** C, B, D, A — TT, TF, FT, FF, so no case is missed or doubled (Laos, Grade 10, Ch.I L1).

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Order these by the number of truth-table rows, smallest first: (A) a compound of 3 propositions (B) a compound of 1 proposition (C) a compound of 4 propositions (D) a compound of 2 propositions
**Answer:** B, D, A, C — 2, 4, 8, 16 rows by the 2ⁿ rule (Laos, Grade 10, Ch.I L1).

**4. (Sub lesson 3)** Put the parts of Armenia's logic book in their teaching order: (A) necessary and sufficient conditions (B) statements, true and false (C) definition, axiom, theorem (D) converse and opposite assertions
**Answer:** B, C, D, A — §§1, 4, 6, 7 of the Grade 12 logic book (pp.9, 24, 36, 52).
