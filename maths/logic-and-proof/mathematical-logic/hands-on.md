# Hands-on — Mathematical Logic

The lesson judged sentences, joined them, and tabulated them, the way the books open the topic.

## Proposition hunt
Write ten sentences from your day: five that can be judged true or false, and five that cannot (a question, a command, a wish, a greeting, a request). Swap lists with a partner and mark each one P (proposition) or not-P, with the truth value for every P. This is the opening sort of Laos's Grade 10 logic chapter (Ch.I L1, p.1).

## Two switches, one lamp
Draw two switches in a row on one wire to a lamp (series), then two switches on two branches (parallel). For each drawing, list the four switch settings and mark when the lamp lights. Series lights only when BOTH switches are on — that is ∧. Parallel lights when AT LEAST ONE is on — that is ∨. Laos's book models conjunction and disjunction with exactly these circuits (Grade 10, Ch.I L1, pp.1–13).

## The four-row table by hand
Take p: "n is even" and q: "n is a multiple of 3", with n = 6. Write the truth values of p, q, p∧q, p∨q, p⇒q, p⇔q and ~p. Then repeat for n = 4 and n = 5. You have built three rows of the tables the books build in full (Iraq, Grade 10 Scientific, Ch.1, pp.4–20).

## Negate the row of pencils
Line up pencils so that "every pencil is red" is true. Say its negation before touching anything: "there is a pencil that is not red." Now make the negation true by swapping one pencil. Then start from "there is a blue pencil" and negate that: "every pencil is not blue." The negation rules are Laos's (Ch.I L3, p.20) and Iran's (Statistics and Probability, Ch.1, p.13).
