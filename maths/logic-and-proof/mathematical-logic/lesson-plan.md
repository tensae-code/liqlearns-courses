# Lesson plan — Mathematical Logic

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 13 — Logic and Proof · **Lesson:** Mathematical Logic
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 100 of 136, Unit 13 — Logic and Proof (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** mathematical logic — propositions and their truth values, the connectives (not, and, or, exclusive or, if…then, if and only if), truth tables, logical equivalence and De Morgan's laws, tautologies and contradictions, open sentences, and the quantifiers ∀ and ∃ with their negations.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the sets lesson and the expressions lesson — the learner can already read symbols and say whether a sentence about numbers is true. It needs nothing else.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Decide whether a sentence is a proposition and give its truth value, the way Laos's Grade 10 book opens its logic chapter (Ch.I L1, p.1) and Yemen's Grade 10 book opens with "Taiz is a city in Yemen" true and 7 = 4 + 5 false (Unit 1, p.7).
2. Join propositions with ∧, ∨, exclusive or, ⇒, ⇔ and ¬, and read each compound sentence aloud, as Iraq's Grade 10 Scientific book teaches in its first chapter (Ch.1, pp.4–20).
3. Build a truth table for a compound proposition and know it has 2ⁿ rows for n propositions, as Laos's Grade 10 book teaches (Ch.I L1, pp.1–13).
4. Show two propositions equivalent by their truth tables, and use De Morgan's laws ~(p∨q) ≡ ~p∧~q and ~(p∧q) ≡ ~p∨~q, as Cambodia's Grade 10 Part 1 book reaches De Morgan (Ch.1, p.17) and Laos's Grade 10 book proves the laws by table (Ch.I L2, p.14).
5. Recognise a tautology and a contradiction, and rewrite p⇒q as ~p∨q and as its contrapositive ~q⇒~p, as Laos's Grade 10 book teaches (Ch.I L2, pp.14–19).
6. Use ∀ and ∃ in open sentences and negate a quantified statement correctly, as Laos's Grade 10 book teaches (Ch.I L3, p.20), Iraq's Grade 10 Scientific book teaches (Ch.1 §1-7, pp.4–20) and Iran's Statistics and Probability book teaches (Ch.1, pp.11–13).
7. Say what "definition", "axiom" and "theorem" mean, and tell a converse from the statement itself, as Armenia's Grade 12 logic book teaches (pp.24, 36).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Two switches and a lamp drawn on paper help for the circuit part — no real wiring is needed.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner judges six sentences true, false, or "not a proposition at all" before any rule is given (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — propositions; the connectives; truth tables; equivalence and De Morgan; tautology and contradiction; open sentences and quantifiers; definition, axiom, theorem (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the truth-table part: a picture of a truth table filling itself row by row, the last column settling into its pattern — the student predicts each next row before it lands.
   - ADD IMAGE at the circuit part: a picture of two switches in series lighting one lamp, beside two switches in parallel lighting one lamp → the student says which picture is "and" and which is "or", as Laos's book models them.
   - ADD IMAGE at the quantifier part: a picture of a row of pencils, all red, with one blue pencil crossed out and restored → the student says the ∀ sentence, then its negation with ∃.
3. **Say it to your companion** — trade propositions and negations; each partner must negate the other's quantified sentence (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — logic in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching, ordering — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — judge, join, tabulate and negate five statements of your own (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Calls a question or a command a proposition → a proposition must be able to be true or false; "Close the door" is neither, as Laos's book lists questions, commands, requests and exclamations as non-propositions (Ch.I L1, p.1).
- Thinks "or" always shuts the other one out → the books keep two ors apart: ∨ allows both, and the exclusive or is its own connective (Laos, Ch.I L1, pp.1–13).
- Gets p⇒q wrong → it is false in exactly one row: p true and q false. Every other row is true (Iraq, Ch.1 §1-2, pp.4–20; Laos, Ch.I L1).
- Negates "all" as "none" → the negation of ∀ is ∃ with the inside negated: "not all" means "there is one that is not" (Laos, Ch.I L3, p.20; Iran, Statistics and Probability, Ch.1, p.13).
- This lesson's next door: Proof Methods (Level 101, same unit) — valid arguments built from these connectives.
