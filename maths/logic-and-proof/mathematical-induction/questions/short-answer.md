# Mathematical Induction — short answer

Answer in a word or a short line.

**1. (Level 1)** State the induction hypothesis for a proof about P(n), in one line.
**Answer:** "Suppose P(k) is true for some natural k" (Laos, Grade 11, Ch.2 L7, p.28).

**2. (Level 1)** What is 1 + 3 + 5 + 7, and which square does the pattern predict?
**Answer:** 16 = 4² — the sum of the first n odd numbers is n² (Cambodia, Grade 11 Advanced, pp.24–34).

**3. (Level 2)** In the step for Σk = n(n + 1)/2, write the target equation for k + 1.
**Answer:** 1 + 2 + … + (k + 1) = (k + 1)(k + 2)/2 (Kazakhstan, Abylkasymova Algebra 9, §18, p.153).

**4. (Level 2)** In the 6²ⁿ − 1 proof, the step writes 6²⁽ᵏ⁺¹⁾ − 1 = 36 · 6²ᵏ − 1. How does 35 appear?
**Answer:** 36 · 6²ᵏ − 1 = 36(6²ᵏ − 1) + 35, and the bracket is divisible by 35 by the hypothesis (Kazakhstan, Abylkasymova Algebra 9, §18, p.153).

**5. (Level 3)** Compute n² − n + 41 at n = 41.
**Answer:** 1681 = 41² — composite, so Euler's "always prime" claim is false (Pakistan, Class 11, Unit 8, pp.140–150).

**6. (Level 3)** A "proof" checks P(1), P(2), …, P(40) and stops. What is missing?
**Answer:** The induction step — checking cases never proves "for all n" (Pakistan, Class 11, Unit 8, pp.140–150).

**7. (Level 2)** How many diagonals has a convex hexagon, by the formula n(n − 3)/2?
**Answer:** 9 — 6 × 3 / 2 (Kazakhstan, Abylkasymova Algebra 9, §18, p.153).

**8. (Level 3)** Iraq's Grade 12 Scientific book proves De Moivre's theorem by induction at pp.42–43. In what terms is that proof filed?
**Answer:** "For information only" — as filed, and recorded in those terms (Iraq, Grade 12 Scientific, pp.42–43).
