# Mathematical Induction — ordering

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — The Principle and the Three Steps · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Series and Divisibility Proofs · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Extended Induction, Inequalities and the Counter-Example Warning.

Put the items in the right order.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Put the steps of an induction proof in order: (A) Conclude the statement holds for all n. (B) Check the statement for n = 1. (C) Prove the statement for n = k + 1. (D) Suppose the statement holds for n = k.
**Answer:** B, D, C, A (Laos, Grade 11, Ch.2 L7, p.28).

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** Put the lines of the n² step in order: (A) = (k + 1)². (B) 1 + 3 + … + (2k − 1) + (2k + 1). (C) = k² + (2k + 1), by the hypothesis. (D) So the formula holds for k + 1.
**Answer:** B, C, A, D (Cambodia, Grade 11 Advanced, pp.24–34).

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Order these sums, smallest first: (A) 1 + 2 + … + 10 (B) 1 + 3 + 5 (C) 1 + 2 + … + 4 (D) 1 + 3 + 5 + 7 + 9
**Answer:** B, C, D, A — 9, 10, 25, 55 (Cambodia, pp.24–34; Kazakhstan, Yelemessov §3.1, p.80).

**4. (Sub lesson 3)** Put the events of the Euler story in order: (A) The value at n = 41 turns out to be 41². (B) The values for n = 1 to 40 are all prime. (C) The claim "always prime" is given up. (D) The claim "always prime" is proposed.
**Answer:** D, B, A, C (Pakistan, Class 11, Unit 8, pp.140–150).
