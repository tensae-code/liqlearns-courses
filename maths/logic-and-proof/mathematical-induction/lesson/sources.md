**Acceptance count: taught in 9 countries (Pakistan, Armenia, Vietnam, Greece, Cambodia, Kazakhstan, Laos, Iraq, Syria).** Pakistan carries the fullest single unit: the principle and extended induction, Euler's n² − n + 41 as the standing warning against proof by checking, and series, divisibility, integrality and inequality proofs, with the binomial theorem itself proved by induction (Unit 8, pp.140–168). Armenia teaches the method in both Grade 11 streams, the natural-mathematics stream adding a section of further applications. Vietnam teaches it in its Grade 10 study book as the first formal proof method of the series and at once proves Newton's binomial by it. Greece carries it inside the number-theory chapter of its Grade 11 orientation book. Cambodia opens its Grade 11 Advanced book with sequences and induction, proving the sum of the first n odd numbers in full. Kazakhstan teaches it in four separate Grade 9 Algebra books — with the domino motivation and the series formula (Yelemessov), a starred formal treatment that dissects a false proof (Shynybekov), a full formal section with divisibility and the polygon-diagonals formula (Abylkasymova), and an axiom-style four-step scheme with error-hunting exercises (Soltan). Laos states the principle and its three steps as the opening lesson of its Grade 11 induction chapter. Iraq's Grade 12 Scientific book proves De Moivre's theorem by induction at pp.42–43, filed "for information only" and recorded here in exactly those terms.

Syria gives proof by mathematical induction its canonical place in Grade 12 Part One's sequences unit — the ladder principle, the two steps, and a full exercise apparatus including heredity-without-base-case exposed as insufficient.
