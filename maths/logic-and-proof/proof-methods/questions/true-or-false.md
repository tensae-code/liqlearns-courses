# Proof Methods — true or false

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — The Deductive Method and the Shape of a Proof · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Direct Proof, Two Columns, Contradiction and Contrapositive · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Proving and Refuting ∀ and ∃; Necessary and Sufficient.

Say true or false, and be ready to say why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Checking a claim on a hundred examples proves it for all cases.
**Answer:** False — examples cannot prove a ∀ claim; one counterexample can refute it (Iran, Grade 9, Ch.3, p.33).

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** In a proof, the "required" part is what you are given at the start.
**Answer:** False — the required part is what must be shown; the givens are the start (Kuwait, Grade 9 Term 2, Unit 7, pp.135–199).

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** A proof by contradiction ends as soon as any hard step appears.
**Answer:** False — it ends when the assumption forces an impossibility, and then the conclusion must be stated (Kazakhstan, Turapbekov Geometry 7, §3, p.36).

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** Proving the contrapositive proves the original implication.
**Answer:** True — p⇒q and ~q⇒~p are equivalent (Laos, Grade 10, Ch.I L3, pp.20–29).

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Failing to find a witness refutes an ∃ statement.
**Answer:** False — refuting ∃x, P(x) means proving ∀x, ~P(x), a full proof (Armenia, Grade 12 logic book, §5, p.30).

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** A necessary condition for Q must hold whenever Q holds.
**Answer:** True — that is what "necessary" means: Q⇒P (Armenia, Grade 12 logic book, §7, p.52).
