# Proof Methods — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Level 1)** In the deductive method, a new theorem may be built only from:
a) Measurements of many examples  b) Definitions, axioms and results already proved  c) The teacher's say-so
**Answer:** b) Definitions, axioms and results already proved — proofs are the point of the book (Greece, Euclidean Geometry Vol. A, Ch.1).

**2. (Level 1)** In Kuwait's proof frame, the three parts in order are:
a) Proof / givens / required  b) Required / proof / givens  c) Givens / required / proof
**Answer:** c) Givens / required / proof — the frame used through Unit 7 (Kuwait, Grade 9 Term 2, Unit 7, pp.135–199).

**3. (Level 1)** Which single find refutes "Every prime number is odd"?
a) The prime 7  b) The prime 2  c) The number 9
**Answer:** b) The prime 2 — one counterexample kills a ∀ claim (Armenia, Grade 12 logic book, §5, p.30).

**4. (Level 2)** A proof by contradiction begins by assuming:
a) The statement is true  b) The statement is false  c) An easier statement
**Answer:** b) The statement is false — then reason to an impossibility (Kazakhstan, Turapbekov Geometry 7, §3, p.36).

**5. (Level 2)** In a two-column proof, the right-hand column holds:
a) The diagrams  b) The justifications  c) The answers
**Answer:** b) The justifications — Pasos on the left, Justificación on the right (Nicaragua, Grade 9).

**6. (Level 2)** "q is true, and p⇒q, so p is true" is:
a) Modus ponens  b) Modus tollens  c) The fallacy of affirming the consequent
**Answer:** c) The fallacy of affirming the consequent — Laos names it invalid (Grade 10, Ch.I L3, pp.20–29).

**7. (Level 3)** To prove ∃x, P(x), it is enough to:
a) Check many x  b) Produce one x and check P(x) for it  c) Prove ∀x, P(x)
**Answer:** b) Produce one x and check P(x) for it — a witness proves an existence claim (Armenia, Grade 12 logic book, §5, p.30).

**8. (Level 3)** "n is even" is, for "n is a multiple of 4":
a) Sufficient but not necessary  b) Necessary but not sufficient  c) Neither
**Answer:** b) Necessary but not sufficient — every multiple of 4 is even, but 6 is even without being a multiple of 4 (Armenia, Grade 12 logic book, §7, p.52).
