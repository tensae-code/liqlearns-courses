# Lesson plan — Proof Methods

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 13 — Logic and Proof · **Lesson:** Proof Methods
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 101 of 136, Unit 13 — Logic and Proof (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** how mathematicians prove things — the deductive method from definitions and axioms, the shape of a proof (givens, what is required, the chain of reasons), direct proof, the two-column layout, proof by contradiction and by contrapositive, proving and refuting "for all" and "there exists" statements, and necessary and sufficient conditions.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Mathematical Logic (Level 100, same stage) — the learner can already read connectives, build truth tables and negate quantified statements. It needs nothing else.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what the deductive method is — each new result following from definitions, axioms and results already proved — the way Greece's Euclidean Geometry Volume A opens (Ch.1) and Kazakhstan's Geometry 7 books open their courses (Smirnov & Tuyakov, Ch.1 §1, p.5; Shynybekov, introduction, RU pp.5–6).
2. Set out a proof in the givens / required / proof shape, as Kuwait's Grade 9 Term 2 book uses throughout its triangle unit (Unit 7, pp.135–199).
3. Write a short direct proof in two columns — steps on the left, justifications on the right — as Nicaragua's Grade 9 book writes its similarity proofs in Pasos / Justificación columns and its Grade 8 book introduces two-column-style reasoning (Grade 8, Unit 6, pp.105–124).
4. Follow Egypt's "Steps to Write a Proof" in order when proving lines parallel (Grade 7 Term 1, Unit 4-2, p.81).
5. Prove a statement by contradiction — assume the opposite, reach an impossibility, conclude — as Kazakhstan's Turapbekov Geometry 7 gives the method its own section (§3, p.36) and Armenia's Geometry 7 uses it inside the third congruence criterion (Ch.II §9, pp.34–76).
6. Prove a statement by proving its contrapositive instead, and test an argument for the affirming-the-consequent fallacy, as Laos's Grade 10 book teaches validity (Ch.I L3, pp.20–29).
7. Prove a ∀ statement by taking an arbitrary member, refute a ∀ statement by one counterexample, and prove an ∃ statement by producing a witness, as Armenia's Grade 12 logic book teaches ( §5, p.30).
8. Say whether a condition is necessary, sufficient, both, or neither, as Armenia's Grade 12 logic book closes ( §7, p.52).
9. Diagnose a flawed printed proof and say which step smuggled in an unjustified claim, the exercise Iran's Grade 9 book sets with its bisector proof (Ch.3, p.37).

## If the learner is stuck
- Proves by examples → Iran's Grade 9 opener: the senses and examples deceive; one counterexample (the obtuse triangle's altitudes) kills a "for all" claim, but a hundred drawings cannot prove one (Ch.3, p.33).
- Writes a proof as one long paragraph and loses the thread → split it the Kuwait way: write the givens, write what is required, then the chain (Unit 7, pp.135–199); or the Nicaragua way: one step per row, reason beside it.
- Assumes what must be proved → that is the flaw Iran's Grade 9 book prints on purpose at p.37: a step used Ĉ₁ = Ĉ₂ before anything gave it. Every step needs a reason that is already earned.
- Reaches a contradiction and stops without concluding → the contradiction is not the end; the end is the sentence "so the assumption is false, and the statement is true" (Turapbekov, Geometry 7, §3, p.36).
- This lesson's next door: Mathematical Induction (Level 102, same stage) — the one proof method built for statements about all natural numbers.
