# Hands-on — Proof Methods

The lesson built proofs the books' ways: givens first, then the chain of reasons.

## Kill the claim, then prove one
Claim: "The altitudes of every triangle meet at a point inside the triangle." Draw an acute triangle and check it holds. Then draw a wide obtuse triangle and draw its altitudes carefully — they meet outside. One drawing has refuted the claim, exactly as Iran's Grade 9 book opens its proof chapter (Ch.3, p.33). Now change the claim to "The altitudes of every acute triangle meet inside" and say, in words, what a proof would still have to do that no drawing can.

## The vertical-angles proof, two ways
Draw two crossing lines and label the angles 1, 2, 3 around the crossing. First write the proof as Kuwait's book frames it: Givens — two lines cross. Required — angle 1 = angle 3. Proof — the chain. Then rewrite the same proof in two columns, steps left and justifications right, in the Pasos / Justificación style of Nicaragua's Grade 9 book. China's Grade 6 review proves this same result among its mathematical-thinking items (Grade 6 Volume 2, review, pp.100–104).

## Suppose the opposite
Prove: "If n² is even, then n is even" — by contradiction. Assume n is odd, write n = 2k + 1, square it, and watch n² come out odd, against the given. Write the closing sentence in full: "So the assumption is false, and n is even." This is the method Kazakhstan's Turapbekov Geometry 7 gives its own section ( §3, p.36).

## Necessary, sufficient, neither — a sorting game
Make four cards: "n is a multiple of 4", "n is even", "n ends in 0", "n is a multiple of 3". For the claim "n is even", sort each other card: does it guarantee evenness (sufficient)? Must it hold whenever n is even (necessary)? Both? Neither? Check each sort with numbers before you settle it — the classification game of Armenia's Grade 12 logic book ( §7, p.52).
