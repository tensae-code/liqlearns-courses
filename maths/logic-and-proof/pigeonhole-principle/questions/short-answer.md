# The pigeonhole principle — short answer

Answer in a word or a short line.

**1. (Level 1)** State the pigeonhole principle in one sentence.
**Answer:** If more things go into fewer boxes, some box must hold at least two of them.

**2. (Level 1)** Thirteen people are in a room. Why must two of them share a birth month?
**Answer:** There are only 12 months (the boxes) and 13 people (the things) — 13 = 12 + 1, so one month is used at least twice (Iran, Discrete Mathematics, p.82).

**3. (Level 2)** China works 7 ÷ 3 for seven things in three drawers. What are the quotient and remainder, and what do they guarantee?
**Answer:** 7 ÷ 3 = 2 remainder 1; every drawer can take 2, the leftover 1 lands somewhere, so some drawer holds at least 3.

**4. (Level 2)** Ten pigeons fly to nine holes. What is guaranteed?
**Answer:** Some hole holds at least two pigeons — 10 things, 9 boxes.

**5. (Level 2)** A teacher puts 10 books onto 3 shelves, as evenly as she can. How many books must the fullest shelf hold?
**Answer:** At least 4 — 10 ÷ 3 = 3 remainder 1, so one shelf takes the extra book.

**6. (Level 3)** Twenty-five pupils: prove some birth month is shared by at least three pupils.
**Answer:** 25 = 2·12 + 1; two full rounds through the 12 months leave one pupil over, so some month holds at least 3.

**7. (Level 3)** Eleven whole numbers are written down. Prove two of them have a difference divisible by 10.
**Answer:** There are only 10 possible last digits (0–9); with 11 numbers, two share a last digit, and numbers with the same last digit differ by a multiple of 10.

**8. (Level 3)** Kazakhstan's book pictures the principle as rabbits into hutches. With 9 hutches, how many rabbits force some hutch to hold at least 3?
**Answer:** 19 rabbits — 2·9 + 1, the generalised form with k = 2, n = 9 (§6.5*, optional as filed).
