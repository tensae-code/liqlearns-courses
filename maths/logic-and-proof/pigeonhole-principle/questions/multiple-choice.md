# The pigeonhole principle — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Level 1)** Five pencils are put into four boxes. What must be true?
a) Every box holds exactly one pencil  b) Some box holds at least two pencils  c) One box stays empty
**Answer:** b) Some box holds at least two pencils — five things, four boxes: the fifth pencil has no box of its own.

**2. (Level 1)** In a drawer there are socks of 3 colours. How many socks must you pull out, without looking, to guarantee a matching pair?
a) 2  b) 3  c) 4
**Answer:** c) 4 — the first three can all be different colours; the fourth must repeat one.

**3. (Level 2)** Seven balls are shared into three drawers. Using 7 ÷ 3 = 2 remainder 1, what is guaranteed?
a) Some drawer holds at least 3  b) Every drawer holds exactly 2  c) Some drawer holds 7
**Answer:** a) Some drawer holds at least 3 — two in each drawer leaves one ball over, and it lands somewhere.

**4. (Level 2)** Nine letters are posted into eight pigeonholes. What is guaranteed?
a) Nothing — letters can go anywhere  b) Some hole holds at least two letters  c) Every hole holds a letter
**Answer:** b) Some hole holds at least two letters — 9 things into 8 boxes forces a share.

**5. (Level 3)** A class of 30 pupils have error counts of 0 to 8 on a test — 9 possible counts. At least how many pupils share the same count?
a) 3  b) 4  c) 9
**Answer:** b) 4 — 30 = 3·9 + 3, so after three full rounds the leftovers push some count to four (North Macedonia, Grade 8).

**6. (Level 3)** Twenty-one objects go into five boxes. Using the generalised form, some box holds at least how many?
a) 4  b) 5  c) 6
**Answer:** b) 5 — 21 = 4·5 + 1, the kn + 1 form with k = 4, n = 5 (Iran, Discrete Mathematics).
