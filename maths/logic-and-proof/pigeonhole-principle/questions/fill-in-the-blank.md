# The pigeonhole principle — fill in the blank

Fill each blank with the missing word or number.

**1. (Level 1)** If there are more things than boxes, some box holds at least ______ things.
**Answer:** two (2) — that is the plain principle.

**2. (Level 1)** In China's Grade 6 book, the pigeonhole principle is called the ______ principle.
**Answer:** drawer — the things are shared into drawers (Grade 6, Unit 5, pp.68–71).

**3. (Level 2)** 7 ÷ 3 = 2 remainder ______, so seven things in three drawers force some drawer to hold at least 3.
**Answer:** 1 — the single leftover thing is what breaks the even sharing.

**4. (Level 2)** To guarantee two pupils share a birth month, a class needs at least ______ pupils.
**Answer:** 13 — one more than the 12 months.

**5. (Level 3)** In the generalised form, kn + 1 objects in n boxes force some box to hold at least ______.
**Answer:** k + 1 — Iran's Discrete Mathematics states it in exactly this form (pp.82–84).
