# Lesson plan — Problem-solving strategies

**Course:** Maths · **Stage:** Stage 13 — Logic and Proof · **Lesson:** Problem-solving strategies
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 104 of 136, Stage 13 — Logic and Proof (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** how to attack a problem you do not yet know how to solve — the steps every book agrees on, and a named toolbox of strategies: draw it, list systematically, eliminate, spot the pattern, guess and check, split into sub-problems, solve a simpler problem, and turn it into symbols — plus China's tables, assumption method and differencing.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on ordinary arithmetic and on the earlier lessons' topics — the strategies are ways of working, not new mathematics. It needs nothing else.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Run the shared frame on any problem: Iran's Grade 7 book sets four steps — understand, choose a strategy, solve, look back (Ch.1, pp.1–2) — and Greece's Grade 5 book sets five, splitting the start into read-and-distinguish and ending with answer and reflect (Ch.3, pp.11–12).
2. Name Iran's eight strategies and say what each one does: drawing a figure (p.2), systematic listing (p.3), eliminating unwanted cases (p.4), pattern-spotting (p.5), guess and check (p.6), sub-problems (p.7), solving a simpler problem (p.8), symbolic methods (p.9).
3. Recognise the same strategies cycling chapter by chapter through Iran's primary books — every chapter of the Grade 2 and Grade 3 books carries one named problem-solving unit (Grade 2, pp.8–135; Grade 3, contents pp.8–134).
4. Follow Iraq's way: every chapter of its Grade 1 book ends its teaching with a problem-solving-plan lesson — use a model, use a representation, make a model, write a number sentence, the four steps, logical reasoning, draw a picture (Grade 1, pp.26–184) — the plan is a lesson in its own right, in every chapter through Grade 8 as filed.
5. Solve a logic puzzle with China's elimination tables and a 4×4 proto-Sudoku grid (Grade 2, Unit 9, pp.109–112).
6. Solve chickens-and-rabbits two ways — by listing and by the assumption method — as China's Grade 4 book does with the classic 35 heads and 94 feet (Unit 9, pp.103–107).
7. Use differencing on a two-object puzzle, as in China's Grade 3 book's cup-and-bottle puzzle (p.114), and read a 0/1 logic table and a symbol-substitution puzzle, as in China's Grade 6 review (pp.100–104).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil — drawing and listing are done by hand. Counters or coins for guess-and-check work.

## If the learner is stuck
- Starts calculating before understanding → go back to step one: say the problem in your own words and say what would count as an answer. Greece's Grade 5 book makes this its own first step — read and distinguish what is given from what is asked (Ch.3, pp.11–12).
- Picks a strategy and it stalls → a stalled strategy is information, not failure: name a second one. Iran's Grade 7 review set (pp.10–12) mixes all eight on purpose, because real problems do not announce their strategy.
- Guesses wildly in guess-and-check → guess, check, and use the miss: too big or too small tells you which way to move the next guess. That steering is the strategy.
- Drowns in a big problem → sub-problems (Iran, Grade 7, p.7): answer the small questions hiding inside it, in order, the way Iran's Grade 1 book splits a pencil problem into three numbered sub-questions (Grade 1, p.163).
- Ends with a number and stops → look back, Iran's fourth step: does the answer fit the story? Iraq's plan lessons build the same check into every chapter.
- This lesson's next door: Counting principles (Level 105, Stage 14) — systematic listing grown up into the counting rules.
