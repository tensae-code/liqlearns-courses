# Mathematical Communication — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, expressed as its SUB
LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure — README, "Units, Lessons and
Sub Lessons"). One lesson, one level (Level 166). The lesson
was built new in the expansion era (Andorra expansion merge,
2026-11-11), founded on curriculum-requirement evidence only,
so there are no old depth layers to map: the sub lessons below
are its depths as built, cut at the founding competency's own
shape — first saying a concept, a process and a result in
mathematical language, then the diversity of representations
the competency names, then carrying the same mathematics from
one representation into another. The questions live in the type
files; this file is the by-sub-lesson view.

Pass marks: the founding programmes state the competency and no
pass mark, so no mark is invented — each sub lesson's pass mark
is stated as the observable thing the learner can do.

## Sub lesson 1 — Saying It in Mathematical Language
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: given a finished piece of mathematics, communicate it completely — name the concept, show the process step by step, and state the result with its meaning — and turn a short word phrase ("five less than a number") into its correct symbolic form.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Many Representations
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: name the representations (words, symbols, tables, graphs, diagrams) and recognise the same mathematics wearing two different ones — for example the table (1→2, 2→4, 3→6) and the sentence "y is double x" as one and the same mathematics.

## Sub lesson 3 — Translating Between Representations
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: translate a piece of mathematics from one representation into another (words into symbols, a table into a rule said in words, a graph into a faithful sentence) and check the translation by naming every fact the original carried — values, units, scale, which is biggest — and confirming none was lost.

## What "maxed" means here

The learner does what Andorra's four programmes require as a
competency in their own right: they communicate mathematical
concepts, processes and results in mathematical language,
using a diversity of representations, and they can carry the
same mathematics from one representation into another without
losing a fact — the requirement as stated in the Primera
programme's competency C3 (Decret 376/2021, Annex VII, printed
pp. 8–21) and as C4 in the Segona (Decret 355/2023, printed
pp. 8–24) and the two Batxillerat programmes (Decret 374/2023;
Decret 242/2025; printed pp. 8–21). Teacher evidence
(curriculum requirement) — no pupil textbook teaches this
lesson yet.
