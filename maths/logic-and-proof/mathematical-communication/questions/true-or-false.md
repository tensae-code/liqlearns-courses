# True or false — Mathematical Communication

Sub-lesson markers: every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to — Sub lesson 1 — Saying It in Mathematical Language · Sub lesson 2 — The Many Representations · Sub lesson 3 — Translating Between Representations. This lesson was built new with its sub lessons (Andorra expansion merge, 2026-11-11), founded on curriculum-requirement evidence only, so its markers are original — there are no older depth layers to map from.

1. (Sub lesson 1) Writing only the final answer communicates the whole mathematics.
   **Answer: False** — the process and the meaning of the result are missing; a complete communication carries the concept, the process and the result. 3. (Sub lesson 2) A table and a graph can carry exactly the same mathematics.
   **Answer: True** — they are two representations of it; the table shows the exact pairs, the graph the pattern. Item constructed for this lesson from its founding requirement. 6. (Sub lesson 3) Translating from words into symbols can expose a mistake in the words.
   **Answer: True** — "five less than a number" must become n − 5; writing the symbols forces the order to be decided, and a sloppy sentence is caught by its own translation.
