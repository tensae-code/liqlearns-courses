# Hands-on — Mathematical Communication

## Activity 1 — Say the whole thing (10 minutes)

Take a sum you finished today (for example 36 + 48 = 84).
Communicate it completely, out loud, in three parts: name the
concept ("this is addition — joining two quantities"), show
the process step by step ("first the units, then the tens"),
and state the result with its meaning ("84, the two groups
together"). Then swap: a partner communicates one of theirs
and you check that all three parts are present. This is Sub
lesson 1 done with your own work.

## Activity 2 — One mathematics, four clothes (15 minutes)

Take the rule "y is three times x." Put it in as many
representations as you can: the symbols (y = 3x), a table
(1→3, 2→6, 3→9, 4→12), a graph (draw the axes, plot the
pairs, read the rising pattern), and a diagram or drawing of
your own choosing (three rows of counters for x = 1, 2, 3, for
example). Lay the four side by side and say, for each one,
where the "three times" lives inside it. This is Sub lesson 2
done on paper.

## Activity 3 — Carry it and check it (15 minutes)

Partner A writes a short word problem with units (for example:
"a tank holds 40 litres; 6 litres are poured out each hour").
Partner B translates it twice: into symbols (40 − 6h) and
into a table (hours 0, 1, 2 → litres 40, 34, 28). Then both
partners run the check together: name every fact the original
carried — the 40, the 6, the litres, the hours, that the
amount goes DOWN — and confirm each one survived in both
translations. Swap roles with a new problem. This is Sub
lesson 3 as a game: the check catches what the carrying
dropped.
