## Do it first
Mix juice: 6 glasses of orange juice go into one jug — that is
the recipe's ratio, 6 glasses to 1 jug. Now mix paint: 6 scoops
of yellow to 4 scoops of red. Mix a bigger batch: 9 scoops of
yellow to 6 of red. Is it the same colour? Yes — because 6:4 and
9:6 are the same ratio, both equal to 3:2. A ratio is a recipe
written in numbers: it says how much of one thing goes with how
much of another.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a Ratio Is, and Equivalent Ratios
**1. What a ratio is.** A ratio compares two quantities of the
same kind, and it is written with a colon: 2:1, 3:2, 5:7. It is
read "two to one", "three to two", "five to seven". The order
matters: 2 chocolate cakes and 1 orange cake is the ratio 2:1
chocolate to orange, and 1:2 orange to chocolate. Armenia's
Grade 6 book teaches ratio as its own lesson, with recipes.
Jordan's Grade 6 book teaches ratio with 6 glasses of orange
juice per jug. Egypt's Grade 6 book teaches ratio with tape
diagrams and double number lines. Greece's Grade 6 book teaches
ratio as its own chapter. Sierra Leone's JSS 1 book teaches ratio
as its own lesson. Libya's Grade 5 book teaches finding a ratio
with 2 chocolate cakes and 1 orange cake. Ghana's B6 teacher
manual teaches ratio with banku mixing: 4 cups of cornmeal, 2
cups of cassava, 6 cups of water — the ratio 4:2:6, which is
2:1:3. South Africa's Grade 4 workbook shows ratio as a picture
example, and its Grade 5 workbook teaches ratio tables.

**2. Equivalent ratios.** Multiply or divide BOTH terms of a
ratio by the same number and the ratio stays the same: 2:3 is
the same as 4:6, 6:9 and 8:12. But adding the same number to
both terms does NOT keep it the same: Egypt's Grade 6 book
shows Amany's error — adding 2 to each term of 8:12 gives
10:14, which is not the same ratio, because 8/12 is 2/3 and
10/14 is 5/7. Jordan's Grade 6 book teaches equivalent ratios
as its own lesson. Egypt's Grade 6 book teaches equivalent ratios
with tape diagrams. Greece's Grade 6 book teaches equivalent
ratios as its own chapter. Sierra Leone's JSS 1 book teaches
equivalent ratios as its own lesson. Libya's Grade 5 book teaches
equivalent ratios and comparing three quantities. Ghana's B6
teacher manual teaches equivalent ratios with men to women,
5:7: with 45 men there are 63 women, because 45 ÷ 5 = 9 and
9 × 7 = 63.

**3. Simplifying ratios and sharing in a ratio.** Divide both
terms by their highest common factor: 12:18 simplifies to 2:3.
To share a whole in a ratio, add the parts first, then divide:
share 72 pupils in the ratio 7:11 — boys to girls. The parts add
to 18, 72 ÷ 18 = 4 per part, so there are 7 × 4 = 28 boys and
11 × 4 = 44 girls, and 28 + 44 = 72. Ghana's B6 teacher manual
works exactly this sharing — boys to girls 7:11, 28 boys, 44
girls, 72 altogether — and teaches ratio in simplest form.
Sierra Leone's JSS 1 book teaches ratio in simplest form,
sharing in a ratio, ratios with three or more terms, and dividing
a length in a ratio. Mauritania's Grade 6 book teaches unequal
shares. Libya's Grade 6 book teaches ratio with money in dinars,
cement and sand mixes, fish counts and water volumes in the
ratio 5:2.

## Sub lesson 2 — Sharing in a Ratio, and Ratio with Fractions and Percent
**4. Ratio, fractions and percent are the same comparison.** A
ratio 2:3 means 2 out of 5 parts are the first thing — the
fraction 2/5, which is 40%. Sierra Leone's JSS 1 book teaches
ratios with fractions, percentages and decimals as its own
lesson. Libya's Grade 6 book teaches ratio and fractions
together. Ghana's B6 teacher manual teaches the relation of
ratio to fractions and multiplication. South Africa's Grade 7
workbooks teach ratio beside rate.

## Sub lesson 3 — Proportion, Unit Rate, Scale, Working Together
**5. Unit rate and best buy.** A unit rate is the value of one
unit: Basma does 570 jumps in 6 minutes, so her rate is 570 ÷ 6
= 95 jumps per minute. To find the best buy, work out the price
of one unit of each and compare: the smaller unit price wins.
Egypt's Grade 6 book teaches unit rate with Basma's 570 jumps
in 6 minutes and best-buy popcorn. Armenia's Grade 6 book
teaches ratio through recipes, where the unit rate is the amount
per one serving. South Africa's Grade 7 workbooks teach rate as
its own worksheet, beside ratio.

**6. Proportion: two ratios that are equal.** A proportion says
two ratios are the same: 2/3 = 8/12. To check it, cross-multiply:
2 × 12 = 24 and 3 × 8 = 24 — the cross products are equal, so
the proportion is true. To find a missing term, cross-multiply
and divide: in x/3 = 8/12, 12x = 24, so x = 2. Greece's Grade 6
book teaches cross products with the saying "crosswise I find
x". Armenia's Grade 6 book teaches equality of ratios and cross
multiplication as two lessons. Egypt's Grade 6 book teaches
proportion with cross-multiplication. Sierra Leone's JSS 1 book
teaches simple proportion with cross-multiplication.

**7. Direct proportion and the rule of three.** When one quantity
doubles, the other doubles too: 3 kg of rice cost 6, so 5 kg cost
10 — the ratio of price to mass stays the same. The rule of
three finds the missing value: if 3 kg cost 6, then 1 kg costs 2,
and 5 kg cost 10. Greece's Grade 6 book teaches direct and
inverse proportion and the rule of three. Armenia's Grade 6 book
teaches direct proportion. Mauritania's Grade 6 book teaches
proportionality and uniform motion — distance is directly
proportional to time at a steady speed.

**8. Scale and maps.** A scale is a ratio: 1 cm on the map stands
for 10 km on the ground, a scale of 1:1,000,000. If two towns are
3 cm apart on the map, they are 30 km apart in real life.
Armenia's Grade 6 book teaches map and scale. Greece's Grade 6
book teaches scale as a ratio. Vietnam's Grade 5 book teaches
map scale. Mauritania's Grade 6 book teaches proportionality and
map scale.

**9. Working together.** If one worker finishes a job in 6 hours
and another in 3 hours, together they do 1/6 + 1/3 = 1/2 of the
job in one hour, so the job takes 2 hours. Armenia's Grade 6 book
teaches working together. Kazakhstan's Grade 5 book teaches
joint work with the whole job taken as 1.

## Say it to your companion
Read these aloud to your companion: "two to one", "three to two",
"five to seven". Then your companion gives you a recipe — 4 cups
of flour to 2 cups of sugar — and you say the ratio two ways:
"four to two" and, simplified, "two to one". Trade: your
companion gives you a sharing — 60 sweets in the ratio 2:3 — and
you say how many each gets before you write it: 24 and 36.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write the ratio: 6 red pens and 4 blue pens, red to blue.
   Simplify it.
2. Are 2:3 and 8:12 equivalent? Check by cross-multiplying.
   Is 8:12 the same as 10:14?
3. Share 60 in the ratio 2:3. Share 100 in the ratio 1:4.
4. A car travels 240 km in 3 hours. What is its speed per hour?
   How far does it go in 5 hours at the same speed?
5. On a map with scale 1 cm : 10 km, two towns are 4.5 cm apart.
   How far apart are they really?
