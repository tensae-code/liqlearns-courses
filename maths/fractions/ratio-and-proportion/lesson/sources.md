**Acceptance count: taught in 13 countries (Kazakhstan, Jordan,
South Africa, Egypt, Greece, Armenia, Sierra Leone, Mauritania,
Libya, Vietnam, Ghana, Laos, Yemen).** Armenia carries the fullest ratio
course — ratio, equality of ratios and recipes, cross
multiplication, direct proportion, map and scale, working
together; Jordan teaches ratio and equivalent ratios in Grade 6;
Egypt teaches ratio, equivalent ratios, proportion with
cross-multiplication, unit rate and best buy in Grade 6 Term 2;
Greece teaches ratio, equivalent ratios, proportions, cross
products, direct and inverse proportion, the rule of three, and
scale as a ratio in Grade 6; Sierra Leone teaches ratio,
equivalent ratios, ratio of a whole, simplification, sharing,
three or more terms, dividing a length in ratio, and simple
proportion in JSS 1 Term 2; Mauritania teaches proportion in
Grade 5 and proportionality with uniform motion, map scale and
unequal shares in Grade 6; Libya teaches finding a ratio,
equivalent ratios and comparing three quantities in Grade 5,
and ratio with fractions, proportion, money, mixes and counts
in Grade 6; Vietnam teaches ratio, map scale and finding two
numbers from sum and ratio / difference and ratio in Grade 5,
with no ratio beyond percent in Grade 6; Ghana teaches ratio
and proportion only as teacher-manual evidence (B6's banku
mixing, equivalent ratios, simplest form, sharing and the
relation to fractions); South Africa teaches ratio as a picture
example in Grade 4, ratio tables in Grades 5–6, ratio and rate
in Grade 7, and ratio and rate again in Grade 9; Kazakhstan
teaches proportional division in Grade 4 and joint work in
Grade 5, but no ratio or proportion as such — stated honestly
above. Laos carries ratio and proportion from proportional tables in Grade 4, through direct and inverse proportion in Grade 5, to ratio, coefficient k, the fourth proportional by cross products and inverse proportion in Grades 6–7. Yemen carries ratio and proportion from ratio in Grade 6 through proportion, enlargement, scale drawing and proportional division (including inheritance), to Thales' theorem and similarity in Grade 8.
