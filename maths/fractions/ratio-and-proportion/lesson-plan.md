# Lesson plan — Ratio and Proportion

**Course:** Maths · **Stage:** 4, Fractions, Decimals and Percentages · **Lesson:** Ratio and Proportion
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 16 of the ladder — the sixth
and last lesson of Stage 4 (CURRICULUM.md). It stands on the
Fractions lesson, the Decimals lesson and the Percentages lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** ratio and proportion —
what a ratio is, equivalent ratios, simplifying and sharing in
a ratio, ratio with fractions and percent, unit rate and best
buy, proportion and cross-multiplication, direct proportion and
the rule of three, scale and maps, and working together.
**This lesson's own level:** it needs the Fractions lesson and
the Percentages lesson, and nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown
changing — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds per pour.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the Fractions lesson (Level 11) and the
  Percentages lesson (Level 15). Recommended, never forced.
- This is the last lesson of Stage 4. Stage 5 (Geometry) comes
  next.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Write a ratio, read it, and say why the order matters.
2. Find equivalent ratios and simplify a ratio.
3. Share a whole in a ratio.
4. Turn a ratio into a fraction and a percent.
5. Find a unit rate and pick the best buy.
6. Check a proportion by cross-multiplication and find a missing
   term.
7. Use direct proportion and the rule of three.
8. Read a map scale and work out real distances.
9. Work out how long a job takes when people work together.

## What you need
Paint or coloured water and cups for mixing, a map, a ruler,
pencil and paper.

## If the learner is stuck
- Adds the same number to both terms to make an "equivalent"
  ratio → multiplying or dividing both terms keeps a ratio;
  adding does not. 8:12 is not 10:14.
- Writes the ratio the wrong way round → say the words first:
  "chocolate to orange" is 2:1, "orange to chocolate" is 1:2.
  The words fix the order.
- Shares in a ratio by dividing by the first term → add the
  parts first, then divide. 60 in 2:3 is 5 parts of 12, not 2
  parts of 30.
- Forgets which way cross-multiplication goes → crosswise:
  multiply the top of one side by the bottom of the other, and
  the two products must match.
