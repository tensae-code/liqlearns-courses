# Hands-on — Ratio and Proportion

The lesson mixed real things before it wrote any ratio, the way
every book starts it.

## Mix the paint
With watercolours or coloured water, mix 6 spoonfuls of yellow
with 4 of red. Now mix 9 yellow with 6 red. Hold the two pots
side by side: the same colour, because 6:4 and 9:6 are the same
ratio. Now add 2 spoonfuls of yellow only to the first pot: the
colour changes — adding to one side breaks the ratio, and your
eyes prove it.

## Share the sweets
Take 60 small sweets or counters. Share them in the ratio 2:3:
make 5 equal piles of 12, give 2 piles to one person and 3 to
the other — 24 and 36. Now share 100 in the ratio 1:4: 5 piles
of 20 — 20 and 80. The piles show the rule: add the parts,
divide, multiply.

## Map measuring
Take a real map with a scale bar. Measure the distance between
two towns in centimetres and work out the real distance. Then
find two towns you know the real distance between and work
backwards to what the map should show. If the map says 1 cm is
10 km and the towns are 4.5 cm apart, they are 45 km apart —
walk it on the map with your fingers.

## Recipe scaling
With a parent, take a recipe that serves 4 and scale it to serve
6: every amount multiplies by 6/4, which is 3/2. A recipe with
4 cups of flour needs 6 cups. Cook the scaled recipe and eat the
proof.
