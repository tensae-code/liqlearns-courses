# Hands-on — Decimals

The lesson measured real things before it wrote any decimal, the
way every book starts it.

## Tenths on a strip
Cut a paper strip into 10 equal parts. Shade 3 parts and write
0.3 under them. Shade 5 more and write 0.8. Now cut another strip
into 100 equal parts (fold in tenths, then tenths again) and shade
25: that is 0.25, and it is the same as 1/4 of the strip. The
strip shows what the point means: how many small parts of the
whole you hold.

## Money maths
Lay out coins and price tags: 2.35, 1.70, 0.50. Add two prices
with the points lined up on paper, then check with the coins.
Take 5.6 and pay 2.35 — count the change. The point is the
anchor: line it up and the money adds itself.

## The metre line
Stand a metre stick up. Find 0.3 m, 0.5 m, 0.75 m on it. Now
multiply by 10: 0.3 m is 3 m? No — move the point: 0.3 × 10 is
3.0, and 0.3 m × 10 is 3 m. Walk it: ten steps of 0.3 m land at
3 m. Then divide: 4.8 m shared into 0.6 m pieces makes 8 pieces
— lay them out and count.

## Decimal shopping
With a parent, read five real prices in a shop and write each
one as a fraction: 1.25 is 1 1/4, 0.75 is 3/4, 2.50 is 2 1/2.
Add two of them and work out the change from 5.00. The shop is
the worksheet.
