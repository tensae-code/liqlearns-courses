# Lesson plan — Decimals

**Course:** Maths · **Stage:** 4, Fractions, Decimals and Percentages · **Lesson:** Decimals
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 14 of the ladder — the fourth
lesson of Stage 4 (CURRICULUM.md). It stands on the Fractions
lesson and on Adding and Subtracting Fractions.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** decimals — tenths,
hundredths and thousandths, reading and writing them, turning
fractions into decimals and back, comparing, ordering and
rounding them, and adding, subtracting, multiplying and dividing
them.
**This lesson's own level:** it needs the Fractions lesson's
meaning of a fraction, and nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown
changing — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds per move.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the Fractions lesson (Level 11). Recommended,
  never forced.
- Percentages are the next lesson — this lesson only does decimals.
- Note the mark: this course writes decimals with a point
  (0.5). Kazakhstan and South Africa write them with a comma
  (0,5) — the same number, a different mark.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Read and write decimals to thousandths, and say what each
   place after the point is worth.
2. Turn fractions into decimals and decimals into fractions, and
   say which fractions cannot become decimals exactly.
3. Compare, order and round decimals.
4. Add and subtract decimals, point under point.
5. Multiply decimals, counting the places.
6. Divide decimals, making the divisor whole first.
7. Multiply and divide decimals by 10, 100 and 1000 by moving
   the point.

## What you need
A metre stick or ruler, paper strips to cut into tenths, price
tags or coins, pencil and paper.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner reads price tags and cuts a
   strip into tenths before any rule (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — tenths, hundredths, thousandths;
   fractions and decimals; comparing, ordering, rounding; adding
   and subtracting; multiplying; dividing; by 10, 100, 1000
   (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the tenths part: a picture of a strip splitting
     into 10 equal parts, one at a time, 0.1, 0.2, 0.3… lighting
     up — the student counts the tenths aloud as they appear.
   - ADD IMAGE under adding and subtracting: a picture of a
     column sum with the decimal points lined up in a straight
     vertical line → the student checks that their own points
     line up the same way.
   - ADD IMAGE under multiplying: a picture of a 0.3 by 0.4
     rectangle on a hundredths grid, 12 small squares shaded →
     the student counts the shaded squares and says why the
     answer has two decimal places.
   - ADD AUDIO in the reading part: a voice reading "zero point
     five", "one point two five" while the decimal shows → the
     student reads three more decimals aloud first.
3. **Say it to your companion** — read decimals aloud and turn
   prices into fractions (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the
     strip splitting and the point moving in the super-simple
     voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served
   at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — fractions to decimals, ordering, the four
   operations, a ribbon problem (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Lines the numbers up on the right instead of the point → the
  point is the anchor: point under point, always. 4.7 + 2.85
  needs a zero: 4.70 + 2.85.
- Puts the point in the wrong place when multiplying → count the
  places: one place plus one place is two places. 0.3 × 0.4 is
  0.12, not 1.2.
- Divides by a decimal and the answer comes out tiny → shift the
  point in BOTH numbers. 4.8 ÷ 0.6 is 48 ÷ 6, which is 8.
- Says 0.45 is bigger than 0.5 → compare place by place: 0.5 is
  0.50, and 50 hundredths beat 45 hundredths.
