# Variation — the lesson

Course: Maths · Stage: Stage 4 — Fractions, Decimals, Percentages, Ratio · Lesson: Variation
Built from: Sierra Leone, Pupils' Handbook for Senior Secondary Mathematics, SSS 2, Term 1 (L17–L20) — Jordan, Grade 7 Student's Book, Term 2 (Unit 5 — direct and inverse variation) — Pakistan, Mathematics 10 (Science Group), Ilmi Kitab Khana, 2017 (Unit 3 Variations, pp.49–73)

## Do it first
Hang a weight on a spring. Double the weight — the stretch doubles. Now draw a rectangle of area 24. Double its length — its width does not double; it halves. Two quantities can follow each other in opposite ways. This lesson names the ways, one by one, and gives each its equation. Before you read on: a car's fuel use and the distance it drives — which way do they follow? (They grow together — the first way below.)

## Then the explanation
**Direct variation.** y varies directly as x when doubling x doubles y, tripling x triples y. The ratio y/x never changes, and we write **y = kx**, where k is that fixed ratio — the constant of variation. Sierra Leone's SSS 2 book opens its variation block with exactly this (L17 Direct Variation, p.45), and Jordan's Grade 7 book teaches the same base form with tables and graphs of proportional relationships (Unit 5, L4 Direct variation, p.23). Work it: y = 12 when x = 3, so k = 12 ÷ 3 = 4 and the law is y = 4x. Then y when x = 7 is 4 × 7 = 28, with no new thinking — k carries the pair everywhere. Pakistan's Class 10 book starts its Variations unit one step earlier, with ratio and proportion themselves — a ratio's first term is the antecedent and its second the consequent, and in a proportion a:b::c:d the product of the extremes equals the product of the means (Pakistan, Mathematics 10 (Science Group), Unit 3 Variations, §3.1, printed pp.49–73). It then lets direct variation climb into powers: a falling body follows d = 16t², so the distance varies directly as the *square* of the time, and an area varies as the square of a radius (Pakistan, Class 10, Unit 3, Ex. 3.2, printed pp.49–73). The doubling test grows with it: double the time and the falling distance does not double — it grows four times, exactly as the square says.

**Inverse variation.** y varies inversely as x when doubling x halves y. Now the product xy never changes, and we write **y = k/x**. Sierra Leone teaches it straight after direct, for the contrast (L18 Inverse Variation, p.48); Jordan's book gives the inverse base form its own lesson too (L5 Inverse variation, p.30). Work it: y = 6 when x = 4, so k = xy = 24 and the law is y = 24/x. Then y when x = 8 is 24 ÷ 8 = 3 — x doubled, y halved, as promised. Speed and time for one fixed journey live here: faster, sooner, product fixed. Pakistan pushes inverse variation into powers too: y can vary as the inverse square of x, and its real-life problems use light that weakens as the inverse square of the distance — double the distance and the light falls to a quarter, not a half (Pakistan, Class 10, Unit 3, Ex. 3.2 and Ex. 3.7, printed pp.49–73).

**Joint variation.** One quantity can follow two others at once: z varies jointly as x and y when **z = kxy**. Sierra Leone's next lesson builds it on the two before (L19 Joint Variation, p.51). Work it: z = 30 when x = 2 and y = 3, so k = 30 ÷ (2 × 3) = 5 and the law is z = 5xy. Then z when x = 3 and y = 4 is 5 × 3 × 4 = 60. The area of a rectangle varies jointly as its length and width — that is why the formula is a product. Pakistan's joint variation mixes the two directions at once: gravitation varies jointly as the two masses and inversely as the square of the distance between them, G ∝ m₁m₂/d², and a beam's strength varies jointly as its breadth and the square of its depth, s ∝ bd² (Pakistan, Class 10, Unit 3, §3.3 and Ex. 3.7, printed pp.49–73). Read the shape before the numbers: a product on top pulls up, a product underneath pulls down.

**Partial variation.** Sometimes part of a quantity stands still while the rest varies: **y = a + bx** — a constant part a, plus a part bx that varies directly as x. Sierra Leone closes the block with it (L20 Partial Variation, p.54): part constant, part varying. A taxi fare is its everyday face: a starting charge that never moves, plus a charge per kilometre that grows. Work it from two pairs: y = 11 when x = 2, and y = 17 when x = 4. The growth from x = 2 to x = 4 is 17 − 11 = 6 over 2 steps of x, so b = 3. Then a = 11 − 3 × 2 = 5. The law is y = 5 + 3x — check it at x = 4: 5 + 12 = 17. Jordan's books stop at the direct and inverse base forms; joint and partial variation are Sierra Leone's contribution to this lesson, and they complete the set of four.

**Pakistan's extra tools — working inside a proportion.** Pakistan's unit does not stop at naming the kind. It gives you third, fourth and mean proportionals and continued proportion (Pakistan, Class 10, Unit 3, Ex. 3.3, printed pp.49–73), then five named moves for reshaping a true proportion — invertendo, alternendo, componendo, dividendo and componendo-dividendo — and a k-method: set each equal ratio equal to k, and conditional equalities fall out (Pakistan, Class 10, Unit 3, §3.2 and §3.4, Ex. 3.4 and Ex. 3.6, printed pp.49–73). Work its own check: two numbers in the ratio 3:4 become the ratio 5:6 when 2 is added to each — call the numbers 3x and 4x, and (3x + 2)/(4x + 2) = 5/6 gives x = 1, so the numbers are 3 and 4. And when a:b = 5:8, the same machinery gives (3a + 4b) : (5a + 7b) = 47:81, because 3 × 5 + 4 × 8 = 47 and 5 × 5 + 7 × 8 = 81. Its closing problems put variation to work in the world — Hooke's law for a spring, electric current varying as E/R, kinetic energy and labour costs (Pakistan, Class 10, Unit 3, Ex. 3.7, printed pp.49–73) — the spring you stretched at the start of this lesson is one of its own examples.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: to name any variation, use the doubling test. Double x and watch y: doubles → direct; halves → inverse; grows but not by doubling → suspect a still part hiding inside — partial.

## Say it to your companion
Say the four laws whole: "Direct: y equals k x." "Inverse: y equals k over x, so x y equals k." "Joint: z equals k x y." "Partial: y equals a plus b x — part still, part moving." Then your companion names everyday pairs — (days, pay for days worked), (people sharing a pizza, size of each share), (a phone bill with a monthly fee plus per-minute charges) — and you name the kind and defend it with the doubling test.

## Do It Yourself
1. y varies directly as x, and y = 18 when x = 6. Find k, write the law, and find y when x = 9.
2. y varies inversely as x, and y = 5 when x = 8. Find k, write the law, and find y when x = 10.
3. z varies jointly as x and y, and z = 24 when x = 2 and y = 4. Find the law, and find z when x = 5 and y = 3.
4. A hire charge is partly fixed and partly per day: 2 days cost 26 and 5 days cost 50. Find the fixed part and the per-day part, and price 8 days.
5. Name the kind, with a reason: (a) the cost of rice and its weight; (b) the time to empty a tank and the number of pumps working; (c) a plumber's bill: call-out charge plus an hourly rate.
6. In Pakistan's way: two numbers are in the ratio 3:4, and when 2 is added to each the ratio becomes 5:6. Find the two numbers. Then, for a distance that follows d = 16t², find the distance when t = 3.
