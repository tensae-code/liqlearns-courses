**Acceptance count: taught in 10 countries (Sierra Leone, Jordan, Pakistan, Malaysia, Sudan, India, Afghanistan, Ecuador, Albania, Tajikistan).** Sierra Leone carries the whole lesson: its SSS 2 Term 1 handbook teaches variation as a block of four lessons — direct (L17, p.45), inverse (L18, p.48), joint (L19, p.51) and partial (L20, p.54), each with numerical and word problems — and returns to variation in review problems later in the SSS course. Jordan carries the base forms only: in Grade 7 Term 2, Unit 5, direct variation (L4, p.23) and inverse variation (L5, p.30), built on unit rate and proportion; Jordan teaches no joint or partial variation there, and the lesson does not claim it does. Pakistan carries a full Variations unit in Class 10 (Unit 3, printed pp.49–73): ratio and proportion first, then direct and inverse variation including variation with powers, third, fourth and mean proportionals and continued proportion, the theorems on proportions (invertendo, alternendo, componendo, dividendo, componendo-dividendo), joint variation, the k-method, and real-life variation problems, with a unit summary on pp.72–73; the filing lists no partial variation for Pakistan, so partial variation still rests on Sierra Leone alone.

Malaysia gives variation a whole Form 5 chapter — direct, inverse, joint and combined — the fullest single-book variation treatment in the course. Partial variation is not taught there; that part of the lesson stands on the other countries.

Sudan teaches variation whole in Grade 10 — direct, inverse, joint and partial variation, and variation with powers and roots — and takes a growth rate from two values in Grade 11.

India enters variation from Mathematics Class 8: direct proportion: x/y constant; unitary-style tables; map-scale application.

Afghanistan enters through its Ministry of Education course — the Grade 1 course in two language editions (Pashto and Dari, one work with identical page maps) and Dari student books for Grades 4, 6, 7, 8, 10, 11 and 12, all printing year 1398 HS except the Grade 8 book, which prints 1399 HS on its cover and title page and 1398 HS on its specifications page. From its books: from Grade 6: direct and inverse proportion; from Grade 8: types of proportion: direct proportion; types of proportion: inverse proportion; compound proportion.

Albania enters through its Mediaprint teacher's books (Libër Mësuesi, in Albanian) — TEACHER EVIDENCE only: no pupil's book is in hand for any grade, so Albania is never cited as a pupil-textbook source; what the teacher's books evidence is what is taught and in what order, through their lesson plans, worked answers and answer keys. From its books: from Grade 9 (teacher's book): Direct proportion: building proportion equations and solving real problems with algebra. from Grade 10 (teacher's book): Ratio and direct proportion; scaling by powers of ten mentally. from Grade 11 (teacher's book): Gravitational force at the surface: inverse-square modelling problem (from the annual plan); Direct proportion (y ∝ x); More direct proportion (y ∝ x², y ∝ x³, y ∝ √x — test Q4 on printed p. 161 uses exactly these relations); Inverse proportion; Direct/inverse proportion and exponential dependence in real problems (printed pp. 146–147).

Tajikistan enters through its Ministry-approved pupil's textbooks in Tajik (Cyrillic), all published in Dushanbe — Математика for Grade 5 (Маориф, 2022) and Grade 6 (5th edition, Маориф, 2021), Алгебра for Grades 7 (4th improved edition, 2023) and 8 (4th edition, 2023), and Геометрия — the continuation of stereometry — for Grade 11 (3rd edition, Лавҳ, 2020). From its books: from Grade 6: Directly proportional quantities (doubling one doubles the other; Inversely proportional quantities (work crews, price/quantity, feed stock, rectangle…; from Grade 7: Direct proportionality: constant ratio, formula y = kx in all but name; from Grade 8: Inverse proportionality: the function y = k/x, its properties and graph (hyperbola).
