# Lesson plan — Variation

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 4 — Fractions, Decimals, Percentages, Ratio · **Lesson:** Variation
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 57 of 136, inside Unit 4 — Fractions, Decimals, Percentages, Ratio (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** variation — how one quantity follows another: directly, inversely, jointly, and partly (part constant, part varying), each with its constant k.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on ratio and proportion and on the unitary method (Level 56) — the learner can already scale through the one. It needs nothing else.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the unitary method (Level 56) — variation is the unitary idea written with letters: a fixed constant joins the pair. Recommended, never forced.
- Recommended first: ratio and proportion (Unit 4) — direct and inverse proportion are the base forms. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Write direct variation as y = kx, find k from one pair of values, and use it for any other pair (Sierra Leone, SSS 2 Term 1, L17 Direct Variation, p.45; Jordan, Grade 7 Term 2, L4 Direct variation, p.23).
2. Write inverse variation as y = k/x (so xy = k), find k, and use it (Sierra Leone, L18 Inverse Variation, p.48; Jordan, L5 Inverse variation, p.30).
3. Write joint variation — one quantity varying as the product of two or more others — and find its constant (Sierra Leone, L19 Joint Variation, p.51).
4. Handle partial variation: one part constant, one part varying, y = a + bx, finding a and b from two given pairs (Sierra Leone, L20 Partial Variation, p.54).
5. Work inside a proportion in Pakistan's way: name antecedent and consequent, extremes and means, use product of extremes = product of means, and find third, fourth and mean proportionals and continued proportion (Pakistan, Class 10, Unit 3 Variations, §3.1 and Ex. 3.1, Ex. 3.3, printed pp.49–73).
6. Use variation with powers and mixed joint forms, and reshape a proportion with the theorems (invertendo, alternendo, componendo, dividendo, componendo-dividendo) and the k-method: a falling body d = 16t², gravitation G ∝ m₁m₂/d², (3a + 4b) : (5a + 7b) = 47:81 when a:b = 5:8 (Pakistan, Class 10, Unit 3 Variations, Ex. 3.2, Ex. 3.4–Ex. 3.7, printed pp.49–73).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A spring or a rubber band and some weights help for the hands-on, but coins and a strip of elastic will do.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner stretches a spring: double the weight, the stretch doubles; then a rectangle of fixed area: double the length, the width halves — two different kinds of following, before any names (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — direct variation y = kx; inverse variation y = k/x; joint variation; partial variation y = a + bx (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the direct part: a picture of a spring stretching further as weights are added one by one, the stretch numbers doubling when the weights double — the student reads the stretch for each load and checks the ratio stays fixed.
   - ADD IMAGE in the inverse part: a picture of three rectangles all of area 24 — long and thin, middling, short and wide — the student multiplies length × width on each and finds 24 every time.
   - ADD IMAGE in the partial part: a picture of a taxi meter card reading "start 5, then 3 for each kilometre" → the student works out the fare for 1, 4 and 10 kilometres from the card.
3. **Say it to your companion** — name the kind of variation in everyday pairs and defend the name (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the variation chant in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — find k in three kinds, and build one partial-variation fare table of your own (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes up direct and inverse → test with doubling: double x. If y doubles, direct; if y halves, inverse. Sierra Leone teaches the two in back-to-back lessons for exactly this contrast (L17–L18, pp.45, 48).
- Finds k but cannot use it → write the full equation with k in it first (y = 4x), then substitute; never jump from the pair straight to the answer.
- Partial variation feels like two problems → it is: the constant part a is what y is when x is 0; find b from how fast y grows, then check with the second pair (Sierra Leone, L20, p.54).
