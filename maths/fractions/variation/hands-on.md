# Hands-on — Variation

The lesson followed real quantities with hands before letters.

## The stretching spring
Hang a spring or elastic strip. Add weight in equal steps — one coin, two coins, three — and measure the stretch each time. Table it: load 1, 2, 3; stretch grows in equal steps. Divide stretch by load each time: the same number. That fixed number is k — direct variation you can feel.

## Rectangles of one area
Cut or draw rectangles that all have area 24: 24 × 1, 12 × 2, 8 × 3, 6 × 4. Measure and multiply each pair. The product never moves: 24. Now double a length and watch the width halve — inverse variation on paper.

## The taxi card
Make a fare card: "start 5, then 3 for each kilometre." Price trips of 1, 2, 5 and 9 kilometres. Split each fare into the still part (5, always) and the moving part (3 × kilometres). That split is partial variation: y = a + bx with a = 5, b = 3.

## Name that pair
Cards with pairs: (workers, days for one job), (speed, time for one journey), (price per pen, number of pens one coin buys), (length of a shadow, height of the stick). Sort the cards: direct, inverse, or neither — and say the doubling test out loud for each.
