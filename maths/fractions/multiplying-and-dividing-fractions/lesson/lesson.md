# Multiplying and Dividing Fractions — the lesson

Course: Maths · Stage 4: Fractions, Decimals and Percentages · Lesson: Multiplying and Dividing Fractions
Built from: Kazakhstan, Grade 5 — Jordan, Grade 5 Term 1 and Grade 6
Term 1 — Egypt, Grade 5 Term 2 and Grade 6 Term 2 — Greece, Grade 5
and Grade 6 — Armenia, Grade 5 — Sierra Leone, Primary 4–5 lesson
plans and JSS 1 Term 1 — Mauritania, Grade 5 and Grade 6 — Libya,
Grade 4 and Grade 5 — Vietnam, Grade 4 Volume 2 and Grade 6 Volume 2
— Ghana, JICA teacher manual B6 (teacher evidence).

## Do it first
Take half a pizza. Now take half of that half. How much pizza do
you have? Fold a paper half in half again: you hold one quarter
of the whole pizza. 1/2 of 1/2 is 1/4. Multiplying fractions is
taking a fraction OF a fraction — and the word "of" is the clue
the books teach: "of" means multiply.

Now the other way round: you have 3 whole pizzas. How many
half-pizzas can you cut from them? Six. 3 ÷ 1/2 = 6. Dividing by
a half gives a bigger answer than the number you started with —
that surprises everyone the first time, and it is right: each
whole holds two halves.

## Then the explanation
**1. A fraction times a whole number — repeated addition.**
3 × 2/5 is 2/5 + 2/5 + 2/5, which is 6/5, or 1 1/5. Kazakhstan's
Grade 5 book teaches fraction × natural number exactly this way:
(a/b) · n = a·n/b. Egypt's Grade 5 book teaches multiplying a
fraction or a mixed number by a whole number with repeated
addition, a number line and a diagram. Sierra Leone's Primary 4
teacher plans teach multiplication of like fractions, then
related fractions, then proper fractions, all with denominators
up to 12, in paired practice lessons. Libya's Grade 5 book
teaches the product of a fraction by a whole number as its own
lesson, and the fraction of a set beside it: 2/5 of 30 fruit is
12 fruit.

**2. A fraction times a fraction — top times top, bottom times
bottom.** 2/3 × 3/4: draw a rectangle, shade 2/3 one way and 3/4
the other way, and the overlap is the answer — 6/12, which is
1/2. Kazakhstan's Grade 5 book builds this from the area of a
7/8 dm by 3/5 dm rectangle on a grid: (a/b)·(c/d) = ac/bd.
Egypt's Grade 5 book teaches multiplying fractions with area
models first, then the rule, then simplifying. Jordan's Grade 5
book teaches multiplying fractions as numerator × numerator and
denominator × denominator, then simplify — and its Grade 6 book
teaches multiplying mixed numbers the same way, after turning
them improper. Greece's Grade 5 book teaches multiplying a
natural number or a fraction by a fraction, with reciprocal
numbers beside it. Vietnam's Grade 4 book teaches fraction
multiplication and then the fraction of a number as its own
lessons.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: cancel BEFORE you multiply.
> In 2/3 × 3/4, the 2 and the 4 share 2, and the 3 and the 3
> cancel outright — what is left is 1/2 × 1/1, which is 1/2.
> Small numbers are easier than big ones.

**3. Mixed numbers — make them improper first.** 1 1/2 × 2/3:
turn 1 1/2 into 3/2, then multiply: 3/2 × 2/3 = 6/6 = 1. Egypt's
Grade 5 book teaches multiplying fractions and mixed numbers
through the distributive property, then multiplying mixed numbers
using improper fractions. Jordan's Grade 6 book teaches
multiplying mixed numbers in its own lesson. Kazakhstan's Grade 5
book multiplies mixed numbers through improper fractions too, and
adds a warning the books repeat: a zero factor gives 0, and a
fraction as a coefficient — 5/8 of x — behaves the same way.

**4. Dividing fractions — turn it round and multiply.** To divide
by a fraction, you multiply by its reciprocal — the fraction
flipped over. The reciprocal of 2/3 is 3/2. So 2/3 ÷ 1/6 is
2/3 × 6/1, which is 12/3, which is 4. Kazakhstan's Grade 5 book
derives the rule instead of just stating it: from a rectangle of
area 9/25 and side 2/5, the missing side is (9/25) ÷ (2/5), found
by multiplying both sides by the reciprocal 5/2. Jordan's Grade 5
book teaches dividing fractions via the reciprocal. Egypt's Grade
6 book models division with paper folding and tape diagrams, then
connects multiplication to division with fact families and defines
the reciprocal by inverting. Greece's Grade 5 book teaches
dividing fractions with art posters as its context. Armenia's
Grade 5 book teaches reciprocal numbers and dividing fractions
together, in one lesson pair.

**5. Whole numbers and unit fractions — the two easy divisions.**
Dividing a whole number by a unit fraction asks how many of those
small pieces fit in the whole: 3 ÷ 1/4 = 12, because each whole
holds four quarters. Dividing a unit fraction by a whole number
shares the piece out: 1/3 ÷ 4 = 1/12, because one third shared
between four people is one twelfth each. Egypt's Grade 5 book
teaches both as its own lessons, with area models and with
divide-or-multiply inverse pairs — 1/3 ÷ 4 against 1/3 × 1/4.
Libya's Grade 5 book teaches fraction divided by a whole number
as its own lesson. Sierra Leone's JSS 1 book teaches division of
fractions by inverting the second fraction and multiplying, with
reciprocal vocabulary. Mauritania's Grade 6 book teaches fraction
division as its own lesson — its Grade 5 book only reaches the
idea through "fraction of" work, and says so honestly.

**6. A fraction of a number, and the whole from its fraction —
again, and working together.** Finding a fraction of a number is
multiplication: 2/5 of 30 is 12. Finding the number from its
fraction is division: 2/5 of a number is 12, so the number is
12 ÷ 2/5 = 30. Kazakhstan's Grade 5 book teaches both problem
types systematically, each also solved by an equation. Vietnam's
Grade 6 book closes its fraction chapter with exactly these two
problems. And Kazakhstan's Grade 5 book adds the joint-work
problem: the whole job is taken as 1, each worker's rate is
1 over their time, the rates add, and the together-time is 1
over the combined rate — two pumps fill a cistern in 15 minutes
on this model.

## Say it to your companion
Give your companion a division: 3/4 ÷ 1/2. Let them say the plan
first — "flip the second one and multiply: 3/4 × 2/1 is 6/4,
which is 1 1/2" — then you check it by asking: is the answer
bigger than 3/4? It must be, because dividing by a half makes
things bigger. Trade: your companion gives you 2/5 × 15, and
you say "of means multiply" before you write anything.

## Do It Yourself
1. 3 × 2/7, 5 × 3/4 (simplify it), 1/2 × 1/3.
2. 2/3 × 3/4, 3/5 × 5/6, 1 1/2 × 2/3.
3. 4 ÷ 1/2, 1/2 ÷ 4, 2/3 ÷ 1/6, 5/6 ÷ 2/3.
4. A recipe needs 2/3 of a cup of flour for one cake. How much
   flour for 3 cakes? For 1/2 a cake?
5. 3/4 of a number is 18. What is the number? Check your answer
   by multiplying back.
