# Hands-on — Multiplying and Dividing Fractions

The lesson folded and flipped real things before it wrote any
rule, the way every book starts it.

## Half of a half
Fold a paper circle in half, then fold the half in half again.
Open it: four quarters, and you hold one. 1/2 of 1/2 is 1/4.
Now fold a strip into thirds one way and quarters the other way:
the overlap of 2/3 and 3/4 is 6/12 of the strip — 1/2. The
rectangle shows the rule your eyes can see: top times top,
bottom times bottom.

## Cut the pizzas
Draw three whole pizzas. Cut each one into halves and count:
six halves. Cut them into quarters instead: twelve quarters.
Say both divisions aloud: 3 ÷ 1/2 = 6, 3 ÷ 1/4 = 12. The
smaller the piece, the more pieces fit — that is why dividing
by a fraction makes the answer bigger.

## Flip cards
Write fractions on cards: 2/3, 3/4, 5/2, 1/6. Flip each card
over — on the back, write its reciprocal: 3/2, 4/3, 2/5, 6.
Now deal a division: pick two cards, flip the second, and
multiply. The flip is the whole trick, and your hands did it
before your pencil did.

## Recipe maths
With a parent, halve a recipe: it needs 2/3 of a cup of flour
for one cake, so half a cake needs 1/3 of a cup. Then double it:
two cakes need 1 1/3 cups. Measure the flour and pour it — the
cups prove the fractions.
