**Acceptance count: taught in 12 countries (Kazakhstan, Jordan,
South Africa, Egypt, Greece, Armenia, Sierra Leone, Mauritania,
Libya, Vietnam, Ghana, Laos).** Kazakhstan carries the first share on
clock faces and the full Grade 5 fraction course with the
comparing and part-of-a-number rules; Jordan carries the youngest
path (halves and quarters only in Grade 1, then the full first
course in Grade 3 and equivalence with mixed numbers in Grade 4);
Egypt carries the model-first course — unit fractions, the number
line, and equivalence as patterns; Greece carries lived sharing
(6 sweets for 3 children, identical chocolate bars) and the
fraction-as-division chapter; Armenia carries the naming lesson
(numerator, denominator) and the coordinate-ray plotting rule;
Sierra Leone carries the picture-first primary path (teacher
plans, no pupil book) and the JSS 1 first course; Mauritania
carries fractions only from Grade 5 — its early grades teach no
fractions at all, stated honestly above; Libya carries parts of a
whole in Grade 2, numerator/denominator and equivalence in Grade
3, and the fraction of a set in Grade 4; Vietnam carries the
"one part out of how many" start and the Grade 4 fraction course;
Ghana carries fractions only as teacher-manual evidence (no pupil
textbook in the set), folding strips and bottle tops; South Africa
carries halves and quarters in Grade 2, fractions to sixths in
Grade 3, and the first definitions of proper, mixed and improper
in Grade 6. Laos carries fractions from their first appearance in Grade 3, through whole–decimal–fraction links and equivalence in Grades 4–5, to rational numbers, repeating decimals and irrationals in Grade 7.
