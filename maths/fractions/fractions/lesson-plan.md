# Lesson plan — Fractions

**Course:** Maths · **Stage:** 4, Fractions, Decimals and Percentages · **Lesson:** Fractions
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 11 of the ladder — the first lesson of
Stage 4 (CURRICULUM.md). It stands on Stage 1 (counting, place value,
comparing) and Stage 3 (multiplication and division, including sharing).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** what a fraction is — a share of
a whole or of a set, written numerator over denominator — how to read
and write it, place it on a number line, compare it, find fractions
equal to it, and tell proper, improper and mixed forms apart.
**This lesson's own level:** it needs nothing before it except
counting and sharing. It is the base the next two lessons stand on.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown
changing — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds per fold.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: counting and comparing (Stage 1), and sharing
  into equal groups (Stage 3, division). Recommended, never forced.
- Adding, subtracting, multiplying and dividing fractions are the
  next two lessons — this lesson only says what a fraction IS.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Split a whole into equal parts and name the share: half,
   quarter, third, and any unit fraction.
2. Read and write a fraction, and say what its numerator and its
   denominator each mean.
3. Find a fraction of a set (1/2 of 6 bottle tops is 3).
4. Place fractions on a number line and compare two fractions.
5. Find fractions equal to a given fraction, and tell proper,
   improper and mixed numbers apart.
6. Find a fraction of a number, and a number from its fraction.

## What you need
Paper circles and strips to fold, scissors, bottle tops or counters,
a pencil and a ruler for the number line.

   a fraction of a set; the number line; comparing; equivalent

## If the learner is stuck
- Says 1/4 is bigger than 1/2 → the books' own fix: fold both.
  The half-piece is bigger because two parts are bigger pieces
  than four parts. Same numerator, smaller denominator, bigger
  fraction.
- Writes the fraction upside down (denominator on top) → the
  denominator is DOWN, in the ground: it says how many parts the
  whole was split into. The numerator is UP, taken up: how many
  parts are taken.
- Thinks 2/4 is bigger than 1/2 → fold a strip into quarters and
  shade two; fold another into halves and shade one. Same length
  shaded — same amount, different writing.
- Cannot see a fraction of a set → count the whole group first,
  split it into equal groups, take the groups the numerator says.
