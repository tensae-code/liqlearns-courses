# Hands-on — Fractions

The lesson split real things before it wrote anything, the way
every book starts it.

## Fold and shade
Take paper circles and strips. Fold a circle into halves, open
it, shade one half. Fold another into quarters, shade two
quarters. Fold a third into eighths, shade four eighths. Lay the
three side by side: the shaded amount is the same every time.
Write under each one: 1/2, 2/4, 4/8. Same amount, different
writing — that is what "equivalent" means, and your hands found
it before the word did.

## Share the sweets
Put 12 bottle tops on the table. Share them fairly between 2
people, then between 3, then between 4, then between 6. Each
time, say the fraction one person gets — 1/2, 1/3, 1/4, 1/6 —
and how many tops that is. Then share between 4 and take 3
shares: 3/4 of 12 is 9 tops. The group is the whole; the shares
are the fractions.

## The fraction number line
Draw a long line on the floor with chalk or tape, from 0 to 2.
Mark 0, 1 and 2 first. Then walk the fractions: stand on 1/2,
then 1/4, then 3/4, then 1 1/2. Each time, say whether you moved
right (bigger) or left (smaller). Finish by standing on two
fractions that are equal — 1/2 and 2/4 — and feeling that they
are the same spot.

## Kitchen fractions
With a parent, cut a real food into equal parts: an orange into
quarters, a flat bread into eighths. Serve the pieces and name
each serving as a fraction: "You get 2/8 — that is 1/4 of the
bread." Eat the proof: equal parts taste the same.
