# Hands-on — Adding and Subtracting Fractions

The lesson added pieces before it added numbers, the way every
book starts it.

## Pizza pieces
Cut paper circles into quarters, fifths and sixths. Put 1/4 and
2/4 together and count the pieces: 3/4. Take 1/5 away from 3/5
and count what is left: 2/5. Every sum and difference is a pile
of same-size pieces first, and numbers second.

## The fraction wall
Draw a fraction wall to twelfths on a long strip of paper: one
whole, halves, thirds, quarters, sixths, twelfths. To add 1/2
and 1/3, lay both rows against the twelfths row and read off:
3/6 + 2/6 is 5/6 — or in twelfths, 6/12 + 4/12 is 10/12, which
is the same 5/6. The wall finds the common denominator with
your eyes.

## Borrow a whole
Take three whole paper pizzas and a half-pizza. You must give
away 1 1/4 pizzas. Take one whole pizza, cut it into quarters,
and give the quarters away with the half. Count what is left:
2 1/4 pizzas. The borrowed whole became four quarters — that
is what "borrowing" looks like.

## Ribbon stories
Cut a ribbon 5/6 of a metre long (mark it on a metre stick).
Cut off 1/3 of a metre. Measure what is left: 1/2 a metre.
Now write the sum that says what you did: 5/6 − 1/3 = 1/2.
The story came first; the sum is its record.
