# Hands-on — Percentages

The lesson shaded a 100-grid before it wrote any rule, the way
every book starts it.

## The 100-grid
Draw a 10-by-10 grid. Shade 25 squares and write 25% under it.
Shade 25 more: 50%. Shade 25 more: 75%. Shade the last 25: 100%
— the whole grid. Now shade 1 square: 1%. Shade 10: 10%. The grid
is the percent: every percent question is a question about how
many squares are shaded.

## Percent of your class
Count your class (or your family at the table). If 12 out of 48
walk to school, what percent is that? Work it: 12/48 × 100 =
25%. Now count something else — how many drink water with lunch,
how many wear glasses — and turn each count into a percent of
the group. The group is the 100-grid, whatever its size.

## Shop discount
With a parent, find a real price tag and a sale sign: 25% off,
15% off, 50% off. Work the sale price two ways — 1% first, then
the fraction way — and check they agree. Then add a tax: if the
shop adds 10%, what is the final price? The sale sign is the
worksheet, and the till is the answer key.

## Tip the bill
At a meal, take the bill and work a 15% tip the way Egypt's
Grade 6 book does: 10% first, then half of that again for 5%,
then add them. A 340 bill gives 34 + 17 = 51. Check it against
the phone calculator — the two ways must agree.
