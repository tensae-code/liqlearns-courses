# Lesson plan — Percentages

**Course:** Maths · **Stage:** 4, Fractions, Decimals and Percentages · **Lesson:** Percentages
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 15 of the ladder — the fifth
lesson of Stage 4 (CURRICULUM.md). It stands on the Fractions
lesson and the Decimals lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** percentages — what a
percent is, turning fractions and decimals into percents and
back, finding a percentage of a number, finding the whole,
finding the percentage, increase and decrease, and money:
discount, tax and tip.
**This lesson's own level:** it needs the Fractions lesson and
the Decimals lesson, and nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown
changing — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds per square.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the Fractions lesson (Level 11) and the
  Decimals lesson (Level 14). Recommended, never forced.
- Ratio and Proportion is the next lesson — this lesson only
  does percentages.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a percent is: a number out of 100.
2. Turn fractions and decimals into percents and percents into
   fractions and decimals.
3. Find a percentage of a number, two ways.
4. Find the whole from a part and a percent, and find what
   percentage one number is of another.
5. Work out a new price after an increase or a decrease.
6. Work out a discount, a tax and a tip on a real bill.

## What you need
A 10-by-10 grid on paper, coloured pencils, price tags, pencil
and paper.

## If the learner is stuck
- Writes 25% as 25/10 → the percent sign means out of 100,
  never out of 10. 25% is 25/100.
- Finds 20% of 50 as 20 → find 1% first: 1% of 50 is 0.5, so 20%
  is 10. Or 20/100 × 50 = 10.
- Adds two discounts together (20% then 10% is not 30% off) →
  the second cut acts on the already-cut price. Work it in two
  steps, never one.
- Mixes up "percent of" and "percent off" → "20% of 50" is 10;
  "20% off 50" is 50 − 10 = 40. The words "off" and "of" are
  different jobs.
