# Percentages — the lesson

Course: Maths · Stage 4: Fractions, Decimals and Percentages · Lesson: Percentages
Built from: Kazakhstan, Grade 4 Part 3 and Grade 5 — Jordan, Grade 5
Term 2 and Grade 6 Term 2 — Egypt, Grade 6 Term 2 — Greece, Grade 5
and Grade 6 — Armenia, Grade 6 — Sierra Leone, Primary 5 lesson
plans and JSS 1 Term 1 — Mauritania, Grade 5 and Grade 6 — Libya,
Grade 6 — Vietnam, Grade 5 Volume 2 and Grade 6 Volume 2 — Ghana,
JICA teacher manual B4 (teacher evidence) — South Africa, Rainbow
Workbooks Grades 6, 7 and 8.

## Do it first
Take a 10-by-10 grid — 100 little squares. Shade 25 of them. You
have shaded 25 out of 100. That is 25 percent, written 25%.
Shade 50: 50%. Shade all 100: 100% — the whole thing. A percent
is just a fraction with 100 underneath, given its own name and
its own sign. Every percent question in this lesson is a question
about a 100-grid.

## Then the explanation
**1. Percent means "out of 100".** The word percent comes from
"per hundred", and the sign % means /100. 1% is 1/100. 25% is
25/100, which is 1/4. 50% is 1/2. 75% is 3/4. 100% is the whole.
Kazakhstan's Grade 4 book defines percent exactly this way — its
"Remember!" box states that the share 1/100 is called a percent
and 1/100 = 1% — and finds 1% by dividing the whole into 100
equal parts, then p% as 1% × p, worked on money, mass and
length. Jordan's Grade 5 book introduces percent as a fraction
of 100 in its decimals unit. Egypt's Grade 6 book opens its
percent unit by exploring percent on 100-grids. Greece's Grade 5
book teaches the percent concept as its own chapter. Armenia's
Grade 6 book opens its percent chapter with absolute and
relative comparisons, then the percent itself. Sierra
Leone's JSS 1 book starts with "part of 100" and its Primary 5
teacher plans teach percent as part of 100. Ghana's B4 teacher
manual teaches percent from decimal fractions with the note that
"cent means 100". South Africa's Grade 6 workbooks teach
percentages beside fractions and decimals.

**2. Fractions, decimals and percents are three writings of one
number.** 1/4 = 0.25 = 25%. 3/5 = 0.6 = 60%. 7/10 = 0.7 = 70%.
To turn a fraction into a percent, make the denominator 100
where you can, or divide and multiply by 100: 3/20 is 15/100,
which is 15%. To turn a percent into a decimal, drop the sign
and divide by 100: 35% is 0.35. Kazakhstan's Grade 5 book carries
percent as its own chapter, with conversion between fractions,
decimals and percents. Jordan's Grade 6 book teaches percent
with fractions and percent with decimals as two lessons. Egypt's
Grade 6 book teaches the percent–fraction–decimal conversions
as its own lesson. Greece's Grade 5 book teaches percent
conversions, and its Grade 6 book estimates percentages. Armenia's
Grade 6 book teaches relative comparison and cross
multiplication before the percent itself. Sierra Leone's JSS 1 book teaches converting between
fractions, decimals and percents, and its Primary 5 teacher plans
teach the same conversions. South Africa's Grade 6 workbooks
teach percentages with fractions and percentages with decimals
side by side.

**3. Finding a percentage of a number.** Two ways, and they are
the same way underneath: find 1% first, then multiply — or turn
the percent into a fraction and multiply. 20% of 50 dinars is
1% of 50 (which is 0.5) × 20, which is 10 dinars; or 20/100 × 50,
which is also 10. Jordan's Grade 6 book teaches "percent of a
number" as its own lesson and prices a bicycle at 50 dinars.
Kazakhstan's Grade 4 book finds p% as 1% × p. Egypt's Grade 6
book teaches finding a percentage of a number as its own lesson.
Greece's Grade 6 book teaches finding a percentage as its own
chapter. Armenia's Grade 6 book works percent problems, including
finding a whole from a percent. Sierra Leone's JSS 1 book teaches "percentage of a
quantity", and its Primary 5 teacher plans teach percent of a
quantity. Libya's Grade 6 book teaches finding a percentage of
a quantity: a salary of 2,400 dinars a month, spending 25% and
30% of it — 600 dinars and 720 dinars.

**4. Finding the whole, and finding the percentage.** If 30% of a
number is 60, the whole is 60 ÷ 30 × 100, which is 200 — or
60 ÷ 0.30, which is also 200. If 15 out of 60 pupils walk to
school, that is 15/60 × 100, which is 25%. Egypt's Grade 6 book
teaches finding the whole from a part and a percent, and finding
the percentage from a part and a whole, as two lessons. Greece's
Grade 6 book teaches finding the initial value and the rate as
its own chapters. Armenia's Grade 6 book teaches finding a
number from its percentage and works further percent problems.
Sierra Leone's JSS 1 book teaches "one
quantity as a percentage of another" and "finding the whole
from a percentage". Libya's Grade 6 book teaches a quantity as
a percentage of another, including across units — centimetres
against metres, millilitres against litres.

**5. Increase and decrease.** A price rises by 20%: the new price
is 100% + 20% = 120% of the old one. A price falls by 15%: the
new price is 85% of the old one. Sierra Leone's JSS 1 book
teaches percentage increase, percentage decrease and the new
quantity, with Hawa's profit rising by 21%. South Africa's Grade
7 workbooks teach percentage increase and decrease as their own
worksheet. Greece's Grade 6 book teaches the final value after
a change and the initial value before it. Armenia's Grade 6 book
carries further percent work after its percent problems.
Egypt's Grade 6 book asks about successive discounts — a price
cut twice is not the sum of the two cuts, because the second
cut acts on the already-cut price.

**6. Money: discount, tax and tip.** A discount is a percentage
off the price: a computer with a 15% discount costs 85% of its
price. A tax is a percentage added on: 5% tax on a 340 LE lunch bill
is 17 LE. A tip is a percentage added by choice:
15% tip on the same bill is 51 LE, so the bill with tax and
tip is 340 + 17 + 51 = 408 LE. Egypt's Grade 6 book works
exactly this lunch bill — 340 LE, 5% tax, 15% tip — and teaches
tax, tip and discount as applications. Libya's Grade 6 book
works a 15% computer discount. Jordan's Grade 6 book works a
discount on its 50-dinar bicycle. Sierra Leone's Primary 5
teacher plans teach percent in money problems, and its JSS 1
book teaches percent with money.

## Say it to your companion
Read these aloud to your companion: 25% is "twenty-five percent",
and it is also "one quarter" and "zero point two five". Then your
companion gives you a percent — 60% — and you say it three ways:
"sixty percent", "three fifths", "zero point six". Trade: your
companion gives you a price — 200 — and a discount — 10% — and
you say the new price before you write it: 180.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write as percents: 1/2, 1/4, 3/5, 7/10, 0.35, 0.08.
2. Write as fractions and decimals: 20%, 45%, 75%, 6%.
3. 20% of 50, 30% of 90, 75% of 240, 5% of 340.
4. 40% of a number is 80. What is the number? 12 out of 48 is
   what percent?
5. A jacket costs 120 and is discounted by 25%. What do you pay?
   Then a 10% tax is added to the discounted price. What is the
   final price?
