# Lesson plan — The Unitary Method

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 4 — Fractions, Decimals, Percentages, Ratio · **Lesson:** The Unitary Method
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 56 of 136, inside Unit 4 — Fractions, Decimals, Percentages, Ratio (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the unitary method — go from many to ONE, then from one to as many as you need.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on multiplying and dividing, and on ratio and proportion — the learner can already share and scale simple amounts. It needs nothing else.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The Unitary Method Many to One** — given the price or measure of many, finding the one by dividing. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **The Unitary Method One to Many, Direct Proportion** — passing through the one and multiplying up to any number, in direct proportion. (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **The Unitary Method Inverse Proportion, and Choosing the Kind** — inverse proportion through the one, and choosing direct or inverse before calculating. (Was depth Level 3.)
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson (no hardness ranking is written in this lesson's question files).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: ratio and proportion (Stage 4) — the unitary method is proportion worked one step at a time. Recommended, never forced.
- Recommended first: multiplying and dividing whole numbers and money amounts. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Find the value of ONE from the value of many, by dividing (Pakistan's Grade 5 book names and drills the unitary method, pp.89–90).
2. Find the value of any number of items from the value of one, by multiplying (Bangladesh's Class 5 book, §3.3 Unitary Method, p.17 — price of one item, then of many).
3. Solve direct-proportion problems by the many → one → many road (Pakistan, Grade 5, pp.92–93).
4. Solve inverse-proportion problems — more workers, fewer days — by passing through one (Pakistan, Grade 5, pp.94–95).
5. Say which kind a problem is before calculating: direct (both grow together) or inverse (one grows, the other shrinks) (Pakistan, Grade 5, pp.91–92).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Real prices help: a handful of pens, eggs or exercise books with a price for the bunch.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner is told 5 pens cost 75 and asked what 8 pens cost, in their own way, before any method is shown (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — many → one; one → many; direct proportion by the unitary road; inverse proportion by the unitary road (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the many → one part: a picture of 5 pens with a price tag of 75 shrinking to 1 pen with a tag of 15 — the student watches the price divide as the pens leave, one by one.
   - ADD IMAGE in the one → many part: a picture of a market stall price card reading "1 egg = 8, so 30 eggs = 240" → the student reads the card and says the two steps aloud: divide, then multiply.
   - ADD IMAGE in the inverse part: a picture of 6 workers digging a pit marked "10 days", then 12 workers marked "5 days" → the student explains why doubling the workers halves the days.
3. **Say it to your companion** — trade price problems and worker problems, each solved aloud through the ONE (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the unitary chant in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — price one, then many, at a real stall or from a real receipt; and a workers-and-days problem from home (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Jumps straight to the big number and guesses → Pakistan's Grade 5 road always passes through ONE first (pp.89–90): write the "1 … =" line before anything else.
- Divides when the problem is inverse → ask the sense question first: "More workers — more days or fewer days?" Fewer means inverse: find what ONE worker needs, then share it out (Pakistan, pp.94–95).
- Loses the unit → every line carries its word: "1 pen costs 15", never a bare 15.
