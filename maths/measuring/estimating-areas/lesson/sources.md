**Acceptance count: taught in 9 countries (South Sudan, Malaysia, Rwanda, India, Mongolia, North Macedonia, Albania, Bhutan, Costa Rica).** South Sudan first carried this lesson alone: its Secondary 4 Mathematics student book teaches area estimation as one run in Unit 2 — the area of basic and irregular shapes (p.20), approximating an irregular area on grid paper by counting squares (pp.21–24), then the mid-ordinate rule (pp.25–26) and the trapezium rule (pp.26–29). The filing is explicit that the S4 Additional Mathematics book does NOT teach area estimation, so nothing is cited from it. No other read country's filing teaches these rules as a topic; the lesson is therefore built at the size this one source supports, and its worked numbers are the builder's own examples of the book's three named methods, not quotations from the book. North Macedonia enters from its main set — Grade 6 (2023).

Malaysia joins South Sudan in this lesson with the first of its three methods: Form 1 opens its area work by estimating an irregular area on a grid, counting full squares and half-or-more squares, before any area formula is derived. The mid-ordinate rule and the trapezium rule remain South Sudan's alone.

Rwanda joins this lesson in Year 6, estimating the area bounded by a circle by counting squares on squared paper before the area is stated in terms of r².
