Match each item on the left with the one right partner on the right.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Match each method to what it does.
Methods: (i) counting squares  (ii) mid-ordinate rule  (iii) trapezium rule
Descriptions: (a) strips treated as trapeziums from edge ordinates  (b) whole and part grid squares counted  (c) strips treated as rectangles from middle ordinates
**Answer:** (i)–(b), (ii)–(c), (iii)–(a) — the three methods in the order South Sudan's S4 book teaches them (pp.21–29).

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** Match each data set (strip width 2 m) to its mid-ordinate estimate.
Data: (i) mid-ordinates 2, 4  (ii) mid-ordinates 1, 3, 5  (iii) mid-ordinates 3, 3, 3, 3
Estimates: (a) 24 m²  (b) 12 m²  (c) 18 m²
**Answer:** (i)–(b) 2 × 6, (ii)–(c) 2 × 9, (iii)–(a) 2 × 12 (South Sudan, S4, pp.25–26).

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** Match each part of the trapezium-rule formula to its job.
Parts: (i) strip width ÷ 2  (ii) first + last ordinates  (iii) 2 × middle ordinates
Jobs: (a) the shared ordinates, counted for both trapeziums they touch  (b) half the strip width, the trapezium average factor  (c) the two end ordinates, counted once each
**Answer:** (i)–(b), (ii)–(c), (iii)–(a) (South Sudan, S4, pp.26–29).
