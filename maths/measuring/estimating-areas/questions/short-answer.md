# Estimating Areas — short answer

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Estimating Areas — Counting Squares · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Estimating Areas — the Mid-Ordinate Rule · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Estimating Areas — the Trapezium Rule.

Answer in a word or a short line.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Why can a leaf's area not be found exactly with the rectangle formula?
**Answer:** Its edge is curved — it has no single length and width (South Sudan, S4, p.20).

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** A shape covers 22 whole grid squares and parts joining to about 6 more; each square is 1 m². Estimate the area.
**Answer:** About 28 m² — 22 + 6 (South Sudan, S4, pp.21–24).

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Write the mid-ordinate rule in words.
**Answer:** Area ≈ strip width × (sum of the mid-ordinates) (South Sudan, S4, pp.25–26).

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** Mid-ordinates for 5 strips of width 4 m are 6, 9, 11, 8, 5 (metres). Estimate the area.
**Answer:** About 156 m² — 4 × 39 (South Sudan, S4, pp.25–26).

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Edge ordinates at 2 m spacing are 0, 4, 6, 7, 6, 4, 0. Estimate the area by the trapezium rule.
**Answer:** About 54 m² — (2 ÷ 2) × [(0 + 0) + 2 × 27] = 54 (South Sudan, S4, pp.26–29).
