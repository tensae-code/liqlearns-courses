Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** A shape on grid paper covers 14 whole squares and part squares that join into about 4 whole ones. Each square is 1 cm². The estimated area is about ______.
a) 10 cm²  b) 18 cm²  c) 56 cm²
**Answer:** b) 18 cm² — 14 + 4 joined squares (South Sudan, S4, pp.21–24).

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** When counting squares, a part square that covers clearly less than half a grid square is ______.
a) counted as 1  b) counted as 0  c) counted as 2
**Answer:** b) counted as 0 — small parts are balanced by the bigger parts counted as wholes (South Sudan, S4, pp.21–24).

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Strips are 2 m wide and the mid-ordinates are 3, 5, 6, 4 (metres). The mid-ordinate estimate is ______.
a) 18 m²  b) 36 m²  c) 9 m²
**Answer:** b) 36 m² — 2 × (3 + 5 + 6 + 4) = 2 × 18 (South Sudan, S4, pp.25–26).

**4. (Sub lesson 3)** Ordinates at 3 m spacing are 0, 4, 5, 0. The trapezium-rule estimate is ______.
a) 13.5 m²  b) 27 m²  c) 40.5 m²
**Answer:** b) 27 m² — (3 ÷ 2) × [(0 + 0) + 2 × (4 + 5)] = 1.5 × 18 (South Sudan, S4, pp.26–29).

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Two rules give 54 m² and 57 m² for the same plot. The right conclusion is ______.
a) one rule must be wrong  b) both are estimates, and their closeness supports them  c) the true area is exactly 55.5 m²
**Answer:** b) both are estimates, and their closeness supports them — no rule is exact on a curved edge (South Sudan, S4, pp.20–29).
