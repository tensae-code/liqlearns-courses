# Lesson plan — Temperature and Traditional Measures

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 8 — Measuring, Area and Volume · **Lesson:** Temperature and Traditional Measures
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 69 of 136, in Unit 8 — Measuring, Area and Volume (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** measuring heat in degrees Celsius and Fahrenheit and converting between them — and the measures people used before standard units: body units, land units and traditional weights, which the books of five countries still teach by name.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Measuring (units and converting). It needs nothing else.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Measuring (Unit 8) — temperature is one more measure with its own units, and traditional measures are the non-standard units that lesson begins with. Recommended, never forced.
- Errors and Accuracy (Level 68, same unit) is a helpful neighbour — body measures differ from person to person, which is exactly an accuracy problem. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Read a thermometer in degrees Celsius and say what the reading means against the fixed points — ice melting at 0 °C, water boiling at 100 °C (South Africa, Grade 5 Workbook 2, WS94–95; Grade 6 Workbook 2, WS85–86).
2. Convert between Celsius and Fahrenheit with the correct formulas, °F = °C × 9/5 + 32 and °C = (°F − 32) × 5/9 (South Sudan, Pupil's Book 5, §2.5, pp.42–43; Nepal, Grade 4, Lesson 7 §7.2, pp.105–111).
3. Measure length the old way, with the body units Nicaragua's Grade 1 book names — jeme, brazada, paso, pulgada — and say why body units cannot be the standard (Nicaragua, Grade 1 NICAMATE 2, Unit 9, pp.146–153).
4. Work with Nepal's land units — 1 ropani = 16 aana, 1 aana = 31.79 m², with paisa and daam below the aana, and katha and dhur in the other system (Nepal, Grade 9, Unit 5 §5.2, Exercise 5.2, p.104).
5. Name the traditional measures of Mauritania, Kazakhstan and Laos and use the values Mauritania's book gives: cubit ≈ 49.5 cm, raṭl ≈ 312.5 g, ṣāʿ ≈ 2.5 L (Mauritania, 6AS-LO, Ch. 1, pp.8–13; Kazakhstan, Grade 5, Part 1, §2, p.12; Laos, Grade 5, L31, pp.76–89).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A thermometer if you have one. Your own hands and feet — the oldest measuring tools there are.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner measures a table first in hand-spans, then in centimetres, and compares the two answers with a partner's (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — temperature and the thermometer; the two scales and converting; body units; land units; traditional weights and measures by country (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE at the temperature part: a picture of a thermometer standing in ice water beside one standing in steam, marked 0 °C and 100 °C → the student reads both scales at both points.
   - ADD GIF at the converting part: a picture of a temperature sliding from 0 °C to 100 °C while its Fahrenheit twin slides from 32 °F to 212 °F → the student calls out the Fahrenheit value at 20, 37 and 50.
   - ADD IMAGE at the body-units part: a picture of a child and an adult measuring the same table in hand-spans, getting different counts → the student says why the counts differ.

3. **Say it to your companion** — convert temperatures for your companion, then measure the room in pasos and compare counts (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — temperature and the old measures in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching, ordering — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — convert five temperatures, and measure one room two ways: body units and standard units (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Forgets the 32 → the scales do not start at the same zero: 0 °C is 32 °F. Going to Fahrenheit, multiply first, then add 32. Coming back, take 32 away first, then multiply. A conversion formula without the 32 is simply wrong.
- Gets a Fahrenheit answer below the Celsius one for a warm day → above −40 the Fahrenheit number is always the bigger one for warm temperatures; 50 °C is 122 °F, never 90 °F.
- Counts body units and expects everyone to agree → that disagreement is the lesson: your span is yours alone. Standard units exist so the answer is the same for everyone.
- Mixes the two Nepali land systems → ropani/aana/paisa/daam belong together; katha/dhur belong together. Do not cross them.
