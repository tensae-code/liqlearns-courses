# Hands-on — Temperature and Traditional Measures

The lesson read heat on a thermometer and measured the old way, with the body.

## Three waters
Fill three bowls: cold water, room water, warm water. If you have a thermometer, read each one in Celsius and write the readings in order. If you do not, order the bowls by touch and say which could be near 0 °C, which near body temperature (about 37 °C), and which too hot to hold a hand in. Touch gives the order; the thermometer gives the number.

## Convert and check back
Take five temperatures: 0 °C, 20 °C, 37 °C, 50 °C, 100 °C. Convert each to Fahrenheit (× 9/5, then + 32). Then convert each answer back (− 32, then × 5/9). You must land on the number you started with — if you do not, the 32 went in the wrong place.

## The span fight
Measure a table in hand-spans (jeme). Your partner measures the same table in their hand-spans. Compare the counts: they differ, because hands differ. Now measure in centimetres: one answer, the same for both. Write one sentence on why the world needed standard units — Nicaragua's Grade 1 book starts measuring with exactly these body units (jeme, brazada, paso, pulgada), and this activity shows why it cannot end there.

## A field in ropani
Draw a square field of 2 ropani. Divide each ropani into its 16 aana and count the aana in the field (32). Using 1 aana = 31.79 m², work out the field's area in square metres (32 × 31.79 = 1,017.28 m²). The same field, two languages of measure — Nepal's books set cost problems on land in exactly these units.
