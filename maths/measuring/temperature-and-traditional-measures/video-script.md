# Video script — Temperature and Traditional Measures

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the
lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are
made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

How hot is it? Put the thermometer in ice water.
Zero! **Zero degrees Celsius.** Ice melts at zero.

Now in steam, over boiling water. **One hundred!**
Water boils at one hundred degrees Celsius.

America says it in Fahrenheit. Ice is **thirty-two**.
Boiling is **two hundred and twelve**.
To change Celsius to Fahrenheit: times nine, divide by five,
and **do not forget — add thirty-two!**
Thirty-seven Celsius — your body — is ninety-eight point six Fahrenheit.

Long ago, people measured with the body.
A span of the hand! A step! An arm stretched wide!
But my span is not your span. So the answers fought.
In Nepal, land is ropani and aana — sixteen aana in one ropani.
In Mauritania, the cubit, the raṭl, the ṣāʿ.
Every country had its measures. Then the world chose standard ones —
so a measure means the same everywhere.
