# Lesson plan — Perimeter and Area

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** 8, Measuring, Area and Volume · **Lesson:** Perimeter and Area (lesson 2 of 4 in the unit)
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 8, "Measuring, Area and Volume" — the second lesson of the unit (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the distance round a shape (perimeter) and the flat space it covers (area), for rectangles, triangles, parallelograms and trapeziums.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the measuring lesson (a perimeter is a length; an area is counted in square units) and on the shapes lesson (the shapes whose perimeters and areas these are). It needs nothing else.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Perimeter and Area — Perimeter** — finding a perimeter by adding the sides, and by the short cuts for a square (4l), a rectangle (2l + 2w) and a triangle (a + b + c), in the right unit. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Perimeter and Area — Areas of Rectangles, Squares and Triangles** — areas with A = l × w, A = l² and A = ½bh, finding a missing side from a known area, with the height perpendicular to the base. (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **Perimeter and Area — Parallelograms, Trapeziums and Composite Shapes** — areas with A = base × height and A = ½(a + b)h, splitting a composite shape and adding its pieces, and why the same area can have different perimeters. (Was depth Level 3.)

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Measuring, then Shapes — perimeter is a
  length walked round a shape you can name. Recommended, never
  forced.
- Circle perimeter and area (circumference, πr²) belong to the
  circles lesson in Unit 7; this lesson works the straight-sided
  shapes, and the circles lesson is the companion, not a repeat.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Find the perimeter of any polygon by adding its sides, and by
   the short cuts: square 4l, rectangle 2l + 2w, triangle a + b + c.
2. Find areas with A = l × w (rectangle), A = l² (square),
   A = ½ × base × height (triangle), A = base × height
   (parallelogram) and A = ½(a + b)h (trapezium).
3. Split a composite shape into pieces, find each piece, and add
   them — and know that the same area can have different
   perimeters, and the same perimeter can hold different areas.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A ruler. Squared paper. Scissors.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner walks round a table (perimeter)
   and covers it with books (area) (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — perimeter by adding and by short cuts;
   area by counting squares, then by formulas; the trapezium;
   composite shapes (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE at the perimeter part: a picture of a garden with
     a fence round it and the fence length written on each side —
     the student adds the sides to find the fence.
   - ADD GIF at the triangle-area part: a picture of a rectangle
     cut along its diagonal into two triangles — the student sees
     each triangle is half the rectangle.
   - ADD IMAGE at the trapezium part: a picture of a trapezium
     turned into a parallelogram by doubling it — the student
     sees why its area is half the sum of the two parallel sides
     times the height.
3. **Say it to your companion** — find perimeters and areas for
   your companion, and say which formula you used and why
   (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the
     perimeter walk and the area cover in the super-simple voice
     (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served
   at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — measure a real room and a real field and
   find their perimeters and areas (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Uses the slant side of a triangle or parallelogram as its height
  → the books' own rule: the height is PERPENDICULAR to the base,
  the straight drop, not the slant. Sierra Leone says it plainly:
  base and height must be perpendicular.
- Mixes perimeter and area → perimeter is a length (cm), area is
  a covering (cm²). Fencing is perimeter; carpet is area.
- Forgets the half in the triangle → cut the rectangle in half:
  a triangle is half of its rectangle, so its area is half of
  length × width. The half is the whole point.
- This lesson's next door: Volume and Surface Area (same unit) —
  area grows up into volume, and surface area is area counted
  over a solid's faces.
