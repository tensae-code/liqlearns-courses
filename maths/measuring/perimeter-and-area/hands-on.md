# Hands-on — Perimeter and Area

The lesson added sides and counted squares on paper; here the
hands walk and cover real things.

## Walk the perimeter
Walk round a table, a room, a garden bed. Count your steps, then
measure each side with a tape or a metre stick and add them. The
steps and the tape must agree, roughly. Now walk round a bigger
shape and a smaller one: the bigger walk is the bigger perimeter.

## Cover the area
Cover a tabletop with books, then with sheets of paper. Count how
many fit. Now cover it with squared paper and count the squares —
that count is the area in square units. The books and the squares
are the same idea: area is how many unit squares cover a shape.

## Cut the triangle
Draw a rectangle on squared paper, 6 squares by 4. Count its area:
24 squares. Cut it along the diagonal. Two triangles, each 12
squares. That is why the triangle's area is half of base times
height — you just cut it in half with your own hands.

## Same area, different fence
Egypt's own investigation: make rectangles with area 24 cm² — 6
by 4, 8 by 3, 12 by 2, 24 by 1. Work out each one's perimeter:
20, 22, 28, 50. Same area, very different fences. The long thin
one needs the longest fence. Now make shapes with perimeter 20 cm
and different areas. The square holds the most.
