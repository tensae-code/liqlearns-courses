# Hands-on — Scale Drawing and Maps

The lesson converted lengths on paper; here the hands measure a
real room and draw it small.

## Draw your room at 1:100
Measure your room's length and width with a tape. At 1:100 every
real metre becomes 1 cm on paper — Laos's Grade 6 book plans a
room at exactly this scale (L21, printed pp.167–174). Draw the
walls, then measure and add one big thing in the room (a bed, a
table) at the same scale. Check: does your drawing's longest wall
match the tape's number divided by 100?

## The Kuwait Towers model
Kuwait's Grade 10 project models towers 187 m, 147 m and 113 m
tall at 1:500, where 1 cm of model is 5 m of tower (printed
pp.81–82). On paper, draw three lines for the three towers at
that scale: 37.4 cm, 29.4 cm and 22.6 cm. The tallest line will
not fit across one page — that is the point: a scale is chosen
to fit the page.

## Map of my place
South Africa's Grade 3 workbook starts map work by drawing the
places themselves — library, school, clinic, hospital, police
station, shopping centre — and giving directions between them
(Worksheet 68). Draw your own street or village the same way,
then write three direction sentences with compass points: "The
clinic is north-east of the school."

## Grid enlargement
Laos's Grade 6 book enlarges and reduces figures on grids (L21,
printed pp.167–174), and Yemen's Grade 6 book teaches
enlargement and reduction just before scale drawing (5-3,
printed p.17). Copy a small drawing square by square onto a
grid of squares twice as big, then onto a grid half as big.
Every length doubles, then halves — the shape never changes.
