**Acceptance count: taught in 16 countries (Laos, Kuwait, Libya, Vietnam, Mauritania, Yemen, South Africa, Nepal, Greece, Jordan, Indonesia, Syria, Malaysia, Rwanda, Sudan, India).** Laos carries the lesson whole: scale met in Grade 5 (L52), defined and worked in both directions in Grade 6 (L21–L22, with bar scales and scale changes), restated with a 2× enlargement in Grade 7 (L43), and kept as a proportion application in Grade 9 (L6). Kuwait carries it as a real modelling project — the Kuwait Towers at 1:500 — with scale drawing worked inside proportion (Grade 10 Term 1). Libya carries it as its own named section (Grade 8, §2-8). Vietnam carries map scale and applications in Grade 5 (Bài 37). Mauritania carries proportionality with map scale in Grade 6. Yemen carries enlargement/reduction and then scale drawing as consecutive taught lessons (Grade 6 Part 2, 5-3 and 5-4). South Africa carries the groundwork: drawing and reading maps of real places in Grade 3 and position on a map grid in Grade 4. Nepal carries compass points and word-form scales on a map of Nepal (Grade 7, Unit 10) — compass points, not three-figure bearings. Greece carries the closing part alone: geographic coordinates (latitude/longitude) as an application of spherical coordinates (Grade 8, Ch. 4). Jordan carries scale drawing as a named lesson in its congruence-and-similarity unit (Grade 7 Term 2, Unit 6, L2). Indonesia carries it inside ratio: scale factor and map/plan scale taught as ratio applications in its Grade 7 Ratio chapter (Ch. 3, printed pp.83–122).

Syria defines scale as a proportionality coefficient in Grade 7 and reuses scale drawing inside the Grade 8 proportion exercises.

Malaysia teaches scale drawing as a whole Form 3 chapter — scale found two ways, maps read, drawings made on three kinds of paper, and the area ratio investigated rather than assumed.

Rwanda teaches scale drawing in Year 5 as its own unit: the scale as a unit-less ratio, drawings constructed, and actual and drawing lengths found from each other.

Sudan teaches the scale of a drawing in Grade 5.

India enters scale drawing and maps from Math-Magic Class 5 and Mathematics Class 8: matching map to photo; maps vs pictures: symbols, fixed scale, no perspective; reading and drawing maps.
