# Lesson plan — Scale Drawing and Maps

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 8 — Measuring, Area and Volume · **Lesson:** Scale Drawing and Maps
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 64 of 136, in Unit 8 — Measuring, Area and Volume (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a drawing or map is the real thing shrunk (or grown) by one fixed ratio — the scale — and with that one ratio you can go both ways: real length to drawing length, and drawing length back to real length.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on ratio and proportion (a scale IS a ratio) and on measuring lengths. It needs nothing else.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a scale is — scale = drawing length ÷ real length, in the same unit — and read scales like 1:100 and 1:500,000 (Laos, Grade 6 student book, L21, printed pp.167–174).
2. Turn a real length into a drawing length, and a drawing length back into a real length, with the two formulas: drawing = real × scale; real = drawing ÷ scale (Laos, Grade 6 student book, L22, printed pp.175–182).
3. Read and use a bar scale — for example 1 cm = 5 km on the bar means the ratio scale 1:500,000 (Laos, Grade 6 student book, L21, printed pp.167–174).
4. Draw a real place or object to scale, the way Kuwait's Grade 10 book models the Kuwait Towers at 1:500 (Kuwait, Grade 10 Term 1 student book, project, printed pp.81–82) and the way South Africa's workbooks draw and read maps of real places (South Africa, Grade 3 Workbook 2, Worksheet 68; Grade 4 Book 2, Worksheets 133–134).
5. Use the eight compass points on a map — points, not three-figure bearings (Nepal, Grade 7 student book, Unit 10, printed pp.84–88).
6. Say where a place is with geographic coordinates — latitude and longitude — the part Greece's Grade 8 book teaches as an application of spherical coordinates (Greece, Grade 8 pupil book, Ch. 4, printed pp.201–235).
7. Name and use the scale factor — the one multiplier that turns every real length into its drawing length — and treat a map scale or plan scale as a ratio at work, the way Indonesia's Grade 7 book teaches scale inside its Ratio chapter (Indonesia, Grade 7 Merdeka student book, Ch. 3, printed pp.83–122).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A ruler. A tape measure if you have one. Squared paper helps for enlarging and reducing.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes the two lengths up → the scale is always drawing ÷ real, and both lengths must be in the SAME unit first. Laos's books convert before they divide: 1 cm = 5 km becomes 1 cm = 500,000 cm before it becomes 1:500,000.
- Reads 1:500,000 as "1 cm is 500,000 km" → the second number is in the same unit as the first: 1 cm on the map is 500,000 cm in real life, and 500,000 cm is 5 km.
- Thinks a bigger second number means a bigger drawing → it is the other way: 1:500,000 shrinks far more than 1:100. An enlargement flips the ratio the other way — Laos's 5:1 makes the drawing five times the real thing.
- Calls Nepal's compass points "bearings" → Nepal's Grade 7 book teaches the eight compass points (N, NE, E, …) and the angles between them (N–E is 90°, N–NE is 45°). Three-figure bearings are a different lesson (Bearings, Stage 9) — do not rename the points.
- This lesson's next door: Estimating Areas (same stage) — once a map can be read for lengths, its irregular shapes can be read for areas.
