# Hands-on — Volume and Surface Area

The lesson counted cubes and unwrapped nets on paper; here the
hands fill boxes and unwrap them.

## Fill the box
Take a small box and fill it with sugar cubes, dice or paper
cubes. Count the cubes in one layer, count the layers, and
multiply. Now measure the box's length, width and height and use
V = l × w × h. The two answers must agree. If they do not, one of
your counts is off — count again.

## Unwrap the net
Take an empty cardboard box. Cut it open along its edges and lay
it flat — that flat shape is its net. Measure each face and write
its area on it. Add the six faces: that is the surface area. Fold
it back up: the same six faces are the box's skin again.

## The tin
Take a cylindrical tin. Measure its radius and its height. Its
volume is πr²h. Now wrap a piece of paper round it: the paper is
a rectangle, as tall as the tin and as long as its circumference
(2πr). Unwrap it and measure it: its area is the curved surface.
Add the two circular ends (2πr²) and you have the whole surface
area — Sierra Leone's own net method.

## Water and litres
Fill a 1-litre jug with water and pour it into a cuboid box that
measures 10 cm by 10 cm by 10 cm. It fills it exactly: 1 litre is
1,000 cm³. Now you know why a volume in cm³ and a capacity in
litres are the same thing in different clothes.
