## Do it first
Take a box. Fill it with little cubes — sugar cubes, dice, paper
cubes. Count them. That count is the volume: how much the box
holds. Now empty it and unwrap it: cut along the edges and lay it
flat. Six rectangles. That flat shape is the net, and the six
rectangles added together are the surface area: how much skin the
box has. One box, two numbers — what it holds, and what covers it.

## Then the explanation
**Volume** is how much space a solid takes up, or how much it
holds. It is counted in **cubic units**: a cube 1 cm by 1 cm by
1 cm has volume 1 cm³. Volume is three lengths multiplied, so its
unit is cm³ (or m³). And 1,000 cm³ is 1 litre — volume and
capacity are the same thing in different clothes.

**Counting cubes.** Fill a cuboid with unit cubes. Count the cubes
in one layer, count the layers, and multiply. A box 4 cm long, 3 cm
wide and 2 cm high holds 4 × 3 = 12 cubes in one layer and 2
layers: 24 cubes. Its volume is 24 cm³. Egypt's Grade 5 book
starts exactly here, counting unit cubes before any formula.

**Cuboid and cube.** The counting gives the formula:

- Cuboid: **V = length × width × height**. A cuboid 8 cm by 4 cm
  by 5 cm has volume 8 × 4 × 5 = 160 cm³ (Libya's Grade 8 book
  works this very cuboid). A cuboid 30 cm by 8 cm by 6 cm has
  volume 1,440 cm³ (Egypt's Grade 5 book works this Senet board).
- Cube, side l: **V = l³** (l × l × l). A cube of side 3 cm has
  volume 27 cm³. A cube of side 6 cm has volume 216 cm³.

The formulas work backwards too: a cuboid has volume 160 cm³ and
two edges 8 cm and 4 cm — the third edge is 160 ÷ 8 ÷ 4 = 5 cm.

**Prisms.** A **prism** is a solid whose cross-section stays the
same all the way through — a triangular prism, a rectangular
prism (a cuboid), a hexagonal prism. Its volume is:

**V = area of the cross-section × length**

A triangular prism has a triangular face of area 12 cm² and a
length of 10 cm: its volume is 120 cm³. A cuboid is a prism whose
cross-section is a rectangle — and l × w × h is the same rule in
disguise (the rectangle's area times the height). Sierra Leone's
JSS 2 book teaches it this way: volume as space taken up, cross-
section × height.

**Cylinders.** A cylinder is a prism whose cross-section is a
circle:

**V = πr²h** — the circle's area (πr²) times the height

A cylinder of radius 3 cm and height 10 cm has volume
3.14 × 9 × 10 = 282.6 cm³. Jordan's Grade 7 book teaches the
prism and the cylinder together, and the pyramid and the cone
after them: a pyramid's volume is one third of its prism's
(⅓ × base area × height), and a cone's is one third of its
cylinder's.

**Surface area** is the area of the solid's skin — all its faces
added together, counted in cm². The way to find it is the net:
unfold the solid flat, find each face's area, and add them.

- Cube, side l: six square faces, each l². **SA = 6l².** A cube of
  side 3 cm has surface area 6 × 9 = 54 cm².
- Cuboid, l by w by h: three pairs of equal rectangles.
  **SA = 2(lw + lh + wh).** A cuboid 8 cm by 4 cm by 5 cm has
  surface area 2 × (32 + 40 + 20) = 2 × 92 = 184 cm².
- Triangular prism: two triangular faces plus three rectangles
  (one for each side of the triangle). Add the areas in the net.
- Cylinder: two circular ends plus one curved rectangle. The
  rectangle is as tall as the cylinder and as long as its
  circumference (2πr). **SA = 2πr² + 2πrh.** A cylinder of radius
  3 cm and height 10 cm has surface area 2 × 3.14 × 9 +
  2 × 3.14 × 3 × 10 = 56.52 + 188.4 = 244.92 cm².

**Volume or surface area?** A volume is what a solid HOLDS (cm³,
litres). A surface area is what COVERS it (cm²). "How much water
does the tank hold?" is volume. "How much paint covers the box?"
is surface area. "How much paper wraps the tin?" is surface area.

> **Tip — from us, not from the books.** Count the multiplications:
> three lengths multiplied means a volume (cm³); two lengths
> multiplied means an area (cm²). If your volume answer comes out
> in cm², you multiplied one length too few.

## Say it to your companion
Your companion gives you a cuboid, 5 cm by 4 cm by 3 cm. You find
its volume (60 cm³) and its surface area (2 × (20 + 15 + 12) =
94 cm²). Then they give you a cylinder, radius 2 cm, height 5 cm
— volume 3.14 × 4 × 5 = 62.8 cm³. Trade five times, then the hard
round: a cube has volume 125 cm³. What is its side? (5 cm — 5 ×
5 × 5 = 125.) And its surface area? (6 × 25 = 150 cm².)

## Do It Yourself
1. Find a box in your home. Measure its length, width and height.
   Find its volume and its surface area.
2. A cuboid is 10 cm by 6 cm by 4 cm. Find its volume and its
   surface area.
3. A cube has side 5 cm. Find its volume and its surface area.
4. A cylinder has radius 4 cm and height 7 cm. Find its volume
   (π ≈ 3.14) and its surface area.
