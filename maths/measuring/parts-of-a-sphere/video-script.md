# Video script — Parts of a Sphere

Super-simple voice. The tutor talks; a ball and cutting planes stay on screen. Every fact spoken is one of the lesson's own book facts.

---

Take a ball. One flat cut. The little piece that comes off has a name: a spherical segment. It has a height — the thickness of the slice. And it has a volume: pi times the height squared, times the radius minus a third of the height. A real formula, for a piece of a ball.

Now cut again, with a second plane, parallel to the first. The slice trapped between the two planes is a layer — some call it a zone. Its volume is a difference: one big segment minus one small segment. The book gives the finished formula, and it is honest — it says: this can be shown.

Now the third piece. Take the segment, and add the cone that runs from its flat face to the centre of the ball. That is a spherical sector — like an ice-cream cone with a ball scoop melting into it. Its volume: two-thirds pi R squared H.

And the skins of these pieces have formulas too. The cap's skin: two pi R H. The zone's skin: two pi R H — the same! Only the radius of the ball and the height of the slice matter, not where the slice sits.

A whole ball is one formula. But balls get cut — into segments, layers and sectors — and now each piece has its name and its measure.
