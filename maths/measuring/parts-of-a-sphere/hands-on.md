# Hands-on — Parts of a Sphere

## Activity 1 — Cut an orange into the three parts (10 minutes)

Take an orange or a ball of clay. Make one straight cut near the top: the piece that comes off is a spherical segment — measure its height H with a ruler and the ball's radius R across the cut face's widest circle (the ball's diameter through the centre). Make a second cut parallel to the first: the slice between the planes is a spherical layer; measure its height and the radii of its two circular faces. Name each piece aloud as you make it, as in Tajikistan's Grade 11 book (Topic 35, printed pp. 123–125).

## Activity 2 — Recompute the book's worked segment (15 minutes)

A sphere of radius 5 cm is cut by a plane at distance 3 cm from the centre (the book's worked problem, printed p. 129). On paper: (1) find the cap height H = 5 − 3 = 2 cm; (2) find the cut circle's radius r = √(25 − 9) = 4 cm; (3) the cap's curved area 2πRH = 20π cm²; (4) the base disc πr² = 16π cm²; (5) total 36π cm². Then go one step past the book: the same segment's volume by the derived formula, πH²(R − H/3) = 52π/3 cm³.

## Activity 3 — The rotated sector (15 minutes)

Draw a circle of radius R and mark a 120° sector whose bisector is a diameter, as in the book's worked problem (printed p. 125). Explain in writing why spinning it about that diameter gives a spherical sector with H = R − R cos 60° = R/2, then compute its volume (2/3)πR²(R/2) = πR³/3. Check with R = 6 cm: the sector's volume is 72π cm³. Note, as the book does, which step is derived (the segment's volume, by the integral, printed p. 124) and which is only stated (the sector's volume formula itself).
