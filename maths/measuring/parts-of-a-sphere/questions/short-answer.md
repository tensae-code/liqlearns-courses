# Short answer — Parts of a Sphere

1. (Layer 2) A ball of radius 5 cm is cut 3 cm from its centre. Find the volume of the smaller segment.
   **Answer: The cap height is H = 2 cm, so V = πH²(R − H/3) = π · 4 · (5 − 2/3) = 52π/3 cm³.** Tajikistan, *Геометрия* (Grade 11), printed p. 124 (the cut is the book's worked configuration of printed p. 129).

2. (Layer 3) Why does the book's rotated 120° sector give a spherical sector of height R/2?
   **Answer: The sector turns about its bisector diameter; the cap it sweeps reaches from the sphere's pole down to the plane of the sector's two radii, which stand at 60° to the axis — that plane is R cos 60° = R/2 from the centre, so the cap height is R − R/2 = R/2, and V = (2/3)πR²(R/2) = πR³/3.** Tajikistan, *Геометрия* (Grade 11), printed p. 125.

3. (Layer 4) A zone near a ball's pole and a zone around its middle have the same height. Compare their curved areas, and say how sure the book lets you be.
   **Answer: They are equal — the zone area is 2πRH, depending only on the ball's radius and the zone's height. The book states this formula without derivation, so the lesson carries it as stated, exactly as the book gives it.** Tajikistan, *Геометрия* (Grade 11), printed p. 129.

4. (Layer 1) What extra piece turns a spherical segment into a spherical sector, and how does the combination change if the segment is bigger than a hemisphere?
   **Answer: The cone from the ball's centre to the segment's base circle. For a segment smaller than a hemisphere the sector is segment plus cone; for a larger segment it is segment minus cone.** Tajikistan, *Геометрия* (Grade 11), printed p. 125.
