# Hands-on — Cavalieri's Principle

The lesson compared slices in the mind; here the hands slide
real slices and pour real water.

## The leaning stack
Stack 20 coins (or a pack of cards, or sheets of paper) into a
neat straight tower. Its volume is the stack's base area times
its height. Now push the stack gently sideways into a lean —
each coin shifted a little, none added, none taken. The tower
leans; the amount of material is exactly the stack you started
with. That is Zu Geng's principle with your own fingers
(China, B edition, Ch. 11, §11.1.6, printed p.82).

## Three pyramids in a prism
Make (or draw and cut out the net of) a triangular prism and
the three pyramids China's A-edition book splits it into (Ch. 8,
§8.3, printed pp.121–123). If you have sand or rice: fill one
pyramid, pour it into the prism shape — it takes three pyramid
fills to fill the prism. The third in V = ⅓Sh stops being a rule
and becomes a count.

## The pour check
Take a straight glass and a slanted (or differently shaped)
glass of the same height and the same base width. Fill one
with water and pour it into the other. Cavalieri's principle
predicts the outcome before you pour — say your prediction out
loud first, then pour.
