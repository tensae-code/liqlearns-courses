**Acceptance count: taught in 1 country (China).** China carries this lesson alone, in two senior volumes that meet the same principle from two sides: the B-edition Compulsory Volume 4 names it as Zu Geng's principle (祖暅原理) in §11.1.6 (printed p.82) and builds the volumes of prism, cylinder, pyramid, cone and sphere on it, with the ancient Chinese sphere-volume reading at printed p.86 as enrichment in the same chapter; the A-edition Compulsory Volume 2 presents it as an inquiry in §8.3 (printed pp.121–123), taking prism and cylinder V = Sh from the cuboid and pyramid and cone V = ⅓Sh from splitting a triangular prism into three equal-volume pyramids. No other read country's filing teaches the principle as a named topic; the lesson is built at the size this one country's two books support, and its numeric examples are the builder's own applications of the books' formulas, not quotations from the books.
