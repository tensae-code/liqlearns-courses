# Cavalieri's Principle — true or false

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Cavalieri's Principle — Equal Sections, Equal Volumes · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Cavalieri's Principle — V = Sh for Prisms and Cylinders · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Cavalieri's Principle — V = ⅓Sh for Pyramids and Cones.

Say true or false, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** A leaning prism has a smaller volume than a straight prism with the same base and height.
**Answer:** False — its slices are the same areas, shifted; by Zu Geng's principle the volumes are equal (B edition, §11.1.6, p.82).

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** A cylinder's volume is its base area times its height.
**Answer:** True — V = Sh, built from the cuboid in the A-edition inquiry (§8.3, pp.121–123).

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** A cone has the same volume as a cylinder with the same base and height, because their base slices are equal.
**Answer:** False — their slices differ at every height above the base, so the principle does not apply; the cone is ⅓ of the cylinder.

**4. (Sub lesson 3)** In China's B-edition book, the volumes of the prism, cylinder, pyramid, cone and sphere are built on Zu Geng's principle.
**Answer:** True — §11.1.6, printed p.82, with the sphere-volume reading at p.86.
