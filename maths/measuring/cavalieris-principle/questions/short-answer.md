# Cavalieri's Principle — short answer

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Cavalieri's Principle — Equal Sections, Equal Volumes · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Cavalieri's Principle — V = Sh for Prisms and Cylinders · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Cavalieri's Principle — V = ⅓Sh for Pyramids and Cones.

Answer in a word or a short line.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** In China's B-edition book, whose name does the principle carry?
**Answer:** Zu Geng (祖暅原理) — Compulsory Volume 4, B edition, §11.1.6, p.82.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** Two solids have the same height, and their cross-sections match in area at the base and at the top, but not in between. Does the principle say their volumes are equal?
**Answer:** No — the sections must be equal at EVERY height (B edition, §11.1.6, p.82).

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Write the volume formula for a prism or cylinder, and say what S and h are.
**Answer:** V = Sh — S is the base area, h the height (A edition, §8.3, pp.121–123).

**4. (Sub lesson 3)** Why is a pyramid's volume ⅓Sh and not Sh?
**Answer:** A triangular prism splits into three pyramids of equal volume, so one pyramid is one third of the prism's Sh (A edition, §8.3, pp.121–123).

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** A cone has base area 24 cm² and height 5 cm. Find its volume.
**Answer:** 40 cm³ — ⅓ × 24 × 5.
