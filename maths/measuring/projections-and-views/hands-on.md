# Hands-on — Projections and Views

The lesson drew solids flat — from three sides, from a corner, and unfolded.

## Three views of a tin
Put a tin (or a box) on the table. Draw it three times on squared paper: from the front, from directly above, from the side. Label each drawing with its viewpoint. A tin gives a rectangle, a circle and a rectangle — if your top view is not a circle, you were not directly above. Swap solids with a partner and rebuild: from their three drawings alone, name and sketch their solid.

## Shadow tracing
On a sunny day, stand a stick upright and trace its shadow with chalk or stones every hour. The shadow is the sun's projection of the stick on the ground — Laos's Grade 7 book builds the whole topic this way, from sun rays and pole shadows. Say how the shadow's length and direction changed, and what the projection plane is.

## The birdhouse viewpoints
Find a small structure — a birdhouse if there is one, a stool or a kennel if not — and photograph or draw it from four viewpoints, as Laos's book does with its birdhouse: front, side, back, above. Put the four pictures in a row and have a partner match each picture to its viewpoint without being told.

## Unfold a box
Take a small cardboard box. Cut along edges and unfold it flat, keeping all faces joined. Draw the net and count the faces. Mark which face lands opposite which when it folds back. Fold it back up to check. Then draw a different true net of the same box — a cube has more than one.
