# Projections and Views — true or false

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Projections and Views — the Three Views · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Projections and Views — Drawings That Show Depth · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Projections and Views — Reading Drawings.

Say true or false. If it is false, say why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** The front view of a ball is a circle.
**Answer:** True — every view of a sphere is a circle; that is what makes the sphere the strange case in the companion round.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** One view of a solid is always enough to rebuild it exactly.
**Answer:** False — one view hides one whole dimension; the three views together fix the solid.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** In an axonometric drawing, parallel edges of the solid stay parallel in the drawing.
**Answer:** True — that is what separates projection drawing from perspective, where parallels meet at a vanishing point.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** Every arrangement of six squares joined edge to edge folds into a cube.
**Answer:** False — the squares must not overlap when folded; a straight row of six, for example, leaves one square with nowhere to go and one face missing.

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Monge's descriptive geometry, in China's reading, projects a solid onto two perpendicular planes.
**Answer:** True — orthographic projection on two perpendicular planes is Monge's method (reading as filed).
