# Area Under a Straight-Line Graph

## You do it first

Draw the line y = 2x between x = 0 and x = 4, and shade everything between the line and the x-axis. What shape have you shaded? How would you find its exact area?

If you said "a triangle" — you already own this whole lesson. What follows is that one idea, in its two shapes, plus the trick for when the line dips below the axis. Malaysia's Form 3 book teaches it as the closing exploration of its straight-line chapter. (Malaysia, Mathematics Form 3, Exploring Mathematics, printed p. 251.)

## The triangle case — line through the origin

Under y = 2x from x = 0 to x = 4, the region is a triangle with base 4 (along the axis) and height 8 (the line's value at x = 4: y = 2 × 4 = 8).

    Area = ½ × base × height = ½ × 4 × 8 = 16 square units

The rule: for a line through the origin, read the height at the right-hand edge and halve base × height.

## The trapezium case — line not through the origin

Under y = x + 1 from x = 0 to x = 3, the region has two vertical sides — height 1 at x = 0 and height 4 at x = 3 — with width 3 between them. That is a trapezium lying on its side:

    Area = ½ × (sum of the parallel sides) × (distance between them)
         = ½ × (1 + 4) × 3 = 7.5 square units

The two "parallel sides" are the line's heights at the two edges. (Malaysia, Mathematics Form 3, Exploring Mathematics, printed p. 251.)

## When the line crosses the axis

If the line dips below the x-axis inside the interval, split the region where the line crosses (solve y = 0). Find each piece's area as a shape. Areas below the axis are usually counted as positive sizes of regions — but say what you are doing: "the area of the region below the axis is 2" is a size; its position is below. (Later, in integrals, areas below the axis come out negative by sign — the two lessons agree on the size and differ on the sign convention, and each says so.)

## Where you have met this before

The area under a **speed–time graph is the distance travelled** — Malaysia's Form 4 book computes distances exactly this way, with triangles and trapeziums under straight pieces of graph. Same shapes, same areas, a story on top. (Malaysia, Mathematics Form 4, Ch. 7 §7.2, printed pp. 195–207 — filed in speed-distance-and-time.)

> **Tip — from us, not from the books:** always sketch and shade before you compute. Half the errors in this topic are not arithmetic — they are computing the area of a shape that is not the one under the line.

## Say it to your companion

Explain to your companion how you decide whether the region under a line will be a triangle or a trapezium — and let them test you with y = 3x − 2 between x = 1 and x = 4 (heights 1 and 10, width 3: a trapezium, area ½ × 11 × 3 = 16.5).

## Do It Yourself

1. Find the area under y = 3x from x = 0 to x = 5.
2. Find the area under y = 2x + 3 from x = 0 to x = 4.
3. Find the area between y = 8 − 2x and the x-axis from x = 0 to x = 4 — name the shape first.
4. A speed–time graph is a straight line from (0, 0) to (10, 20). How far does the object travel in those 10 seconds?
