# Lesson plan — Area Under a Straight-Line Graph

**One thing:** the area between a straight-line graph and the x-axis, found exactly as the area of the triangle or trapezium sitting under the line — geometry, not calculus.

**Unit:** Measuring, Area and Volume (Unit 8). **Level 140** (Malaysia merge, 2026-11-07).

**Why this lesson exists:** Malaysia's Form 3 book ends its straight-line chapter with an Exploring Mathematics activity that computes areas under straight-line graphs from the shapes beneath them. Estimating areas by counting squares is already a lesson (estimating-areas), and the calculus version is inside integrals; the exact area under a straight line, from pure shape areas, had no home. Under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06), it gets its own lesson. It is deliberately NOT placed inside integrals: the method here is the triangle and trapezium area formulas every Form 3 learner already owns, and it is also the geometry behind the speed–time graph areas taught in Form 4.

## The climb

1. **The region under a line** — between the graph, the x-axis and two vertical edges, the region is a familiar shape.
2. **Line through the origin** — the region is a triangle: area = ½ × base × height, the height read from the line at the right edge.
3. **Line not through the origin** — the region is a trapezium: area = ½ × (sum of the two parallel sides) × distance between them, the two heights read from the line at the two edges.
4. **Areas below the axis and in pieces** — split the region at the crossing point and treat each piece on its own.
