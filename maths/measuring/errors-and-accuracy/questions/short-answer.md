# Errors and Accuracy — short answer

Give a short answer, with the working where it helps.

**1. (Level 1)** The true length is 120 cm. You measure 118 cm. Find the absolute error.
**Answer:** 2 cm — the size of the gap between 118 and 120.

**2. (Level 1)** A width is given as 8 cm to the nearest cm. Write the range the true width can lie in.
**Answer:** From 7.5 cm to 8.5 cm — a rounded value can be out by at most half the last place kept.

**3. (Level 2)** A true mass is 25 kg. A scale reads 24 kg. Find the relative error and the percentage error.
**Answer:** Absolute error 1 kg; relative error 1 ÷ 25 = 0.04; percentage error 4%.

**4. (Level 2)** A true length is 400 m. A measurement gives 404 m. Find the percentage error.
**Answer:** 1% — absolute error 4 m; 4 ÷ 400 = 0.01 = 1%.

**5. (Level 2)** Which is more trustworthy: a measure of 10 kg with a 0.5 kg error, or a measure of 200 kg with a 0.5 kg error? Give the percentage errors.
**Answer:** The 200 kg measure — its percentage error is 0.25%, against 5% for the 10 kg measure, though both absolute errors are 0.5 kg.

**6. (Level 3)** A value is rounded to 3.7 (to the nearest tenth) before it is used. What is the biggest possible error the rounding hides?
**Answer:** 0.05 — half of the last place kept, the tenth.

**7. (Level 3)** A hall's crowd is reported as "exactly 1,000 people" from a quick look at the door. What is wrong with the degree of accuracy?
**Answer:** It pretends to exact knowledge nobody has — a quick look can only support "about 1,000"; the degree of accuracy must match how the number was obtained.
