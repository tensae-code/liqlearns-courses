# Hands-on — Errors and Accuracy

The lesson measured real things and asked how wrong each measure could be.

## The ruler's limit
Measure the same book three times with a ruler, reading to the nearest millimetre each time. Write the three readings. If they differ, the spread between them is your measuring error at work — no reading is the "true" length, each is the true length plus or minus a small error.

## Half a place
Take a length you measured as 24 cm (to the nearest cm). The true length can be anywhere from 23.5 cm to 24.5 cm. Mark that range on a drawn line. Now measure the same thing to the nearest millimetre and mark the new, much smaller range. The finer the degree of accuracy, the smaller the biggest possible error — half the last place kept.

## Same gap, different weight
Weigh (or read the printed mass of) a small packet and a big bag. Imagine each scale is 10 g out. Work the percentage error for each: the same 10 g gap is a large percentage of the small packet and a tiny percentage of the big bag. Say in a whole sentence which measure you would trust less, and why.

## Choose the accuracy
List five jobs: a road between towns, a room, a book, a dose of medicine, a football pitch. For each, choose a degree of accuracy (nearest km, m, cm, mm, or finer) and defend the choice in one sentence: what goes wrong if the measure is one unit of that degree out?
