# Hands-on — Measuring

The lesson measured on paper; here the hands measure real things.

## Guess, then measure
Pick five things: your hand, a book, a table, a doorway, the
room's longest wall. Guess each length first, in the unit that
fits — cm for the hand, m for the wall. Then measure with the
ruler or with steps. Write guess and measure side by side. The gap
between them is what practice closes.

## The kitchen scales
Weigh things in your kitchen: an apple, a bag of rice, a bottle
of water. Read the grams and kilograms off the packet or the
scales. A litre of water weighs a kilogram — check it: weigh a
full litre bottle, then an empty one. The difference is about
1,000 g. Length, mass and capacity meet in that bottle.

## Pour and count
Greece's own picture: one litre of water fills almost four
glasses. Pour a litre jug into glasses and count them. Then pour
half a litre: about two glasses. Now you know what a litre looks
like in your own glasses — and why a teaspoon is millilitres, not
litres.

## The units ladder
Draw the ladder on paper: mm — cm — m — km, with 10, 100 and
1,000 written on the rungs. Walk a number up and down it: 2,500
mm is 250 cm is 2.5 m. Then the mass ladder (g — kg — tonne) and
the capacity ladder (mL — L). Three ladders, one rule: up means
divide, down means multiply.
