# Lesson plan — Measuring

**Course:** Maths · **Stage:** 8, Measuring, Area and Volume · **Lesson:** Measuring (lesson 1 of 4 in the stage)
**Place in the curriculum:** Stage 8, "Measuring, Area and Volume" — the first lesson of the stage (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** measuring length, mass and capacity in metric units, and converting between those units.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on counting and on place value (converting is multiplying and dividing by 10, 100 and 1,000). It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown changing.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: place value and multiplying by 10, 100 and
  1,000 — converting is just moving between those steps.
  Recommended, never forced.
- Time is measured too, but time is not in this lesson: the books
  teach time with its own units (seconds, minutes, hours), and it
  is not a metric conversion.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Measure a length in millimetres, centimetres, metres and
   kilometres, and convert between them (10 mm = 1 cm,
   100 cm = 1 m, 1,000 m = 1 km).
2. Measure a mass in grams, kilograms and tonnes, and convert
   between them (1,000 g = 1 kg, 1,000 kg = 1 tonne).
3. Measure a capacity in millilitres and litres, and convert
   between them (1,000 mL = 1 L); and choose the sensible unit
   for the thing in front of them.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A ruler (with millimetres). Kitchen scales or
a bag of rice/flour with its weight printed on it. A jug or a
bottle with millilitres marked on it.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner measures their own hand, a book
   and the room, and guesses before measuring (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — length units and converting; mass units
   and converting; capacity units and converting; choosing the
   right unit (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE at the length part: a picture of a ruler, a metre
     stick and a road sign, side by side — the student says which
     unit fits each job.
   - ADD GIF at the converting part: a picture of a number sliding
     along a units ladder — millimetres to centimetres to metres
     to kilometres — the student says the new number at each rung.
   - ADD IMAGE at the capacity part: a picture of a teaspoon, a
     cup, a jug and a water tank — the student says which unit
     fits each one.
3. **Say it to your companion** — convert measures for your
   companion and explain each step (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the units
     ladder in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos
     are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served
   at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — measure five things in your home in the
   right units and convert each one (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Converts the wrong way (2 m = 2000 cm instead of 200 cm) → the
  books' own ladder: each step is ×10 or ×1,000, never ×100
  between metres and centimetres. Say the ladder aloud: mm–cm is
  10, cm–m is 100, m–km is 1,000.
- Adds a length in cm to a length in m without converting → the
  units must match first. Convert, then add. Sierra Leone's rule:
  same units first.
- Picks kilometres for a pencil → ask "how many of this unit
  would it take?" A pencil is about 15 cm, not 15 km and not
  15 mm. The sensible unit gives a small sensible number.
- This lesson's next door: Perimeter and Area (same stage) — a
  perimeter is a length, and it is measured in exactly these
  units.
