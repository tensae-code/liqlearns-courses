## Do it first
Hold out your hand. How long is it — from wrist to fingertip?
Guess first, then measure with a ruler. Most adult hands are
about 18 cm. Now measure a book, then the table, then the room.
The numbers grow, and the unit must grow with them: centimetres
for the hand, metres for the room. Measuring is choosing the
right unit and then counting how many of it fit.

## Then the explanation
**Length.** Length is how long, how wide, how tall, how far.
The metric units, smallest to biggest:

- **millimetre (mm)** — a grain of rice is about 5 mm; a pencil
  lead is about 2 mm.
- **centimetre (cm)** — your finger is about 1 cm wide; a book
  is about 25 cm tall.
- **metre (m)** — a big step is about 1 m; a room is a few metres.
- **kilometre (km)** — a road between towns; a 15-minute walk is
  about 1 km.

They are tied together in tens and hundreds:

**10 mm = 1 cm · 100 cm = 1 m · 1,000 m = 1 km**

To convert, walk the ladder. Down the ladder (big unit to small),
multiply. Up the ladder (small unit to big), divide. 2 m = 2 × 100
= 200 cm. 250 cm = 250 ÷ 100 = 2.5 m. 3 km = 3 × 1,000 =
3,000 m. 1,500 m = 1.5 km. A reading like 4 cm 7 mm means 4 cm
and 7 mm — 47 mm all together (Egypt's Grade 2 book reads it
exactly that way). And a length must be measured from the zero
mark of the ruler, not from its end — Jordan's Grade 2 book
teaches the centimetre that way.

Before standard units, the books start with non-standard ones:
Sierra Leone's primary plans measure with hand-spans, paces and
sticks, and Vietnam's Grade 1 measures length with non-standard
units too. Non-standard units teach what measuring IS — counting
how many of one thing fit along another — and standard units
make the answer the same for everyone.

**Mass.** Mass is how heavy a thing is. The units:

- **gram (g)** — an apple is about 150 g; a letter is about
  20 g.
- **kilogram (kg)** — a bag of rice is 5 kg; a child is about
  30 kg.
- **tonne (t)** — a lorry, a container, an elephant: tonnes.

**1,000 g = 1 kg · 1,000 kg = 1 tonne**

So 4 kg 500 g is 4,500 g (South Africa's Grade 4 workbook shows
it exactly that way), and 2,500 g is 2.5 kg. Libya's Grade 2 book
measures mass first in kilograms, then in grams, then adds and
subtracts masses — always in matching units.

**Capacity.** Capacity is how much a container holds. The units:

- **millilitre (mL)** — a teaspoon holds about 5 mL; a small
  medicine cup holds 30 mL.
- **litre (L)** — a jug holds 1 or 2 L; a bucket holds about
  10 L.

**1,000 mL = 1 L**

So 2 L is 2,000 mL, and 750 mL is 0.75 L. Greece's Grade 1 book
gives the picture to remember: one litre of water fills almost
four glasses, and half a litre fills about two. Volume and
capacity meet here: 1,000 cm³ is 1 litre — Sierra Leone's JSS 1
book converts between them (a palm-oil tin is the worked
example), and that link is what the volume lesson builds on.

**Choosing the right unit.** The sensible unit is the one that
gives a small sensible number. A pencil is about 15 cm — not
15 mm (too small to be true) and not 15 m (absurd). A road is
measured in kilometres, a room in metres, a fingernail in
millimetres. If your answer comes out in the thousands, you
probably picked too small a unit; if it comes out as a tiny
decimal, you picked too big a one.

> **Tip — from us, not from the books.** Say the ladder before
> you convert: "mm to cm is 10, cm to m is 100, m to km is
> 1,000; grams to kilograms is 1,000; millilitres to litres is
> 1,000." If you can say the ladder, you cannot convert the
> wrong way.

## Say it to your companion
Your companion gives you a measure: 3 m 50 cm. You convert it
to centimetres (350 cm) and to millimetres (3,500 mm). Then they
give you 2 kg 250 g — you convert it to grams (2,250 g). Trade
five times, then the hard round: a jug holds 2.5 L. How many
millilitres is that? (2,500 mL.) And how many 250 mL cups does
it fill? (Ten.)

## Do It Yourself
1. Measure five things in your home — a hand, a book, a table,
   a doorway, a room — each in its sensible unit. Write each
   measure with its unit.
2. Convert: 5 m to cm; 350 cm to m; 2 km to m; 4,000 m to km.
3. Convert: 3 kg to g; 2,750 g to kg; 5 L to mL; 1,250 mL to L.
4. A bottle holds 1.5 L. How many 250 mL cups can you fill from
   it? Show the conversion first, then the division.
