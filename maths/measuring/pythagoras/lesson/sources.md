**Acceptance count: taught in 15 countries (Egypt, Greece,
Armenia, Kazakhstan, Vietnam, Sierra Leone, Libya, Mauritania,
South Africa, Laos, Indonesia, Nicaragua, Ethiopia, South Sudan, Iraq).** Egypt carries the fullest single treatment —
the theorem, its converse, classifying triangles by comparing
squares, the 13-foot ladder and a Pythagoras practical in Grade
8. Sierra Leone carries the cleanest ladder in JSS 3 — the
3-4-5 check, finding the hypotenuse, finding the other sides,
and the rectangle diagonal to one decimal place. Kazakhstan
carries three proofs in Grade 8: by area dissection, from the
cosine rule, and in use for distance between two points.
Greece carries the theorem inside its areas chapter in Grade 8
and its metric-relations chapter in Grade 11. Armenia carries
the theorem and its converse in Geometry 8. Vietnam carries the
theorem and its applications, including measuring heights and
distances, in Grade 8. Libya carries the theorem with
trigonometry in Grade 10 — and Libya's honest limit is stated:
Grades 5–9 teach no Pythagoras at all. Mauritania carries the
theorem in 3AS. South Africa carries it in Grades 8 and 9.
Ghana is counted separately and honestly: its Basic 8
curriculum states Pythagoras' theorem as a standard, but no
pupil textbook in Ghana's set teaches it, so Ghana is
curriculum evidence only, not a teaching country. Jordan is
counted separately and honestly too: Jordan uses the theorem as
a tool in Grade 9 coordinate geometry and names the Pythagorean
identity in Grade 9 trigonometry, but teaches it as a topic in
no held book — the Grade 7 and Grade 8 books state its absence
explicitly. The brief's claim that Jordan teaches Pythagoras in
depth is corrected here against the filing: Jordan does not. Laos carries Pythagoras as an area proof in Grade 8 and uses it again, applied twice, to derive distance in three dimensions in Grade 10. Indonesia carries Pythagoras as guide-only evidence — the Grade 8 Merdeka teacher guide teaches the theorem, triples, special right triangles and applications (Bab 2, pp. 87–130), but no Merdeka Grade 8 student book is in hand, so it is worded as guide evidence throughout, like Ghana's teacher-manual evidence. Nicaragua carries Pythagoras whole in Grade 9 — the theorem proved by area dissection, finding hypotenuse and legs, and the cone, pyramid, space-diagonal and triangle/hexagon-area applications — and uses it again in Grade 10 trigonometry and for distance in the plane in Grade 11. Honest negative: the converse of Pythagoras' theorem is not taught as a named topic, and no Pythagorean triples are taught as a topic. Ethiopia carries Pythagoras in Grades 8 and 9 — Euclid's theorem with Pythagoras and its converse proved, the 10 m ladder, and Pythagoras used inside circle problems in Grade 8, and right-angled triangles with Pythagoras and its converse and locating √2, √3, √5… by Pythagoras construction in Grade 9. Grade 7's Green Legacy problem uses 5-12-13 as a known triple, not as a teaching of Pythagoras, and Grades 1–6 teach no Pythagoras. South Sudan carries Pythagoras' theorem from Grade 7 (a² + b² = c², the 3-4-5 check, hypotenuse 13 from 5 and 12), through Grade 8 (6-8-10 and the 24 m wall, 7 m out, 25 m ladder), to Secondary 2, where the proofs are left as learner tasks ("Proof 2"/"Proof 3") and the Teacher's Guide supplies Bhaskara's proofs (pp. 19–20, teacher evidence). Iraq carries Pythagoras in Grade 8 — applications of the theorem with its converse and irrationals on the number line — and uses it again inside trigonometric ratios in Grade 9, with the book's 15² = 255 misprint corrected to 225.
