# Lesson plan — Pythagoras

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** 8, Measuring, Area and Volume · **Lesson:** Pythagoras (lesson 4 of 4 in the unit)
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 8, "Measuring, Area and Volume" — the fourth lesson of the unit (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the right-triangle rule — the square of the hypotenuse equals the sum of the squares of the other two sides — and how to use it to find a missing side.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the angles lesson (a right angle is the rule's condition), on squares and square roots (Stage 5), and on the perimeter-and-area lesson (the rule is proved by areas). It needs nothing else.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Pythagoras — the Rule** — a² + b² = c² with c the hypotenuse, the side opposite the right angle and the longest side, and 3² + 4² = 5². (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Pythagoras — Finding the Hypotenuse** — finding a hypotenuse with c = √(a² + b²), used for the diagonal of a rectangle and for a ladder against a wall. (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **Pythagoras — Finding a Shorter Side and the Converse** — finding a shorter side with a = √(c² − b²), and checking whether a triangle is right-angled by testing whether the squares add up. (Was depth Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson (no hardness ranking is written in this lesson's question files).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Angles (the right angle), powers and roots
  (squares and square roots), and Perimeter and Area (the rule is
  proved by areas). Recommended, never forced.
- Trigonometry belongs to Stage 9; this lesson finds sides with
  squares and square roots only, and never uses sine, cosine or
  tangent.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say the rule: in a right-angled triangle, a² + b² = c², where c
   is the hypotenuse — the side opposite the right angle, and the
   longest side.
2. Find the hypotenuse from the other two sides (c = √(a² + b²)),
   including the 3-4-5 triangle and the diagonal of a rectangle.
3. Find a shorter side from the hypotenuse and the other side
   (a = √(c² − b²)), and check whether a triangle is right-angled
   with the converse.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A ruler. Squared paper. A ladder against a
wall, or a picture of one.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner draws a 3-4-5 triangle on squared
   paper and counts the squares on each side (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the rule and the hypotenuse; finding the
   hypotenuse; finding a shorter side; the converse
   (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE at the rule part: a picture of a right-angled
     triangle with a square built on each side, the squares
     labelled 9, 16 and 25 — the student sees 9 + 16 = 25.
   - ADD GIF at the hypotenuse part: a picture of a ladder

3. **Say it to your companion** — find missing sides for your
   companion, and say which form of the rule you used

5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.

## If the learner is stuck
- Adds the sides instead of their squares (3 + 4 = 7, not 5) →
  the books' own correction: the rule is about the SQUARES of the
  sides. Square first, then add, then take the square root.
- Forgets the square root at the end → c² = 25 means c = 5, not
  25. The answer is a length, not an area — take the root.
- Uses the rule on a triangle with no right angle → the rule
  works ONLY for right-angled triangles. No right angle, no
  Pythagoras. Check the corner first.
- Mixes up which side is the hypotenuse → the hypotenuse is
  opposite the right angle, and it is always the longest side.
  If your "hypotenuse" is shorter than another side, you picked
  the wrong one.
- This lesson's next door: Right-Triangle Trigonometry (Stage 9)
  — the same right triangle, now with ratios as well as squares.
