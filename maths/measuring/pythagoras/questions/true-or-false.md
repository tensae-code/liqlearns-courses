# Pythagoras — true or false

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Pythagoras — the Rule · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Pythagoras — Finding the Hypotenuse · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Pythagoras — Finding a Shorter Side and the Converse.

Say true or false. If it is false, say why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Pythagoras' theorem works for every triangle.
**False** — it works only for right-angled triangles. No right angle, no Pythagoras.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** In a 3-4-5 triangle, 9 + 16 = 25.
**True** — the squares of the legs add to the square of the hypotenuse (Sierra Leone, JSS 3, L42).

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** To find the hypotenuse you add the legs and take the square root.
**False** — you square the legs first, then add, then take the square root: √(a² + b²).

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** The diagonal of a rectangle is the hypotenuse of the two triangles it makes.
**True** — the diagonal cuts the rectangle into two right-angled triangles (Sierra Leone, JSS 3, L45).

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** To find a shorter side you subtract the squares: a = √(c² − b²).
**True** (Sierra Leone, JSS 3, L44).

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** If a² + b² is less than c², the triangle is acute.
**False** — if a² + b² is less than c², the triangle is obtuse; if more, it is acute (Egypt, Grade 8, 3-2).
