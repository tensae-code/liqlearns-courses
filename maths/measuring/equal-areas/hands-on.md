# Hands-on — Equal Areas

The lesson slid vertices on paper; here the hands cut, slide and
count for real, the way Yemen's Grade 8 lessons open (Unit 7,
lessons 7-7–7-8, printed pp.87–94).

## Count the trio
On squared paper draw Yemen's opening trio: a 5 × 2 rectangle, an
L-shape, and a slanted parallelogram, each covering 10 unit
squares. Count every shape's squares out loud. Three shapes, one
count: that sameness is what "equivalent" names.

## Slide the vertex
Draw a base 8 squares long and a line parallel to it 4 squares
above. Put a triangle's third vertex on the line, far left.
Count or compute its area: ½ × 8 × 4 = 16. Slide the vertex
along the line — middle, far right — drawing each new triangle.
Leaning, upright, leaning the other way: every one's area is
16. Cut one out and lay it on another: they do not match. Equal
area, different shape, in your hands.

## Lean the parallelogram
Cut a paper parallelogram, base 6 cm, height 4 cm. Push it
sideways so it leans hard, keeping the base on the table line
and the top edge at the same height. The slant side is longer
than before; the area is still 6 × 4 = 24 cm². Measure the
height with a set square to prove the lean never changed it.
