# Equal Areas — short answer

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Equal Areas — What Equivalent Means · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Equal Areas — Equivalent Triangles · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Equal Areas — Equivalent Parallelograms.

Answer in a word or a short line.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** State Yemen's definition of equivalent shapes.
**Answer:** Two shapes are equivalent if they have equal area (G8 Pt2, 7-7, p.87).

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** Two triangles are equivalent but look completely different. Name one thing they must share and one thing they need not share.
**Answer:** They must share their area; they need not share sides, angles or shape — equivalence is not congruence (G8 Pt2, 7-7, p.87).

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Why do all triangles on the same base with the vertex on a line parallel to the base have the same area?
**Answer:** They share the base, and the parallel line fixes the height — the perpendicular distance between the parallels is the same everywhere (G8 Pt2, 7-7, p.87).

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** A triangle has base 12 cm and height 6 cm (area 36 cm²). Give a base and height of a different triangle equivalent to it.
**Answer:** For example base 9 cm, height 8 cm — ½ × 9 × 8 = 36 cm² (any pair with base × height = 72).

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** A parallelogram has base 9 cm and height 5 cm. A rectangle is 9 cm × 5 cm. Are the parallelogram and rectangle equivalent? Why?
**Answer:** Yes — both areas are 45 cm²; the parallelogram has base × height = 9 × 5, lean or no lean (G8 Pt2, 7-8, p.94).
