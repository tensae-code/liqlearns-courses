# Video script — Determinants and Cramer's Rule

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the
lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are
made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Take a square of four numbers. Two rows, two columns.

Multiply the main diagonal. Multiply the other diagonal. Take one away from the other. That ONE number is the DETERMINANT.

If the determinant is ZERO, the square is flat — it has no inverse, and its equations have no single answer. If it is not zero, everything works!

Three by three? Copy the first two columns again on the right. Add the three diagonals going down. Subtract the three going up. That is Sarrus' rule — the big determinant, done by hand.

Now CRAMER'S RULE. To find x, build a new determinant: swap the answers into the x column — and divide by the first determinant. To find y, swap the answers into the y column — and divide again. One swap per unknown!

And the secret at the end: matrices can hide a message. Turn letters into numbers, multiply by a coding matrix — the message is scrambled. Multiply by the INVERSE matrix — the message comes back!

Now you. Four numbers in a square. Main diagonal minus other diagonal. Say the determinant!
