# Hands-on — Complex Numbers

## The failing equation
Try to solve x² = −1, x² = −4 and x² = −9 with real numbers only. Each one fails the same way: a square is never negative. Write down why each fails, then write the new number i that fixes all three: x = ±i, ±2i, ±3i.

## Argand walk
Draw the complex plane on squared paper. Plot 3 + 4i, −2 + i, 1 − 3i and −4 − 2i. For each one, measure its distance from zero with a ruler (that is the modulus) and its angle from the real axis with a protractor (that is the argument). Check 3 + 4i: distance 5, angle about 53°.

## Conjugate pairs
Take 1 + 2i. Its conjugate is 1 − 2i — same real part, imaginary part flipped. Plot both: they mirror each other across the real axis, the way Mauritania's book shows. Multiply them: (1 + 2i)(1 − 2i) = 5, a real number. Try it with 5i: its conjugate is −5i.

## De Moivre turns
Take the number with modulus 1 and argument 30°. Multiply it by itself: the angle doubles to 60°. Again: 90°. Again: 120°. Walk it round the circle, doubling the angle each time — that is De Moivre's theorem in your hands.
