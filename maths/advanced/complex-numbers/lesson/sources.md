**Acceptance count: taught in 8 countries (Egypt, Jordan, Mauritania, Laos, Yemen, Indonesia, South Sudan, Iraq).** Egypt carries the subject from its first lesson (the imaginary unit, the algebraic form, powers of i) to its Grade 12 finish (trigonometric form, De Moivre, cubic roots of unity); Jordan carries complex numbers, their operations and Argand-plane loci; Mauritania carries complex numbers in two chapters of its C stream and one chapter of its D stream, with conjugates worked in full. Indonesia carries complex numbers in Grade 11 Tingkat Lanjut — the algebraic form, the complex plane, operations, conjugate, modulus and argument — but stops there: no trigonometric form, no De Moivre, no polar-form powers or roots. Iraq carries complex numbers whole in a single full chapter in Grade 12 Scientific — the need to extend R, operations on C, the conjugate, square roots of a complex number, solving quadratics in C, cube roots of unity, geometric representation, polar form and De Moivre's theorem, with worked examples verified in the filing (ex. 3 p. 10; ex. 6 p. 13; ex. 9–10 pp. 17–19; ex. 14–15 pp. 21–22; ex. 16–17 pp. 24–25; ex. 19–21 pp. 29–30; ex. 22 p. 34; ex. 23 p. 37; ex. 26 p. 40; ex. 28 p. 43; ex. 31 pp. 45–46). The Literary track never meets complex numbers.

**Honest limit:** only eight read countries teach complex numbers at all. Sierra Leone's SSS never teaches them; Libya has no complex numbers anywhere; Vietnam teaches none; Greece teaches none; Kazakhstan's set tops out at Grade 9; Armenia teaches none; Mauritania's 3AS/4AS streams teach none. This lesson stands on Egypt, Jordan, Mauritania, Laos, Yemen, Indonesia, South Sudan and Iraq, and says so plainly. Laos carries complex numbers whole in Grade 12 — modulus, conjugate, trigonometric form, De Moivre's formula and nth roots. Yemen carries complex numbers whole in Grade 12 science — operations, polar form, powers and roots, and solving quadratics over the complex numbers. South Sudan carries complex numbers whole in Secondary 4 — a + bi, i = √−1, the Argand diagram, conjugates, operations and polar form with uses in solving quadratics and electronics in the regular Mathematics book, and polar form, De Moivre's theorem, roots and the exponential form z = re^(iθ) in Additional Mathematics — with the corrections taught, never as printed: Secondary 4 Mathematics p. 17 Exercise 6 Q3(a) is malformed ("z = 5(cos 45° − 5i sin 45°" — correct: z = 5(cos 45° − i sin 45°)); the Mathematics teacher's guide pp. 14–15 answers are wrong in places ((i)(2i)(−7i) = 14i not 12i; (3i)(−2+6i) = −18−6i not −8−6i; (4+3i)(2−i) = 11+2i not 14+2i; (1+2i)/(3−4i) = −1/5+(2/5)i; (1−5i)(−9+2i) = 1+47i not 1−41i) and its p. 8 "i is the only imaginary number" is false, so the student book is cited, not the guide keys; Additional Mathematics p. 194 prints the cube roots of 8 wrong — the correct roots are 2, −1+i√3, −1−i√3; p. 189 derives cos 3θ with errors in the working (the last term y² should be y³), so the result cos 3θ = 4cos³θ − 3cosθ is cited, not the printed working; and the Additional teacher's guide p. 92 "sin 3θ = 3 sin x + 4 sin³x" is wrong (correct: 3 sin θ − 4 sin³θ) and is not cited. Iraq carries complex numbers whole in a single full chapter in Grade 12 Scientific — the need to extend R, operations on C, the conjugate, square roots of a complex number, solving quadratics in C, cube roots of unity, geometric representation, polar form and De Moivre's theorem.
