## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the quadratic-equations lesson (some quadratics have no real roots) and the logarithms lesson (a new number system, learned the same way). Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say why complex numbers exist: x² = −1 has no real solution.
2. Write a complex number as a + bi, and name its real and imaginary parts.
3. Add, subtract, multiply and divide complex numbers, using conjugates.
4. Find the modulus and argument, and write the trigonometric form.
5. Use De Moivre's theorem for powers, and find roots of unity.
6. Draw loci in the complex plane (the Argand diagram).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A squared-paper Argand diagram to draw on.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner tries to solve x² = −1 with real numbers, and watches it fail (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — why i exists; the form a + bi; operations and conjugates; modulus and argument; trigonometric form and De Moivre; loci (lesson/lesson.md).
3. **Say it to your companion** — give your companion a complex number and ask for its conjugate, its modulus, and its place on the Argand diagram (lesson/lesson.md).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — operate on two complex numbers, plot them, and find a root of unity (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Forgets i² = −1 when multiplying → write i² out as −1 every time until it is automatic: (a + bi)(c + di) always ends with a −bd.
- Divides by a complex number directly → multiply top and bottom by the conjugate; the bottom becomes a real number.
- Mixes modulus and argument → modulus is the DISTANCE from zero; argument is the ANGLE from the real axis. Distance and angle, two different things.
- This lesson's next door: matrices (same unit) — another new kind of number-like object with its own arithmetic.
