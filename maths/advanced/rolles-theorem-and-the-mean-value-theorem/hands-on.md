# Hands-on — Rolle's theorem and the Mean Value Theorem

The lesson proved and used the two theorems on drawn curves.

## The hill in your hand
Draw a smooth hill starting at height 2 and ending at height 2. Mark the top and draw the short flat tangent there. Under the drawing, write Rolle's three conditions and tick each one against your picture (Greece, W12, Ch.2).

## Speedometer justice
A trip covers 120 km in 2 hours. Write the average speed. Then sketch any speed graph you like for the trip — starting slow, rushing, crawling — and mark a moment when the speedometer must read exactly the average. Say which theorem forces that moment to exist (Yemen, Grade 12 Science, 6-7, contents/PDF p.183).

## Break the theorem
Draw a path from (0, 0) to (2, 0) with a sharp corner at the top, like a roof. The endpoint heights are equal, but there is no flat tangent anywhere. Write which condition failed — differentiability at the corner — and why the theorem stays silent.

## The derivative race
Compute lim(x→0) sin x / x twice: once from the Lesson 87 table, once by L'Hôpital's rule (differentiate top and bottom separately: cos x / 1 → 1). Write one sentence on why both routes must agree (Cambodia, PRIVATE Derivatives booklet, pp.53–54).
