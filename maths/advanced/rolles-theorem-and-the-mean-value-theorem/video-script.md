# Video script — Rolle's theorem and the Mean Value Theorem

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the
lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are
made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Walk up a hill and down again, and finish at the SAME height you started.
Somewhere on the way, the ground was FLAT under your feet — at the top.
That is **Rolle's theorem**. Same height at both ends, a smooth path —
somewhere inside, the slope is zero.

Now a car trip. You drove 100 kilometres in one hour. Average speed: 100.
Did your speedometer ever say EXACTLY 100? It must have — maybe many times!
That is the **Mean Value Theorem**. Somewhere inside the journey,
the instant speed equals the average speed. The tangent matches the chord.

And when a limit gives you 0 over 0 — a race where both sides vanish —
let the DERIVATIVES race instead. That is **L'Hôpital's rule**.

Now you. Draw the hill. Mark the flat point. That is the theorem, in your hand.
