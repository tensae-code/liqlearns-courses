# Lesson plan — Rolle's theorem and the Mean Value Theorem

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 12 — Advanced Mathematics · **Lesson:** Rolle's theorem and the Mean Value Theorem
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 89 of 136, Unit 12 — Advanced Mathematics (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Rolle's theorem and the Mean Value Theorem — a flat tangent must occur between equal heights, and some point's tangent matches the whole journey's slope — with L'Hôpital's rule as the closing part.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Continuity (Level 88) and the derivatives lesson — the learner can already test continuity and differentiate. It needs nothing else.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Continuity (Level 88, same stage) — both theorems need continuity on the closed interval. Recommended, never forced.
- Recommended first: the derivatives lesson (same stage) — both theorems need differentiability on the open interval, and L'Hôpital's rule is differentiation at work. Recommended, never forced.
- Recommended first: Limits (Level 87, same stage) — L'Hôpital's rule is a rule about limits. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State Rolle's theorem with its three conditions and find the point c it guarantees, as Greece's Grade 12 Analysis book teaches it (W12, Ch.2, pp.91–184).
2. State the Mean Value Theorem and find c for a given function and interval, as Yemen's Grade 12 Science book teaches both theorems in one lesson (Unit 6, 6-7, contents/PDF p.183).
3. Follow the proof of both theorems — the Mean Value Theorem proved from Rolle's theorem with an auxiliary function — as Cambodia's PRIVATE Derivatives booklet proves them (pp.50–52).
4. Check the conditions before applying either theorem, and say what goes wrong when a condition fails.
5. Use the Mean Value Theorem to connect average and instantaneous rates, and to show a function with zero derivative everywhere is constant.
6. Apply L'Hôpital's rule to 0/0 and ∞/∞ limits, and reduce other indeterminate forms to quotients first, as Cambodia's PRIVATE Derivatives booklet teaches (pp.53–54) and Greece's book names De L'Hospital's rules (W12, Ch.2).

## If the learner is stuck
- Applies Rolle when the endpoint heights differ → that is the Mean Value Theorem's job: Rolle is the special case f(a) = f(b), with a flat (zero-slope) tangent.
- Forgets a condition → recite the pair: continuous on the closed interval, differentiable on the open one; then Rolle adds equal endpoint values.
- Solves for c and finds it outside the interval → the theorem guarantees c strictly inside (a, b); a root outside is a wrong root or an arithmetic slip.
- Uses L'Hôpital on a limit that is not 0/0 or ∞/∞ → check the form first; the rule is only for those two forms, and other forms must be turned into quotients first (Cambodia, PRIVATE Derivatives booklet, pp.53–54).
- This lesson's next door: integrals (same stage) — the theorems here are the bridge the fundamental theorem stands on.
