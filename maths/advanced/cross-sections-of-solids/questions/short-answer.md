# Short answer — Cross-Sections of Solids

1. (Layer 2) Why can a section of a prism never have a dent — a corner pointing inwards?
   **Answer: By Theorem 2 a section of a prism is a convex polygon, and a convex polygon has no inward corner — every interior angle is below 180°.** Tajikistan, *Геометрия* (Grade 11), printed p. 13.

2. (Layer 3) A cone's base area is 36π cm². A section parallel to the base is taken halfway down from the apex. What is the section's area, and which result gives it?
   **Answer: 9π cm² — by Theorem 15's second corollary the areas stand as the squares of the height ratios: (1/2)² = 1/4 of 36π.** Tajikistan, *Геометрия* (Grade 11), printed p. 69.

3. (Layer 3) In the book's worked problem, an oblique cone (H = 15 cm, R = 6 cm) is cut parallel to the base 10 cm above the base, and the section's radius comes out 2 cm. Show the step that gives 2.
   **Answer: The section is 15 − 10 = 5 cm from the apex; by Theorem 15 the section radius divides the base radius in the same ratio as the heights: 6 × 5/15 = 2 cm (and the area is π·2² = 4π cm²).** Tajikistan, *Геометрия* (Grade 11), printed p. 70.

4. (Layer 4) The book states: "Through two points of the sphere one and only one great circle can be drawn." Give the case where this fails and say why.
   **Answer: Antipodal points — the two ends of a diameter. Every diametral plane through that diameter cuts the sphere in a great circle through both points, and there are infinitely many such planes, so there are infinitely many great circles, not one.** Tajikistan, *Геометрия* (Grade 11), printed p. 85 — taught corrected, from the book's own definition of antipodal points at printed p. 81.
