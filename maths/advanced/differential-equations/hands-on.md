# Hands-on — Differential Equations

## Separate on paper
Take dy/dx = 2x. Write it as dy = 2x dx. All the y's are on the left already, all the x's on the right. Integrate both sides: y = x² + C. Now do dy/dx = y: write dy/y = dx, integrate: ln y = x + C, so y = Aeˣ. Feel the move: separate, then integrate.

## Growth in a jar
Put 100 beans in a jar and add 10% more each day (growth). Make a table: day 0: 100, day 1: 110, day 2: 121. The rate is proportional to the amount — dy/dx = ky. Solve it: y = 100e^(0.1x). Check the table against the formula.

## One point picks one curve
Solve dy/dx = 2x to get y = x² + C — a family of curves, one for each C. Now demand the curve pass through (0, 3): C = 3, and the answer is one curve, y = x² + 3. Plot the family and point at the one that passes through (0, 3).

## Check by differentiating
Take the answer y = x² + 3. Differentiate it: dy/dx = 2x. Put it back in the equation dy/dx = 2x: it fits. That check is the proof — the books' way of knowing a solution is right.
