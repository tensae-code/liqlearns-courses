**Acceptance count: taught in 4 countries (Jordan, Greece, Mauritania, Laos).** Jordan carries the lesson as the last step of its integration course, after substitution, partial fractions and parts, with the initial condition taught the year before; Greece carries the equations in elementary separable first-order form inside its integral-calculus chapter; Mauritania carries a whole chapter on differential equations in each of its two terminal streams.

**Honest limit, stated in the lesson:** this is the thinnest advanced lesson — three countries, one lesson or section each, all in separable first-order form. No read country teaches second-order equations, integrating factors, or any other method in the held set (Libya has no differential equations; Vietnam has none; Sierra Leone's SSS never teaches them; Kazakhstan's set tops out at Grade 9; Egypt teaches no differential equations as a topic; Armenia's Grade 12 guide teaches integrals, combinatorics and equations but no differential equations). This lesson teaches only what those three countries teach, and says so. Laos carries differential equations whole in Grade 12 — first-order and second-order linear equations with constant coefficients — with the falling-body model's d²y/dx² = 9.8 noted as a notation quirk for t, mathematics unaffected.
