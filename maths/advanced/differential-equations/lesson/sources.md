**Acceptance count: taught in 10 countries (Jordan, Greece, Mauritania, Laos, Iraq, Cambodia, Kuwait, Pakistan, Syria, Rwanda).** Jordan carries the lesson as the last step of its integration course, after substitution, partial fractions and parts, with the initial condition taught the year before; Greece carries the equations in elementary separable first-order form inside its integral-calculus chapter; Mauritania carries a whole chapter on differential equations in each of its two terminal streams. Iraq carries differential equations whole — a full chapter in Grade 12 Scientific (introduction, solving DEs, particular and general solutions, first-order first-degree ODEs, methods of solving DEs and the homogeneous equation y′ = f(y/x)), filed at opener/marker level since the chapter's worked examples were not individually read — and so joins Jordan, Greece, Mauritania and Laos as only the fifth country teaching the topic; the previous countries in this band (Nicaragua, Ethiopia, South Sudan) did not. The Literary track never meets differential equations. Cambodia carries differential equations whole — what a differential equation is, order, general and particular solutions, initial conditions, separable equations, linear first-order equations, homogeneous second-order equations with the characteristic equation, non-homogeneous second-order equations and physics models in Grade 12; the homogeneous-equation method, Bernoulli equations and Laplace transforms are not taught anywhere in the set. Kuwait carries differential equations in Grade 12 Term 2 (scientific track) — order and degree, and constant-coefficient second-order linear equations in their three discriminant cases, with a drug model; no separation of variables and no integrating factor is taught there.

Rwanda teaches differential equations whole in Advanced Mathematics Senior Six Unit 5 — classification, separable, homogeneous and linear first-order equations, second-order constant-coefficient equations by undetermined coefficients and variation of parameters, and cooling and circuit applications.

**Honest limit, stated in the lesson:** this is the thinnest advanced lesson — six countries, one lesson or section each, mostly in separable first-order form (Laos and Cambodia go further, to second-order linear equations with constant coefficients). No read country teaches integrating factors or any other method in the held set (Libya has no differential equations; Vietnam has none; Sierra Leone's SSS never teaches them; Kazakhstan's set tops out at Grade 9; Egypt teaches no differential equations as a topic; Armenia's Grade 12 guide teaches integrals, combinatorics and equations but no differential equations). This lesson teaches only what those six countries teach, and says so. Laos carries differential equations whole in Grade 12 — first-order and second-order linear equations with constant coefficients — with the falling-body model's d²y/dx² = 9.8 noted as a notation quirk for t, mathematics unaffected. Iraq carries differential equations whole — a full chapter in Grade 12 Scientific (introduction, solving DEs, particular and general solutions, first-order first-degree ODEs, methods of solving DEs and the homogeneous equation y′ = f(y/x)), filed at opener/marker level since the chapter's worked examples were not individually read.

Pakistan (Punjab) stocks this lesson partially, from §3.8 of the Class 12 mathematics book's integration chapter: first-order separable equations only, with their applications — no higher-order or linear theory.

Syria's differential equations are two theorems in Grade 12 Part One — y′ = ay and y′ = ay + b, both with unique solutions under an initial condition — plus the particular-solution reduction taught through guided problems; no separable or second-order equations are taught, and Part Two's general test examines exactly this scope.
