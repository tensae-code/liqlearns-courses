Complex numbers gave us ONE new unit: i, with i squared = minus 1.

This reading asks: why stop at one? Add j. Add k. THREE imaginary units — i, j and k — and every one of them squares to minus 1.

A quaternion is a + bi + cj + dk. One real part, three imaginary parts. Four numbers in one.

Add them part by part, like complex numbers. But MULTIPLY — careful! i times j is k. And j times i is MINUS k. The order changes the answer!

That is the shock of this reading: a number system where multiplication does not commute — and it still works.

Now you. Say the cycle: i j is k, j k is i, k i is j. Backwards, all minus. That is quaternions.
