# Lesson plan — Quaternions

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 12 — Advanced Mathematics · **Lesson:** Quaternions
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 99 of 136, Unit 12 — Advanced Mathematics (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** quaternions as an extended reading — numbers a + bi + cj + dk with three imaginary units i, j, k obeying i² = j² = k² = −1, added part by part, multiplied by a table in which the order matters: ij = k but ji = −k.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the complex-numbers lesson — the learner already knows i² = −1 and the form a + bi. It needs nothing else. It is a small, honest lesson: its only source is a two-page extended reading, and it is built at that size.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the complex-numbers lesson (a quaternion is the complex idea pushed one step further: one imaginary unit becomes three). Recommended, never forced.
- Recommended first: the De Moivre lesson is NOT needed — this reading stands on complex numbers alone. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a quaternion is — a number a + bi + cj + dk with a real part and three imaginary parts — as China's B-edition Compulsory Volume 4 extended reading presents it (Ch.10, extended reading, printed pp.47–48).
2. State the defining rules i² = j² = k² = −1 (China, B-edition Compulsory Volume 4, Ch.10 extended reading, printed pp.47–48).
3. Add and subtract quaternions part by part (China, B-edition Compulsory Volume 4, Ch.10 extended reading, printed pp.47–48).
4. Multiply unit quaternions by the table — ij = k, jk = i, ki = j, and the reversed products negative: ji = −k, kj = −i, ik = −j — and say why the multiplication is non-commutative (China, B-edition Compulsory Volume 4, Ch.10 extended reading, printed pp.47–48).
5. Form the conjugate a − bi − cj − dk and the modulus √(a² + b² + c² + d²), extending the complex conjugate and modulus (China, B-edition Compulsory Volume 4, Ch.10, printed pp.47–48, read with the chapter's complex-number work).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — two pages of reading carry the whole lesson.

2. **The explanation** — the form a + bi + cj + dk; the rules i² = j² = k² = −1; addition part by part; the multiplication table and non-commutativity; conjugate and modulus (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE at the form part: a picture of a quaternion as a point in four parts, one real axis and three imaginary axes sketched as a fan → the student names the four parts of 2 + 3i − j + 5k.

3. **Say it to your companion** — explain, with ij and ji, what non-commutative means and why quaternions cannot avoid it (lesson/lesson.md).

4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching, ordering — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.

## If the learner is stuck
- Treats j as another name for i → j is a separate unit: i² = j² = −1, but i ≠ j, and their product is neither of them — ij = k (China, B-edition Vol. 4, extended reading, pp.47–48).
- Assumes pq = qp → test it on units: ij = k but ji = −k. Order matters; check every product's order before swapping (China, B-edition Vol. 4, extended reading, pp.47–48).
- Loses a sign in the table → travel the cycle i → j → k → i for positive products (ij = k, jk = i, ki = j); travel it backwards for the negatives.
- Expects a bigger theory → there is none in the source: this topic is an extended reading of two pages, filed as an extended reading, and the lesson honestly ends where the reading ends.
