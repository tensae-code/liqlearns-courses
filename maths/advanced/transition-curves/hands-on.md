# Hands-on — Transition Curves: Joining Curves Smoothly

## Activity 1 — Feel the kink (10 minutes)

Draw the two straights y = 0 (up to x = 0) and y = 1 (from
x = 2) on graph paper. First join them with two straight
segments through the point (1, ½) and run your finger along
the join: feel the corner at (1, ½) — and the corners where
the segments meet the straights. Then plot the cubic
f(x) = −¼x³ + ¾x² at x = 0, ½, 1, 1½, 2 and draw its smooth
S. Run your finger again: no corner anywhere. This is Sub
lesson 1 felt by hand.

## Activity 2 — Hunt the curvature jump (15 minutes)

Using f′′(x) = −(3/2)x + 3/2 for the cubic join, tabulate
f′′ at x = 0, ½, 1, 1½, 2, and mark where the sign changes.
Then write one sentence, as if to a road builder, explaining
why a car entering the join at x = 0 still gets a jolt even
though there is no kink. This is Sub lesson 2 in your own
words.

## Activity 3 — Check the champion (15 minutes)

Take the quintic f(x) = 10x³ − 15x⁴ + 6x⁵. Make a
six-row checklist — value, slope, curvature at x = 0; value,
slope, curvature at x = 1 — and tick each one off with a
computation (you will need f′(x) = 30x²(1 − x)² and
f′′(x) = 60x(1 − x)(1 − 2x)). Then plot f at x = 0, ¼, ½,
¾, 1 and compare its shape with the cubic's S. This is Sub
lesson 3 checked by hand.
