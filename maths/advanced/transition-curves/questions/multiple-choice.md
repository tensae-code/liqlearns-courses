# Multiple choice — Transition Curves: Joining Curves Smoothly

Sub-lesson markers: every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to — Sub lesson 1 — Meeting Without a Kink · Sub lesson 2 — Matching the Bending · Sub lesson 3 — Designing the Join. This lesson was built new with its sub lessons (Germany expansion merge, 2026-11-11), so its markers are original — there are no older depth layers to map from.

1. (Sub lesson 1) At a join between two curve pieces, which condition stops the join from having a kink (a corner)?
   (a) The two pieces have equal values there
   (b) The two pieces have equal first derivatives there ✔
   (c) The two pieces have equal second derivatives there
   (d) The two pieces have the same colour
   **Answer: (b)** — equal values stop a step; equal slopes stop the corner. Item constructed for this lesson from its founding teaching. Germany, *Lambacher Schweizer Mathematik 10, Ausgabe Baden-Württemberg* (student book, Grade 10), Kapitel IV, GFS-Thema (Trassierungen), printed p. 122.

2. (Sub lesson 1) The cubic join f(x) = −¼x³ + ¾x² climbs from y = 0 to y = 1 on [0, 2]. Halfway along, f(1) is:
   (a) ¼
   (b) ½ ✔
   (c) ¾
   (d) 1
   **Answer: (b)** — f(1) = −¼ + ¾ = ½. Item constructed for this lesson from its founding teaching. Germany, *Lambacher Schweizer Mathematik 10, Ausgabe Baden-Württemberg* (student book, Grade 10), Kapitel IV, GFS-Thema (Trassierungen), printed p. 122.

3. (Sub lesson 2) Whether a graph is bending at a point — and how strongly — is read from:
   (a) f(x)
   (b) f′(x)
   (c) f′′(x) ✔
   (d) the x-intercept
   **Answer: (c)** — the second derivative carries the curvature; a jump in it at a join is the curvature jump. Item constructed for this lesson from its founding teaching. Germany, *Lambacher Schweizer Mathematik 10, Ausgabe Baden-Württemberg* (student book, Grade 10), Kapitel IV, GFS-Thema (Trassierungen), printed p. 122.

4. (Sub lesson 3) A join must match value, slope and curvature at BOTH ends — six conditions. The lowest-degree polynomial that can meet all six has degree:
   (a) 3
   (b) 4
   (c) 5 ✔
   (d) 6
   **Answer: (c)** — six conditions need six coefficients, and a degree-5 polynomial has exactly six. Item constructed for this lesson from its founding teaching.
