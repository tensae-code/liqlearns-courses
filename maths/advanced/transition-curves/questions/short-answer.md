# Short answer — Transition Curves: Joining Curves Smoothly

Sub-lesson markers: every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to — Sub lesson 1 — Meeting Without a Kink · Sub lesson 2 — Matching the Bending · Sub lesson 3 — Designing the Join. 1. (Sub lesson 1) For f(x) = −¼x³ + ¾x², verify the join at x = 2 with the straight y = 1.
   **Answer:** f(2) = −¼·8 + ¾·4 = −2 + 3 = 1, so the values match (no step); f′(x) = −¾x² + (3/2)x gives f′(2) = −3 + 3 = 0, matching the level straight (no kink). 2. (Sub lesson 2) The cubic join has no step and no kink, yet a driver still feels it. Explain why, with the numbers.
   **Answer:** the bending jumps. The straights have f′′ = 0, but the join's f′′(x) = −(3/2)x + 3/2 gives f′′(0) = 3/2 at the entry (and −3/2 at the exit): the curvature changes suddenly at both ends — a curvature jump, felt as a sudden change in steering. 3. (Sub lesson 3) A join on [0, 1] must match value, slope and curvature at both ends. How many conditions is that, and what is the lowest polynomial degree that can meet them? Name a piece that does it.
   **Answer:** 3 conditions × 2 ends = 6 conditions; a polynomial needs at least 6 coefficients, so degree 5. The quintic f(x) = 10x³ − 15x⁴ + 6x⁵ meets all six on [0, 1].
