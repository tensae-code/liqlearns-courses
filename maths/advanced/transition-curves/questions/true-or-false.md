# True or false — Transition Curves: Joining Curves Smoothly

Sub-lesson markers: every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to — Sub lesson 1 — Meeting Without a Kink · Sub lesson 2 — Matching the Bending · Sub lesson 3 — Designing the Join. This lesson was built new with its sub lessons (Germany expansion merge, 2026-11-11), so its markers are original — there are no older depth layers to map from.

1. (Sub lesson 1) Two pieces can meet with equal values at the join and still form a corner there, if their slopes differ. **True** — equal values remove the step; only equal slopes remove the kink. Item constructed for this lesson from its founding teaching. Germany, *Lambacher Schweizer Mathematik 10, Ausgabe Baden-Württemberg* (student book, Grade 10), Kapitel IV, GFS-Thema (Trassierungen), printed p. 122.

2. (Sub lesson 2) If f′′ jumps at a join, a driver feels a sudden change in the bending — the curvature jump the Trassierung is designed to avoid. **True** — the steering must change suddenly where the curvature does. Item constructed for this lesson from its founding teaching. Germany, *Lambacher Schweizer Mathematik 10, Ausgabe Baden-Württemberg* (student book, Grade 10), Kapitel IV, GFS-Thema (Trassierungen), printed p. 122.

3. (Sub lesson 2) For the cubic join f(x) = −¼x³ + ¾x², f′′(0) = 0, so its curvature matches the straight it leaves. **False** — f′′(x) = −(3/2)x + 3/2, so f′′(0) = 3/2, not 0: the curvature jumps at the start of the join. Item constructed for this lesson from its founding teaching. Germany, *Lambacher Schweizer Mathematik 10, Ausgabe Baden-Württemberg* (student book, Grade 10), Kapitel IV, GFS-Thema (Trassierungen), printed p. 122.

4. (Sub lesson 3) A cubic has four coefficients, so it can meet at most four independent join conditions. **True** — each condition is one equation the coefficients must satisfy; six conditions need a quintic.
