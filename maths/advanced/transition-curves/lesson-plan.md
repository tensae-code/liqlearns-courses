A lesson that teaches how two curve pieces are joined so the
join cannot be felt: the pieces must agree in value (no step),
in first derivative (no kink) and in second derivative (no
jump in curvature — the Krümmungsruck), and how the number of
join conditions fixes the degree of the polynomial piece that
can meet them. Germany's Grade 10 book (Lambacher Schweizer
*Mathematik 10*, Ausgabe Baden-Württemberg) teaches exactly
this as its GFS-Thema "Trassierungen" in Kapitel IV (printed
p. 122): joining road sections smoothly with polynomial
pieces, avoiding a curvature jump. No lesson in the course
treated joining curve pieces at all — the expansion read
flagged it NEW LESSON NEEDED and this lesson is built from
that flag. Prerequisite (soft hint only — every lesson stands
alone): derivatives for what f′ and f′′ measure. A learner who
opens this lesson directly still gets the whole thing: the
three conditions are stated, tested on a worked cubic join,
and then met in full by a quintic join here.
