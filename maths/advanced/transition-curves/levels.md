# Transition Curves: Joining Curves Smoothly — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, expressed as its SUB
LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure — README, "Units, Lessons and
Sub Lessons"). One lesson, one level (Level 171). The lesson
was built new in the expansion era (Germany expansion merge,
2026-11-11), so there are no old depth layers to map: the sub
lessons below are its depths as built, cut at the founding
section's own shape — first the join that matches value and
slope, then the curvature condition the cubic join still
fails, then the full design with all six conditions met. The
questions live in the type files; this file is the
by-sub-lesson view.

Pass marks: the founding section names its topic
(Trassierungen — smooth road-alignment joins) and states no
pass mark, so no mark is invented — each sub lesson's pass
mark is stated as the observable thing the learner can do.

## Sub lesson 1 — Meeting Without a Kink
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: state the two conditions a join must meet at a point to have no step and no kink (equal values, equal first derivatives), and verify both on the cubic join f(x) = −¼x³ + ¾x² where it meets y = 0 at x = 0 and y = 1 at x = 2.

## Sub lesson 2 — Matching the Bending
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: explain what f′′ measures at a join, show that the cubic join's curvature jumps at its ends (f′′(0) = 3/2 against the straight's 0), and state the third join condition — equal second derivatives — that removes the curvature jump.

## Sub lesson 3 — Designing the Join
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: count the conditions a full join imposes (value, slope and curvature at both ends = six), name the lowest polynomial degree that can meet them (five), and check the quintic 10x³ − 15x⁴ + 6x⁵ against all six conditions on [0, 1].

## What "maxed" means here

The learner designs a join the way Germany's Grade 10 book
teaches it in its Trassierungen GFS-Thema: conditions written
down first, the polynomial degree chosen to fit them, and the
finished piece checked condition by condition — value, slope
and curvature agreeing at both ends, so nothing jumps
(Lambacher Schweizer *Mathematik 10*, Ausgabe
Baden-Württemberg, Kapitel IV, GFS-Thema, printed p. 122).
