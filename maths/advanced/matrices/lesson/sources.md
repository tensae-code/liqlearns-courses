**Acceptance count: taught in 14 countries (Egypt, Jordan, Libya, Mauritania, Laos, Yemen, Indonesia, Nicaragua, Ethiopia, South Sudan, Iraq, Nepal, Cambodia, Kuwait).** Egypt carries the full matrix unit from organising data to inverses and Cramer's rule; Jordan carries matrix algebra through inverses and Cramer in its business course; Libya carries matrices and determinants with cofactors and compatibility; Mauritania carries matrices beside linear systems in two streamed books, with the Gauss method worked on a 4 × 4 system. Indonesia carries matrices in Grade 11 Tingkat Lanjut — concept, types, operations, determinant and inverse — in the 2021 student book and now in the 2024 Revisi student book itself, which goes past determinant and inverse to solving systems — a 2-variable system through the inverse and a 3-variable system by Cramer's rule; no eigenvalues in either edition. Nicaragua carries matrices whole in Grade 10 — the student book teaches 3×3 systems with full matrix algebra, inverses by Gauss–Jordan, determinants with Sarrus and Cramer, and a cryptography application, while the module covers the same ground as a subset (systems by reduction, determinants, Sarrus and Cramer, no matrix algebra or inverse). Ethiopia carries matrices and determinants whole in Grade 11 — matrix concepts, operations, types, row operations, inverses of order 2 and 3, solving systems by row operations, minors and cofactors, determinants, inverses via determinants and Cramer's rule — with the triangle-angles system worked by row reduction; matrices and determinants exist only in Grade 11. South Sudan carries matrices only as 2×2 in Secondary 2 — order, equality, addition and subtraction, scalar multiplication, multiplication, the identity matrix, the 2×2 determinant ad−bc, the inverse via AB = I and solving simultaneous equations using matrices — with no 3×3 determinants or inverses, so it covers this lesson only in part. Iraq carries matrices whole in Grade 11 — the full course in the Scientific track (definition, types, addition, scalar multiplication, multiplication, the inverse formula, 2×2 and 3×3 determinants and solving systems by inverse and by Cramer's rule) and the partial course in the Literary track (addition, scalar multiplication, 2×2 and 3×3 determinants and Cramer's rule, with no matrix multiplication and no inverse as a standalone method). G12 has no matrices in either track. Teaching note: the Literary book states (pp. 95–96) that Δ = 0 means no solution; the lesson teaches the fuller statement — Δ = 0 can also mean infinitely many solutions. Nepal carries matrices only in part — only in the Optional books: introduction, addition, subtraction and scalar multiplication in Grade 9 Optional, and transpose, multiplication, determinants and 2×2 inverses in Grade 10 Optional; the compulsory Grade 9 and Grade 10 books teach no matrices, and Nepal teaches no 3×3 matrices or determinants anywhere. Cambodia carries matrices only in part — and only from the Grade 11 Basic student book, since the Grade 12 booklets teach no matrices at all: definition, types, equality, transpose, addition, subtraction, scalar multiplication and multiplication, 2×2 and 3×3 determinants and Cramer's rule; no inverse in the student book (the self-study pack carries it) and no Gauss method anywhere in the set. Kuwait carries matrices in Grade 10 Term 2 — organising data, addition, subtraction and multiplication, the identity, the 2×2 determinant and inverse, and solving two-equation systems by the inverse matrix and Cramer's rule; matrices stop at 2×2 in this book, and its matrices are displayed right-to-left (the values here are cited in standard left-to-right order).

**Honest limit:** Libya's matrices chapter lists no matrix inverse and no solving of systems by matrices — the chapter stops at operations and transpose, and this lesson says so. Sierra Leone's SSS never teaches matrices; Vietnam teaches none; Greece teaches none in the read set; Kazakhstan's set tops out at Grade 9; Armenia teaches none. Laos carries matrices whole in Grade 12 — matrices, determinants and inverses, linear systems and linear programming. Yemen carries matrices whole in Grade 11, in both tracks — matrices and determinants, inverses and solving systems. Its literary book's worked example on printed p. 70 prints two determinants with dropped minus signs (|4 5; 3 2| printed 7, true −7; |1 2; 6 −4| printed 16, true −16) — the lesson teaches the correct values, never +7/+16.

**Flagged, not taught as printed:** the Grade 11 student book's printed p. 200 (§3.8) prints the last entry of the third row of the intermediate augmented matrix as −248; the correct value is −148 (R3 + 2R2: 192 + 2(−170) = −148), as the book's own equation line below shows (−2z = −148 ⇒ z = 74). The lesson teaches −148.
