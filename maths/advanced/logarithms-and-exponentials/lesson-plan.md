# Lesson plan — Logarithms and Exponentials

**Course:** Maths · **Stage:** Stage 12 — Advanced Mathematics · **Lesson:** Lesson 46 of 52
**Place in the curriculum:** Stage 12, the first lesson of the stage (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** logarithms and exponentials — powers with real exponents, the exponential function, and the logarithm as its inverse, with the log laws

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the powers-and-indices lesson and the functions lesson — logs are powers read backwards, and the log function is an inverse function. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Use powers with rational and real exponents, and the index laws, on them.
2. Describe the exponential function, its graph, and growth and decay models.
3. Say what a logarithm is: the logarithm is the power the base must be raised to.
4. Use the log laws — product, quotient, power — to simplify and to solve.
5. Draw the logarithmic function as the mirror of the exponential function.
6. Solve exponential and logarithmic equations.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil, a calculator (the books use one for logs). A doubling example to watch — coins doubling, or a plant doubling its leaves.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner doubles a number again and again, then asks the backwards question: how many doublings make 32? (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — real exponents; the exponential function; the logarithm; the log laws; the log function and its graph; exponential and logarithmic equations (lesson/lesson.md).
3. **Say it to your companion** — give your companion a power and ask for the log, then swap: a log, and ask for the power (lesson/lesson.md).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — model a real growth and a real decay, and solve two equations from the books (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Reads log as a number to multiply by → the log is a POWER: log₂ 8 = 3 because 2³ = 8. Always ask "2 to what power is 8?"
- Adds logs instead of multiplying inside → log(ab) = log a + log b: the plus outside is a times inside. Say it as "plus outside, times inside".
- Forgets a log needs a positive number inside → you cannot take the log of zero or a negative number; the books never do.
- This lesson's next door: complex numbers (same stage) — the next lesson asks what happens when even logs and powers cannot solve an equation.
