**Acceptance count: taught in 16 countries (Egypt, Jordan, Greece, Armenia, Vietnam, Libya, Mauritania, Laos, Yemen, Indonesia, Nicaragua, Ethiopia, South Sudan, Iraq, Bangladesh, Cambodia).** Egypt carries the full arc from rational exponents through the log laws to exp/log calculus; Jordan carries exponential growth and decay, the log laws and exp/log equations; Greece carries the exponential and logarithmic functions in one chapter; Armenia carries both streams' exp/log chapters with equations and inequalities; Vietnam carries the arc from real exponents to exp/log equations; Libya carries the index laws and logarithms from Grade 10 and exp/log calculus in Grade 12; Mauritania carries log and exponential functions in both terminal streams. Indonesia carries exponentials and logarithms from exponent laws, the exponential function and logarithms as the inverse of exponentiation in Grade 10 — including the Revisi Pengayaan on logarithms — to logarithmic functions in Grade 11 Tingkat Lanjut; exponential and logarithmic equations are not named topics in the held books. Nicaragua carries exponentials and logarithms from integer powers in the Grade 7 module, through the first full exposure in the Grade 10 module — aˣ, eˣ, compound interest, growth and decay, inverse functions, log laws and both kinds of equations — to the more formal second pass in Grade 11, with powers, roots and rational exponents, graphs with stated properties, exponential and logarithmic equations and base-10 logarithms, but without eˣ, natural logarithms or the module's applications. Ethiopia carries logarithms and exponentials whole in Grade 10 — exponent laws through irrational exponents, logarithms as inverses with the log laws, common and natural logarithms with the printed log table, graphs of aˣ and logₐx as reflections in y = x, exp/log equations with domain restrictions, and compound-interest, population-growth and pH applications — with exponent laws as background in Grade 9; logarithms as a standalone topic exist only in Grade 10. Iraq carries logarithms and exponentials from the behaviour and graphs of the exponential function aˣ in Grade 10 Scientific, through the full logarithm chapters in both Grade 11 tracks — logarithmic function, laws, common and natural logarithms, calculator use and the shared applications (Richter magnitude, pH, sound intensity, rocket speed, continuous compound interest, geometric mean) — to the natural logarithm inside Grade 12 Scientific integration, cited at opener/marker level only. Cambodia carries logarithms and exponentials whole — the exponential and logarithmic functions with properties, graphs, equations and inequalities, derivatives and limits in Grades 11–12, with change of base in Grade 11 Advanced and exponential growth and decay in the Differential Equations booklet; rational exponents as a topic and log tables are not pinned anywhere in the set.

**Honest limit:** Sierra Leone's SSS never teaches logarithms, and Vietnam teaches no logs or exponentials before Grade 11 Volume 2 — those countries are not counted here, and this lesson stands on the sixteen countries that do teach the topic. Laos carries exponentials and logarithms whole in Grade 11 — both functions with graphs, properties, equations, inequalities, maxima and minima and domain and range. Yemen carries logarithms and exponentials from powers and roots in Grade 10 to the exponential function and logarithms — concept, rules, common and natural logs and applications — in Grade 11, in both tracks. Ethiopia's Grade 10 student book printed p. 139 reads "log₃(1/27) = −3", correctly — a text-layer reading of "log₃27 = −3" is an extraction artefact, not a book error. South Sudan carries this lesson only in part — indices (fractional, zero and negative) in Secondary 2 as background, and derivatives and integrals of exponential and logarithmic functions, including ln as log base e, in Secondary 4 Additional Mathematics; it teaches no logarithms as a standalone topic, no log laws and no log equations. Its Secondary 2 teacher's guide (p. 6) states e = 2.78 — wrong; e ≈ 2.718, and the lesson teaches the corrected value. Bangladesh carries logarithms and exponentials only in part — exponents with multiplication and division in Class 7, and the full logarithm chapter in Class 9: exponential equations, base and argument limitations, natural and common logs, the log laws with proofs and applications to compound interest, depreciation, earthquake magnitude and sound intensity; it draws no graph of the exponential or the logarithmic function and names no rational exponents, so those rest on Egypt, Vietnam and the others — with three corrections taught, never as printed: the p. 61/64 claim that Napier first expressed the logarithm with e as the base (Napier's logarithms were not base-e); the p. 74 claim that R = 0 means no earthquake took place (magnitude 0 means the amplitude equals the standard) and that Richter invented the seismograph (he did not); and the p. 80 exercise asking for "the depreciation of the land" while the scenario is fertility increasing 3% a year (depreciation vs appreciation confused, no well-defined answer). The natural log is named but never used, and there are no log tables, no characteristic/mantissa and no antilog — values come from a calculator/device (p. 70).
