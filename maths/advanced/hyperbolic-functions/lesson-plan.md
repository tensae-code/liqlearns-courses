# Lesson plan — Hyperbolic Functions

**Course:** Maths · **Stage:** Stage 12 — Advanced Mathematics · **Lesson:** Hyperbolic Functions
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 95 of 136, Stage 12 — Advanced Mathematics (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** hyperbolic functions and their inverses — sinh, cosh, tanh and their partners, built from eˣ and e⁻ˣ: their definitions, graphs, identities, derivatives, log forms and inverse functions.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the logarithms-and-exponentials lesson and the derivatives lesson — the learner can already use eˣ, ln x and the basic derivative rules. It needs nothing else.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the logarithms-and-exponentials lesson (every hyperbolic function is built from eˣ and e⁻ˣ). Recommended, never forced.
- Recommended first: the derivatives lesson (the second half of this lesson differentiates the hyperbolic functions). Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define sinh x, cosh x and tanh x from eˣ and e⁻ˣ, and sinh, cosh and tanh of 0, the way Laos's Grade 12 book opens its hyperbolic-functions chapter (Ch.7, L19, printed p.207).
2. Sketch the graphs of sinh, cosh and tanh and state each domain and range, as Laos's Grade 12 book teaches in L20–L21 (printed pp.210, 217).
3. Use the identity cosh²x − sinh²x = 1 and its partners 1 − tanh²x = sech²x and coth²x − 1 = cosech²x (Laos, Grade 12, Ch.7, L19–L22, printed pp.207–221).
4. Write the hyperbolic and inverse hyperbolic functions in log form, as Pakistan's Class 12 book lists them among the types of functions (Ch.1, ch-pp.10–11).
5. Differentiate all six hyperbolic functions — with d/dx cosech x = −coth x cosech x and d/dx sech x = −sech x tanh x taught corrected, because Pakistan's Class 12 summary on Ch.2 ch-p.62 drops both minus signs (Ch.2, ch-pp.61–66).
6. Define the inverse hyperbolic functions, state their domains, and differentiate sinh⁻¹x, cosh⁻¹x and tanh⁻¹x (Laos, Grade 12, Ch.8, L25, printed p.243; Pakistan, Class 12, Ch.2, ch-pp.61–66).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A calculator that can do eˣ. Squared paper for the three graphs.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner computes (e¹ + e⁻¹)/2 and (e¹ − e⁻¹)/2 on a calculator and squares and subtracts them, meeting cosh² − sinh² = 1 before it is named (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the six functions from eˣ; the graphs, domains and ranges; the identities; the log forms; the derivatives, two of them corrected; the inverse hyperbolics (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the definitions part: a picture of the two curves y = eˣ and y = e⁻ˣ, with half their sum and half their difference drawn growing between them — the student names which new curve is cosh and which is sinh.
   - ADD IMAGE at the graphs part: a picture of the three graphs sinh, cosh and tanh on one page, cosh sitting like a hanging chain with its lowest point at (0, 1) → the student reads off each function's domain and range.
   - ADD IMAGE at the derivatives part: a picture of the six derivative rules as a paired table, sinh with cosh and tanh with sech², the two corrected minus signs marked → the student says the two corrected rules aloud.
3. **Say it to your companion** — explain why cosh never goes below 1, and state the two corrected derivative rules from memory (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — hyperbolic functions in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — evaluate, prove, differentiate and invert, the books' ways (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes sinh with sin → sinh is built from eˣ, not from a circle: sinh x = (eˣ − e⁻ˣ)/2. The h stands for hyperbolic, and Laos's book gives the hyperbolic functions a chapter of their own, apart from the trigonometric chapter (Grade 12, Ch.7, p.207).
- Thinks cosh can be negative → cosh x is half the sum of two positive numbers, eˣ and e⁻ˣ, so it is always positive — in fact always at least 1 (Laos, Grade 12, L21, p.217).
- Loses the minus signs in the derivatives of sech and cosech → those two rules carry a minus, taught corrected here: Pakistan's printed summary drops them (Ch.2, ch-p.62); the quotient-rule working gives the negative.
- Tries cosh⁻¹ of 0 → cosh⁻¹ is defined only for x ≥ 1, because cosh itself never goes below 1 (Laos, Grade 12, L25, p.243).
- This lesson's next door: Taylor and Maclaurin Series (Level 96, same stage) — functions rebuilt as endless polynomials.
