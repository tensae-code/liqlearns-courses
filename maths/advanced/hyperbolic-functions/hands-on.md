# Hands-on — Hyperbolic Functions

The lesson built the hyperbolic functions out of eˣ and e⁻ˣ.

## Build them from e
On a calculator, find e¹ and e⁻¹. Add them and halve: that is cosh 1 ≈ 1.543. Subtract them and halve: that is sinh 1 ≈ 1.175. Now square both and subtract: 1.543² − 1.175² ≈ 1. You have checked cosh²x − sinh²x = 1 at x = 1 with your own hands — the identity Laos's book builds its chapter on (Grade 12, Ch.7, L19, p.207).

## The hanging chain
Hang a light chain or necklace between two hands at the same height. Its curve is a cosh curve, scaled — lowest in the middle, rising both ways, never below its lowest point. Sketch it, mark the lowest point (0, 1) on the pure cosh graph, and say its domain (all x) and range (y ≥ 1), the way Laos's book states them (Grade 12, L21, p.217).

## Sign hunt for the corrected rules
Differentiate sech x = 1/cosh x with the quotient rule, step by step: the top's derivative is 0, so the answer must carry the minus from −sinh x on the bottom — you get −sech x tanh x. Do the same for cosech x. The minus is really there; Pakistan's printed summary on Ch.2 ch-p.62 drops it, and this activity is how you prove the corrected rule yourself.

## Inverse by swapping
Plot y = sinh x and draw the line y = x. Reflect the curve in the line: the reflected curve is y = sinh⁻¹x, defined for every x — the swap of domain and range Laos's book teaches for inverse hyperbolics (Grade 12, Ch.8, L25, p.243). Now try the same with cosh: only the right half (x ≥ 0) reflects into a function — that is why cosh⁻¹ needs x ≥ 1.
