# Hands-on — Parametric Equations

The lesson plotted curves from a travelling parameter, the way Cambodia's Grade 12 Advanced book opens the chapter (Ch.9, p.235).

## Table, points, curve
For x = t, y = t², fill a table for t = −3, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3. Plot the seven points on squared paper and join them in t-order, with arrows showing the direction of travel. Then eliminate t and write the Cartesian equation under your curve: y = x² (Cambodia, Grade 12 Advanced, Ch.9, printed p.235).

## Half a curve
Repeat with t ≥ 0 only. The same elimination gives y = x², but your drawing is only the right half — the t-interval is part of the curve's description, and eliminating the parameter throws that part away. Say in one sentence what was lost.

## Walk the projectile
For Cambodia's projectile, x = 24√2 t, y = −16t² + 24√2 t (v₀ = 48 cm/s, §9.1, printed p.236), compute the position at t = 0, 0.5, 1, 1.5 and at landing. Plot the five points and join them: an arc. Check t = 1 against the book: (24√2, 24√2 − 16). Then find the landing time from y = 0 (t = 3√2/2 ≈ 2.12) and the landing x (72 cm).

## Circle by parameter
Tabulate x = 3 sin θ, y = 3 cos θ for θ = 0, π/2, π, 3π/2, 2π and plot. The points walk a circle of radius 3 — the parametric circle form Cambodia's guide sets out for this chapter (guide printed pp.227–236). Square and add your two equations to prove x² + y² = 9.
