# Lesson plan — Parametric Equations

**Course:** Maths · **Stage:** Stage 12 — Advanced Mathematics · **Lesson:** Parametric Equations
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 97 of 136, Stage 12 — Advanced Mathematics (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** parametric equations and parametric curves — a curve given by x = f(t), y = g(t) as a parameter travels: plotting it point by point, eliminating the parameter, the slope dy/dx, and the projectile motion the books work in full.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the functions lesson and the coordinates-and-graphs lesson — the learner can already read (x, y) points and a function's graph. It needs nothing else.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the functions lesson and the coordinates-and-graphs lesson (a parametric curve is a moving point (x, y)). Recommended, never forced.
- Recommended first: the derivatives lesson for the slope of a parametric curve, dy/dx = (dy/dt)/(dx/dt). Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a parametric equation is — x = f(t), y = g(t) on an interval — and plot a curve point by point from a table of t, as Cambodia's Grade 12 Advanced book opens the topic (Ch.9, printed p.235).
2. Eliminate the parameter to get a Cartesian equation, as in the chapter's opening example x = t, y = t² → y = x² (Cambodia, Grade 12 Advanced, Ch.9, printed p.235).
3. Write and read the parametric equations of circles and ellipses, as Cambodia's Grade 12 Advanced teacher guide sets them out for the chapter (guide printed pp.227–236).
4. Work a projectile in parametric form with the book's own numbers — v₀ = 48 cm/s at 45°, g = 32 cm/s², x = 24√2 t, y = −16t² + 24√2 t — taught with v₀ = 48, never the guide's misprinted 45 (Cambodia, Grade 12 Advanced, Ch.9 §9.1, printed p.236).
5. Eliminate t from the projectile to get its path y = −x²/72 + x, and find where it lands (Cambodia, Grade 12 Advanced, Ch.9 §9.1, printed p.236).
6. Recognise parametric curves as a named final-year topic in a second country: Mauritania's 7AS-C book gives them a chapter of their own, "Courbes paramétrées" (Ch.13, printed p.159).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Squared paper. A calculator for the √2 values.

## If the learner is stuck
- Plots y against t instead of y against x → the curve lives in the (x, y) plane; t only drives. Each t gives ONE point (f(t), g(t)) — plot points, not two graphs (Cambodia, Grade 12 Advanced guide, printed pp.227–236).
- Eliminates the parameter and loses the curve's extent → eliminating gives the path's shape, but the t-interval still says which part is travelled: x = t, y = t² with t ≥ 0 is only the right half of y = x².
- Uses the guide's v₀ = 45 for the projectile → teach 48: the student book's v₀ = 48 cm/s is the value its own equations require — with g = 32 cm/s², y = −x²/72 + x follows from 48, not from 45 (Cambodia, Grade 12 Advanced, §9.1, printed p.236).
- Differentiates y with respect to x directly → go through t: dy/dx = (dy/dt) ÷ (dx/dt), provided dx/dt ≠ 0.
- This lesson's next door: Barycentres (Level 98, same stage) — points weighted and balanced.
