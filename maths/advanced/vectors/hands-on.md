# Hands-on — Vectors

## The walk home
Walk three steps north, then four steps east, on a grid. Draw both arrows, then draw the straight arrow from your start to your finish. Measure it: 5 steps. That is vector addition, done with your feet — the triangle the books draw.

## Parallelogram on paper
Draw two arrows from the same point, one going right and one going up. Complete the parallelogram and draw its diagonal: the diagonal is the sum, the way Egypt's book teaches the parallelogram rule. Now draw the difference: the arrow from the tip of one to the tip of the other.

## Dot product with shadows
Draw two arrows with 60° between them. The dot product is the size of one times the shadow of the other on it. Work it out with cos 60° = 1/2. Now open the angle to 90°: the shadow is gone, and the dot product is zero. That is why perpendicular vectors have dot product zero.

## Vectors in space
Build a corner of a room with three edges meeting: along the floor one way, along the floor the other way, and up. Those are the three directions of space. Write a vector as three numbers (x, y, z) and find its length with the 3-D Pythagoras: √(x² + y² + z²).
