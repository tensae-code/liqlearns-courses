# Lesson plan — Vectors

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 12 — Advanced Mathematics · **Lesson:** Lesson 49 of 52
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 12, the fourth lesson of the unit (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** vectors — quantities with size and direction, in two and three dimensions, and how to add, subtract and multiply them

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the coordinates-and-graphs lesson (vectors live on coordinates) and the matrices lesson (another object with its own arithmetic). Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Tell a scalar from a vector, and a directed line segment from a position vector.
2. Write a vector in coordinates and with unit vectors, and find its magnitude.
3. Add and subtract vectors, including by the parallelogram rule, and multiply by a number.
4. Find the dot (scalar) product of two vectors, and the angle between them.
5. Work with vectors in space: 3-D coordinates, lines in space, and the cross product.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A squared-paper grid to draw arrows on, and a ruler and protractor.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner walks three steps north then four steps east, and finds the straight-line way home (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — scalars and vectors; vectors in coordinates; adding and subtracting; the dot product; vectors in space and the cross product (lesson/lesson.md).
3. **Say it to your companion** — give your companion two arrows and ask for their sum, their difference, and their dot product (lesson/lesson.md).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — draw a journey as vectors, add them, and find a dot product and an angle (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE at the adding part: a picture of two arrows joined head-to-tail, with the sum arrow drawn from the first tail to the last head — the student traces the sum with a finger.
   - ADD GIF at the dot-product part: a picture of one arrow casting its shadow onto another as the angle between them changes, the shadow longest at 0° and gone at 90° — the student says when the dot product is zero.

## If the learner is stuck
- Adds vectors like numbers → vectors add head-to-tail: the direction matters as much as the size; draw the arrows first.
- Forgets the dot product is a number → the dot product is a scalar, not an arrow: a·b = |a||b|cos θ is one number.
- Thinks two vectors are equal when they are the same length → equal vectors need the same size AND the same direction.
- This lesson's next door: derivatives (same unit) — the next lesson asks how fast a moving point's position vector changes.
