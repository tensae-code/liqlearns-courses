**Acceptance count: taught in 19 countries (Egypt, Jordan, Greece, Vietnam, Libya, Mauritania, Laos, Yemen, Indonesia, Ethiopia, South Sudan, Iraq, Nepal, Cambodia, Kuwait, Iran, China, Pakistan, Syria).** Egypt carries vectors from directed segments and the parallelogram rule to 3-D vectors with dot and cross products; Jordan carries plane vectors with the dot product and space vectors with lines in space; Greece carries the orientation group's vector chapter with the inner product; Vietnam carries plane vectors with the dot product and space vectors with coordinates; Libya carries plane vectors with the scalar product; Mauritania carries vector calculus in two chapters. Indonesia carries vectors in Grade 10 Merdeka — 2-D and 3-D vectors with components, position and unit vectors, addition, subtraction and scalar multiplication — and plane vectors with the dot product, angles, projection and geometric proofs in the Grade 11 Tingkat Lanjut Revisi student book; the general Grade 10 Revisi drops vectors entirely. Ethiopia carries vectors from 2-D basics in Grade 9 — directed segments and column vectors, magnitude and direction, the triangle and parallelogram laws, scalar multiples and position vectors — to vectors with dot and cross products in Grade 11, in two dimensions with i/j notation; there are no 3-D vectors in Ethiopia. South Sudan carries vectors from translation review, position vectors and addition in Secondary 2, through column vectors, position vectors, mid-point, magnitude, scalar multiplication, parallel vectors and 3D coordinates and vectors in Secondary 4 Mathematics, to i, j, k notation, position vectors in 3D, the vector equation of a line, the scalar (dot) product and the angle between vectors in Secondary 4 Additional Mathematics — with one correction: Additional Mathematics p. 174 writes the dot-product example as a·b = … = 16; the correct sum is 22 (2×3+0×3+4×4 = 22), and the final answer θ = 35.2° is correct because it uses 22; the lesson teaches 22. Iraq carries vectors only in part — plane vectors only in Grade 10 Scientific (the vector concept, bound/position vectors, length and direction, adding vectors and multiplying by a real number, and expressing vectors via the unit vectors U1 = (1,0) and U2 = (0,1)). It teaches no 3D vectors anywhere — space geometry is synthetic throughout — and no dot or cross product, so Iraq enters this lesson as a partial. Nepal carries vectors only in part — only in the Optional books: introduction, the triangle law, operations, scalar multiplication, magnitude, direction and unit vectors in Grade 9 Optional, with no dot product, and the scalar (dot) product with midpoint and division theorems in Grade 10 Optional; the compulsory Grade 9 and Grade 10 books teach no vectors, and Nepal teaches no cross product anywhere. Cambodia carries vectors whole — plane vectors with operations and the dot product in Grade 10, space vectors with the cross and triple products, lines, planes and spheres in Grades 11–12, with direction cosines, angles and distances; vector functions are the only part not taught anywhere in the set. Kuwait carries vectors only in part, and only as workbook exercises: the Grade 11 Term 1 workbook (scientific track) drills plane vectors — components, magnitude and direction, unit vectors, addition and subtraction, and the scalar product — but that term's student book is not in hand, so no student-book teaching is claimed for Kuwait in this lesson. Iran begins vectors with directed segments and the translation vector in Grade 7, teaches full two-dimensional vectors in Grade 8, and reaches three dimensions — dot, cross and triple products — in Grade 12 Geometry (3); Grade 11 Geometry (2) contributes translation vectors only.
