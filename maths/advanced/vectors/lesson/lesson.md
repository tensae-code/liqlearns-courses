# Vectors — the lesson

Course: Maths · Unit 12 — Advanced Mathematics · Lesson 49 of 52
Built from: Egypt, Secondary 1 Term 2 and Pure Mathematics Grade 12 — Jordan, Grade 10 Term 2 and Grade 12 Term 2 — Greece, Mathematics Grade 11 (orientation) — Vietnam, Grades 10 and 12 — Libya, Grade 12 Science — Mauritania, 7AS-C.

## Do it first
Walk three steps north, then four steps east. You are five steps from where you started, in a straight line — three, four, five. Your two walks added up to one straight walk. That straight walk is the sum of your two vectors, and you found it with your feet before any formula.

## Sub lesson 1 — Arrows: Size and Direction
**Scalars and vectors.** A **scalar** has size only: a temperature, a mass, a time. A **vector** has size AND direction: a walk, a force, a velocity. Egypt's Secondary 1 book starts with scalar versus vector quantities, then **directed line segments** — arrows with a start and an end — and **position vectors**, arrows drawn from the origin to a point. Vietnam's Grade 10 book opens its vector chapter with the same first ideas. A vector is drawn as an arrow: its length is its **magnitude** (or modulus), and the way it points is its direction. Two vectors are **equal** when they have the same magnitude and the same direction, wherever they are drawn.

## Sub lesson 2 — Coordinates and the Dot Product
**Vectors in coordinates.** On a grid, a vector is written by how far it goes across and how far it goes up: the vector (3, 4) goes 3 across and 4 up, and its magnitude is 5, by Pythagoras. Egypt's book writes vectors in terms of the **unit vectors** — the arrows of length 1 along the axes — and teaches magnitude and equality of vectors. Jordan's Grade 10 book teaches vectors in the coordinate plane as its own unit. Vietnam's book teaches vectors in the coordinate plane, and its Grade 12 book teaches vectors in space with a coordinate system. A **position vector** names a point by the arrow from the origin to it.

**Adding and subtracting.** Add two vectors **head-to-tail**: put the second arrow's tail on the first arrow's head, and the sum is the arrow from the first tail to the last head. Egypt's book teaches addition with the **parallelogram rule** — the sum is the diagonal of the parallelogram the two arrows make — and subtraction and **scalar multiplication** (stretching an arrow by a number, or flipping it when the number is negative). Jordan's book teaches adding and subtracting vectors, and Greece's book teaches addition and subtraction of vectors, multiplication of a number by a vector, and coordinates in the plane. Vietnam's book teaches the sum and difference of two vectors, and the product of a vector with a number.

**The dot product.** The **dot product** (or scalar product) of two vectors is a number, not an arrow: a·b = |a||b|cos θ, where θ is the angle between them. When the vectors point the same way, the dot product is at its biggest; when they are perpendicular (θ = 90°), cos θ = 0, and the dot product is zero — the books' test for perpendicular vectors. Jordan's Grade 10 book teaches the **scalar (dot) product**, including work, and its Grade 12 book teaches the scalar product in space. Greece's book teaches the **inner (dot) product** of vectors. Vietnam's book teaches the dot product of two vectors. Libya's book teaches the scalar product of a vector and the magnitude of a vector. Egypt's Grade 12 book teaches **vector multiplication** — dot and cross products and their geometric meaning.

## Sub lesson 3 — Vectors in Space
**Vectors in space and the cross product.** In three dimensions a vector has three numbers (x, y, z), and its magnitude is √(x² + y² + z²). Egypt's Grade 12 book teaches the **3-D orthogonal coordinate system**, **vectors in space**, and the **cross product**: a × b is a new vector, perpendicular to both a and b, whose size is |a||b|sin θ — the area of the parallelogram the two vectors make. Jordan's Grade 12 book teaches vectors in space, **lines in space** (a point plus a direction vector), and the scalar product. Vietnam's Grade 12 book teaches vectors in space, the coordinate system in space, and coordinate expressions for vector operations. Egypt's Secondary 1 book already uses direction vectors to write the **equation of a straight line** from a point and a direction vector, and from two points.

> **Tip — from us, not from the books:** dot product and cross product are easy to mix up. Say it this way: the DOT product dots two arrows down to ONE NUMBER (how much of one lies along the other); the CROSS product crosses two arrows into a NEW ARROW (sticking out sideways from both). Dot is a number, cross is an arrow.

## Say it to your companion
Give your companion two arrows on squared paper — say (3, 0) and (0, 4). Ask them for the sum (3, 4), the difference (3, −4), the magnitudes (3, 4, 5), and the dot product (0 — they are perpendicular). Then swap: they give you two arrows, and you answer. Finish by asking: "Are these two vectors equal?" — and check size AND direction.

## Do It Yourself
1. Walk (or draw) 3 steps north and 4 steps east. Draw the sum arrow and measure it: 5 steps.
2. Add (2, 3) and (−1, 5). Subtract them. Find both magnitudes.
3. Find the dot product of (1, 2) and (3, 4): 1×3 + 2×4 = 11. Find the angle between them (cos θ = 11/(√5 × 5)).
4. Write a vector in space, (1, 2, 2), and find its magnitude: 3.
