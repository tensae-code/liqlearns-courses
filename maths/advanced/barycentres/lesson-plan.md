# Lesson plan — Barycentres

**Course:** Maths · **Stage:** Stage 12 — Advanced Mathematics · **Lesson:** Barycentres
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 98 of 136, Stage 12 — Advanced Mathematics (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** barycentres — the balance point of weighted points: the barycentre of two points, of a system of points, its vector condition and coordinates, grouping (associativity), and the centroid of a triangle as the equal-weights case.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the vectors lesson and the coordinates lesson — the learner can already use position vectors and coordinates. It needs nothing else.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define the barycentre of two weighted points (A, α) and (B, β) by α·GA + β·GB = 0 (vectors), and find it on the segment AB, closer to the heavier point (Laos, Grade 11, Ch.9, L33, printed p.249).
2. Compute a two-point barycentre in coordinates: G = (αA + βB)/(α + β), part by part (Laos, Grade 11, Ch.9, L33, printed p.249).
3. Define and compute the barycentre of a system of points, with the coordinate formula x_G = (αx_A + βx_B + γx_C)/(α + β + γ) for three points (Laos, Grade 11, Ch.9, L34, printed p.254).
4. Group points in stages (associativity): replace two points by their barycentre carrying their total weight, then continue (Laos, Grade 11, Ch.9, L34, printed p.254).
5. Recognise the centroid of a triangle as the barycentre with equal weights, on the medians (Laos, Grade 11, Ch.9, L33–L34, printed pp.249, 254).
6. Recognise the barycentre as a chapter in its own right across Mauritania's streams: 5AS-M (Ch.6, printed p.57), 5AS-D (Ch.6, printed p.47), 6AS-M (Ch.4, "Barycentre d'un système de points pondérés", printed p.51) and 6AS-C (Ch.4, printed p.51).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A ruler. Two or three small weights or coins for the balance test.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner balances a ruler on a finger, first bare, then with a coin taped near one end, and watches the balance point move towards the weight before the word barycentre is used (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — two weighted points and the vector condition; coordinates; a system of points; grouping; the centroid as equal weights (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the two-points part: a picture of a segment AB with weights growing at B, the balance point G sliding along the segment towards B — the student says which end G approaches as β grows.
   - ADD IMAGE at the system part: a picture of a triangle ABC with its three medians drawn and the centroid marked where they meet → the student checks the centroid is the average of the three vertices' coordinates.
   - ADD IMAGE at the grouping part: a picture of three weighted points, two of them first merged into one heavier point, then merged with the third → the student states the total weight carried at each merge.
3. **Say it to your companion** — explain why the barycentre sits closer to the heavier point, using the vector condition, not just the picture (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Puts G nearer the lighter point → go back to the balance: the heavier end needs the shorter arm. In α·GA + β·GB = 0, the bigger weight multiplies the shorter vector (Laos, Grade 11, L33, p.249).
- Averages the coordinates without the weights → the formula weights each coordinate first: (αA + βB)/(α + β); plain averaging is only the equal-weights case (Laos, Grade 11, L34, p.254).
- Loses track in a system → group: merge two points into their barycentre with their weights added, then merge that point with the next (Laos, Grade 11, L34, p.254).
- Weights summing to zero → if α + β = 0 (and α ≠ 0) there is no barycentre: the vector condition cannot be met by any point. Keep to ordinary positive weights in this lesson.
