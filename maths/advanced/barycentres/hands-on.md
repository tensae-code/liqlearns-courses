# Hands-on — Barycentres

The lesson balanced weighted points, the way Laos's Grade 11 book teaches them (Ch.9, L33–L34, pp.249, 254).

## Ruler and coins
Balance a ruler on one finger and mark the balance point: the midpoint. Tape one coin at the 0 cm end and balance again — the point moves towards the coin. Tape a second coin at the same end: it moves further. The heavier end always wins the shorter arm — the picture behind α·GA + β·GB = 0 (Laos, Grade 11, L33, printed p.249).

## Two points on paper
Draw A at (0, 0) with weight 2 and B at (6, 0) with weight 1. Compute G = (2A + 1B)/3 = (2, 0). Measure GA = 2 and GB = 4 on your drawing and check the vector condition in lengths: 2 × GA = 1 × GB — the weights and the arms trade exactly (Laos, Grade 11, L33, printed p.249).

## Grouping a system
Take A(0, 0) weight 1, B(6, 0) weight 1, C(0, 6) weight 2. First merge A and B: their barycentre is (3, 0) with weight 2. Then merge (3, 0) weight 2 with C(0, 6) weight 2: G = (1.5, 3). Now compute directly from the formula x_G = (αx_A + βx_B + γx_C)/(α + β + γ): ((0 + 6 + 0)/4, (0 + 0 + 12)/4) = (1.5, 3). Same point, both ways — grouping works (Laos, Grade 11, L34, printed p.254).

## Balance a triangle
Cut a cardboard triangle and find where it balances flat on a fingertip. Draw its medians and mark their meeting point: the balance point and the medians' meeting point are the same — the centroid, the equal-weights barycentre of the three vertices (Laos, Grade 11, L33–L34, printed pp.249, 254). Laos's earlier notable-lines lesson finds the same point by balancing a paper triangle on a pin (Grade 11, Ch.9 as filed; the balancing activity is the book's own).
