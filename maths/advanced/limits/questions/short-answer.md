# Limits — short answer

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — The Limit Idea and One-Sided Limits · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Computing Limits · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Infinity, Sequences, the Sandwich Theorem and ε-δ.

Answer in a word or a short line.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Using a table, what does (x² − 1)/(x − 1) approach as x → 1?
**Answer:** 2 — at 0.9, 0.99, 0.999 the values are 1.9, 1.99, 1.999.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** A function is 1 for x < 0 and 3 for x > 0. What is lim(x→0⁻) f(x)?
**Answer:** 1 — approaching from the left, the values sit at 1.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Find lim(x→3) (x² − 9)/(x − 3).
**Answer:** 6 — factorise to (x − 3)(x + 3), cancel, substitute: 3 + 3.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** Find lim(x→−2) (x² + 5x + 6)/(x + 2).
**Answer:** −1 — factorise to (x + 2)(x + 3), cancel, substitute: −2 + 3.

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Find lim(x→∞) (3x² + x)/(x² − 5).
**Answer:** 3 — divide top and bottom by x²; the limit is the ratio of leading coefficients.

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** The sequence has n-th term 1 + 1/n. What is its limit?
**Answer:** 1 — 1/n → 0, so the terms settle on 1.

**7. (Sub lesson 3)** Find lim(x→∞) (5 − x)/x, and name the horizontal asymptote of the graph of y = (5 − x)/x.
**Answer:** −1; the asymptote is y = −1 — divide through by x: (5/x) − 1 → −1, an infinite branch settling on −1 (Mauritania, 7AS-LO, Ch.2, p.24).

**8. (Sub lesson 2)** Find lim(x→4) (x² − 16)/(x − 4).
**Answer:** 8 — factorise to (x − 4)(x + 4), cancel, substitute: 4 + 4 (Jordan, Grade 11 Term 1 Exercise Book, Unit 3, p.46 — workbook evidence).

**9. (Sub lesson 3)** The function g is (x² − 9)/(x − 3) for x ≠ 3, and g(3) = 6. Is g continuous at x = 3? Say why in one line.
**Answer:** Yes — its limit at 3 is 6 and that equals g(3), so the limit meets the function's own value (Jordan, Grade 11 Term 1 Student Book, Limits and continuity, p.50; its companion Exercise Book, limits/continuity set, p.34).
