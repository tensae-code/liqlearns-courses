# Hands-on — Limits

The lesson worked limits from tables, graphs and the books' methods.

## Closer and closer table
For f(x) = (x² − 1)/(x − 1), compute f at x = 0.9, 0.99, 0.999, then at x = 1.1, 1.01, 1.001. Write what both lists head for. This is the estimating-from-a-table method South Sudan's book opens with (Secondary 4 Additional, pp.3–13).

## Two sides of a jump
Draw a graph that sits at height 1 for x < 2 and at height 3 for x ≥ 2. Read off the left-hand limit, the right-hand limit and f(2). Say, in a full sentence, why the two-sided limit at 2 does not exist.

## Halving walk
On paper, mark a start point and a wall 32 steps apart. Halve the remaining distance each time: 16, 8, 4, 2, 1, 1/2, 1/4 … Add the steps: the total heads for 32 and never passes it — a geometric series settling on its limit, the way Cambodia's Grade 12 Advanced book treats geometric-sequence limits (Ch.1, pp.1–18).

## The squeeze in your hands
Compute sin x / x (x in radians) at x = 0.5, 0.2, 0.1, 0.01 on a calculator. Watch it close on 1. Then check cos x / x at x = 100: your calculator gives about +0.00862 — Kuwait's book prints the sign wrong on p.47, and this lesson teaches the corrected value.
