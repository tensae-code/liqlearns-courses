**Acceptance count: taught in 14 countries (Greece, Pakistan, Libya, Vietnam, Armenia, Iran, Egypt, Cambodia, Kuwait, Laos, South Sudan, Indonesia, Mauritania, Jordan).** Greece teaches limits as a full chapter of its Grade 12 Analysis book, from the limit at a point through limits at infinity. Pakistan teaches the limit theorems, the standard limits and the sandwich theorem in Class 11 and again in Class 12, with the Class 12 p.25 limit at infinity taught corrected as −∞. Libya teaches indeterminate values, one-sided limits and limits at infinity in its Grade 11 Science limits chapter. Vietnam teaches limits of sequences then limits of functions in Grade 11. Armenia builds sequence limits from infinitesimals and reaches the number e. Iran teaches limits intuitively with no ε-δ in Hesaban (1), then infinite limits and asymptotes in Hesaban (2). Egypt teaches limits numerically, graphically and algebraically, including trigonometric limits, in Pure Grade 11. Cambodia teaches sequence limits in Grade 12 Advanced, the ε-δ definition in Grade 12 Basic, and formal proofs by definition in its PRIVATE Khem Putthi Limits and Continuity booklet. Kuwait teaches limits, limits involving ±∞, indeterminate forms and trigonometric limits in Grade 12 Term 1, with its p.47 table signs taught corrected. Laos teaches the intuitive definition, one-sided limits and the existence condition in Grade 10. South Sudan teaches the full limits unit — estimation, theorems, one-sided limits, limits at infinity and the squeeze theorem — in Secondary 4 Additional. Indonesia teaches limits as a standalone chapter, including left/right limits, indeterminate forms and trigonometric limits, in Grade 12 Tingkat Lanjut. Mauritania teaches limits with continuity as one chapter across its senior streams — 6AS-M and 6AS-C (Ch.3, p.31), 6AS-D (Ch.IV, p.64) and 6AS-LO (Ch.3, p.28) — and returns to limits inside the final-year functions study in 7AS-C (Ch.5, p.63), 7AS-D (Ch.2, p.23), 7AS-LM (Ch.2, p.23) and 7AS-LO (Ch.2, p.24, with comparisons and infinite branches). Jordan teaches limits and continuity in its held Grade 11 Term 1 student book (Academic Track) — Unit 2, Limits and Derivatives, Lesson 1 at p.50, done properly as the formal basis Grade 10 skipped — with the lesson drilled in that book's own companion exercise book (the -6262 copy, limits/continuity set at p.34); separately, its Grade 11 Term 1 orphan exercise book — a different book whose companion student book is not in hand — also drills limits and continuity in its Limits and Derivatives unit (p.46), and that second book counts as workbook evidence only.
