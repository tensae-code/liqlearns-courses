# Lesson plan — Limits

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 12 — Advanced Mathematics · **Lesson:** Limits
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 87 of 136, Unit 12 — Advanced Mathematics (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** limits — what a function or a sequence gets close to: limits of sequences and of functions, one-sided limits, limits at infinity, the sandwich theorem, and the ε-δ definition where the books teach it.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the functions and sequences lessons — the learner can already read a function's values and a sequence's terms. It needs nothing else.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what lim(x→a) f(x) = L means and estimate a limit from a table and a graph, the way South Sudan's Secondary 4 Additional book opens its limits unit (pp.3–13).
2. Find one-sided limits and use them to decide whether a two-sided limit exists, as Libya's Grade 11 Science book teaches in its limits chapter (pp.111–138).
3. Use the limit theorems (sum, product, quotient, power, root) to compute limits, as Pakistan's Class 11 book states them (Unit 12, pp.218–236).
4. Remove a 0/0 form by factorising and cancelling or by multiplying by the conjugate, as Iran's Hesaban (1) teaches (p.141).
5. Find limits at infinity and infinite limits, and read off horizontal and vertical asymptotes, as Iran's Hesaban (2) teaches (pp.56–67).
6. Find the limit of a sequence, decide whether it converges, and find the limit of a geometric sequence and series, as Cambodia's Grade 12 Advanced book teaches (Ch.1, pp.1–18).
7. Use the sandwich (squeeze) theorem, including lim sin θ / θ = 1 as θ → 0, as Pakistan's Class 12 book teaches (Ch.1, ch-pp.18–31).
8. State the ε-δ definition of a limit and use it in a simple proof, as Cambodia's Grade 12 Basic book teaches (Ch.1 §1.1, pp.2–27) and Cambodia's PRIVATE Limits and Continuity booklet proves for nine limit types (pp.9–31).
9. Decide whether a function is continuous at a point by checking that its limit there exists and equals the function's own value there, as Jordan's Grade 11 Term 1 student book teaches in Limits and continuity (Unit 2, p.50), drilled in its companion exercise book (limits/continuity set, p.34).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A calculator for the tables. A ruler for drawing graphs.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner computes 1/2, 1/4, 1/8, … and (x²−1)/(x−1) at x = 0.9, 0.99, 0.999 and says what value each list is heading for (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the limit idea; one-sided limits; the limit theorems; removing 0/0; limits at infinity; sequence limits; the sandwich theorem; ε-δ where taught (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the limit-idea part: a picture of a point sliding along a curve towards x = 2 from both sides, its height settling near one value — the student says the value it settles near.
   - ADD IMAGE at the one-sided part: a picture of a graph with a jump at x = 1, the left height and the right height marked differently → the student reads both one-sided limits and says whether the limit exists.
   - ADD IMAGE at the infinity part: a picture of the graph of y = 1/x flattening towards the x-axis and shooting up near x = 0 → the student names the horizontal and the vertical asymptote.
   - ADD GIF at the sandwich part: a picture of sin x / x trapped between two curves that both close on 1 at 0 — the student says why the middle curve must also land on 1.
3. **Say it to your companion** — explain in your own words why a limit can exist at a point where the function itself is not defined (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — limits in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — compute five limits the books' ways and build one table of your own (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the limit is the value AT the point → work the table for (x²−1)/(x−1) at x = 0.9, 0.99, 0.999: the function is not defined at x = 1 at all, yet the values plainly head for 2.
- Gets 0/0 and stops → 0/0 is not an answer, it is a signpost: factorise and cancel, or multiply top and bottom by the conjugate, the way Iran's Hesaban (1) does (p.141).
- Mixes up the two sides → always write lim(x→a⁻) and lim(x→a⁺) separately first; the two-sided limit exists only when both agree, as Laos's Grade 10 book states (pp.166–175).
- Confuses a limit at infinity with an infinite limit → at infinity, x runs away and we watch f(x); an infinite limit is f(x) itself running away at one x.
- This lesson's next door: Continuity (Level 88, same unit) — a limit that meets the function's own value.
