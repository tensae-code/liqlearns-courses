# Lesson plan — Geometry in Space

**Course:** Maths · **Stage:** Stage 12 — Advanced Mathematics · **Lesson:** Geometry in Space
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 91 of 136, in Stage 12 — Advanced Mathematics (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the synthetic geometry of lines and planes in space — how a plane is fixed, the positions lines and planes can take, and the parallelism and perpendicularity theorem systems, with skew lines, the three-perpendiculars theorem and the dihedral angle.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on plane geometry — parallel and perpendicular lines in a plane, and the angle between two lines. It uses no coordinates and no vectors in space; those are the next lesson's home.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: plane geometry of parallels and perpendiculars (every plane inside space obeys it — China's books state this as a basic fact), and angles (the angle between two lines in space is measured by moving to parallel lines). Recommended, never forced.
- A soft hint: if you know how a plane is determined in ordinary life — three legs of a stool, a door on its hinges — this lesson will be easier for you. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the basic facts that fix a plane — through three points not on one line there is exactly one plane, and its corollaries — the way China's B-edition book teaches them (Chapter 11, pp.91–94).
2. List the positions two lines, a line and a plane, and two planes can take in space, and recognise skew lines, the way Armenia's Geometry 10 teaches them (Chapter I, §§1–3, pp.5–33).
3. Use the parallelism criteria and properties — line to plane and plane to plane — the way Vietnam's Grade 11 books teach them (Volume 1, printed pp.78, 84, 88).
4. Use the perpendicularity criteria: a line perpendicular to a plane, tested on two intersecting lines in the plane, the way Kuwait's Grade 11 Term 2 book teaches it (Unit 10, 10-3, pp.130–136).
5. Apply the three-perpendiculars theorem, the way Kuwait's book teaches it inside the same lesson (Unit 10, pp.130–136) and Iraq's Grade 11 Science book teaches it (Chapter 7, pp.164–189).
6. Measure a dihedral angle by its plane angle, and recognise perpendicular planes, the way Greece's Euclidean Geometry Volume B teaches them (Chapter 12, pp.115–144) and Yemen's Grade 12 Science book defines the dihedral angle (Unit 5, p.147).
7. Find distances in space — point to plane, and the line–plane angle by projection — the way Vietnam's Grade 11 Volume 2 teaches them (printed pp.38 and 54).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A box or a book as a model room corner. Two pencils as lines in space. A sheet of card as a plane.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner holds two pencils in the air and tries every way two lines can sit in space, including the way that never happens in a plane (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the basic facts; positions in space and skew lines; parallelism; perpendicularity and the three-perpendiculars theorem; dihedral angles and perpendicular planes; distances and projection (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE at the basic-facts part: a picture of a plane held by three points not on one line, a line and a point off it, and two crossing lines — the student says which single plane each picture fixes.
   - ADD GIF at the skew-lines part: a picture of two lines in a box, one along the top front edge and one along a side edge, turning so the student can see they never meet and never lie flat together — the student says why they are not parallel and not intersecting.
   - ADD GIF at the three-perpendiculars part: a picture of a slanted line meeting a plane, its shadow-projection on the plane, and a line drawn in the plane at right angles to the shadow — the student watches the right angle pass to the slanted line itself.
   - ADD IMAGE at the dihedral part: a picture of an open book standing on a table, the two pages as half-planes and the plane angle opened between them — the student points to the edge, the two half-planes and the plane angle.
3. **Say it to your companion** — name a pair of edges of the room and answer your companion's questions about them in whole sentences (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — positions, parallels, perpendiculars, in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — classify every pair of edges of a box, prove a line parallel to a plane, and measure a dihedral angle (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Calls skew lines "parallel because they never meet" → not meeting is not enough: parallel lines must also lie in one plane. Skew lines never meet AND no plane holds both (China, B-edition, pp.97–99; Armenia, Geometry 10, §3, pp.27–33).
- Tests a line against only one line in the plane for perpendicularity → the criterion needs two intersecting lines in the plane; one line is not enough (Kuwait, Grade 11 Term 2, pp.130–136).
- Measures a dihedral angle with a slanted angle → the plane angle is taken in a plane at right angles to the edge, from a point on the edge, one ray in each face (Greece, Volume B, Chapter 12).
- Reaches for coordinates or vectors → this lesson is synthetic only: Kuwait's space geometry, for one, is filed as synthetic only, with no vectors or coordinates in space. Coordinates and vectors in space live in the next lesson, analytic geometry in space (Level 92).
- This lesson's next door: analytic geometry in space (Level 92) — the same lines and planes, now with coordinates, vectors and equations.
