# Lesson plan — De Moivre's Theorem

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 12 — Advanced Mathematics · **Lesson:** De Moivre's Theorem
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 94 of 136, in Unit 12 — Advanced Mathematics (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the trigonometric (polar) form of a complex number, multiplication and division in that form, De Moivre's theorem for powers, and the nth roots of a complex number.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the complex-numbers lesson — the learner can already work with a + bi, the modulus and the conjugate — and on trigonometric functions. It needs nothing else.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the complex-numbers lesson (modulus and argument are this lesson's raw materials), and trigonometric functions (the form is built from cos and sin). Recommended, never forced.
- A soft hint: if you know where a complex number sits on the Argand diagram, this course will be easier for you — you might try it first. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Write a complex number in trigonometric (polar) form z = r(cos θ + i sin θ) from its modulus and argument, the way South Sudan's Additional Mathematics Book 4 teaches polar form (Unit 6, printed pp.182–184), Laos's Grade 12 book teaches the trigonometric form (Chapter 4, Lesson 13, p.134) and Kuwait's Grade 11 Term 2 book teaches it (Unit 7, Lesson 7-2, printed pp.25–32).
2. Multiply and divide complex numbers in trigonometric form — moduli multiply and divide, arguments add and subtract — the way Kuwait's book teaches division in trig form (Lesson 7-2, printed pp.25–32).
3. Use De Moivre's theorem to raise a complex number to a power, the way South Sudan's book derives and uses it (Unit 6, printed pp.185–189) and Laos's book states it as De Moivre's formula (Lesson 13, p.134).
4. Use De Moivre's theorem to derive the multiple-angle identities cos 3θ = 4cos³θ − 3cosθ and sin 3θ = 3 sin θ − 4 sin³θ, taught corrected — the corrected sin 3θ identity is the one filed from South Sudan's guide (see sources).
5. Find all nth roots of a complex number and say why there are exactly n of them, spaced evenly around a circle, the way South Sudan's book teaches roots (Unit 6, printed pp.190–195) and Laos's book teaches nth roots (Lesson 13, p.134).
6. Give the cube roots of 8, taught corrected: 2, −1 + i√3, −1 − i√3 — South Sudan's Additional book prints them wrong on p.194; the corrected values are the ones filed and taught here.
7. Say plainly what Kuwait does NOT teach: Kuwait's book teaches the trig form and division in trig form but NO De Moivre and NO nth roots — never claim them for Kuwait.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A drawn Argand diagram with a unit circle. A protractor for the root spacing.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner plots 1 + i, measures its distance from zero and its angle, and rewrites it with those two numbers (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the trigonometric form; multiplication and division in trig form; De Moivre's theorem; the multiple-angle identities; nth roots; the cube roots of 8, taught corrected (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE at the trig-form part: a picture of the Argand diagram with the point 1 + i, its distance r marked along the arrow from zero and its angle θ opened from the real axis — the student names r and θ for the point shown.
   - ADD GIF at the De Moivre part: a picture of a point on the unit circle at 30° being multiplied by itself again and again, landing at 60°, 90°, 120° — the student watches the angle multiply while the distance stays 1.
   - ADD GIF at the roots part: a picture of the three cube roots of 8 landing one by one on a circle of radius 2, at 0°, 120° and 240° — the student checks the three landings are evenly spaced.
3. **Say it to your companion** — give your companion a complex number in a + bi form and answer their questions in whole sentences (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the trig form, De Moivre and roots in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — write trig forms, use De Moivre, and find cube and fourth roots (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Finds r but guesses θ → draw the point first: θ is measured from the positive real axis, and the quadrant fixes its sign and size; Kuwait's book insists a trig form needs r ≥ 0 and an argument in [0, 2π) (Lesson 7-2, pp.25–32).
- Adds the moduli when multiplying → moduli multiply, arguments add; for division, moduli divide, arguments subtract (Kuwait, Lesson 7-2).
- Finds only one nth root → there are always n roots: add 2π to the argument again and again, divide by n each time, until the roots repeat; the roots sit evenly around a circle (South Sudan, Additional Book 4, pp.190–195).
- Copies South Sudan's printed cube roots of 8 → the book prints them wrong on p.194: taught corrected here as 2, −1 + i√3, −1 − i√3. Cube any one of them to check: (−1 + i√3)³ = 8.
- Reaches for De Moivre in Kuwait's book → it is not there: Kuwait teaches the trig form and division in trig form only, NO De Moivre and NO nth roots, as filed.
- This lesson's next door: hyperbolic functions (Level 95) — a new family built from exponentials.
