**Acceptance count: taught in 16 countries (Egypt, Jordan, Greece, Armenia, Vietnam, Libya, Mauritania, Laos, Yemen, Indonesia, Ethiopia, South Sudan, Iraq, Cambodia, Kuwait, Pakistan).** Egypt carries integration from its first introduction to definite integrals with areas and volumes of revolution; Jordan carries the full methods course — substitution, partial fractions, parts — with areas and volumes; Greece carries indefinite and definite integrals with the F(x) area function and the Mean Value Theorem; Armenia carries the integral chapter in its Grade 12 guide (guide evidence only, the student book not held); Vietnam carries antiderivatives, integrals and their geometric applications; Libya carries areas, volumes of revolution and motion; Mauritania carries integral calculus in three streamed books. Indonesia carries integrals in Grade 12 Tingkat Lanjut — indefinite and definite integrals with Riemann sums and the fundamental theorem, applied to area, economics and physics — in the 2022 student book and now in the 2025 Revisi student book itself, which names substitution and parts as methods; volumes of revolution are not a named topic in the held TL books. Ethiopia carries introductory integration only, in Grade 12 — definite and indefinite integrals with Riemann summation and the area under a curve (the area under y = x² from 0 to 2 is 8/3, with lower and upper sums 2.28 and 3.08 for n = 10); there are no integration methods beyond the introduction and no differential equations. Iraq carries integrals in both Grade 12 tracks — the full integration chapter in the Scientific track (areas bounded by curves, upper and lower sums, the definition, the fundamental theorem, the definite and indefinite integral, the natural logarithm, areas and volumes of revolution), cited at opener/marker level only since its worked examples were not individually read, and the lighter integration course in the Literary track (indefinite and definite integrals with geometric and economic applications and areas under and between curves), read in full — including the verified examples ∫₄⁰ x(x−1)(x−2)dx = −16, correct because the limits are reversed, and the area between y = x and y = ∛x on [−1,1], 1/2 unit². Volumes of revolution are Scientific-only. Cambodia carries integrals whole — primitives, the definite integral as a limit of rectangle areas and by the fundamental theorem, substitution, integration by parts, partial fractions, trigonometric techniques, areas, volumes of revolution and arc length in Grade 12. Kuwait carries integrals whole in Grade 12 Term 2 (scientific track) — indefinite and definite integrals, substitution, trig and exponential/logarithmic integrals, integration by parts and partial fractions, and the applications (areas in the plane, volumes of solids of revolution, arc length).

**Armenia, worded plainly:** Armenia's Grade 12 student book teaches logic only — the integral chapter lives in the new-format semester-1 teacher's guide, whose student book is not held. The Armenian rows above are teacher-guide evidence, not pupil-book teaching, and the lesson says so. Laos carries integrals whole in Grade 10 — indefinite and definite integrals, the fundamental theorem, areas and volumes of revolution — with the book's ∫₁⁶4dx = 5 slip corrected to 20. Yemen carries integrals in Grade 12, in both tracks — basic indefinite and definite integrals with area applications in the literary track, and substitution, parts, rational functions, areas and volumes of revolution in the science track. South Sudan carries integrals only in part in Secondary 4 — integration as the reverse of differentiation with general curve equations and area under a curve in the regular Mathematics book, and area under curves, area by horizontal elements, integration by substitution, integrals of exponential, logarithmic and trigonometric functions and kinematics in Additional Mathematics — with two corrections: Mathematics pp. 95–96 area-by-integration examples are wrong (Example 2: the integrand is not the stated curve and the arithmetic is wrong — correct for the stated curve is ∫₁³(x²+x)dx = 38/3 ≈ 12.67; Example 3: the antiderivative has the wrong sign, "64/3" is copied as "64/8" and the final answer "−2/3" is impossible for an area — correct is ∫₂⁴(x²+x)dx = 74/3 ≈ 24.67), so the p. 94 example is cited instead (∫₁⁵(x²+2x)dx = 196/3, correct). And integration by parts is mentioned once (Additional Mathematics p. 103) but never taught anywhere in the set, so the lesson's integration-by-parts part rests on other countries.

Pakistan (Punjab) carries integrals in one place — Chapter 3 of the Class 12 mathematics book (eLearn.Punjab v1.1), from anti-differentiation and the standard formulae through substitution, parts and partial fractions to the fundamental theorem and areas under curves.
