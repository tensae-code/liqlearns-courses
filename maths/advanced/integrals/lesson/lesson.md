# Integrals — the lesson

Course: Maths · Unit 12 — Advanced Mathematics · Lesson 51 of 52
Built from: Egypt, Pure Mathematics Grades 11–12 — Jordan, Grades 11–12 — Greece, Grade 12 Analysis — Armenia, Grade 12 teacher's guide — Vietnam, Grade 12 Volume 2 — Libya, Grade 12 Science — Mauritania, 6AS-C, 7AS-C and 7AS-D.

## Do it first
Draw y = x² from x = 0 to x = 2 on squared paper. Count the squares under the curve — whole squares, and halves for the cut ones. You will get about two and two-thirds squares. Keep that number. By the end of this lesson you will find the exact area with an integral, and it will be 8/3 — the counting and the calculus will agree.

## Sub lesson 1 — Antiderivatives
**Antiderivatives.** An **antiderivative** of f is a function F whose derivative is f. **Integration** is the reverse of differentiation: it undoes what a derivative did. Egypt's Grade 11 book introduces integration as the reverse of differentiation, with indefinite integrals of basic forms; Jordan's Grade 11 book teaches indefinite integration, then the **initial condition** (one point the curve passes through, which fixes the constant); Armenia's Grade 12 guide teaches the antiderivative first, then the indefinite integral. The **indefinite integral** is written ∫f(x)dx = F(x) + C, where C is the **constant of integration** — because differentiating any constant gives zero, a whole family of functions shares one derivative, and the + C says so. The standard integrals the books carry: ∫xⁿ dx = xⁿ⁺¹/(n+1) + C, ∫eˣ dx = eˣ + C, ∫1/x dx = ln x + C, ∫sin x dx = −cos x + C, ∫cos x dx = sin x + C. Egypt's Grade 12 book integrates the exponential and logarithmic functions; Libya's book integrates the 1/x form and the exponential function, and integrals of sin and cos forms.

## Sub lesson 2 — Methods of Integration
**The methods.** Three methods carry the harder integrals. **Substitution**: when you can see the inner derivative sitting inside, substitute u for the inner function — Jordan's Grade 12 book teaches integration by substitution, and Egypt's Grade 12 book teaches methods of integration with a standard integrals table and substitution. **Partial fractions**: when the integrand is a fraction that splits into simpler fractions, split it first — Jordan's book teaches integration by partial fractions. **Parts**: ∫u dv = uv − ∫v du, for products where one part gets simpler when differentiated — Jordan's book teaches integration by parts. Greece's Grade 12 book teaches integration methods beside the indefinite integral, and Libya's book teaches the standard integrals with substitution in its Grade 12 calculus.

## Sub lesson 3 — Definite Integrals, Areas and Volumes
**The definite integral.** The **definite integral** ∫ₐᵇ f(x)dx is a number, not a family: it is F(b) − F(a), the antiderivative at the top bound minus the antiderivative at the bottom bound. Egypt's Grade 12 book teaches definite integration, and Jordan's Grade 11 book teaches definite integration after the initial condition. Armenia's guide teaches the definite integral with its main properties and the **Newton–Leibniz formula** — the name the books give to F(b) − F(a). Greece's book teaches the definite integral, and the function **F(x) = ∫f(t)dt from α to x** — an area that grows as x moves, whose derivative is f itself. That is the **fundamental link**: differentiation and integration undo each other, and the area function proves it.

**Areas and volumes.** The definite integral measures area: the area under a curve y = f(x) from a to b is ∫ₐᵇ f(x)dx, and the area between two curves is the integral of their difference. Egypt's Grade 12 book applies the definite integral to areas of plane regions and **volumes of solids of revolution** about both axes; Jordan's Grade 11 book teaches areas and volumes, and its Grade 12 book teaches areas and volumes of revolution; Vietnam's Grade 12 book teaches the geometric applications of integrals — areas and volumes; Libya's book teaches the area between a curve and the x-axis, the area between two curves, the volume of a solid of revolution, and motion in a straight line; Armenia's guide applies the integral to the volume of a solid of revolution, rectilinear motion, and the work of a variable force. Greece's book teaches the area of a plane region, with the Mean Value Theorem of integral calculus beside it.

> **Tip — from us, not from the books:** a definite integral is a subtraction in disguise: F(top) minus F(bottom). If your answer for an area comes out negative, you have the bounds the wrong way round, or the curve sits below the axis — the area is the size, and sizes are never negative.

## Say it to your companion
Give your companion a derivative — say 2x. Ask them for the function it came from (x² + C), and for the area under it from 0 to 2 (8/3). Then swap: they give you a derivative — say cos x — and you answer (sin x + C, and the area from 0 to π/2 is 1). Check each other's + C every time.

## Do It Yourself
1. Integrate: 3x², eˣ, 1/x, sin x, cos x. Write the + C every time.
2. Find ∫₀² x² dx. (Answer: 8/3.) Find ∫₀¹ eˣ dx. (Answer: e − 1.)
3. Use substitution: ∫ 2x(x² + 1)³ dx = (x² + 1)⁴/4 + C.
4. Find the area between y = x² and y = x from 0 to 1. (Answer: 1/6.)
5. Find the volume when y = x from 0 to 1 is spun round the x-axis. (Answer: π/3.)
