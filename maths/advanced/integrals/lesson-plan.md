## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the derivatives lesson (an integral undoes a derivative) and the logarithms lesson (some integrals are logs). Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what an antiderivative is, and write the indefinite integral with its constant.
2. Use the standard integrals and the methods: substitution, partial fractions and parts.
3. Find a definite integral, and use the Newton–Leibniz formula.
4. Find the area under a curve, between two curves, and the volume of a solid of revolution.
5. Use the function F(x) = ∫f(t)dt, and say how integration links to differentiation.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A curve drawn on squared paper, to count squares under it.

## If the learner is stuck
- Forgets the + C → an indefinite integral is a family of functions, all the same up to a constant: the + C is part of the answer.
- Mixes the bounds in a definite integral → the definite integral is F(upper) − F(lower): upper first, lower taken away.
- Picks the wrong method → substitution when you can see the inner derivative; parts when you see a product; partial fractions when you see a fraction that splits.
- This lesson's next door: differential equations (same unit) — the last lesson uses integrals to solve equations that hold a derivative.
