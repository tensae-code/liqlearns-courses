# Hands-on — Integrals

## Squares under a curve
Draw y = x² from x = 0 to x = 2 on squared paper. Count the squares under the curve to guess the area (about 2 and two-thirds). Now integrate: ∫₀² x² dx = 8/3. The counting and the calculus agree.

## Undo race
Take the derivatives 2x, 3x², cos x, eˣ and 1/x. Undo each one: x² + C, x³ + C, sin x + C, eˣ + C, ln x + C. Swap with a partner and check each other's + C — no + C, no mark.

## Substitution by sight
Integrate ∫ 2x·(x² + 1)³ dx. Can you see the inner derivative? The derivative of x² + 1 is 2x, sitting right there. Substitute u = x² + 1 and the integral falls open: (x² + 1)⁴/4 + C.

## Volume of revolution
Take the line y = x from 0 to 1 and spin it round the x-axis: it makes a cone. The books' formula gives volume π∫₀¹ x² dx = π/3. Make the cone from paper and see the shape the integral measured.
