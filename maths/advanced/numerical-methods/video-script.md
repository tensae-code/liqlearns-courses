# Video script — Numerical Methods

Super-simple voice. The tutor talks; the curve, the bracket and the tangents change as they are named.

---

Some equations have no formula. X-squared minus five equals zero has one — but most real equations do not. So we hunt the answer instead.

First, trap it. The function is negative here, positive there. Somewhere between, it crossed zero. That crossing is a root, and now it cannot escape the bracket. Shrink the bracket with a table, or read the crossing off a graph.

Between two known points, a straight line gives a quick estimate. Inside the points, that is interpolation. Outside them, extrapolation — useful, and less safe.

Then close in. Newton–Raphson: stand on the curve, slide down the tangent to the axis, stand there, repeat. Two or three slides and you are on top of the root.

Or rearrange. Turn the equation into x equals g of x, and feed the answer back in, again and again. Choose the rearrangement well and the values settle on the root. Choose badly and they run away — same equation, different fate.

Trap it, then close in. That is numerical methods.
