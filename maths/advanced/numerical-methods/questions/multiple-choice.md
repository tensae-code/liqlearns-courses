1. (Sub lesson 1) f is continuous, f(1) = −3, f(4) = 9. You can conclude:
   A. There is no root in (1, 4) B. There is a root in (1, 4) C. The root is exactly 2 D. f is increasing
   **Answer: B** — a sign change on a continuous function forces a crossing. Rwanda, Advanced Mathematics, Learner's Book, Senior Five, Unit 4, printed pp. 111–115.

2. (Sub lesson 2) One Newton–Raphson step for x² − 5 = 0 from x₀ = 2 gives:
   A. 2.2 B. 2.5 C. 2.25 D. 2.125
   **Answer: C** — 2 − (−1)/4 = 2.25. Rwanda, Advanced Mathematics, Learner's Book, Senior Five, Unit 4, printed pp. 118–122.

3. (Sub lesson 2) Newton–Raphson is most likely to fail when the starting guess is:
   A. Very close to the root B. Where f′(x) is close to 0 C. An integer D. Inside a sign-change bracket
   **Answer: B** — the step divides by f′(x). Rwanda, Advanced Mathematics, Learner's Book, Senior Five, Unit 4, printed pp. 118–122.

4. (Sub lesson 3) Iterating x = 5/x for x² − 5 = 0 from x₀ = 2 gives 2, 2.5, 2, 2.5, … This shows that:
   A. The equation has no root B. √5 = 2.5 C. This rearrangement does not converge, though another may D. Iteration never works
   **Answer: C** — convergence is a property of the rearrangement; x = (x + 5/x)/2 converges to √5. Rwanda, Advanced Mathematics, Learner's Book, Senior Five, Unit 4, printed pp. 122–126.
