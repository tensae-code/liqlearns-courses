# Video script — Vector Spaces

Super-simple voice. The tutor talks; the planes, lines and discs change as they are named.

---

Take all of three-dimensional space. Now take a plane through the origin. Add two vectors in the plane — you stay in the plane. Stretch one — you stay in the plane. A set that never lets you escape like that is a subspace. That is the whole test: closed under adding, closed under scaling.

Two subspaces meet. Their overlap is always a subspace — solve both sets of conditions together and the overlap appears. Their union is not: two lines through the origin make a cross, and adding one vector from each line jumps off the cross — unless one line already sits inside the other. That is the guides' criterion.

Join them instead. The sum is every vector from the first plus every vector from the second — the smallest subspace holding both. Count its size with Grassmann: dimension of the first, plus dimension of the second, minus the overlap, counted twice if you forget.

If the overlap is only the zero vector, the sum is direct — every vector in it splits one way only, no other.

And inside any space: mix vectors with scalars and you get their combinations; all possible mixes are the span. Vectors none of which is a mix of the others are independent. Independent and spanning — that is a basis. Count it: that count is the dimension. Measure any vector against the basis: those measures are its coordinates.

Closed, met, summed, counted, based. That is vector spaces.
