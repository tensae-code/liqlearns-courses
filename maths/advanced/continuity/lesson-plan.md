# Lesson plan — Continuity

**Course:** Maths · **Stage:** Stage 12 — Advanced Mathematics · **Lesson:** Continuity
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 88 of 136, Stage 12 — Advanced Mathematics (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** continuity — when a function's graph can be drawn without lifting the pen: the three conditions at a point, removable and jump discontinuities, and continuity on an interval.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Limits (Level 87) — the learner can already find one-sided and two-sided limits. It needs nothing else.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Limits (Level 87, same stage) — continuity is a limit meeting the function's own value. Recommended, never forced.
- Recommended first: the functions lesson (you need f(a) itself, not just values near a). Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Test the three conditions for continuity at a point — f(a) exists, the limit exists, and the two are equal — the way Laos's Grade 10 book states them (Ch.VI L16, pp.166–175).
2. Find and name a removable discontinuity and repair it by redefining one point, as Pakistan's Class 11–12 books teach (Unit 12, pp.218–236; Ch.1, ch-pp.32–35).
3. Find and name a jump discontinuity from the one-sided limits, as Pakistan's books teach (Ch.1, ch-pp.32–35).
4. Decide continuity on an interval, open and closed, as Kuwait's Grade 12 book teaches in its closing continuity lesson (Unit 1, 1-7, pp.61–71).
5. Use the continuity theorems — sums, products and quotients of continuous functions are continuous — as Kuwait's book states them (1-6, pp.54–60).
6. Use the Intermediate Value Theorem to show an equation has a root in an interval, as Cambodia's PRIVATE Limits and Continuity booklet states it (pp.20–22).
7. Check continuity of the elementary functions and of trigonometric functions, as Armenia's Grade 11 book (Ch.4/5, §§1–2) and Yemen's Grade 12 Science book (6-1, contents/PDF p.154) teach.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A pen you are not allowed to lift for the drawing test.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner draws three graphs without lifting the pen: one unbroken, one with a hole, one with a jump, and says which are "in one piece" (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the three conditions; removable discontinuities; jump discontinuities; continuity on an interval; the continuity theorems and the Intermediate Value Theorem (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the three-conditions part: a picture of a pencil drawing a curve towards a point, stopping at a hole, the point itself drawn one step higher — the student says which condition failed.
   - ADD IMAGE at the removable part: a picture of the graph of (x² − 1)/(x − 1) with an empty circle at x = 1 and the filled point shown being dropped into the hole → the student says the single value that repairs it.
   - ADD IMAGE at the jump part: a picture of a step graph, the left piece ending low and the right piece starting high at the same x → the student reads both one-sided limits and names the break.
   - ADD GIF at the interval part: a picture of a pen tracing a whole curve from a to b, crossing a marked height line on the way — the student says why every height between the endpoints must be crossed.
3. **Say it to your companion** — give your companion a broken graph; they must name the break and the failed condition (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — continuity in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — test five functions for continuity the books' way (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Checks only the limit → continuity needs three checks, not one: the value exists, the limit exists, and they are equal (Laos, Grade 10, pp.166–175).
- Calls every break a jump → compare the sides: if both one-sided limits agree, the break is a hole — removable; if they disagree, it is a jump and no single point can repair it.
- Thinks continuity on an interval means checking the endpoints only → every interior point needs the two-sided test; the endpoints take the matching one-sided test, as Kuwait's book sets it out (pp.61–71).
- Nepal's worked warning: for f(x) = (x² + x − 6)/(x + 3), the limit as x → −3 is −5 but f(−3) is undefined, so the function is discontinuous there (Nepal, Grade 10 Optional, Lesson 12, pp.275–293).
- This lesson's next door: Rolle's theorem and the Mean Value Theorem (Level 89, same stage) — continuity is one of their two entry conditions.
