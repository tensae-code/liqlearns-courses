# Hands-on — Continuity

The lesson tested continuity with the pen test and the three conditions.

## The no-lift challenge
Draw four graphs, pen never leaving the paper until each is done: a straight line; the curve y = x²; a curve with a hole at x = 1 (draw the empty circle); a step at x = 2. Mark each one "one piece" or "broken", and for each broken one write which of the three conditions failed (Laos, Grade 10, pp.166–175).

## Repair the hole
Take f(x) = (x² − 1)/(x − 1) for x ≠ 1. Compute its limit at 1, then write the repaired definition: the same formula for x ≠ 1, and the single value at x = 1 that makes it continuous. Check all three conditions for your repaired function.

## Nepal's broken point
Work f(x) = (x² + x − 6)/(x + 3) at x = −3: factorise the top, find the limit, then try to find f(−3). Write the verdict and the failed condition, the way Nepal's Grade 10 Optional book works it (Lesson 12, pp.275–293).

## The crossing hunt
Draw any unbroken curve from (0, −2) to (3, 5). Draw the horizontal line y = 1. Your curve must cross it — mark the crossing. This is the Intermediate Value Theorem with a pencil: continuous on the interval, so every height between −2 and 5 is taken (Cambodia, PRIVATE Limits and Continuity booklet, pp.20–22).
