# Hands-on — Conic Sections

## Cut the cone
Take a cone-shaped cup or a paper cone. Imagine — or draw — three flat cuts: level, slanted, and steep enough to run along the side. Sketch the edge each cut leaves. Name them: circle, ellipse, parabola. For the hyperbola you need a double cone: two cones point to point, cut steeply through both.

## The string ellipse
Push two pins into a board — these are the foci F₁ and F₂. Loop a piece of string around both pins, longer than the distance between them. Put a pencil inside the loop, pull the string taut, and move the pencil, keeping it taut all the way round. You draw an ellipse, Iran's Geometry (3) way (pp.47–48). At any point of your curve, measure PF₁ + PF₂: it is the string's length, the constant 2a, every time.

## Balance on the parabola
Draw a line (the directrix) and a point F not on it (the focus). Pick points on a sheet and measure: distance to F, distance to the line. Keep only the points where the two distances are equal. Join them: a parabola — the e = 1 curve of Laos's Grade 10 book (pp.278–282).

## Chase the asymptotes
On squared paper draw the hyperbola x²/16 − y²/9 = 1 from its vertices (±4, 0). Draw the dashed lines y = (3/4)x and y = −(3/4)x. Plot points of the curve further and further out: the branches come closer and closer to the dashed lines and never touch them.
