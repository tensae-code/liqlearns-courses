# Conic Sections — the lesson

Course: Maths · Stage: Stage 12 — Advanced Mathematics · Lesson: Conic Sections (Level 90 of 136)
Built from: Greece, Grade 11 orientation book (W7) — Iraq, Grade 11 Science and Grade 12 Science — Pakistan, Class 12 — Laos, Grade 10 — Kuwait, Grade 12 Term 2 — Yemen, Grade 12 Science — Indonesia, Grade 12 Tingkat Lanjut (2022) and Tingkat Lanjut Revisi (2025) — Vietnam, Grade 10 Volume 2 and Grade 10 study book — Iran, Geometry (3) and Mathematics (3) — Cambodia, Grade 12 Basic — Nicaragua, Grade 11 — Mauritania, 7AS-C — Nepal, Grade 10 Optional (introduction only)

## Do it first
Take a double cone — two cones joined at their points. Slice it with one flat cut, four ways. Level: the edge is a circle. Tilted a little: the edge is a closed, stretched circle. Tilted until the cut runs parallel to the cone's side: the edge is one open curve that never closes. Tilted steeply through both halves: the edge is two open curves. Before you read any definition: guess which of these four curves a planet's path, a torch beam's edge and a cooling tower's side could be. Keep your guesses; the lesson will sort them.

## Then the explanation
**Cutting the cone.** A **conic section** is the curve a plane cuts from a (double) cone. Greece's Grade 11 orientation book teaches the full conic course in one chapter — circle, parabola, ellipse, hyperbola, and then classification from the general equation (Chapter 3, printed pp.79–134, introduction pp.79–80). Pakistan's Class 12 book opens the same way, conics from cutting a double cone (Chapter 6, ch-p.2). Iraq's Grade 11 Science book introduces conics by slicing a double cone, with figures of all four curves (p.41), then teaches the circle in full (Chapter 3, pp.39–53); the other three conics wait for Grade 12 there. Nepal's Grade 10 Optional book only introduces the parts of a cone (§8.3, within pp.139–174) — an introduction only, with no conic equations worked out; the equations in this lesson come from the other countries.

**One definition for all three.** Each conic is a **locus**: the path of a point P that moves under one rule. The rule uses a fixed point F, the **focus**, a fixed line, the **directrix**, and a fixed number e, the **eccentricity**:

PF ÷ (distance from P to the directrix) = e, at every point of the curve.

Laos's Grade 10 book builds the whole topic on exactly this: MF/MH = e, where H is the foot of the perpendicular from M to the directrix (Chapter XI, Lesson 24, pp.278–282). Then e sorts the family: **e = 1 gives the parabola, 0 < e < 1 gives the ellipse, e > 1 gives the hyperbola.** Iraq's Grade 12 Science book defines the conic the same way, as a locus with ratio e (Chapter 2, 2-1, p.51), and Pakistan's Class 12 book gives the focus–directrix definition with eccentricity at ch-p.26. Kuwait's Grade 12 Term 2 book gives eccentricity a lesson of its own (Unit 7, 7-4, pp.126–133), after one lesson each on the parabola, ellipse and hyperbola (pp.100–125). Mauritania's 7AS-C stream carries conics as a baccalaureate chapter (Chapter 14, printed p.169).

**The parabola.** The parabola is the e = 1 case: every point is as far from the focus as from the directrix. With vertex at the origin, focus (c, 0) and directrix x = −c, its equation is **y² = 4cx** (Laos, Grade 10, Lesson 25, p.283). The line through the focus at right angles to the axis cuts the curve in the **latus rectum**, of length 4c. Iraq's Grade 12 book works the definition both ways: focus (3, 0), vertex at the origin gives y² = 12x (p.53 onwards), and a directrix y = 7 gives x² = −28y (p.59). Iran's Geometry (3) derives y² = 4ax from the locus, tabulates the four orientations, and works translated parabolas and the latus-rectum endpoints (pp.50–56). Nicaragua's Grade 11 book writes it as y² = 4px from focus and directrix, with the y-axis form beside it (pp.98–103). Vietnam's Grade 10 study book gives the parabola its own lesson (Bài 7, printed p.54), after the ellipse and hyperbola lessons, and Cambodia's Grade 12 Basic book defines the parabola as the locus of points equidistant from focus F and directrix Δ, with its equation and conversion to standard form (Chapter 6, 6.1, pp.178–191). One corrected value from Pakistan's book: a translated parabola worked there has focus **(−1/26, 3/52)** — the book prints (1/26, 3/52); the minus sign belongs there, and this lesson teaches it corrected (Chapter 6, ch-p.75).

**The ellipse.** The ellipse is the locus where the distances to two foci **add** to a constant: PF₁ + PF₂ = 2a. Iran's Geometry (3) builds it with a loop of string around two pins — the string construction — proves b² + c² = a² from it, and defines eccentricity as c/a (pp.47–49); its ceiling, filed as the book has it, is that no Cartesian ellipse equation is stated there. The equation comes from the other books: with centre at the origin and foci (±c, 0), **x²/a² + y²/b² = 1 with a² = b² + c²** (Laos, Grade 10, Lesson 27, p.299). Here a is the semi-major axis, b the semi-minor axis, the vertices are (±a, 0) and (0, ±b), and **e = c/a < 1**. Indonesia's Tingkat Lanjut Revisi book teaches the ellipse in full — elements first (foci, centre, axes 2a and 2b, vertices, eccentricity, directrices, latus rectum, pp.18–20), then the equation for centre O(0,0) and for centre P(m,n), horizontal and vertical (pp.20–37). Nicaragua's Grade 11 book proves the same equation from PF₁ + PF₂ = 2a (pp.104–109), and Vietnam's study book opens its conic topic with the ellipse (Bài 5, printed p.39). Worked check: x²/25 + y²/9 = 1 has a = 5, b = 3, c = 4, foci (±4, 0), e = 4/5, latus rectum 2b²/a = 18/5. One corrected value from Pakistan's book: an ellipse latus rectum worked there is **8/3**, not the printed 4/3 (Chapter 6, ch-p.42) — taught corrected here. And one from Cambodia's book: for x²/100 + y²/25 = 1 the bottom vertex is **(0, −5)**, not (−5, 0) as an answers graph prints it (answers, printed p.252) — taught corrected here; Cambodia's ellipse part itself is filed from the chapter contents, the teacher guide's lesson (guide printed p.200) and the answers, its section start not read in detail, and it is worded that way in the sources.

**The hyperbola.** The hyperbola is the locus where the distances to two foci **differ** by a constant: |PF₁ − PF₂| = 2a. Its equation is **x²/a² − y²/b² = 1 with c² = a² + b²**, foci (±c, 0), vertices (±a, 0), e = c/a > 1, and two **asymptotes**, the lines y = ±(b/a)x that the branches approach and never touch (Laos, Grade 10, Lesson 28, p.308). Nicaragua's Grade 11 book proves the equation by squaring twice and setting c² − a² = b², asymptotes included (pp.110–117). Yemen's Grade 12 Science book teaches all three conics as loci with their equations — parabola p.105, ellipse p.110 defined by the constant sum, hyperbola p.118 (Unit 4, pp.104–129). Vietnam's study book gives the hyperbola its own lesson (Bài 6, printed p.47). Worked check: x²/16 − y²/9 = 1 has a = 4, b = 3, c = 5, foci (±5, 0), e = 5/4, asymptotes y = ±(3/4)x. One corrected set from Pakistan's book: a hyperbola worked there has **c² = 41, foci (±√41, 0) and e = √41/4** — the book prints c² = 34, foci (±√34, 0), e = √34/4 (Chapter 6, ch-pp.49–50); this lesson teaches the corrected values. Two honest limits, kept as filed: Indonesia's Revisi book does **not** teach hyperbola equations — the hyperbola appears there in the enrichment pages by definition, elements and asymptotes only, its equation left to an external link (p.77) — so no hyperbola equation in this lesson is cited to Revisi; the equations come from Laos, Nicaragua, Yemen, Iraq, Pakistan, Greece and the 2022 Tingkat Lanjut book (Bab 1, pp.1–64), which does teach the hyperbola as a full conic. And Iran's Geometry (3) does **not** teach the hyperbola at all — explicitly not taught there — so Iran is cited in this lesson for the ellipse and parabola only.

**Moving and turning the axes.** A conic does not change its shape when the axes move. **Translation** shifts the centre or vertex to (h, k): x is replaced by (x − h), y by (y − k) — Laos's Grade 10 book gives the parabola's translated form (y − k)² = 4c(x − h) (p.283), Iraq's Grade 12 book gives translation lessons for each conic in turn (2-3 p.60, 2-5 p.75, 2-7), and Nicaragua's book is the contrast case: every conic there is centred at the origin, with no translated conics taught. **Rotation** of the axes is Yemen's Grade 12 tool for simplifying a conic equation that carries an xy term (Unit 4, 4-5 translation p.124, 4-6 rotation p.129); Pakistan's book teaches translation and rotation of axes in the same run (ch-pp.63–67).

**Tangents and the general equation.** Pakistan's Class 12 book finds tangents and normals to all three conics (ch-pp.52–62), and Indonesia's 2022 Tingkat Lanjut book teaches tangent lines to conics inside its analytic-geometry chapter (Bab 1, pp.1–64). Every conic also hides inside one general second-degree equation Ax² + Bxy + Cy² + Dx + Ey + F = 0: Greece's chapter ends by classifying conics from the general equation Ax² + By² + Γx + Δy + E = 0 (pp.79–134), Pakistan's book classifies by h² − ab and treats degenerate conics — a conic fallen apart into lines or a point (ch-pp.68–78). Vietnam closes the topic by unifying the three conics in one lesson (study book, Bài 8, printed p.57; the three are first met together in Volume 2, Bài 22, printed p.48).

> **Tip — from us, not from the books:** read a conic off its equation in this order: sign first (plus between the squares is an ellipse, minus is a hyperbola, one square only is a parabola), then the denominators give a and b, then the right c-relation for that curve gives the foci and e. Sign, sizes, foci — three steps, every time.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: "A point moves so that its distance to a fixed point divided by its distance to a fixed line is always e." They ask: "What is e?" Answer in whole sentences for all three cases: "If e is 1, the curve is a parabola. If e is less than 1, the curve is an ellipse. If e is more than 1, the curve is a hyperbola." Then swap: they give you an equation, and you say the curve, its foci and its eccentricity, whole.

## Do It Yourself
1. For y² = 12x: write the focus, the directrix and the latus rectum. (Focus (3, 0), directrix x = −3, latus rectum 12 — the way Iraq's Grade 12 book works it, p.53.)
2. For x²/25 + y²/9 = 1: find c, the foci and e. (c = 4, foci (±4, 0), e = 4/5.)
3. For x²/16 − y²/9 = 1: find c, the foci, e and the asymptotes. (c = 5, foci (±5, 0), e = 5/4, y = ±(3/4)x.)
4. Translate the ellipse of task 2 so its centre moves to (2, −1), and write its new equation. ((x − 2)²/25 + (y + 1)²/9 = 1.)
5. Say, in one sentence each, why Indonesia's Revisi book cannot be your source for a hyperbola's equation, and why Iran's Geometry (3) cannot be your source for the hyperbola at all.
