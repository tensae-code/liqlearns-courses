# Lesson plan — Conic Sections

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 12 — Advanced Mathematics · **Lesson:** Conic Sections
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 90 of 136, in Unit 12 — Advanced Mathematics (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the three conic sections — parabola, ellipse and hyperbola — as loci defined by a focus and a directrix with eccentricity e, with their standard and translated equations, elements and asymptotes.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on coordinate geometry (the distance formula, the equation of a line and of a circle) and on loci. It needs nothing from calculus.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: coordinate geometry and the circle's equation (a conic is a locus written in coordinates), and loci (each conic is first a moving-point rule, then an equation). Recommended, never forced.
- A soft hint: if you know the distance formula and how to complete the square, this lesson will be easier for you — you might try those first. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the curve a plane cut of a double cone gives, the way Greece's Grade 11 orientation book opens its conic chapter (Chapter 3, printed pp.79–80) and Pakistan's Class 12 book opens Chapter 6 (ch-p.2).
2. State the one focus–directrix definition MF/MH = e and sort the three curves by e, the way Laos's Grade 10 book teaches it (Chapter XI, Lesson 24, pp.278–282).
3. Write and use the parabola y² = 4cx and its translated form, read its focus, directrix and latus rectum, the way Laos's Grade 10 book teaches it (Lesson 25, p.283).
4. Write and use the ellipse x²/a² + y²/b² = 1 with a² = b² + c² and e = c/a, the way Laos's Grade 10 book teaches it (Lesson 27, p.299) and Iran's Geometry (3) builds it by the string construction (pp.47–49).
5. Write and use the hyperbola x²/a² − y²/b² = 1 with c² = a² + b² and asymptotes y = ±(b/a)x, the way Laos's Grade 10 book teaches it (Lesson 28, p.308) — noting that Indonesia's Revisi book teaches the hyperbola's definition and elements only, never its equation.
6. Move a conic by translation of axes and simplify a conic by rotation of axes, the way Yemen's Grade 12 Science book teaches it (Unit 4, pp.124 and 129).
7. Use the corrected values where a book printed a slip: an ellipse latus rectum of 8/3, a hyperbola with c² = 41, foci (±√41, 0) and e = √41/4, and a parabola focus of (−1/26, 3/52) (Pakistan, Class 12, Chapter 6, ch-pp.42, 49–50 and 75, taught corrected).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A torch or a cone-shaped cup and a knife-cut picture for the cone slices. String and two pins for the ellipse. A ruler for the directrix work.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner cuts a cone in imagination three ways — level, slanted, steep — and guesses each curve's name before any definition is given (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — cone-cutting; the one locus definition with e; the parabola; the ellipse; the hyperbola; moving and turning the axes; tangents, the general second-degree equation and classification (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the cone-cutting part: a picture of a plane slicing a double cone, tilting slowly so the cut curve changes from circle to ellipse to parabola to hyperbola — the student names each curve as it appears.
   - ADD IMAGE at the focus–directrix part: a picture of a point F, a line, and a moving point P with the two distances PF and PH drawn and their ratio marked — the student says which curve e < 1, e = 1 and e > 1 each give.
   - ADD GIF at the ellipse part: a picture of a loop of string around two pins, a pencil keeping the string taut and drawing the ellipse — the student watches the sum of the two distances stay constant.
   - ADD IMAGE at the hyperbola part: a picture of a hyperbola with its two asymptotes drawn dashed, the branches approaching them — the student writes the asymptotes' equations from a and b.
3. **Say it to your companion** — give your companion a focus, a directrix and a value of e, and answer their questions in whole sentences (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — one definition, three curves, in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — find the elements of one parabola, one ellipse and one hyperbola, and translate one conic (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes the ellipse and hyperbola relations → for the ellipse c is inside: a² = b² + c² with a the biggest; for the hyperbola c is outside: c² = a² + b² with c the biggest. The sign in the equation tells you which: plus is ellipse, minus is hyperbola.
- Forgets what e does → go back to MF/MH = e: e = 1 balances on the parabola, e < 1 pulls in to an ellipse, e > 1 pushes out to a hyperbola (Laos, Grade 10, pp.278–282).
- Reads a translated conic as a new curve → translation only moves the centre or vertex: replace x by (x − h) and y by (y − k); the shape, a, b and c do not change (Yemen, Grade 12 Science, p.124).
- Copies a book's printed slip → the corrected values in this lesson are the taught values: latus rectum 8/3, c² = 41 with e = √41/4, focus (−1/26, 3/52), and the vertex (0, −5). A printed answer that disagrees is the slip, not the rule.
