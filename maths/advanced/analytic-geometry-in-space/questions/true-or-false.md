# Analytic Geometry in Space — true or false

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Coordinates, Octants and Distance in Space · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Vectors in Space and Their Products · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Equations of Lines and Planes in Space.

Say true or false, and why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Two coordinates are enough to fix a point in space.
**Answer:** False — space needs three coordinates; two leave the depth unsettled (Laos, Grade 10, Lesson 23, p.253).

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** The XY, XZ and YZ planes are called the coordinate planes.
**Answer:** True — they are the three planes through the origin holding two axes each (Egypt, Pure Grade 12, Lesson 3-1, p.48).

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** If u · v = 0, the vectors u and v are perpendicular.
**Answer:** True — the dot product is zero exactly at a right angle (Laos, Grade 10, Lesson 23).

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** The cross product of two vectors is a single number.
**Answer:** False — the cross product is a vector; the mixed product is the single number (Egypt, Pure Grade 12, Lesson 3-3, p.63).

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** One point alone fixes a plane in space.
**Answer:** False — a point and a normal fix a plane; through one point pass infinitely many planes (Egypt, Pure Grade 12, Lesson 4-2, p.90).

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** Cambodia's guide value 3x+2y+4z−42 = 0 is the correct plane through (5,3,6) with normal (3,2,4).
**Answer:** False — taught corrected: 3·5 + 2·3 + 4·6 = 45, so the plane is 3x+2y+4z−45 = 0 (Cambodia, Grade 11 Advanced teacher guide, p.236).
