# Video script — Analytic Geometry in Space

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the
lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are
made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Flat paper has two ways to go: across, and up. Space has THREE.

Across. Deep. And up. Three lines, meeting at one corner. That corner is zero.

Every point in space gets THREE numbers. How far across. How deep. How high. Three numbers — and you have found the point!

The three flat walls through zero cut space into EIGHT parts. Eight octants. Like eight corners of eight rooms, all meeting at zero.

How far is one point from another? Use the right-angle rule TWICE. Once flat, on the floor. Once standing up. Two steps — and you have the distance in space!

A vector in space is an arrow with three parts: across, deep, up. Its length comes from the same right-angle rule. And it makes THREE angles, one with each axis direction. The cosines of those three angles are its direction cosines — they always square and add to ONE.

Two vectors? Multiply them two ways. The dot way gives a NUMBER — zero means they stand at a right angle. The cross way gives a new ARROW, standing square to both.

A line in space: one point, one direction — and the line is fixed. A plane in space: one point, one arrow standing straight out of it — and the plane is fixed.

Now you. Point at a corner of your room. Give it three numbers. That is analytic geometry in space!
