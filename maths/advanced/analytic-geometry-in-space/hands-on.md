# Hands-on — Analytic Geometry in Space

## The box corner
Take a box and call one bottom corner the origin. Run your finger along the three edges that meet there: those are the X, Y and Z axes. Pick a point inside the box and measure its three distances from the three faces through the origin — those distances are its coordinates (x, y, z), the way Laos's Grade 10 book opens the topic on a cuboid (Lesson 23, p.253).

## Pythagoras twice
Put two points at different heights in the box. First measure the flat distance between the points below them on the floor — Pythagoras once. Then stand a ruler up from the lower point to the higher point's height — Pythagoras again, standing on the flat length. Check your two-step answer against the straight ruler distance between the points.

## Direction cosines with a stick
Hold a stick slanting in the box corner. Its across, deep and up parts are its three components. Measure the stick's length, divide each part by the length, and check: the three quotients squared add to 1. Those quotients are the direction cosines (Laos, Grade 10, Lesson 23).

## A plane from a point and an arrow
Hold a sheet of card as a plane. Push a pencil straight through it: the pencil is the normal, the hole is the point. The sheet cannot turn any more — one point and one normal fix a plane, the fact behind Egypt's plane equation (Pure Grade 12, Lesson 4-2, p.90). Tilt the pencil and watch the sheet follow it.
