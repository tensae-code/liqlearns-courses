# Lesson plan — Analytic Geometry in Space

**Course:** Maths · **Stage:** Stage 12 — Advanced Mathematics · **Lesson:** Analytic Geometry in Space
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 92 of 136, in Stage 12 — Advanced Mathematics (maths/CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** coordinates and vectors in space — the 3-D coordinate system and its octants, distance in space, direction cosines, the dot, cross and mixed products, and the equations of lines and planes in space.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on plane vectors and coordinates, and on the synthetic geometry of lines and planes in space (Level 91) — the learner can already work with vectors in a plane and knows what a line and a plane in space are. It needs nothing else.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: vectors in the plane (a vector in space is the same idea with one more component), and geometry in space, Level 91 (the synthetic positions of lines and planes that this lesson now measures). Recommended, never forced.
- A soft hint: if you know coordinates in the plane and the distance formula there, this course will be easier for you — you might try it first. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the axes, the coordinate planes and the eight octants, and give the coordinates of a point in space, the way Laos's Grade 10 book opens the topic on a cuboid (Chapter X, Lesson 23, printed p.253).
2. Find the distance between two points in space by using Pythagoras twice, the way Laos's Grade 10 book derives it (Lesson 23, printed pp.253–277).
3. Write a vector in space with the basis i, j, k, find its magnitude and its unit vector, and find its direction cosines, the way Laos's Grade 10 book teaches them (Lesson 23, printed pp.253–277) and Egypt's Pure Grade 12 book teaches vectors in space (Unit Three, Lesson 3-2, p.54).
4. Use the dot product in space to test perpendicularity and find the angle between two vectors, the way Laos's Grade 10 book does in its right-triangle proof (Lesson 23, printed pp.253–277).
5. Use the cross product and the mixed product, the way Egypt's Pure Grade 12 book teaches vector multiplication (Unit Three, Lesson 3-3, p.63) and Cambodia's Grade 12 Advanced book verifies a cross product (Chapter 7, §7.1, p.174).
6. Write the equation of a line in space in parametric and symmetric form, the way Egypt's Pure Grade 12 book teaches it (Unit Four, Lesson 4-1, p.80), Laos's Grade 12 book teaches it (Chapter 10, Lesson 28, p.270) and Jordan's Grade 12 Term 2 book teaches lines in space (Unit 6, Lesson 2, p.124).
7. Write the equation of a plane from a point and a normal vector, the way Egypt's Pure Grade 12 book teaches it (Unit Four, Lesson 4-2, p.90) and Cambodia's Grade 11 Advanced book works it (Chapter 8, Lesson 2, printed pp.216–234 — the corrected values, see sources).
8. Find the angle between a line and a plane, the way Laos's Grade 12 book teaches it (Chapter 10, Lesson 29, p.278).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A box as a model of the three axes meeting at a corner. Squared paper for the plane pictures.

1. **Do it first** — the learner names the corner of a box where three edges meet, then gives the three distances of a point inside the box from the three faces (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the 3-D coordinate system and octants; distance in space; vectors in space and direction cosines; the dot product; the cross and mixed products; equations of lines; equations of planes; the line–plane angle (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE at the octants part: a picture of a box corner with the three axes X, Y and Z drawn out of it and the three coordinate planes shaded — the student points to the octant where all three coordinates are positive.
   - ADD GIF at the distance part: a picture of two points in a box, a flat right triangle drawn on the floor of the box first, then a second right triangle standing on it up to the higher point — the student watches Pythagoras used twice, once flat and once standing.

4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes up the order of the coordinates → a point is always (across, deep, up): x first, then y, then z, the order Laos's Grade 10 book fixes with its cuboid (Lesson 23, p.253).
- Finds the distance flat and stops → space needs Pythagoras twice: once across the floor, then once standing on that length (Laos, Grade 10, Lesson 23).
- Direction cosines do not square-sum to 1 → check the magnitude first; each direction cosine is a component divided by the magnitude, so cos²α + cos²β + cos²γ = 1 always (Laos, Grade 10, Lesson 23).
- Writes a plane's equation from three points with no normal → find two vectors in the plane and cross them: the cross product is the normal (Egypt, Pure Grade 12, Lesson 3-3, p.63, used in Lesson 4-2, p.90).
- Cambodia guide values: the Grade 11 Advanced teacher guide prints the plane through (5,3,6) with normal (3,2,4) as 3x+2y+4z−42 = 0; taught corrected here as 3x+2y+4z−45 = 0, and its distance value is taught corrected as √29 — see lesson/sources.md.
- This lesson's next door: determinants and Cramer's rule (Level 93) — the cross product and the coplanarity test in this lesson are already written with 3×3 determinants there.
