A hard function. e to the x. Sin x. Can a plain polynomial copy it?

Yes — if the polynomial is endless, and if every coefficient is chosen by differentiating.

At zero: the value. Then x times the first derivative at zero. Then x squared times the second derivative, over 2 factorial. Then x cubed, over 3 factorial. On and on.

For e to the x, every derivative at zero is 1 — so the series is 1 + x + x²/2 + x³/6 + … Add five terms: 2.718. That is e, built out of plain powers!

Centre it somewhere else — that is **Taylor**. Around 30 degrees, sin 31 degrees falls out in three small terms: **0.5150**.

Now you. Take two terms. Take three. Watch the copy get closer. That is a series.
