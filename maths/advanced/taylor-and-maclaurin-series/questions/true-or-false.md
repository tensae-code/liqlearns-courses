# Taylor and Maclaurin Series — true or false

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — The Maclaurin Template and eˣ · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — The Standard Maclaurin Series · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Taylor Series and Computing Numbers.

Say true or false. If it is false, say why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Every coefficient in a Maclaurin series is a derivative at 0 divided by a factorial.
**Answer:** True — that is the template (§2.17, ch-pp.74–81).

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** The Maclaurin series of eˣ works for every x.
**Answer:** True — no range condition is needed for eˣ (§2.17, ch-pp.74–78).

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** The Maclaurin series of sin x contains an x² term.
**Answer:** False — the even derivatives of sin at 0 are 0; only odd powers appear (§2.17, ch-pp.74–78).

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** The series for 1/(1+x) gives the function at x = 2.
**Answer:** False — it needs |x| < 1; at x = 2 the partial sums never settle (§2.17, ch-pp.74–78).

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Pakistan's book spells the section heading "TAILOR SERIES".
**Answer:** True as a statement about the printing — a misprint for Taylor, the mathematician's name (§2.17, ch-pp.78–81).

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** Maclaurin is the Taylor series with centre 0.
**Answer:** True — Taylor is the general build, Maclaurin its centre-0 case (§2.17, ch-pp.74–81).
