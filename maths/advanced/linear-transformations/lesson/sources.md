**Acceptance count: taught in 4 countries (Venezuela, North Macedonia, Rwanda, Dominica).** Venezuela is the one read country whose course gives linear transformations a chapter of their own: its Grade 11 book's Lesson 6 (printed pp. 143–155) runs from the photograph that motivates affine versus linear maps, through the two defining laws and their verification on the zero map, the derivation map and every 2 × 2 matrix map, the proved first properties, the five kinds of linear map, and kernel and image as vector spaces, to Sylvester's rank–nullity theorem worked and rotations proved linear. Elsewhere in the library linear algebra is the spaces themselves (the vector-spaces lesson, Rwanda) and the arithmetic of matrices and determinants — the maps, their kernels and images, and the rank–nullity count are taught nowhere else, so under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06) the topic gets its own lesson, built at the size of its source. The book's one over-strong claim — that T sends a basis of V to a basis of Im(T) — is taught corrected, as recorded in the table: the images of a basis span Im(T), but they need not be independent. North Macedonia enters from its main set — the Grade 12 Linear Algebra and Analytic Geometry elective.

Rwanda enters from its Advanced books: the matrix of a linear transformation in three dimensions in Senior Five (Unit 7 §7.2, printed pp. 237–241) and kernel, range and elementary operations in Senior Six (Unit 7 §§7.1–7.2, printed pp. 347–353).
