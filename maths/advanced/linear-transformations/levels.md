# Levels — Linear Transformations

One lesson, one level (Level 155). The depth layers inside it are ranks.

## Depth layers

- **Layer 1 — The two laws.** State what makes a map linear — T(u + v) = T(u) + T(v) and T(ku) = kT(u) — and verify it on the zero map, on a matrix map and on the derivative map for polynomials; tell a linear map from an affine one (a map that moves the origin is not linear). Pass mark: 4 of 5.
- **Layer 2 — First properties.** Prove T(0) = 0 and T(−v) = −T(v) from the two laws; use preservation of linear combinations, T(Σ kᵢvᵢ) = Σ kᵢT(vᵢ); and convict a map of non-linearity by exhibiting one failed law, as the book does from graphs. Pass mark: 4 of 5.
- **Layer 3 — The five kinds.** Define and recognise monomorphism, epimorphism, isomorphism, endomorphism and automorphism for linear maps. Pass mark: 4 of 5.
- **Layer 4 — Kernel and image.** Compute N(T) and Im(T) for a given map (the kernel by solving a linear system); know both are vector spaces; and state the image-of-a-basis fact correctly — the images of a basis span Im(T) but need not be independent. Pass mark: 4 of 5.
- **Layer 5 — Rank–nullity.** Use dim V = dim N(T) + dim Im(T) to find one dimension from the other, verify it on a worked map, and show a rotation about the origin is linear with its formula from the angle-sum identities. Pass mark: 4 of 5.

## What "maxed" means here

The learner can meet any map between vector spaces and test it against the two laws; can prove the first properties instead of quoting them; can compute a kernel and an image and say what they mean; and can run the rank–nullity count on a real example — the exact skills Venezuela's Grade 11 book teaches in its linear-transformation lesson (printed pp. 143–155), with its one over-strong claim about bases taught corrected.
