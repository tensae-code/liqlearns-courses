# Short answer — Linear Transformations

1. (Layer 2) Prove that T(0) = 0 for every linear transformation T.
   **Answer: T(0) = T(0 + 0) = T(0) + T(0) by the addition law, and the only vector equal to its own double is 0.** Venezuela, La Matemática y el Vivir Bien (Grade 11), printed pp. 147–148.

2. (Layer 1) Apply the shear T(x, y) = (x + 2y, y) to the four corners of the unit square.
   **Answer: (0, 0) → (0, 0), (1, 0) → (1, 0), (1, 1) → (3, 1), (0, 1) → (2, 1) — the square becomes a parallelogram, with (0, 0) and (1, 0) fixed.** Venezuela, La Matemática y el Vivir Bien (Grade 11), printed p. 147.

3. (Layer 2) Show that T(x) = x², on the real numbers, is not linear.
   **Answer: it breaks the addition law: T(1 + 1) = 4, but T(1) + T(1) = 1 + 1 = 2.** Venezuela, La Matemática y el Vivir Bien (Grade 11), printed p. 144 (the book's method of disproof, pp. 144, 150).

4. (Layer 5) For T(x, y) = (x + y, x + y), find the kernel and the image, and verify rank–nullity.
   **Answer: the kernel is the line {(x, −x)} (where x + y = 0), dimension 1; the image is the diagonal line spanned by (1, 1), dimension 1; and 1 + 1 = 2 = dim ℝ², as Sylvester's theorem requires.** Venezuela, La Matemática y el Vivir Bien (Grade 11), printed pp. 152–154.
