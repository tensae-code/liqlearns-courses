**Acceptance count: taught in 16 countries (Egypt, Jordan, Greece, Armenia, Vietnam, Libya, Mauritania, Laos, Yemen, Indonesia, Ethiopia, South Sudan, Iraq, Cambodia, Kuwait, Iran).** Egypt carries differentiation from first principles to implicit and parametric differentiation, higher derivatives and related rates; Jordan carries differentiation from slope estimation without limits to the chain rule, implicit differentiation and related rates; Greece carries the full analysis chapter with the Mean Value Theorem, extrema, convexity and De L'Hospital; Armenia carries the derivative through velocity and acceleration to the second-order derivative; Vietnam carries the definition, the rules, the second derivative and the applications; Libya carries composite, product, quotient and implicit differentiation with maxima and minima; Mauritania carries derivation in four streamed books. Laos carries derivatives whole in Grade 10 — the definition, all the rules including the chain rule, higher orders, and applications from related rates to concavity and differentials. Yemen carries derivatives from limits and continuity and the derivative rules in Grade 11, in both tracks, to the full differentiation unit in Grade 12 science — chain and implicit rules, log, exponential and trig derivatives, Rolle and the mean value theorem, maxima and minima and curve sketching. Indonesia carries derivatives in Grade 12 Tingkat Lanjut — limits as background, then the definition, the chain rule, tangents, increasing/decreasing and stationary functions, extrema and applications — in the 2022 student book and now in the 2025 Revisi student book itself, whose chapter adds derivatives of trigonometric and exponential functions; derivatives of logarithmic functions and implicit differentiation are not named topics in the held TL books (one Revisi exercise uses implicit differentiation untaught). Ethiopia carries derivatives whole in Grade 12 — the limit, the derivative as a rate of change built from the limit definition, the rules of differentiation including the power rule, and applications to maxima and minima, velocity and acceleration, tangents and normals; calculus exists only in Grade 12. South Sudan carries derivatives whole in Secondary 4 — the derivative as instantaneous rate of change and slope of a tangent from first principles, the sum, constant-multiple and power rules, tangents and normals, stationary points and kinematics in the regular Mathematics book, and the derivative from secants with the product and quotient rules, implicit differentiation and derivatives of exponential, logarithmic and trigonometric functions in Additional Mathematics — with two corrections: Mathematics p. 78 gives the gradient 14 at x = 2, which is right, but the point (2,3) is not on the curve y = 3x²+2x+1 (at x = 2, y = 17); the correct point is (2,17). And p. 85 says "Gradient at x = 2 is positive" in the stationary-points example, contradicting its own table — the gradient at x = 2 is zero. Iraq carries derivatives from the full derivative course in Grade 11 Scientific — geometric interpretation, physical applications, the rules, the chain rule, tangent and normal, implicit differentiation and derivatives of circular functions, with the corrected worked values (f(x) = x²+5x+3 → f′(2) = 9; S(t) = 3t²+5t+8 → V(t) = 6t+5; v(3) = 41, v(2) = 38) — through applications of differentiation in Grade 12 Scientific, cited at opener/marker level only, to the full differentiation course in Grade 12 Literary, which adds the Literary-only economics applications (marginal cost, average cost, minimum average cost) and uses the book's own convex/concave convention (convex if f″ < 0, concave if f″ > 0), kept as the book has it and not silently 'corrected'. Cambodia carries derivatives whole — first principles, the rules, the chain rule, standard functions, second derivatives, tangents, increasing and decreasing, maxima and minima, graph analysis, related rates, Rolle's theorem and the mean value theorem, concavity and inflection, implicit differentiation, rates of change and kinematics in Grades 11–12; parametric differentiation is the only part not taught anywhere in the set. Kuwait carries derivatives whole in Grade 12 Term 1 (scientific track) — limits and continuity first, then the derivative from its limit definition through the rules, the trig derivatives, the chain rule, higher orders and implicit differentiation, and the applications unit (extrema, monotonicity, curve graphing, optimisation), with the book's p. 47 limit-table signs and its p. 161 profit slip taught corrected; derivatives of exponential and logarithmic functions are not taught in this book. Iran builds the derivative on limits taught in Grade 11 — Hesaban (1) stops at limits and never names the derivative — and teaches differentiation whole in Grade 12 Hesaban (2), with the trigonometric derivatives proved and the chain rule stated, plus a compressed second treatment in Grade 12 Mathematics (3) that adds a dedicated optimisation lesson. Iran never reaches integration, so it does not appear in the integrals lesson.
