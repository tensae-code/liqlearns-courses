# Lesson plan — Derivatives

**Course:** Maths · **Unit:** Unit 12 — Advanced Mathematics · **Lesson:** Lesson 50 of 52
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 12, the fifth lesson of the unit (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** derivatives — rates of change, how to differentiate, and what derivatives tell you about a function

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the functions lesson and the coordinates-and-graphs lesson (a derivative is the slope of a curve), and the limits the books teach first. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a rate of change is, average and instantaneous, and find it from a graph.
2. Find a derivative from first principles, and say what it means as a slope and as a rate.
3. Use the rules of differentiation: sum, product, quotient and chain.
4. Differentiate the special functions: powers, trigonometric, exponential and logarithmic functions.
5. Find higher derivatives, including the second derivative.
6. Use derivatives: increasing and decreasing, maxima and minima, tangents, related rates and curve sketching.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A curve to draw — a ball's height over time, or a hill's slope.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner watches a ball rise and fall, and asks how fast it is moving at one instant (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — rates of change; the derivative; the rules; the special functions; higher derivatives; what derivatives are for (lesson/lesson.md).
3. **Say it to your companion** — give your companion a curve and ask for the slope at three points: going up, flat, going down (lesson/lesson.md).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — differentiate five functions, and find a maximum the books' way (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE at the derivative part: a picture of a curve with a tangent line touching it at one point, and a secant line cutting it at two points close together — the student says which line's slope is the derivative.
   - ADD GIF at the maxima part: a picture of a ball thrown up, its height curve rising, flattening at the top, then falling, with the tangent line flattening at the top — the student says where the derivative is zero.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes the average rate with the instantaneous rate → the average rate is over an interval; the instantaneous rate is at one point, when the interval shrinks to nothing.
- Forgets the chain rule's inner derivative → differentiate the outside, then multiply by the derivative of the inside; the inside is never left undifferentiated.
- Sets the derivative to zero and stops → a zero derivative is a candidate for a maximum or a minimum, not the answer: check what happens on each side.
- This lesson's next door: integrals (same stage) — the next lesson runs differentiation backwards.
