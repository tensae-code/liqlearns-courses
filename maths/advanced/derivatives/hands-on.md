# Hands-on — Derivatives

## The ball's speed
Draw the height curve of a thrown ball (a parabola opening downward). Pick three points: on the way up, at the top, on the way down. Draw a short tangent line at each and feel its slope: steep up, flat, steep down. The slope IS the speed at that instant.

## First principles on paper
Take f(x) = x². Work out the gradient of the chord from x to x + h: ((x + h)² − x²)/h = 2x + h. Now let h shrink to nothing: the answer is 2x. You have just differentiated from first principles, the way Egypt's book does.

## Rule race
Differentiate x³, 3x², sin x and eˣ, one rule each. Then differentiate (x² + 1)³ with the chain rule: outside first (3(x² + 1)²), then times the inside's derivative (2x). Check: the inside is never left undifferentiated.

## The box with no lid
Take a square card and cut squares from the corners to fold a box. The volume is a function of the cut size. Differentiate it, set the derivative to zero, and find the cut that makes the biggest box — the books' maximum problem, made with your hands.
