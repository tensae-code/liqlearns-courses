# Maths course — CURRICULUM (the path)

His order (2026-11-05): put what we have in the app — build the
maths lessons from the countries already read, and let later
countries join by merge, the way new English countries joined
the 51 English lessons. This file is the plan a learner follows,
starting from someone who can barely count and ending able to
do the secondary-school mathematics the books teach. The lessons
themselves live in their stage folders below; this plan is the
path through them.

**Levels, not terms (his ruling, 2026-10-26, carried over from
English):** the plan's big parts are LEVELS: a learner climbs
to the next level when they are ready, not when a calendar says
so. A level is a BASE for the next level — what the levels fix
is the ORDER, what must be standing before the next thing can
stand on it — not a wall around a level's topics.

**One lesson is one level (his ruling, 2026-10-27, carried
over):** every lesson is itself a level, counted from its
prerequisite chain, and small steps inside a lesson are ranks.
Maths is a LEVEL course, like English.

**One lesson explains one thing only.** Parts inside the thing
are depth/ranks; a different thing beside it gets its own
lesson. Lessons stand alone for direct entry (his ruling,
2026-11-04): a learner opening a lesson wants THAT thing only,
so prerequisites appear only as a soft hint, never a forced
start or gate.

## The first page (what learners are told)

Everything in this course comes from government textbooks from
around the world — government-approved books only; that is the
research this course is built on. The one exception is the
**Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns — a clearer way to say a thing, or help from other
material — written to help you understand. Tips are ours; the
lessons are the books'.

The plan is built from the books actually read so far — 11
countries fully read + citation-checked (Kazakhstan, Jordan,
South Africa, Egypt, Greece, Armenia, Sierra Leone, Mauritania,
Libya, Vietnam, Ghana) — and GROWS as each next closed
country's books are read: a new book's material is slotted
into the lesson it belongs to, and a new block the size of a
stage becomes its own next stage rather than being stuffed
into an old one. Lessons already built are marked ✅.

**Honest negatives, stated up front:** a topic no read
country teaches gets NO lesson — coverage is never invented.
Ghana's set holds NO pupil textbook at all (teacher manuals
and curricula only); where a lesson uses Ghana, it is worded
as teacher evidence. Sierra Leone's JSS never teaches surds,
logarithms, the quadratic formula, or sine/cosine rules, and
its SSS never teaches matrices, calculus, complex numbers, or
the binomial theorem — those lessons stand on the other
countries that do teach them. Several countries never teach
complex numbers, matrices, or calculus at all. Two worked
answers flagged wrong in the filings are never taught: Ghana's
Lower workbook "15 − 3 = 18" and Sierra Leone's "0⁰ = 0".

**Pencil and Lines is Level 1** of the whole platform and
already exists as its own shared course — maths lessons do
NOT rebuild it. The counting lesson below points to it as its
soft-hint prerequisite.
