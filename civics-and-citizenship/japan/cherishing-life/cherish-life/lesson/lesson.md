# Cherish Life

## Do it first

Put your hand on your chest and feel your heart beating. Stay quiet for a moment and notice one more sign that you are alive right now.

## Sub lesson 1 — When do you feel alive?

You feel that you are alive when your hand is warm, when your heart beats fast, when you eat something delicious, when you study happily. Life shows itself in these small moments — notice them.

## Sub lesson 2 — The grave visit and the baby hamsters

In Kawai Masao's story, elephants cover their dead with grass and leaves and never forget them, and Naoto promises to visit his uncle's grave as his family visits his grandfather's. In the photo diary, a hamster mother carries her tiny babies ever so gently in her strong mouth, like treasure.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: cherish life — your one life and every life around you is precious and alive right now. Give one real example from your own life in Japan, and name the lesson's key words: life, inochi, alive, precious. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Japanese as well as in English — Japan's own books teach Moral Education (道徳, Doutoku) in Japanese, through stories and self-check pages, for life at home and at school, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: cherish life — your one life and every life around you is precious and alive right now.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
