# Palms to the Sun

## Do it first

Hold your palms up toward the sun or a bright light, the way the song says, and look at the red glow through your fingers.

## Sub lesson 1 — We are all alive

Yanase Takashi's song sings that earthworms and crickets, ants and dragonflies are all alive — and everyone who is alive is a friend. Nobody alive is alone in being alive.

## Sub lesson 2 — Your blood is flowing

Hold your palms to the sun and you can see your own blood flowing, bright red. That flowing blood is your life itself, warm and moving in your hands.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: we are all alive — hold your palms to the sun: your life flows in you, and every living thing is a friend. Give one real example from your own life in Japan, and name the lesson's key words: song, alive, sun, friends. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Japanese as well as in English — Japan's own books teach Moral Education (道徳, Doutoku) in Japanese, through stories and self-check pages, for life at home and at school, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: we are all alive — hold your palms to the sun: your life flows in you, and every living thing is a friend.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
