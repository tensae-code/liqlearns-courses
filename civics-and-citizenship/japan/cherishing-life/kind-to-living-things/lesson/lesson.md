# Kind to Living Things

## Do it first

Care for one living thing today — a plant, an insect you watch without harming, a pet — and do the care properly, the whole job.

## Sub lesson 1 — Peace the polar bear

At Tobe Zoo a baby polar bear's mother would not raise him, so the keepers raised Peace themselves — the first time in Japan humans raised a polar bear. If you keep a pet, raise it with care for its whole life: that is what its life asks of you.

## Sub lesson 2 — Fabre, who loved insects

The boy Fabre watched ants for hours, asking how they carry such big loads and find their way home. Grown up, he wrote the great Insect Book — and when an observation ended he thanked his insects and let them go home.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: be kind to living things — care for the creature in your charge to the end, and wonder at every small life. Give one real example from your own life in Japan, and name the lesson's key words: animals, kindness, Fabre, polar bear. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Japanese as well as in English — Japan's own books teach Moral Education (道徳, Doutoku) in Japanese, through stories and self-check pages, for life at home and at school, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: be kind to living things — care for the creature in your charge to the end, and wonder at every small life.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
