# One Irreplaceable Life

## Do it first

Sit quietly with one living thing — a tree, an insect, a pet — for two minutes. Say what is great about it, and why its one life matters.

## Sub lesson 1 — Respect every life

Respect the lives of yourself and others — because each life is one only, irreplaceable, and limited, it can never be treated lightly.

## Sub lesson 2 — Nature's greatness, and beyond

Know nature's greatness, and feel that which is greater than ourselves — life sits inside something vast.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: each of us has just one, irreplaceable, limited life — so respect life, know nature's greatness, and feel what is greater. Give one real example from your own life in Japan, and name the lesson's key words: life, irreplaceable, nature, respect. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Japanese as well as in English — Japan's own books teach Moral Education (道徳, Doutoku) in Japanese, through stories and self-check pages, for life at home and at school, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: each of us has just one, irreplaceable, limited life — so respect life, know nature's greatness, and feel what is greater.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
