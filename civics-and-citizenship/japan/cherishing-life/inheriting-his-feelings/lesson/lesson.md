# Inheriting His Feelings

## Do it first

Think of an older person who has taught you something. Write down what they gave you, and one way you will carry it on. Tell them, if you can.

## Sub lesson 1 — Visiting, every day

A boy visits his grandfather — who may die within three months — every day, spending the limited time face to face.

## Sub lesson 2 — Carrying on what he gave

Inheriting his feelings means carrying forward what his grandfather gave him — the love and teaching do not end with the life.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: when someone you love is dying, you can carry on what they gave you — their feelings become yours to live. Give one real example from your own life in Japan, and name the lesson's key words: grandfather, inherit, feelings, dying. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Japanese as well as in English — Japan's own books teach Moral Education (道徳, Doutoku) in Japanese, through stories and self-check pages, for life at home and at school, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: when someone you love is dying, you can carry on what they gave you — their feelings become yours to live.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
