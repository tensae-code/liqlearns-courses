# People Who Shine at Work

## Do it first

Name three jobs people do in your town. For each one, say who it helps, and one careful thing that worker must do well.

## Sub lesson 1 — Work that shines

Across many fields, people shine in their work — the heart surgeon prepares again and again, because a patient's life depends on the preparation.

## Sub lesson 2 — What work means

Work is not only a job: done with care, it serves other people, and that service is what makes it shine.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: people shine in their work when they give it their whole heart — because other people's lives can depend on it. Give one real example from your own life in Japan, and name the lesson's key words: work, shine, care, serve. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Japanese as well as in English — Japan's own books teach Moral Education (道徳, Doutoku) in Japanese, through stories and self-check pages, for life at home and at school, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: people shine in their work when they give it their whole heart — because other people's lives can depend on it.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
