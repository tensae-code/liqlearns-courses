# Learning About Other Countries

## Do it first

Find one thing in your home or classroom that came from another country, and one greeting from another country that you can say.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Olympics and Paralympics

At the Olympics and Paralympics the athletes of many countries strive together — a letter written from the Tokyo Games of 1964, and photographs of London 2012. The whole world meets, competes and cheers in one place.

## Sub lesson 2 — Things and greetings from other countries

Look around you: clothes, food, music and games that came from other countries are everywhere. And each country greets in its own way — hello, namaste — as the book's world-greetings pages showed. The book closes by writing down what you learned in Moral Education, in Grade 1 and in Grade 2.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: learn about other countries — their things are already in your life, their athletes strive beside yours, and their greetings differ from yours. Give one real example from your own life in Japan, and name the lesson's key words: countries, world, Olympics, greetings. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Japanese as well as in English — Japan's own books teach Moral Education (道徳, Doutoku) in Japanese, through stories and self-check pages, for life at home and at school, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: learn about other countries — their things are already in your life, their athletes strive beside yours, and their greetings differ from yours.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
