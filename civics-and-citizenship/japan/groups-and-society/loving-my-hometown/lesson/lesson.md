# Loving My Hometown

## Do it first

Name four treasures of your hometown: a person, a place in nature, a food or craft, and a story. Say one way you can care for one of them.

## Sub lesson 1 — The treasures of home

Your hometown's people, daily life, nature and culture are treasures — loving your hometown (furusato) starts by seeing them as such.

## Sub lesson 2 — Love that keeps

Love for a hometown shows in care: keeping its places clean, learning its stories, and passing them on.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: my hometown is a treasure — its people, its life, its nature and its culture are mine to love and keep. Give one real example from your own life in Japan, and name the lesson's key words: hometown, furusato, treasure, love. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Japanese as well as in English — Japan's own books teach Moral Education (道徳, Doutoku) in Japanese, through stories and self-check pages, for life at home and at school, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: my hometown is a treasure — its people, its life, its nature and its culture are mine to love and keep.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
