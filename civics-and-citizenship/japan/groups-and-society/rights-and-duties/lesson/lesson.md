# Rights and Duties

## Do it first

List two rights you hold and two duties you owe at school. For each duty, name the right — yours or someone else's — that it protects.

## Sub lesson 1 — What is a right

A right is what each person may rightly claim and have respected — yours, and equally everyone else's.

## Sub lesson 2 — What is a duty

A duty is what each of us must fulfil while living together in society — rights are kept safe when duties are done.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: living together in society, each of us holds rights and fulfils duties — the two stand together. Give one real example from your own life in Japan, and name the lesson's key words: rights, duties, society, together. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Japanese as well as in English — Japan's own books teach Moral Education (道徳, Doutoku) in Japanese, through stories and self-check pages, for life at home and at school, not only for the classroom.

3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
