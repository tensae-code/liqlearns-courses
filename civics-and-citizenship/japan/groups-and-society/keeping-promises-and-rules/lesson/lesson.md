# Keeping Promises and Rules

## Do it first

Keep one promise and one rule today on purpose — a turn, a queue, a shared thing used carefully — and notice how the day runs because of it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Why promises and rules exist

Talk about what would happen if there were no rules at all. The town, the road and the park are full of things everyone uses and no one person owns — use them carefully, keep your turn, share, and never dirty or break them.

## Sub lesson 2 — The yellow bench

Takashi and Tetsuo fly their paper planes from the top of the park's yellow bench, and their muddy shoes cover it with dirt. Then a little girl sits down in the mud — and the boys see, too late, what a shared thing asks of everyone who uses it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: keep promises and rules — they guard everyone's safety and pleasant days, and shared things belong to everyone. Give one real example from your own life in Japan, and name the lesson's key words: rules, promises, shared things, safety. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Japanese as well as in English — Japan's own books teach Moral Education (道徳, Doutoku) in Japanese, through stories and self-check pages, for life at home and at school, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: keep promises and rules — they guard everyone's safety and pleasant days, and shared things belong to everyone.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
