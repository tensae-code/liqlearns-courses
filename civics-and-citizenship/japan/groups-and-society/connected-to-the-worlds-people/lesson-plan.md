## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **One star, seven billion** — The world's peoples live connected on one planet; Sakamoto Ryoma's charge — that Japan must look to the world — opens the book's last theme.
2. **What can I do** — With awareness as a Japanese person, ask what you yourself can do for the world's peoples — connection begins with one person's chosen act.

Gate pool: the prove-it check for each sub lesson is at most 5 questions, mixed with earlier parts, picked as the hardest from the rank right under the wanted sub lesson — drawn from this lesson's question files (questions/).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: more than seven billion people share one star — with awareness as a Japanese person, ask what you can do.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street, school or community in Japan.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the learner's own life in Japan and the book's own teaching, as Japan's Moral Education books work through short illustrated stories, songs and self-check pages.
