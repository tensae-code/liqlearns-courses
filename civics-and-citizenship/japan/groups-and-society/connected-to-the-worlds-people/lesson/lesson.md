# Connected to the World's People

## Do it first

Name one country far from Japan and one thing its people and yours share. Then name one act of connection open to you — learning, greeting, helping, sharing.

## Sub lesson 1 — One star, seven billion

The world's peoples live connected on one planet; Sakamoto Ryoma's charge — that Japan must look to the world — opens the book's last theme.

## Sub lesson 2 — What can I do

With awareness as a Japanese person, ask what you yourself can do for the world's peoples — connection begins with one person's chosen act.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: more than seven billion people share one star — with awareness as a Japanese person, ask what you can do. Give one real example from your own life in Japan, and name the lesson's key words: world, connected, Ryoma, peoples. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Japanese as well as in English — Japan's own books teach Moral Education (道徳, Doutoku) in Japanese, through stories and self-check pages, for life at home and at school, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: more than seven billion people share one star — with awareness as a Japanese person, ask what you can do.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
