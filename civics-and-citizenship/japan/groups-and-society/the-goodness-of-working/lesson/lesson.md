# The Goodness of Working

## Do it first

Do one real job today — at home or in class — from its start to its proper finish, and notice how you feel when it is done well.

## Sub lesson 1 — My job

A child writes about her lunch duty: today she wiped the spilt milk properly and washed the cloth clean, the teacher thanked her, and she resolved to try again tomorrow. School duties and helping at home are real work too.

## Sub lesson 2 — Kuma the bear postman

Kuma is the forest's postman. He delivers letters and parcels house by house, climbs the snowy mountain road to old Goat's house imagining his happy face, and even on days with no mail he talks with everyone — the forest waits for him gladly.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: feel the goodness of working — work done with all your heart for others shines, and feels good. Give one real example from your own life in Japan, and name the lesson's key words: work, job, duty, postman. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Japanese as well as in English — Japan's own books teach Moral Education (道徳, Doutoku) in Japanese, through stories and self-check pages, for life at home and at school, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: feel the goodness of working — work done with all your heart for others shines, and feels good.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
