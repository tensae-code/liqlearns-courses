# Drawn to Japanese Culture

## Do it first

Look at one tree or one familiar place near your home as if for the first time. Say three beautiful things about it that you had stopped noticing.

## Sub lesson 1 — Yakumo comes to Japan

Koizumi Yakumo — Lafcadio Hearn — came from across the sea and was drawn to Japanese culture, writing it down for the world.

## Sub lesson 2 — Seeing the trees truly

Yakumo wrote of why Japan's trees are so beautiful to one who truly sees them — loving a culture begins with really looking.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: a person from far away can truly see your culture's beauty — and teach you to see it too. Give one real example from your own life in Japan, and name the lesson's key words: culture, Yakumo, beauty, seeing. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Japanese as well as in English — Japan's own books teach Moral Education (道徳, Doutoku) in Japanese, through stories and self-check pages, for life at home and at school, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: a person from far away can truly see your culture's beauty — and teach you to see it too.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
