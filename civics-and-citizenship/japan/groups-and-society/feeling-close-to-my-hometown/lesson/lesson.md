# Feeling Close to My Hometown

## Do it first

Name three things about your own town that you would put in a town newspaper — a place, a person, an event — and say why each one matters.

## Sub lesson 1 — My town newspaper

Make a newspaper of your town: its festivals and events, the look of its people, your own recommended place, and the town you hope for in the future. What you grow up in is your town's everyone's joy.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Gion Festival

A Kyoto boy practises the festival music for a month before the Gion Festival. He is scolded, tells his father he wants to quit — and his father tells him everyone has practised through scoldings, guarding this festival for a thousand years. On the day, riding the great float, he is glad he kept going.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: feel close to your hometown — its festivals, people and places are treasures its people guard together. Give one real example from your own life in Japan, and name the lesson's key words: hometown, furusato, festival, town. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Japanese as well as in English — Japan's own books teach Moral Education (道徳, Doutoku) in Japanese, through stories and self-check pages, for life at home and at school, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: feel close to your hometown — its festivals, people and places are treasures its people guard together.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
