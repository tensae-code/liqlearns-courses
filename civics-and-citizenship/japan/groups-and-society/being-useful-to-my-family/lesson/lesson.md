# Being Useful to My Family

## Do it first

Do one useful thing for your family today without being asked — make a drink, tidy something, play with a little brother or sister — and watch their faces.

## Sub lesson 1 — The people who treasure me most

Your family are the people who treasure you most. Read their messages to you — and remember the sick day when mother kept changing the cooling sheet on your forehead and grandmother kept coming to look in on you.

## Sub lesson 2 — What I can do for my family

A boy makes tea for his father, tired from work, and rubs his shoulders; a girl plays in the garden with her little brother so her mother can cook. Their thanks and smiles warm the helper's own heart too.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: think how to be useful to your family — the people who treasure you most — and let helping warm your heart. Give one real example from your own life in Japan, and name the lesson's key words: family, helping, useful, home. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Japanese as well as in English — Japan's own books teach Moral Education (道徳, Doutoku) in Japanese, through stories and self-check pages, for life at home and at school, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: think how to be useful to your family — the people who treasure you most — and let helping warm your heart.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
