# Laws and Rules for Living Together

## Do it first

Name three rules — at home, at school, on the road. For each, say whose safety or fairness it protects, including someone whose standpoint differs from yours.

## Sub lesson 1 — One person in society

As one person living in society, you live among people of many standpoints — Fukuzawa Yukichi's teaching and the Hibi no Oshie point the same way: conduct yourself so shared life holds.

## Sub lesson 2 — Rules that let us live as people

The laws and rules that must be kept are not chains: they are what lets people live as people, safely and fairly, side by side.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: laws and rules exist so people of many standpoints can live as people, among people. Give one real example from your own life in Japan, and name the lesson's key words: laws, rules, society, Fukuzawa. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Japanese as well as in English — Japan's own books teach Moral Education (道徳, Doutoku) in Japanese, through stories and self-check pages, for life at home and at school, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: laws and rules exist so people of many standpoints can live as people, among people.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
