# About the Civics and Citizenship — Japan course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Moral Education books (Watashitachi no Doutoku,
Grades 1–2, 3–4 and 5–6). The one exception is the **Tip
boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Japan inside the Civics and
Citizenship folder (one course per country). Inside it, the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place
is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the
ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced
gate. The school grades the books come from (Grades 1–6)
appear only in each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as
provenance.

The course has **three stocked levels**, from the three
books filed — one six-year climb of the same four content
areas (self, others, life and nature, society), restated at
each band:

1. **Level 1 — Myself, People, Life and Everyone**
   (21 lessons, in the titles `myself/`, `others/`,
   `cherishing-life/` and `groups-and-society/`). The
   whole Grades 1–2 book: looking at myself (tell us
   about yourself; a regular and pleasant day; my own
   tasks done properly; good things done willingly; an
   honest and cheerful heart); with people (pleasant
   behaviour and greetings; kindness with a warm heart —
   the Wolf on the Bridge; getting along with friends;
   grateful to the people who help us); touching life
   (cherish life; palms to the sun; kind to living things
   — Peace the polar bear and Fabre; a fresh and open
   heart); together with everyone (keeping promises and
   rules — the Yellow Bench; the goodness of working;
   being useful to my family; making school life fun;
   feeling close to my hometown — the Gion Festival); and
   the closing review (finding the good points — with
   Hasegawa Machiko; getting along with everyone; learning
   about other countries). **Book 1 stands COMPLETE: its
   complete merged volume was recovered from the
   mid-size-books-6 release and read in full in the
   unread-queue phase, 2026-11-22.**
2. **Level 2 — Raising Myself and Relating to Others**
   (9 lessons, in the titles `myself/`, `others/` and
   `groups-and-society/`). Raising myself (write about
   myself; think well and live with moderation; the road
   before me; I can do it — Takahashi Naoko), relating to
   others (sincerity toward everyone; living by helping
   each other — the guide-dog family), and society (people
   who shine at work; loving my hometown; drawn to
   Japanese culture — Koizumi Yakumo).
3. **Level 3 — Polish Myself, Connect, Cherish Life,
   Engage with Society** (16 lessons, in the titles
   `myself/`, `others/`, `cherishing-life/` and
   `groups-and-society/`). The four areas as verbs: polish
   myself, connect with others, cherish life (one
   irreplaceable life; a grandfather's dying), engage with
   society (rights and duties defined outright; laws and
   rules; my role in the group; the world's peoples, with
   Sakamoto Ryoma).

**Japan's own way:** moral education (道徳) for the
youngest children is taught entirely through short
illustrated stories, songs and self-check pages — the
child tidies, persists, helps and apologises inside
stories (a boy who oversleeps, the Tortoise and the Hare,
Ninomiya Kinjiro, a cricket who learns to apologise), then
colours in a self-check balloon or chart for each habit.
No rules are lectured; every value arrives as a story plus
a "check yourself" page. The middle-years book keeps the
same shape — four content areas, each a set of numbered
value themes opened by story readings, poems, real-person
columns and self-check pages — but the stories grow up: a
marathon runner's small daily goals, a guide-dog family,
loving one's hometown, and Lafcadio Hearn (Koizumi
Yakumo) loving Japan's trees. The final elementary book
turns reflective — autonomy and responsibility are
discussed in the child's own words, a grandfather's dying
is followed day by day, rights and duties are defined
outright, and the volume closes looking outward: Sakamoto
Ryoma opening Japan to the world, and a teacher's charge
to think for oneself. The four areas are now named as
verbs: polish myself, connect with others, cherish life,
engage with society.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later;
the books' own shape, walked in order):

- *One climb, four areas:* "Start with yourself — your
  day, your tasks, your honest heart. Then raise yourself
  and learn to live with others, with sincerity. Then
  polish yourself, connect, cherish the one life you have,
  and take your place in society and the world. That is
  Japan's Moral Education — the same four areas, climbed
  for six years."
