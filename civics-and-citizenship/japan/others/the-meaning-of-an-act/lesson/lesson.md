# The Meaning of an Act

## Do it first

Think of someone having a hard day. Do one act of consideration for them — something done, not only felt — and notice what changes.

## Sub lesson 1 — Close to another's feelings

Draw close to another's feelings: try to stand where they stand and feel what the moment is like for them.

## Sub lesson 2 — Consideration is action

Miyazawa Shoji's poem teaches that the heart is invisible, but consideration (kokorozukai) is visible — it is action toward another person.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the heart cannot be seen, but consideration can — because consideration is an act done for another. Give one real example from your own life in Japan, and name the lesson's key words: consideration, kokorozukai, feelings, act. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Japanese as well as in English — Japan's own books teach Moral Education (道徳, Doutoku) in Japanese, through stories and self-check pages, for life at home and at school, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: the heart cannot be seen, but consideration can — because consideration is an act done for another.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
