# Getting Along with Everyone

## Do it first

Look at one moment today when children had to share, queue or join in — and judge: what was the getting-along thing to do there?

## Sub lesson 1 — What should I do?

Picture scenes ask the question: children queueing, a shared toy, a game someone wants to join, a snowman built together. Some acts help everyone get along and some must never be done — learn to tell them apart and choose.

## Sub lesson 2 — The rabbit who thought again

Read the little story of the rabbit, told in the open book's own pages, and think it through: what happened, what should the rabbit have done, and what would you do?

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: get along with everyone — judge each moment and choose the act that lets everyone be together gladly. Give one real example from your own life in Japan, and name the lesson's key words: everyone, getting along, judging, kindness. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Japanese as well as in English — Japan's own books teach Moral Education (道徳, Doutoku) in Japanese, through stories and self-check pages, for life at home and at school, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: get along with everyone — judge each moment and choose the act that lets everyone be together gladly.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
