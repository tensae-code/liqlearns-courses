# Learning from Edo Manners

## Do it first

Walk one narrow path or corridor today the Edo way: slow down, make room, let the other pass first. Then make room for someone to sit beside you.

## Sub lesson 1 — Manners for living together

Edo manners are the wisdom of a long peace for living comfortably together — small courtesies that keep shared life smooth.

## Sub lesson 2 — Room on the road, room to sit

Pass carefully on a narrow road, and make room for others to sit — the same heart in both: leave space for the other person.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the manners of Edo kept a crowded city comfortable for 300 years — make room for others, in body and in mind. Give one real example from your own life in Japan, and name the lesson's key words: manners, Edo, courtesy, room. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Japanese as well as in English — Japan's own books teach Moral Education (道徳, Doutoku) in Japanese, through stories and self-check pages, for life at home and at school, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: the manners of Edo kept a crowded city comfortable for 300 years — make room for others, in body and in mind.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
