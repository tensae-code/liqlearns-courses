# Noguchi Hideyo and Yellow Fever

## Do it first

Name one person whose work protects strangers — a doctor, a firefighter, a researcher. Say what their work costs them and what it gives everyone else.

## Sub lesson 1 — The scientist who went out

Noguchi Hideyo, the world-famous medical scientist from Japan, travelled where yellow fever was, to fight it for people he would never meet.

## Sub lesson 2 — Struck down in the fight

In the year illness struck him down, his work stood: a life spent against disease is a life spent for others.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Noguchi Hideyo fought disease for the whole world — and the fight itself took his life. Give one real example from your own life in Japan, and name the lesson's key words: science, service, sacrifice, world. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Japanese as well as in English — Japan's own books teach Moral Education (道徳, Doutoku) in Japanese, through stories and self-check pages, for life at home and at school, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: Noguchi Hideyo fought disease for the whole world — and the fight itself took his life.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
