# Getting Along with Friends

## Do it first

Play or talk with a friend today, and do one thing that makes the time good for both of you — then tell them one thing you like about being their friend.

## Sub lesson 1 — A friend halves sorrow and doubles joy

The friendship poem teaches that a friend is someone who halves your sorrow and doubles your joy — Poppo the dove made friends through talking and through letters, and a friend beside you makes you want to try hard.

## Sub lesson 2 — The squirrel who couldn't swim

The animals tell Squirrel he cannot come to the island because he cannot swim, and the island's games feel flat without him. The turtle carries him over on his back, the others say sorry — and everyone agrees the day is better with Squirrel there.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: get along with friends — share their joys and sorrows, and find a way for everyone to be included. Give one real example from your own life in Japan, and name the lesson's key words: friends, friendship, together, include. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Japanese as well as in English — Japan's own books teach Moral Education (道徳, Doutoku) in Japanese, through stories and self-check pages, for life at home and at school, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: get along with friends — share their joys and sorrows, and find a way for everyone to be included.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
