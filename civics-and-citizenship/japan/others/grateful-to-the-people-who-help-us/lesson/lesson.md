# Grateful to the People Who Help Us

## Do it first

Say a real thank-you today to one person who looks after you — at home, at school or on your street — and say what you are thanking them for.

## Sub lesson 1 — So many thank-yous

Many people look after us every day: the sixth-graders, grandmother, the person who cooks the school lunch, the crossing guard who keeps the walk to school safe. There are so many thank-yous to say.

## Sub lesson 2 — A thank-you card from the heart

Make a thank-you card the book's way: write the name of the person, write your thanks from your heart, and write your own name and the date — a small card that carries a real feeling.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: be grateful to the people who help us — notice them, thank them, and say it from the heart. Give one real example from your own life in Japan, and name the lesson's key words: gratitude, thank you, helpers, letter. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Japanese as well as in English — Japan's own books teach Moral Education (道徳, Doutoku) in Japanese, through stories and self-check pages, for life at home and at school, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: be grateful to the people who help us — notice them, thank them, and say it from the heart.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
