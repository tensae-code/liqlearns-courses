# Pleasant Behaviour and Greetings

## Do it first

Greet three people today the pleasant way — in the morning, at noon and with thanks — and notice how each greeting feels, to you and to them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Greetings that connect hearts

The day is full of greetings — good morning, hello, thank you, from teachers and from family. When you greet, your heart and the other person's heart connect; grow your own greeting tree with the greetings you want to be able to say.

## Sub lesson 2 — The island with no greetings

Keita visits an island of monkeys where nobody greets anyone, and finds it silent and strange. Day by day his own cheerful greetings spread, until the whole island rings with them — and the world's countries each greet in their own way.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: pleasant behaviour begins with greetings — greet the people of your day warmly, and hearts connect. Give one real example from your own life in Japan, and name the lesson's key words: greetings, aisatsu, manners, pleasant. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Japanese as well as in English — Japan's own books teach Moral Education (道徳, Doutoku) in Japanese, through stories and self-check pages, for life at home and at school, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: pleasant behaviour begins with greetings — greet the people of your day warmly, and hearts connect.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
