# Helen and Annie Sullivan

## Do it first

Teach someone one small thing today — a word, a skill, a game — with real patience. Notice what your learner needed from you to succeed.

## Sub lesson 1 — The teacher who stayed

Annie Sullivan came to teach Helen Keller, who could neither see nor hear, and stayed — opening language, and with it the world, to her pupil.

## Sub lesson 2 — Learning breaks through

With patience and invention, teacher and learner broke through silence and darkness together — effort on both sides made it possible.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: a great teacher can open the whole world to one learner — Annie Sullivan did it for Helen Keller. Give one real example from your own life in Japan, and name the lesson's key words: teacher, Helen Keller, patience, learning. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Japanese as well as in English — Japan's own books teach Moral Education (道徳, Doutoku) in Japanese, through stories and self-check pages, for life at home and at school, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: a great teacher can open the whole world to one learner — Annie Sullivan did it for Helen Keller.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
