# A Regular and Pleasant Day

## Do it first

Look at your desk or schoolbag. Tidy one thing that is out of place, then say the three parts of a good morning: get up on time, eat breakfast, and arrive ready.

## Sub lesson 1 — Tidy things, tidy day

Keep your desk, locker and things tidy at school and at home, and check yourself on a chart — Ruppe, who cannot get up and scatters his schoolbag, shows what an untidy start costs.

## Sub lesson 2 — A good daily rhythm

Set your day's rhythm: rise early, eat breakfast, and sleep enough, so each day starts pleasant and in order.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: a regular, pleasant day is built by small habits — tidying your things and keeping a good daily rhythm. Give one real example from your own life in Japan, and name the lesson's key words: regular, tidy, rhythm, habits. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Japanese as well as in English — Japan's own books teach Moral Education (道徳, Doutoku) in Japanese, through stories and self-check pages, for life at home and at school, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: a regular, pleasant day is built by small habits — tidying your things and keeping a good daily rhythm.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
