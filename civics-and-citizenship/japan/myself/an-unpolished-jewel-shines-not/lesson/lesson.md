# An Unpolished Jewel Shines Not

## Do it first

Name one rough edge in yourself you want to polish. Say what polishing it looks like in one ordinary day this week, and do that day.

## Sub lesson 1 — The jewel and the polishing

The old teaching from the Book of Rites says a jewel unpolished does not shine — people, too, become bright only by being worked on.

## Sub lesson 2 — The self I want to change

Notice honestly the self you want to change, polish that part, and let your own character show — no one else's shine can replace yours.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: like a jewel, a person shines only when polished — notice the self you want to change, and show your own character. Give one real example from your own life in Japan, and name the lesson's key words: jewel, polish, character, change. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Japanese as well as in English — Japan's own books teach Moral Education (道徳, Doutoku) in Japanese, through stories and self-check pages, for life at home and at school, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: like a jewel, a person shines only when polished — notice the self you want to change, and show your own character.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
