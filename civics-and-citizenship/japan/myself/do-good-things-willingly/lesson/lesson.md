# Do Good Things Willingly

## Do it first

Think of one small good thing you can do today — at home, at school or on the road. Do it without being asked, then say why it was good.

## Sub lesson 1 — Good things, however small

Do the good thing you can see — pick up litter, leave the wall clean, offer your seat to an older person on the train — willingly, not because you were told.

## Sub lesson 2 — Say no clearly, like Ponta

Ponta refuses to follow his friend into the forbidden, dangerous hill and says so clearly — doing good also means refusing what is wrong, out loud.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: when you think a thing is good, do it willingly — however small it is. Give one real example from your own life in Japan, and name the lesson's key words: good, willingly, courage, refuse. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Japanese as well as in English — Japan's own books teach Moral Education (道徳, Doutoku) in Japanese, through stories and self-check pages, for life at home and at school, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: when you think a thing is good, do it willingly — however small it is.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
