# Be Honest and Cheerful

## Do it first

Think of a time you hid a mistake or blamed someone else. Practise the words you should have said: what happened, and 'I'm sorry.'

## Sub lesson 1 — The things I must never do

Never lie, never take others' things, never hit, bully, hide the truth or speak ill of others — an honest heart starts by refusing these.

## Sub lesson 2 — Sorry, and a calmer face

Saying sorry brings relief — the child who blamed his younger brother feels it, and Koro the cricket, seeing his sulky face in a moonlit dewdrop, learns to apologise and control his temper.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: an honest, open heart is a cheerful heart — say sorry, own your mistakes, and keep your temper. Give one real example from your own life in Japan, and name the lesson's key words: honest, cheerful, sorry, temper. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Japanese as well as in English — Japan's own books teach Moral Education (道徳, Doutoku) in Japanese, through stories and self-check pages, for life at home and at school, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: an honest, open heart is a cheerful heart — say sorry, own your mistakes, and keep your temper.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
