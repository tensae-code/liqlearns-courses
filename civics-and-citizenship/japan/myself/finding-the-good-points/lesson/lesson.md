# Finding the Good Points

## Do it first

Find one good point in a friend today — something they do well or kindly — and tell them what you found.

## Sub lesson 1 — My good points, my friends' good points

Everyone has splendid good points — you, and every one of your friends. Find them: the friend who is kind to everyone, the one who reads so many books, the one whose legs are fast. Teach each other your good points and write them in this book, in Grade 1 and in Grade 2.

## Sub lesson 2 — Machiko, who loved manga

Hasegawa Machiko drew pictures from when she was small and was good at likenesses. She thought up Sazae-san, and kept drawing the manga everyone loves — through hard times too — because drawing was what she loved and was good at.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: find the good points — yours and your friends': everyone has them, and naming them grows them. Give one real example from your own life in Japan, and name the lesson's key words: good points, strengths, friends, manga. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Japanese as well as in English — Japan's own books teach Moral Education (道徳, Doutoku) in Japanese, through stories and self-check pages, for life at home and at school, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: find the good points — yours and your friends': everyone has them, and naming them grows them.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
