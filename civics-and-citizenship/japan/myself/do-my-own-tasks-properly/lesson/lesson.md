# Do My Own Tasks Properly

## Do it first

Name one job that is yours at home and one at school. Do the home one today without being asked, and check: did you finish it properly, to the end?

## Sub lesson 1 — My tasks at school and home

Do your own jobs properly — at school and at home, like the child whose own job is cleaning the bath, done every day without being told.

## Sub lesson 2 — Small efforts pile up

The Tortoise beats the Hare by never stopping, and Ninomiya Kinjiro's small daily efforts piled up into a whole life — steady effort wins.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the tasks that are yours to do, you do yourself — properly, and to the end. Give one real example from your own life in Japan, and name the lesson's key words: tasks, effort, persistence, properly. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Japanese as well as in English — Japan's own books teach Moral Education (道徳, Doutoku) in Japanese, through stories and self-check pages, for life at home and at school, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: the tasks that are yours to do, you do yourself — properly, and to the end.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
