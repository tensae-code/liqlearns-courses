# I Can Do It

## Do it first

Pick one goal. Break it into three small steps you can do on three days. Do step one today, and say the words: I can do it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Small goals, one step at a time

Takahashi Naoko set small daily goals and climbed them one step at a time, like a long staircase — that is how 'I can surely do it' becomes true.

## Sub lesson 2 — From crying child to the top

Naoko began as a child who cried when she ran; step by step, the small goals carried her to the top of her sport.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: big goals are climbed like a long staircase — one small daily goal at a time. Give one real example from your own life in Japan, and name the lesson's key words: goals, steps, persistence, can-do. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Japanese as well as in English — Japan's own books teach Moral Education (道徳, Doutoku) in Japanese, through stories and self-check pages, for life at home and at school, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: big goals are climbed like a long staircase — one small daily goal at a time.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
