# Lesson plan — The King

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Morocco · **Title:** Constitutional Institutions · **Lesson:** The King
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — Constitutional Institutions (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the King stands at the head of Morocco's system of rule, and his powers are written in the constitution itself.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The King's place in the system of rule** — Morocco's system of rule places the King at its head: he stands at the top of the state's authority.
2. **The King's constitutional powers** — The constitution gives the King named powers — things he may do in the life of the state.
3. **Reading the powers from the articles** — The lesson reads the King's powers straight from the constitution's own articles, as this book always works: from the document, not from a summary.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: the King stands at the head of Morocco's system of rule, and his powers are written in the constitution itself.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or town — or, for this book's document lessons, from the public life of Morocco they live in.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.
