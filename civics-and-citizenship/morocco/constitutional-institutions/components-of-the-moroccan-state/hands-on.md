# Hands-on — Components of the Moroccan State

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a big triangle. At its three corners write: land, people, rulers. This lesson names the three components every state stands on, and then looks at the symbols that stand for Morocco's state.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: a state stands on three components — its territory, its people and its authority — and Morocco's state is known by its own symbols. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
