# Public Freedoms

## Do it first

Name four things people do together in public: meet, gather, publish, travel. This lesson names the freedoms behind those acts, and the guarantees that protect them.

## Sub lesson 1 — The freedoms Moroccans hold

The public freedoms are the freedoms Moroccans hold in their shared life: freedom of association, of assembly, of the press, of movement, and their companions.

Each freedom names something citizens may do openly, together, in the life of the country.

## Sub lesson 2 — The guarantees that protect them

A freedom written down needs protection: the law carries guarantees that guard the public freedoms.

The guarantees are what stand between a freedom on paper and a freedom a citizen can actually use.

## Sub lesson 3 — One family of freedoms

The lesson gathers the freedoms as one family: together they make the open space in which citizens act in public.

Association, assembly, press and movement are not four separate gifts — they are the doors of one room.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Moroccans hold public freedoms — to associate, to assemble, in the press, to move — and guarantees in law protect those freedoms. Give one real example from your own life or from the Morocco you live in, and name the lesson's key words: public freedoms, association, assembly, press, movement, guarantees, الحريات العامة. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
