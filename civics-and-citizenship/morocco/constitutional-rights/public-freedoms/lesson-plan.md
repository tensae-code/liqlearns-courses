# Lesson plan — Public Freedoms

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Morocco · **Title:** Constitutional Rights · **Lesson:** Public Freedoms
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — Constitutional Rights (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Moroccans hold public freedoms — to associate, to assemble, in the press, to move — and guarantees in law protect those freedoms.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The freedoms Moroccans hold** — The public freedoms are the freedoms Moroccans hold in their shared life: freedom of association, of assembly, of the press, of movement, and their companions.
2. **The guarantees that protect them** — A freedom written down needs protection: the law carries guarantees that guard the public freedoms.
3. **One family of freedoms** — The lesson gathers the freedoms as one family: together they make the open space in which citizens act in public.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Moroccans hold public freedoms — to associate, to assemble, in the press, to move — and guarantees in law protect those freedoms.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or town — or, for this book's document lessons, from the public life of Morocco they live in.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the state's own papers (وثائق — constitutional articles, laws and official documents), as the book itself works.

## If the learner is stuck
- Lists freedoms but drops the guarantees → pair every freedom with its guard: a freedom on paper, and the guarantee that lets the citizen actually use it.
- Go back to the one thing: Moroccans hold public freedoms — to associate, to assemble, in the press, to move — and guarantees in law protect those freedoms. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
