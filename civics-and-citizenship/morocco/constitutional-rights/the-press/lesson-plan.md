# Lesson plan — The Press

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Morocco · **Title:** Constitutional Rights · **Lesson:** The Press
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — Constitutional Rights (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the press is a profession with its own ethics — and it stands among the citizen's freedoms, because a free press carries the country's public life.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The press as a profession** — The press (الصحافة) is a profession: the work of gathering news and publishing it, done by people trained and answerable for that work.
2. **The ethics of the press** — The press carries its own ethics — the rules of right conduct inside the craft, binding on those who practise it.
3. **The press among the citizen's freedoms** — Freedom of the press stands among the citizen's public freedoms, in the family this course has already met.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: the press is a profession with its own ethics — and it stands among the citizen's freedoms, because a free press carries the country's public life.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or town — or, for this book's document lessons, from the public life of Morocco they live in.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the state's own papers (وثائق — constitutional articles, laws and official documents), as the book itself works.
