# About the Civics and Citizenship — Morocco course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this
course comes from government textbooks — this country's own
Social Studies book for Collège Year 2, in its Citizenship
Education strand (التربية على المواطنة). The one exception is
the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Morocco inside the Civics and Citizenship
folder (one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is
by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what
they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a
recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school grades
the books come from appear only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

The course as built has **one stocked level**, with one level
sketched before it and not stocked:

1. **Level 1 — sketched, NOT stocked (0 lessons).** The Collège
   Year 1 citizenship strand is known only from the series'
   order, as sketched in the placements file. No Collège Year 1
   book is in hand this pass, so no lesson is built for this
   level. The level stands in the ladder as a sketch only; see
   the Honesty notes below.
2. **Level 2 — constitutional institutions, then constitutional
   rights (14 lessons, stocked).** Morocco's Collège Year 2
   Social Studies book teaches citizenship as one of three equal
   strands, and its Citizenship Education strand runs in two
   axes: first the state's own machinery — the components of
   the Moroccan state, the constitution, the King, Parliament,
   the Government, the courts — then the citizen's rights and
   the bodies through which citizens act — civil and political
   rights, public freedoms, political parties, unions and
   professional bodies, the press, economic, social and cultural
   rights, local democracy, and associations. Every lesson
   stands on a **student book** and counts in the acceptance
   reckoning below.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
book's own shape, walked in order):

- *Institutions first, then rights:* "Start with the state
  itself — what it stands on, its constitution, its King, its
  Parliament, its Government, its courts. Then take your rights:
  the civil and political, the economic, social and cultural,
  your public freedoms — and the bodies that make them real:
  parties, unions, the press, your communal council, and the
  association you can found yourself."

## The ways of the books (their own shapes, preserved)

**Fadaa al-Ijtima'iyat, Collège Year 2** — citizenship is one of
three equal strands inside a single Social Studies book, and it
is taught from the state's own papers — nearly every lesson
works directly on constitutional articles, laws and official
documents (وثائق), so the pupil meets the constitution as a
working text, not a summary. Its ladder is institutions first
(what the state is, its constitution, king, parliament,
government, courts), then the citizen's rights and the bodies —
parties, unions, press, associations, local councils — through
which citizens act. *(Student book.)*
