# Civics and Citizenship — Bhutan — curriculum

One course for Bhutan inside the Civics and Citizenship
folder, built from Bhutan's own government textbooks:
the Values and Citizenship Education (VCE) student
workbooks and teachers' handbooks for Classes PP, I, II
and III (Royal Education Council, Paro, 2017), and the
Bhutan Civics coursebooks and teachers' guides for
Classes IX & X and XI & XII (Department of Curriculum
and Professional Development, Ministry of Education,
2022 reprints) — all English, all COMPLETE as filed (see
ABOUT.md for the full book list, the reading routes, and
the four UNREAD books that are NOT in this course). The
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's
place is found by what they prove. The path is
recommended, never forced — a learner can jump in
higher; the "start here instead" hints point down this
ladder. The grades the books come from appear only in
provenance (ABOUT.md and each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`).

The ladder follows Bhutan's own books' progression, as
the two filings' ladder sketches lay it out, reconciled
into one ladder: citizenship begins as five core values
practised by a young child in daily conduct (Level 1),
then the citizen's machinery opens with the Constitution,
rights and duties (Level 2), elections and parties
(Level 3), the working government (Level 4), and the
theoretical top — state, monarchy and public service
(Level 5). The first filing's sketch also names planned
rungs from the combined History and Civics books for
Classes VII and VIII (citizenship and the local
community; government, rights and the constitution) —
those books are UNREAD as filed (see ABOUT.md), so no
level in this course is stocked from them; the ladder as
built is the ladder the read books actually teach.

## Level 1 — Values at Home and School — 32 lessons

Bhutan's own books open here: Values and Citizenship Education teaches the youngest children five core values — Respect, Responsibility, Compassion, Integrity, Loyalty — as weekly practised activities at home and school, grounded by the teachers' handbooks in Gross National Happiness (head, hands and heart), and the same five-value ladder repeats class by class (PP, I, II, III) with new stories. Lessons: Values and Gross National Happiness · The Five Values and Their Parts · How a Values Lesson Is Built · Values Beyond the Classroom · What Respect Is · Courtesy and Good Manners · Learn Respect to Earn Respect · The Aims of Learning Respect · Respect for Others · Gratitude · Taking Turns · Punctuality · Doing Things on Time · Determination · Trying Until You Succeed · Duty and Right Conduct · Being Responsible · Generosity · Being Friendly · Sharing · Care · Caring for Living Things · Kindness · Helpfulness · Helping Others · Honesty · Being Honest · Trustworthiness · Honouring Teachers · Obedience · Obeying Elders · Loyalty and Its Parts.

## Level 2 — The Constitution, Rights and Duties — 3 lessons

The Classes IX–X coursebook opens the citizen's machinery with the Constitution itself: how it was made, its salient features, and the fundamental rights and duties it gives citizens — before any organ of government is taught. Lessons: How Bhutan's Constitution Was Made · Features of Bhutan's Constitution · Fundamental Rights and Duties.

## Level 3 — Elections and Parties — 2 lessons

Elections and parties come next in the coursebook's own order (its Unit Two): how political parties form and what they do, and how elections are conducted in Bhutan. Lessons: Forming Political Parties · How Elections Work.

## Level 4 — The Government — 6 lessons

The coursebook's Unit Three teaches the working government: Parliament, the executive, the judiciary, the constitutional bodies, local government at Dzongkhag/Gewog level, and Gross National Happiness as a principle of good governance. Lessons: The Legislature · The Executive · The Judiciary · The Constitutional Bodies · Local Government in Bhutan · Gross National Happiness and Good Governance.

## Level 5 — State, Monarchy and Public Service — 7 lessons

The Classes XI–XII coursebook is the theoretical top of the ladder: what society, a state and a nation are, forms of government, what a constitution is, the Druk Gyalpo's role in a democratic constitutional monarchy, the Prime Minister and Council of Ministers, the principles of state policy, and the apolitical civil service. Lessons: Society, State and Nation · Forms of Government · What a Constitution Is · The Monarch in a Democratic Constitutional Monarchy · The Prime Minister and the Council of Ministers · Principles of State Policy · The Civil Service.

**Totals: 50 lessons** (Level 1: 32 · Level 2: 3 · Level 3: 2 · Level 4: 6 · Level 5: 7 — from twelve read books: the VCE student workbooks for Classes PP, I, II and III and the Bhutan Civics coursebooks for Classes IX & X and XI & XII (student books), with their six teachers' handbooks/guides as labelled evidence; 124 placements stocking 50 lessons).
