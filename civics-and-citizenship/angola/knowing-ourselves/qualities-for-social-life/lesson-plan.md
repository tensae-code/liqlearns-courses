# Lesson plan — Qualities for Social Life

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Angola · **Title:** knowing-ourselves · **Lesson:** Qualities for Social Life
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — Knowing Ourselves (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a good citizen carries moral qualities into daily life — and I can judge my own conduct each day and do more at home.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — Knowing Ourselves — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The qualities of a good citizen** — Qualities such as honesty, respect, responsibility and kindness are what make living together possible.
2. **Judging my own conduct** — Each day I can look at what I did, name where I fell short, and choose a better act tomorrow — including doing more at home.

Gate pool: the prove-it check for each sub lesson is at most 5 questions, mixed with earlier parts, picked as the hardest from the rank right under the wanted sub lesson — drawn from this lesson's question files (questions/).

## Before this lesson
- The lessons before this one in path order are recommended first. Recommended, never forced — a learner may jump in here directly.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: a good citizen carries moral qualities into daily life — and I can judge my own conduct each day and do more at home.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street, school or community in Angola.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the learner's own life in Angola and the book's own teaching, as Angola's Educação Moral e Cívica books work tema by tema from self-knowledge outward.
