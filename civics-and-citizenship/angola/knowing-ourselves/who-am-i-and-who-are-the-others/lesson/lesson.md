# Who Am I and Who Are the Others

## Do it first

Say your name and one thing about you that is the same as a classmate's, and one thing that is different. Then name one person in your family and one thing you have received from them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Alike and different

We share much with others and differ from them too; naming both is the start of knowing ourselves.

## Sub lesson 2 — My heritages and my family

What I receive from my family and my past is part of who I am, and other families hand down their own heritages.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: each person is alike and different at once — knowing my heritages, my family and other families is how I learn who I am and who the others are. Give one real example from your own life in Angola, and name the lesson's key words: self, others, family, heritage. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Portuguese as well as in English — Angola's own books teach Educação Moral e Cívica in Portuguese, for life at home and in the community, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: each person is alike and different at once — knowing my heritages, my family and other families is how I learn who I am and who the others are.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
