# Hands-on — Who Am I and Who Are the Others

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say your name and one thing about you that is the same as a classmate's, and one thing that is different. Then name one person in your family and one thing you have received from them.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: each person is alike and different at once — knowing my heritages, my family and other families is how I learn who I am and who the others are. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your community. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
