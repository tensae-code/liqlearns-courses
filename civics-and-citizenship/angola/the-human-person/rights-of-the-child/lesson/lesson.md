# Rights of the Child

## Do it first

Name three rights you hold as a child. For each one, name the adult or place that protects it in your life.

## Sub lesson 1 — The rights of the child

A child holds rights from birth: to be cared for, protected, taught and heard.

## Sub lesson 2 — Rights in daily life

Those rights are real in ordinary places — at home, at school and in the street — not only in books.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: every child has rights — to care, protection, schooling and a name — simply because the child is a child. Give one real example from your own life in Angola, and name the lesson's key words: children's rights, protection, care. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Portuguese as well as in English — Angola's own books teach Educação Moral e Cívica in Portuguese, for life at home and in the community, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: every child has rights — to care, protection, schooling and a name — simply because the child is a child.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
