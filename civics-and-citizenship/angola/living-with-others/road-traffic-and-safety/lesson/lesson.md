# Road Traffic and Safety

## Do it first

Walk (in your mind) from home to school. Name every traffic sign or danger you pass, and say what a pedestrian must do at each.

## Sub lesson 1 — The elements of traffic

Traffic is made of pedestrians, vehicles and signs; each sign speaks to everyone on the road.

## Sub lesson 2 — Rights and duties on the road

Pedestrian and driver, motorist, motorcyclist and cyclist — each has rights on the road and duties that keep it safe.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the road has its elements — pedestrians, vehicles and traffic signs — and pedestrians and drivers each hold rights and duties there. Give one real example from your own life in Angola, and name the lesson's key words: traffic, pedestrian, safety, signs. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Portuguese as well as in English — Angola's own books teach Educação Moral e Cívica in Portuguese, for life at home and in the community, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: the road has its elements — pedestrians, vehicles and traffic signs — and pedestrians and drivers each hold rights and duties there.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
