# Tolerance Makes a Good Democrat

## Do it first

Name one way a classmate differs from you. Say one tolerant act toward that difference, and one intolerant act to avoid.

## Sub lesson 1 — Tolerance and intolerance

Tolerance lets different people live together in peace; intolerance pushes others out and harms everyone.

## Sub lesson 2 — Tolerance as a virtue

Putting up with difference calmly — and respecting it — is part of what makes a good democrat.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: tolerance is a democratic virtue — when I am tolerant of people who differ from me, I can be a good democrat. Give one real example from your own life in Angola, and name the lesson's key words: tolerance, democracy, respect. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Portuguese as well as in English — Angola's own books teach Educação Moral e Cívica in Portuguese, for life at home and in the community, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: tolerance is a democratic virtue — when I am tolerant of people who differ from me, I can be a good democrat.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
