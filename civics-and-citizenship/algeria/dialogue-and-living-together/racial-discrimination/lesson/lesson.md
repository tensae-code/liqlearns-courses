# Racial Discrimination

## Do it first

Imagine someone refused a place, a game or a kind word only because of where their family comes from or how they look. There is a name for that unfairness, and this lesson teaches it — so it can be refused.

## Naming the wrong

Racial discrimination (التمييز العنصري) is treating a person worse — excluding, insulting or denying them their share — because of their race or origin. The book names it plainly, in a field about living together, because living together fails wherever it stands.

## Refusing it is part of living together

To refuse discrimination is to treat every person by their worth, not their origin: include them, speak fairly of them, and stand against the unfair act when you see it. Equality in dignity is the ground dialogue stands on.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: racial discrimination is treating people unfairly because of their race — the book names it so it can be refused: people are equal in dignity whatever their origin. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: discrimination, equality, dignity. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
