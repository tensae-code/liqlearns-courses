# Hands-on — Dialogue and Why It Matters

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Think of a time two people disagreed and talked it through instead of fighting. What did the talking do? This lesson names it: dialogue — and why it matters.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: dialogue is people talking and listening together to understand each other and solve things — and it matters because it is how people settle matters without quarrelling. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
