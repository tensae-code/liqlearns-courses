# Landmarks of My Homeland

## Do it first

Think of the oldest place you know in your homeland — an old ruin, monument or ancient site. Say what you think it shows about the people who lived there long ago. This lesson names such places: the archaeological landmarks of my homeland.

## Old places that tell the homeland's story

Archaeological landmarks (المعالم الأثرية) are the old sites and remains in our homeland — places built long ago that still stand and show how people lived in earlier times.

## Landmarks are heritage in stone

These landmarks are part of the national heritage: they prove the homeland's long history, and every citizen can point to them with pride — and with a duty to protect them.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: my homeland has archaeological landmarks — old places and remains that show its long history, and they belong to its heritage. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: landmarks, archaeological, homeland. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
