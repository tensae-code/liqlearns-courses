# Hands-on — Landmarks of My Homeland

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Think of the oldest place you know in your homeland — an old ruin, monument or ancient site. Say what you think it shows about the people who lived there long ago. This lesson names such places: the archaeological landmarks of my homeland.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: my homeland has archaeological landmarks — old places and remains that show its long history, and they belong to its heritage. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
