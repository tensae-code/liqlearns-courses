# Our Heritage Is a Treasure

## Do it first

Look around your home and street. Name three old things — a building, an object, a custom — that were there before you were born and that people still value. This lesson names all of them together: our heritage.

## Heritage is what the past hands to the present

Heritage — تراثنا — is everything valuable that earlier generations made, built, practised and kept, and then handed down to us: old buildings and objects, crafts, customs, arts and knowledge.

## A treasure we did not make, but must keep

The book calls heritage a precious treasure (كنز ثمين) because we did not create it ourselves — we received it. A treasure is not spent carelessly; it is guarded, valued and passed on again.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: our heritage is a precious treasure — the valuable things our homeland's past generations made, kept and handed down to us. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: heritage, treasure, handed down. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: our heritage is a precious treasure — the valuable things our homeland's past generations made, kept and handed down to us.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
