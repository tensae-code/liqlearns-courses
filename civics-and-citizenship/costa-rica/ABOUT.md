# About the Civics and Citizenship — Costa Rica course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Educación Cívica (Civic Education) books. The one
exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us,
the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are
the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Costa Rica inside the Civics and
Citizenship folder (one course per country). Inside it, the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place
is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the
ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced
gate. The school grades the books come from appear only in
each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

Costa Rica's own way, preserved whole, as filed: the CONED
distance-learning anthologies teach civics as the young
citizen's immediate safety and belonging first (Grade 7:
safe communities, road culture, disaster risk), widening
year by year to identity, gender equity, intercultural
living, human rights, and finally the political system
itself. Grade 8 turns inward and outward at once — identity
(personal, age-group, communal, cultural, national, taught
through Costa Rican symbols from the flag to the carreta
típica and the pre-Columbian spheres), then gender identity
rebuilt for equity under named Costa Rican laws, then
living interculturally in one society. Grade 9 is the
machinery year — participation practised first in the
student government and the local community, human rights in
their three generations with Costa Rica's own protection
mechanisms, and democracy strengthened through political
control and the intermediary organisations of Costa Rican
society. The senior book (Grades 10–11, shared) studies the
world's political regimes and ideologies against Costa
Rica's own presidential democracy — its institutions, the
abolition of the army, its electoral system and parties —
and ends with the state as guarantor of equal opportunity
(health, education, work) and the young citizen's democratic
attitudes, leadership, deliberation and negotiation.

The course as built has **five stocked levels**, from the
filing for this build (`costa-rica-placements.md` — 4
records / 4 distinct books, all fetched from their recorded
CONED/UNED source URLs after the GitHub tree grab failed

file's byte size matches its record exactly (7,610,620 /
4,109,605 / 5,976,462 / 8,478,819), and title pages were
verified. These books are recorded as pushed, so the
fetched copies were deleted after reading, not staged).
Track: standalone, as filed for every book. Self-assessment
exercises and answer keys are inventory-only, not placed,
as filed. 77 placements in all, stocking 77 lessons — the
coined lesson names were deduped before building: every
coined name in the filing was already distinct, no name is
used twice, and every placement stocks into exactly one
lesson:

1. **Level 1 — Safe Communities, the Road and Emergencies
   (16 lessons).** What Educación Cívica studies and the
   citizen's responsibilities, citizen security, national
   security and its institutions, and building safe
   environments; what road safety is, road safety in Costa
   Rica, the Traffic Law, responsible drivers and traffic
   signs, Costa Rica's road system, and peaceful living on
   the road; what disaster prevention is, natural and
   human-made events and risk, the emergency law and the
   National Emergencies Commission, and risk management in
   my community and at school (Grade 7 book, Unidades Uno,
   Dos and Tres).
2. **Level 2 — Identity, Gender Equity and Intercultural
   Living (17 lessons).** The identity concepts, the
   educational institution and its alternative
   conflict-resolution mechanisms, the community and
   communal life and traditions, the elements of national
   identity, and people of patriotic value; the gender
   concepts, gender identities, the socialising agents,
   the laws for gender equity, everyday life and
   co-responsibility, and citizenship and sexism;
   intercultural concepts, the laws on rights and duties,
   intercultural enrichment, and peaceful conflict handling
   (Grade 8 book, Unidades Uno, Dos and Tres).
3. **Level 3 — Participation, Human Rights and Democracy
   (21 lessons).** The basic concepts of participation,
   student elections and other forms of participation at
   school, student government and local government, the
   young adult in the community, development associations
   and groups, and local citizen participation mechanisms;
   the basic concepts of human rights, what human rights
   are, the Universal Declaration and the three
   generations, the laws and mechanisms that protect them,
   rights in Costa Rica's Constitution, and collective
   rights and the state; the basic concepts of democracy,
   why democracy matters, democracy's origins, contemporary
   democracy in Costa Rica, horizontal and vertical
   political control, and the intermediary organisations
   (Grade 9 book, Capítulos uno, dos and tres).
4. **Level 4 — Political Regimes, Costa Rican Democracy
   and Elections (14 lessons).** What a political regime
   is, authoritarianism and populism, democracy and its
   characteristics, liberalism, socialism and anarchism,
   and social Christianity and fascism; the Costa Rican
   political system, Costa Rica's public institutions,
   political communication and agendas, and challenges and
   public accountability; the electoral system and
   electoral culture, elective offices and who may stand,
   suffrage and the direct mechanisms, political parties,
   and campaigns, propaganda and government programmes
   (shared Grades 10–11 book, Décimo año Capítulos 1, 2
   and 3).
5. **Level 5 — Equal Opportunity and Democratic Practices
   (9 lessons).** The state and public policy, health
   policy (La Caja and the EBAIS clinics), education
   policy, work and employment policy, and the state's
   challenges — poverty and school dropout; democratic
   values and anti-values, citizen projects in the
   community, democratic leadership, and deliberation and
   negotiation (shared Grades 10–11 book, Undécimo año
   Capítulos 4 and 5).

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later;
the books' own shape, walked in order):

- *Costa Rica's own order:* "First, the safety of the
  places you actually live: your community kept safe, the
  road you travel, and the emergency you prepare for. Then
  who you are — your identity and Costa Rica's, symbol by
  symbol — gender rebuilt for equity under the law, and
  living with other cultures in one society. Then taking
  part: student elections, local government, your rights
  in three generations and the mechanisms that protect
  them, and democracy defended from above and below. Then
  the political system whole: the world's regimes and
  ideologies measured against Costa Rica's own
  institutions, its elections and its parties. And at the
  top, the state guaranteeing equal opportunity — health,
  education, work — and you practising democracy yourself:
  values, projects, leadership, deliberation and
  negotiation."
