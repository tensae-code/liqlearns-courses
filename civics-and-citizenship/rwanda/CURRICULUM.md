# Civics and Citizenship — Rwanda course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Rwanda inside the Civics and Citizenship
folder, organised by **levels, not grades**: a learner climbs
when they have proved the level, not when a school calendar
says so. The school grades the books come from appear only in
each lesson's sources (provenance), never as the course's
organisation. The path follows Rwanda's own order as its
History and Citizenship books lay it out — citizenship taught
inside the national story: democracy, rights and duties first,
then the state and unity, then rights as instruments,
democratisation and Rwanda in the region, closing on
tolerance and respect. The filing for this build
(`rwanda-placements-b.md`) carries all 49 placements from
three read books (one student book, two teacher's guides),
and every placement stocks into exactly one lesson, so the
course holds 49 lessons.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own History and Citizenship books — except the **Tip
boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'. See
ABOUT.md for the full book list and the acceptance
reckoning. **Levels 2–3 of this course rest on teacher
guides alone — the S2 and S3 student books are filed
RECORD-ONLY, UNREAD (corrupt downloads) — and those levels
are labelled guides-only throughout.**

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **Democracy, Rights, Peace and Rwandan Values** — what
   democracy means, its principles and its forms; the
   concept of human rights, preventing violations, and the
   duties and responsibilities of a citizen; conflict and
   violence — what they are, their causes, consequences
   and transformation; genocide — its meaning, features
   and stages, set beside other mass crimes; dignity and
   self-reliance in Rwandan society, the government's
   measures for it and what it implies for society;
   disability — its concept and types, causes and
   consequences, and prevention inside inclusive
   education; and values and their sources, closing on
   Rwandan cultural values and taboos. 19 lessons, in six
   titles: `democracy/` (1 lesson), `rights-and-duties/`
   (3 lessons), `peace-and-conflict/` (7 lessons),
   `dignity-and-self-reliance/` (3 lessons), `inclusion/`
   (3 lessons), `family-and-values/` (2 lessons).
2. **The State, Unity and Social Cohesion** — the duties
   of a citizen toward the nation, the obligations of the
   state toward its population, and how duties and
   obligations are balanced in Rwanda; state and
   government defined and distinguished, types of states
   and forms of government, and the organs, role and
   functions of the state and of government;
   interdependence and unity, personal and national
   identity (being Umunyarwanda) and patriotism, unity in
   diversity, and social cohesion with its factors and
   challenges; the hindrances to dignity and self-reliance
   and the activities that promote them; causes and
   effects of disability and measures to prevent it; and
   conflict and dialogue between parents and children.
   15 lessons, in six titles: `rights-and-duties/`
   (3 lessons), `the-state/` (3 lessons), `unity/`
   (4 lessons), `dignity-and-self-reliance/` (2 lessons),
   `inclusion/` (2 lessons), `family-and-values/`
   (1 lesson). **This level rests on a teacher's guide
   alone — evidence only, acceptance 0.**
3. **Rights as Instruments, Democratisation and Rwanda in
   the Region** — national unity after the Genocide
   against the Tutsi; national and international human
   rights instruments; the democratisation process, its
   indicators, and Rwanda compared with the sub-region;
   understanding oneself in reference to Rwanda and East
   Africa, and the importance of regional integration;
   conflict transformation through national laws — its
   definition, the sources of Rwandan codes and laws, and
   the legal mechanisms and organs; the political,
   economic and socio-cultural factors for national
   independence, and promoting and sustaining
   self-reliance; the categories of children with special
   needs, ways to help them and the impact of inclusive
   education; and tolerance and respect — the effects of
   bias, prejudice, intolerance and stigma on individuals
   and families. 15 lessons, in six titles:
   `peace-and-conflict/` (3 lessons), `rights-and-duties/`
   (2 lessons), `democracy/` (2 lessons), `unity/`
   (2 lessons), `dignity-and-self-reliance/` (3 lessons),
   `inclusion/` (2 lessons), `family-and-values/`
   (1 lesson). **This level rests on a teacher's guide
   alone — evidence only, acceptance 0.**

**Totals: 49 lessons** (Level 1: 19 · Level 2: 15 ·
Level 3: 15 — from three read books: the History and
Citizenship Senior One Student's Book (REB, 2024, COMPLETE

are RECORD-ONLY, UNREAD; 49 placements stocking
49 lessons).
