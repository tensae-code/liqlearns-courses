# Civics and Citizenship — Rwanda course — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

Civics belongs to the country: this course is Rwanda's own —
built from Rwanda's History and Citizenship books (Rwanda
Basic Education Board: the Senior One Student's Book, 2024,
and the Senior 2 and Senior 3 Teacher's Guides, 2020,
English). Inside the course the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: the titles below ARE the levels'
titles, in path order — what the learner proves, step by
step. The grades the books come from live only in each
lesson's sources.

The path's own logic is Rwanda's, from the filing's ladder
sketch: citizenship is taught inside the national story —
democracy, rights and duties arrive after the kingdom and
pre-colonial Rwanda units, and the ladder runs through
genocide, dignity and self-reliance (Agaciro) to family
values: the citizen is formed as a Rwandan first. Level 1
is the Senior One student book (democracy; rights with
duties; conflict, violence and genocide; dignity and
self-reliance; inclusion; family values); Level 2 (Senior
Two, teacher's-guide evidence) adds the state itself and
balances the citizen's duties against the state's
obligations, with unity and social cohesion as the glue;
Level 3 (Senior Three, teacher's-guide evidence) places
Rwanda's citizen in the region and the world — rights as
instruments, democracy as a process, conflict answered by
national law — and closes on tolerance and respect. That
ladder IS the path order (recommended, never forced — a
learner can jump in higher; the "start here instead" hints
point down this ladder). Level 1 stands on a student book;
Levels 2–3 stand on teacher guides — evidence only,
acceptance 0, labelled in every lesson's sources.

## The titles

**Level 1 — democracy, rights, peace and Rwandan values**
(the Senior One books' order):

- `democracy/` — Level 1. Forms and Principles of
  Democracy (what democracy means, its principles, and its
  forms — direct and representative). This sits first
  because Rwanda's own course opens its citizenship strand
  with democracy itself.
- `rights-and-duties/` — Level 1. Concept of Human Rights
  (the concept and characteristics of human rights) ·
  Preventing Rights Violations (ways of preventing human
  rights violations) · Citizen Duties and Responsibilities
  (the duties and responsibilities of a citizen). Rights
  arrive together with duties, as the book teaches them.
- `peace-and-conflict/` — Level 1. Conflict and Violence
  (what they are and how they appear) · Causes of Conflict
  · Consequences of Conflict · Conflict Transformation ·
  Meaning of Genocide · Features and Stages of Genocide ·
  Genocide and Mass Crimes (genocide set beside other mass
  crimes). The ladder runs through conflict and genocide
  before it can build peace.
- `dignity-and-self-reliance/` — Level 1. Dignity and
  Self-Reliance (as Rwandan values) · Government Measures
  for Self-Reliance (the measures the Government of Rwanda
  has put in place) · Implications for Society (what
  dignity and self-reliance imply for Rwandan society).
- `inclusion/` — Level 1. Concept and Types of Disability
  · Causes and Consequences of Disability · Preventing
  Disability (inside inclusive education).
- `family-and-values/` — Level 1. Values and Their
  Sources (what values are and where they come from) ·
  Rwandan Cultural Values and Taboos. The first level
  closes where the citizen is formed: the family.

**Level 2 — the state, unity and social cohesion** (the
Senior Two books' order; teacher's-guide evidence):

- `rights-and-duties/` — Level 2. Duties Toward the Nation
  · Obligations of the State (toward its population) ·
  Balancing Duties and Obligations (how the two are
  balanced in Rwanda). The citizen's duties deepen into a
  two-way account with the state.
- `the-state/` — Level 2. State and Government (their
  definitions and the difference between them) · Types of
  States and Forms of Government · Organs and Functions
  (the organs, role and functions of the state and of
  government). New at this level: the state itself.
- `unity/` — Level 2. Interdependence and Unity · Identity
  and Patriotism (personal identity, national identity —
  being Umunyarwanda — and patriotism) · Unity in
  Diversity · Social Cohesion (what it is, the factors
  that influence it and the challenges to it).
- `dignity-and-self-reliance/` — Level 2. Hindrances to
  Dignity (the hindrances, why dignity matters, and
  international cooperation toward Rwandan aspirations) ·
  Promoting Dignity (activities that promote dignity and
  self-reliance, and the challenges to them).
- `inclusion/` — Level 2. Causes and Effects of Disability
  · Measures to Prevent Disability.
- `family-and-values/` — Level 2. Parents and Children
  (conflict and misunderstanding between them, and
  dialogue that develops respect for each other's rights
  to hold different values).

**Level 3 — rights as instruments, democratisation and
Rwanda in the region** (the Senior Three books' order;
teacher's-guide evidence):

- `peace-and-conflict/` — Level 3. National Unity (after
  the Genocide against the Tutsi) · Conflict
  Transformation and Law (conflict transformation defined,
  and the sources of Rwandan codes and laws) · Legal
  Mechanisms (the legal mechanisms and organs in conflict
  transformation). Conflict returns, now answered by
  national law.
- `rights-and-duties/` — Level 3. National Rights
  Instruments (national human rights instruments and the
  protection of human rights) · International Rights
  Instruments. Rights become instruments, national and
  international.
- `democracy/` — Level 3. Democratisation Process (the
  process and its indicators) · Democratisation Compared
  (Rwanda compared with the sub-region). Democracy returns
  as a living process.
- `unity/` — Level 3. Rwanda and East Africa
  (understanding oneself in reference to Rwanda and East
  Africa) · Regional Integration (its importance in East
  Africa). Unity widens to the region.
- `dignity-and-self-reliance/` — Level 3. Political
  Factors of Independence · Economic and Cultural Factors
  (economic and socio-cultural factors for national
  independence) · Sustaining Self-Reliance (promoting and
  sustaining it). Self-reliance becomes national
  independence.
- `inclusion/` — Level 3. Children with Special Needs
  (their different categories) · Helping and Inclusive
  Education (ways to help learners with special needs and
  the impact of inclusive education).
- `family-and-values/` — Level 3. Tolerance and Respect
  (the effects of bias, prejudice, intolerance and stigma
  on individuals and families). The course closes where it
  began — with people living together — now at the level
  of tolerance and respect.
