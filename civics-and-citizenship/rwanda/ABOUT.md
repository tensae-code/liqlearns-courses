# About the Civics and Citizenship — Rwanda course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own History and Citizenship books. The one exception is the
**Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Rwanda inside the Civics and
Citizenship folder (one course per country). Inside it, the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place
is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the
ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced
gate. The school grades the books come from appear only in
each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

Rwanda's books are combined **History and Citizenship**
books: citizenship is taught inside the national story.
Only the citizenship strand is placed in this course; the
history units are named in each book's contents map below
and are not placed — they belong to the History course.

The course as built has **three stocked levels**, from the
single filing for this build (`rwanda-placements-b.md` —
0 census records / 30 link leads triaged, the leads being
15 REB e-learning files each carried twice; 3 distinct
books read, 2 further student books filed RECORD-ONLY and
UNREAD; 49 placements in all, stocking 49 lessons — every
placement stocks into exactly one lesson):

1. **Level 1 — Democracy, Rights, Peace and Rwandan
   Values (19 lessons).** The Senior One student book's
   citizenship strand: forms and principles of democracy;
   human rights, preventing violations, and citizen duties
   and responsibilities; conflict and violence — its forms,
   causes, consequences and transformation — and genocide,
   its meaning, features and stages, set beside other mass
   crimes; dignity and self-reliance (Agaciro) and the
   government's measures for it; disability and inclusive
   education; and family and personal values, closing on
   Rwandan cultural values and taboos.
2. **Level 2 — The State, Unity and Social Cohesion
   (15 lessons).** The Senior Two year, on teacher's-guide
   evidence: the citizen's duties toward the nation set
   against the obligations of the state, and how the two
   are balanced; the state and government themselves —
   definitions, types, organs and functions; interdependence
   and unity, personal and national identity (being
   Umunyarwanda) and patriotism, unity in diversity and
   social cohesion; the hindrances to dignity and
   self-reliance and the activities that promote them;
   disability again, deeper; and parents and children —
   conflict, misunderstanding and dialogue.
3. **Level 3 — Rights as Instruments, Democratisation
   and Rwanda in the Region (15 lessons).** The Senior
   Three year, on teacher's-guide evidence: national unity
   after the Genocide against the Tutsi; human rights as
   national and international instruments; the
   democratisation process and Rwanda compared with the
   sub-region; Rwandans in reference to East Africa and
   regional integration; conflict transformation through
   national laws and their legal mechanisms; the political,
   economic and socio-cultural factors for national
   independence and sustaining self-reliance; children with
   special needs and inclusive education; and tolerance and
   respect — the effects of bias, prejudice, intolerance
   and stigma on individuals and families.

Rwanda's own way, preserved whole, as filed: citizenship
is taught inside the national story — democracy, rights
and duties arrive after the kingdom and pre-colonial
Rwanda units, and the ladder runs through genocide,
dignity and self-reliance (Agaciro) to family values: the
citizen is formed as a Rwandan first. In Senior 2 the
citizen meets the state itself — rights balanced against
duties and the state's obligations, then unity in
diversity and social cohesion as the glue, before the
return to dignity, disability and family. Senior 3 turns
outward and upward — rights become instruments (national
and international), democracy becomes a process Rwanda is
compared on, and the citizen is placed inside East Africa
and inside Rwanda's own laws for transforming conflict.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later;
the books' own shape, walked in order):

- *A Rwandan first, then a citizen of the region:*
  "First, the ground you stand on: what democracy is and
  the forms it takes; your human rights, how violations
  are prevented, and the duties you owe as a citizen; what
  conflict and violence are, where they come from and
  what they cost — and genocide, named and understood, so
  it never returns; dignity and self-reliance, the worth
  Rwanda calls Agaciro; disability and the inclusive
  school; and the values your family and culture hand you.
  Then the state itself: your duties to the nation and
  the nation's obligations to you, what a state and a
  government are, unity in diversity and the cohesion
  that holds a society together. And last, the wide
  view: your rights written into national and
  international instruments, democracy as a living
  process, Rwanda inside East Africa, conflict answered
  by law, independence kept by self-reliance — and
  tolerance and respect for every person."
