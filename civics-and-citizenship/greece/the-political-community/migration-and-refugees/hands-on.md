# Hands-on — Migration and Refugees

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
On a map, trace one journey a family might make to start life in a new country. Write down why they left, what they gain, what they lose, and what the new place gains and must manage. This lesson gives that journey its categories and its rules.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: migration has categories and causes and carries social and economic consequences for both the places left and the places reached — and refugees, people fleeing danger, stand under special protection, with the Schengen agreement governing movement in much of Europe. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
