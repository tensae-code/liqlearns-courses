# Social Problems

## Do it first

Name one problem in your town that no single family caused and no single family can fix. This lesson gives such problems their method: define them, find their causes, weigh their consequences, and tackle them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Defining a social problem

A social problem is a harmful condition affecting many people, recognised as calling for common action — not merely a private trouble.

The definition matters: what a society names a problem, it organises to solve.

## Sub lesson 2 — Causes and consequences

Social problems have causes that can be traced and consequences that spread — to families, to the economy, to trust in common life.

Tracing both stops the argument at blame and starts it at remedy.

## Sub lesson 3 — Tackling them — poverty and unemployment

Poverty and unemployment are the book's standing examples: conditions to be tackled by policy, work, education and solidarity together.

Tackling is the citizen's part of the lesson: problems named and understood can be acted on.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: a social problem is a condition that harms many people and calls for common action — it has causes and consequences that can be named, and it can be tackled, as poverty and unemployment show. Give one real example from your own life or from the Greece you live in, and name the lesson's key words: social problems, poverty, unemployment. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
