# Lesson plan — Social Problems

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Greece · **Title:** The Political Community · **Lesson:** Social Problems
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — Society, the State and the Economy, and the Free, Responsible and Active Citizen (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a social problem is a condition that harms many people and calls for common action — it has causes and consequences that can be named, and it can be tackled, as poverty and unemployment show.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — Society, the State and the Economy, and the Free, Responsible and Active Citizen — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Defining a social problem** — A social problem is a harmful condition affecting many people, recognised as calling for common action — not merely a private trouble.
2. **Causes and consequences** — Social problems have causes that can be traced and consequences that spread — to families, to the economy, to trust in common life.
3. **Tackling them — poverty and unemployment** — Poverty and unemployment are the book's standing examples: conditions to be tackled by policy, work, education and solidarity together.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: a social problem is a condition that harms many people and calls for common action — it has causes and consequences that can be named, and it can be tackled, as poverty and unemployment show.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or town — or, for this book's lessons, from the public life of Greece they live in.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the learner's own life in Greece and the book's own teaching, as the book itself works.
