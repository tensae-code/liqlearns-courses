# Hands-on — Elections, Parties and Media

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Imagine election day in your town. Write down: who you can choose between, how you learned what each side promises, and who tells you the result. This lesson follows those three actors: elections, parties and the media.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: elections let citizens choose their rulers, political parties organise choices and programmes, and the mass media carry information — and influence — between citizens and power. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
