# Elections, Parties and Media

## Do it first

Imagine election day in your town. Write down: who you can choose between, how you learned what each side promises, and who tells you the result. This lesson follows those three actors: elections, parties and the media.

## Sub lesson 1 — Elections

Elections are how citizens choose who will govern and can remove them peacefully at the next vote.

A real election offers a real choice, honestly counted.

## Sub lesson 2 — Political parties

Parties gather people who share a programme for the country, present candidates, and organise the choice elections offer.

Without parties, voters would face scattered individuals instead of clear alternatives.

## Sub lesson 3 — The mass media

The mass media (MME) inform citizens about public life — and also influence opinion by what they show and how they show it.

A careful citizen compares sources and separates reporting from persuasion.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: elections let citizens choose their rulers, political parties organise choices and programmes, and the mass media carry information — and influence — between citizens and power. Give one real example from your own life or from the Greece you live in, and name the lesson's key words: elections, political parties, mass media. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
