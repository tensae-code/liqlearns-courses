# Hands-on — The Individual and the State

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Think of the place you live. Write down: who keeps it safe, who makes its rules, who pays for its roads and schools — and what you yourself owe to that shared life. This lesson names that organised community: the state.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: a state (a Politeia) is an organised political community, and a citizen belongs to it with a real relation to it — not as a bystander, but as a member. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
