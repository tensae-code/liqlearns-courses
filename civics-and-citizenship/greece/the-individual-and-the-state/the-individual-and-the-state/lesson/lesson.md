# The Individual and the State

## Do it first

Think of the place you live. Write down: who keeps it safe, who makes its rules, who pays for its roads and schools — and what you yourself owe to that shared life. This lesson names that organised community: the state.

## Sub lesson 1 — What the state is

The state is an organised political community: people living together under common rules and common institutions.

It is not just a building or an office — it is the organised form of a people's shared life.

## Sub lesson 2 — The citizen's relation to the state

A citizen is a member of the state, not a stranger to it: the state protects the citizen, and the citizen takes part in and supports the common life.

Belonging runs both ways — protection and services one way, participation and duty the other.

## Sub lesson 3 — Why the relation matters

When citizens understand the state, they can use its institutions, judge them, and improve them instead of standing outside them.

That understanding is the starting point for every lesson that follows in this level.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: a state (a Politeia) is an organised political community, and a citizen belongs to it with a real relation to it — not as a bystander, but as a member. Give one real example from your own life or from the Greece you live in, and name the lesson's key words: state, Politeia, citizen. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
