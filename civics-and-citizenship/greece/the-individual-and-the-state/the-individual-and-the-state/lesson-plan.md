## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What the state is** — The state is an organised political community: people living together under common rules and common institutions.
2. **The citizen's relation to the state** — A citizen is a member of the state, not a stranger to it: the state protects the citizen, and the citizen takes part in and supports the common life.
3. **Why the relation matters** — When citizens understand the state, they can use its institutions, judge them, and improve them instead of standing outside them.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the state is only the government of the day → the state is wider and lasts longer than any one government: it is the organised political community itself, and governments serve inside it.
- Go back to the one thing: a state (a Politeia) is an organised political community, and a citizen belongs to it with a real relation to it — not as a bystander, but as a member. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
