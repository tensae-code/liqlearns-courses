# Rights and Obligations

## Do it first

Make two lists about yourself as a citizen: what you may claim (to learn, to speak, to be safe) and what you owe (to obey the law, to respect others, to contribute). This lesson shows the two lists belong to one citizenship.

## Sub lesson 1 — Rights of the citizen

Citizens hold rights — to life, freedom, education, expression and more — that the state must respect and protect.

Rights are not gifts from the government of the day; they belong to the citizen.

## Sub lesson 2 — Obligations of the citizen

Citizens also owe obligations: to obey the law, respect the rights of others, defend the country and contribute to common costs.

Obligations are the price of a shared life that protects everyone.

## Sub lesson 3 — Rights and obligations together

A healthy state keeps the pair in balance: rights claimed with responsibility, obligations demanded with respect for rights.

That balance is the citizen's daily practice, not only a text in the Constitution.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: citizens hold rights the state must respect, and obligations they owe in return — the two travel together. Give one real example from your own life or from the Greece you live in, and name the lesson's key words: rights, obligations, citizen. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
