# Hands-on — Regimes and the Constitution

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
List three different ways a country could choose its rulers — one person ruling alone, a few ruling, or the people ruling. This lesson names those forms of government and shows the book that stands above them all: the constitution.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: countries are ruled under different forms of government (politeumata), and a constitution is the highest set of rules that organises the state and limits its power. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
