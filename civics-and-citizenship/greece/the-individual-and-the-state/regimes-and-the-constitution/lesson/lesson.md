# Regimes and the Constitution

## Do it first

List three different ways a country could choose its rulers — one person ruling alone, a few ruling, or the people ruling. This lesson names those forms of government and shows the book that stands above them all: the constitution.

## Sub lesson 1 — Forms of government

A politeuma is the form a state's government takes — who holds power, how they get it, and how they may use it.

Comparing forms of government means asking the same questions of each: who rules, by what right, and with what limits.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Constitution

The Constitution is the state's highest law: it organises the institutions, divides power, and protects the citizen's basic rights.

Every other law and every act of government must agree with it.

## Sub lesson 3 — Constitution and citizen

Because the Constitution limits power, it is the citizen's shield as well as the state's plan.

Knowing it tells a citizen both how the state must work and what no one may do to them.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: countries are ruled under different forms of government (politeumata), and a constitution is the highest set of rules that organises the state and limits its power. Give one real example from your own life or from the Greece you live in, and name the lesson's key words: politeuma, constitution, form of government. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
