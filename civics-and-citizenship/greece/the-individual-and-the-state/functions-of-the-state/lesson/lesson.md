# Functions of the State

## Do it first

Think of one rule that affects your day. Write down: who made it, who makes sure it is kept, and who would decide if people argued about it. This lesson shows those are three different functions of one state.

## Sub lesson 1 — The law-making function

One function of the state is to make the laws — the general rules everyone lives under.

In Greece this work belongs to the elected Parliament.

## Sub lesson 2 — The executive function

A second function carries the laws out: governing day to day, running services, and keeping the country safe.

This is the work of the government and the administration it directs.

## Sub lesson 3 — The judging function

A third function judges: courts settle disputes and decide cases under the laws, independently.

Independence matters — a judge must follow the law, not the wishes of the powerful.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the state does its work through distinct functions — making laws, carrying them out, and judging disputes under them. Give one real example from your own life or from the Greece you live in, and name the lesson's key words: functions of the state, legislature, executive, judiciary. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
