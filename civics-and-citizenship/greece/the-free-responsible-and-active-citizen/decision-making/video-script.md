# Video script — Decision-Making

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Decisions — individual and collective — are made well by criteria: naming the options, weighing them against values and rules, and keeping the collective interest in view.

Some decisions are individual — one person's to make; others are collective — a group's, a community's, a state's — and the two kinds ask different care.

A collective decision binds people who did not all choose it, so its making must be fair as well as wise.

Right decisions are tested against criteria: the facts, the values at stake, and the rules that bind the deciders.

Naming the criteria before choosing stops impulse from dressing up as judgement.

In collective decisions the measure is the collective interest — the good of the whole the decision binds — not the advantage of the strongest part.

That measure is the citizen-decider's compass in this lesson.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
