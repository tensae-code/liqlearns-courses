# Decision-Making

## Do it first

Recall a decision your class or family made together that went well — and one that went badly. Write down what differed. This lesson turns that difference into a method for deciding.

## Sub lesson 1 — Individual and collective decisions

Some decisions are individual — one person's to make; others are collective — a group's, a community's, a state's — and the two kinds ask different care.

A collective decision binds people who did not all choose it, so its making must be fair as well as wise.

## Sub lesson 2 — Criteria, values and rules

Right decisions are tested against criteria: the facts, the values at stake, and the rules that bind the deciders.

Naming the criteria before choosing stops impulse from dressing up as judgement.

## Sub lesson 3 — The collective interest

In collective decisions the measure is the collective interest — the good of the whole the decision binds — not the advantage of the strongest part.

That measure is the citizen-decider's compass in this lesson.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: decisions — individual and collective — are made well by criteria: naming the options, weighing them against values and rules, and keeping the collective interest in view. Give one real example from your own life or from the Greece you live in, and name the lesson's key words: decision-making, criteria, collective interest. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
