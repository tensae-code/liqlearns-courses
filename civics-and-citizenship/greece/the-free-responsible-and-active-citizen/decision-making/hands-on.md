# Hands-on — Decision-Making

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Recall a decision your class or family made together that went well — and one that went badly. Write down what differed. This lesson turns that difference into a method for deciding.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: decisions — individual and collective — are made well by criteria: naming the options, weighing them against values and rules, and keeping the collective interest in view. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
