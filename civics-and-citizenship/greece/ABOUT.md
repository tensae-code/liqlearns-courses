# About the Civics and Citizenship — Greece course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this
course comes from government textbooks — this country's own
Social and Political Education book for the Gymnasium and its
Political Education (Πολιτική Παιδεία) book for the Lykeio. The
one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us,
the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the
books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Greece inside the Civics and Citizenship
folder (one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is
by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what
they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a
recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school grades
the books come from appear only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

The course as built has **three stocked levels**, following
Greece's own order as its two books lay it out:

1. **Level 1 — The individual and the state (6 lessons).** The
   Gymnasium book's civics strand (its Part B, Unit II): the
   individual and the state, regimes and the Constitution,
   elections, parties and media, the functions of the state,
   administration, and rights paired with obligations.
2. **Level 2 — Greece, Europe and the world (2 lessons).** The
   same book closing outward: the European Union (Unit III) and
   the international community (Unit IV).
3. **Level 3 — Society, the state and the economy, and the
   free, responsible and active citizen (13 lessons).** The
   Lykeio Πολιτική Παιδεία whole: society, the state and the
   economy as one system; Greek society's institutions and
   values; the political community's ladder (Municipality,
   Region, State, European Union), socialisation, migration and
   social problems; and the course's centre — the free,
   responsible and active citizen, who decides, sustains, and
   takes part. Within Level 3 the lessons are grouped by the
   book's own themes (its chapter order is preserved inside
   each title, and every lesson's sources carry its chapter
   number).

Greece's own way, preserved whole: the Gymnasium course binds
sociology and political education in one book — the pupil first
meets society (groups, institutions, socialisation), then turns
to the state: constitutions, elections/parties/media, the
functions of the state, administration, and rights paired with
obligations — then outward to the EU and the international
community. Πολιτική Παιδεία at Lykeio then teaches the citizen
inside three interlocking systems — society, state and economy
— from Aristotle's polis and citizen, through the institutions
and values of Greek society (the Greek Orthodox tradition, the
family, education), the political community's own ladder
(Municipality, Region, State, European Union), to the course's
centre: the free, responsible and active citizen, taught across
11 hours with environmental, consumer and health education.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
books' own shape, walked in order):

- *The individual and the state, then outward:* "Start with
  yourself and the state: what the state is, its forms and its
  Constitution, its elections, parties and media, its
  functions, its administration, and your rights with your
  obligations. Then step outward — to the European Union, and
  to the whole international community."
- *The free, responsible and active citizen:* "Then the deeper
  course: society, state and economy as one system, from the
  polis of Aristotle; the institutions and values of Greek
  society; the ladder from Municipality to European Union; and
  at the centre, the citizen this course builds — free,
  responsible and active — deciding well, sustaining the
  natural world, and facing migration and social problems with
  facts."
