# Society — From Agrarian to Information

## Do it first

Ask an older person in your family how news, work and shopping were done when they were young. Write down what has changed. This lesson gives that change its map: the forms society takes across time.

## Sub lesson 1 — Agrarian society

In agrarian society, most people work the land, live in small communities, and knowledge travels slowly, face to face.

Land, season and family set the shape of life.

## Sub lesson 2 — The road of change

Society's form changes as production and communication change — industry, cities and mass media each remake work and community.

Change in the tools of life becomes change in the life itself.

## Sub lesson 3 — The information and knowledge society

In the information and knowledge society, information moves instantly and knowledge itself is a main resource and a main form of work.

Learning, in such a society, never finishes — which is why this course treats the citizen as a learner for life.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: societies change their form across history — from agrarian society to the information and knowledge society — and each form shapes how people live, work and learn. Give one real example from your own life or from the Greece you live in, and name the lesson's key words: agrarian society, information society, knowledge society. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
