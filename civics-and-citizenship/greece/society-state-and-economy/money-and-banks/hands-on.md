# Hands-on — Money and Banks

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Try a trade without money: offer a classmate something you have for something you want, and notice what goes wrong when each wants what the other does not have. This lesson follows the road from that barter problem to money and banks.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: money grew out of barter as the common measure and means of exchange, it works because of its properties and functions, and it takes modern forms held and moved through banks. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
