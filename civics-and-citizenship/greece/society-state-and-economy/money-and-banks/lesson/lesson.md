# Money and Banks

## Do it first

Try a trade without money: offer a classmate something you have for something you want, and notice what goes wrong when each wants what the other does not have. This lesson follows the road from that barter problem to money and banks.

## Sub lesson 1 — From barter to the money economy

Barter — goods for goods — fails when the two sides do not want what each other has; the money economy solves that with a common means of exchange.

Money lets any good be exchanged for any other, through one shared middle term.

## Sub lesson 2 — Properties and functions of money

Money works because it is accepted by all, measurable, divisible and lasting; its functions are to measure value, to serve as a means of exchange, and to store value.

Test any would-be money against those properties and the failures show at once.

## Sub lesson 3 — Modern forms and banks

Modern money takes forms beyond coins and notes — deposits and electronic payment — and banks hold, move and lend it.

The bank is therefore part of every citizen's economic life, and understanding it is part of citizenship.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: money grew out of barter as the common measure and means of exchange, it works because of its properties and functions, and it takes modern forms held and moved through banks. Give one real example from your own life or from the Greece you live in, and name the lesson's key words: money, barter, banks, exchange. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
