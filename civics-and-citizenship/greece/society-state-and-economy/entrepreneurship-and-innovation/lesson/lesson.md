# Entrepreneurship and Innovation

## Do it first

Think of a small business near you. Write down: what need it serves, and one new thing it could do better than its rivals. This lesson names the two forces in your notes: entrepreneurship and innovation.

## Sub lesson 1 — The entrepreneur and the enterprise

The entrepreneur is the person who organises an enterprise — taking an idea, gathering resources, and carrying the risk of making it real.

The enterprise is the organised result: people, means and a purpose in the market.

## Sub lesson 2 — Innovation

Innovation is doing things in new ways — a new product, a new method, a new market — not only inventing, but improving.

It is a habit an enterprise practises, not a single lucky event.

## Sub lesson 3 — Competitiveness and outward orientation

An enterprise that innovates can compete — at home and beyond its borders; outward orientation means looking to wider markets, not only the street it stands on.

For a citizen, the lesson is wider than business: new ways, honestly tested, are how common life improves too.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: entrepreneurship turns an idea into an enterprise, and innovation — doing things in new and better ways — makes enterprises competitive and outward-looking. Give one real example from your own life or from the Greece you live in, and name the lesson's key words: entrepreneur, enterprise, innovation, competitiveness. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
