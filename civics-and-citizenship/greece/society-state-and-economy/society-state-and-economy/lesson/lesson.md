# Society, the State and the Economy

## Do it first

Watch yourself for one morning: you live with others, you use and exchange things, and you follow shared rules. This lesson shows those are one life seen three ways — society, economy and the state — starting from Aristotle's polis.

## Sub lesson 1 — The human being: social, economic, political

A human being lives with others (social), provides for life's needs with others (economic), and shares in ruling and being ruled (political).

The three are not separate people — they are one person in one shared life.

## Sub lesson 2 — The polis and the citizen

From Aristotle, the book takes the polis: the self-governing community, and the citizen as the one who shares in its common life.

Citizenship is therefore active membership, not a spectator's seat.

## Sub lesson 3 — The political art and the politician

Politics is an art practised for the common good, and the politician is judged by its qualities — serving the whole, not the self.

That measure — does it serve the common life? — runs through the whole Lykeio course.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the human being is at once a social, an economic and a political being — the maker of the polis, a citizen of it, and a practitioner of the political art. Give one real example from your own life or from the Greece you live in, and name the lesson's key words: polis, citizen, political art, politician. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
