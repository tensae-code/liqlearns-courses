# Video script — Institutions and Values of Greek Society

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Greek society is held together by its institutions and values — among them the greek orthodox tradition, the family as the cell of individual and collective life, education, and freedom.

An institution is a lasting, organised form of common life; values are what a society holds good and passes on through its institutions.

The two belong together: values live inside institutions, and institutions are judged by their values.

The book names the Greek Orthodox tradition's place in Greek society, the family as the cell of individual and collective life, and education as the institution that forms each new generation.

Each forms the person in a different circle: belief and meaning, first belonging, and learning.

Freedom stands among the society's core values — the space in which persons and institutions alike can live and grow.

A society's values are not museum pieces: they are practised, or they fade.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
