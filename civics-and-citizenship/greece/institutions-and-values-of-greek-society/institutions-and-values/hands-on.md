# Hands-on — Institutions and Values of Greek Society

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Name the places that formed you: your home, your school, your place of worship or community. This lesson calls them by their book name — institutions — and names the values they carry into Greek society.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Greek society is held together by its institutions and values — among them the Greek Orthodox tradition, the family as the cell of individual and collective life, education, and freedom. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
