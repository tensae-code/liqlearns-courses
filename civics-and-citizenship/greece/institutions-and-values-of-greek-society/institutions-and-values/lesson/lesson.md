# Institutions and Values of Greek Society

## Do it first

Name the places that formed you: your home, your school, your place of worship or community. This lesson calls them by their book name — institutions — and names the values they carry into Greek society.

## Sub lesson 1 — Institutions and values

An institution is a lasting, organised form of common life; values are what a society holds good and passes on through its institutions.

The two belong together: values live inside institutions, and institutions are judged by their values.

## Sub lesson 2 — Religion, the family and education

The book names the Greek Orthodox tradition's place in Greek society, the family as the cell of individual and collective life, and education as the institution that forms each new generation.

Each forms the person in a different circle: belief and meaning, first belonging, and learning.

## Sub lesson 3 — Freedom

Freedom stands among the society's core values — the space in which persons and institutions alike can live and grow.

A society's values are not museum pieces: they are practised, or they fade.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Greek society is held together by its institutions and values — among them the Greek Orthodox tradition, the family as the cell of individual and collective life, education, and freedom. Give one real example from your own life or from the Greece you live in, and name the lesson's key words: institutions, values, family, education, freedom. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
