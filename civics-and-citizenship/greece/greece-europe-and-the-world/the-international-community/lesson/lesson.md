# The International Community

## Do it first

Name one problem no country can solve alone — a warming sea, a pandemic, a war's refugees. This lesson looks outward: how states live together and work together beyond their borders.

## Sub lesson 1 — States living together

No state lives in isolation: the international community is the whole of states in relation with one another.

Relations run through diplomacy, agreements and everyday exchange.

## Sub lesson 2 — International organisations

States build international organisations to work on common matters — peace, health, trade, and the protection of rights.

Through them, small and large states can act together on what none can settle alone.

## Sub lesson 3 — Greece's place

Greece takes part in the international community as a member of its organisations and a party to its agreements.

The citizen's world therefore reaches from the municipality to the whole community of states.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: states live together in an international community, joined by organisations, agreements and common rules, and Greece takes its place in that community. Give one real example from your own life or from the Greece you live in, and name the lesson's key words: international community, international organisations, cooperation. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
