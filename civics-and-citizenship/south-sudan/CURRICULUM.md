# Civics and Citizenship — South Sudan course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for South Sudan inside the Civics and
Citizenship folder, organised by **levels, not grades**: a
learner climbs when they have proved the level, not when a
school calendar says so. The school grades the books come
from appear only in each lesson's sources (provenance),
never as the course's organisation. The path follows South
Sudan's own order as its books lay it out, from the two
filings' ladder sketches (`south-sudan-placements.md` —
6 records / 6 distinct books, and
`south-sudan-placements-b.md` — the 2 missing student's
books read from 8 link leads) reconciled into one ladder:
the four years of the Secondary Citizenship series
(Ministry of General Education and Instruction, Republic of
South Sudan, ©2018, English), machinery first and the world
last, with a real community project closing every year.
The filings stock 79 placements into 58 lessons: the
coined lesson names were deduped before building, by
lesson name — every placement stocks into exactly one
lesson. Glossaries in the second filing's books are
inventory-only, not placed, as filed.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this
country's own Secondary Citizenship books — except the
**Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people
making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the
books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book list and the
acceptance reckoning. **48 of the 58 lessons stand on
student books; 10 lessons stand on teacher guides alone
(evidence only, acceptance 0, labelled as such in every
such lesson's sources).**

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **Belonging, Rights and the Machinery of the New Nation** — The Secondary 1 books (Student's Book and Teacher's Guide): what citizenship is — the legal right to belong, South Sudanese citizenship by birth to a South Sudanese mother or father, naturalisation and dual citizenship, and the citizen's duties — then human rights and conflict resolution, Parliament and the forms of government, local government, public revenue and finance, the community and the environment, and the electoral system, closing with the first community project. Two lessons climb on into later years inside themselves: the Electoral System climbs to its Secondary 2 step, and the Community Project climbs through Secondary 2 and Secondary 3, as sub lessons. 8 lessons, in 6 title(s): `citizenship-and-rights/` (2 lessons); `parliament-and-government/` (2 lessons); `government-finance/` (1 lesson); `community-and-environment/` (1 lesson); `elections-and-voting/` (1 lesson); `taking-part/` (1 lesson).
2. **The Citizen in Action — Community, Peace and Debate** — The Secondary 2 books (Student's Book, supplied by the second filing, and Teacher's Guide): how communities function and how community action is taken, democratic processes, community activities, conflict and its resolution from the local level to the international — its causes, its levels, the institutions that resolve it, promoting peace, and non-violence and its leaders, theories and advocates — then NGOs and active citizenship, debating and informed argument, and the state through South Sudan's own founding documents (the 1994 SPLM Convention, the Interim and Transitional Constitutions), its organs of government, its courts and law, the laws that protect (the Child Act 2008, the Human Rights Commission Act 2009, the Local Government Act 2009) and the principles of governance, the world as a global community, and agriculture and sustainable development. 24 lessons, in 10 title(s): `taking-part/` (3 lessons); `democracy/` (1 lesson); `community-action/` (2 lessons); `citizenship-and-rights/` (1 lesson); `peace-and-conflict/` (7 lessons); `voice/` (2 lessons); `parliament-and-government/` (1 lesson); `the-state/` (5 lessons); `south-sudan-in-the-world/` (1 lesson); `livelihood/` (1 lesson).
3. **Social Change, Rights and the Media** — The Secondary 3 books (Student's Book and Teacher's Guide): bringing about social change — opportunities for individuals and groups, and working together for change — legal and human rights at national and international level, the significance of the media in society, HIV and AIDS and the role of media and voluntary groups to bring about change, and environmental conservation linked to peace. The year's community project stocks into the Community Project lesson at Level 1, as its third sub lesson. 6 lessons, in 4 title(s): `taking-part/` (2 lessons); `citizenship-and-rights/` (1 lesson); `media-and-society/` (2 lessons); `community-and-environment/` (1 lesson).
4. **South Sudan in the World — Charters, Work and Peace-Making** — The Secondary 4 books (Student's Book, supplied by the second filing, and Teacher's Guide): charters for peace and conflict resolution — the United Nations, the UN in peace and conflict, the African Union and AU peace and security — systems of government around the world and governments compared (Kenya, Britain, the USA), how the economy functions and taxes, the rights of consumers, employers and employees and South Sudan's labour law, peace-making and the peace makers of South Sudan, the need for sustainable development, sustainable development and the environment and disasters, closing with organising a community project. 20 lessons, in 8 title(s): `citizenship-and-rights/` (2 lessons); `peace-and-conflict/` (6 lessons); `south-sudan-in-the-world/` (1 lesson); `the-state/` (2 lessons); `government-finance/` (1 lesson); `livelihood/` (6 lessons); `community-and-environment/` (1 lesson); `taking-part/` (1 lesson).

**Totals: 58 lessons** (Level 1: 8 · Level 2: 24 · Level 3: 6 · Level 4: 20 — from eight read books: the Secondary Citizenship Student's Books and Teacher's Guides for Secondary 1, 2, 3 and 4 (Ministry of General Education and Instruction, Republic of South Sudan, 2018 series, English, no ISBN printed in any book; the Secondary 4 Student's Book's year is not printed on the held pages, as filed); 79 placements stocking 58 lessons).
