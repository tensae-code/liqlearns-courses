# Civics and Citizenship — South Sudan course — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

Civics belongs to the country: this course is South Sudan's
own — built from South Sudan's Secondary Citizenship books
(Ministry of General Education and Instruction, Republic of
South Sudan, ©2018 series, English): the Student's Books and
Teacher's Guides for Secondary 1 and Secondary 3, the
Teacher's Guides for Secondary 2 and Secondary 4 from the
first filing, and the Student's Books for Secondary 2 and
Secondary 4 supplied by the second filing. Inside the course
the organisation is by **levels, not grades**: the titles
below ARE the levels' titles, in path order — what the
learner proves, step by step. The grades the books come
from live only in each lesson's sources.

The path's own logic is South Sudan's, from the two
filings' ladder sketches reconciled into one ladder: the
four school years of the Secondary Citizenship series, in
the series' own order. Citizenship for the world's newest
nation is taught machinery-first — what citizenship legally
is, the citizen's duties, Parliament, local government,
public money, elections — and every year ends with the
learner running a real community project (first filing).
The second filing's own sketch calls its Secondary 2 book
the ladder's action step — the citizen participates,
resolves and builds — and its Secondary 4 book the closing
step, placing South Sudan's citizen inside the charters
and economies of the wider world. So Level 1 (Secondary 1)
teaches belonging and the machinery, Level 2 (Secondary 2)
teaches the citizen in action — community, peace without
violence, debate, and the state through South Sudan's own
founding documents and laws — Level 3 (Secondary 3) turns
to social change, rights at national and international
level, and the media, and Level 4 (Secondary 4) faces
outward: the UN and AU charters, other countries'
governments, work and consumption rights, peace-making and
sustainable development. That ladder IS the path order
(recommended, never forced — a learner can jump in higher;
the "start here instead" hints point down this ladder).

The coined lesson names in the two filings were deduped
before building, by lesson name: 79 placements stock 58
lessons. Every placement stocks into exactly one lesson,
and each placement's provenance row (book + printed page,
exactly as filed) is in that lesson's `lesson/sources.md`.
Unit folders are the filings' own theme names; a theme
folder can hold lessons from more than one level (each
lesson's own level is stated in its lesson plan and levels
file) — South Sudan's themes return year by year, and the
course keeps them together as the books do. Two lessons
climb across levels inside themselves, as sub lessons: the
Community Project (Secondary 1, then 2, then 3) and the
Electoral System (Secondary 1, then 2).
