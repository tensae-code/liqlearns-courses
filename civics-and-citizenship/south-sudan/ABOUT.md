# About the Civics and Citizenship — South Sudan course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Secondary Citizenship books. The one exception is the
**Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for South Sudan inside the Civics and
Citizenship folder (one course per country). Inside it, the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place
is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the
ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced
gate. The school grades the books come from appear only in
each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

South Sudan's own way, preserved whole, as filed in the
first filing: citizenship for the world's newest nation is
taught machinery-first — what citizenship legally is (birth
to a South Sudanese parent, naturalisation, dual
citizenship), the citizen's duties as listed in the book
(defend the country, promote democracy and the rule of law,
end ethnic/religious/political divisions, fight corruption,
vote), then Parliament, local government, public money,
elections — and every year ends with the learner running a
real community project. The second filing's own way, as
filed: citizenship is taught as action — take part in
elections, resolve conflict without violence, debate, then
run a real community project; the state is introduced
through its own founding documents (the 1994 SPLM
Convention, the Interim and Transitional Constitutions, the
Child Act and the Local Government Act) — and the final
book faces outward: the UN and AU charters first, then
other countries' governments (Kenya, Britain, the USA) held
up against South Sudan's own, then work and consumption
rights under South Sudan's labour law, and peace-making
told through South Sudan's own peace makers.

The course as built has **four stocked levels**, from the
two filings for this build (`south-sudan-placements.md` —
6 records / 6 distinct books read; and
`south-sudan-placements-b.md` — 6 census records and 8 link
leads triaged, of which the 2 citizenship leads are the
missing student's books). The two filings' ladder sketches
are reconciled into one ladder: the first filing's levels
are the four school years (Secondary 1–4); the second
filing's own Level 1 is its Secondary 2 book and its
Level 2 is its Secondary 4 book, so in this course those
placements sit at Level 2 and Level 4. All books: Republic
of South Sudan, Ministry of General Education and
Instruction (MoGEI), Secondary Citizenship series, ©2018,
developed with the Curriculum Foundation (UK), Longhorn and
Mountain Top publishers (Kenya), funded through
UNICEF/Global Partnership for Education; each year's
Student's Book and Teacher's Guide cover the national
syllabus, as filed in the first filing. 79 placements in
all, stocking 58 lessons — the coined lesson names were
deduped before building, by lesson name: every coined name
used more than once (the student's book and teacher's guide
of the same year, and the community project that returns
every year) stocks into exactly one lesson, and every
placement's provenance row is in that lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`:

1. **Level 1 — Belonging, Rights and the Machinery of the
   New Nation (8 lessons).** What citizenship is, human
   rights and conflict resolution, Parliament and forms of
   government, local government, public revenue and
   finance, community, environment and sustainability, the
   electoral system, and the community project (Secondary 1
   books; the Electoral System lesson climbs to its
   Secondary 2 step and the Community Project lesson
   climbs through Secondary 2 and Secondary 3, as sub
   lessons).
2. **Level 2 — The Citizen in Action — Community, Peace
   and Debate (24 lessons).** How communities function,
   democratic processes, community activities, conflict
   resolution local and international, conflict and its
   causes, levels of conflict, conflict resolution,
   institutions that resolve conflict, promoting peace,
   advocates for non-violence, non-violence and leaders,
   theories of non-violence, NGOs and active citizenship,
   developing informed argument, debating, informed
   argument, Parliament and the courts making law, the
   founding documents, organs of government, courts and
   law, laws that protect, principles of governance, the
   world as a global community, and agriculture and
   sustainable development (Secondary 2 books).
3. **Level 3 — Social Change, Rights and the Media
   (6 lessons).** Bringing about social change, legal and
   human rights at national and international level, the
   significance of the media, working together for change,
   HIV and AIDS, the media and voluntary groups, and
   environmental conservation and peace (Secondary 3
   books; the year's community project stocks into the
   Level 1 Community Project lesson, as its third sub
   lesson).
4. **Level 4 — South Sudan in the World — Charters, Work
   and Peace-Making (20 lessons).** Charters for peace and
   conflict resolution, the United Nations, the UN in
   peace and conflict, the African Union, AU peace and
   security, systems of government around the world,
   systems of government, governments compared, how the
   economy functions, taxes, rights of consumers,
   employers and employees, rights of consumers, rights of
   employees and employers, labour law, peace-making,
   peace makers of South Sudan, the need for sustainable
   development, sustainable development, environment and
   disasters, and organising a community project
   (Secondary 4 books).
