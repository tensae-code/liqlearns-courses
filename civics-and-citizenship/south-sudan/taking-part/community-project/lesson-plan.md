# Lesson plan — Community Project

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — South Sudan · **Title:** Taking Part · **Lesson:** Community Project
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — Belonging, Rights and the Machinery of the New Nation (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Opportunities in a community, the project cycle and project management steps, requirements to participate, community projects as active citizenship, evidencing a project's impact, and communication in community projects. This lesson stands on a student book.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The Secondary 1 project** — The first community project: opportunities in a community, the project cycle and project management steps, requirements to participate, evidencing a project's impact, and communication in community projects — as the Secondary 1 books teach it.
2. **The Secondary 2 project** — The year's community project at Secondary 2: running a community project for active citizenship (the student book's worked example is planting trees) — opportunities, aims and objectives, and the project's impacts.
3. **The Secondary 3 project** — The year's community project at Secondary 3, climbed one level higher.

Gate pool: at most 5 questions for the prove-it check, mixed with earlier parts, picked as the hardest from the rank right under the wanted sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Stands in path order after **The Electoral System** — do that lesson first if this one feels hard (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Opportunities in a community, the project cycle and project management steps, requirements to participate, community projects as active citizenship, evidencing a project's impact, and communication in community projects.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, school or town — or, for this book's lessons, from the public life of South Sudan they live in.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the learner's own life in South Sudan and the book's own teaching, as the book itself works.
