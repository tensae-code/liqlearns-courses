# Civics and Citizenship — Afghanistan course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Afghanistan inside the Civics and
Citizenship folder, organised by **levels, not grades**:
a learner climbs when they have proved the level, not
when a school calendar says so. The school grades the
books come from (Grades 7, 8 and 10) appear only in each
lesson's sources (provenance), never as the course's
organisation. The path follows Afghanistan's own books'
order: civics begins not with the state but with the
human being as a creature of needs — material and
spiritual — then the family, then groups, then the
country's own three ways of life: nomad, village and
city; then education and economic life, and a tour of
the services a citizen actually touches — post,
telephone, relief, insurance, municipality, police —
after the economics that pays for them; then the upper
course turns sociological — culture, groups and
institutions analysed as systems, closing on development
and why nations fall behind. That ladder IS the
placements file's ladder sketch. **The ladder is
provisional where the six UNREAD books would sit** (the
Patriotism books for Grades 7–9 and Civics for Grades
9, 11 and 12 are phantom pushes, absent from the tree,
as filed in ABOUT.md): when they are secured, the
Patriotism strand will likely add a belonging/symbols
band low in the ladder and the senior Civics books will
extend the top.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything
in this course comes from government textbooks — this
country's own تعلیمات مدنی (Civics) student books (for
the Ministry of Education, وزارت معارف) — except the
**Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people
making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the
books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book list and the
acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **Level 1 — Needs and the Family** — the human being
   as a creature of needs: material needs, which nature,
   God's gift, supplies and which we must use reasonably
   and protect, and non-material needs — knowing oneself,
   knowing the world, knowing the Creator, and
   connection with other people; then the family that
   first meets them: why we need a family (it raises the
   child for life in society and supports its members
   economically and emotionally), and the successful
   family (affection, responsibility, cooperation,
   consultation and order — and tolerating one another's
   views) (`our-needs/`, `the-family/`). 3 lessons.
2. **Level 2 — Groups and Ways of Life in Afghanistan**
   — what a group is (shared features, lasting mutual
   relations, primary and secondary groups) and the
   mutual effects of the individual and the group, for
   good or for ill; then the country's own three ways
   of life: how the nomads (Kuchi) live, how village
   people live (family farming and hashar, the
   collective work on dams, bridges, canals and
   harvests, against shortages of land, water and
   tools), and urban life in Afghanistan (electricity
   shortage, polluted air, overcrowding)
   (`social-groups/`, `ways-of-life-in-afghanistan/`).
   5 lessons.
3. **Level 3 — Education and Economic Life** — why we
   need education (the school's gifts: knowledge, moral
   upbringing and initiative) and learning with no fixed
   time or place; then how our economic needs are met,
   the role of money in our lives, and economic
   relations in the world (`education/`,
   `money-and-economic-life/`). 5 lessons.
4. **Level 4 — Social Service Institutions and Mass
   Media** — getting to know the social service
   institutions: post and telecommunications (letters
   and parcels inside and outside the country; landline,
   mobile, fax and internet), relief and insurance
   (floods, earthquakes and war; property and liability
   cover), and the municipality (sharwali, شاروالی) and
   the police (national police and traffic police);
   then getting to know mass media and media and society
   (`social-service-institutions/`, `mass-media/`).
   5 lessons.
5. **Level 5 — Culture** — culture (the whole pattern
   of learned behaviour, values and rules a people
   lives by), characteristics of culture, culture and
   society, small cultures (subcultures within the
   larger culture, and cultural conflict), and cultural
   contacts (including cultural invasion as the book
   names it) (`culture/`). 5 lessons.
6. **Level 6 — Social Groups and Social Institutions**
   — the social group at secondary depth, how groups
   form, and group leadership; then what a social
   institution is (an organised system of relations
   answering a basic need — family and marriage first,
   then education from cradle to grave, and religion;
   institutions as givers of roles and norms, and the
   functions of political institutions in summary), and
   how institutions work together (their mutual
   relations) (`social-groups/`,
   `social-institutions/`). 5 lessons.
7. **Level 7 — Development** — what development is,
   cultural development, social and political
   development, economic development, the factors of
   development and backwardness, and development and
   the environment (`development/`). 6 lessons.

**Totals: 34 lessons, 38 placements stocked** (Level 1:
3 · Level 2: 5 · Level 3: 5 · Level 4: 5 · Level 5: 5 ·
Level 6: 5 · Level 7: 6 — all from three student books:
*Civics* for Grades 7, 8 and 10 (Ministry of Education
(وزارت معارف), Afghanistan, 1398 SH / 2019, Dari, all
COMPLETE as filed); see ABOUT.md).

*Standing note on the count: the three books' 38
placements coin 35 distinct unit/lesson names; the
title `social-groups/` is coined at two levels (Levels
2 and 6, as filed), and three coined lessons carry more
than one placement — stocked as one lesson each with
sub lessons (What Needs Do We Have, 2 placements; The
Successful Family, 2 placements; What Is a Social
Institution, 3 placements). So 38 placements make 34
lessons, every placement stocked exactly once.*
