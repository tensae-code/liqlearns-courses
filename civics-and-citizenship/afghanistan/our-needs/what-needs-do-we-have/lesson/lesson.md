# What Needs Do We Have

## Do it first

Write down everything you needed today from the moment you woke up: food, water, clothing, shelter — and then the needs no hand can touch: to know, to understand, to be with other people. Keep both lists; the lesson sorts them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Material needs — what nature gives

Human needs are material and non-material, and nature is the God-given source of the material ones, to be used reasonably and protected.

## Sub lesson 2 — Non-material needs — knowing and connecting

The non-material needs — knowing oneself, knowing the world, knowing the Creator, and connection with other people.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: human needs are of two kinds — material needs, which nature, God's gift, supplies and which we must use reasonably and protect, and non-material needs: knowing oneself, knowing the world, knowing the Creator, and connection with other people. Give one real example from your own life in Afghanistan, and name the lesson's key words: needs, material and non-material. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Afghanistan's own books teach civics starting from need — the human being and what he needs, the family that first meets those needs, then groups and the country's own three ways of life — before turning to education, economic life, the services and media a citizen lives among, and at last to culture, institutions and development studied as systems. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life, really look: your own needs, family, group and community in Afghanistan are this course's first text.
