# Relief and Insurance

## Do it first

Write what your family would do in the first day after a flood or an earthquake — and who, beyond the family, you would expect to come. Then write what a family could have arranged beforehand against a fire. Keep both answers; the lesson names the two institutions.

## What the book teaches

The relief organisation answers disaster — floods, earthquakes and war — and insurance spreads life's risks, covering property and liability so one blow does not ruin one family.

## The lesson in full

When disaster strikes a whole place at once — a flood, an earthquake, a war — no family can answer alone; the relief organisation exists for that hour, bringing aid to the stricken. Insurance works the other way round: many people pay small sums in advance, and from the shared fund the one whose property is lost or who bears liability for harm is covered. Together the two institutions keep misfortune from becoming ruin.

When a lesson asks you to look at your own life, really look: your own needs, family, group and community in Afghanistan are this course's first text.
