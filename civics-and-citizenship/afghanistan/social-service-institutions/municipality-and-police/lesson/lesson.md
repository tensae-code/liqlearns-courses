# Municipality and Police

## Do it first

Walk your street in your mind and list what is provided for everyone: the road, its cleaning, its lighting, its safety. Beside each item write whose job it is. Keep the list; the lesson names the offices.

## What the book teaches

The municipality (شاروالی) runs the city's services, the national police keep order and security, and the traffic police govern the roads — the institutions a citizen meets in his own street.

## The lesson in full

A city cannot run itself street by street: the municipality — the sharwali — organises the city's services, its streets and markets and cleanliness. Safety has its own institution: the national police, who keep order and security for everyone. And where the city's movement needs governing, the traffic police direct the roads and answer for their safety. These are the social service institutions nearest the citizen's daily round.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the municipality (شاروالی) runs the city's services, the national police keep order and security, and the traffic police govern the roads — the institutions a citizen meets in his own street. Give one real example from your own life in Afghanistan, and name the lesson's key words: municipality (sharwali), police. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Afghanistan's own books teach civics starting from need — the human being and what he needs, the family that first meets those needs, then groups and the country's own three ways of life — before turning to education, economic life, the services and media a citizen lives among, and at last to culture, institutions and development studied as systems. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life, really look: your own needs, family, group and community in Afghanistan are this course's first text.
