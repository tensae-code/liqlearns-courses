# Civics and Citizenship — Afghanistan course — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

Civics belongs to the country: this course is
Afghanistan's own — built from Afghanistan's تعلیمات
مدنی (Civics) student books for Grades 7, 8 and 10 (for
the Ministry of Education (وزارت معارف), Dari). Inside
the course the organisation is by **levels, not
grades**: the titles below are the units the placements
coin from the books' own chapters, and the lessons
inside them climb the stocked levels in path order —
what the learner proves, step by step. The grades the
books come from live only in each lesson's sources.

The path's own logic is the books': start from need,
not from the state — the human being's material and
non-material needs, the family that first meets them,
then groups and the country's own three ways of life
(nomad, village, city); then schooling and economic
life, and a tour of the services and media the citizen
lives among; then turn analytical — culture, groups and
institutions as systems — and close on development.
That ladder IS the path order (recommended, never
forced — a learner can jump in anywhere; the "start
here instead" hints point down this ladder), and it is
provisional where the six UNREAD books would sit (see
ABOUT.md). Every lesson stands on student books —
taught in 1 country (Afghanistan); see ABOUT.md for the
book list and the acceptance reckoning.

## The titles

- `our-needs/` — Level 1. The Grade 7 book's opening
  chapter: what needs a human being has — material
  needs, supplied by nature, God's gift, to be used
  reasonably and protected, and non-material needs:
  knowing oneself, knowing the world, knowing the
  Creator, connection with other people. It sits first
  because the book itself starts there — the Afghan
  course starts from need, not from the state.
- `the-family/` — Level 1. Why we need a family — it
  raises the child for life in society and supports its
  members economically and emotionally — and how a
  family succeeds: affection, responsibility,
  cooperation, consultation and order, and tolerating
  one another's views. It follows needs because the
  family is where those needs are first met.
- `social-groups/` — Levels 2 and 6. The one title
  coined twice, as filed: at Level 2, the Grade 7
  book's groups — what a group is (primary and
  secondary) and the mutual effects of the individual
  and the group; at Level 6, the Grade 10 book returns
  to the same title at secondary depth — the social
  group, how groups form, and group leadership. The
  title is kept whole at both levels rather than split,
  because the books themselves teach it twice, once
  from life and once as a system.
- `ways-of-life-in-afghanistan/` — Level 2. The Grade 7
  book's own tour of the country: how the nomads
  (Kuchi) live, how village people live (with hashar,
  the collective work), and urban life in Afghanistan.
  It closes Level 2 because the circle that began with
  one person's needs ends with the whole country's
  three ways of meeting them.
- `education/` — Level 3. The Grade 8 book's opening
  chapter: why we need education — knowledge, moral
  upbringing and initiative — and learning with no
  fixed time or place. It opens Level 3 because the
  second book begins where a citizen's powers begin:
  with his schooling.
- `money-and-economic-life/` — Level 3. How our
  economic needs are met, the role of money, and
  economic relations in the world. It follows education
  because the book takes up the economics that pays for
  the services of the next level right after schooling.
- `social-service-institutions/` — Level 4. Getting to
  know the social service institutions: post and
  telecommunications, relief and insurance, and the
  municipality (sharwali) and the police. It opens
  Level 4 because these are the services a citizen
  actually touches, one by one, in his own street and
  province.
- `mass-media/` — Level 4. Getting to know mass media
  and their kinds, and media and society. It closes the
  ladder's middle because after the institutions that
  serve the citizen's body and city comes the one that
  speaks to his mind — and to everyone's, at once.
- `culture/` — Level 5. The Grade 10 book's opening
  chapter, and the course's turn from living to
  analysing: what culture is, its characteristics,
  culture and society, small cultures, and cultural
  contacts. It opens the upper levels because
  everything above it — groups, institutions,
  development — stands inside a culture.
- `social-institutions/` — Level 6. What a social
  institution is — in three parts, from family and
  marriage through education and religion to roles,
  norms and the political institutions in summary — and
  how institutions work together. It follows the groups
  of its own level because institutions are what formed
  groups grow into when a basic need must be answered
  permanently.
- `development/` — Level 7. The Grade 10 book's closing
  chapter and the course in hand's top: what
  development is, cultural development, social and
  political development, economic development, the
  factors of development and backwardness, and
  development and the environment. It closes the ladder
  because the student who has studied culture, groups
  and institutions as systems is at last asked where
  the whole society is going — and what it owes the
  natural world it started from.
