# Lesson plan — What Is a Social Institution

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Afghanistan · **Title:** Social Institutions · **Lesson:** What Is a Social Institution
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 6 — Social Groups and Social Institutions (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A social institution is an organised system of relations answering a basic need of society — the family and marriage first, then education and religion — giving members their roles and norms, with political institutions making law, keeping security, providing services and defending the whole.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 6 — Social Groups and Social Institutions — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **An institution answers a basic need** — an institution as an organised system of relations answering a basic need, with family and marriage as the first example
2. **The great institutions one by one** — the great institutions one by one — family, education from cradle to grave, and religion
3. **Roles, norms and political institutions** — institutions as givers of roles and norms, and the functions of political institutions — law-making, security, services, defence — in summary

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street, school or community in Afghanistan.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the learner's own life in Afghanistan and the book's own teaching, as Afghanistan's own books teach civics starting from need — the human being and what he needs, the family that first meets those needs, then groups and the country's own three ways of life — before turning to education, economic life, the services and media a citizen lives among, and at last to culture, institutions and development studied as systems.
