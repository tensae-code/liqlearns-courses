# What Is a Social Institution

## Do it first

List the needs your society must answer every single generation — children raised, the young taught, disputes settled, the land defended. Beside each, write the lasting arrangement that answers it. Keep the pairs; the lesson names what you have listed.

## Sub lesson 1 — An institution answers a basic need

An institution as an organised system of relations answering a basic need, with family and marriage as the first example.

## Sub lesson 2 — The great institutions one by one

The great institutions one by one — family, education from cradle to grave, and religion.

## Sub lesson 3 — Roles, norms and political institutions

Institutions as givers of roles and norms, and the functions of political institutions — law-making, security, services, defence — in summary.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: a social institution is an organised system of relations answering a basic need of society — the family and marriage first, then education and religion — giving members their roles and norms, with political institutions making law, keeping security, providing services and defending the whole. Give one real example from your own life in Afghanistan, and name the lesson's key words: social institution, roles and norms. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Afghanistan's own books teach civics starting from need — the human being and what he needs, the family that first meets those needs, then groups and the country's own three ways of life — before turning to education, economic life, the services and media a citizen lives among, and at last to culture, institutions and development studied as systems. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life, really look: your own needs, family, group and community in Afghanistan are this course's first text.
