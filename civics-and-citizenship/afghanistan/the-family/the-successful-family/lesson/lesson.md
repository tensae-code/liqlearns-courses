# The Successful Family

## Do it first

Think of one decision your family made together and one that was forced on everyone by one person. Which worked better? Hold your answer; the lesson names the marks of the family that succeeds.

## Sub lesson 1 — The marks of a successful family

The marks of a successful family — affection, responsibility, cooperation, consultation and order.

## Sub lesson 2 — Tolerating one another's views

The successful family continued — tolerating one another's views and not imposing one's own opinion.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: a successful family is marked by affection, responsibility, cooperation, consultation and order — and by tolerating one another's views instead of imposing one's own opinion. Give one real example from your own life in Afghanistan, and name the lesson's key words: successful family, consultation. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Afghanistan's own books teach civics starting from need — the human being and what he needs, the family that first meets those needs, then groups and the country's own three ways of life — before turning to education, economic life, the services and media a citizen lives among, and at last to culture, institutions and development studied as systems. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life, really look: your own needs, family, group and community in Afghanistan are this course's first text.
