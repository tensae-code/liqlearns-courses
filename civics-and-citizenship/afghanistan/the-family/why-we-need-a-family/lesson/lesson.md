# Why We Need a Family

## Do it first

Name the people in your family and one thing each of them does for the others — earning, cooking, teaching, comforting. Keep your list; the lesson shows what the family is for.

## What the book teaches

The family raises the child for life in society and supports its members economically and emotionally — it is the first place a person is prepared for life among people.

## The lesson in full

No child raises himself. The family feeds and shelters its members, earns and provides for them, and carries them through sickness and trouble — its economic support. And it does what money cannot: it loves, comforts and steadies its members — its emotional support. Above all it raises the child step by step for life in society, so that the person who leaves the family can stand among people.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the family raises the child for life in society and supports its members economically and emotionally — it is the first place a person is prepared for life among people. Give one real example from your own life in Afghanistan, and name the lesson's key words: family, support. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Afghanistan's own books teach civics starting from need — the human being and what he needs, the family that first meets those needs, then groups and the country's own three ways of life — before turning to education, economic life, the services and media a citizen lives among, and at last to culture, institutions and development studied as systems. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life, really look: your own needs, family, group and community in Afghanistan are this course's first text.
