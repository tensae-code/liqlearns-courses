# What Is Culture

## Do it first

Write down five things you do the way your people do them — greetings, food, dress, celebrations, ways of speaking to elders. You learned every one; nobody is born with them. Keep the list; the lesson gives the whole pattern its name.

## What the book teaches

Culture is the whole pattern of learned behaviour, values and rules a people lives by — everything a society teaches its members about how to live.

## The lesson in full

Culture is not one custom but the whole pattern: the behaviours a people learns and hands on, the values it holds about what is good and shameful, and the rules it lives by. It is learned, not inherited in the blood — a child takes it from the people around him, which is why peoples differ and why a person's own way of life feels natural to him. The Grade 10 course opens here because everything after it — groups, institutions, development — stands inside a culture.

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: culture is the whole pattern of learned behaviour, values and rules a people lives by — everything a society teaches its members about how to live. Give one real example from your own life in Afghanistan, and name the lesson's key words: culture, learned behaviour. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Afghanistan's own books teach civics starting from need — the human being and what he needs, the family that first meets those needs, then groups and the country's own three ways of life — before turning to education, economic life, the services and media a citizen lives among, and at last to culture, institutions and development studied as systems. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life, really look: your own needs, family, group and community in Afghanistan are this course's first text.
