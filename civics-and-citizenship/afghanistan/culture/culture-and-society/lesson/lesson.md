# Culture and Society

## Do it first

Try to picture your village or street with its people but no shared pattern — no common language, no shared rules of greeting, work or marriage. Then picture the pattern with no people living it. Write what is missing each time. Keep both; the lesson joins the pair.

## What the book teaches

Culture and society hold each other up — society is the people living together, culture is the pattern they live by, and neither stands without the other.

## The lesson in full

Society names the people and their relations; culture names the pattern of behaviour, values and rules those people share. A society without culture could not hold together — its members would not know how to meet, work, marry or mourn. A culture without a society is only a memory in books. Each generation receives both together and hands both on, changed a little by its own living.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: culture and society hold each other up — society is the people living together, culture is the pattern they live by, and neither stands without the other. Give one real example from your own life in Afghanistan, and name the lesson's key words: culture and society, shared pattern. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Afghanistan's own books teach civics starting from need — the human being and what he needs, the family that first meets those needs, then groups and the country's own three ways of life — before turning to education, economic life, the services and media a citizen lives among, and at last to culture, institutions and development studied as systems. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life, really look: your own needs, family, group and community in Afghanistan are this course's first text.
