# Learning Has No Fixed Time or Place

## Do it first

Name one thing you learned outside school, and one thing someone much older than you learned late in life. Keep both; the lesson asks whether learning needs a particular time and place — and answers.

## What the book teaches

Learning is not bound to one age or one classroom — a person can learn young and old, at school and outside it, all through life.

## The lesson in full

The classroom is one place of learning, not the only one. People learn at home, at work, from neighbours and from their own mistakes; they learn as children and they go on learning as adults and in old age. The lesson frees the learner from two mistakes: that learning ends when schooling ends, and that it can only happen sitting in one room at one age.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: learning is not bound to one age or one classroom — a person can learn young and old, at school and outside it, all through life. Give one real example from your own life in Afghanistan, and name the lesson's key words: learning, lifelong learning. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Afghanistan's own books teach civics starting from need — the human being and what he needs, the family that first meets those needs, then groups and the country's own three ways of life — before turning to education, economic life, the services and media a citizen lives among, and at last to culture, institutions and development studied as systems. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life, really look: your own needs, family, group and community in Afghanistan are this course's first text.
