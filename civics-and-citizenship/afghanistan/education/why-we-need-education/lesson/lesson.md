# Why We Need Education

## Do it first

Write three things you can do today because someone taught or raised you to them — one of knowledge, one of behaviour, one you started by yourself. Keep the three; the lesson names the school's three gifts.

## What the book teaches

Education and upbringing form the person — and the school's gifts are knowledge, moral upbringing and initiative: what a person knows, how he behaves, and his power to begin.

## The lesson in full

The book first says what education and upbringing mean, and gives their short history — people have always handed on what they know and how they live. The school is that handing-on made deliberate. Its gifts are three: knowledge, so the pupil knows the world truly; moral upbringing, so he behaves well among people; and initiative, so he can begin work himself instead of only waiting to be told.

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: education and upbringing form the person — and the school's gifts are knowledge, moral upbringing and initiative: what a person knows, how he behaves, and his power to begin. Give one real example from your own life in Afghanistan, and name the lesson's key words: education, upbringing. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Afghanistan's own books teach civics starting from need — the human being and what he needs, the family that first meets those needs, then groups and the country's own three ways of life — before turning to education, economic life, the services and media a citizen lives among, and at last to culture, institutions and development studied as systems. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life, really look: your own needs, family, group and community in Afghanistan are this course's first text.
