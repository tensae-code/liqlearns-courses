# Getting to Know Mass Media

## Do it first

List every way news reached your family this week — a voice, a screen, a printed page, a phone. Beside each, write how many people the same message could reach at the same time. Keep the list; the lesson gives these channels their name.

## What the book teaches

The mass media are the channels that carry news and messages to a whole people at once, and they have kinds a citizen can name and tell apart.

## The lesson in full

Some messages travel person to person; mass media travel to everyone at once. The lesson names them and their kinds — the channels by which news, teaching and entertainment reach a whole society in the same hour. To know them as institutions, with names and kinds, is the first step to using them well instead of only being used by them.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the mass media are the channels that carry news and messages to a whole people at once, and they have kinds a citizen can name and tell apart. Give one real example from your own life in Afghanistan, and name the lesson's key words: mass media, kinds of media. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Afghanistan's own books teach civics starting from need — the human being and what he needs, the family that first meets those needs, then groups and the country's own three ways of life — before turning to education, economic life, the services and media a citizen lives among, and at last to culture, institutions and development studied as systems. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life, really look: your own needs, family, group and community in Afghanistan are this course's first text.
