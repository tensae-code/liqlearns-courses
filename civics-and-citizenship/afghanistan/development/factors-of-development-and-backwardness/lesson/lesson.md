# Factors of Development and Backwardness

## Do it first

Take two places you know — or two countries you have studied — one moving forward, one stuck. Write what differs between them: land, learning, order, work, leadership. Keep your factors; the lesson names its own.

## What the book teaches

Development has factors that move a country forward and factors that hold it back — and a citizen who can name both can judge his own country's road.

## The lesson in full

Nothing in the chapter is fate. Countries move forward where the factors of development gather — an educated people, working institutions, security, resources used well — and they are held back where the opposite factors rule. The lesson weighs both sets openly, including backwardness by name, so the student leaves able to diagnose a country's road rather than merely lament or celebrate it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: development has factors that move a country forward and factors that hold it back — and a citizen who can name both can judge his own country's road. Give one real example from your own life in Afghanistan, and name the lesson's key words: factors of development, backwardness. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Afghanistan's own books teach civics starting from need — the human being and what he needs, the family that first meets those needs, then groups and the country's own three ways of life — before turning to education, economic life, the services and media a citizen lives among, and at last to culture, institutions and development studied as systems. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life, really look: your own needs, family, group and community in Afghanistan are this course's first text.
