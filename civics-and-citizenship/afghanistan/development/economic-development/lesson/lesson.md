# Economic Development

## Do it first

List what your country would produce more of, and better, if its economy developed for twenty years — and what new work its people would do. Keep the list; the lesson studies the economy's development as one form of the whole.

## What the book teaches

Economic development is the growth of a country's economy — more and better production, work and provision for its people's needs.

## The lesson in full

An economy develops when it produces more and better — farms yielding more, workshops and industries growing, roads and power reaching further, and the people's needs met more fully than the generation before. It is the form of development most easily counted, which is why the book sets it in its place: one form among several, standing on the cultural and social-political ground the last lessons laid.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: economic development is the growth of a country's economy — more and better production, work and provision for its people's needs. Give one real example from your own life in Afghanistan, and name the lesson's key words: economic development, production and work. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Afghanistan's own books teach civics starting from need — the human being and what he needs, the family that first meets those needs, then groups and the country's own three ways of life — before turning to education, economic life, the services and media a citizen lives among, and at last to culture, institutions and development studied as systems. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life, really look: your own needs, family, group and community in Afghanistan are this course's first text.
