# What Is Development

## Do it first

Write what a developed village, town or country would look like to you — not richer only, but changed. List what changed. Keep the list; the lesson gives the word its meaning before the forms of it are taken one by one.

## What the book teaches

Development is a people's forward movement — growth and change in how a society lives, works and organises itself, judged as a whole and not by one number.

## The lesson in full

Development names more than having more. It is the movement of a whole society — its economy, its culture, its institutions, its people's lives — toward a fuller and better-ordered life. The closing chapter of the Grade 10 book takes the word first in itself, then in its cultural, social-political and economic forms, then asks what drives it and what holds it back, and finally what it owes the natural world.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: development is a people's forward movement — growth and change in how a society lives, works and organises itself, judged as a whole and not by one number. Give one real example from your own life in Afghanistan, and name the lesson's key words: development, forward movement. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Afghanistan's own books teach civics starting from need — the human being and what he needs, the family that first meets those needs, then groups and the country's own three ways of life — before turning to education, economic life, the services and media a citizen lives among, and at last to culture, institutions and development studied as systems. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life, really look: your own needs, family, group and community in Afghanistan are this course's first text.
