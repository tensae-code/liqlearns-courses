# Development and the Environment

## Do it first

List what development in your place takes from nature — land, water, wood, grazing — and write what happens if each is used up. Keep the account; the lesson closes the book by settling it.

## What the book teaches

Development has an account with the natural world — it draws on nature's stores and can protect or exhaust them, so true development must reckon with the environment it depends on.

## The lesson in full

Every form of development draws on the natural world: farms on soil and water, building on timber and stone, industry on fuel and air. Used reasonably and protected, nature carries development generation after generation; exhausted or poisoned, it stops carrying anyone. The book's last word joins its first: the source of our material needs is a trust, and a development that destroys its own ground is not development but spending.
