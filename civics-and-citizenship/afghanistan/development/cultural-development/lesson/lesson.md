# Cultural Development

## Do it first

Name one way your people's shared life is richer than your grandparents' — a school in the village, books, a craft revived — and one way it is poorer. Keep both; the lesson studies culture itself as something that develops.

## What the book teaches

Cultural development is the growth of a people's culture — its knowledge, arts and shared life deepening and spreading through the society.

## The lesson in full

A culture is not fixed. It develops as knowledge spreads, as arts and crafts are practised and taught, as more of the people share in the society's store of learning and making. Cultural development is that growth measured in the people's own pattern of life — the first form of development the book examines, because the other forms grow from it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: cultural development is the growth of a people's culture — its knowledge, arts and shared life deepening and spreading through the society. Give one real example from your own life in Afghanistan, and name the lesson's key words: cultural development, growth of a culture. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Afghanistan's own books teach civics starting from need — the human being and what he needs, the family that first meets those needs, then groups and the country's own three ways of life — before turning to education, economic life, the services and media a citizen lives among, and at last to culture, institutions and development studied as systems. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life, really look: your own needs, family, group and community in Afghanistan are this course's first text.
