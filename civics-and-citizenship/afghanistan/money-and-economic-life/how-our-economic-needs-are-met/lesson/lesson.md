# How Our Economic Needs Are Met

## Do it first

Follow one thing your family used today — bread, say — backwards: who made it, who grew its wheat, who carried it, who paid whom? Write the chain as far as you can. The lesson is about that chain.

## What the book teaches

A household and a country meet their economic needs by work and production — earning, making and providing what people need to live.

## The lesson in full

Needs do not meet themselves. Behind every meal and garment stands work: farming, making, carrying, trading, serving. A household earns by its members' work and provides what it cannot make by exchange; a country does the same at its own scale, producing what it can and trading for the rest. Economic life is the organised answer to the question the first book asked: what do we need?

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: a household and a country meet their economic needs by work and production — earning, making and providing what people need to live. Give one real example from your own life in Afghanistan, and name the lesson's key words: economic needs, work and production. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Afghanistan's own books teach civics starting from need — the human being and what he needs, the family that first meets those needs, then groups and the country's own three ways of life — before turning to education, economic life, the services and media a citizen lives among, and at last to culture, institutions and development studied as systems. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life, really look: your own needs, family, group and community in Afghanistan are this course's first text.
