# River Transport and Ports

## Do it first

Before roads, there were rivers. Follow one in your mind to the sea: boats, landings, a port. This lesson travels it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Transport on rivers

River transport (transporte fluvial) carries passengers and cargo along rivers — cheap, capacious, and older than any road. Where rivers run navigable, towns and trade grow along their banks.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ports, then and now

A port is where water transport meets land: ships load and unload, goods change hands, customs and warehouses gather. Ports held a first-order role since antiquity, and the world's most important ports today are the hinges of world trade.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Rivers were humanity's first highways: river transport moves people and goods on the water, and ports — where ships load and unload — have held a first-order role since antiquity; the world's greatest ports move its trade today. Give one real example from your own life or from the Guatemala you live in, and name the lesson's key words: river transport, port, cargo. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
