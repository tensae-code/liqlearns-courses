This lesson is about one thing.
Rivers were humanity's first highways: river transport moves people and goods on the water, and ports — where ships load and unload — have held a first-order role since antiquity; the world's greatest ports move its trade today.

River transport (transporte fluvial) carries passengers and cargo along rivers — cheap, capacious, and older than any road. Where rivers run navigable, towns and trade grow along their banks.

A port is where water transport meets land: ships load and unload, goods change hands, customs and warehouses gather. Ports held a first-order role since antiquity, and the world's most important ports today are the hinges of world trade.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
