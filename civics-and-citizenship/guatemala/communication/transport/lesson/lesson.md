# Transport — Ways and Means

## Do it first

Count the kinds of transport you have ridden in your life. This lesson orders them — ways and means — and lands in Guatemala's own streets.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ways and means

The ways (vías) are the paths — roads, railways, rivers, sea lanes, air routes; the means are the vehicles that use them. Every country combines them by its geography and wealth, and a country's transport is a map of how its people actually live and trade.

## Sub lesson 2 — Moto-taxis in Guatemala

Guatemala's moto-taxis — motorcycle taxis carrying passengers through towns and city edges — are local transport invented by need: cheap, nimble where buses and cars do not serve, and part of daily Guatemalan movement.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Transport moves people and goods by ways and means that differ by country — roads and rails, rivers and air — and Guatemala has its own answers, like the moto-taxis that carry its towns. Give one real example from your own life or from the Guatemala you live in, and name the lesson's key words: ways (vías), means of transport, moto-taxi. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
