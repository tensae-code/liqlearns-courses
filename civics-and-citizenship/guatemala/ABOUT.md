# About the Civics and Citizenship — Guatemala course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this
course comes from government textbooks — this country's own
Ciencias Sociales y Formación Ciudadana books (Ministerio de
Educación de Guatemala, MINEDUC). The one exception is the
**Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Guatemala inside the Civics and
Citizenship folder (one course per country). Inside it, the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place is
found by what they prove, and each level stands on the ones
before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced gate.
The school grades the books come from appear only in each
lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

The course as built has **three stocked levels**, with one
level sketched before them and not stocked:

1. **Level 1 — sketched, NOT stocked (0 lessons).** The primary
   Ciencias Sociales y Formación Ciudadana student books for
   Grades 5 and 6 (MINEDUC 2025). Both are UNREAD: the library's
   secured copies are DAMAGED pushes (truncated uploads,
   unopenable — the library does not actually hold either book).
   They are filed in full under "Record-only — not yet in the
   course" below, exactly as filed in
   `guatemala-placements.md` (0 placements). The level stands in
   the ladder as a sketch only, so the folder knows what is owed
   once complete copies are secured.
2. **Level 2 — cultures, rights, and the violences a child
   meets (6 lessons, stocked).** From the Grade 7 (1ro. básico)
   teacher guide: Guatemala's cultures as the ground of
   formation; rights and duties at school and in society; what
   violence is; violence against children, in the family and at
   school — answered by a culture of peace.
   **Teacher-guide evidence only.**
3. **Level 3 — protecting named people (5 lessons, stocked).**
   From the Grade 8 (2do. básico) teacher guide: gender violence
   and violence against indigenous women; violence against the
   elderly; violence against people with disabilities; racism at
   school — and the cultures deepened: the cultures' own values
   as peace resources. **Teacher-guide evidence only.**
4. **Level 4 — new violences (4 lessons, stocked).** From the
   Grade 9 (3ro. básico) teacher guide: violence revisited;
   violence by teachers and staff; cyberviolence; violence
   against people because of sexual orientation or gender
   identity. The Grade 9 guide's cultures introduction is the
   second placement stocked into Level 3's Cultures and Peace
   lesson (the lesson is counted once). **Teacher-guide
   evidence only.**

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
guides' own shape, walked in order):

- *Together we build peace:* "Start with the peoples of
  Guatemala — Maya, Xinka, Garífuna, Ladino — and what each of
  them teaches. Take your rights and your duties together. Then
  name the violences, one by one — against children, in the
  family, at school; against women, the elderly, people with
  disabilities; through racism, through screens, through the
  adults themselves, over who a person is — and answer every one
  the same way: together we build peace. Juntos construimos la
  paz."

## The ways of the books (their own shapes, preserved)

**Ciencias Sociales y Formación Ciudadana I, Grade 7 guide** —
citizen formation in básico is built as peace-building ("Juntos
construimos la paz" — together we build peace): the
social-science course is taught through Guatemala's own
cultures (Maya values such as mitijul — diligent work — and the
Kaqchikel value of protecting all that has life) and through
naming and preventing the violences pupils actually meet —
against children, in the family, at school. *(Teacher guide —
evidence.)*

**Ciencias Sociales y Formación Ciudadana II, Grade 8 guide** —
the second básico guide turns to violence against specific
people — women (above all indigenous women), the elderly,
people with disabilities — and to racism at school; citizen
formation is taught as protecting the dignity of each named
group. *(Teacher guide — evidence.)*

**Ciencias Sociales y Formación Ciudadana III, Grade 9 guide**
— the final básico guide reaches the violences of the digital
and institutional world — cyberviolence and cyberbullying,
violence by teachers and staff, and violence against people for
their sexual orientation or gender identity — closing the
peace-building arc at the point where pupils are about to leave
básico. *(Teacher guide — evidence.)*

**The guides' shared shape** — each guide runs: I. Introducción
(how to use the guide, and the learning elements of Guatemala's
cultures — Maya, Xinka, Garífuna, Ladino) · II. Abordaje
metodológico (four learning experiences, each in
initial/development/final phases of activities). The placed
strand is the Formación Ciudadana teaching — rights, duties,
cultures of Guatemala and the prevention of violence; the
guides' general methodology apparatus is named, not placed.
*(Teacher guides — evidence.)*
