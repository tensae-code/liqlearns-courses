# Social Investigation

## Do it first

Your school has a problem — say, pupils arriving late. Guessing helps no one. This lesson shows how to investigate a social problem properly.

## Sub lesson 1 — Studying to solve

Social investigation begins in a real situation: something in society that needs understanding. It studies the situation in order to identify the problem exactly — its causes and its people — because a problem well identified is already on the way to its solution.

## Sub lesson 2 — Sources of information

The investigation draws on the social sciences' sources of information: written documents and statistics, direct observation, and the testimony of the people who live the situation. Each source is chosen for the question asked.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Social investigation studies a real situation to identify its problems and solve them — and it works from the sources of information the social sciences use: documents, observation, testimony and data. Give one real example from your own life or from the Guatemala you live in, and name the lesson's key words: social investigation, source of information, identify a problem. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
