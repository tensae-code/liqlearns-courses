# Hands-on — Historical Sources

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
If you wanted to prove what your town was like 100 years ago, what could you look at? List your evidence. This lesson names those things: sources.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: To interpret historical facts and social problems we use sources — documents, testimonies, objects and images left by the past or gathered about the present — and each source must be asked who made it and why. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the community life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
