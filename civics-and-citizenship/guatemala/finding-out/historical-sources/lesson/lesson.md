# Historical Sources

## Do it first

If you wanted to prove what your town was like 100 years ago, what could you look at? List your evidence. This lesson names those things: sources.

## Sub lesson 1 — Sources of information

A source is anything that gives information about the past or a social problem: written documents, spoken testimony, photographs, buildings, objects. The historian and the social investigator interpret facts by comparing sources, not by trusting one alone.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: To interpret historical facts and social problems we use sources — documents, testimonies, objects and images left by the past or gathered about the present — and each source must be asked who made it and why. Give one real example from your own life or from the Guatemala you live in, and name the lesson's key words: source, testimony, interpret. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
