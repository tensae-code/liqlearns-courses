# The Card File

## Do it first

Write three facts about your town on three separate cards. Shuffle; then sort them. Separate cards you can reorder — that simple power is this lesson's tool: the fichero.

## Sub lesson 1 — Classifying in a card file

The card file (fichero) is a technique for handling much information: each item goes on its own card, and the cards are classified and ordered — by theme, author or date — so any item can be found in seconds and new cards slip into their place.

## Sub lesson 2 — Bibliographic cards

A bibliographic card records one source completely: author, title, publisher, year — the source's identity card. With it, the investigator can find the source again and cite it honestly in the report.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: When an investigation gathers much information, the card file keeps it usable: one card per item, ordered — and the bibliographic card records a book or source exactly, so any source can be found and cited again. Give one real example from your own life or from the Guatemala you live in, and name the lesson's key words: card file (fichero), bibliographic card, cite. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
