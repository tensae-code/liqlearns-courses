# Hands-on — The Card File

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write three facts about your town on three separate cards. Shuffle; then sort them. Separate cards you can reorder — that simple power is this lesson's tool: the fichero.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: When an investigation gathers much information, the card file keeps it usable: one card per item, ordered — and the bibliographic card records a book or source exactly, so any source can be found and cited again. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the community life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
