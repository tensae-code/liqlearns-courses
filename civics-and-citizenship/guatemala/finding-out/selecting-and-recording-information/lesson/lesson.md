# Selecting and Recording Information

## Do it first

You have one bag for a market trip and a list in your head. Choosing what goes in, and writing the list, is this lesson's skill — applied to information.

## Sub lesson 1 — Objectives and plan first

Selection begins before gathering: set the investigation's objectives and a work plan — what will be sought, where, and how it will be recorded. The plan keeps the investigator from drowning in information that does not serve the question.

## Sub lesson 2 — Recording in order

Recording is writing down what was selected, in order, with its source, so it can be found again, checked and used in the report. A note without its source is information half lost.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: A social investigation selects its information on purpose: first objectives and a work plan, then choosing what fits the question and recording it in order, so nothing needed is lost. Give one real example from your own life or from the Guatemala you live in, and name the lesson's key words: selection, work plan, recording. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
