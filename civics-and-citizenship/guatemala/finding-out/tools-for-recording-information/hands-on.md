# Hands-on — Tools for Recording Information

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
You interview three elders about your town's history with nothing to write on. By evening, half is gone. This lesson is about the tools that stop knowledge escaping.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: An investigation gathers information with tools — notebooks, cards, recordings, tables — chosen and used in order, so what is gathered is recorded completely and can be trusted later. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the community life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
