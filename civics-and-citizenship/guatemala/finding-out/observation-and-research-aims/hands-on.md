# Hands-on — Observation and Research Aims

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Watch your street or classroom for two full minutes without speaking. Write down everything you notice. You have just used this lesson's technique: observation.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Observation is the social sciences' first technique: looking carefully and recording what is seen. Every investigation then needs objectives — clear statements of what it seeks to find out, set step by step before the work begins. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the community life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
