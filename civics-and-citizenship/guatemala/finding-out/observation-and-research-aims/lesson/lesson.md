# Observation and Research Aims

## Do it first

Watch your street or classroom for two full minutes without speaking. Write down everything you notice. You have just used this lesson's technique: observation.

## Sub lesson 1 — Observation

Observation means looking with a purpose: watching people, places and events carefully, and recording what is seen without inventing or skipping. It is a basic technique of the social sciences because society must first be seen clearly before it can be understood.

## Sub lesson 2 — Setting objectives

An investigation without objectives wanders. The objectives say, step by step, what the investigation wants to achieve: first the general aim, then the specific aims that lead to it. They are set before collecting information, and the whole work is checked against them at the end.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Observation is the social sciences' first technique: looking carefully and recording what is seen. Every investigation then needs objectives — clear statements of what it seeks to find out, set step by step before the work begins. Give one real example from your own life or from the Guatemala you live in, and name the lesson's key words: observation, objective, investigation. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
