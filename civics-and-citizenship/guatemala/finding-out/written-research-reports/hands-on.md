# Hands-on — Written Research Reports

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Tell a friend about something you investigated, in the wrong order — answer first, question last — and watch their confusion. This lesson gives the right order: the research report.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: A written research report presents an investigation in order: the theme, the background, the guiding questions — and then what was found — so others can follow, check and use the work. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the community life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
