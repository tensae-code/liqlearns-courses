# Written Research Reports

## Do it first

Tell a friend about something you investigated, in the wrong order — answer first, question last — and watch their confusion. This lesson gives the right order: the research report.

## Sub lesson 1 — The report's shape

The written report opens with the theme — what was investigated — and its background: what was already known and why the question matters. Then come the guiding questions, the questions that steered the whole investigation and that the report must answer.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: A written research report presents an investigation in order: the theme, the background, the guiding questions — and then what was found — so others can follow, check and use the work. Give one real example from your own life or from the Guatemala you live in, and name the lesson's key words: research report, background, guiding questions. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
