# Hands-on — Violence against People with Disabilities

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Think of one place — a classroom, a bus, a home — and one thing that makes it hard for a person with a disability. Then one support that would help. This lesson starts from the guides' own case: people with different capacities need more support.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: people with disabilities are owed more support, not violence — violence against them is named and refused. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the community life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
