# Violence against People with Disabilities

## Do it first

Think of one place — a classroom, a bus, a home — and one thing that makes it hard for a person with a disability. Then one support that would help. This lesson starts from the guides' own case: people with different capacities need more support.

The third experience names violencia contra los discapacitados — violence against people with disabilities: harm done to people because of, or careless of, their disability.

Its case carries the lesson's whole teaching in its title: "Personas con capacidades diferentes necesitan más apoyo" — people with different capacities need more support. The owed answer to difference is support; violence is its betrayal.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: people with disabilities are owed more support, not violence — violence against them is named and refused. Give one real example from your own life or from the Guatemala you live in, and name the lesson's key words: violencia contra los discapacitados, people with disabilities, different capacities, more support. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: people with disabilities are owed more support, not violence — violence against them is named and refused.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
