# Hands-on — Discrimination and HIV

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Illness is hard enough without neighbours turning away. This lesson teaches the facts about HIV — and names the discrimination against those who live with it as a wrong.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: People living with HIV and AIDS face discrimination on top of illness — and the answer taught is human rights: HIV and AIDS explained plainly, the discrimination named, and rights defended, as Amnesty International defends them. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the community life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
