# Discrimination and HIV

## Do it first

Illness is hard enough without neighbours turning away. This lesson teaches the facts about HIV — and names the discrimination against those who live with it as a wrong.

## Sub lesson 1 — HIV, AIDS and discrimination

HIV is a virus that attacks the body's defences; AIDS is the advanced illness it can cause. People living with them suffer a second burden: discrimination — rejection at work, school and even in care — born of fear and ignorance. The book answers with human rights: the epidemic is fought with medicine and with rights together, as Amnesty International's work shows.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: People living with HIV and AIDS face discrimination on top of illness — and the answer taught is human rights: HIV and AIDS explained plainly, the discrimination named, and rights defended, as Amnesty International defends them. Give one real example from your own life or from the Guatemala you live in, and name the lesson's key words: HIV, AIDS, discrimination. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
