# Lesson plan — Gender Violence

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Guatemala · **Title:** Dignity and Protection · **Lesson:** Gender Violence
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — Dignity and Protection (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** gender violence — above all violence against indigenous women — has forms and causes that can be named, and it can be prevented.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).
**Footing:** teacher-guide evidence only — every placement for this lesson is marked TEACHER (see lesson/sources.md).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Gender violence and violence against indigenous women** — The second básico guide opens by turning to violence against specific people. Its first experience names violencia de género — gender violence — and within it, violence against indigenous women, who meet violence both as women and as indigenous people.
2. **Prevention** — What has causes can be prevented. The course's answer follows its whole arc: name the violence, refuse the idea that any person is worth less, and build the culture of peace from the values of Guatemala's peoples — in which every person, and every woman of every people, is owed protection and respect.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: gender violence — above all violence against indigenous women — has forms and causes that can be named, and it can be prevented.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or town — or, for this course's lessons, from the school and community life they live in.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the learner's own school, family and community life, as the guides' learning experiences work.
