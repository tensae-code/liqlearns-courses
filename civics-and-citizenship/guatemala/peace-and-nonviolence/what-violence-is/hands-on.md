# Hands-on — What Violence Is

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Think of one act that hurt someone — with hands, with words, or by leaving them out. Write what happened and who was hurt. This lesson names what violence is, so it can be answered.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: violence is harm done to people — it has forms and roots — and its opposite is a culture of peace. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the community life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
