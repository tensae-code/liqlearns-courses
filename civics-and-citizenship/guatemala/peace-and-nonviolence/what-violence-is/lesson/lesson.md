# What Violence Is

## Do it first

Think of one act that hurt someone — with hands, with words, or by leaving them out. Write what happened and who was hurt. This lesson names what violence is, so it can be answered.

## Sub lesson 1 — What violence is

Violence is harm done to people. The first learning experience of the básico course stops and names it plainly, before any of its particular forms: a pupil who can name violence can begin to refuse it.

Violence is not only hitting. It takes forms — in acts, in words, in the way people are treated and left out.

## Sub lesson 2 — Forms and roots

The experience works through violence's forms and its roots: where violence comes from, what feeds it, and how it grows when nobody names it.

Naming the roots matters because peace is not built by punishing single acts alone — it is built by changing what violence grows from.

## Sub lesson 3 — The culture of peace as its opposite

Against violence the guides set its opposite: a culture of peace — the way of living together that the whole básico course is named for: "Juntos construimos la paz" — together we build peace.

Peace here is not only the absence of harm. It is a culture: values, habits and ways of treating people, learned and practised — beginning with the values of Guatemala's own peoples.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: violence is harm done to people — it has forms and roots — and its opposite is a culture of peace. Give one real example from your own life or from the Guatemala you live in, and name the lesson's key words: violence, forms of violence, roots of violence, culture of peace, Juntos construimos la paz. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
