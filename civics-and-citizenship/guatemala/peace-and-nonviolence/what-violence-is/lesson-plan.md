## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What violence is** — Violence is harm done to people. The first learning experience of the básico course stops and names it plainly, before any of its particular forms: a pupil who can name violence can begin to refuse it.
2. **Forms and roots** — The experience works through violence's forms and its roots: where violence comes from, what feeds it, and how it grows when nobody names it.
3. **The culture of peace as its opposite** — Against violence the guides set its opposite: a culture of peace — the way of living together that the whole básico course is named for: "Juntos construimos la paz" — together we build peace.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: violence is harm done to people — it has forms and roots — and its opposite is a culture of peace.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or town — or, for this course's lessons, from the school and community life they live in.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the learner's own school, family and community life, as the guides' learning experiences work.

## If the learner is stuck
- Go back to the one thing: violence is harm done to people — it has forms and roots — and its opposite is a culture of peace. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
- Gives the words but no example → ask for the lesson happening once in their own home, school or street today; the example is the proof.
