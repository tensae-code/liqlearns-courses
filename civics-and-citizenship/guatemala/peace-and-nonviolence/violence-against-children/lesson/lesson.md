# Violence against Children

## Do it first

Write down one way a child can be hurt by the people around them, and one value from your own family's or people's way of life that protects children. This lesson puts the two side by side.

The second learning experience names violencia infantil — violence against children: the harms children meet from the people and places around them.

A child who is hurt is not to blame for the harm. Naming the violence is the first step to stopping it, and the course teaches pupils to name it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: violence against children is named and refused — and Guatemala's cultural values are the answer the course builds against it. Give one real example from your own life or from the Guatemala you live in, and name the lesson's key words: violencia infantil, violence against children, cultural values, protection of children. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: violence against children is named and refused — and Guatemala's cultural values are the answer the course builds against it.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
