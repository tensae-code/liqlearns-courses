# Where America's Peoples Live

## Do it first

Name every American country you can, north to south. This lesson fills the continent in — where each people lives, its capital and its tongue.

## Sub lesson 1 — A continent of countries

From north to south, America is a chain of countries across about 15,000 km. The book's table sets each country beside its capital and its languages, so the continent can be read as peoples in places, not empty land.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: America's peoples live spread across some 15,000 kilometres of continent — country by country, each with its capital and its languages, from the far north to the far south. Give one real example from your own life or from the Guatemala you live in, and name the lesson's key words: capital, country, distribution. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: America's peoples live spread across some 15,000 kilometres of continent — country by country, each with its capital and its languages, from the far north to the far south.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
