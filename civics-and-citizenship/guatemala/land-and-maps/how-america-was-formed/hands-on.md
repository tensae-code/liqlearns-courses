# Hands-on — How America Was Formed

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Build a small landscape of earth or clay: a hill, a flat, a hollow for water. The real continent was shaped on a scale beyond imagining. This lesson tells how.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: America's land itself has a formation: the continent took its shape over immense time as its masses, mountains and coasts were formed — the stage on which all its history stands. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the community life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
