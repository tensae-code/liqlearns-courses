# Landforms — Mountains and Volcanoes

## Do it first

Look out at the land around you and name every different shape it takes: high, flat, hollow. This lesson names those forms across all America.

## Sub lesson 1 — Forms of relief

Relief is the shape of the Earth's surface in a place. Across America's great territorial extension the relief runs from high cordilleras and single mountains down through plateaus and valleys to coastal plains — and each form shapes climate, farming and settlement.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mountains and volcanoes

Mountains are the land's great heights, often chained in cordilleras. Volcanoes are mountains of a special kind — built by eruptions from the Earth's interior, and Central America's chain of them marks the land Guatemala stands on.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The land's relief takes forms — mountains and cordilleras, volcanoes, valleys and plains — spread across America's whole territory, shaping where people live and travel. Give one real example from your own life or from the Guatemala you live in, and name the lesson's key words: relief, cordillera, volcano. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
