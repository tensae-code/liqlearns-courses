# Hands-on — Maps

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw your street from memory, marking your home, a shop and a corner. You have just made a map. This lesson teaches what maps are and how to read them properly.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: A map is a partial representation of the Earth — a drawing of a territory on a flat surface, with signs and a scale — and reading one means turning those signs back into the real land. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the community life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
