# Maps

## Do it first

Draw your street from memory, marking your home, a shop and a corner. You have just made a map. This lesson teaches what maps are and how to read them properly.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a map is

No map shows everything: a map represents part of the Earth on a flat surface, choosing what to show — borders, rivers, towns, roads — and shrinking it by a scale so a whole territory fits on one sheet.

## Sub lesson 2 — Reading a map

To read a map, use its signs: the legend explains the symbols, the cardinal points give direction, and the scale turns centimetres on paper into real distance on the land.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: A map is a partial representation of the Earth — a drawing of a territory on a flat surface, with signs and a scale — and reading one means turning those signs back into the real land. Give one real example from your own life or from the Guatemala you live in, and name the lesson's key words: map, scale, legend. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
