# Hands-on — Waters — Rivers and Lakes

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Pour water on sloped ground and watch where it runs and where it pools. Rivers and lakes work exactly so, at the scale of a continent. This lesson follows the water.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: America's waters gather and flow: rivers run as surface currents from the heights to the seas, and lakes hold water collected in the hollows of the relief. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the community life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
