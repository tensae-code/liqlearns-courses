# Lesson plan — Waters — Rivers and Lakes

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Guatemala · **Title:** Land and Maps · **Lesson:** Waters — Rivers and Lakes
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — Land and Maps (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** America's waters gather and flow: rivers run as surface currents from the heights to the seas, and lakes hold water collected in the hollows of the relief.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).
**Footing:** student book — every placement for this lesson comes from a Guatemala primary student book (see lesson/sources.md).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Surface currents** — Rivers are surface currents running from high ground to lakes and seas.
2. **Lakes** — A lake is water held in a hollow of the relief, fed by rivers and rain.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- If you know Landforms — Mountains and Volcanoes, this lesson will be easier for you — you might try it first. It is a soft hint, never a forced start.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: America's waters gather and flow: rivers run as surface currents from the heights to the seas, and lakes hold water collected in the hollows of the relief.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or town — or, for this course's lessons, from the school and community life they live in.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the learner's own school, family and community life, as the books' activities work.

## If the learner is stuck
- Go back to the one thing: America's waters gather and flow: rivers run as surface currents from the heights to the seas, and lakes hold water collected in the hollows of the relief. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
- Gives the words but no example → ask for the lesson happening once in their own home, school or street today; the example is the proof.
- This lesson's next door: the next lesson in path order — Productive and Economic Activities.
