# Civics and Citizenship — Guatemala course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Guatemala inside the Civics and Citizenship folder,
organised by **levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they
have proved the level, not when a school calendar says so. The
school grades the books come from appear only in each lesson's
sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation. Level 1
follows the primary books' own order — America and the child's
own ground first, then conquest, colony and independence, then
the widening world of Grade 6 — and Levels 2–4 follow Guatemala's
básico guides: citizen formation built as peace-building —
"Juntos construimos la paz" (together we build peace) — grounded
in Guatemala's own cultures (Maya, Xinka, Garífuna, Ladino) and
in rights and duties, then widening the circle of protection
year by year.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this
course comes from government textbooks — this country's own
Ciencias Sociales y Formación Ciudadana books — except the
**Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'. See
ABOUT.md for the full book list, the repair of the two primary
student books, and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **The Primary Years** — the primary Ciencias Sociales y
   Formación Ciudadana student books (Grades 5–6, MINEDUC
   2025), read in full 2026-11-25 after the library's damaged
   copies were repaired with byte-exact MINEDUC downloads.
   America first — its land, its first peoples, Mesoamerica —
   and the child's own ground (school government, child rights,
   equity, observation); then discovery, conquest, colony and
   independence; population, citizens' rights and duties, work;
   the twentieth century, disasters, participation and the
   rights of Guatemala's peoples. Grade 6 widens the same
   ladder to the world: Prehistory and the first civilisations,
   the continents, the Middle Ages, the modern and contemporary
   ages, both world wars and the Cold War — deepening the
   citizen strand (every named discrimination, tax culture,
   workers' rights, truth reports, the Belize case).
   **99 lessons**, in fourteen titles: `land-and-maps/` ·
   `first-peoples/` · `conquest-and-colony/` ·
   `cultures-of-america/` · `population-and-development/` ·
   `america-in-the-twentieth-century/` · `world-history/` ·
   `work-and-workers/` · `natural-disasters/` ·
   `communication/` · `finding-out/` · `rights-and-duties/` ·
   `peace-and-nonviolence/` · `guatemalas-cultures/`.
   *(Student books — every Level 1 lesson stands on a student
   book. The two books also stock growth parts into 24 lessons
   of Levels 2–4 and of Level 1 itself.)*
2. **Cultures, Rights, and the Violences a Child Meets** —
   Guatemala's cultures as the ground of formation (the learning
   elements of the Maya, Xinka, Garífuna and Ladino peoples);
   rights and duties at school and in society; then violence
   itself, violence against children, violence in the family and
   violence at school — each answered by a culture of peace.
   6 lessons, in three titles: `guatemalas-cultures/`
   (1 lesson) · `rights-and-duties/` (1 lesson) ·
   `peace-and-nonviolence/` (4 lessons). *(Teacher-guide
   evidence for the original parts — every lesson's sources say
   so; three of these lessons also carry student-book growth
   parts from the primary books.)*
3. **Protecting Named People** — violence against specific
   people: gender violence and violence against indigenous
   women, violence against the elderly, violence against people
   with disabilities, and racism at school; closing with the
   cultures deepened — Guatemala's cultures' own values as peace
   resources. 5 lessons, in two titles:
   `dignity-and-protection/` (4 lessons) ·
   `guatemalas-cultures/` (1 lesson, deepened again at Level 4).
   *(Teacher-guide evidence for the original parts; all five
   lessons carry student-book growth parts.)*
4. **New Violences** — violence revisited at tercero básico
   depth; violence by teachers and staff; cyberviolence; and
   violence against people because of sexual orientation or
   gender identity — closing the peace-building arc where pupils
   are about to leave básico. 4 lessons, in one title:
   `new-violences/` (4 lessons). The level also deepens
   `guatemalas-cultures/cultures-and-peace/` (its second
   placement; the lesson is counted once, at Level 3).
   *(Teacher-guide evidence only.)*

**Totals: 114 lessons** (Level 1: 99 — student books ·
Level 2: 6 · Level 3: 5 · Level 4: 4 — Levels 2–4 on
teacher-guide evidence for their original parts, with
student-book growth parts stocked into 8 of their 15 lessons).
