# Hands-on — Natural Phenomena and Disasters

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Rain on an empty mountain is weather; rain that floods a town is a disaster. This lesson draws that line — and catalogues what crosses it.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: A natural phenomenon becomes a disaster when it strikes where people live: weather and water phenomena, phenomena inside the Earth, landslides — and some disasters are provoked by human beings themselves, like oil spills. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the community life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
