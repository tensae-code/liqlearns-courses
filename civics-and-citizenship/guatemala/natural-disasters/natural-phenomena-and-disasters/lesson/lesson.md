# Natural Phenomena and Disasters

## Do it first

Rain on an empty mountain is weather; rain that floods a town is a disaster. This lesson draws that line — and catalogues what crosses it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Phenomena: weather, water, earth

Natural phenomena are the Earth's own events: meteorological and hydrological ones — storms, floods, droughts — and phenomena from the interior of the Earth — earthquakes and volcanoes. Landslides (deslaves), where soaked or shaken slopes give way, join the list of what communities must know.

## Sub lesson 2 — Disasters provoked by humans

Not every disaster is natural: human beings provoke disasters too — oil spills poisoning seas and coasts among them. The difference matters: what humans cause, humans can prevent.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: A natural phenomenon becomes a disaster when it strikes where people live: weather and water phenomena, phenomena inside the Earth, landslides — and some disasters are provoked by human beings themselves, like oil spills. Give one real example from your own life or from the Guatemala you live in, and name the lesson's key words: natural phenomenon, landslide (deslave), oil spill. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
