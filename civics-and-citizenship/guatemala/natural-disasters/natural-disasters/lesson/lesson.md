# Natural Disasters — Earthquakes

## Do it first

Shake a table gently, then hard, with cups on it. The difference between the two shakings — and what falls — is this lesson: disasters, measured and felt.

## Sub lesson 1 — Disasters and earthquakes

A natural disaster is a natural event that overwhelms a community: earthquakes, born of movements inside the Earth, shake the ground in seconds. Knowing them — what they are and where they strike — is the first step to living safely with them.

## Sub lesson 2 — Measuring quakes; effects; Chile 2010

Earthquakes are measured by instruments that grade their magnitude and intensity. Their effects depend on that strength — and on the soil, the buildings and the preparation of the place. The great Chile earthquake of 2010, one of the strongest ever recorded, showed both the power of the shaking and the value of prepared, well-built cities.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Natural disasters strike without asking — earthquakes among the most feared: science measures their strength, and their effects depend not only on the shaking but on the land and buildings where it happens, as Chile learned in 2010. Give one real example from your own life or from the Guatemala you live in, and name the lesson's key words: natural disaster, earthquake, magnitude. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
