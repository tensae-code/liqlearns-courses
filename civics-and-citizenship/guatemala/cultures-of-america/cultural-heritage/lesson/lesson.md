# Cultural Heritage

## Do it first

Your grandmother's recipe exists nowhere on paper, yet it is an inheritance. This lesson widens that idea to whole peoples: cultural heritage, tangible and intangible.

## Sub lesson 1 — Heritage of humanity

Patrimonio cultural is the inheritance of the past: monuments, sites, works and knowledge received from those before us. UNESCO's world heritage names the places and works that belong, in value, to all humanity — and charges every country with protecting its share.

## Sub lesson 2 — Intangible heritage

Not all heritage can be touched. Intangible cultural heritage lives in practice: theatre and dance, festivals, crafts, oral traditions — carried in people, not in stone, and lost the day a community stops practising them.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Cultural heritage is what we received from the past and must hand on: world heritage in UNESCO's sense — places and works of all humanity — and intangible heritage that cannot be touched: theatre, traditions, living practices. Give one real example from your own life or from the Guatemala you live in, and name the lesson's key words: cultural heritage, world heritage, intangible heritage. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
