# Lesson plan — American Expressions

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Guatemala · **Title:** Cultures of America · **Lesson:** American Expressions
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — Cultures of America (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** America's cultural expressions — its music, dances, foods, crafts and celebrations — are the living identity of its peoples, and states and international organisations protect them: Guatemala ratified UNESCO's protection in 2006.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).
**Footing:** student book — every placement for this lesson comes from a Guatemala primary student book (see lesson/sources.md).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Expressions of a continent** — America's cultural expressions carry each people's history and identity, kept alive by their communities.
2. **Expressions that identify a place** — Traditional sweets and the Day of the Faithful Departed are expressions that identify a region or country.
3. **Protected heritage** — Guatemala ratified UNESCO's heritage protection in 2006 to safeguard its peoples' expressions.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.
