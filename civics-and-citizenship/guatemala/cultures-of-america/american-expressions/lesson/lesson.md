# American Expressions

## Do it first

Name one song, dish or celebration that could only come from your region. This lesson is about those expressions across America — and why the world protects them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Expressions of a continent

Every people expresses itself: in music and dance, food and dress, crafts and festivals. These American expressions carry each region's history and identity, and they live because communities keep practising and teaching them.

## Sub lesson 2 — Expressions that identify a place

Traditional sweets made for feast days, and the commemoration of the Day of the Faithful Departed with its foods and visits to the dead, are expressions that identify a region or a country — you know where you are by what is celebrated and how.

## Sub lesson 3 — Protected heritage

Because expressions can be lost, states and international organisations protect them. Guatemala ratified UNESCO's convention for safeguarding intangible cultural heritage in 2006, committing to keep its peoples' expressions alive.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: America's cultural expressions — its music, dances, foods, crafts and celebrations — are the living identity of its peoples, and states and international organisations protect them: Guatemala ratified UNESCO's protection in 2006. Give one real example from your own life or from the Guatemala you live in, and name the lesson's key words: cultural expression, intangible heritage, UNESCO. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
