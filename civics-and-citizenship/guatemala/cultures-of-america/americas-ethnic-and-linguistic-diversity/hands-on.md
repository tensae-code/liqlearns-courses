# Hands-on — America's Ethnic and Linguistic Diversity

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Count the languages spoken in your own classroom or street. Now multiply that by a continent. This lesson celebrates America's diversity of peoples and languages.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: America is a continent of many peoples and many tongues: indigenous peoples, descendants of Europeans and Africans, and mixtures of all — speaking hundreds of languages beside the colonial ones. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the community life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
