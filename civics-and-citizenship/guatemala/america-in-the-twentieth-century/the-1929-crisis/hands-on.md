# Hands-on — The Crisis of 1929

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
One market falls in one city — and a farmer on another continent cannot sell his crop. This lesson traces the crash of 1929 and its worldwide chain.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: In 1929 the world's economy crashed: banks and markets collapsed from the United States outward — falling on a Europe already ruined by the First World War — and ordinary people everywhere paid in lost work and hunger. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the community life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
