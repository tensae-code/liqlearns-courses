# Hands-on — Forms of Subjection

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A person can be controlled by chains — or by fear, or by an empty purse. Powers control peoples the same several ways. This lesson names the forms of subjection.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Peoples can be subjected in more than one form — by conquest, by economic control, and by authoritarianism at home: rule that silences the people it governs. Naming the forms is the first defence against them. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the community life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
