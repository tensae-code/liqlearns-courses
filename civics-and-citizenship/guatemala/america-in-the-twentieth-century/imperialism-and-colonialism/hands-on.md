# Hands-on — Imperialism and Colonialism

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
The colonial era seemed finished in America — then Europe's powers seized most of Africa and much of Asia in a generation. This lesson names that new wave: imperialism.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: At the end of the nineteenth century the great powers — Britain and France foremost — raced to seize colonies across Africa and Asia: imperialism, the extension of a state's power over other peoples, with colonialism as its rule on the ground. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the community life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
