# Hands-on — The Cold War

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Two giants glare at each other for forty years, never quite fighting, while every smaller country feels the glare. This lesson is the Cold War.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: After 1945 the world divided between two superpowers — the United States and the Soviet Union — in a Cold War: a struggle of ideology, arms and influence that never became direct war between them, yet marked every country, down to the divided Olympics of Moscow 1980. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the community life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
