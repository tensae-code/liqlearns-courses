# Hands-on — America's Political Situation

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
When a school's rules stop fitting its pupils, pressure to change them builds. Whole countries feel that pressure. This lesson reads America's political situation in the 1900s.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Across twentieth-century America, the need for reform drove political change in many countries — peoples pressing against old orders, and governments changing, peacefully or by force, in answer. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the community life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
