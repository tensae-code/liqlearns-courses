# Lesson plan — Cyberviolence

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Guatemala · **Title:** New Violences · **Lesson:** Cyberviolence
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — New Violences (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** violence can travel through screens — cyberviolence and cyberbullying have actors, myths, forms, and ways to prevent them.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 4 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).
**Footing:** teacher-guide evidence only — every placement for this lesson is marked TEACHER (see lesson/sources.md).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Cyber actors and the myths of cyberbullying** — The experience begins with the actores cibernéticos — the cyber actors: the people who take part in violence online, in their roles — and with the mitos sobre el acoso escolar cibernético, the myths about school cyberbullying: the false beliefs that let it hide, such as treating online harm as not real harm.
2. **Types and forms of cyberbullying** — Next the experience works through the tipos y modalidades — the types and forms of cyberbullying and cyberviolence: the ways harm is done through messages, images, accounts and networks.
3. **Preventing cyberviolence** — The experience closes with cómo prevenir la violencia cibernética — how to prevent cyberviolence: what pupils, schools and families do so the harm does not happen, and so it stops when it starts.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: violence can travel through screens — cyberviolence and cyberbullying have actors, myths, forms, and ways to prevent them.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or town — or, for this course's lessons, from the school and community life they live in.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the learner's own school, family and community life, as the guides' learning experiences work.
