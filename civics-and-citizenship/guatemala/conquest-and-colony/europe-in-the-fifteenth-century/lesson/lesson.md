# Europe in the Fifteenth Century

## Do it first

Think of one way your grandparents' daily life differed from yours. Whole continents change like that. This lesson opens Europe as it stood just before the voyages to America.

## Sub lesson 1 — Europe's situation

Fifteenth-century Europe was a world of kingdoms and growing trade, hungry for the spices and goods of the East and pressed by the powers that blocked the old routes. That hunger, with new ships and knowledge, pushed Europeans toward the sea.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Renaissance

The Renaissance was Europe's cultural awakening: a rebirth of learning, art and curiosity about the world, looking back to Greece and Rome and forward to new knowledge. It formed the minds that would question, measure and explore.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: In the 1400s Europe was changing: old certainties loosened, trade and cities grew, and the Renaissance — its great cultural movement — revived learning and art; out of that restless Europe the great voyages came. Give one real example from your own life or from the Guatemala you live in, and name the lesson's key words: fifteenth century, Renaissance, trade routes. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
