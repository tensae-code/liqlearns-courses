# Hands-on — Europe in the Fifteenth Century

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Think of one way your grandparents' daily life differed from yours. Whole continents change like that. This lesson opens Europe as it stood just before the voyages to America.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: In the 1400s Europe was changing: old certainties loosened, trade and cities grew, and the Renaissance — its great cultural movement — revived learning and art; out of that restless Europe the great voyages came. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the community life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
