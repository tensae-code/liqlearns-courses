# Colonial Government

## Do it first

Draw the chain of command in your school: pupil, teacher, director, ministry. Colonial America had a longer chain, reaching across the ocean. This lesson draws it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Governed from Spain

Colonial government was organised from Spain: the king and his councils at the top, viceroys and captains-general ruling whole regions, and governors, magistrates and town councils below. Every authority answered upward to the crown, not to the peoples governed.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Church in government

The Church was part of colonial government, not beside it: bishoprics divided the territory, parishes reached every town, and the Church kept registers, taught, and enforced the official religion among the peoples.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The colonies were governed from Spain through a chain of authorities — king, councils, viceroys and local officials — and the Church governed beside them, through bishoprics and parishes in every town. Give one real example from your own life or from the Guatemala you live in, and name the lesson's key words: viceroy, bishopric, parish. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
