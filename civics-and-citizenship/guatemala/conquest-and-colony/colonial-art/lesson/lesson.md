# Colonial Art and Music

## Do it first

Listen for a church bell or a traditional song: its style crossed an ocean, but local hands made it ours. This lesson is about colonial art and music.

## Sub lesson 1 — Art of the colonial era

Colonial art filled the churches and cities Spain built: painting, sculpture and architecture in European styles, executed largely by indigenous and mestizo artists whose hands and eyes changed what they made into something American.

## Sub lesson 2 — Music — Gaspar Fernández

Music sounded in every colonial church and festival. Gaspar Fernández, composer and chapel master, stands as a name for the era's music — European sacred forms composed and performed in America, in America's cathedrals.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Across the three colonial centuries America made its own art — churches, painting, sculpture and music like that of Gaspar Fernández — European forms filled with American hands, materials and feeling. Give one real example from your own life or from the Guatemala you live in, and name the lesson's key words: colonial art, chapel master, mestizo. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
