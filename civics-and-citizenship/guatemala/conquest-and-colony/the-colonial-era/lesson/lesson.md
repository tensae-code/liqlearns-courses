# The Colonial Era

## Do it first

Imagine your town ruled for 300 years by officials sent from across the sea, and its people made to work for them. That was the colonial era — this lesson opens it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Power and wealth

The colonial era is the long period when Spain ruled its American lands as colonies. What drew and held the colonisers was power and wealth — silver, gold, land and labour — drawn out of the colonies for the crown and the settlers.

## Sub lesson 2 — The mita

Among the systems of forced labour stood the mita: turns of indigenous people obliged to work for the Spaniards, above all in the mines. It shows the colony's underside — wealth for some, built on the forced work of the peoples.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: For three centuries America lived under colonial rule: power and wealth drew the colonisers, and systems like the mita put indigenous people at the service of the Spaniards' mines and works. Give one real example from your own life or from the Guatemala you live in, and name the lesson's key words: colony, mita, colonial era. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
