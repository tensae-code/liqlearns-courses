# Colonial Cities of America

## Do it first

Stand in your town's central plaza and look around: church, municipal building, grid streets. That pattern is 400 years old. This lesson reads it.

## Sub lesson 1 — A shared pattern

Colonial cities across America repeat one pattern: the plaza at the centre, the church and the seats of government on its sides, streets laid in a grid, and the powerful living nearest the centre. The pattern itself taught who ruled.

## Sub lesson 2 — Cuzco

Cuzco was the Incas' political and ceremonial capital long before Spain. The colonial city was built over and from it — Inca walls carrying colonial houses — so one city holds both worlds in its stones.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: America's colonial cities share a pattern — a central plaza with church and government around it, streets in a grid — laid over older worlds: Cuzco, the Incas' political and ceremonial capital, became a colonial city on Inca foundations. Give one real example from your own life or from the Guatemala you live in, and name the lesson's key words: plaza, grid, ceremonial capital. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
