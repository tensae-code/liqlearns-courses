# Hands-on — Colonial Cities of America

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Stand in your town's central plaza and look around: church, municipal building, grid streets. That pattern is 400 years old. This lesson reads it.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: America's colonial cities share a pattern — a central plaza with church and government around it, streets in a grid — laid over older worlds: Cuzco, the Incas' political and ceremonial capital, became a colonial city on Inca foundations. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the community life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
