# Hands-on — Independence of America

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A young person leaves their parents' house to run their own. Whole peoples did that with empires. This lesson tells how America did it — and what did not change.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: America's colonies broke from Europe in their own ways and times — Haiti, South America, Brazil and the rest — and Central America's own act founded the Provincias Unidas del Centro de América in 1823; independence changed governments, but much of colonial society stood unchanged. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the community life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
