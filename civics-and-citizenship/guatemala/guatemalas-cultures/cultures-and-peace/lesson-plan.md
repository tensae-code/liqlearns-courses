# Lesson plan — Cultures and Peace

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Guatemala · **Title:** Guatemala's Cultures · **Lesson:** Cultures and Peace
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 (deepened at Level 4) — Guatemala's Cultures (CURRICULUM.md).

**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 (deepened at Level 4) — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).
**Footing:** teacher-guide evidence only — every placement for this lesson is marked TEACHER (see lesson/sources.md).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The cultures' values as peace resources** — Every básico guide opens with the same introduction: the learning elements of the diverse cultures of Guatemala. At segundo básico the teaching deepens: the cultures' own values are peace resources — the materials peace is built from, carried inside the country's peoples themselves.
2. **The foundation that returns** — At tercero básico the same introduction stands again at the head of the guide: Guatemala's cultures' learning elements as the course's foundation. The repetition is the teaching — each year of básico begins again from the peoples of Guatemala before it names that year's violences.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Guatemala's cultures are the course's standing foundation — their own values are peace resources, returned to and deepened in every básico year.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or town — or, for this course's lessons, from the school and community life they live in.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the learner's own school, family and community life, as the guides' learning experiences work.
