# Video script — Learning from Our Cultures

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Guatemala's citizen formation stands on the learning elements of its own peoples — maya, xinka, garífuna and ladino: their territory and nature, their ancestral knowledge and their community values.

Guatemala is a country of peoples: Maya, Xinka, Garífuna and Ladino. Citizen formation in básico begins here — not with rules, but with the peoples who live in the country and what each of them teaches.

Each people's learning elements are the ground of the course: a pupil first learns who shares the country before learning how to live together in it.

The guides name the learning elements of the diverse cultures of Guatemala: territory and nature — the land a people lives from and cares for; ancestral knowledge — what the elders and the generations before have learned and handed down; and community values — the ways a people holds together and treats its members.

Among the values the guides lift up are diligent work — the Maya value of mitijul — and the Kaqchikel value of protecting all that has life. These are not decorations on the course: they are the resources the course builds peace from.

Citizen formation, in Guatemala's own way, grows out of its cultures. A citizen is formed by learning from the peoples of their own country — their knowledge, their values, their care for land and life.

Keep this order in mind for the whole course: cultures first, then rights and duties, then peace built against violence. The cultures come first because they are the ground the rest stands on.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
