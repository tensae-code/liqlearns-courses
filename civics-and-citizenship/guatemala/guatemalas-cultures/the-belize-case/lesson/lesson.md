# The Belize Case

## Do it first

Two neighbours dispute a boundary for two hundred years — and agree to let a court decide by law. That, in one line, is this lesson: the Belize case.

## Sub lesson 1 — From the Kingdom of Guatemala to the logwood permissions

Under Spain, the territory was part of the Kingdom of Guatemala. Britain's presence began with logwood cutters, permitted by Spain in the treaties of 1783–1786 to cut dyewood — a permission to cut, Guatemala holds, not a gift of sovereignty. From that root grows the whole case.

## Sub lesson 2 — A differendum for the court

The result is a territorial differendum — a formal disagreement between states over land — between Guatemala and Belize. Its taught ending is not war but law: resolution by an international court, under international law, with both peoples' futures settled peacefully.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Guatemala's claim over Belize is its own case in international law: from the Kingdom of Guatemala under Spain, through Britain's logwood permissions of 1783–1786, to a territorial differendum to be resolved by an international court under international law. Give one real example from your own life or from the Guatemala you live in, and name the lesson's key words: differendum, sovereignty, international law. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
