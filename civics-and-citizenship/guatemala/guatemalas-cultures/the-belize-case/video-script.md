# Video script — The Belize Case

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Guatemala's claim over Belize is its own case in international law: from the Kingdom of Guatemala under Spain, through Britain's logwood permissions of 1783–1786, to a territorial differendum to be resolved by an international court under international law.

Under Spain, the territory was part of the Kingdom of Guatemala. Britain's presence began with logwood cutters, permitted by Spain in the treaties of 1783–1786 to cut dyewood — a permission to cut, Guatemala holds, not a gift of sovereignty. From that root grows the whole case.

The result is a territorial differendum — a formal disagreement between states over land — between Guatemala and Belize. Its taught ending is not war but law: resolution by an international court, under international law, with both peoples' futures settled peacefully.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
