# Hands-on — The Belize Case

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Two neighbours dispute a boundary for two hundred years — and agree to let a court decide by law. That, in one line, is this lesson: the Belize case.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Guatemala's claim over Belize is its own case in international law: from the Kingdom of Guatemala under Spain, through Britain's logwood permissions of 1783–1786, to a territorial differendum to be resolved by an international court under international law. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the community life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
