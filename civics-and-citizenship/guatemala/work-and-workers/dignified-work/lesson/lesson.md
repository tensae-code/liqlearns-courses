# Dignified Work

## Do it first

Imagine two workshops: in one, workers have water, light, rest and safe tools; in the other, none of these. This lesson says which one the law and dignity demand.

## Sub lesson 1 — Work as a right, with conditions

Work is every person's right — and not any work at any price. Dignified work means conditions worthy of a person: fair hours and pay, rest, and respect, so that working builds a life instead of consuming it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Hygiene and safety

Minimum hygiene and safety at work are part of dignity: clean water and air, safe tools and machines, protection against dangers of the job. An unsafe workplace injures the worker to enrich the work — the opposite of dignified.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Work is a right, and it must be dignified: fair conditions for the worker, with minimum hygiene and safety at work, so no one's health or life is the price of their wage. Give one real example from your own life or from the Guatemala you live in, and name the lesson's key words: dignified work, hygiene at work, safety at work. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
