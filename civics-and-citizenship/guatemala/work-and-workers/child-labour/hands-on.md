# Hands-on — Child Labour

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Picture a child your age carrying loads at a market all day instead of sitting in your classroom. This lesson names that for what it is.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Child labour is economic activity done by children and young people — work that takes their childhood, schooling and health — and it is named as a wrong against them, to be prevented, not accepted. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the community life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
