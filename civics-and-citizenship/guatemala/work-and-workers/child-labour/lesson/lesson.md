# Child Labour

## Do it first

Picture a child your age carrying loads at a market all day instead of sitting in your classroom. This lesson names that for what it is.

## Sub lesson 1 — A wrong named

Child labour is work done by children and young people in economic activities — in fields, markets, workshops and homes — often heavy, dangerous or long, stealing school and rest. The book names it plainly as a wrong against children, against their rights to education, health and play.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Child labour is economic activity done by children and young people — work that takes their childhood, schooling and health — and it is named as a wrong against them, to be prevented, not accepted. Give one real example from your own life or from the Guatemala you live in, and name the lesson's key words: child labour, economic activity, prevention. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
