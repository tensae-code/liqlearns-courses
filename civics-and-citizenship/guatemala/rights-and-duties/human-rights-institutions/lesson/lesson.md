# Human Rights Institutions

## Do it first

If someone's rights were hurt in your town, where could they go for help? List every office or person you can think of. This lesson names the institutions built for exactly that.

## Sub lesson 1 — Guardians of rights

Rights need guardians. Human rights institutions — national offices, ombudsmen and organisations across America — exist to watch that governments and people respect the rights of all, and to act when those rights are broken.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Inter-American Court

Above the national guardians stands a regional one: the Inter-American Court of Human Rights, which watches over the principles of human rights across the continent and judges cases where people have not found justice at home. It is the region's court of last resort for human rights.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Across America there are institutions and organisations whose work is to watch over human rights — and above the national ones stands the Inter-American Court of Human Rights, the region's court for those principles. Give one real example from your own life or from the Guatemala you live in, and name the lesson's key words: human rights, institution, ombudsman. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
