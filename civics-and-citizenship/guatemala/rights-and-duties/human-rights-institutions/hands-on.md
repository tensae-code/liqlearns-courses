# Hands-on — Human Rights Institutions

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
If someone's rights were hurt in your town, where could they go for help? List every office or person you can think of. This lesson names the institutions built for exactly that.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Across America there are institutions and organisations whose work is to watch over human rights — and above the national ones stands the Inter-American Court of Human Rights, the region's court for those principles. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the community life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
