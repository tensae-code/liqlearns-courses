# Hands-on — Economic, Social and Cultural Rights

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A person may vote freely but be hungry, unschooled and unhoused. This lesson is about the rights that fill that gap: the rights to live decently.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Economic, social and cultural rights are the rights that give people an appropriate standard of living — work, health, education, housing, food and culture — the material ground on which all other rights stand. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the community life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
