# Citizenship

## Do it first

Name one thing adults in your town may do that children may not — and one thing good adults do for their town without being paid. Both halves are this lesson: citizenship.

## Sub lesson 1 — Citizenship at majority

Citizenship is the legal quality of the full member of a country, granted at the age of majority — 18 in Guatemala. It carries rights: labour rights, civic rights like voting, and rights to a life of quality — and it asks commitment in return.

## Sub lesson 2 — Full citizenship

Full citizenship for democracy is more than holding the status: it is commitment to one's country and community — participating, deciding, and working for the common good, so democracy is lived and not only named.

## Sub lesson 3 — Citizenship in action

Un Techo Para Mi País — A Roof for My Country — is a youth-led Latin American organisation whose young citizens build emergency housing with families in poverty. It is citizenship in action: organised young people working for others' right to a home.

## Sub lesson 4 — The functions of citizenship

Citizenship has functions — the things citizens actually do: vote and be voted for, hold and watch over public office, defend the constitution, pay what the law requires and take part in the community's life. A citizen is known by these doings.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Citizenship is the quality and right a person gains at majority — 18 in Guatemala — carrying labour, civic and quality-of-life rights and the vote; full citizenship is commitment to country and community, and citizens prove it in action, like the young people of Un Techo Para Mi País. Give one real example from your own life or from the Guatemala you live in, and name the lesson's key words: citizenship, majority, common good. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
