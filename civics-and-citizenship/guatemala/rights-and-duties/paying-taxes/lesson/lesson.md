# Paying Taxes

## Do it first

Your school's roof, the road to your town, the health post: who paid for them? This lesson follows the money: taxes.

## Sub lesson 1 — What taxes are for

A tax is a contribution the law requires from citizens and businesses. Gathered together, taxes are the state's most important income — from them come schools, hospitals, roads and every public service no family could buy alone.

## Sub lesson 2 — Tax culture

Tax culture is the citizen's side of the bargain: knowing why taxes exist, paying them honestly, and watching that they are well used. The book's project — an information leaflet on tax culture — teaches citizens to spread that commitment.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Taxes are the state's most important income — the money citizens contribute so the state can serve everyone — and tax culture is the citizen's commitment to that common wellbeing. Give one real example from your own life or from the Guatemala you live in, and name the lesson's key words: tax, tax culture, public services. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
