# How Laws Are Approved

## Do it first

Your school makes a new rule. Who proposes it, who discusses it, who decides? A country's laws follow the same path, formally. This lesson walks it in Guatemala.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Legislative power at work

The power to make laws is the Legislative power, held by the Congress of the Republic of Guatemala. A law begins as an initiative, is studied and debated by the deputies, is voted — and, approved and sanctioned, becomes binding on all. Knowing the path is knowing how the rules that govern us are born.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: In Guatemala, laws are approved by the Legislative power — the Congress of the Republic: a proposal is presented, debated and voted, and only then becomes a law that binds everyone. Give one real example from your own life or from the Guatemala you live in, and name the lesson's key words: Legislative power, Congress of the Republic, initiative. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
