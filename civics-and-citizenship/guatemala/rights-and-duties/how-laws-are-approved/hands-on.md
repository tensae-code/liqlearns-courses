# Hands-on — How Laws Are Approved

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Your school makes a new rule. Who proposes it, who discusses it, who decides? A country's laws follow the same path, formally. This lesson walks it in Guatemala.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: In Guatemala, laws are approved by the Legislative power — the Congress of the Republic: a proposal is presented, debated and voted, and only then becomes a law that binds everyone. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the community life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
