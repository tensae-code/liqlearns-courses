# Hands-on — Democracy in America

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Your class must choose between two games. Hands go up; the majority decides; the minority accepts and plays. That small act is this lesson's great word: democracy.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Democracy means government of the people — from the Greek demos, the people, and kratos, government or power — and as a form of government in America it rests on citizens choosing and watching over their rulers. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the community life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
