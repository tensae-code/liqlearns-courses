# Hands-on — Children's Rights

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
List three things every child should always have — and three things no one should ever do to a child. This lesson shows those lists written into law, for the whole world and for Guatemala.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Children and young people have their own rights, gathered in the Convention on the Rights of the Child — the first international instrument holding them all — and protected in Guatemala by the Ley de Protección Integral de la Niñez y Adolescencia. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the community life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
