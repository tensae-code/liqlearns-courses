# Hands-on — America's Population Today

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Why is your town where it is — water, road, flat land? Whole continents settle by the same logic. This lesson counts America's people and maps where they gather.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: America's population today is counted in hundreds of millions (UNFPA figures) and is not spread evenly: it concentrates in cities and fertile, coastal lands, and thins across mountains, deserts and forests. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the community life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
