# Human Development

## Do it first

Two children with the same cleverness: one has a school, a clinic and books; the other has none. Their lives will differ. This lesson names that difference: human development.

## Sub lesson 1 — Opportunities, not just money

Human development measures what a country's people can actually do and be: the opportunities the country gives its population — health, education, a decent standard of living. A rich country that gives few opportunities develops its people less than its wealth promises.

## Sub lesson 2 — Across the American countries

Set side by side, America's countries stand at very different levels of human development — the fruit of their histories, resources and choices. The comparison is a mirror: it shows each country what its people still lack in opportunity.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Human development is the opportunities a country gives its people — to live long and healthy, to learn, to earn a decent life — and it differs country by country across America. Give one real example from your own life or from the Guatemala you live in, and name the lesson's key words: human development, opportunity, standard of living. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
