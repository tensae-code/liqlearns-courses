# Migration and Transculturation

## Do it first

Has anyone in your family moved to another town or country? What did they take with them, and what new things did they learn? This lesson is about movement — and mixing.

## Sub lesson 1 — Population movements

Migration is people changing where they live — from country to city, country to country — driven by work, land, violence or hope. Latin America's history is written in such movements, and every movement changes both the place left and the place reached.

## Sub lesson 2 — Transculturation

Where peoples live together, cultures do not merely sit side by side: they exchange, mix and create new forms — foods, music, words, customs. That living exchange is transculturation, and it has made Latin America's cultures what they are.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: People move — within Latin America and beyond it — seeking work, safety and a better life; and where peoples meet, cultures mix: that exchange and blending of cultures is transculturation. Give one real example from your own life or from the Guatemala you live in, and name the lesson's key words: migration, transculturation, emigrant. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
