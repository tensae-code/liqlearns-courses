# America's Natural Resources

## Do it first

Look around your home and separate: what did people make, and what did nature give? This lesson is about nature's half — America's natural resources.

## Sub lesson 1 — Given by nature

A natural resource comes from nature without human intervention: the river's water, the forest's timber, the soil's fertility, the mountain's minerals. Societies live by using them — wisely, they last; wastefully, they run out.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unevenly spread

America's resources differ by region: the book's table of forest production by region shows one resource alone varying hugely across the continent. Each region's development starts from the resources it actually has.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Natural resources are what nature gives without human making — water, forests, soils, minerals — and America's regions differ greatly in them, as its forest production alone shows region by region. Give one real example from your own life or from the Guatemala you live in, and name the lesson's key words: natural resource, forest production, region. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
