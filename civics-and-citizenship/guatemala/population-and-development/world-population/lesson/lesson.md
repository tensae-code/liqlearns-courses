# World Population

## Do it first

Estimate the people in your school, your town, your country — then hear the world's number. This lesson counts humanity, and asks what the count means.

## Sub lesson 1 — Counting the world; density

The world's population runs to thousands of millions. Population density — people per square kilometre — varies enormously: crowded deltas and cities against near-empty deserts, tundras and forests. Density, not the raw total, is what a place feels.

## Sub lesson 2 — Population and quality of life

Numbers alone do not tell how people live. Set population against quality of life — the world human-development map shows countries of similar size living at wholly different levels — and the count becomes a question about opportunities, not just heads.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The world's population is counted in thousands of millions and is spread with very uneven density — and population only means something set against quality of life, as the world map of human development shows. Give one real example from your own life or from the Guatemala you live in, and name the lesson's key words: world population, population density, quality of life. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
