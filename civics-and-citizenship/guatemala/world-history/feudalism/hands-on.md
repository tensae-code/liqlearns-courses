# Hands-on — Feudalism

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
No king can protect your village; the local lord can — for a price paid in loyalty and work. That bargain, multiplied across Europe, is this lesson: feudalism.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: When Rome's central power fell, Europe organised itself as feudalism: land held in exchange for service, lords and vassals bound by vassalage, and serfs working the land as the system's organising element. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the community life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
