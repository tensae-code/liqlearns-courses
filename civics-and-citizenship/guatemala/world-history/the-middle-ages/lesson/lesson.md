# The Middle Ages

## Do it first

Between the ancient world and ours stands a long middle age. This lesson crosses it — invasions, an eastern empire, and a new faith.

## Sub lesson 1 — Invasions and Byzantium

As Rome's western power fell, peoples the Romans called invaders moved across Europe and founded the kingdoms that became its nations. In the east, the Byzantine Empire — Rome's continuation, centred on Constantinople — preserved Roman law and learning for a thousand years.

## Sub lesson 2 — Islam

In Arabia arose Islam, a faith that spread with extraordinary speed across the Middle East, North Africa and into Spain — building a civilisation of cities, science and trade that medieval Europe both fought and learned from.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Middle Ages are the thousand years between Rome's fall and the modern age: peoples invaded and remade Europe, the Byzantine Empire kept Rome's east alive, and Islam arose and spread a civilisation of its own. Give one real example from your own life or from the Guatemala you live in, and name the lesson's key words: Middle Ages, Byzantine Empire, Islam. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
