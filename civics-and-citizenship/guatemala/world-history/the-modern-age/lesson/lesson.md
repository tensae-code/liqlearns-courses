# The Modern Age

## Do it first

Imagine every book in the world copied by hand, one at a time. Then imagine a machine printing hundreds. This lesson is about the age that machine helped begin.

## Sub lesson 1 — Renaissance, Humanism and the printing press

The Modern Age begins, by the book's reckoning, in 1453. Its spirit was the Renaissance — the rebirth of classical learning and art — and Humanism, which placed the human being, their reason and their dignity, at the centre of study. And the printing press multiplied it all: books printed by the hundred spread reading and new ideas across Europe.

## Sub lesson 2 — Maritime and commercial expansion

When the Ottomans took Constantinople, the old trade routes to Asia were squeezed shut — and Europe answered by sea. The age of the great discoveries was maritime and commercial expansion: new routes, new trades, and the voyages that would reach America.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Modern Age opens in 1453 and changes the world's mind: the Renaissance and Humanism put the human being at the centre of learning, and the printing press multiplied books — and ideas — beyond all previous measure. Give one real example from your own life or from the Guatemala you live in, and name the lesson's key words: Modern Age, Humanism, printing press. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
