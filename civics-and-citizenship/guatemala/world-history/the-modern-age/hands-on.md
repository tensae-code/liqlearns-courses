# Hands-on — The Modern Age

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Imagine every book in the world copied by hand, one at a time. Then imagine a machine printing hundreds. This lesson is about the age that machine helped begin.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The Modern Age opens in 1453 and changes the world's mind: the Renaissance and Humanism put the human being at the centre of learning, and the printing press multiplied books — and ideas — beyond all previous measure. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the community life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
