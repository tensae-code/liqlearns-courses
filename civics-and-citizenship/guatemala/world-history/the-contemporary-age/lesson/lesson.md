# The Contemporary Age

## Do it first

The world you live in — votes, rights, factories, schools — was argued into being in one age. This lesson enters it: the Contemporary Age.

## Sub lesson 1 — The French Revolution

The French Revolution overthrew the old order in France in the name of liberty, equality and fraternity — proclaiming the rights of man and the citizen. Its ideas crossed every border and mark the opening of the Contemporary Age.

## Sub lesson 2 — The nineteenth century's ideas

Europe's nineteenth century argued over new political and economic ideas — liberalism's freedoms and markets, socialism's answer in the name of workers and equality — while industry, science and culture (its sociocultural development) remade daily life.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The Contemporary Age — our own — opens with the French Revolution's blow for liberty and equality, and runs through the nineteenth century's new ideas: liberalism, socialism and a sociocultural world remade by industry and education. Give one real example from your own life or from the Guatemala you live in, and name the lesson's key words: Contemporary Age, French Revolution, socialism. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
