# Mesoamerica

## Do it first

Name three things the towns around yours share — a food, a market day, a celebration — and one thing your town does differently. This lesson is about a whole region that shared a culture in that same way.

## Sub lesson 1 — A shared territory

Mesoamerica is not a country: it is the wide territory where many peoples shared one cultural base — maize farming, great stone cities, calendars, markets and similar beliefs about the world.

## Sub lesson 2 — Common and apart

The peoples of Mesoamerica were alike and different at once: they traded, borrowed ideas and built in similar ways, yet each people — Maya, and their neighbours — kept its own language, rulers and customs.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Mesoamerica is a shared cultural territory — southern and central Mexico, Guatemala and their neighbours — whose peoples held much in common: farming, calendars, cities and beliefs, while each people kept its own language and ways. Give one real example from your own life or from the Guatemala you live in, and name the lesson's key words: Mesoamerica, cultural territory, maize. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
