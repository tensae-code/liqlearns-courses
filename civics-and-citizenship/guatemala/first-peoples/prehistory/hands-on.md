# Hands-on — Prehistory

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Walk your own timeline backwards: you, your parents as children, your grandparents, and beyond — until no one can name names. That deep, unnamed time is this lesson: Prehistory.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Prehistory runs from the first humans, some 200,000 years ago, to about 3,500 BC: the Palaeolithic of nomadic hunter-gatherers, the Neolithic, when people settled and farmed, and the Age of Metals that closed it. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the community life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
