# Prehistory

## Do it first

Walk your own timeline backwards: you, your parents as children, your grandparents, and beyond — until no one can name names. That deep, unnamed time is this lesson: Prehistory.

## Sub lesson 1 — Palaeolithic, Neolithic, metals

The Palaeolithic was the long age of nomads: small bands hunting and gathering, moving with the seasons. The Neolithic changed everything — sedentarisation: people settled in one place, farmed, kept animals and built villages. Last came the Age of Metals, when copper, bronze and iron tools replaced stone and the prehistoric world approached history.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Prehistory runs from the first humans, some 200,000 years ago, to about 3,500 BC: the Palaeolithic of nomadic hunter-gatherers, the Neolithic, when people settled and farmed, and the Age of Metals that closed it. Give one real example from your own life or from the Guatemala you live in, and name the lesson's key words: Prehistory, Neolithic, sedentarisation. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
