# The First Civilisations

## Do it first

Why is your town where it is? Follow the water. The first civilisations did the same at the scale of history. This lesson visits them one by one.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a civilisation is

A civilisation is more than a village: it has cities, a government, laws, crafts, writing or records, and a shared way of life held across a territory. The first civilisations arose where rivers made farming rich enough to feed cities.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mesopotamia

Mesopotamia means the land between the rivers — the Tigris and the Euphrates. There cities, writing, laws and empires rose first, in the plains of today's Iraq, and its peoples set down knowledge the world still uses.

## Sub lesson 3 — Ancient Egypt

Egypt grew along the Nile, headed by the Pharaoh — 'Great House' — king and god at once. Around him stood a society of priests, officials, artisans and farmers, building pyramids and temples that still stand.

## Sub lesson 4 — The Creto-Mycenaean civilisation

In the Aegean Sea world, the Cretan and then Mycenaean civilisations traded, built palaces and wrote — the bridge between the river civilisations and Greece.

## Sub lesson 5 — Classical Greece

Classical Greece was a world of city-states across the Hellenic peninsula and the coasts of Asia Minor — Athens and Sparta among them — giving the world democracy's first practice, philosophy, theatre and the Olympic games.

## Sub lesson 6 — Rome — republic and empire

Rome grew from a city into a world power. In its Republic, power was shared among institutions — senate, magistrates, assemblies. In its Empire, power concentrated in one emperor. The change from shared to concentrated power is Rome's great political lesson.

Give one real example from your own life or from the Guatemala you live in, and name the lesson's key words: civilisation, Pharaoh, republic. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
