# Prehistoric Societies of America

## Do it first

List everything your family ate yesterday. Beside each item, write whether someone hunted or gathered it, grew it, or bought it. This lesson is about the peoples who lived by the first way only.

## Sub lesson 1 — Palaeolithic life

In the Palaeolithic, the oldest age, people lived in small groups that moved with the animals and the seasons. They hunted, fished and gathered wild fruits and roots, made tools of stone, and left paintings and remains that tell us how they lived.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Mesolithic change

In the Mesolithic, the age between the old stone age and farming, life began to shift: people knew their territories better, used finer tools, and moved toward the settled life that farming would later bring.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Before writing and cities, America's first peoples lived as hunter-gatherers: the Palaeolithic age of hunting and gathering, then the Mesolithic, when that life slowly began to change. Give one real example from your own life or from the Guatemala you live in, and name the lesson's key words: Palaeolithic, Mesolithic, hunter-gatherers. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
