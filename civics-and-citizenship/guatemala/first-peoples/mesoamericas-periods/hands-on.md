# Hands-on — Mesoamerica's Periods

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Divide your own life into three periods — small child, child, now — and name one big change in each. Historians do the same for Mesoamerica; this lesson walks its three periods.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Mesoamerican history is told in periods: the Preclassic, when farming peoples and the Olmecs rose; the Classic, the age of great cities like Tikal of the Great Jaguar; and the Postclassic, early and late, down to the Aztecs. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the community life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
