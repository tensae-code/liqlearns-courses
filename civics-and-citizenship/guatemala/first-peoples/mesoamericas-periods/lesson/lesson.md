# Mesoamerica's Periods

## Do it first

Divide your own life into three periods — small child, child, now — and name one big change in each. Historians do the same for Mesoamerica; this lesson walks its three periods.

## Sub lesson 1 — Preclassic and Classic

In the Preclassic period, farming villages grew into the first ceremonial centres, and the Olmecs raised the earliest great culture. In the Classic period that followed, city-states flourished — Tikal, remembered by its Great Jaguar, among the greatest Maya cities.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Postclassic

The Postclassic, early and late, came after the Classic cities declined: new powers rose, among them the Aztecs in Mexico, and it was into this late world that the Spanish arrived.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Mesoamerican history is told in periods: the Preclassic, when farming peoples and the Olmecs rose; the Classic, the age of great cities like Tikal of the Great Jaguar; and the Postclassic, early and late, down to the Aztecs. Give one real example from your own life or from the Guatemala you live in, and name the lesson's key words: Preclassic, Classic, Postclassic. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
