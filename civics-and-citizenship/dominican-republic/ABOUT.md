# About the Civics and Citizenship — Dominican Republic course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Educación Moral, Cívica y Ética Ciudadana
(Moral, Civic and Citizenship Ethics Education) books.
The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is
from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the
lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for the Dominican Republic inside the Civics and
Citizenship folder (one course per country). Inside it, the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place
is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the
ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced
gate. The school grades the books come from appear only in
each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

The Dominican Republic's civics-type teaching in hand is the
Ministerio de Educación de la República Dominicana
(MINERD) "Libro Abierto" Serie 1 set for the subject
Educación Moral, Cívica y Ética Ciudadana, as filed: a
student guide plus a teacher orientations volume per
grade, for Grades 4–6 of Primary and 1°–6° of Secondary
(census grades 4–12) — 18 records / 18 distinct books,
all in Spanish. The filing's own summary of how the set
was read: `grab.py tree` returned HTTP 404 for these
records (GRABFAIL markers from the first reader) — the
books are not tree files; 17 of the 18 live as GitHub
Releases assets (mid-size-books-3 / -4 / -5) and were
fetched with `grab.py url` on the Releases route, byte
size verified against the asset size (the Grade 4
student book needed one retry after a transient
disconnect; the Grade 12 teacher guide's first fetch was
stopped mid-download by the owner's DISK HOLD of
2026-11-17 and was completed, byte-verified and read by
the finisher reader). The Grade 8 teacher guide is
absent from the tree, from every Release and from disk —
filed UNREAD (Book 10 below). Printed page = PDF page − 1
throughout the series, as filed.

The Dominican Republic's own way, preserved whole, as filed:
civics is taught as four term-long learning projects per
year, each worked in four phases (explore / think / act /
reflect), so every concept is met inside a campaign or
school exhibition the pupils themselves organise; the
nation is introduced as "my country, my house". The
Constitution is the first project of the second year —
a child meets the State through its fundamental law
before meeting its offices — and child protection is
taught through the country's own Ley 136-03. The symbols
return year after year, in the last primary years with
the laws that regulate their use: reverence becomes law.
Road, tax and environmental education are taught
together, as one citizen responsibility, in every book.
The teacher is cast first as a moral model — coherence
between what the teacher teaches and what the teacher
practises is the guides' first teaching instrument — and
one shared teacher doctrine (what the subject is, why
and what for it is taught, the teacher as model of
integrity, the competency approach, methodological
suggestions) fronts every teacher guide, placed in full
at the Grade 4 guide and filed as a shared-doctrine
placement in each later guide. The final year turns to
Dominican thought itself: the historical making of
national identity, democracy in the Republic, the
Constitution, and Duarte's own constitutional project
read between theoretical liberalism and practical
conservatism.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later;
the books' own shape, walked in order):

- *The Dominican Republic's own order:* "First, my country, my
  house: how society is organised, what nation, State and
  government are, the State as social, democratic and
  ruled by rights, the territory, our identity and
  values, the powers of the State, and living together
  though different. Then the Constitution opens the year:
  fundamental rights, the Legislative Power, nationality
  and citizenship, our heritage, and the law that
  protects children. Then society turned outward: the
  Judicial Power, the municipality, participation, tax
  rights and duties, and harmony in the world. Then the
  pillars, for citizens of tomorrow: rights and duties,
  sovereignty, the Constitution with its powers and
  functions — then identity, dialogue, peace-building and
  guardianship of the planet. Then rights in the Republic
  worked through conflict, and the symbols with the laws
  that govern their use. Then conscious citizenship:
  human rights, governance and democracy as a State of
  law, and values that transform the school. Then the
  roots of the nation, safe routes and conscious taxes,
  and harmony built on listening. Then the constitutions
  themselves — how the Dominican State emerged, the
  succession of constitutions and the rights they
  guarantee, and the American and French influence on
  them. And last, Dominican thought: identity, democracy,
  the Constitution and Duarte's project — and the
  graduate as guardian of the nation's symbols, soil and
  sovereignty, proposing solutions to real conflicts. All
  the way up, two strands never leave: the national
  symbols, returning deeper every year, and roads, taxes
  and the environment, taught together as one citizen
  responsibility."
