# Florian Branger — Guilt and Internment

## Do it first

Write two columns: 'his fault' and 'the system's fault'. As you work this lesson, put every fact about Florian Branger into one column or the other — and notice which column the authorities of his day used, and which one they never opened.

## Sub lesson 1 — In open sea — and interned

Branger's story — told under the heading 'in open sea, and interned' — is of a man repeatedly interned in a work institution for offences and for his way of life.

Internment here is not a sentence after a single trial: it returns again and again, and it can rest on a judgement about how a man lives, not only on what he has done. That breadth is what the case examines.

## Sub lesson 2 — Punishment or welfare?

The Sek I teaching works the case between punishment and welfare: which norms were applied to men like Branger, and how guilt should be judged when the two are mixed.

Keep the two apart as you judge: punishment answers an offence and ends; welfare internment claims to help and can last as long as the office decides. Branger received the second while being treated as though he had earned the first.

## Sub lesson 3 — Press, critics and the cemetery inquiry

At Sek II depth the case adds the public argument of the time: a 1932 newspaper article in the NZZ, C. A. Loosli's critique, and the inquiry into the work institution's cemetery.

Contemporaries were not silent: press and critics challenged internment in Branger's own era. The lesson uses their words to test the excuse that nobody could have known better — and follows the cemetery inquiry as the system's grimmest record.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Florian Branger was interned again and again in a work institution for his offences and his 'way of life' — his case asks how far that was guilt by his own fault, and how far the fault of the system that kept interning him. Give one real example from your own life or from the Graubünden cases, and name the lesson's key words: Florian Branger, internment, work institution, guilt. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
