# Uschi Waser — Children of the Road

## Do it first

Write down what a child needs to stay in one place: one home, one school, people who stay. Now count what happens to that list if the child is moved again and again. Uschi Waser was moved 22 times — this lesson follows her case.

## Sub lesson 1 — A victim of the children of the road

Uschi Waser was a victim of Pro Juventute's 'children of the road' action against Yenish families, which removed children from their families because of their travelling way of life.

Her case is not one family's failure: it is one child inside an action aimed at a whole people. That is why her story stands second in the reader — it widens the unit from single decisions to a programme.

## Sub lesson 2 — Twenty-two placements

Waser's life was shaped by 22 placements — moved from place to place, never given the stable life the measures claimed to provide; the Sek I teaching presents her as the defenceless victim of the action, in the context of the family dissolutions of the early 1970s.

Count the cost plainly: every placement ends schooling, friendships and trust, and begins them again from nothing. The lesson asks what 'a stable life' would actually have required — and who kept taking it away.

## Sub lesson 3 — Whom did the judge believe?

The reader asks 'A chi credette il giudice?' — whom did the judge believe? At Sek II depth the case is worked from Pro Juventute's own summary documentation and Dr Siegfried's letters as sources.

Belief is the lesson's sharp point: in a hearing, the office's file and the affected person's word do not weigh the same. Pupils judge for themselves, from the organisation's own records, whose account the system was built to believe.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Uschi Waser, a Yenish victim of Pro Juventute's 'children of the road' action, was moved through 22 placements in search of a stable life that the system never gave her — and when it came to judgement, the judge believed the authorities. Give one real example from your own life or from the Graubünden cases, and name the lesson's key words: Uschi Waser, children of the road, Yenish, Pro Juventute. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
