# Video script — Ruedi Hofer

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Ruedi Hofer was passed from place to place as a placed child — seriously injured, exploited and abused along the way — and his story asks when a bad time finally ends.

Hofer's life, as the reader tells it, is a life of being passed from place to place — a placed child with no place that was his own.

Like Waser's 22 placements, the moving itself is the first harm: no continuity of care, no adult who knows the whole story, no one place answerable for the whole child.

The Sek I teaching names what happened to Hofer in his placements: he was seriously injured, exploited and abused as a placed child — and it notes what Hofer himself values, his dog.

The detail of the dog matters and the teaching keeps it: inside a record of harm stands what the man himself holds dear. Judging his case means holding both — the abuse the system allowed, and the life he made his own.

The reader follows Hofer's story to the question of its ending: when does a bad time finally end for a person the system passed on?

An ending is not only the last placement. The next lesson takes up what the state later did about cases like his — apologies, contributions, and working through the past — and asks whether any of it ends the bad time.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
