# Behind the Facades of a Home

## Do it first

Picture a children's home from outside: clean windows, a tidy yard, a good name in the village. Now write what an outsider cannot see from that picture. Cornelia Studer lived behind that facade — this lesson reads her account of what was inside.

## Sub lesson 1 — Life behind the facades

Cornelia Studer's memoirs tell life inside a children's home from the child's side — what the daily order felt like to the children who lived under it.

A memoir is a different kind of source from a file: it records what no office wrote down. The lesson sets the two kinds of record side by side and asks what each one can and cannot prove.

## Sub lesson 2 — Humiliation and hard labour

The Sek I teaching names what the memoirs show: behind the facades stood humiliation and hard labour for the children placed in the home.

Name it plainly, as the teaching does: children placed for their welfare were shamed and worked. The gap between the home's purpose and its practice is the case's whole charge.

## Sub lesson 3 — The home's records and the reckoning

At Sek II depth the case is worked from the home's own records and memories together (documents to D11), and the reader follows Studer's reckoning with the institution in 2016.

The 2016 reckoning closes the circle the case opens: the child who could not be heard returns as an adult who can — and the institution must finally answer to its own records and to hers.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: behind the orderly facades of a children's home, Cornelia Studer's memoirs tell of humiliation and hard labour — and in 2016 she returned to reckon with the institution that raised her. Give one real example from your own life or from the Graubünden cases, and name the lesson's key words: Cornelia Studer, children's home, memoirs. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
