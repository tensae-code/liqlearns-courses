# Video script — The Albin Family

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
The Albin family — a married couple with eight children, hit by poverty, illness and moral judgement — lost their children because the authorities decided the parents could not raise them.

The Albins were a married couple with eight children; poverty, illness and moral judgement of their way of life brought the authorities into their home.

The reader tells the case under its own heading — eight children and no family — because that is the outcome: a family that existed was dissolved, and the children were placed elsewhere.

The authorities decided the parents could not raise their children; the case is told through the social worker's own file notes, and the Sek I teaching works it as family dissolution and out-of-family placement, with document work D1–D5.

Reading the file notes is the hard part: the decision sounds orderly and caring on paper. The lesson asks what the notes assume about poverty and morals — and what they never ask the parents.

At Sek II depth the case is re-worked from the guardianship authority's own decisions, with documents to D7, including the Piccolo Consiglio's considerations of 1955.

With the full record, the question sharpens: not only what was decided, but how each office passed the decision on, and where — if anywhere — someone could have said no.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
