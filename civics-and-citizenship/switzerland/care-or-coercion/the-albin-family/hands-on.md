# Hands-on — The Albin Family

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Imagine a family with eight children, little money, and illness at home. Write two lists: what help this family could be offered, and what could be taken from them in the name of help. The Albin case is the true story of a family where the second list won.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: the Albin family — a married couple with eight children, hit by poverty, illness and moral judgement — lost their children because the authorities decided the parents could not raise them. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
