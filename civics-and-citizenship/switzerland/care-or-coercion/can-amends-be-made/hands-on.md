# Hands-on — Can Amends Be Made?

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Think of a wrong done to someone years ago, too late to undo. Write what could still be done now: what could be said, what could be paid, what could be changed, what could be remembered. This lesson sets your list against what Switzerland actually did for the victims of coercive welfare measures.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: amends for coercive welfare injustice come in three parts — rehabilitation, official apologies and solidarity contributions, together with working through the past — and whether they can ever be enough stays the unit's open closing question. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
