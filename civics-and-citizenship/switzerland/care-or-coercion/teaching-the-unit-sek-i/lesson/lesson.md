# Teaching the Unit at Sek I

## Do it first

Take any official note about a person — a file note, a report, a decision. Read it twice: first for what it says, then for what it does not say and whose voice is missing. That second reading is how this unit is taught at Sek I, and this lesson shows how.

## Sub lesson 1 — Working with the reader

The Sek I companion organises the teaching around source work with the pupil reader: each case is worked through its own documents (D1–D5 per case) before any judgement is made.

The method is deliberate: pupils meet the authorities' words first, in the file notes and decisions themselves, so their judgement of help versus coercion rests on evidence, not on being told what to think.

## Sub lesson 2 — Lehrplan 21 competences

The unit is competence-oriented under Lehrplan 21: pupils practise reading sources, weighing perspectives and forming a reasoned judgement about rights and state power.

The competences name what the pupil can do at the end — interrogate a document, reconstruct what an authority decided and why, and defend a judgement about whether a fundamental right was violated.

## Sub lesson 3 — Roots in poor law and new research

The teacher's background traces the measures to their roots in cantonal poor law, sets out what recent research has shown, and records the arguments welfare professionals themselves made at the time.

Knowing the roots matters: coercive measures did not begin as cruelty for its own sake — they grew out of poor-relief law and professional ideas of their day, which is exactly why they could be carried out by ordinary officials.
