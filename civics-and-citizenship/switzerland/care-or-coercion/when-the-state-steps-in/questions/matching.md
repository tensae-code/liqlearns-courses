# When the State Steps In — matching

Match each part of the lesson with its teaching.

**1.** What coercive welfare measures were — a)

**2.** The law behind it and the children of the road — b)

**3.** The questions this unit asks — c)


Meanings:
a) Coercive welfare measures were official measures, presented as help, by which authorities intervened in people's lives: children were taken from their parents and placed in homes, institutions or other families, and adults were interned.
b) Behind the measures stood legal instruments, including measures of the Confederation, and actions such as Pro Juventute's 'children of the road', which took about 600 children from Yenish families.
c) The unit asks three questions: when may authorities intervene in a person's life, what rights and duties do those affected have, and what do those affected want as against what society wants?

**Answer key:** 1. a; 2. b; 3. c
