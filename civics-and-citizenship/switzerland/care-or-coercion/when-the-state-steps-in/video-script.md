# Video script — When the State Steps In

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
In Graubünden, welfare authorities once stepped into people's lives with coercive measures — taking children from families, placing them in homes and institutions and interning adults — violating fundamental rights while oversight failed; this lesson asks when the state may intervene at all.

Coercive welfare measures were official measures, presented as help, by which authorities intervened in people's lives: children were taken from their parents and placed in homes, institutions or other families, and adults were interned.

The measures fell hardest on poor families. They were carried out in the name of welfare and order, but they reached deep into private life — who a child lived with, where an adult had to stay, how a family was judged.

Behind the measures stood legal instruments, including measures of the Confederation, and actions such as Pro Juventute's 'children of the road', which took about 600 children from Yenish families.

The 'children of the road' action shows the pattern at its sharpest: a private welfare organisation, working with the authorities, removed children from a whole community because of the way that community lived.

The unit asks three questions: when may authorities intervene in a person's life, what rights and duties do those affected have, and what do those affected want as against what society wants?

Those questions run through every case that follows. Each family and each person in this unit is a test of them: was this help, or was it coercion — and who got to decide?

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
