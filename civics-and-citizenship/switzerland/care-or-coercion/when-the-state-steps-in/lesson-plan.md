# Lesson plan — When the State Steps In

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Switzerland · **Title:** Care or Coercion? · **Lesson:** When the State Steps In
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — Care or Coercion? (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** in Graubünden, welfare authorities once stepped into people's lives with coercive measures — taking children from families, placing them in homes and institutions and interning adults — violating fundamental rights while oversight failed; this lesson asks when the state may intervene at all.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What coercive welfare measures were** — Coercive welfare measures were official measures, presented as help, by which authorities intervened in people's lives: children were taken from their parents and placed in homes, institutions or other families, and adults were interned.
2. **The law behind it and the children of the road** — Behind the measures stood legal instruments, including measures of the Confederation, and actions such as Pro Juventute's 'children of the road', which took about 600 children from Yenish families.
3. **The questions this unit asks** — The unit asks three questions: when may authorities intervene in a person's life, what rights and duties do those affected have, and what do those affected want as against what society wants?

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
