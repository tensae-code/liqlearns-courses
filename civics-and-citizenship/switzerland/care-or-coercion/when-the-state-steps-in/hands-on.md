# Hands-on — When the State Steps In

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Think of a decision an authority can make about a family — a school, an office, a court. Write down: who decides, what they can order, and what right of the family is at stake. Keep your notes: this whole unit is about what happened in Graubünden when exactly that power was used.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: in Graubünden, welfare authorities once stepped into people's lives with coercive measures — taking children from families, placing them in homes and institutions and interning adults — violating fundamental rights while oversight failed; this lesson asks when the state may intervene at all. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
