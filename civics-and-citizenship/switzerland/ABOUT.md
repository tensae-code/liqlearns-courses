# About the Civics and Citizenship — Switzerland course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this
course comes from government textbooks — this country's own
Graubünden teaching unit on coercive welfare measures, *Sorge
oder Zwang? / Assistenza o coercizione? / Agid u sforz?* (Care or
coercion?), Kanton Graubünden, 1st edition 2020, Lehrmittel
Graubünden — a History / Ethics, Religion and Community
(RZG/ERG) unit. The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in
a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours;
the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Switzerland inside the Civics and
Citizenship folder (one course per country). Inside it, the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place is
found by what they prove, and each level stands on the ones
before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced gate.
The school grades the books come from appear only in each
lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

The course as built has **two stocked levels**, both inside the
one unit title `care-or-coercion/` — the unit the books
themselves form:

1. **Level 1 — Care or Coercion? at Sekundarstufe I (8
   lessons).** The pupil reader's introduction and five cases,
   with the Sek I teacher companion's teaching of the same
   cases: what coercive welfare measures were, how the state
   stepped into families in Graubünden, and the first judgement
   of help versus coercion and violated fundamental rights.
2. **Level 2 — Care or Coercion? at Sekundarstufe II (1 new
   lesson, and 5 Level 1 lessons deepened).** The Sek II
   companion re-works the same five cases from the full
   documentary record — council decisions, welfare-organisation
   files, press criticism, and the state's rehabilitation and
   apologies — judging the machinery, and whether amends can
   be made. The deepened lessons are The Albin Family, Uschi
   Waser — Children of the Road, Behind the Facades of a Home,
   Florian Branger — Guilt and Internment, and Can Amends Be
   Made?; the new lesson is Teaching the Unit at Sek II.

Switzerland's own way, preserved whole: civics here is taught
as one cantonal case-study unit in the country's own three
school languages (German, Italian, Romansh): pupils work from
original sources on how Swiss authorities once took children
from poor families, placed them in homes and institutions and
interned adults — and judge for themselves where help became
coercion, what fundamental rights were violated, and whether
amends can be made (official apologies 2010/2013/2017 and
solidarity contributions are part of the story). The teacher
companions turn each reader case into source-work lessons under
Lehrplan 21 — pupils interrogate the authorities' own documents
(D1–D5 per case at Sek I; to D7/D10/D11 at Sek II) before
judging. At Sek II the same five cases are re-worked with the
full documentary record, so older pupils judge the machinery of
the welfare state, not only the stories.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
books' own shape, walked in order):

- *Care or coercion?:* "One canton, five true cases. First,
  what the measures were and the law behind them — then the
  Albin family's eight children, Uschi Waser's 22 placements,
  Cornelia Studer behind the facades of a home, Florian
  Branger interned again and again, and Ruedi Hofer passed
  from place to place. At the end, the question the whole
  unit asks: can amends be made? Then, one level up, the same
  cases again — this time from the state's own files."
