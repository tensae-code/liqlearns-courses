# Civics and Citizenship — Nicaragua — curriculum

One course for Nicaragua inside the Civics and
Citizenship folder, built from Nicaragua's own government
textbooks: the Ministerio de Educación (MINED)
Convivencia y Civismo student books for Grades 2–6 of
Primary and the Convivencia y Desarrollo Rural
self-study modules for Grades 7, 9 and 10 of rural
distance secondary (Educación Secundaria a Distancia en
el Campo), all in Spanish, as filed in the placements
filing for this build (`nicaragua-placements.md` — see
ABOUT.md for the full book list, the verdicts, and every
label carried exactly as filed). The organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by
what they prove. The path is recommended, never forced
— a learner can jump in higher; the "start here instead"
hints point down this ladder. The grades the books come
from appear only in provenance (ABOUT.md and each
lesson's `lesson/sources.md`).

The ladder follows Nicaragua's own books' progression,
as the filing's ladder sketch lays it out: a single
spine from Grade 2 to Grade 10 — "Convivencia y
Civismo" in primary, "Convivencia y Desarrollo Rural"
in the rural secondary modules. Convivencia y Civismo
starts from living together — self-knowledge, emotions,
solidarity and good manners in home, school and
community — and builds the citizen outward: pride in
the patria and its symbols, Nicaraguan cultural
identity, a first meeting with the Constitution and
children's rights, road safety, taxes, and working for
the common good. Cross-cutting families run at every
level: living-together (values and convivencia),
national-identity and my-patria (symbols, heroes,
Central America), being-a-citizen (rights, Constitution,
State), road-safety, protecting-myself (health and
sexuality), media-and-taxes (consumer and taxpayer),
mother-earth (environment) and the-common-good.

## Level 1 — Living Together Begins — 21 lessons

The first Convivencia y Civismo book (Grade 2): living together begins — self-knowledge, emotions, solidarity and good manners in home, school and community; pride in the patria and its symbols; Nicaraguan cultural identity; a first meeting with the Constitution and children's rights; road safety; danger noticed in time; advertising and taxes; and organising for the common good. Lessons: Knowing and Managing Myself · Solidarity Conflict and Respect · Words Responsibility and Honesty · Helping At Home School and Community · Rules and Good Manners · My Patria and Its Symbols · Heroes and Patriotic Fiestas · Music Dance and Typical Dress · Customs Traditions and Celebrations · The Constitution · Human Rights and Childrens Rights · A Name and a Nationality · Rights Duties and the Childhood Code · Walking and Circulating Safely · Pedestrian Zones and Bicycles · Using Transport · Health Habits · Limits in Affectionate Relations · Advertising and Children · Why Taxes Are Paid · Organising for the Common Good.

## Level 2 — The Same Spine, Deepened — 23 lessons

Grade 3 deepens Grade 2's ladder: identity and self-love first, then national culture and dances, the patria dressed in gala, citizenship explicitly respecting human rights — including women's rights under Law 779 and the Declaration of Mother Earth — road norms, self-protection with the Red Cross, and learning to buy. Lessons: My Own Identity and Self Love · Growing and Behaving · Friends Cooperation and Helping · Manners and Behaviour · Impulses Communication and Respect · Our National Culture · Nicaraguan Dances · My Beautiful Patria · Symbols and Gala · Remembering Heroes · We Are All Equal · My Rights and Responsibilities · Equal Opportunities · Womens Rights Law 779 · Declaration of Mother Earth · Signs and Circulation Norms · Bicycle Safety · Behaviour on Transport · Taking Care of Myself · The Red Cross · Eating and Cleanliness · Preventing Natural Disasters · Learning to Buy.

## Level 3 — The Carta Magna and the Powers of the State — 23 lessons

Grade 4 organises the same world by named themes: identity and convivencia, Nicaragua's cultural regions (Pacific, Caribbean Coast, north and centre), the patria with Central America, the carta magna itself with the Powers of the State, human rights with equality between boys and girls, men and women, and consumer education joined to citizen taxation. Lessons: Accepting Myself With Optimism · Companionship Communication Friendship · Urbanidad and Good Manners · Autonomous Decisions · My Values and Responsibility · Nicaraguan Cultural Identity · Regions and Their Traditions · Music Myths Crafts and Food · My Patria · Patriotic and National Symbols · Proceres and Forjadores · Constitutional Principles · Powers of the State · Human Rights and the Universal Declaration · Childrens Rights and Duties · Equality of Rights · Road Culture and Signs · Land Transport · Healthy Life and Sexuality Education · Blood Is Life · Consumer Education · Expenses and Taxes · Working for the Common Good.

## Level 4 — Stories, Central America and the Authorities — 34 lessons

Grade 5 teaches through stories with morals (the carpentry assembly, the flight of the geese, the wolf and the dog) and widens every theme: the patria now includes all of Central America's symbols and heroes, the Constitution comes with its authorities (Executive and Judicial powers), rights include circulation and citizen security, and the consumer unit ends at the DGI tax office. Lessons: Unique and Knowing Myself · The Carpentry Assembly and Sincerity · The Flight of the Geese · Communicating With Others · Negotiated Agreement · Traditions of my People · Dances Dress and Music · Religious Festivities · Typical Foods · The Patria Our Great Home · Civic Duties and Rights · Central American Symbols · Heroes Worthy of Imitation · Know and Respect Our Laws · Executive and Judicial Powers · The Universal Declaration · Social Rights · Freedom As Responsible Attitude · Right to Circulation · Right to Citizen Security · Children As Rights Holders · Knowing Childrens Rights · Living With Equal Rights · Road Network Basics · Avoiding Accidents and Signs · Pedestrian Zones and the Traffic Light · Norms for Circulating · Responsible Sexuality and Sexual Health · Hiv Aids and Myths · What Is Courtship · Blood a Drop of Hope · Inheritance and Saving · The Dgi and Taxes · Working in Alliances.

## Level 5 — The Values Book — Primary Closes — 31 lessons

Grade 6, the final primary book, is the values book: personal and collective identity values practised to live in peace, diversity of Nicaragua's ethnic, cultural and linguistic peoples, Central American heroes, the Constitution's own account of the State's powers, gender and generational focus and empowerment, and tax education by name. Lessons: Personal Identity Values · Autonomy and Creativity · Solidarity and Collaboration · Criticism and Respect · United for the Common Good · Conflict Resolution and Diversity · Patronal and Traditional Fiestas · Palo De Mayo · Unique Fiestas and Dances · Saints Fiestas and Dances · Legends and Proverbs · Central American National Symbols · Similarities and Differences of Symbols · Central American Heroes · Knowing Our Constitution · The Powers in the Constitution · My Rights and Duties · Freedom of Thought and Social Rights · Rest and Free Time · Child Rights Convention and Code · Gender Focus and Empowerment · Road Safety Knowledge · Traffic Signs · Road Development and Driving Norms · Sexuality Education · Rights of People With Hiv · Responsibility in Couples · Pregnancy Prevention and Blood · Consumers Have Rights · Tax Education · Vanguard in Values.

## Level 6 — Rights and Mother Earth in the Countryside — 14 lessons

The Grade 7 rural module binds rights to the land: human rights and their restitution in the countryside and in education, peasant family values and women's protagonism, the rural community's development, and love of Mother Earth with Nicaragua's environmental strategy and the Universal Declaration of the Rights of Mother Earth. Filed PARTIAL: the agricultural-technique lessons of its Unit II (horticulture, fertilisation, irrigation, pests, poultry-keeping and their management, printed pp. 36–81) are named in the contents map and not placed — they are farming instruction, not civics. Lessons: Fundamental Human Rights · Duties · The Universal Declaration · Restitution of Rights · Women Protection and Protagonism · Peasant Family Values · Rural Development · Support Programmes for Peasant Production · Caring for the Planet and Environment · Biodiversity and Deterioration · Government Action for the Environment · Climate Change and the National Strategy · Friendly Energy · Rights of Mother Earth.

## Level 7 — The Constitution, Title by Title — 13 lessons

The Grade 9 rural module reads the Constitution title by title — fundamental principles, the State, nationality, then the rights chapters (individual, political, social, family, labour) — adds the Labour Code's protection of working children and adolescents, and turns love of Mother Earth into energy: its forms and sources, clean home technologies, water harvesting and safe rubbish handling. Filed PARTIAL: its Unit II (agricultural and livestock production) is named in the contents map, not placed. Lessons: The Constitution Its Content · The State and Nationality · Individual and Political Rights · Social Family and Labour Rights · Education and Culture in the Constitution · The Labour Code and Child Work · Sex and Gender Concepts · Paternity Maternity and Teen Pregnancy · Energy Its Properties and Forms · Sources of Energy · Technologies That Protect the Environment · Water Harvesting · Safe Rubbish Handling.

## Level 8 — Life Skills, the Family Code and the Taxpayer — 16 lessons

The Grade 10 rural module opens with the ten life skills, then the Family Code as an instrument of rights and duties, then the tax law every citizen must meet (income tax and VAT) — and closes love of Mother Earth as rural tourism: Nicaragua's own routes (coffee, water, colonial and volcanoes) and tourism entrepreneurship as community work. Filed PARTIAL: its Unit II (industrial crops and farm planning) is named in the contents map, not placed. Lessons: Life Skills Managing Tension and Emotions · Creative and Critical Thinking · Problems Conflicts and Decisions · Life Skills in Entrepreneurship · The Family Code · Articles of the Family Code · Tax Law and Tax Power · Income Tax Ir · Value Added Tax Iva · Tourism Concept and Kinds · Kinds of Rural Tourism · Sustainable Rural Tourism Attractions · Tourism Services · Nicaraguas Tourist Routes · Socioeconomic Importance of Rural Tourism · Tourism Entrepreneurship.

## Where the build stands

- **Built in full, from the filing**: all eight levels
  above are stocked — 176 placements stocking 175
  lessons. The build is complete as filed; nothing in
  the filing is left unbuilt.
- **Dedupe**: the coined lesson names in the filing
  were deduped before building: 176 placements stock
  175 lessons. One coined name appears twice —
  Working in Alliances, filed at Level 4 (Grade 5,
  printed p. 158) and again at Level 5 (Grade 6,
  printed p. 160) — it is ONE lesson whose parts are
  the two books' treatments, in the books' own order;
  the lesson sits at the first level where its name
  appears (Level 4), and each part's provenance row
  (book + printed page, exactly as filed) is in that
  lesson's `lesson/sources.md`. Lessons with two or
  more parts carry sub lessons in filing order;
  single-part lessons stand whole, with no sub lessons.
- **Footing**: all 175 lessons stand on student books
  or student modules (176 student placements). No
  lesson stands on a teacher guide or a programme —
  the filing names none — so no lesson in this course
  stands at acceptance 0 (see ABOUT.md).
- **PARTIAL books, carried as filed**: Book 2 (Grade 7
  module), Book 7 (Grade 9 module) and Book 8 (Grade
  10 module) are filed PARTIAL — their civics parts are
  placed in full and their agricultural parts are named
  in the contents maps in ABOUT.md, not placed. The
  other five books are filed COMPLETE.
- **A counting note, carried plainly**: the filing's
  per-book tallies sum to 175, but the filing carries
  176 placement lines — Book 5's tally reads "33
  placements" while 34 placement lines are filed under
  it. This build stocks all 176 filed lines; the
  filing's lines, not its tally, are the count used
  here.
- **Not built, named plainly**: the Grade 8 and Grade
  11 rural modules were not among the filing group's
  records (the ladder sketch says so itself), so no
  level in this course pretends to their content; the
  ladder passes from the Grade 7 module (Level 6) to
  the Grade 9 module (Level 7) exactly as filed.
  Glossary and bibliography sections in the books are
  inventory-only in the filing, not placed, and no
  lesson pretends to them. The Grade 7 module is filed
  UNHELD in the census and STAGED under the
  push-don't-delete rule (see ABOUT.md); this build
  itself downloaded and staged nothing (DISK HOLD in
