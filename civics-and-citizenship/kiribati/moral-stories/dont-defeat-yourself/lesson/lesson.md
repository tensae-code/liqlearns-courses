# Don't Defeat Yourself

## Do it first

Think of a time you nearly gave up on something — learning, work, a duty. What would finishing have needed? Write it down. This closing text of the Year 7 readers teaches the refusal to give up on oneself.

Don't Defeat Yourself teaches that a person's own surrender is the deepest defeat: keep standing, keep trying, and the difficulty stays outside you instead of becoming you.

The filing carries this text from the book's contents page (the contents-verified read of Term Three); its provenance row below is carried exactly as filed.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: do not defeat yourself: the person who gives up on himself has lost before anyone else could beat him. Give one real example from your own life or from the Kiribati you live in, and name the lesson's key words: perseverance, giving up. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: do not defeat yourself: the person who gives up on himself has lost before anyone else could beat him.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
