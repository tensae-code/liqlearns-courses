# The Change That Brings Happiness

## Do it first

Name one habit — in yourself or in a home you know — that makes family life harder. Now name what life in that home would look like if the habit stopped. Hold that picture: this lesson's story walks it.

Teeta smoked and drank heavily. When his friend died of drink and tobacco, Teeta saw where his own road ended, and he gave up smoking, alcohol and grog. He became a committed father, and his family's happiness came from that change.

The story is by Vicky Claude, a named Kiribati author, and it teaches the way this course teaches: a village character, a turning point, a moral turn — not a rule or a definition.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: a person can change: Teeta, a heavy smoker and drinker, sees his friend die of drink and tobacco, gives up smoking, alcohol and grog, and becomes a committed father — change of habit is the road to family happiness. Give one real example from your own life or from the Kiribati you live in, and name the lesson's key words: change, habit, smoking, alcohol, father, Vicky Claude. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: a person can change: Teeta, a heavy smoker and drinker, sees his friend die of drink and tobacco, gives up smoking, alcohol and grog, and becomes a committed father — change of habit is the road to family happiness.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
