# Think Before You Act

## Do it first

Recall something you did quickly that you wished afterwards you had thought about first. Write what the pause would have changed. This lesson — the first text of Term Three — teaches that pause.

Thinking before you act means stopping, picturing what the act will do — to you and to others — and only then choosing. The act you choose after thinking is yours; the act done without thinking chooses you.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: pause and think before you act — the space between wanting to do a thing and doing it is where character works. Give one real example from your own life or from the Kiribati you live in, and name the lesson's key words: think, pause, act. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: pause and think before you act — the space between wanting to do a thing and doing it is where character works.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
