# Hands-on — Government in Kiribati

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Name who leads your country, who leads its government, and one thing the government does for your island. This lesson maps the whole of it: government in Kiribati.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Kiribati is governed democratically: a head of state and a head of government, citizens with democratic rights, a government with set functions, and levels of government reaching from the nation to the island. This lesson stands on the KCSE Civic syllabus alone — evidence only, labelled as such. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
