# Lesson plan — Law Making and the Maneaba

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Kiribati · **Title:** State, Law and Custom · **Lesson:** Law Making and the Maneaba
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — State, Law and Custom (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** laws are made in steps by the Maneaba ni Maungatabu, Kiribati's national parliament: a bill is proposed, passed, assented to, and becomes an act — and a country with no laws has no common protection. This lesson stands on the KCSE Civic syllabus alone — evidence only, labelled as such.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Bills, assent and acts** — A proposed law is a bill — private or government. Passed by the Maneaba ni Maungatabu and given assent, a bill becomes an act: law that binds everyone.
2. **The steps and the Maneaba's functions** — The syllabus teaches the steps of law-making in order and the functions of the Maneaba ni Maungatabu, the national parliament where Kiribati's laws are made.
3. **Why law-making matters** — The syllabus asks why law-making matters and what happens with no laws: without made law there is no shared rule to protect the weak, settle disputes or order common life.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
