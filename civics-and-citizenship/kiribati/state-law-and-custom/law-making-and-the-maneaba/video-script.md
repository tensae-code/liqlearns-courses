# Video script — Law Making and the Maneaba

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

This lesson is about one thing.
Laws are made in steps by the Maneaba ni Maungatabu, Kiribati's national parliament: a bill is proposed, passed, assented to, and becomes an act — and a country with no laws has no common protection. This lesson stands on the KCSE Civic syllabus alone — evidence only, labelled as such.

A proposed law is a bill — private or government. Passed by the Maneaba ni Maungatabu and given assent, a bill becomes an act: law that binds everyone.

The syllabus teaches the steps of law-making in order and the functions of the Maneaba ni Maungatabu, the national parliament where Kiribati's laws are made.

The syllabus asks why law-making matters and what happens with no laws: without made law there is no shared rule to protect the weak, settle disputes or order common life.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
