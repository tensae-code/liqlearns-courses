# Civics and Citizenship — Kiribati course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Kiribati inside the Civics and Citizenship
folder, organised by **levels, not grades**: a learner climbs
when they have proved the level, not when a school calendar
says so. The school grades the books come from appear only in
each lesson's sources (provenance), never as the course's
organisation. The path follows Kiribati's own order as its
books lay it out: junior Moral Education first — character
through Kiribati stories, then the guides' strands from the
pupil's own responsibilities to social citizenship — and above
that foundation the senior KCSE course: state, law and custom
first, constitutional machinery second, then a full year of
law and justice in working detail. The two filings' ladder
sketches are reconciled into this one ladder: the junior
filing's L1 is Level 1; the senior filing's L1–L4 are Levels
2–5.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Moral Education readers and guides and its KCSE Social
Studies (Civic) syllabus — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in
a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours;
the lessons are the books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book
list, the record-only leads, and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **Moral Stories and First Citizenship** — character learned
   through Kiribati stories first (an ordinary person made
   extraordinary, the change that brings happiness, one good
   turn, honour in the family, thinking before acting, living
   a good life, being responsible, an alcoholic father's cost
   to his family, not defeating yourself), then the guides'
   strands: individuality and responsibilities, traditional,
   social and spiritual values, understanding civic
   participation, and social citizenship — belonging in the
   family, ownership and collaboration in the community, the
   responsible and accountable citizen. 15 lessons, in five
   titles: `moral-stories/` (9 lessons),
   `individuality-and-responsibilities/` (1 lesson),
   `traditional-social-and-spiritual-values/` (1 lesson),
   `understanding-civic-participation/` (1 lesson),
   `social-citizenship/` (3 lessons).
2. **State, Law and Custom** — government in Kiribati
   (democratic government, the head of state and head of
   government, citizenship and democratic rights, the functions
   and levels of government), the sources of law, law-making
   in the Maneaba ni Maungatabu (bills, assent and acts), and
   custom and customary law. 4 lessons, in one title:
   `state-law-and-custom/` (4 lessons).
3. **The Constitution, Rights and the Courts** — the
   Constitution and the hierarchy of laws (the supreme law),
   the democratic government system (sovereignty, the three
   arms, checks and balances), fundamental rights, citizens'
   rights, and the court system's shape: its structure and
   hierarchy, its types of courts and their jurisdiction, and
   the adversarial and inquisitorial models. 7 lessons, in
   three titles: `the-constitution/` (2 lessons),
   `rights-and-citizenship/` (2 lessons),
   `courts-and-dispute-resolution/` (3 lessons).
4. **Criminal and Civil Law** — criminal and civil
   proceedings and the difference between them, classification
   of offences, burden and standard of proof, the key stages
   in proceedings, civil proceedings and the nature of civil
   claims (torts and protected interests), and conducting
   civil proceedings (the parties and the remedies). 6
   lessons, in one title: `criminal-and-civil-law/`
   (6 lessons).
5. **The People Who Run Justice** — the roles of judges and
   magistrates, judicial functions and the elements of a
   judgment, the roles of court officers, clerks and
   registrars; then the roles of police, the roles of
   prosecutors, and the bar, lawyers, paralegals and legal
   aid — the citizen's helpers closing the course. 6 lessons,
   in two titles: `judges-and-court-officers/` (3 lessons),
   `police-prosecutors-and-lawyers/` (3 lessons).

**Totals: 38 lessons** (Level 1: 15 · Level 2: 4 · Level 3: 7 ·
Level 4: 6 · Level 5: 6 — from six books: three Year 7 student
readers, two teacher guides, and the KCSE Civic syllabus;
38 placements stocking 38 lessons).
