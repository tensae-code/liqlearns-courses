# Criminal and Civil Proceedings

## Do it first

A man steals a boat; a woman is owed money for fish she sold. One case is criminal, one civil. Which is which — and who brings each case? This lesson teaches the difference at the root of Year 11's law study.

## Sub lesson 1 — Criminal, non-criminal and civil cases

The syllabus sorts cases: criminal, non-criminal and civil, and teaches the difference between criminal and civil law.

## Sub lesson 2 — The criminal court's function and structure

The criminal court has its own function and structure in the magistrates' and high courts; the syllabus teaches both levels.

## Sub lesson 3 — Juries and assessors

Juries and assessors take part in criminal proceedings; the syllabus teaches who they are and what they do.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: criminal law and civil law are different roads: criminal cases answer offences against the community, civil cases settle disputes between parties — and the criminal court, with its juries and assessors, is built for the first road. This lesson stands on the KCSE Civic syllabus alone — evidence only, labelled as such. Give one real example from your own life or from the Kiribati you live in, and name the lesson's key words: criminal law, civil law, jury, assessors. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
