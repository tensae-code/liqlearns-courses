# Hands-on — Burden and Standard of Proof

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
You accuse someone of taking your property. Who must prove it — you, or must they prove they did not? And how certain must the court be? This lesson teaches the two ideas that answer those questions.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: who must prove, and how surely: the burden of proof says who carries the proving, the standard of proof says how convincing it must be — and both differ between criminal and civil cases. This lesson stands on the KCSE Civic syllabus alone — evidence only, labelled as such. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
