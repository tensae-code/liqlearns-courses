# Civics and Citizenship — Kiribati course — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

Civics belongs to the country: this course is Kiribati's own
— built from Kiribati's Moral Education readers and teacher
guides for Years 7–8 and its KCSE Social Studies (Civic)
Syllabus for Years 10–11 (all English; the readers and guides
COMPLETE, the syllabus COMPLETE). Inside the course the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: the titles below
ARE the levels' titles, in path order — what the learner
proves, step by step. The grades the books come from live
only in each lesson's sources.

The path's own logic is Kiribati's, reconciled from the two
filings' ladder sketches: the junior foundation first
(stories and first citizenship, Years 7–8 Moral Education),
because that is the foundation the senior civics stands on;
then the senior syllabus' own two-year order — state, law and
custom first, constitutional machinery second, then a full
year of law and justice in working detail. That ladder IS the
path order (recommended, never forced — a learner can jump in
higher; the "start here instead" hints point down this
ladder).

## The titles

**Level 1 — moral stories and first citizenship** (Years 7–8
Moral Education: the three Year 7 readers and the two teacher
guides):

- `moral-stories/` — Level 1 (the Year 7 readers, Terms
  One–Three). From Ordinary to Extraordinary (an ordinary
  person becoming extraordinary through character — the
  opening text of the Year 7 course) · The Change That Brings
  Happiness (Teeta gives up smoking, alcohol and grog and
  becomes a committed father — change of habit as the road to
  family happiness) · One Good Turn Deserves Another (kindness
  returned) · Honour in the Family (the Bita and Erea story:
  honour God first, and love, honour and respect flow from it)
  · Think Before You Act (the pause before acting) · Live a
  Good Life (what living a good life means) · Be Responsible
  (responsibility as a habit) · An Alcoholic Father (what a
  father's drinking does to a family) · Don't Defeat Yourself
  (not giving up on oneself).
- `individuality-and-responsibilities/` — Level 1 (Year 7
  teacher guide, Strand 1). Individuality and
  Responsibilities (the year's first strand — the pupil's own
  character and duties; teacher-guide evidence only).
- `traditional-social-and-spiritual-values/` — Level 1 (Year 7
  teacher guide, Strand 2). Traditional, Social and Spiritual
  Values (Kiribati's traditional, social and spiritual values
  as a teaching strand; teacher-guide evidence only).
- `understanding-civic-participation/` — Level 1 (Year 7
  teacher guide, Strand 3). Understanding Civic Participation
  (civic participation — the junior course's explicit civics
  strand; teacher-guide evidence only).
- `social-citizenship/` — Level 1 (Year 8 teacher guide,
  Term 1, Strand 4). Sense of Belonging in the Family
  (belonging in the family as the first circle of citizenship)
  · Ownership and Collaboration in the Community (owning and
  working together in the community) · Responsible and
  Accountable Citizen (the responsible, accountable citizen as
  the strand's end). All three: teacher-guide evidence only.

**Level 2 — state, law and custom** (KCSE Year 10, Term 1 —
the senior filing's L1):

- `state-law-and-custom/` — Level 2. Government in Kiribati
  (democratic government, head of state and head of
  government, citizenship and democratic rights, the functions
  and levels of government) · Sources of Law (identifying,
  describing and explaining the sources of law in Kiribati) ·
  Law Making and the Maneaba (bills, assent and acts; the
  steps of law-making and the functions of the Maneaba ni
  Maungatabu) · Custom and Customary Law (custom, customs
  practised today, and the difference between custom and
  customary law). Syllabus evidence only.

**Level 3 — the Constitution, rights and the courts** (KCSE
Year 10, Terms 2–3 — the senior filing's L2):

- `the-constitution/` — Level 3. The Constitution and
  Hierarchy of Laws (what a constitution is, Kiribati's
  Constitution, the hierarchy of laws, the supreme law) ·
  Democratic Government System (democracy and sovereignty, the
  three arms, checks and balances).
- `rights-and-citizenship/` — Level 3. Fundamental Rights
  (rights protected under the Constitution and their place in
  a healthy family) · Citizens' Rights (citizenship, how one
  becomes a citizen, and a right that supports democratic
  government).
- `courts-and-dispute-resolution/` — Level 3. Court Structure
  and Hierarchy (the courts' order and each court's function)
  · Types of Courts (jurisdiction; criminal, civil and lands
  courts) · Adversarial and Inquisitorial Models (the two
  models of justice and how the adversarial system operates).
  All of Level 3: syllabus evidence only.

**Level 4 — criminal and civil law** (KCSE Year 11, Term 1 —
the senior filing's L3):

- `criminal-and-civil-law/` — Level 4. Criminal and Civil
  Proceedings (the difference between criminal and civil law;
  the criminal court, juries and assessors) · Classification
  of Offences (the classes of offences, including the third
  intermediate class) · Burden and Standard of Proof (who
  proves, and how surely, in criminal and civil cases) · Key
  Stages in Proceedings (the critical stages and their
  differences) · Civil Proceedings and Claims (torts, their
  types, and the interests civil law protects) · Conducting
  Civil Proceedings (plaintiff, claimant and petitioner; how
  proceedings are conducted and the remedies practised in
  Kiribati). Syllabus evidence only.

**Level 5 — the people who run justice** (KCSE Year 11,
Terms 2–3 — the senior filing's L4):

- `judges-and-court-officers/` — Level 5. Roles of Judges and
  Magistrates (where their power comes from, their roles, its
  limits) · Judicial Functions (the two main functions, the
  dimensions of judicial administration, the elements of a
  judgment) · Court Officers and Clerks (the differing
  responsibilities of court officers, clerks and registrars,
  and their effectiveness in delivering justice).
- `police-prosecutors-and-lawyers/` — Level 5. Roles of
  Police (their significant powers, critical functions, and
  effectiveness in keeping peace and order) · Roles of
  Prosecutors (the three main functions and the role's ethical
  and professional standards) · The Bar, Lawyers and Legal Aid
  (the bar and lawyers — functions, qualifications, regulation,
  code of ethics — and paralegals and legal aid). All of
  Level 5: syllabus evidence only.
