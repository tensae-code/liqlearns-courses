# Hands-on — Court Officers and Clerks

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
A judgment is given — but the file is lost, the date unrecorded, the order never issued. Has justice been delivered? This lesson teaches the officers who make a court's decisions real.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: a court runs on more than judges: court officers, clerks and registrars carry differing responsibilities, split between administrative and judicial parts — and their effectiveness decides whether justice is actually delivered. This lesson stands on the KCSE Civic syllabus alone — evidence only, labelled as such. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
