# About the Civics and Citizenship — Kiribati course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this
course comes from government textbooks — this country's own
Moral Education readers and guides and its KCSE Social Studies
(Civic) syllabus. The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a
tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are
ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Kiribati inside the Civics and
Citizenship folder (one course per country). Inside it, the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place is
found by what they prove, and each level stands on the ones
before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced gate.
The school grades the books come from appear only in each
lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

The course as built has **five stocked levels**, from two
filings combined into one course
(`kiribati-placements.md`, the senior KCSE filing, and
`kiribati-placements-b.md`, the junior Moral Education filing):

1. **Level 1 — Moral stories and first citizenship (15
   lessons).** The junior foundation the senior civics stands
   on, exactly as the second filing's ladder sketch lays it
   out: nine story lessons from the Year 7 Moral Education
   readers (character learned through Kiribati stories — change
   of habit, honour in the family, responsibility), then the
   teacher guides' strands carrying the pupil from
   individuality and responsibilities through traditional,
   social and spiritual values to civic participation and
   social citizenship (belonging, community collaboration, the
   accountable citizen).
2. **Level 2 — State, law and custom (4 lessons).** The KCSE
   filing's L1: government in Kiribati, the sources of law,
   law-making in the Maneaba ni Maungatabu (the national
   parliament), and custom and customary law.
3. **Level 3 — The Constitution, rights and the courts (7
   lessons).** The KCSE filing's L2: the Constitution and the
   hierarchy of laws, the democratic government system,
   fundamental rights, citizens' rights, and the court system's
   shape — structure, types of courts, and the adversarial and
   inquisitorial models.
4. **Level 4 — Criminal and civil law (6 lessons).** The KCSE
   filing's L3: criminal and civil proceedings, classification
   of offences, burden and standard of proof, key stages in
   proceedings, and civil proceedings, claims and remedies in
   depth.
5. **Level 5 — The people who run justice (6 lessons).** The
   KCSE filing's L4: judges and magistrates, judicial
   functions, court officers and clerks — then police,
   prosecutors, and the citizen's helpers (the bar, lawyers,
   paralegals and legal aid).

The combined ladder renumbers the two filings' sketches into
one: the junior filing's L1 foundation stays Level 1, and the
senior filing's L1–L4 sit above it as Levels 2–5, in the senior
syllabus' own two-year order. Kiribati's own way, preserved
whole: civics is taught almost entirely as law in the Kiribati
setting — the state and its custom (including customary law
and law-making in the Maneaba ni Maungatabu) first, then the
Constitution and rights, then a deep two-year study of courts,
procedure and the people who work in them — and underneath it,
junior Moral Education taught through short locally written
stories: a named Kiribati author, a village character, a moral
turn, rather than through rules or definitions.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
books' own shape, walked in order):

- *Stories first, then the state:* "First, the stories
  Kiribati's own readers tell — an ordinary person made
  extraordinary, a father's change that brings his family
  happiness, honour in the family, thinking before you act —
  and the guides' road from your own responsibilities to
  belonging, community and the accountable citizen. Then the
  state itself: government, the sources of law, law-making in
  the Maneaba ni Maungatabu, and custom beside the law. Then
  the Constitution, your fundamental and citizens' rights, and
  the courts. Then the law at work — criminal and civil cases,
  offences, proof and remedies. And last, the people who run
  justice: judges, court officers, police, prosecutors — and
  the lawyers and legal aid who stand beside the citizen."

## Honesty notes (course level)

- **What the filings do and do not contain.** The filings
  contain: one senior syllabus (Years 10–11 Civic), three
  Year 7 student readers, and two teacher guides (Year 7
  Terms 1–3; Year 8 Term 1) — six distinct books, 38
  placements. They do not contain any Year 7/8 pupil readers
  for terms beyond those named, a Year 8 reader, or the Year 8
  Terms 2–3 guides: the junior filing names that absence as
  library debt in its group log ("no lead names the Year 7/8
  pupil readers for Terms beyond these, a Year 8 reader, or
  the Year 8 Terms 2–3 guides"). Those books are therefore
  **not in this course**, and no lesson pretends to them.
- **Guides-and-syllabus footing, labelled, never hidden.**
  Only 9 of the 38 lessons stand on student books. Levels 2–5
  rest on a syllabus alone and Level 1's strand lessons on
  teacher guides alone; every such lesson's sources say so and
  count 0 in the acceptance reckoning.
- **Assessment sections are inventory-only, not placed.** The
  KCSE syllabus' benchmarks and assessment sections (printed
  pp. 11–12) are named in its contents map above and feed no
  lesson, exactly as filed.
- **Two provenance rows carry no printed page, exactly as
  filed.** "One Good Turn Deserves Another" is filed with its
  book and no printed page; "Don't Defeat Yourself" is filed
  from the Term Three reader's contents page. Both rows are
  carried exactly as filed — no page was invented for either.
- **No wrong-file label is carried, because neither filing
  carries one.** Neither placements file states a wrong-file
  or cross-country label for any Kiribati record; nothing of
  that kind has been added here.
- **UNREAD books: none named as held-but-unread.** The
  record-only leads above are not unread textbooks (a WASH
  programme page, syllabus documents whose content the guides
  carry, and one duplicate Drive copy). The library debt named
  in the first note is books no lead names — absent, not
  UNREAD.
- **No ISBN is claimed for any book** — no ISBN is printed in
  any of the six books, as filed.
- **No book was downloaded for this build** (DISK HOLD in
  force); everything was built from the two placements
  filings.
