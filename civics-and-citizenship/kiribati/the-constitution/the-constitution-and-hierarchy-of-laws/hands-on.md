# Hands-on — The Constitution and Hierarchy of Laws

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Two rules clash: a local rule says one thing, the Constitution another. Which wins? Write your answer before reading on — this lesson teaches why the answer is never in doubt in Kiribati.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: the Constitution is the supreme law of Kiribati: it stands at the top of a hierarchy of laws, and every other law must agree with it. This lesson stands on the KCSE Civic syllabus alone — evidence only, labelled as such. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
