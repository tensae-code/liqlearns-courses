# Hands-on — Democratic Government System

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
If one office held all power — making laws, enforcing them and judging disputes — what could stop it abusing that power? Write your answer. This lesson shows the Constitution's answer: three arms, each checking the others.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: democracy means the people are sovereign, and the Constitution splits their power among three arms of government that check and balance one another. This lesson stands on the KCSE Civic syllabus alone — evidence only, labelled as such. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
