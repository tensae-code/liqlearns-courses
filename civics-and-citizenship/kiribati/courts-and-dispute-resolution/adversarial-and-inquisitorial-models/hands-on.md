# Hands-on — Adversarial and Inquisitorial Models

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Two ways to find the truth in a dispute: let each side argue its case, or let the judge dig for the facts. Which do you trust more, and why? This lesson teaches both models — and the one Kiribati works by.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: justice has two great models: the adversarial, where two sides contest before a neutral judge, and the inquisitorial, where the court itself investigates — Kiribati's system is taught through the adversarial model. This lesson stands on the KCSE Civic syllabus alone — evidence only, labelled as such. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
