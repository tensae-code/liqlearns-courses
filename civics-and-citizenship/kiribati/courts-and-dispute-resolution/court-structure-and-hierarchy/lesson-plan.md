# Lesson plan — Court Structure and Hierarchy

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Kiribati · **Title:** Courts and Dispute Resolution · **Lesson:** Court Structure and Hierarchy
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — The Constitution, Rights and the Courts (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Kiribati's courts stand in a hierarchy, each with its function, headed in their own spheres by the Chief Justice, the Chief Registrar and the Chief Magistrate. This lesson stands on the KCSE Civic syllabus alone — evidence only, labelled as such.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The court's officers named** — The syllabus first names the court and its heads: the Chief Justice, the Chief Registrar and the Chief Magistrate, and what each title means.
2. **Hierarchy and function** — Kiribati's courts stand in a hierarchy; the syllabus teaches the order of the courts and the function of each court within it.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
