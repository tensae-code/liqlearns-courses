# State and National Power

## Do it first

Two captains cannot steer one boat the same way at once. Write one job you would give the whole crew and one job you would leave to each small team. The FSM had to make exactly that division — this lesson tells how, and why the old way failed.

## Sub lesson 1 — Sharing power between state and nation

The FSM divides power: some affairs belong to the national government, others remain with the states, and the constitution is the referee of the division.

Sharing is the federation's core bargain — the states gave up some power to gain a nation, and kept the rest.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Trust Territory's mistakes

Under the Trust Territory, power sat far away and centralised — decisions about islanders' lives were made over their heads.

The book reads that time as a set of mistakes to learn from: distance, central control and weak island voice.

## Sub lesson 3 — The lesson built into the FSM

The FSM's division of power is the correction: strong states, a limited national government, and the people's own levels close to home.

When you judge any FSM power dispute, ask the book's question first: does this repeat the Trust Territory's mistake, or correct it?
