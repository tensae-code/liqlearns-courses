# Lesson plan — State and National Power

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Micronesia · **Title:** FSM Government Today · **Lesson:** State and National Power
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — The FSM Government Today (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** power in the FSM is shared between the states and the national government — a division designed by learning from the Trust Territory's mistakes.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Sharing power between state and nation** — The FSM divides power: some affairs belong to the national government, others remain with the states, and the constitution is the referee of the division.
2. **The Trust Territory's mistakes** — Under the Trust Territory, power sat far away and centralised — decisions about islanders' lives were made over their heads.
3. **The lesson built into the FSM** — The FSM's division of power is the correction: strong states, a limited national government, and the people's own levels close to home.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks sharing power means splitting everything in half → the division is by kind of affair, written in the constitution, not a fifty-fifty split of every job.
- Go back to the one thing: power in the FSM is shared between the states and the national government — a division designed by learning from the Trust Territory's mistakes. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
