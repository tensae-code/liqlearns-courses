# Taking Part in Politics

## Do it first

Write three ways a person in your town can affect a public decision without holding any office. Keep the list open — this lesson adds the book's own account of how FSM citizens take part in politics.

## Sub lesson 1 — Voting — the citizen's first tool

Voting chooses the representatives and, through them, the direction of the government — the act the municipalities first practised in Level 2.

A vote unused is a share of self-government handed to whoever bothers to use theirs.

## Sub lesson 2 — Taking part between elections

Participation continues between elections: speaking on public questions, joining community action, and making one's view known to those in office.

A citizen who only votes is present one day in years; a participating citizen is present whenever the community decides.

## Sub lesson 3 — Watching those in power

Taking part includes watching: following what leaders do, asking for accounts, and using the checks the constitution gives the people.

Participation is the people's own check — the one all the written checks finally depend on.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: government by the people only works when the people take part — voting, speaking, organising and watching those in power. Give one real example from your own life or from the FSM you live in, and name the lesson's key words: participation, politics, voting. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
