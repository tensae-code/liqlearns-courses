# The Three Branches

## Do it first

Take one law you know. Write who wrote it, who makes sure it happens, and who would judge a quarrel about it. If your three answers are three different offices, you have already met this lesson's three branches.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Congress — the legislative branch

The FSM Congress is the national legislature: the people's elected representatives meeting to make the nation's laws.

It is the direct heir of the Congress of Micronesia from Level 2 — now the law-maker of a sovereign federation.

## Sub lesson 2 — The President — the executive branch

The President heads the executive branch: carrying out the laws, running the national government's daily work and representing the FSM.

Executive power is action power — bounded by the laws the Congress makes and the constitution above both.

## Sub lesson 3 — The courts — the judicial branch

The courts form the judicial branch: judging cases, settling disputes about what the law means and whether it has been obeyed.

Three branches, three verbs — make, carry out, judge: the separation of powers from Level 2, standing in today's FSM.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the FSM national government works in three branches — the Congress makes the laws, the President carries them out, and the courts judge under them. Give one real example from your own life or from the FSM you live in, and name the lesson's key words: three branches, FSM Congress, President. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
