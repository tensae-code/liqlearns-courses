# Hands-on — The Three Branches

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Take one law you know. Write who wrote it, who makes sure it happens, and who would judge a quarrel about it. If your three answers are three different offices, you have already met this lesson's three branches.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: the FSM national government works in three branches — the Congress makes the laws, the President carries them out, and the courts judge under them. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
