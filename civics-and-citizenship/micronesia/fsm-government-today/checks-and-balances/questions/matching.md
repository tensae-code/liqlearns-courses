# Checks and Balances — matching

Match each part of the lesson with its teaching.

**1.** How the branches check each other — a)

**2.** Sharing power, not hoarding it — b)

**3.** Where the checks strain — c)


Meanings:
a) Each branch holds tools against the others: laws can be blocked, actions reviewed, appointments and spending watched, judgments enforced or resisted through lawful channels.
b) Checks and balances turn the separation of powers from three sealed boxes into one shared government, each part inside the others' sight.
c) The book names the problems honestly: checks can slow government, blur responsibility, or become weapons in personal and political fights.

**Answer key:** 1. a; 2. b; 3. c
