# About the Civics and Citizenship — Micronesia course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this
course comes from government textbooks — this country's own
Micronesian Government book (Micronesian Government: Yesterday,
Today, and Tomorrow). The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a
tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are
ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Micronesia (the Federated States of
Micronesia) inside the Civics and Citizenship folder (one course
per country). Inside it, the organisation is by **levels, not
grades**: a learner's place is found by what they prove, and
each level stands on the ones before it as a recommended-first
path — never a forced gate. The school grades the books come
from appear only in each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as
provenance.

The course as built has **three stocked levels**, all from the
one student book in hand:

1. **Level 1 — How Micronesians governed themselves (5
   lessons).** Unit One of the book: traditional leadership
   across Micronesia, the Chuuk model, the Pohnpei model, the
   Yap model, and the common themes — how leaders gained, held
   and were checked in their power.
2. **Level 2 — The road back to self-government (4 lessons).**
   Unit Three of the book: the first elections and the
   municipalities, separation of powers, the Congress of
   Micronesia and the fight for independence, and the writing of
   the FSM Constitution with its Founding Fathers.
3. **Level 3 — The FSM government today (9 lessons).** Unit
   Four of the book, its Chapter 11 whole: the three levels of
   government, state and national power, the three branches,
   checks and balances, the courts and traditional justice, the
   bureaucracy, traditional leaders inside today's government,
   taking part in politics, and a law-making case study — the
   FSM Amnesty Bill.

Micronesia's own way, preserved whole: the FSM's civics is
taught as one long story of self-government — how Micronesians
governed themselves traditionally, how that was overlaid by
foreign rule, how islanders won self-government back step by
step (municipal elections first), and how the federation they
built divides power between four states and the nation.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
book's own shape, walked in order):

- *One long story of self-government:* "First, how
  Micronesians governed themselves — Chuuk, Pohnpei and Yap,
  each in its own way, and the themes they share. Then the road
  back: first elections in the municipalities, powers separated,
  a Congress of Micronesia, independence, and a constitution
  written by the Founding Fathers. Then the government those
  founders built, as it stands today: three levels, four states
  and a nation, three branches checking each other, traditional
  leaders inside the state, and you — taking part."
