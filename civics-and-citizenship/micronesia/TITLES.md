# Civics and Citizenship — Micronesia course — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

Civics belongs to the country: this course is Micronesia's own
— built from the Federated States of Micronesia's Micronesian
Government student book (English, 2008 first edition,
COMPLETE). Inside the course the organisation is by **levels,
not grades**: the titles below ARE the levels' titles, in path
order — what the learner proves, step by step. The grades the
book comes from live only in each lesson's sources.

The path's own logic is Micronesia's: one long story of
self-government — how Micronesians governed themselves
traditionally, how islanders won self-government back step by
step (municipal elections first), and how the federation they
built divides power between four states and the nation. That
ladder IS the path order (recommended, never forced — a learner
can jump in higher; the "start here instead" hints point down
this ladder).

## The titles

**Level 1 — how Micronesians governed themselves** (Unit One
of the book, Traditional Leadership in the FSM):

- `traditional-leadership/` — Level 1 (Unit One, printed
  pp. 11–108). How Micronesians Governed Themselves (the
  traditional leadership systems across Micronesia and how
  authority worked in them) · The Chuuk Model (Chuuk's
  traditional leadership model) · The Pohnpei Model (Pohnpei's
  traditional leadership model, its titles and ranks) · The Yap
  Model (Yap's traditional leadership model) · Themes in
  Traditional Leadership (the common themes — how leaders
  gained, held and were checked in their power).

**Level 2 — the road back to self-government** (Unit Three of
the book, The Long Road to Independence):

- `the-road-to-self-government/` — Level 2 (Unit Three,
  printed pp. 163–249). First Elections and Municipalities
  (the first elections and the municipalities — magistrate,
  legislative council and municipal judge — as the first step
  to self-government) · Separation of Powers (legislative,
  executive and judicial power defined and separated, against
  the traditional joining of powers in one leader) · The
  Congress of Micronesia (the first islander legislature,
  voting for it, and the campaign for independence) · Writing
  the Constitution (the Micronesian Constitutional
  Convention, deciding what the new constitution should say,
  and the Founding Fathers of the FSM).

**Level 3 — the FSM government today** (Unit Four of the book,
Micronesian Government Today, Ch.11):

- `fsm-government-today/` — Level 3 (Unit Four, printed
  pp. 250–288). The Three Levels of Government (national,
  state and municipal government and what each level does) ·
  State and National Power (how power is shared between the
  states and the national government, and the lessons of the
  Trust Territory time) · The Three Branches (the FSM
  Congress, the President and the courts) · Checks and
  Balances (how the branches check each other, and where the
  checks strain) · The Courts and Traditional Justice (the FSM
  Supreme Court and how traditional justice meets the court
  system) · The Bureaucracy (the government bureaucracy and
  the pull of personal and family ties inside it) ·
  Traditional Leaders and the Government (the place of
  traditional leaders inside today's FSM government) · Taking
  Part in Politics (how citizens take part in politics) ·
  Case Study: The Amnesty Bill (a real FSM law-making case
  studied whole).
