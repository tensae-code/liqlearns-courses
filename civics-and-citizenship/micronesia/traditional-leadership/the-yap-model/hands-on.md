# Hands-on — The Yap Model

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Think of a rule in your home or school that nobody wrote down but everybody obeys. Write who enforces it and what happens if it is broken. Yap's model, like that rule, ran on shared knowledge of how things are done.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Yap's traditional leadership model — Yap's own ordered way of holding and exercising authority. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
