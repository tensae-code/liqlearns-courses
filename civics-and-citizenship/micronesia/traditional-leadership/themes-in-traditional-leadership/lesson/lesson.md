# Themes in Traditional Leadership

## Do it first

Look back at the three models you have met — Chuuk, Pohnpei and Yap. Write one thing all three share, and one thing where they differ. This lesson names the shared themes the book itself draws out.

## Sub lesson 1 — How leaders gained power

Across the systems, leaders gained power through recognised paths — birth and lineage, titles, achievement and the community's acceptance working together in different mixes.

No system left gaining power to chance or force alone: the path itself was part of the order.

## Sub lesson 2 — How leaders held power

Leaders held power by carrying their duties — providing, judging fairly, keeping the peace and honouring the obligations of their place.

A leader who served the group kept standing; the holding of power was a daily performance, not a one-time win.

## Sub lesson 3 — How power was checked

Traditional power was checked: by custom, by other ranks and leaders, by the community's respect being withdrawable, and by the duties built into the leader's place.

This is the theme the whole course returns to — the modern constitution's checks and balances have an older Micronesian ancestor.
