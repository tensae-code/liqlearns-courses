# Hands-on — How Micronesians Governed Themselves

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Think of the leaders you know in your own community — a chief, an elder, a family head. For each one, write: how did they become a leader, what can they decide, and what stops them deciding wrongly? This lesson looks at how Micronesia answered those same questions traditionally.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: long before any written constitution, Micronesians governed themselves through traditional leadership systems — and authority in them was real, ordered and answerable. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
