# Hands-on — Separation of Powers

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Take one decision from your school or town — say, setting a rule and punishing someone who breaks it. Write who makes the rule, who carries it out, and who judges the case. If one person does all three, write what could go wrong. That danger is this lesson's subject.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: government power is safer divided — legislative, executive and judicial power defined and separated, against the traditional joining of powers in one leader. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
