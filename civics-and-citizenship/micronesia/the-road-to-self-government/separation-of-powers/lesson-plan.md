# Lesson plan — Separation of Powers

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Micronesia · **Title:** The Road to Self-Government · **Lesson:** Separation of Powers
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — The Road Back to Self-Government (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** government power is safer divided — legislative, executive and judicial power defined and separated, against the traditional joining of powers in one leader.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The three powers named** — Legislative power makes the laws, executive power carries the laws out, and judicial power judges cases under the laws.
2. **Why separate them** — Separated, each power limits the others: the law-maker cannot also be the judge of his own law, and the executor must answer to both.
3. **Against the traditional joining** — The book sets this against the traditional systems, where powers were often joined in one leader and checked by custom instead.
