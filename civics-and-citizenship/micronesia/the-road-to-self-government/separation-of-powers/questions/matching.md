# Separation of Powers — matching

Match each part of the lesson with its teaching.

**1.** The three powers named — a)

**2.** Why separate them — b)

**3.** Against the traditional joining — c)


Meanings:
a) Legislative power makes the laws, executive power carries the laws out, and judicial power judges cases under the laws.
b) Separated, each power limits the others: the law-maker cannot also be the judge of his own law, and the executor must answer to both.
c) The book sets this against the traditional systems, where powers were often joined in one leader and checked by custom instead.

**Answer key:** 1. a; 2. b; 3. c
