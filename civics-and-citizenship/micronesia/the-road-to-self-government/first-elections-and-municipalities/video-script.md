# Video script — First Elections and Municipalities

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Self-government came back step by step — and the first step was the municipalities, where islanders first elected their own magistrates, councils and judges.

The first elections let islanders choose their own local leaders by vote — told in the book through Kosrae's and Etal's first elections.

Voting was new machinery, but its purpose was old: a community choosing who should lead it.

Each municipality had its officers — a magistrate to lead it, a legislative council to make its local rules, and a municipal judge to settle its cases.

In those three offices a whole small government stood: leading, law-making and judging, at the level closest to people's daily lives.

The municipalities matter beyond their size: they were the training ground where Micronesians practised elected self-government again.

From the municipalities the road runs upward — to the Congress of Micronesia and then to independence.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
