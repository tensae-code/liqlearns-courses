# Hands-on — The Four Elements of the Subject

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: The Four Elements of the Subject. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Indonesia's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The subject's four-element architecture at Fase A — Pancasila (flag, anthem, Garuda symbols and sila, the formulators of Pancasila, applying its values at home), UUD NRI 1945 (rules in the family), Bhinneka Tunggal Ika (own and others' identities), NKRI — as the outcome map for grades 1–2; the Fase A outcome map restated for grade 2, tying each chapter to the four elements (Pancasila, UUD NRI 1945, Bhinneka Tunggal Ika, NKRI); the four-element outcome map at Fase B (grades 3–4), where identity, home-as-part-of-NKRI, school rules and rights/obligations enter; the Fase B learning-outcome flow (ATP) completed at grade 4, mapping the four elements across both Fase B years; the four-element outcome map at Fase C (grades 5–6), where the norm system and deliberation join the earlier elements; the Fase C outcome map completed at grade 6 — the four elements at their primary-school fullest (norms, rights and obligations in national life; provinces of the NKRI; deliberation); the four-element outcome map at Fase D as it lands in grade 8 — the UUD NRI 1945 element carrying the constitution and the hierarchy of legislation; the four-element outcome map at the close of Fase D — Pancasila, Constitution, Bhinneka Tunggal Ika and NKRI taught as one related system; the four-element outcome map at Fase E (senior secondary), where legal culture and the citizen's standing join the elements; the four-element outcome map at Fase F (grades 11–12) — constitutional history, harmony in diversity and the forms of the state; the four-element outcome map completed at the end of Fase F — the whole school course's outcomes in one frame. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
