# About the Civics and Citizenship — Hungary course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own *Állampolgári ismeretek* (Citizenship Knowledge) books.
The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is
from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the
lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Hungary inside the Civics and
Citizenship folder (one course per country). Inside it, the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place
is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the
ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced
gate. The school grades the books come from appear only in
each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

Hungary's own way, preserved whole, as filed: every lesson
of the Grade 8 book opens with a picture and a quotation
from Hungary's Fundamental Law (Alaptörvény), so the
constitution frames even family and household topics; the
book moves from the family outward — household money, the
home settlement and its council, nation and homeland
duties, the state's institutions, everyday administration,
consumer protection and the big public systems. The
final-year book revisits the Grade 8 circles at adult
depth — family and nation again, then freedom and
responsibility (human rights, democracy, the Fundamental
Law), the Hungarian state organ by organ, and a heavy
practical strand: enterprise, banks, the state's economic
role, household finance, official business and
consumer/environmental protection, all aimed at the
érettségi and adult life. Hungary's own two civics books
form a spiral — the Grade 12 book explicitly deepens the
Grade 8 circles for the school-leaving citizen.

The course as built has **two stocked levels**, from the

  filing contains 2 distinct read books (the
  *Állampolgári ismeretek* student books for Grades 8 and
  12), 88 placements, and the books' own contents maps.
