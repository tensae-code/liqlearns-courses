# Hands-on — The International Community and Its Principles

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write down the names of any world organisations you have heard of — a world body, a regional body, any body where states meet. Put them in the order you think they were founded. This lesson builds the international community from just such a timeline, then names the principles it works by.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: the international community is the community of states and their organisations, and it works by shared principles — independence and non-interference, equality between states, international cooperation, and the peaceful settlement of disputes. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the news of the world. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
