# Lesson plan — The International Community and Its Principles

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Tunisia · **Title:** The International Community · **Lesson:** The International Community and Its Principles
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — The International Community (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the international community is the community of states and their organisations, and it works by shared principles — independence and non-interference, equality between states, international cooperation, and the peaceful settlement of disputes.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What the international community is** — The international community is the whole body of states and the organisations they have built together to deal with one another.
2. **Its working principles** — The community works by principles its members accept. First, independence and non-interference: each state governs its own internal affairs, and no state interferes inside another.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes up the principles → lay the four principles out as four cards (independence and non-interference, equality, cooperation, peaceful settlement) and match each card to one sentence about what states do.
- Go back to the one thing: the international community is the community of states and their organisations, and it works by shared principles — independence and non-interference, equality between states, international cooperation, and the peaceful settlement of disputes. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
