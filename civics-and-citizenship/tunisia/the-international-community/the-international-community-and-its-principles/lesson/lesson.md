# The International Community and Its Principles

## Do it first

Write down the names of any world organisations you have heard of — a world body, a regional body, any body where states meet. Put them in the order you think they were founded. This lesson builds the international community from just such a timeline, then names the principles it works by.

## Sub lesson 1 — What the international community is

The international community is the whole body of states and the organisations they have built together to deal with one another.

The book builds the idea from a timeline: organisation after organisation appears as states find more they must handle together, until the community itself — not any single state — is the thing being studied.

## Sub lesson 2 — Its working principles

The community works by principles its members accept. First, independence and non-interference: each state governs its own internal affairs, and no state interferes inside another.

Second, equality between states: states stand equal in the community, whatever their size. Third, international cooperation: states work together on what none can settle alone. Fourth, the peaceful settlement of disputes: when states disagree, the dispute is settled peacefully, not by force.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the international community is the community of states and their organisations, and it works by shared principles — independence and non-interference, equality between states, international cooperation, and the peaceful settlement of disputes. Give one real example from your own life or from the world you live in, and name the lesson's key words: international community, independence, non-interference, equality between states, cooperation, peaceful settlement. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
