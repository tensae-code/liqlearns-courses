# Poverty

## Do it first

Write down what a family cannot do when it has almost nothing — not only what it cannot buy, but what it cannot reach: a doctor, a school, clean water, a voice. Your list is the beginning of this lesson: poverty in its forms.

## Sub lesson 1 — The forms of poverty

Poverty takes forms: the lack of income is only the first. Poverty is also the lack of what a life needs — food, health care, schooling, clean water, a secure home — and the lack of a voice in the decisions that govern a life.

To study poverty is to count all its forms, not the money form alone.

## Sub lesson 2 — Its effects

Poverty's effects spread: on health, on learning, on work, on the family, and on a society's ability to develop — each form of poverty producing effects in the others.

That is why the book places poverty among the world challenges facing the citizen: no single state, and no single household, meets it alone.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: poverty is a world challenge with many forms — more than having no money — and its effects reach the whole of a person's and a society's life. Give one real example from your own life or from the world you live in, and name the lesson's key words: poverty, forms of poverty, effects of poverty, world challenges. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
