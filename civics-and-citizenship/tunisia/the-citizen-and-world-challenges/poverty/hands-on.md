# Hands-on — Poverty

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write down what a family cannot do when it has almost nothing — not only what it cannot buy, but what it cannot reach: a doctor, a school, clean water, a voice. Your list is the beginning of this lesson: poverty in its forms.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: poverty is a world challenge with many forms — more than having no money — and its effects reach the whole of a person's and a society's life. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the news of the world. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
