# Environmental Deterioration

## Do it first

Look out of the nearest window, or picture your street. Name one way the environment there is worse than it should be — dirtied water, cut trees, fouled air, wasted land. This lesson takes that one sight and sets it among the world's forms of environmental deterioration.

## Sub lesson 1 — The forms of environmental deterioration

Environmental deterioration takes forms: water dirtied, air fouled, trees and land destroyed, ground wasted — the shared environment worsening in ways that can be named and counted.

The lesson names the forms first, because a challenge that cannot be named cannot be answered.

## Sub lesson 2 — Its effects

Its effects reach the lives built on that environment: health, food, water and livelihood — and they cross borders, so no state meets them inside its own fence alone.

The book closes here for a reason: the citizen who began inside the international community ends facing the challenge that most plainly belongs to the whole community at once.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the environment is deteriorating in many forms, its effects reach every state, and it closes the book's ladder as the challenge no citizen and no state faces alone. Give one real example from your own life or from the world you live in, and name the lesson's key words: environment, environmental deterioration, forms, effects, world challenges. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
