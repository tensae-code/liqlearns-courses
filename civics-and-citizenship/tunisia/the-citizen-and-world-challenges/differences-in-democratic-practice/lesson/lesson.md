# Differences in Democratic Practice

## Do it first

Write down two ways a people can choose its rulers that you have heard of. Do both count as democracy? Keep your answer — this lesson studies how democratic practice differs between states, and what follows from the differences.

## Sub lesson 1 — The forms the difference takes

Democratic practice differs from state to state: states arrange their elections, their representation and their institutions in different forms, while calling the arrangement democracy.

The lesson maps the difference before judging it — what the forms are, and where one state's practice parts from another's.

## Sub lesson 2 — What follows from the differences

Differences in practice have consequences: how citizens take part, how power is checked, and how decisions are made all shift with the form a state has chosen.

The citizen's skill is to read a practice and say what follows from it — for participation, for accountability, and for the citizen's own place in the state.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: states differ in democratic practice — democracy is not done one single way — and a citizen studies the forms the difference takes and what follows from it. Give one real example from your own life or from the world you live in, and name the lesson's key words: democracy, democratic practice, differences between states, forms of democracy. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
