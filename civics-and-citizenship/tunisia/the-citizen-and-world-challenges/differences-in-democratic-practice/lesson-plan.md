# Lesson plan — Differences in Democratic Practice

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Tunisia · **Title:** The Citizen and World Challenges · **Lesson:** Differences in Democratic Practice
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — The Citizen and World Challenges (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** states differ in democratic practice — democracy is not done one single way — and a citizen studies the forms the difference takes and what follows from it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The forms the difference takes** — Democratic practice differs from state to state: states arrange their elections, their representation and their institutions in different forms, while calling the arrangement democracy.
2. **What follows from the differences** — Differences in practice have consequences: how citizens take part, how power is checked, and how decisions are made all shift with the form a state has chosen.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
