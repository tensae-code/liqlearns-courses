# Conflicts

## Do it first

Name one conflict in the world you have heard of. Now ask: who is fighting, over what, and who else is harmed besides the fighters? Hold those answers — this lesson sorts conflicts by their forms and follows their effects.

## Sub lesson 1 — The forms of conflicts

Conflicts take forms: between states and inside states; over land, power, resources and identity; open wars and tensions that have not broken into war.

Sorting a conflict by its form — who is in it, and over what — is the first step to understanding it, before any judgement about it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Their effects

Conflicts' effects fall first on people — lives, homes, livelihoods, schooling — then on states and their economies, and then on the international community, which receives the displaced and must work for settlement.

The citizen studies effects because they are the true size of a conflict: a conflict is as large as everything it harms.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: conflicts in the world take many forms, and their effects fall on people, states and the whole international community the citizen belongs to. Give one real example from your own life or from the world you live in, and name the lesson's key words: conflicts, forms of conflict, effects of conflict, world challenges. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
