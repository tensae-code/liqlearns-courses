# Lesson plan — Conflicts

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Tunisia · **Title:** The Citizen and World Challenges · **Lesson:** Conflicts
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — The Citizen and World Challenges (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** conflicts in the world take many forms, and their effects fall on people, states and the whole international community the citizen belongs to.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The forms of conflicts** — Conflicts take forms: between states and inside states; over land, power, resources and identity; open wars and tensions that have not broken into war.
2. **Their effects** — Conflicts' effects fall first on people — lives, homes, livelihoods, schooling — then on states and their economies, and then on the international community, which receives the displaced and must work for settlement.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: conflicts in the world take many forms, and their effects fall on people, states and the whole international community the citizen belongs to.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school — or, for this book's outward lessons, from the news of the world they live in.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from documents, as the book itself works: read, use, evaluate (أقرأ / أستثمر وأقيّم).

## If the learner is stuck
- Counts only the fighters' losses → the lesson's effects run wider: to civilians, to neighbouring states, and to the community of states that must answer. Follow one effect past the battlefield.
- Go back to the one thing: conflicts in the world take many forms, and their effects fall on people, states and the whole international community the citizen belongs to. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
