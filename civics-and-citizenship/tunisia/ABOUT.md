# About the Civics and Citizenship — Tunisia course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this
course comes from government textbooks — this country's own Civic
Education book for the 3rd year of secondary school, Letters
stream. The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box
is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the
lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Tunisia inside the Civics and Citizenship
folder (one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what they
prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a
recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school grades
the books come from appear only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

The course as built has **one stocked level**, with one level
sketched before it and not stocked:

1. **Level 1 — sketched, NOT stocked (0 lessons).** The G8 Social
   Subjects book's Civic Education unit — interest-axes
   (مدارات اهتمام) on positive interaction with the social
   environment, citizenship values, shared living, civilised
   conduct and human-rights principles — is known only from that
   combined book's preface and cover in this wave. **The unit
   itself was absent from the scan (PARTIAL), so no lesson is
   built from it.** The level stands in the ladder as a sketch
   only; see the book entry and Honesty notes below.
2. **Level 2 — the citizen in the world (9 lessons, stocked).**
   Tunisia's highest civics book in hand teaches entirely
   outward-facing: first the international community and its
   principles; then the citizen as holder of rights at the
   international level — human rights, their universality, and
   rights with sustainable development; then the citizen facing
   current international challenges — differences in democratic
   practice, the science and technology gap, poverty, conflicts,
   and environmental deterioration. Every lesson stands on a
   **student book** and counts in the acceptance reckoning below.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
book's own shape, walked in order):

- *Outward, all the way:* "Start inside the international
  community — what it is, and the principles it works by. Then
  take your rights at that level: what human rights are, how
  they are protected, why they claim to be universal, and how
  they carry development. Last, face the world's challenges as a
  citizen: democracies that differ, the technology gap, poverty,
  conflicts, and a deteriorating environment."

## The ways of the books (their own shapes, preserved)

**Social Subjects, 8th year Basic** — one volume binds History,
Geography and Civic Education between the same covers, each unit
written by its own inspector team; the civics unit is built as
interest-axes (مدارات اهتمام) rather than chapters. *(Student
book; civics unit absent from the scan in hand — see Honesty
notes.)*

**Civic Education, 3rd year Letters** — at the top of its ladder
Tunisia teaches civics entirely outward-facing: the pupil is
placed inside the international community first, then given
rights at the international level, then set against world
challenges; every lesson is worked from documents
(أقرأ / أستثمر وأقيّم — read, use, evaluate) rather than
exposition. *(Student book.)*
