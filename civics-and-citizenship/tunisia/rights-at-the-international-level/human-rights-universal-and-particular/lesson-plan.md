# Lesson plan — Human Rights — Universal and Particular

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Tunisia · **Title:** Rights at the International Level · **Lesson:** Human Rights — Universal and Particular
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — Rights at the International Level (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** human rights claim to be universal — for every person everywhere — and that claim meets the particularity of each place, and a citizen must hold both sides of the question.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What the universal claim means** — The universal claim says human rights belong to every person, everywhere, simply as a human being — no place and no condition removes them.
2. **How universality meets particularity** — Against the claim stands particularity: each society has its own history, culture and ways, and rights arrive inside real places, not in the abstract.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## If the learner is stuck
- Collapses the lesson into one side → set the two words side by side: universal (for everyone, everywhere) and particular (this place, these ways). The lesson is the meeting of the two, not the victory of either.
- Go back to the one thing: human rights claim to be universal — for every person everywhere — and that claim meets the particularity of each place, and a citizen must hold both sides of the question. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
