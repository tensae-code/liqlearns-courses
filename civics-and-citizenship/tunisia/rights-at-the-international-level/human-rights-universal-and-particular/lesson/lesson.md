# Human Rights — Universal and Particular

## Do it first

Take one right from the last lesson. Ask two questions of it: does it belong to every person on earth, or only to people in one place? Write down your answer and one reason. This lesson is that question, worked properly.

## Sub lesson 1 — What the universal claim means

The universal claim says human rights belong to every person, everywhere, simply as a human being — no place and no condition removes them.

That is the claim's strength: a right that stops at a border would not be a human right at all, but a local privilege.

## Sub lesson 2 — How universality meets particularity

Against the claim stands particularity: each society has its own history, culture and ways, and rights arrive inside real places, not in the abstract.

The lesson holds the two together and asks what follows: how a universal right is honoured inside a particular place, and where the tension between the claim and the place must be judged, case by case, from the documents.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: human rights claim to be universal — for every person everywhere — and that claim meets the particularity of each place, and a citizen must hold both sides of the question. Give one real example from your own life or from the world you live in, and name the lesson's key words: human rights, universality, particularity, universal claim. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
