# Hands-on — Human Rights — Universal and Particular

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Take one right from the last lesson. Ask two questions of it: does it belong to every person on earth, or only to people in one place? Write down your answer and one reason. This lesson is that question, worked properly.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: human rights claim to be universal — for every person everywhere — and that claim meets the particularity of each place, and a citizen must hold both sides of the question. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the news of the world. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
