# Human Rights and Sustainable Development

## Do it first

Think of one improvement in your town — a road, a school, clean water. Ask: will it still serve your younger brother or sister in twenty years, and did it respect everyone's rights while it was built? Those two questions are this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — What sustainable development is

Sustainable development is development that can last: it meets present needs without destroying the ability of those who come after to meet theirs.

The word to hold is 'sustainable' — the development must be able to continue, not flare up and leave ruin behind it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Its fields

Sustainable development works across fields that hold together: the economic field (livelihoods and work), the social field (people's lives and services), and the environmental field (the land, water and air development depends on).

A gain in one field bought by wrecking another is not sustainable — the fields stand or fall together.

## Sub lesson 3 — Human rights as a factor of development

Human rights are a factor of development itself: development done with people's rights respected — their participation, their dignity, their claims heard — is the development that lasts.

Rights are therefore not a reward handed out after development arrives; they are one of the things development is made of.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: sustainable development is development that lasts — across its fields, economic, social and environmental — and human rights are a factor of that development, not a decoration on it. Give one real example from your own life or from the world you live in, and name the lesson's key words: human rights, sustainable development, development fields, rights as a factor of development. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
