# Hands-on — Majorities and Minorities

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Recall a vote you lost. List what you kept despite losing — voice, rights, next chance — then say which of those a real minority must never lose.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: a majority is the larger part and a minority the smaller — by number, language, faith or origin — and democracy is tested by how the majority treats the minority. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your community. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
