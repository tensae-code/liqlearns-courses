# Hands-on — The Groups I Belong To

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw your group map: six groups you belong to, one norm from each, and one place where two of them pull against each other. Say how you would judge it.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: each of us belongs to many groups at once — family, class, town, language, faith, interests — and their demands sometimes pull in different directions. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your community. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
