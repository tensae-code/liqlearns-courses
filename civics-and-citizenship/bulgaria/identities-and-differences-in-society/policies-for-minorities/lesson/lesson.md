# Policies for Minorities

## Do it first

Design a one-page policy for one real need of a minority in your region: the need, the programme, who is heard, and how you would know it worked.

## Sub lesson 1 — What minority policies do

Policies turn duties into programmes: mother-tongue learning support, cultural preservation, equal access to work and services, and inclusion of minorities in decisions about them.

## Sub lesson 2 — Judging a policy

A policy is judged by its results in real lives and by whether the minority itself was heard in making it. A programme done to a minority, without it, usually misses.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: minority policies are the state's practical programmes — in education, language, culture, work and inclusion — and they are judged by whether minorities' real lives improve. Give one real example from your own life in Bulgaria, and name the lesson's key words: minority policy, inclusion, education, culture. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Bulgarian as well as in English — Bulgaria's own book teaches Гражданско образование (Civic Education) in Bulgarian, for life in Bulgarian society, not only for the classroom.
