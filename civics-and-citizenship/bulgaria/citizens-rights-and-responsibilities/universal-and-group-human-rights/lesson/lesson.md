# Universal and Group Human Rights

## Do it first

Sort six rights into two piles: held alone as a person, and held together with a group. Defend each sort in one sentence, then name one right that sits in both.

## Sub lesson 1 — Universal human rights

Universal rights — life, freedom, dignity, equality before the law — belong to every human being everywhere, by birth, not by grant of any state.

## Sub lesson 2 — Collective and group-specific rights

Some rights protect people together: minorities' language and culture, peoples' self-determination, a healthy environment. They guard what a person can only hold with their group.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: some human rights belong to every person simply as a person, and some protect people as members of groups — both kinds are human rights. Give one real example from your own life in Bulgaria, and name the lesson's key words: human rights, universal rights, collective rights, minorities. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Bulgarian as well as in English — Bulgaria's own book teaches Гражданско образование (Civic Education) in Bulgarian, for life in Bulgarian society, not only for the classroom.
