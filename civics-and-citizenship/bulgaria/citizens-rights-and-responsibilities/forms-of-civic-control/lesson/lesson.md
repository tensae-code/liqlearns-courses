# Forms of Civic Control

## Do it first

Follow one decision by your municipality or school. List every form of civic control a citizen could use on it, in order from lightest to strongest.

## Sub lesson 1 — Control at and between elections

Elections are the great control, but not the only one: citizens also control power through questions, petitions, lawful protest, court actions and demands for public information.

## Sub lesson 2 — Organised watching

Control works best organised: associations, media and initiatives that follow budgets, decisions and promises, publish what they find and keep asking until answered.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: citizens control power between elections too: by voting, media scrutiny, petitions, protest, courts, transparency requests and organised watching. Give one real example from your own life in Bulgaria, and name the lesson's key words: civic control, accountability, transparency, elections. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Bulgarian as well as in English — Bulgaria's own book teaches Гражданско образование (Civic Education) in Bulgarian, for life in Bulgarian society, not only for the classroom.
