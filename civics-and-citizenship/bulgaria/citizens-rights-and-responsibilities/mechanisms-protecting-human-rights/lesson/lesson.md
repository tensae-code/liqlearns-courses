# Mechanisms Protecting Human Rights

## Do it first

A neighbour's right has been violated by an office that will not listen. Plan their route: first door, next door, and the last door beyond the country — in the right order.

## Sub lesson 1 — National mechanisms

At home the citizen turns to the courts, the ombudsman and the bodies that guard equality and rights — each with its own door, procedure and power to repair a violation.

## Sub lesson 2 — International mechanisms

Beyond the state stand the international mechanisms: treaty committees and the European Court of Human Rights, reached when national protection has been tried and has failed.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: rights protect people only where mechanisms enforce them: courts, ombudsmen, treaty bodies and commissions that hear complaints and order repair. Give one real example from your own life in Bulgaria, and name the lesson's key words: protection mechanisms, courts, ombudsman, European Court of Human Rights. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Bulgarian as well as in English — Bulgaria's own book teaches Гражданско образование (Civic Education) in Bulgarian, for life in Bulgarian society, not only for the classroom.
