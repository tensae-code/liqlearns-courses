# Me and My Civic Position

## Do it first

Choose one live question in your town. Write your civic position in three sentences: your stance, your strongest reason, and the best argument against you — answered fairly.

## Sub lesson 1 — Forming a civic position

A position is built, not inherited whole: learn the facts, weigh the values at stake, hear the other side, then stand somewhere you can defend.

## Sub lesson 2 — Stating and defending it

The citizen states their position openly, argues it with reasons, listens to counter-arguments and may change it when the evidence changes — firmness is not the same as deafness.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: a civic position is a stance a citizen takes on public life — formed from knowledge and values, stated openly and defended with reasons. Give one real example from your own life in Bulgaria, and name the lesson's key words: civic position, argument, values, public life. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Bulgarian as well as in English — Bulgaria's own book teaches Гражданско образование (Civic Education) in Bulgarian, for life in Bulgarian society, not only for the classroom.
