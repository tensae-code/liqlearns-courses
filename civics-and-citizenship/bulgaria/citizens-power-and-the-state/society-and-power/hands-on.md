# Hands-on — Society and Power

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Take one group you belong to — class, team, family. Name who holds power in it, what resources give them that power, and one value that keeps the power fair.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: power lives in every shared life: it is a relation between people with interests and values, the state is its public instrument, and it turns dangerous when it becomes an end in itself. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your community. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
