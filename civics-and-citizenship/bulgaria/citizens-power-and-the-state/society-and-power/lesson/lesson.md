# Society and Power

## Do it first

Take one group you belong to — class, team, family. Name who holds power in it, what resources give them that power, and one value that keeps the power fair.

## Sub lesson 1 — Power as a relation

Power relations appear at every level of living together: they grow from the human will to reach goals and from unequal abilities and resources, and they order the conflicts that clashing interests create. Power is an intersubjective relation, mediated by values both sides hold significant.

## Sub lesson 2 — The state and power's deformations

Guaranteeing public order is impossible without the state — political power — and politics is the mechanism that forms and distributes it. Power deforms when it is treated as an end in itself: coercion and violence, abuse of power, restricted freedom and broken equality, solidarity and justice.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: power lives in every shared life: it is a relation between people with interests and values, the state is its public instrument, and it turns dangerous when it becomes an end in itself. Give one real example from your own life in Bulgaria, and name the lesson's key words: power, society, the state, values. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Bulgarian as well as in English — Bulgaria's own book teaches Гражданско образование (Civic Education) in Bulgarian, for life in Bulgarian society, not only for the classroom.
