# Hands-on — The Limits of Political Power

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw the triangle of power and label its corners. Add a fourth point outside it — the citizen — and draw every line by which the citizen limits each corner.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: political power has limits, and the citizen stands at its triangle's open corner: active civic control is the limit that works before disobedience becomes the answer. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your community. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
