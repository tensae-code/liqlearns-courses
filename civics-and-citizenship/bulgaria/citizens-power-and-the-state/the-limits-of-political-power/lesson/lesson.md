# The Limits of Political Power

## Do it first

Draw the triangle of power and label its corners. Add a fourth point outside it — the citizen — and draw every line by which the citizen limits each corner.

## Sub lesson 1 — The triangle of power

Fill in the institutions that form the so-called triangle of power in Bulgaria — the three corners where public power is concentrated — and learn the abbreviations of the institutions and laws a citizen meets daily, from the European Commission to the Access to Public Information Act.

## Sub lesson 2 — Where control is missing

Ask at which corner of the triangle a possibility for active civic control could be added, and who could exercise it. Then judge honestly: what would provoke citizens en masse to disobedience, and in which sphere of Bulgarian life is civic activity most lacking?

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: political power has limits, and the citizen stands at its triangle's open corner: active civic control is the limit that works before disobedience becomes the answer. Give one real example from your own life in Bulgaria, and name the lesson's key words: limits of power, civic control, institutions, disobedience. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Bulgarian as well as in English — Bulgaria's own book teaches Гражданско образование (Civic Education) in Bulgarian, for life in Bulgarian society, not only for the classroom.
