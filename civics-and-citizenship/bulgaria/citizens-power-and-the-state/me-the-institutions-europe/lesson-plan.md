# Lesson plan — Me, the Institutions, Europe

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Bulgaria · **Title:** citizens-power-and-the-state · **Lesson:** Me, the Institutions, Europe
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — Power, the Economy and Global Problems (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** I am a citizen of Bulgaria and of the European Union at once: the Union's fundamental values are the test for what I see around me, and its member states and capitals are my wider home.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 4 — Power, the Economy and Global Problems — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Values in a painting** — Study Bosch's Haywain triptych and hunt for the European Union's fundamental values — or their violation — inside it. Say what associations the picture raises for civic education: greed, folly and conflict against dignity, freedom and solidarity.
2. **Europe on the map** — Read the map of Europe: name the member states of the European Union and their capitals, and say which you would visit and why. Knowing the map is knowing the extent of your own citizenship.

Gate pool: the prove-it check for each sub lesson is at most 5 questions, mixed with earlier parts, picked as the hardest from the rank right under the wanted sub lesson — drawn from this lesson's question files (questions/).

Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the learner's own life in Bulgaria and the book's own teaching, as Bulgaria's Civic Education book works from the citizen's own public life, outward.
