# Me, the Institutions, Europe

## Do it first

Name the EU's member states you can from memory, then check the map. Pick one capital and find which EU institution, if any, sits there.

## Sub lesson 1 — Values in a painting

Study Bosch's Haywain triptych and hunt for the European Union's fundamental values — or their violation — inside it. Say what associations the picture raises for civic education: greed, folly and conflict against dignity, freedom and solidarity.

## Sub lesson 2 — Europe on the map

Read the map of Europe: name the member states of the European Union and their capitals, and say which you would visit and why. Knowing the map is knowing the extent of your own citizenship.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: I am a citizen of Bulgaria and of the European Union at once: the Union's fundamental values are the test for what I see around me, and its member states and capitals are my wider home. Give one real example from your own life in Bulgaria, and name the lesson's key words: european union, values, citizenship, europe. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Bulgarian as well as in English — Bulgaria's own book teaches Гражданско образование (Civic Education) in Bulgarian, for life in Bulgarian society, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: I am a citizen of Bulgaria and of the European Union at once: the Union's fundamental values are the test for what I see around me, and its member states and capitals are my wider home.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
