# Partnership in Governance

## Do it first

With two or three others, write five basic rules for a place you share, as if they were laws. Then check: who makes, who carries out, who judges a breach?

## Sub lesson 1 — A new beginning

Shipwrecked on an uninhabited island or stranded on an alien planet, survivors must found their common life: each group drafts ten basic rules, equal to laws, that everyone must keep — and answers what problems arise when there are no rules at all.

## Sub lesson 2 — Rules compared

Compare the two groups' rules: what is alike, what differs, and what explains it — including whether the survivors' ages change the rules. Decide what resource of power you will use to regulate relations, and whether you will apply the separation of powers.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: a community governs itself in partnership when its members together write the basic rules, give them the force of laws, and share the power those rules create. Give one real example from your own life in Bulgaria, and name the lesson's key words: governance, rules, partnership, common life. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Bulgarian as well as in English — Bulgaria's own book teaches Гражданско образование (Civic Education) in Bulgarian, for life in Bulgarian society, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: a community governs itself in partnership when its members together write the basic rules, give them the force of laws, and share the power those rules create.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
