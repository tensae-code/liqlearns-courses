# Lesson plan — Separation of Powers in Practice

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Bulgaria · **Title:** citizens-power-and-the-state · **Lesson:** Separation of Powers in Practice
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — Power, the Economy and Global Problems (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the legislative, executive and judicial powers are separated so that each limits the others, and every public problem has an owner — a person, local power or central power — who must take responsibility.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 4 — Power, the Economy and Global Problems — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Three powers, three symbols** — Match each power to its symbol and defend the match: the law paragraph for those who make the rules, the scales for those who judge, the instruments of enforcement for those who execute. Separation is the guarantee of political freedom.
2. **Who takes responsibility** — Work real cases and decide for each whether intervention is needed and by whom: the individual person, the local power, or the central power. State power is a necessary instrument for solving public problems — and in a democracy no one may be punished without legal grounds.

Gate pool: the prove-it check for each sub lesson is at most 5 questions, mixed with earlier parts, picked as the hardest from the rank right under the wanted sub lesson — drawn from this lesson's question files (questions/).
