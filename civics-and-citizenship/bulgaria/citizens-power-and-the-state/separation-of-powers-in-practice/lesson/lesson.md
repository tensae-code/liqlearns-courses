# Separation of Powers in Practice

## Do it first

Draw three columns for the three powers. Put one real decision from your town in each column and say how the other two could check it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Three powers, three symbols

Match each power to its symbol and defend the match: the law paragraph for those who make the rules, the scales for those who judge, the instruments of enforcement for those who execute. Separation is the guarantee of political freedom.

## Sub lesson 2 — Who takes responsibility

Work real cases and decide for each whether intervention is needed and by whom: the individual person, the local power, or the central power. State power is a necessary instrument for solving public problems — and in a democracy no one may be punished without legal grounds.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the legislative, executive and judicial powers are separated so that each limits the others, and every public problem has an owner — a person, local power or central power — who must take responsibility. Give one real example from your own life in Bulgaria, and name the lesson's key words: separation of powers, legislature, executive, judiciary. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Bulgarian as well as in English — Bulgaria's own book teaches Гражданско образование (Civic Education) in Bulgarian, for life in Bulgarian society, not only for the classroom.
