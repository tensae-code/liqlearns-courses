# Hands-on — How Institutions Work Together

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Take one problem in your neighbourhood. Name the institution that owns it, one at the level above that could help, and the first sentence you would say to each.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: institutions interact across local, national and European levels, and the first step of civic skill is knowing which office is elected, which is appointed, and which institution owns a problem. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your community. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
