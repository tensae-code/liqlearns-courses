# How Institutions Work Together

## Do it first

Take one problem in your neighbourhood. Name the institution that owns it, one at the level above that could help, and the first sentence you would say to each.

## Sub lesson 1 — Elected or appointed

Sort the offices by their mechanism: deputy, mayor, MEP, president and municipal councillor are elected; prime minister, chief prosecutor, ombudsman and regional governor reach office by appointment or election by another body. The mechanism decides to whom each office answers.

## Sub lesson 2 — Matching statements to institutions

Read statements from different institutions — a ring road needing national funding, stray dogs needing a municipal campaign, a draft law, a European arrest warrant — and name the institution behind each, and whether interaction is possible at local, national or European level.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: institutions interact across local, national and European levels, and the first step of civic skill is knowing which office is elected, which is appointed, and which institution owns a problem. Give one real example from your own life in Bulgaria, and name the lesson's key words: institutions, elected, appointed, levels of government. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Bulgarian as well as in English — Bulgaria's own book teaches Гражданско образование (Civic Education) in Bulgarian, for life in Bulgarian society, not only for the classroom.
