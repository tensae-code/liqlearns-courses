# My Civic Project for Positive Change

## Do it first

Pick one problem in your school or street. Write its project name and the six phases as one line each, with a real name beside every task.

## Sub lesson 1 — Choosing and naming

A project is a form of work and inquiry that solves set tasks inside a set time. First choose the problem, theme and field — a volunteer action, a social project, an initiative — and brainstorm a name that gathers every idea before they are split into sub-themes.

## Sub lesson 2 — Planning, doing, presenting

Build the schedule and name who is responsible for each activity; gather the materials and information; decide the form of presentation — report, film, poster, interview. The responsible members then work independently to the schedule, and the project closes with presentation, summary and reflection on what went well and what can improve.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: a civic project turns a public problem into positive change through six phases: choose the problem, name the project, plan the schedule and responsibilities, do the work, present it, and learn from feedback. Give one real example from your own life in Bulgaria, and name the lesson's key words: project, planning, positive change, teamwork. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Bulgarian as well as in English — Bulgaria's own book teaches Гражданско образование (Civic Education) in Bulgarian, for life in Bulgarian society, not only for the classroom.
