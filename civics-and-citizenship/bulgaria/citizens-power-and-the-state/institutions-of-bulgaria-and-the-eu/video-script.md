This lesson is about one thing.
Bulgaria is a unitary parliamentary republic whose Constitution sets the National Assembly, the Council of Ministers, the judiciary and the president, and the same citizen also lives inside the institutions of the European Union.

Bulgaria's constitutional system. The Constitution of 12 July 1991 fixes the principles of the state, civil society and economic relations: a republic with parliamentary government, one unitary state with no autonomous territorial formations, and a political regime of liberal democracy. The National Assembly is the only legislative body, elected for four years, and it also controls the executive and votes the state budget.

Unitary state, federation, confederation. A unitary state is governed by one state power and its local organs answer to the centre. A federation is a lasting union of states that keep their independence under common federal organs; a confederation is a temporary union of sovereign states for set goals, founded on international treaties. Bulgaria is a unitary state.
