# Video script — The Labour Market and Public Policies

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
On the labour market the worker sells what is their own — their labour — freely and without coercion, the wage forms where supply meets demand, and public policy steps in where this market's special features hurt people.

Labour as the worker's property. Labour markets are markets for the factor labour: sellers — the workers — offer their labour to buyers — the employers. Because labour is the workers' private property, they decide how much to offer, at what price and to whom; the Constitution states expressly that no one may be forced into labour and workers freely choose their profession and workplace.

A market unlike others. The traded resource is heterogeneous — every worker is unique, and the quality of labour depends on education, qualification, experience, motivation and health. What is traded is time, given over by a labour contract that creates rights but also obligations. Wages form by supply and demand, tied to the demand for the goods that labour produces — and these features breed problems that call for public policy.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
