# The Labour Market and Public Policies — matching

Match each part with its teaching.

**1.** Labour as the worker's property — a)

**2.** A market unlike others — b)


Meanings:
a) Labour markets are markets for the factor labour: sellers — the workers — offer their labour to buyers — the employers. Because labour is the workers' private property, they decide how much to offer, at what price and to whom; the Constitution states expressly that no one may be forced into labour and workers freely choose their profession and workplace.
b) The traded resource is heterogeneous — every worker is unique, and the quality of labour depends on education, qualification, experience, motivation and health. What is traded is time, given over by a labour contract that creates rights but also obligations. Wages form by supply and demand, tied to the demand for the goods that labour produces — and these features breed problems that call for public policy.

**Answer key:** 1. a; 2. b
