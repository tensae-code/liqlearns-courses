# The Labour Market and Public Policies

## Do it first

Write your own labour as an offer: what you can do, what it is worth, and what would raise its price. Then name one rule the state sets on this market and who it protects.

## Sub lesson 1 — Labour as the worker's property

Labour markets are markets for the factor labour: sellers — the workers — offer their labour to buyers — the employers. Because labour is the workers' private property, they decide how much to offer, at what price and to whom; the Constitution states expressly that no one may be forced into labour and workers freely choose their profession and workplace.

## Sub lesson 2 — A market unlike others

The traded resource is heterogeneous — every worker is unique, and the quality of labour depends on education, qualification, experience, motivation and health. What is traded is time, given over by a labour contract that creates rights but also obligations. Wages form by supply and demand, tied to the demand for the goods that labour produces — and these features breed problems that call for public policy.
