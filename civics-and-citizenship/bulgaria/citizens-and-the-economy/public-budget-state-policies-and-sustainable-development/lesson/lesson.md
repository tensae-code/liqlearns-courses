# Public Budget, State Policies and Sustainable Development

## Do it first

Follow one lev of tax: from a shop receipt at home, through the budget, to one public thing it helps pay for on your street. Name each step.

## Sub lesson 1 — Taxes and the budget

State spending is financed from state revenues and from debt, and most revenue comes from taxes — compulsory, non-refundable payments imposed by general rule. Direct taxes are collected straight from the liable persons on income and property; indirect taxes are charged by sellers into prices and passed to the budget, and they also steer behaviour, as with alcohol, cigarettes and fuels.

## Sub lesson 2 — The budget as a public plan

The public budget is the advance account of the revenues and spending of all state institutions for a set period; its basis is the State Budget Law, drawn up and proposed by the government each year and voted by parliament. Through it the state conducts its economic, financial and social policy — to be judged through the prism of sustainable development.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the public budget is how the state pays for its policies: taxes — direct and indirect — and debt fill it, parliament votes it, and sustainable development is the measure of whether the policies it funds are wise. Give one real example from your own life in Bulgaria, and name the lesson's key words: public budget, taxes, state policies, sustainable development. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Bulgarian as well as in English — Bulgaria's own book teaches Гражданско образование (Civic Education) in Bulgarian, for life in Bulgarian society, not only for the classroom.
