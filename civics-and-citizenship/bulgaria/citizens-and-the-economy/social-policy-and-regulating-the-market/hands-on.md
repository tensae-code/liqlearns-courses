# Hands-on — Social Policy and Regulating the Market

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Take two workers — a dentist earning well part-time and a driver earning half as much full-time with three children. Argue the case for helping the second under each idea of fairness.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: the market alone does not deliver social justice, so the state regulates it and runs social policy — and citizens must choose their idea of fairness: equal chances at the start, or an equal standard at the finish. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your community. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
