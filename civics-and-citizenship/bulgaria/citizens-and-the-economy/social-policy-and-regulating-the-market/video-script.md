This lesson is about one thing.
The market alone does not deliver social justice, so the state regulates it and runs social policy — and citizens must choose their idea of fairness: equal chances at the start, or an equal standard at the finish.

Why the state steps in. To raise social justice the state has built instruments that supplement and correct the market mechanism's results — insurance funds and institutions that arrange and guarantee citizens' insurance rights. The social function is one of the state's basic functions, and social policy holds an important place among state policies.

Two ideas of fairness. Fairness at the start: everyone begins from one starting line with equal chances, and the race decides the finish — society helps equalise chances at the career's beginning. Fairness at the finish: since not everyone can start equal, society helps those who fare worse during their careers, so that all finish at an equally high standard of living. Argue both, with life examples.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
