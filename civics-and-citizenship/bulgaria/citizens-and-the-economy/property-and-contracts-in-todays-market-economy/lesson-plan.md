# Lesson plan — Property and Contracts in Today's Market Economy

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Bulgaria · **Title:** citizens-and-the-economy · **Lesson:** Property and Contracts in Today's Market Economy
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — Power, the Economy and Global Problems (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Locke's three fundamental rights — life, freedom and property — still frame today's market economy, and the citizen must be able to argue both sides: how far property's protection should go, and whether political weight should follow wealth.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Athens and the vote** — In mid-4th-century Athens, of 260,000 people only about 40,000 met the requirements to vote — male, adult, not enslaved, not foreign — and some 5,000, mostly wealthy owners, actually attended and decided. Compute the share that ruled, and judge whether it was just.
2. **Locke debated** — Locke counts life, freedom and property as the three fundamental rights, property being the natural continuation of life and freedom, to be protected with the state's full strictness. Debate it: should attacks on property be treated as strictly as attacks on life — and should citizens' political rights be proportional to their property, as Locke argued from labour?
