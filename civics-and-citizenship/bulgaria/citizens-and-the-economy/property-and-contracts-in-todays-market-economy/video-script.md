# Video script — Property and Contracts in Today's Market Economy

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
Locke's three fundamental rights — life, freedom and property — still frame today's market economy, and the citizen must be able to argue both sides: how far property's protection should go, and whether political weight should follow wealth.

Athens and the vote. In mid-4th-century Athens, of 260,000 people only about 40,000 met the requirements to vote — male, adult, not enslaved, not foreign — and some 5,000, mostly wealthy owners, actually attended and decided. Compute the share that ruled, and judge whether it was just.

Locke debated. Locke counts life, freedom and property as the three fundamental rights, property being the natural continuation of life and freedom, to be protected with the state's full strictness. Debate it: should attacks on property be treated as strictly as attacks on life — and should citizens' political rights be proportional to their property, as Locke argued from labour?

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
