# Property and Contracts in Today's Market Economy

## Do it first

Argue Locke's side for two minutes — property as the fruit of labour — then argue against tying political weight to property for two minutes. Say which argument survives.

## Sub lesson 1 — Athens and the vote

In mid-4th-century Athens, of 260,000 people only about 40,000 met the requirements to vote — male, adult, not enslaved, not foreign — and some 5,000, mostly wealthy owners, actually attended and decided. Compute the share that ruled, and judge whether it was just.

## Sub lesson 2 — Locke debated

Locke counts life, freedom and property as the three fundamental rights, property being the natural continuation of life and freedom, to be protected with the state's full strictness. Debate it: should attacks on property be treated as strictly as attacks on life — and should citizens' political rights be proportional to their property, as Locke argued from labour?

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Locke's three fundamental rights — life, freedom and property — still frame today's market economy, and the citizen must be able to argue both sides: how far property's protection should go, and whether political weight should follow wealth. Give one real example from your own life in Bulgaria, and name the lesson's key words: locke, property, fundamental rights, athens. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
