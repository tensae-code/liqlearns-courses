# The Circular Flow of the Economy

## Do it first

Draw the circular flow for your own household: one arrow of labour out, one arrow of money in, one arrow of spending out, one arrow of goods in. Label the real and the money flow.

## Sub lesson 1 — Two actors, two markets

The simplified model divides economic subjects into households and firms, meeting on two markets only: the markets for goods and the markets for labour. Labour is owned by households; land and capital are owned and managed by firms, which are wholly owned by households.

## Sub lesson 2 — Reading the scheme

Read the circular-flow scheme and complete the model: firms can hire the factors they need only on the markets, and households can buy the goods they need only on the markets. Civil society, as part of the model, watches that firms do not pollute and use natural resources responsibly, by the principles of sustainable development.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: households and firms keep the economy turning: households sell labour and buy goods, firms hire labour and sell goods, and two flows — the real flow and the money flow — run in opposite directions between them. Give one real example from your own life in Bulgaria, and name the lesson's key words: circular flow, households, firms, markets. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Bulgarian as well as in English — Bulgaria's own book teaches Гражданско образование (Civic Education) in Bulgarian, for life in Bulgarian society, not only for the classroom.
