This lesson is about one thing.
Reading a real budget table is a civic skill: split Bulgaria's tax revenues into direct and indirect, compute their shares, and argue whether the balance between them is fair.

Bulgaria's revenues on the table. Use the table from the State Budget Law of the Republic of Bulgaria for 2020: total revenues, aid and donations of about 26 billion leva, of which tax revenues about 23 billion — corporate tax, personal income tax, VAT, excises, customs and others. Divide them into direct and indirect and compute each group's approximate percentage.

The fairness debate. Some economists would collect everything indirectly: consumption taxes reward saving and growth, and everyone who draws from the common pot pays in. Others answer that indirect taxes offend social justice, because poorer and vulnerable groups spend a larger share of their income on consumption. Choose a thesis, defend it, and judge Bulgaria's balance.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
