# The Public Budget in Practice

## Do it first

Take the budget figures of any year you can find. Split three taxes into direct and indirect and compute the bigger group's share. Say in one sentence whether you find it fair.

## Sub lesson 1 — Bulgaria's revenues on the table

Use the table from the State Budget Law of the Republic of Bulgaria for 2020: total revenues, aid and donations of about 26 billion leva, of which tax revenues about 23 billion — corporate tax, personal income tax, VAT, excises, customs and others. Divide them into direct and indirect and compute each group's approximate percentage.

## Sub lesson 2 — The fairness debate

Some economists would collect everything indirectly: consumption taxes reward saving and growth, and everyone who draws from the common pot pays in. Others answer that indirect taxes offend social justice, because poorer and vulnerable groups spend a larger share of their income on consumption. Choose a thesis, defend it, and judge Bulgaria's balance.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: reading a real budget table is a civic skill: split Bulgaria's tax revenues into direct and indirect, compute their shares, and argue whether the balance between them is fair. Give one real example from your own life in Bulgaria, and name the lesson's key words: public budget, direct taxes, indirect taxes, fairness. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Bulgarian as well as in English — Bulgaria's own book teaches Гражданско образование (Civic Education) in Bulgarian, for life in Bulgarian society, not only for the classroom.
