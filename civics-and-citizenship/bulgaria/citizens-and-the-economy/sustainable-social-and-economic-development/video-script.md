# Video script — Sustainable Social and Economic Development

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
A country's development is more than its wealth: it is measured by GDP per head, life expectancy, years of schooling and the Gini index together — and judged by whether it can last.

Four indicators. Match each indicator to its aspect: GDP per head at purchasing-power parity to society's capacity to produce goods and services; life expectancy to the quality and accessibility of healthcare; mean years of schooling to education's quality and reach; the Gini index to inequality in income distribution.

Measuring real countries. Work the table of sixteen countries — from Luxembourg and Singapore to Burundi and the Central African Republic — and see how the indicators separate: rich in output yet unequal, long-lived yet poor, schooled yet short-lived. Economic development and societal development are not the same thing, and sustainability asks whether either can continue.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
