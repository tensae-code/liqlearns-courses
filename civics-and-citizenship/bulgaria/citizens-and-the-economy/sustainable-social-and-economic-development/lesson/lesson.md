# Sustainable Social and Economic Development

## Do it first

Choose two countries you know something about. Rank them by the four indicators from memory, then say which single indicator you would improve first in each — and why.

## Sub lesson 1 — Four indicators

Match each indicator to its aspect: GDP per head at purchasing-power parity to society's capacity to produce goods and services; life expectancy to the quality and accessibility of healthcare; mean years of schooling to education's quality and reach; the Gini index to inequality in income distribution.

## Sub lesson 2 — Measuring real countries

Work the table of sixteen countries — from Luxembourg and Singapore to Burundi and the Central African Republic — and see how the indicators separate: rich in output yet unequal, long-lived yet poor, schooled yet short-lived. Economic development and societal development are not the same thing, and sustainability asks whether either can continue.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: a country's development is more than its wealth: it is measured by GDP per head, life expectancy, years of schooling and the Gini index together — and judged by whether it can last. Give one real example from your own life in Bulgaria, and name the lesson's key words: development, indicators, gini index, sustainability. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Bulgarian as well as in English — Bulgaria's own book teaches Гражданско образование (Civic Education) in Bulgarian, for life in Bulgarian society, not only for the classroom.
