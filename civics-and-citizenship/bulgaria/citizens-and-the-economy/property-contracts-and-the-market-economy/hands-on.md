# Hands-on — Property, Contracts and the Market Economy

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
List three things your household owns and one contract it lives by. Say which of your rights would remain exactly the same if you owned nothing at all.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: property and contracts hold a market society together: property is a basic human right — but never a ticket one must hold to have political rights — and the contract is the basic civic bond between members of society. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your community. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
