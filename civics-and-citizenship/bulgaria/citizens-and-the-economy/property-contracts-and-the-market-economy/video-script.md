This lesson is about one thing.
Property and contracts hold a market society together: property is a basic human right — but never a ticket one must hold to have political rights — and the contract is the basic civic bond between members of society.

Property and the free citizen. In modern market economies the means of production are mostly private property, and relations are formally ordered by contracts. In ancient Greece only landowners could take part in political life; owning property was the condition for belonging to civil society, and only a few percent of Athenians decided.

Property as a right, not a condition. In today's developed democracies property is accepted as a basic human right, yet it may not be demanded for participation in political life, because political self-determination is also a basic right, owed unconditionally to every citizen — even though citizens with more property still usually carry more weight in society.
