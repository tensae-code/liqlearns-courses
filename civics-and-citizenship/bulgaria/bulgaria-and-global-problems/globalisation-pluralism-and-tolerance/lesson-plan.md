# Lesson plan — Globalisation, Pluralism and Tolerance

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Bulgaria · **Title:** bulgaria-and-global-problems · **Lesson:** Globalisation, Pluralism and Tolerance
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — Power, the Economy and Global Problems (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** living in a globalised world takes pluralism and tolerance: many peoples, views and ways of life inside one global village, where each person is also a citizen of the world.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 4 — Power, the Economy and Global Problems — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Words for a wired world** — Explain in your own words: the global village, the citizen of the world, the global player, globalisation itself. Then name three leading countries of world industry and say by which criteria — economic, political, ecological — you chose them, and three main causes that drive globalisation.
2. **Milestones on a timeline** — Match the events to their years and judge which belong to globalisation's process: the first intercontinental flight, the first home television, the first container ships, the first industrial robot, the first personal computer, the world computer network, Facebook, the first smartphone, the first mobile message, the first digital currency, the first drone delivery.

Gate pool: the prove-it check for each sub lesson is at most 5 questions, mixed with earlier parts, picked as the hardest from the rank right under the wanted sub lesson — drawn from this lesson's question files (questions/).

1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: living in a globalised world takes pluralism and tolerance: many peoples, views and ways of life inside one global village, where each person is also a citizen of the world.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street, school or community in Bulgaria.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the learner's own life in Bulgaria and the book's own teaching, as Bulgaria's Civic Education book works from the citizen's own public life, outward.
