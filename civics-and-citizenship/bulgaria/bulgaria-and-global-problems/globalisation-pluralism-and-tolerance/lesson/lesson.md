# Globalisation, Pluralism and Tolerance

## Do it first

Write your own definitions of 'global village' and 'citizen of the world' in one sentence each. Then name one thing in your room that proves the first, and one duty that proves the second.

## Sub lesson 1 — Words for a wired world

Explain in your own words: the global village, the citizen of the world, the global player, globalisation itself. Then name three leading countries of world industry and say by which criteria — economic, political, ecological — you chose them, and three main causes that drive globalisation.

## Sub lesson 2 — Milestones on a timeline

Match the events to their years and judge which belong to globalisation's process: the first intercontinental flight, the first home television, the first container ships, the first industrial robot, the first personal computer, the world computer network, Facebook, the first smartphone, the first mobile message, the first digital currency, the first drone delivery.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: living in a globalised world takes pluralism and tolerance: many peoples, views and ways of life inside one global village, where each person is also a citizen of the world. Give one real example from your own life in Bulgaria, and name the lesson's key words: globalisation, pluralism, tolerance, global village. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Bulgarian as well as in English — Bulgaria's own book teaches Гражданско образование (Civic Education) in Bulgarian, for life in Bulgarian society, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: living in a globalised world takes pluralism and tolerance: many peoples, views and ways of life inside one global village, where each person is also a citizen of the world.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
