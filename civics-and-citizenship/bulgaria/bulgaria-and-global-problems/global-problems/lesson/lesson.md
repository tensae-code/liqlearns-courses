# Global Problems

## Do it first

Name one global problem that has already touched your town. Trace its chain: where it began, how it travelled, and where it reached you.

## Sub lesson 1 — Globalisation's open horizons

Globalisation — driven by transport, telecommunications and information technology — shrinks time and distance: finance moves virtually, trade turns world-wide, people, goods and capital move freely, and access to information, other cultures and self-realisation opens without limit. It cannot be stopped by anyone's will; it sets the frame of modern life.

## Sub lesson 2 — Globalisation's other face

Drawn into global processes unprepared, states and regions face growing inequality between poor and rich countries, unfair and inhuman labour conditions in low-wage countries, exploitation of people and natural resources, and climate change. Polarisation grows between those who enjoy globalisation's goods and those who cannot — and migration rises, for economic, political, cultural and now ecological reasons.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: globalisation has bound the world into one interdependent system, and its problems are global too: inequality, exploitation, climate change and migration, which no state can solve alone. Give one real example from your own life in Bulgaria, and name the lesson's key words: global problems, globalisation, inequality, migration. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Bulgarian as well as in English — Bulgaria's own book teaches Гражданско образование (Civic Education) in Bulgarian, for life in Bulgarian society, not only for the classroom.
