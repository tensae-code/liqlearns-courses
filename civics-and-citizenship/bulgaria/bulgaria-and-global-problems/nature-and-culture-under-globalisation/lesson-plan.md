# Lesson plan — Nature and Culture under Globalisation

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Bulgaria · **Title:** bulgaria-and-global-problems · **Lesson:** Nature and Culture under Globalisation
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — Power, the Economy and Global Problems (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** nature has limits that globalised overconsumption is pressing against: the Earth is one interconnected system, and a culture of tolerance, solidarity and cooperation is the human answer to living inside it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 4 — Power, the Economy and Global Problems — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Nature and the limits of exploitation** — A century of excessive industrialisation has destroyed the environment and steadily shrunk natural resources; overconsumption threatens the Earth itself — global waste pollution, harmful production lowering air quality, shrinking water resources, warming with rising seas, extreme weather, dying species and shortages of food and water.
2. **One system, two kinds of law** — The Earth is a whole interconnected system governed by ecological and by social laws, because people are part of it. The ruling linear model — extract resources, process them into final goods, discard — treats nature as inexhaustible; pressed against the planet's limits, it cannot be sustainable, green growth notwithstanding. Gandhi's measure stands over the lesson: the Earth has enough for everyone's need, not for everyone's greed.
