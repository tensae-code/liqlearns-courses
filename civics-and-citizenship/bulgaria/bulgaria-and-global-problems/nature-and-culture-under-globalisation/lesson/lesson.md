# Nature and Culture under Globalisation

## Do it first

Audit one day of your household's consumption: three things taken from nature, three thrown back at it. Mark where the linear chain could bend into a circle.

## Sub lesson 1 — Nature and the limits of exploitation

A century of excessive industrialisation has destroyed the environment and steadily shrunk natural resources; overconsumption threatens the Earth itself — global waste pollution, harmful production lowering air quality, shrinking water resources, warming with rising seas, extreme weather, dying species and shortages of food and water.

## Sub lesson 2 — One system, two kinds of law

The Earth is a whole interconnected system governed by ecological and by social laws, because people are part of it. The ruling linear model — extract resources, process them into final goods, discard — treats nature as inexhaustible; pressed against the planet's limits, it cannot be sustainable, green growth notwithstanding. Gandhi's measure stands over the lesson: the Earth has enough for everyone's need, not for everyone's greed.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: nature has limits that globalised overconsumption is pressing against: the Earth is one interconnected system, and a culture of tolerance, solidarity and cooperation is the human answer to living inside it. Give one real example from your own life in Bulgaria, and name the lesson's key words: nature, globalisation, ecology, limits. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Bulgarian as well as in English — Bulgaria's own book teaches Гражданско образование (Civic Education) in Bulgarian, for life in Bulgarian society, not only for the classroom.
