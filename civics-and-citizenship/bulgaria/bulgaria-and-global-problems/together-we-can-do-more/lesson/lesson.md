# Together We Can Do More

## Do it first

Rank the ten problems for your own town, honestly. Then write the first message you would send to two like-minded partners to start the project on your number one.

## Sub lesson 1 — What the millennials fear

The World Economic Forum's Global Shapers Survey asked over 31,000 people aged 18–35: nearly half named climate change and the destruction of nature the biggest global problem, over 78% would change their way of life to protect the environment, and 91% agreed people are to blame for climate change. The ten problems run from climate through escalating conflicts and wars, inequality and discrimination, poverty, religious conflicts, corruption, food and water security, education access, safety, and lack of economic opportunity.

## Sub lesson 2 — Choosing and carrying out

Choose a theme for your project — solving a social, ecological, demographic or health problem of national significance — and realise it with the help of like-minded people. Together we can do more is the book's last word: the citizen leaves school with a problem chosen, partners found, and a plan in hand.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the young generation has already ranked the world's problems — climate first — and the course ends where citizenship begins: choose one problem of national significance and carry out its project with like-minded partners. Give one real example from your own life in Bulgaria, and name the lesson's key words: cooperation, global problems, youth, project. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Bulgarian as well as in English — Bulgaria's own book teaches Гражданско образование (Civic Education) in Bulgarian, for life in Bulgarian society, not only for the classroom.
