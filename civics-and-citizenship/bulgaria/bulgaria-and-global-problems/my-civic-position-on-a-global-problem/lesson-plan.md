# Lesson plan — My Civic Position on a Global Problem

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Bulgaria · **Title:** bulgaria-and-global-problems · **Lesson:** My Civic Position on a Global Problem
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — Power, the Economy and Global Problems (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a civic position is not an opinion but a plan: imagine the world of 2050, choose one global problem, and build the project that states what you will do about it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 4 — Power, the Economy and Global Problems — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **A look into the future** — By 2050 the world will look very different. Imagine the changes — and the problems travelling with them — in transport, energy, the food industry, telecommunications, trade, financial services, industrial production and healthcare, at local, regional and global level, and choose the problem your position addresses.
2. **The plan-scheme** — Build the project's plan-scheme as in Theme 1: the participants with a project leader, the theme, the materials you will rely on and who researches and summarises them, the form of presentation — presentation, reportage, film, interview, poster, photographs, games — and the preliminary preparation, who takes which task.

Gate pool: the prove-it check for each sub lesson is at most 5 questions, mixed with earlier parts, picked as the hardest from the rank right under the wanted sub lesson — drawn from this lesson's question files (questions/).

1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: a civic position is not an opinion but a plan: imagine the world of 2050, choose one global problem, and build the project that states what you will do about it.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street, school or community in Bulgaria.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the learner's own life in Bulgaria and the book's own teaching, as Bulgaria's Civic Education book works from the citizen's own public life, outward.
