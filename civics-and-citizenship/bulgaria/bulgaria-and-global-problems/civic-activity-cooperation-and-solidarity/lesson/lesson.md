# Civic Activity, Cooperation and Solidarity

## Do it first

Interview — really or in imagination — one person who moved town or country. Write their motive in two lines and mark the move voluntary or forced, with your reason.

## Sub lesson 1 — Five real stories

A Romanian cleaner in Vienna earning three times more but uninsured; a Hungarian waiter in Italy building an independent life; a young Sudanese man fleeing civil war and poverty; a Moroccan woman picking strawberries fourteen hours a day in Spain, illegally and in debt to a smuggler; a Dutch woman whose family spans four countries. Find each one's motive.

## Sub lesson 2 — Voluntary or forced

Mark each story's migration voluntary or forced, and defend the mark. The exercise trains the exact judgement solidarity needs: the same border crossing can be a free choice, an economic necessity, or a flight for one's life — and cooperation with each person starts from knowing which.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: behind every migration statistic is a person: solidarity begins by hearing real stories and telling voluntary migration — chosen for work and independence — from forced migration, driven by war, poverty and danger. Give one real example from your own life in Bulgaria, and name the lesson's key words: migration, solidarity, cooperation, civic activity. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Bulgarian as well as in English — Bulgaria's own book teaches Гражданско образование (Civic Education) in Bulgarian, for life in Bulgarian society, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: behind every migration statistic is a person: solidarity begins by hearing real stories and telling voluntary migration — chosen for work and independence — from forced migration, driven by war, poverty and danger.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
