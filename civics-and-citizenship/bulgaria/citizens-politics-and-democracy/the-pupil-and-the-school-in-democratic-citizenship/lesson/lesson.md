# The Pupil and the School in Democratic Citizenship

## Do it first

Map your school as a democracy: name one right you hold there, one rule, one way pupils take part in decisions, and one change you would argue for fairly.

## Sub lesson 1 — The school as a small democracy

Pupils hold rights and duties at school, meet rules they can question fairly, elect representatives and learn that decisions can be discussed, voted and reviewed.

## Sub lesson 2 — The pupil's part

The pupil's part is to take part: in class decisions, pupil bodies and school life — respectfully, with reasons, accepting fair results and defending others' rights as their own.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the school is the pupil's first political community: democratic citizenship is practised there daily, not only studied. Give one real example from your own life in Bulgaria, and name the lesson's key words: school, pupil rights, participation, democratic citizenship. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Bulgarian as well as in English — Bulgaria's own book teaches Гражданско образование (Civic Education) in Bulgarian, for life in Bulgarian society, not only for the classroom.
