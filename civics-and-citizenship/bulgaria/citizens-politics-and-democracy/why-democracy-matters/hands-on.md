# Hands-on — Why Democracy Matters

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Name one problem in your town. Say how citizens could get it fixed in a democracy, step by step, and what would block the same fix where people have no vote.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: democracy matters because it protects freedom and dignity, lets errors be corrected peacefully, and gives every citizen a share in power. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in your community. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
