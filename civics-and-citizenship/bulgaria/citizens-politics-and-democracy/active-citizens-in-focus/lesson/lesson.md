# Active Citizens in Focus

## Do it first

Pick one active citizen you know of, near or far. Tell their case in five steps: problem, learning, people, action, result. Name the step you could copy first.

## Sub lesson 1 — What active citizens do

The cases show a pattern: notice a problem, learn about it, gather people, act lawfully and publicly, and persist through slow answers and setbacks.

## Sub lesson 2 — What we learn from the cases

Active citizenship is a skill, not a temperament: it can be learned from real cases — what worked, what failed, and what the citizens changed in their approach.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: active citizens are ordinary people who saw a public problem, organised others and stayed with it until something changed. Give one real example from your own life in Bulgaria, and name the lesson's key words: active citizens, initiative, case study, persistence. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Bulgarian as well as in English — Bulgaria's own book teaches Гражданско образование (Civic Education) in Bulgarian, for life in Bulgarian society, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: active citizens are ordinary people who saw a public problem, organised others and stayed with it until something changed.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
