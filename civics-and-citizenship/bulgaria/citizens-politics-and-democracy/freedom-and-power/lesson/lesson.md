# Freedom and Power

## Do it first

Think of one choice you made freely today and one rule that limited a choice. Say what made the first free, and whether the limit protected someone's rights or only blocked you.

## Sub lesson 1 — Freedom and the free person

Freedom is both absence of constraint and self-realisation: the free person acts by their own reason and will, and every person holds natural, inalienable rights no power may take away.

## Sub lesson 2 — Power, values and the philosophers

Power must serve freedom, not crush it. Hegel, Kant, Rousseau, Sartre and Locke each teach how freedom is grounded — in reason, autonomy, the social contract, choice and natural rights — and democracy's values are freedom, equality, justice and solidarity.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: freedom and power belong together: a free person has human rights, and democracy rests on freedom, equality, justice and solidarity. Give one real example from your own life in Bulgaria, and name the lesson's key words: freedom, power, human rights, equality. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Bulgarian as well as in English — Bulgaria's own book teaches Гражданско образование (Civic Education) in Bulgarian, for life in Bulgarian society, not only for the classroom.
