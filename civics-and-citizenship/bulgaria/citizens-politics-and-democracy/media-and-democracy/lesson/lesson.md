# Media and Democracy

## Do it first

Take one news story and find it in two different outlets. Note what agrees, what differs, and one claim you still cannot verify. Say which report you trust more, and why.

## Sub lesson 1 — Media as democracy's watchdog

Free and plural media inform the public, expose abuse, give opponents a voice and let citizens judge power between elections. Where media are captured, that watch fails.

## Sub lesson 2 — Reading media critically

Not everything published is true. The democratic reader checks the source, compares reports, separates fact from opinion and refuses to pass on what they have not verified.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: free media keep democracy alive by informing citizens and watching power — and citizens keep media honest by checking what they read. Give one real example from your own life in Bulgaria, and name the lesson's key words: media, free press, information, critical reading. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Bulgarian as well as in English — Bulgaria's own book teaches Гражданско образование (Civic Education) in Bulgarian, for life in Bulgarian society, not only for the classroom.
