# Civics and Citizenship — Sri Lanka course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Sri Lanka inside the Civics and Citizenship folder,
organised by **levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they
have proved the level, not when a school calendar says so. The
school grades the books come from appear only in each lesson's
sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation. The
path follows Sri Lanka's own books' widening circles, as sketched
at the ends of the filings: civics begins inside the child's own
school, widens to the family, the home region and good conduct,
then to culture and living together, the state's services and
democracy as a way of living, change, social security and the
world of work, and the citizen's machinery — parties, franchise,
local government, resolving conflict. The older Tamil-medium
generation (Grades 10–12) sits as its own labelled band at the
top, as filed.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this
course comes from government textbooks — this country's own
Civic Education books — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box
is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the
lessons are the books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book list,
every gap and label carried exactly as filed, and the acceptance
reckoning.

**A note on the level numbers, stated plainly:** the two filings
number their levels differently. `sri-lanka-placements.md` files
the modern English-medium band thematically (L1 myself, family
and school · L2 community and good conduct · L3 culture, identity
and living together · L4 services, democracy and society's
problems · L5 change, social security and the world of work ·
L6 democratic governance, local government, resolving conflict ·
L7 the older-generation Tamil-medium band), while
`sri-lanka-placements-b.md` files its teacher guides and senior
student books by grade band (L1 Grade 6 · L2 Grade 7 · L3 Grade 8
· L4 Grade 10 · L5 Grade 11). This build keeps every placement's
filed level; where the coined lesson names deduped into one
lesson across the filings, the lesson stands at its earliest
filed level. The level names below reconcile the two sketches
into the one ladder the books themselves climb.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **Myself, My Family and My School** — the course opens inside the child's own school and family — the school's history, plan, symbols and duties, who am I, the family as the smallest unit of society, and the home area and those who help us (Grades 6–7 books and the Grade 6 teacher guide). 33 lessons (35 placements filed at the level).

2. **Community, Good Conduct and Living Together** — living with others — neighbours, inter-relationships, polite language, discipline, telling good from bad, respecting rules and traditions, using resources without waste, protecting public property and the environment — joined to the Grade 7 Life Competencies teaching: self dignity, aims in life, leisure, morals, information and creative thinking. 34 lessons (35 placements filed at the level).

3. **Culture, Identity and the Wider Society** — what culture is, the festivals of Sri Lanka's communities, coexistence and national pride, the national symbols — and, from the Grade 8 teacher guide, the turn outward: public services, the democratic society, the multicultural society, contemporary problems, solving problems and the world of work. 23 lessons (23 placements filed at the level).

4. **Services, Democracy and Society's Problems** — the state's services met directly (health, education, transport, media, welfare), democracy as a way of living, disasters, accidents and road safety — and at Grade 10 depth: democratic governance, decentralisation and devolution of power, the multicultural society, economic systems and resolving conflict in a democratic society. 43 lessons (49 placements filed at the level).

5. **Change, Social Security, Work, Law and Rights** — the individual inside society and what social security asks of each person; adapting to change in technology, the economy and the job market; solving problems and entering the world of work (profiles, CVs, applications, interviews, time and money) — and at Grade 11 depth: law and justice (with Kandyan law), the strata of government, human rights and duties, environmental problems and international relations. 43 lessons (43 placements filed at the level).

6. **Democratic Governance, Local Government and Resolving Conflict** — the citizen's machinery from the Grade 9 book: rights bound to duties, political parties, the franchise and elections, assertiveness and nation building; Sri Lanka's local government institutions and what the public and its representatives owe them; and resolving conflict positively. 13 lessons (13 placements filed at the level).

7. **The Older-Generation Advanced Band (Tamil-medium, Grades 10–12)** — a different, older syllabus generation, kept whole and labelled as the filings file it: Grade 10 Civics and Governance and the Social Education books (systems of government, human-rights protection, a near-full political-science course at Grade 11), closing with the 1976 Grade 12 Political Science — the state, its origin theories, sovereignty, and state and society. 20 lessons (20 placements filed at the level).

**Totals: 209 lessons, 218 placements stocked** (Level 1: 33 lessons · Level 2: 34 lessons · Level 3: 23 lessons · Level 4: 43 lessons · Level 5: 43 lessons · Level 6: 13 lessons · Level 7: 20 lessons).

## Where the build stands

The whole filed ladder is built: Levels 1–7 stocked from both
filings, 209 lessons in all. 66 lessons
stand on teacher-guide evidence only (acceptance 0, labelled in
their sources and in ABOUT.md's acceptance reckoning). The two
NIE Grade 9 Life Competencies and Civic Education teacher guides
(Sinhala and Tamil) are RECORD-ONLY in the filings — their
legacy-font text could not be read to placement standard — and
the Grade 10–11 Map Education book is RECORD-ONLY (a geography
map-skills book); neither is in the course. The Tamil-medium
parallel editions of the Grade 8 and Grade 9 English books were
spot-checked as parallel and placed once under the English
books, as filed.
