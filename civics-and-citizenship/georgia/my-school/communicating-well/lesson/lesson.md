# Communicating Well

## Do it first

Tell a partner one thing twice: first with words only, then letting your face and hands help. Then listen to them for a full minute without interrupting, and say back what you heard.

## Sub lesson 1 — Words and more than words

We communicate with words, and also nonverbally — with face, hands, posture and silence.

## Sub lesson 2 — Listening well

Listening — hearing the other person fully before answering — is what makes communication work.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: communication is verbal and nonverbal — and listening is half of it. Give one real example from your own life in Georgia, and name the lesson's key words: communication, verbal, nonverbal, listening. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Georgian as well as in English — Georgia's own book teaches Citizenship (მოქალაქეობა) in Georgian, for life at home and in the community, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: communication is verbal and nonverbal — and listening is half of it.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
