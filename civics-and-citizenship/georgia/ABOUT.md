# About the Civics and Citizenship — Georgia course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this
course comes from government textbooks — this country's own
Citizenship book (მოქალაქეობა, Grade 7). The one exception is
the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Georgia inside the Civics and
Citizenship folder (one course per country). Inside it, the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place is
found by what they prove, and each level stands on the ones
before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced gate.
The school grade the books come from (Grade 7) appears only in
each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

The course has **one level**, because exactly one Georgia
civics book is in hand — the Grade 7 Citizenship student book.
Higher Georgia grades' citizenship books are not in hand, so
the ladder stands at one level, honestly:

1. **Level 1 — The Community I Live In** (29 lessons, in seven
   titles: `my-community/`, `rights-and-law/`,
   `living-together/`, `my-school/`, `healthy-life/`,
   `heritage/`, `work-and-money/`). The book's own order,
   widening circle by circle: the community I live in
   (relations, belonging, participation, personality,
   leadership) with rights and law inside that circle (human
   rights, law and justice, the person and the law, juveniles
   and crime) and living together (tolerance, diversity, a safe
   environment) → the school community (school culture, a
   democratic school, communication, bullying, conflict,
   safety) → healthy life (physical health, early marriage,
   addiction) → protecting nature and cultural heritage →
   economic relations in the community (professions, the
   market, volunteering, resources).

**Georgia's own way:** citizenship is taught outwards from the
community the child actually lives in — relations, rights and
participation in the community and the school first; law,
safety and tolerance inside that circle; health, heritage and
money last. Every lesson opens with a key question and works
through real-life situations and sources (including the UN
Convention on the Rights of the Child) rather than definitions.
The teacher's book frames Grade 7 citizenship around the
thematic block "the individual and society" and the national
goal of a citizen who knows their rights and duties toward
family, society and state.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
book's own shape, walked in order):

- *Outwards from my community:* "Start where you live: the
  relations around you, your place in society, your right to
  take part. Learn your rights and the law that protects them.
  Live together — tolerance, diversity, safety. Practise it all
  at school. Care for your health, your heritage and your
  country's nature. Then step into work and money — a
  profession, the market, volunteering, planning what you
  have. That is Georgia's Citizenship."
