# Tolerance

## Do it first

Name one way someone you know differs from you — a view, a faith, a custom. Say one respectful thing you can say or do about that difference.

## Sub lesson 1 — Respecting different views and faiths

People around me hold different views and faiths; tolerance respects them even when I do not share them.

## Sub lesson 2 — Respecting different ways of life

A different way of life is not a threat: tolerance lets different people live side by side in peace.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: tolerance means respecting people whose views, faith or way of life differ from my own. Give one real example from your own life in Georgia, and name the lesson's key words: tolerance, respect, difference, faith. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Georgian as well as in English — Georgia's own book teaches Citizenship (მოქალაქეობა) in Georgian, for life at home and in the community, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: tolerance means respecting people whose views, faith or way of life differ from my own.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
