# Juveniles and Crime

## Do it first

Take a case: a young person takes something that is not theirs. Say what the law's response should protect, what it should correct, and what the young person is responsible for.

## Sub lesson 1 — What happens when a minor breaks the law

A minor who breaks the law faces the law's response, shaped for young people — with protection as well as consequences.

## Sub lesson 2 — Responsibility for my acts

Being young does not remove responsibility: each person answers for what they choose to do.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: when a minor breaks the law there are consequences — and every young person is responsible for their own acts. Give one real example from your own life in Georgia, and name the lesson's key words: juvenile, crime, law, responsibility. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Georgian as well as in English — Georgia's own book teaches Citizenship (მოქალაქეობა) in Georgian, for life at home and in the community, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: when a minor breaks the law there are consequences — and every young person is responsible for their own acts.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
