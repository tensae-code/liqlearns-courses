# The Person and the Law

## Do it first

Take one everyday situation — borrowing something, crossing the street, a quarrel. Say what rights and what duties each person in it has.

## Sub lesson 1 — Rights and duties before the law

The law gives every person rights and lays duties on every person at the same time.

## Sub lesson 2 — Responsibility in everyday situations

In everyday situations — at home, at school, in the street — a person answers for their own acts.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: before the law, every person has rights and duties — and responsibility for what they do. Give one real example from your own life in Georgia, and name the lesson's key words: law, rights, duties, responsibility. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Georgian as well as in English — Georgia's own book teaches Citizenship (მოქალაქეობა) in Georgian, for life at home and in the community, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: before the law, every person has rights and duties — and responsibility for what they do.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
