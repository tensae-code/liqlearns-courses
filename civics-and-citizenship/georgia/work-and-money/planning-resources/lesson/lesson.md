# Planning Resources

## Do it first

Take a small budget — a class event or a family week. List the needs first, then the wants, and share out a fixed sum between them. Say what you left out and why.

## Sub lesson 1 — Needs and wants

Planning starts by telling needs from wants: needs come first when resources are shared out.

## Sub lesson 2 — Budgeting in the community

A budget is a plan for resources — what comes in, what goes out, and what is kept for later.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: resources are planned and distributed by needs first — wants after, and the budget kept. Give one real example from your own life in Georgia, and name the lesson's key words: resources, needs, wants, budget. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in Georgian as well as in English — Georgia's own book teaches Citizenship (მოქალაქეობა) in Georgian, for life at home and in the community, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: resources are planned and distributed by needs first — wants after, and the budget kept.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
