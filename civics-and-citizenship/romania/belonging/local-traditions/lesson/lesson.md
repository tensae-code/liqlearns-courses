# Local Traditions

## Do it first

Ask someone older in your family or locality about one local tradition: what is done, when, and why. Write it down so it is not lost.

## What the book teaches

Every locality keeps traditions: feast days and how they are celebrated, crafts, songs, costumes, foods, customs for weddings, harvests and holidays. Traditions are handed down from the old to the young; they tell the community who it is. To know your local traditions — and to respect other places' traditions — is part of belonging.

## The lesson in full

Traditions are the locality's memory; the book places them right after the locality itself, because a place is its people and their handed-down life, not only its streets.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: local traditions are the customs a community hands down — feasts, crafts, songs and ways — and they bind the locality together. Give one real example from your own life in Romania, and name the lesson's key words: traditions, customs, community, handing down. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Romania's own books teach civics in widening circles: the person first — moral traits taught as pairs of opposites — then relations with things, with animals and plants, and with other people; then belonging, moral values and norms in everyday life, closing with the universal rights of the child. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life and your own place, really look: your own life is this course's first text.
