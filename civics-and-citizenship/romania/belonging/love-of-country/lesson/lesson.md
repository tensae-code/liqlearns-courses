# Love of Country

## Do it first

Name three things you love about your country — a place, a person or people, a thing it does well. For one of them, name one act by which you could care for it.

## What the book teaches

Love of country is the feeling that binds the citizen to the wider home: joy in its beauties and achievements, sorrow at its troubles, and the wish to make it better. It is shown not only in words but in acts — learning, working, keeping the laws, caring for its places and people. The book names it plainly as a feeling a citizen cultivates.

## The lesson in full

Feeling follows knowledge in the book's order: locality, traditions, country, symbols — and then the love that all that knowledge is meant to grow.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: love of country is a civic feeling — caring for Romania, being proud of its good, and wishing and working for its betterment. Give one real example from your own life in Romania, and name the lesson's key words: love of country, civic feeling, pride, care. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Romania's own books teach civics in widening circles: the person first — moral traits taught as pairs of opposites — then relations with things, with animals and plants, and with other people; then belonging, moral values and norms in everyday life, closing with the universal rights of the child. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life and your own place, really look: your own life is this course's first text.
