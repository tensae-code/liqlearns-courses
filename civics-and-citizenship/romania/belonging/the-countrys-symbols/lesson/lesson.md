# The Country's Symbols

## Do it first

Draw Romania's flag and say what you know of its colours. Then describe a moment when the anthem is sung and how people behave. Note what the respect shown to the symbol means.

## What the book teaches

A country is recognised by its insignia: the flag, with its colours; the national anthem, sung at solemn moments; and the coat of arms, borne on official acts and buildings. These symbols stand for the whole country and everyone in it. Citizens treat them with respect — standing for the anthem, honouring the flag — because respecting the symbol is respecting the country it stands for.

## The lesson in full

Symbols make the wide circle visible: a child cannot see a whole country, but he can see its flag, hear its anthem, and recognise its arms.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the country's insignia — its flag, its anthem and its coat of arms — stand for Romania, and citizens treat them with respect. Give one real example from your own life in Romania, and name the lesson's key words: flag, anthem, coat of arms, symbols. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Romania's own books teach civics in widening circles: the person first — moral traits taught as pairs of opposites — then relations with things, with animals and plants, and with other people; then belonging, moral values and norms in everyday life, closing with the universal rights of the child. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life and your own place, really look: your own life is this course's first text.
