# My Country

## Do it first

On a map of Romania, find your locality, then three localities far from yours. Say what makes their people your fellow citizens, though you have never met them.

## What the book teaches

Beyond the locality stands the country: Romania, the wider home. A country is one land, one people in many localities, one shared life under the same laws. To know my country is to know that my locality is one part of a larger whole — and that people in distant localities are my fellow citizens, members of the same home.

## The lesson in full

The widening circles of the two books meet here: person, things, nature, groups, locality — and now the country itself, the circle the rest of the course will fill in.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: my country — Romania — is the wider home that holds all its localities and all its people together. Give one real example from your own life in Romania, and name the lesson's key words: country, Romania, fellow citizens, wider home. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Romania's own books teach civics in widening circles: the person first — moral traits taught as pairs of opposites — then relations with things, with animals and plants, and with other people; then belonging, moral values and norms in everyday life, closing with the universal rights of the child. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life and your own place, really look: your own life is this course's first text.

2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: my country — Romania — is the wider home that holds all its localities and all its people together.
3. Teach this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
