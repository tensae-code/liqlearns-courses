# My Locality

## Do it first

Draw or list your locality: its centre, its important places, the people who keep it running. Mark the place you love most and say why.

## What the book teaches

The Grade 4 book widens the circle to belonging, and belonging starts near: my locality — the town or village where I live. To know your locality is to know its places, its people, its life; to value it is to care for it as your own first wider home. The citizen's love of country grows from this small, known ground.

## The lesson in full

Grade 4's Unit 1 revises the Grade 3 book (named, not re-placed); the new climb opens here, with the locality — the largest place a young child can still know street by street.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: my locality is the place I belong to first — my town or village — and knowing and valuing it is the start of belonging. Give one real example from your own life in Romania, and name the lesson's key words: locality, belonging, home place, community. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Romania's own books teach civics in widening circles: the person first — moral traits taught as pairs of opposites — then relations with things, with animals and plants, and with other people; then belonging, moral values and norms in everyday life, closing with the universal rights of the child. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life and your own place, really look: your own life is this course's first text.
