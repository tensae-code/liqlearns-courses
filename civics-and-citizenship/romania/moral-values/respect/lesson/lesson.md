# Respect

## Do it first

Show respect today to someone it is easy to overlook — greet them properly, listen fully, thank them truly. Note what changes in them and in you.

## What the book teaches

Respect returns, deepened from Grade 3: to respect is to honour the worth of every person — the old and the young, the known and the stranger — in greeting, listening, speech and conduct. Disrespect belittles and ignores. Respect is not fear of the powerful; it is owed to all equally, because every person has worth. A community where respect holds is one where everyone can stand upright.

## The lesson in full

The filing itself marks this lesson as respect deepened from Grade 3 — the ladder climbing the same virtue a second time, at the wider circle.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: respect honours every person's worth — against disrespect — and it is owed to all, not only to the powerful. Give one real example from your own life in Romania, and name the lesson's key words: respect, disrespect, worth, all persons. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Romania's own books teach civics in widening circles: the person first — moral traits taught as pairs of opposites — then relations with things, with animals and plants, and with other people; then belonging, moral values and norms in everyday life, closing with the universal rights of the child. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life and your own place, really look: your own life is this course's first text.
