# Good and Evil

## Do it first

Take one story you know well. Point to the good acts and the evil acts in it, and say what each one built or broke. Then weigh one act of your own the same way.

## What the book teaches

The first and deepest value-pair is good and evil. Good is what helps, builds, protects and heals; evil is what harms, breaks and destroys. Stories, and life itself, present the pair in every situation: the helping hand or the hurtful one. Learning to tell good from evil — in tales first, then in one's own acts — is the ground of all the other values.

## The lesson in full

The pair-method of the two books reaches its root here: all the earlier pairs — kindness, honesty, respect — are branches of this one.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: good and evil are the basic moral pair — good builds and helps, evil harms and destroys, and every act can be weighed between them. Give one real example from your own life in Romania, and name the lesson's key words: good, evil, choice, acts. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Romania's own books teach civics in widening circles: the person first — moral traits taught as pairs of opposites — then relations with things, with animals and plants, and with other people; then belonging, moral values and norms in everyday life, closing with the universal rights of the child. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life and your own place, really look: your own life is this course's first text.
