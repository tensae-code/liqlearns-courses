# Responsibility

## Do it first

Take one duty that is truly yours — at home or in class — and do it today without being reminded. If something of yours went wrong recently, answer for it plainly and repair what you can.

## What the book teaches

Responsibility means answering: for your acts, your words, your duties and their consequences. The responsible person does what is theirs to do, admits mistakes, and repairs what they broke. Irresponsibility shrugs — it was not me, it does not matter, someone else will do it. Every trust ever given — a task, a pet, a group, one day a country — rests on responsibility.

## The lesson in full

Responsibility closes the values unit because it is the value that carries all the others into action: a value you will not answer for is only a word.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: responsibility answers for one's own acts and duties — against irresponsibility, which shrugs and blames others. Give one real example from your own life in Romania, and name the lesson's key words: responsibility, irresponsibility, answering, duties. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Romania's own books teach civics in widening circles: the person first — moral traits taught as pairs of opposites — then relations with things, with animals and plants, and with other people; then belonging, moral values and norms in everyday life, closing with the universal rights of the child. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life and your own place, really look: your own life is this course's first text.
