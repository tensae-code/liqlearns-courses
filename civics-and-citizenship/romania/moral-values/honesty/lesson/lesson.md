# Honesty

## Do it first

Keep one small promise today exactly as you made it, and tell one truth that would be easier to bend. Note what honesty costs and what it protects.

## What the book teaches

Honesty is truth in practice: saying what is true, doing your own work, returning what is not yours, keeping your word. Dishonesty deceives — by lying, cheating, stealing or breaking promises — and it corrodes trust wherever it spreads: a class, a family, a market cannot run if no one's word holds. The honest person can be trusted, and trust is the ground others stand on.

## The lesson in full

Grade 3 taught sincerity, truth in speech; Grade 4 widens it to honesty, truth in all dealings — the same virtue at the wider circle.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: honesty says and does what is true — in words, in work and in dealings — against dishonesty, which deceives. Give one real example from your own life in Romania, and name the lesson's key words: honesty, dishonesty, truth, trust. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Romania's own books teach civics in widening circles: the person first — moral traits taught as pairs of opposites — then relations with things, with animals and plants, and with other people; then belonging, moral values and norms in everyday life, closing with the universal rights of the child. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life and your own place, really look: your own life is this course's first text.
