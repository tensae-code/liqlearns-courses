# What Moral Values Are

## Do it first

Name three values your family holds. For each one, name one act it guided — something someone did, or refused to do, because of it.

## What the book teaches

After belonging, the book turns to moral values: the goods held as right and worthy — good itself, honesty, respect, responsibility and the rest of this unit. A value guides twice: it helps us judge acts (this is good, that is bad) and it steers our own conduct. Values are learned from family, school and community, and they show in what a person actually does.

## The lesson in full

Grade 3 taught single traits as pairs of opposites; Grade 4 now gathers them under their proper name — values — before the next unit turns them into norms for everyday life.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: moral values are the goods a person and a community hold as right and worthy — they guide how we judge and how we act. Give one real example from your own life in Romania, and name the lesson's key words: moral values, good, guidance, conduct. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Romania's own books teach civics in widening circles: the person first — moral traits taught as pairs of opposites — then relations with things, with animals and plants, and with other people; then belonging, moral values and norms in everyday life, closing with the universal rights of the child. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life and your own place, really look: your own life is this course's first text.
