# Altruism and Egoism

## Do it first

Do one altruistic act today that costs you something real — time, a turn, a thing you wanted. Notice the difference between how it feels and how keeping everything for yourself feels.

## What the book teaches

Altruism is care for others' good: sharing, helping, giving time and things, sometimes putting another's need first. Egoism is its opposite: only oneself matters, others are tools or obstacles. A community can only live on altruism — families, classes and villages run on people who give. The book teaches the child to recognise both, in stories and in himself.

## The lesson in full

Altruism is where the values unit turns outward: good and evil weighed, then the self measured against others.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: altruism puts others' good alongside or before one's own; egoism thinks only of oneself — the pair decides how a person shares life with others. Give one real example from your own life in Romania, and name the lesson's key words: altruism, egoism, sharing, helping. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Romania's own books teach civics in widening circles: the person first — moral traits taught as pairs of opposites — then relations with things, with animals and plants, and with other people; then belonging, moral values and norms in everyday life, closing with the universal rights of the child. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life and your own place, really look: your own life is this course's first text.
