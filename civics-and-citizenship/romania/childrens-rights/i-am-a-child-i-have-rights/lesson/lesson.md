# I Am a Child, I Have Rights

## Do it first

Name three rights you hold as a child. For each, name who must respect it — family, school, the state — and one sign that it is respected in your life.

## What the book teaches

The book's final unit opens with a declaration: I am a child, I have rights. A right is not a favour or a reward: it belongs to the holder. The child holds rights — to life, to a name and a family, to education, to care and protection, to play, to be heard — because he is a child, before he has earned anything. Knowing your rights is the first step to their being respected.

## The lesson in full

Rights arrive last in the two books' climb, as their capstone: the person formed through traits, relations, belonging, values and norms now stands as a holder of rights.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: a child is a holder of rights — being a child means certain rights belong to you simply because you are a child. Give one real example from your own life in Romania, and name the lesson's key words: child, rights, holder of rights, protection. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Romania's own books teach civics in widening circles: the person first — moral traits taught as pairs of opposites — then relations with things, with animals and plants, and with other people; then belonging, moral values and norms in everyday life, closing with the universal rights of the child. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life and your own place, really look: your own life is this course's first text.
