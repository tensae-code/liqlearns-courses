# Rights and Responsibilities

## Do it first

Take one right you hold. Name the responsibility that stands beside it — what you must do, or not do, because others hold rights too. Keep the pair together in one sentence.

## What the book teaches

The course closes by pairing its two great words: rights and responsibilities. My rights are real, and so are everyone else's: my freedom ends where another's rights begin. Holding rights brings responsibilities — to respect others' rights, to use my own well, to do my duties at home, at school and in the community. The citizen the two books have formed holds both together, and that is where the course ends.

## The lesson in full

Grade 3 ended with rights and duties inside small groups; Grade 4 ends with rights and responsibilities at the scale of all children — the same pairing, at the widest circle, closing the pair of books.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: rights stand together with responsibilities — my rights end where another's begin, and holding rights means answering for how I use them. Give one real example from your own life in Romania, and name the lesson's key words: rights, responsibilities, others' rights, duties. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Romania's own books teach civics in widening circles: the person first — moral traits taught as pairs of opposites — then relations with things, with animals and plants, and with other people; then belonging, moral values and norms in everyday life, closing with the universal rights of the child. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life and your own place, really look: your own life is this course's first text.
