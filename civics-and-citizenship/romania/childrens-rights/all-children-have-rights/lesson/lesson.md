# All Children Have Rights

## Do it first

Think of a child very different from you — another country, another language, another life. Name the rights you share with that child, exactly the same, and say why no difference can remove them.

## What the book teaches

The rights of the child are universal: they belong to all children — every country, every language, rich or poor, girl or boy, well or ill. No child holds more rights than another, and no child can be left outside them. Universality is what makes them rights of the child, not privileges of some children. The book teaches the child to see every other child as a fellow holder of the same rights.

## The lesson in full

From 'I have rights' to 'all children have rights' — the widening circle one last time, now to every child on earth.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: children's rights are universal — they belong to every child, everywhere, without distinction. Give one real example from your own life in Romania, and name the lesson's key words: universal rights, all children, equality, everywhere. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Romania's own books teach civics in widening circles: the person first — moral traits taught as pairs of opposites — then relations with things, with animals and plants, and with other people; then belonging, moral values and norms in everyday life, closing with the universal rights of the child. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life and your own place, really look: your own life is this course's first text.
