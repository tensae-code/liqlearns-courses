# Civics and Citizenship — Romania course — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

Civics belongs to the country: this course is Romania's
own — built from Romania's Educație Civică (Civic
Education) student books for Grades 3 and 4 (for the
Ministerul Educației, Romanian). Inside the course the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: the titles
below are the units the placements coin from the books'
own units, and the lessons inside them climb the stocked
levels in path order — what the learner proves, step by
step. The grades the books come from live only in each
lesson's sources.

The path's own logic is the books': start from the
person and move outward in widening circles of relation
— the person with his moral traits, then relations with
things, with animals and plants, with other people; then
widen again — belonging (locality, traditions, country,
symbols, the European Union), moral values, moral norms
in everyday life — and close with the universal rights
of the child. That ladder IS the path order (recommended,
never forced — a learner can jump in anywhere; the
"start here instead" hints point down this ladder).
Every lesson stands on student books — taught in 1
country (Romania); see ABOUT.md for the book list and
the acceptance reckoning.

## The titles

- `the-person/` — Level 1. The Grade 3 book's opening
  unit: what a person is, and the moral traits taught as
  pairs of opposites — kindness or unkindness, courage
  or cowardice; respect or disrespect, sincerity or
  insincerity; modesty or its lack, trust or distrust.
  It sits first because the book itself starts there —
  the person is the textbook's first text.
- `me-and-things/` — Level 1. Relations with things:
  what things are and why we need them, the things that
  express a person, and property with care against
  negligence. It follows the person because things are
  the nearest world the child's hands meet every day.
- `me-and-nature/` — Level 1. Relations with animals
  and plants: what they are, attention and care,
  gentleness and respect, compassion. It widens the
  circle from handled objects to living beings, with
  the same pairs-of-opposites teaching.
- `me-and-others/` — Level 1. Relations with other
  people, met in small groups: family and friends, play
  and learning groups, and group rules with rights and
  duties inside them. It closes Level 1 because the
  circle that began with the single person ends with
  the person among people — the seed of citizenship.
- `belonging/` — Level 2. The Grade 4 book's belonging
  unit: my locality, local traditions, my country, the
  country's symbols, love of country, and Romania in
  the European Union. It opens Level 2 because the
  second book widens the circle from groups to the
  places and wholes the child belongs to.
- `moral-values/` — Level 2. What moral values are,
  then the great pairs: good and evil, altruism and
  egoism, honesty, respect deepened from Grade 3, and
  responsibility. It follows belonging because the
  citizen who knows where he belongs next learns what
  goods should guide him there.
- `moral-norms/` — Level 2. Moral norms and everyday
  moral-civic behaviour: what norms do, behaviour in
  the family, at school, with friends and in public
  places, and prosocial against antisocial behaviour —
  and how behaviour changes. It follows values because
  norms are values made practical, walked place by
  place through daily life.
- `childrens-rights/` — Level 2. The universal rights
  of the child: I am a child, I have rights; all
  children have rights; rights together with
  responsibilities. It closes the ladder because the
  person formed through traits, relations, belonging,
  values and norms stands at last as a holder of
  rights — the two books' capstone.
