# Gentleness and Respect for Living Things

## Do it first

Hold or approach one animal today — or tend one plant — with deliberate gentleness: slow hands, soft voice. Notice how the living thing answers gentleness differently from roughness.

## What the book teaches

Gentleness handles living things without hurting them — a soft hand with an animal, care not to break a plant. Respect remembers that animals and plants are alive and have their place in the world. Their opposites are brutality, which hurts for no reason, and disrespect, which treats living things as if they were nothing. Both gentleness and respect are habits the hand learns.

## The lesson in full

The same word — respect — that the first unit taught between people returns here between the child and living things: one virtue, widening circles.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: living things are to be treated with gentleness and respect, against brutality and disrespect. Give one real example from your own life in Romania, and name the lesson's key words: gentleness, respect, brutality, living things. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Romania's own books teach civics in widening circles: the person first — moral traits taught as pairs of opposites — then relations with things, with animals and plants, and with other people; then belonging, moral values and norms in everyday life, closing with the universal rights of the child. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life and your own place, really look: your own life is this course's first text.
