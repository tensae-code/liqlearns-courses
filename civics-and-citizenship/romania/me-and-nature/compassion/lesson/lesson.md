# Compassion for Animals and Plants

## Do it first

Think of an animal or plant you have seen suffering or struggling. Say what it needed and what compassion would do. If you can do it today, do it.

## What the book teaches

Compassion feels with a living being that suffers — the hurt animal, the wilting plant — and moves to help. Love cares for living things as goods in themselves. Courage faces the animal or the situation that frightens us calmly, without cruelty and without panic. Their opposites — lack of compassion, hatred and fear — close the heart or turn it against the living world.

## The lesson in full

This closes the nature unit at its deepest point: not only hands that care, but a heart that feels with living things — the bridge from nature to other people, the book's final unit.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: compassion, love and courage govern our relations with animals and plants — against lack of compassion, hatred and fear. Give one real example from your own life in Romania, and name the lesson's key words: compassion, love, courage, fear. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Romania's own books teach civics in widening circles: the person first — moral traits taught as pairs of opposites — then relations with things, with animals and plants, and with other people; then belonging, moral values and norms in everyday life, closing with the universal rights of the child. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life and your own place, really look: your own life is this course's first text.
