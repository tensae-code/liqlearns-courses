# Attention and Care for Animals and Plants

## Do it first

Find one plant or animal near your home or school that needs something today — water, food, shelter, quiet. Give it what it needs, and watch what changes.

## What the book teaches

Living beings near us need attention: noticing the dry plant, the hungry animal, the bird in winter. Attention becomes care — watering, feeding, sheltering, protecting. The opposites are inattention, which does not see, and indifference, which sees and does nothing. The book teaches the child to see, and then to act.

## The lesson in full

Attention first, then care: the order itself is the teaching — most harm to living things begins not in cruelty but in not noticing.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: toward plants and animals the right attitudes are attention and care, against inattention and indifference. Give one real example from your own life in Romania, and name the lesson's key words: attention, care, inattention, indifference. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Romania's own books teach civics in widening circles: the person first — moral traits taught as pairs of opposites — then relations with things, with animals and plants, and with other people; then belonging, moral values and norms in everyday life, closing with the universal rights of the child.
