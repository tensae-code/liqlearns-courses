# About the Civics and Citizenship — Romania course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Educație Civică (Civic Education) student books of
Romania, published for the Ministerul Educației (Ministry of
Education). The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in
a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are
ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Romania inside the Civics and
Citizenship folder (one course per country). Inside it, the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place
is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the
ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced
gate. The school grades the books come from (Grades 3 and 4)
appear only in each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as
provenance.

The course has **two stocked levels**, following Romania's
own books' order — primary civics starts from the person
and moves outward in widening circles of relation:

1. **Level 1 — The Person and My Relations** (14 lessons,
   in the titles `the-person/`, `me-and-things/`,
   `me-and-nature/` and `me-and-others/`). What a person
   is; moral traits taught as pairs of opposites (kindness
   or unkindness, courage or cowardice; respect or
   disrespect, sincerity or insincerity; modesty or lack
   of modesty, trust or distrust); relations with things
   (property and care); relations with animals and plants
   (attention, gentleness, compassion); and relations with
   other people (small groups, their rules, rights and
   duties inside them).
2. **Level 2 — Belonging, Moral Values and Norms, and the
   Rights of the Child** (21 lessons, in the titles
   `belonging/`, `moral-values/`, `moral-norms/` and
   `childrens-rights/`). Belonging — my locality, local
   traditions, my country, the country's symbols, love of
   country, Romania in the European Union; moral values —
   good and evil, altruism and egoism, honesty, respect,
   responsibility; moral norms in everyday life — behaviour
   in the family, at school, with friends, in public
   places, prosocial and antisocial behaviour; and the
   universal rights of the child, held together with
   responsibilities.

**Romania's own way:** primary civics starts from the
person and moves outward in widening circles of relation —
me as a person with moral traits, then my relations with
things, then with animals and plants, then with other
people — every trait taught as a pair of opposites
(kindness or unkindness, courage or cowardice) through
stories and situations. The second book widens the circle
from the person to belonging — my locality, its traditions,
my country and its symbols, Romania in the European Union
— then to moral values and moral norms in everyday life
(family, school, friends, public places), and ends with
the universal rights of the child.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later;
the books' own shape, walked in order):

- *From the person outward to every child's rights:*
  "Start with the person — what a person is, and the
  moral traits, each taught against its opposite:
  kindness, courage, respect, sincerity, modesty, trust.
  Then your relations: with things — property and care;
  with animals and plants — attention, gentleness,
  compassion; with other people — your family, your
  friends, your groups, their rules, your rights and
  duties inside them. Then belong: your locality, its
  traditions, your country and its symbols, your love of
  country, Romania in the European Union. Then the values
  that guide you — good against evil, altruism against
  egoism, honesty, respect, responsibility — and the
  norms that carry them into everyday life: at home, at
  school, with friends, in public. And stand at last as
  what you are: a child, with rights — rights all children
  hold, held together with responsibilities."
