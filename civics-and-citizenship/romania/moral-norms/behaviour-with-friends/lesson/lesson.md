# Behaviour with Friends

## Do it first

Do one loyal act for a friend today — defend them fairly, help them in a real need, or tell them a needed truth kindly. Note what it does to the friendship.

## What the book teaches

Friendship lives by norms: be loyal — stand by your friend; be truthful — no tales behind backs; be fair — in games, in turns, in sharing; help in need — when a friend is stuck, sad or in trouble. A friend who breaks these norms breaks the friendship itself, slowly or at once. Good conduct among friends is how a child first practises faithfulness.

## The lesson in full

Friends were met in Grade 3 as a small group; here the group gets its moral rule-book, written as everyday behaviour.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: among friends, moral norms mean loyalty, truth, fairness in play and help in need — friendship kept by conduct, not only by feeling. Give one real example from your own life in Romania, and name the lesson's key words: friends, loyalty, fairness, help. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Romania's own books teach civics in widening circles: the person first — moral traits taught as pairs of opposites — then relations with things, with animals and plants, and with other people; then belonging, moral values and norms in everyday life, closing with the universal rights of the child. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life and your own place, really look: your own life is this course's first text.
