# Prosocial and Antisocial Behaviour

## Do it first

Name one prosocial act you saw this week and one antisocial one, and what each did to the people around it. Then name one behaviour of your own you want to change, and its first step.

## What the book teaches

Conduct has a social measure: prosocial behaviour helps — cooperating, helping, protecting, building; antisocial behaviour harms — aggression, destruction, deceit, exclusion. The book's hopeful close to the unit: behaviour can change. A person who sees his antisocial acts, wills otherwise, practises the better way and accepts help can become prosocial — no one is sentenced to his worst acts.

## The lesson in full

Change is the unit's last word on norms: norms are not only for judging others — they are the path by which one's own behaviour is remade.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: behaviour that helps society is prosocial; behaviour that harms it is antisocial — and behaviour can change, by will, practice and help. Give one real example from your own life in Romania, and name the lesson's key words: prosocial, antisocial, change, behaviour. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Romania's own books teach civics in widening circles: the person first — moral traits taught as pairs of opposites — then relations with things, with animals and plants, and with other people; then belonging, moral values and norms in everyday life, closing with the universal rights of the child. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life and your own place, really look: your own life is this course's first text.
