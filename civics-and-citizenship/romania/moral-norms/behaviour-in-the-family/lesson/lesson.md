# Behaviour in the Family

## Do it first

Do one piece of your home's work today before being asked, and speak one word of thanks to whoever carries the home most. Note how the family answers.

## What the book teaches

The first field of moral norms is the family. Behaviour in the family means: respect and gratitude toward parents and elders, help with the work of the home, truth spoken inside it, kindness among brothers and sisters, and care when someone is ill or tired. The family is where norms are first learned — and where they are practised every single day.

## The lesson in full

The book walks the norms through life's places in widening order — family first, as Grade 3's groups began with the family too.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: everyday moral life begins in the family: respect for parents and elders, help with the home's work, truth and kindness among its members. Give one real example from your own life in Romania, and name the lesson's key words: family, respect, help, everyday moral life. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Romania's own books teach civics in widening circles: the person first — moral traits taught as pairs of opposites — then relations with things, with animals and plants, and with other people; then belonging, moral values and norms in everyday life, closing with the universal rights of the child. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life and your own place, really look: your own life is this course's first text.
