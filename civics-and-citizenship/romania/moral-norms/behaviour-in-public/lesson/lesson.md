# Behaviour in Public Places

## Do it first

On your next journey or visit to a public place, keep three norms deliberately: queue fairly, give place to someone who needs it more, leave the place clean. Note how strangers answer consideration.

## What the book teaches

Public places — streets, transport, shops, parks, cinemas — are shared with strangers, and norms make sharing possible: queue and wait your turn, keep quiet where quiet is due, give seats to those who need them, keep public things clean and unbroken, cross and travel by the rules. Consideration for people you will never meet is citizenship in its everyday clothes.

## The lesson in full

The norms walk ends at the widest everyday field: the public place, where the child's conduct touches people outside every group he belongs to.

> **Tip:** Romania's own books teach civics in widening circles: the person first — moral traits taught as pairs of opposites — then relations with things, with animals and plants, and with other people; then belonging, moral values and norms in everyday life, closing with the universal rights of the child. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life and your own place, really look: your own life is this course's first text.
