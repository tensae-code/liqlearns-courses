# What Moral Norms Do

## Do it first

Name three moral norms you followed this morning — at home, on the road, arriving at school. For each, name the value standing behind it.

## What the book teaches

Values become conduct through moral norms: the unwritten and written rules that say, in each kind of situation, what is to be done — greet, queue, tell the truth, help, keep quiet here, speak up there. Norms are values made practical. Without them, common life would collide at every step; with them, strangers can live together in trust and order.

## The lesson in full

This unit is the hinge of the Grade 4 book: values (Unit 3) become norms (Unit 4), and norms are then walked through the places of everyday life — family, school, friends, public places.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: moral norms are the rules of conduct that values give rise to — they tell us, case by case, how to behave, and they hold common life together. Give one real example from your own life in Romania, and name the lesson's key words: moral norms, rules of conduct, values, common life. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Romania's own books teach civics in widening circles: the person first — moral traits taught as pairs of opposites — then relations with things, with animals and plants, and with other people; then belonging, moral values and norms in everyday life, closing with the universal rights of the child. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life and your own place, really look: your own life is this course's first text.
