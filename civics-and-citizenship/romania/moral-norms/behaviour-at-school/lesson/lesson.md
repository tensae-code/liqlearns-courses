# Behaviour at School

## Do it first

Keep one school norm today precisely when no one would notice if you broke it — your own work, a kept rule, a cared-for book. Note what held you to it.

## What the book teaches

School is the child's second moral world. Its norms: respect for teachers and for every classmate, honesty in lessons and tests — your own work, truly yours — care for the school's books, rooms and yard, punctuality, and keeping the school's rules even when no one watches. A class where these norms hold is a class where everyone can learn.

## The lesson in full

School norms join the two earlier circles: the care for things of Grade 3 and the learning group, now as the everyday conduct of the pupil-citizen.

> **Tip:** Romania's own books teach civics in widening circles: the person first — moral traits taught as pairs of opposites — then relations with things, with animals and plants, and with other people; then belonging, moral values and norms in everyday life, closing with the universal rights of the child. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life and your own place, really look: your own life is this course's first text.
