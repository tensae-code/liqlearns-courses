# What Things Are

## Do it first

Name ten things you used this morning before school. For each one, say what need it answers. Keep the list; the lesson starts from it.

## What the book teaches

After the person, the book turns to the person's relations with things. Things are the objects around us — made by people, used by people: clothes, tools, books, furniture, toys. People need things: to eat, to dress, to learn, to play, to work. Naming what things are, and why we need them, is the first step toward treating them well.

## The lesson in full

The widening circle of the Grade 3 book moves one step out: from the person himself to the nearest world he handles every day — the world of things.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: things are the objects people make and use, and people need things to live, learn, play and work. Give one real example from your own life in Romania, and name the lesson's key words: things, need, objects, use. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Romania's own books teach civics in widening circles: the person first — moral traits taught as pairs of opposites — then relations with things, with animals and plants, and with other people; then belonging, moral values and norms in everyday life, closing with the universal rights of the child. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life and your own place, really look: your own life is this course's first text.
