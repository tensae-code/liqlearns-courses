# Property and Care for Things

## Do it first

Pick one thing of yours that you have neglected — a book, a toy, a tool — and care for it now: clean it, mend it, put it in its place. Then do the same for one thing that belongs to everyone at school.

## What the book teaches

Things belong to people: that belonging is property — mine, yours, the family's, the school's, everyone's. Because things are needed and express their owners, the right attitude toward them is care: use them properly, keep them, repair them. Its opposite is negligence — carelessness that breaks, loses and wastes what someone needs.

## The lesson in full

Care for things is where the young citizen's responsibility first becomes visible: in what his hands do to the objects of daily life, his own and other people's.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: things belong to someone — property — and the right attitude toward things is care, against negligence. Give one real example from your own life in Romania, and name the lesson's key words: property, care, negligence, belonging. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Romania's own books teach civics in widening circles: the person first — moral traits taught as pairs of opposites — then relations with things, with animals and plants, and with other people; then belonging, moral values and norms in everyday life, closing with the universal rights of the child. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life and your own place, really look: your own life is this course's first text.
