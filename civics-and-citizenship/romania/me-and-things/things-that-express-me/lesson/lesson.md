# The Things That Express Me

## Do it first

Choose three of your things that express you most. Say what each one says about you. Then look at a friend's things and guess what they express — and ask if you guessed right.

## What the book teaches

Some things are more than useful: they express the person. The clothes you choose, your toys and games, the books you keep, the collection you build, your own room arranged your way — each one says something about who you are and what you love. Other people's things express them the same way, which is why their things deserve respect too.

## The lesson in full

The book reads a child's own world — the wardrobe, the toy box, the bookshelf, the collection, the room — as the child's first self-portrait.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: some things express the person who owns them — clothes, toys and games, books, collections, and one's own room. Give one real example from your own life in Romania, and name the lesson's key words: express, clothes, collections, my room. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Romania's own books teach civics in widening circles: the person first — moral traits taught as pairs of opposites — then relations with things, with animals and plants, and with other people; then belonging, moral values and norms in everyday life, closing with the universal rights of the child. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life and your own place, really look: your own life is this course's first text.
