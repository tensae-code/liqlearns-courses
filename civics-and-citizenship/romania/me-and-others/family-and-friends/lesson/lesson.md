# Family and Friends

## Do it first

Name the members of your family and of your group of friends. For each group, say one thing members do for one another. Keep the two lists side by side.

## What the book teaches

The widest circle of the Grade 3 book is other people, met first in small groups. The family is the first group: people bound by love and care, who live together and answer for one another. The group of friends is chosen: people we trust, play with and help. Inside each group, relations — how members treat one another — make the group good or bad to live in.

## The lesson in full

Groups come before rules in the book's order: first belong, know the group from inside — then, two lessons later, learn its rules, rights and duties.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the first small groups we belong to are the family and the group of friends, and each has its own relations inside it. Give one real example from your own life in Romania, and name the lesson's key words: family, friends, small groups, belonging. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Romania's own books teach civics in widening circles: the person first — moral traits taught as pairs of opposites — then relations with things, with animals and plants, and with other people; then belonging, moral values and norms in everyday life, closing with the universal rights of the child. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life and your own place, really look: your own life is this course's first text.
