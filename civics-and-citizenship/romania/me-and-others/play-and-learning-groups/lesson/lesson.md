# Play Groups and Learning Groups

## Do it first

In your next game, keep one rule of fair play on purpose — take your turn, include someone left out. In your next class task, help one classmate understand one thing. Note how each group felt.

## What the book teaches

A child also belongs to a play group and a learning group. The play group holds together by fair play: taking turns, keeping the game's rules, including everyone. The learning group — the class, the study circle — holds together by help: explaining, waiting, sharing what you understood. Each group asks its members for relations that fit its purpose.

## The lesson in full

Play and learning are the child's two public worlds; the book teaches that each world works only when its members relate rightly inside it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the play group and the learning group are small groups with their own ways of relating — fair play in one, help and patience in the other. Give one real example from your own life in Romania, and name the lesson's key words: play group, learning group, fair play, help. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Romania's own books teach civics in widening circles: the person first — moral traits taught as pairs of opposites — then relations with things, with animals and plants, and with other people; then belonging, moral values and norms in everyday life, closing with the universal rights of the child. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life and your own place, really look: your own life is this course's first text.
