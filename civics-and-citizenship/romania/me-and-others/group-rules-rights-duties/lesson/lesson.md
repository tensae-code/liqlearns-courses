# Group Rules, Rights and Duties

## Do it first

Take one group you belong to. Write one right its members have, one duty they carry, and one rule that protects both. Check your three against the group's real life.

## What the book teaches

A group that lasts has rules — agreed ways of behaving that protect everyone in it. Inside the group's rules, each member has rights: to be heard, to be treated fairly, to take part. And each member has duties: to keep the rules, to do their share, to respect the others' rights. Rights and duties come as a pair — that pairing is the seed of all citizenship.

## The lesson in full

The Grade 3 book ends at the citizen's foundation: rules, rights and duties inside the small groups a child knows — the same pattern the Grade 4 book will widen to the country and to all children.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: small groups live by rules, and inside them every member holds both rights and duties. Give one real example from your own life in Romania, and name the lesson's key words: rules, rights, duties, small groups. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Romania's own books teach civics in widening circles: the person first — moral traits taught as pairs of opposites — then relations with things, with animals and plants, and with other people; then belonging, moral values and norms in everyday life, closing with the universal rights of the child. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life and your own place, really look: your own life is this course's first text.
