# Kindness and Courage

## Do it first

Think of one moment today when you could be kind or unkind, and one when you could be brave or give up. Say which you chose, or will choose. Keep the pair in mind; the lesson works with it.

## What the book teaches

A person's character shows in moral traits — the qualities that make conduct good or bad. The book teaches them as pairs: kindness or unkindness, courage or cowardice. Kindness treats others gently and helps; unkindness hurts. Courage does the right thing even when it is hard or frightening; cowardice gives up. The traits are choices, made again in every situation.

## The lesson in full

Romania's own way runs through this whole book: every trait is taught as a pair of opposites, through stories and situations, so the child learns to name both sides and choose the good one.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: moral traits come in pairs of opposites, and a person chooses: kindness against unkindness, courage against cowardice. Give one real example from your own life in Romania, and name the lesson's key words: kindness, courage, unkindness, cowardice. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Romania's own books teach civics in widening circles: the person first — moral traits taught as pairs of opposites — then relations with things, with animals and plants, and with other people; then belonging, moral values and norms in everyday life, closing with the universal rights of the child. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life and your own place, really look: your own life is this course's first text.
