# Modesty and Trust

## Do it first

Do one good thing today without announcing it, and trust a friend with one real task. Note what each choice costs and what it gives.

## What the book teaches

Modesty lets your work speak without boasting; its opposite, lack of modesty, pushes the self forward at every chance. Trust believes in oneself and in others — that you can do what you promise, and that others can too. Distrust, in oneself or in others, closes every door before it is tried. The book teaches both traits as choices that shape how a person lives with people.

## The lesson in full

This closes the book's first unit: the person's moral traits, each one a pair of opposites — kindness, courage, respect, sincerity, modesty and trust, each against its shadow.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: modesty and trust — in oneself and in others — stand against showing off and against distrust. Give one real example from your own life in Romania, and name the lesson's key words: modesty, trust, boasting, distrust. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Romania's own books teach civics in widening circles: the person first — moral traits taught as pairs of opposites — then relations with things, with animals and plants, and with other people; then belonging, moral values and norms in everyday life, closing with the universal rights of the child. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life and your own place, really look: your own life is this course's first text.
