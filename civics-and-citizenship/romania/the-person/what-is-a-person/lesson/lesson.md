# What Is a Person

## Do it first

Look at the people around you — at home, in class — and say of each one, quietly: a person, like me. Then say what makes you a person: what you think, feel and choose. Keep your list; the lesson names what you found.

## What the book teaches

Civic education is the learning of how people live together well. Its first word is the person: you are a person, and so is every other human being — your person, his person, her person. To be a person is to think, to feel, to choose and to answer for your choices, and every person deserves to be treated as a person.

## The lesson in full

The book opens the whole course here: before things, before animals, before groups, the child meets the person — first himself, then every other. Everything later in the course is built on this one recognition.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: a person is oneself and every other human being — civic education begins by knowing what a person is, starting with your own person. Give one real example from your own life in Romania, and name the lesson's key words: person, civic education, oneself, every other. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Romania's own books teach civics in widening circles: the person first — moral traits taught as pairs of opposites — then relations with things, with animals and plants, and with other people; then belonging, moral values and norms in everyday life, closing with the universal rights of the child. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life and your own place, really look: your own life is this course's first text.
