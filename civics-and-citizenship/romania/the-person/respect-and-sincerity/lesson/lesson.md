# Respect and Sincerity

## Do it first

Greet someone today with real attention, and say one true thing plainly when it would be easier to hide it. Notice how each act feels, and how the other person answers.

## What the book teaches

Respect treats every person as someone who matters — listening, greeting, taking care not to offend. Its opposite, disrespect, ignores and belittles. Sincerity says what is true and means what it says; insincerity hides and pretends. Both traits are practised in small daily acts, at home and at school, and other people feel them at once.

## The lesson in full

Like every trait in this book, respect and sincerity are taught against their opposites, so the learner can recognise disrespect and insincerity when they appear — in others and in himself.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: respect against disrespect and sincerity against insincerity are moral traits a person practises in daily conduct. Give one real example from your own life in Romania, and name the lesson's key words: respect, sincerity, disrespect, insincerity. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Romania's own books teach civics in widening circles: the person first — moral traits taught as pairs of opposites — then relations with things, with animals and plants, and with other people; then belonging, moral values and norms in everyday life, closing with the universal rights of the child. When a lesson asks you to look at your own life and your own place, really look: your own life is this course's first text.
