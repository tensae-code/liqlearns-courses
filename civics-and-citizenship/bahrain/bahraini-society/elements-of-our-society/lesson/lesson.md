# The Elements of Our Society

## Do it first

List the parts of your own society in Bahrain: the people and families, the places, the groups and institutions, the things you share. Keep your list; the lesson names what you found

## What the book teaches

A society is not a crowd. It is people bound together by a shared life on one land, and Bahraini society has elements a citizen can name: who its people are, the families and groups they form, the institutions that serve them, and the land and sea they share.

## The lesson in full

The lesson also names the society's features — the marks that distinguish it. To know your society's elements and features is the citizen's first knowledge: you cannot serve a society you cannot describe.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Bahraini society is made of elements that can be named — its people and groups, its land and institutions, its shared life — and it has features that mark it as this society and no other. Give one real example from your own life in Bahrain, and name the lesson's key words: society, elements, features, community. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Bahrain's own books teach civics by building the citizen from his own society outward — society and the modern state first, then living together, then the citizen facing the world and guarding his homeland. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
