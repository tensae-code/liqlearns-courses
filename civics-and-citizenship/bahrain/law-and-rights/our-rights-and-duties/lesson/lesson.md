# Our Rights and Our Duties

## Do it first

Write one right you hold and one duty that goes with it — at home, at school, in Bahrain. Keep the pair; the dossier holds the two sides together

## What the book teaches

This study dossier (ملف) sets rights and duties side by side. Rights are what the citizen may claim; duties are what the citizen owes. The book refuses to teach one without the other.

## The lesson in full

Held together, they make the citizen whole: a person who only claims becomes a burden, a person who only owes becomes a subject — the citizen of Bahrain is taught to carry both sides at once.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: our rights and our duties are the two sides of citizenship held together — a citizen claims his rights and performs his duties, and neither side stands without the other. Give one real example from your own life in Bahrain, and name the lesson's key words: rights, duties, citizenship, balance. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Bahrain's own books teach civics by building the citizen from his own society outward — society and the modern state first, then living together, then the citizen facing the world and guarding his homeland. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
