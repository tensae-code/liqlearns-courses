# Human Rights

## Do it first

List three things every person should be able to count on — safety, learning, fair treatment, being heard. Keep your list; the lesson names them as rights

## What the book teaches

Human rights belong to every person by being human, before any office or favour grants them. The book teaches what they are: the standing of each person to live, learn, be treated fairly and be respected.

## The lesson in full

The citizen's place in them is double: he holds these rights himself, and he lives among others who hold the same rights — so respecting them is part of citizenship, not an extra.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: human rights are the rights every person holds simply as a human being, and the citizen has a place in them — as a holder of rights and as one who respects the rights of others. Give one real example from your own life in Bahrain, and name the lesson's key words: rights, human, dignity, respect. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Bahrain's own books teach civics by building the citizen from his own society outward — society and the modern state first, then living together, then the citizen facing the world and guarding his homeland. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
