# A State of Law

## Do it first

Think of one rule that binds everyone in Bahrain alike — in school, on the road, in the market. Write what would happen if one person were allowed to stand above it. Keep your answer; the lesson names the principle

## What the book teaches

A state of law means the law rules, not persons: government, institutions and citizens alike stand under the law, and no one is placed above it. Bahrain is taught here as exactly such a state.

## The lesson in full

For the pupil, the rule of law is the frame that makes every other lesson in this course hold: rights can be promised, and duties required, because a law above all persons guards them both.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the Kingdom of Bahrain is a state of law — the law stands above every person and office, and the rule of law is the frame of the whole Kingdom. Give one real example from your own life in Bahrain, and name the lesson's key words: law, rule of law, state, justice. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Bahrain's own books teach civics by building the citizen from his own society outward — society and the modern state first, then living together, then the citizen facing the world and guarding his homeland. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
