# Children's Rights

## Do it first

List four things every child needs and should be able to count on. Keep your list; the lesson names them as the child's rights

## What the book teaches

Children's rights are the human rights of the youngest citizens, stated for their stage of life: the right to be cared for and protected, to learn, to health, and to grow up in a family and a name of their own.

## The lesson in full

The pupil meets this lesson as its subject: these are not rights for other children far away, but his own — and knowing them teaches him both to claim his standing and to respect every other child's.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: children hold their own rights — to care, protection, learning and a name and family — and the pupil, himself a child, learns them as rights he already holds. Give one real example from your own life in Bahrain, and name the lesson's key words: children, rights, protection, care. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Bahrain's own books teach civics by building the citizen from his own society outward — society and the modern state first, then living together, then the citizen facing the world and guarding his homeland. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
