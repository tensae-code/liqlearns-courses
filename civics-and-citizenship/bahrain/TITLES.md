# Civics and Citizenship — Bahrain course — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

Civics belongs to the country: this course is Bahrain's
own — built from Bahrain's التربية للمواطنة (Citizenship
Education) student books for the three preparatory
grades (Ministry of Education, Kingdom of Bahrain,
Arabic). Inside the course the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: the titles below are the units
the placements coin from the books' own lessons, and the
lessons inside them climb the stocked levels in path
order — what the learner proves, step by step. The
grades the books come from live only in each lesson's
sources. Level 1 is sketched, not stocked: its Grade 4
primary book is UNREAD, so no title stands at Level 1.

The path's own logic is the books': build the citizen
from his own society outward — society and the modern
state, law and rights, Bahrain among the nations; then
turn to the society the citizen lives in — its culture,
its women, law in daily life, and the virtues of living
together; then run from national unity outward and
upward — democracy, heritage, environment, work and
development, the citizen before the world's challenges —
and close with the homeland's safety and continuing
march. That ladder IS the path order (recommended, never
forced — a learner can jump in anywhere; the "start here
instead" hints point down this ladder). Every lesson
stands on student books — taught in 1 country (Bahrain);
see ABOUT.md for the UNREAD Grade 4 record and the
staging notes carried for the read books.

## The titles

- `bahraini-society/` — Level 2. The Grade 7 book's
  opening lessons: the elements of Bahraini society and
  its features, and how our society grows. It sits first
  because the book itself starts there — the pupil's own
  society is the textbook's first text.
- `the-modern-state/` — Level 2. The modern state in
  Bahrain, the Crown Prince years, the King's
  achievements, and the reform-and-democratic-march
  study dossier. It sits beside `bahraini-society/`
  because the society's organised form — the state and
  its King's reign — is the citizen's second knowledge.
- `law-and-rights/` — Levels 2–3. One title shared by
  two levels, as the books share it: at Level 2,
  Bahrain a state of law, human rights, and the
  rights-and-duties dossier (Grade 7); at Level 3, law
  in our lives and children's rights (Grade 8). It
  spans the levels because law is first the Kingdom's
  frame and then the citizen's daily companion.
- `bahrain-and-the-world/` — Level 2. Bahrain's
  international relations and its Arab belonging. It
  closes Level 2 because the citizen formed at home is
  last placed among the nations and in the Arab world
  his country belongs to.
- `our-culture/` — Level 3. One lesson: popular culture
  in Bahrain. It opens Level 3 because the second book
  turns from the state to the society's own lived
  culture — the citizen's inheritance from his people.
- `our-society/` — Level 3. One lesson: women in
  Bahraini society. It sits with `our-culture/` because
  both are the society seen from inside — what its
  people hand down, and the women who carry its life.
- `living-together/` — Level 3. Dialogue, social
  institutions and citizenship values, tolerance, and
  rejecting violence. It holds the level's heart: the
  working virtues by which different people live in one
  society, closing with violence refused.
- `national-unity/` — Level 4. One lesson: national
  unity. It opens the top level because the final book
  founds everything after it on the people standing as
  one homeland, under a law above all.
- `democracy/` — Level 4. One lesson: the citizen and
  democracy. It follows unity because a united people's
  citizen is next asked for his roles in ruling.
- `heritage/` — Level 4. One lesson: keeping our
  heritage. It sits early in the level because the
  citizen who takes part must also guard what the
  homeland inherited.
- `environment/` — Level 4. One lesson: environmental
  citizenship. It stands beside `heritage/` because
  both are keeping duties — what the past handed down,
  and the nature the homeland lives in.
- `work-and-development/` — Level 4. Work, and
  development. The pair sits in the book's own order:
  work first, as development's foundation, then
  development itself, sustainable goals included.
- `the-citizen-and-the-world/` — Level 4. Facing the
  knowledge gap, media and globalisation, solidarity
  and international cooperation, international
  humanitarian law, and dialogue of civilisations. It
  is the level's widest title because here the citizen
  faces the whole world — its gaps, its floods of
  media, its shared work, its laws for war, and its
  civilisations in dialogue.
- `homeland-safety/` — Level 4. Violence and sabotage
  in our homeland, protecting the safety of our
  homeland, and our homeland triumphs and continues its
  march. It closes the ladder because the course that
  began with the pupil's own society ends with the
  homeland kept safe and marching on — through citizens
  like the one the course has formed.
