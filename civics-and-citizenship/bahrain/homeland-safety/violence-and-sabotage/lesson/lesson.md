# Violence and Sabotage in Our Homeland

## Do it first

Write what sabotage of a public work — a school, a road, a power line — actually destroys for ordinary people. Keep your account; the lesson names the act for what it is

## What the book teaches

The book turns home with an exclamation: violence and sabotage in our homeland! Violence wounds people; sabotage destroys the works the homeland built for everyone — and both are done, terribly, inside the country they wound.

## The lesson in full

The lesson names and rejects them without softening: there is no cause that makes attacking one's own people's safety or works a citizen's act. The pupil who rejected violence at Grade 8 now refuses it at the scale of the homeland.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: violence and sabotage in the homeland are named and rejected — attacks on people and destruction of the country's own works wound Bahrain itself, and the citizen refuses them utterly. Give one real example from your own life in Bahrain, and name the lesson's key words: violence, sabotage, homeland, rejection. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Bahrain's own books teach civics by building the citizen from his own society outward — society and the modern state first, then living together, then the citizen facing the world and guarding his homeland. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
