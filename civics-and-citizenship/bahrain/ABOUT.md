# About the Civics and Citizenship — Bahrain course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own التربية للمواطنة (Citizenship Education) student books
of the Kingdom of Bahrain, published by the Ministry of
Education, Kingdom of Bahrain. The one exception is the
**Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Bahrain inside the Civics and
Citizenship folder (one course per country). Inside it, the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place
is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the
ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced
gate. The school grades the books come from (Grades 7, 8
and 9, the three preparatory grades) appear only in each
lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

The course has **three stocked levels**, following
Bahrain's own books' order — the preparatory Citizenship
Education run builds the citizen from his own society
outward:

1. **Level 2 — Bahraini Society, the Modern State, Law
   and Rights** (11 lessons, in the titles
   `bahraini-society/`, `the-modern-state/`,
   `law-and-rights/` and `bahrain-and-the-world/`). The
   elements of Bahraini society and how it grows; the
   modern state, the Crown Prince years, the King's
   achievements and the reform-and-democratic-march
   dossier; Bahrain as a state of law, human rights, and
   the rights-and-duties dossier; and Bahrain's
   international relations and Arab belonging.
2. **Level 3 — Culture, Society and Living Together**
   (8 lessons, in the titles `our-culture/`,
   `our-society/`, `law-and-rights/` and
   `living-together/`). Popular culture in Bahrain,
   women in Bahraini society, law in our lives,
   dialogue, social institutions and citizenship values,
   tolerance, children's rights, and rejecting violence.
3. **Level 4 — National Unity, the Citizen and the
   World, and the Homeland's Safety** (14 lessons, in
   the titles `national-unity/`, `democracy/`,
   `heritage/`, `environment/`,
   `work-and-development/`,
   `the-citizen-and-the-world/` and `homeland-safety/`).
   National unity and the citizen's role in democracy;
   keeping heritage and environmental citizenship; work
   and development; the citizen before the world's
   challenges (the knowledge gap, media and
   globalisation, solidarity and cooperation,
   humanitarian law, dialogue of civilisations); and the
   homeland's safety — against violence and sabotage,
   protecting the homeland, and the homeland's continuing
   march.

**Level 1 is sketched, not stocked:** the ladder sketch
in the filings places the census's Grade 4 primary book
at Level 1, but that book is UNREAD (below), so no Level
1 lesson is built and nothing from it is claimed.

**Bahrain's own way:** the first preparatory book builds
the citizen from his own society outward — what Bahraini
society is made of and how it developed, the modern state
and the King's reign and reform project, then law and
human rights, and finally Bahrain among the nations. The
second preparatory book turns from the state to the
society the citizen lives in — popular culture and
women's place in it, law in daily life, and the working
virtues of living together. The final preparatory book
runs from national unity outward and upward — democracy,
heritage, environment, work and development — then the
citizen before the world's challenges, and closes with
the homeland's safety.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later;
the books' own shape, walked in order):

- *From my society to the world and home again:* "Start
  with your own society — its elements, its features, how
  it grew — and the modern state it formed: the King's
  road, his achievements, his reform. Then the frame:
  Bahrain a state of law, your human rights, your rights
  held together with your duties. Then Bahrain placed:
  its relations with the world and its Arab belonging.
  Next, live in that society well: its popular culture,
  its women's role, the law in your daily life, dialogue,
  tolerance, children's rights, and violence refused.
  Last, stand as a citizen of the whole: national unity,
  your part in democracy, heritage and environment kept,
  work and development carried, the world's challenges
  faced with vigilance — and your homeland kept safe,
  marching on."
