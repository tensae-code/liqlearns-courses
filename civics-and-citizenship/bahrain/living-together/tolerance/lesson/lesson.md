# Tolerance

## Do it first

Write about a difference between people in your school or town in Bahrain, and one way people live with it peacefully. Keep your example; the lesson names the virtue in it

## What the book teaches

People in one society differ — in opinion, custom, taste and belief. Tolerance is the virtue that lets difference exist without breaking the society: accepting the other as a fellow citizen and neighbour.

## The lesson in full

The book teaches tolerance as strength, not weakness: the tolerant citizen is secure enough in his own way to let others live theirs, and the society that practises it keeps its peace.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: tolerance is accepting difference within the one society — bearing with people whose views, ways or beliefs differ from yours without hatred or exclusion — and it holds shared life together. Give one real example from your own life in Bahrain, and name the lesson's key words: tolerance, difference, peace, acceptance. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Bahrain's own books teach civics by building the citizen from his own society outward — society and the modern state first, then living together, then the citizen facing the world and guarding his homeland. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
