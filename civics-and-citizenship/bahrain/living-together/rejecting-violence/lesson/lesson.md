# Rejecting Violence

## Do it first

Write what violence leaves behind in a class or a family after it is over — in feelings, in trust, in learning. Keep your account; the lesson draws the citizen's conclusion

## What the book teaches

To reject violence is to renounce it entirely: in school, where it poisons learning; in the family, where it breaks trust; and in society, where it tears the shared life the citizen is being formed to protect.

## The lesson in full

The Grade 8 book closes its living-together climb here: the pupil who has learned dialogue, tolerance and institutions is asked to refuse the opposite of them all — force — wherever it appears.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: violence is to be rejected — renounced in school, in the family and in society — because it solves nothing and wounds the whole shared life; the citizen's way is dialogue and law, not force. Give one real example from your own life in Bahrain, and name the lesson's key words: violence, rejection, peace, dialogue. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Bahrain's own books teach civics by building the citizen from his own society outward — society and the modern state first, then living together, then the citizen facing the world and guarding his homeland. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
