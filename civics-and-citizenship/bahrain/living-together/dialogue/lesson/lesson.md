# Dialogue

## Do it first

Remember a disagreement you saw or had. Write how it was handled — by shouting, by silence, or by talking it through. Keep your account; the lesson teaches the third way

## What the book teaches

Dialogue is more than talking: it is speaking and listening in turn, with the aim of understanding and reaching what is right or agreed, instead of merely winning.

## The lesson in full

The book teaches dialogue as a skill of citizenship because a society of different people survives by it: families, classes, neighbours and a whole country settle their differences by dialogue or suffer them unsettled.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: dialogue — talking through differences calmly, listening and answering — is a citizen's skill for living with people who disagree, and it can be learned and practised. Give one real example from your own life in Bahrain, and name the lesson's key words: dialogue, listening, disagreement, skill. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Bahrain's own books teach civics by building the citizen from his own society outward — society and the modern state first, then living together, then the citizen facing the world and guarding his homeland. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
