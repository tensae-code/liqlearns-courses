# Social Institutions and Citizenship Values

## Do it first

Name the institutions that have taught you how to live with others — family, school, and any others. Beside each, write one value it taught you. Keep your list; the lesson binds institutions to values

## What the book teaches

Citizenship values are not learned from speeches alone. They are carried by social institutions: the family that teaches care, the school that teaches fairness and cooperation, and the other bodies where people live and work together.

## The lesson in full

The lesson binds the two together — institutions and values — so the pupil sees where his values came from and what his own part will be in carrying them to the next generation.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the social institutions — family, school and the bodies of shared life — carry the values of citizenship, teaching them by how they treat people every day. Give one real example from your own life in Bahrain, and name the lesson's key words: institutions, values, family, school. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Bahrain's own books teach civics by building the citizen from his own society outward — society and the modern state first, then living together, then the citizen facing the world and guarding his homeland. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
