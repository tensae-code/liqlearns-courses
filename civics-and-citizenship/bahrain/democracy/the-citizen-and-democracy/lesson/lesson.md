# The Citizen and Democracy

## Do it first

List three things an ordinary citizen — not an official — can do that keep democracy alive. Keep your list; the lesson names the citizen's roles

## What the book teaches

Democracy is not a machine that runs itself. The book teaches the citizen's roles in the democratic system: the acts by which ordinary people take part in ruling their country and hold its public life to account.

## The lesson in full

Those roles matter because democracy is consolidated — made firm and lasting — by citizens performing them. A democracy whose citizens stop taking part weakens; one whose citizens act grows roots.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the citizen has real roles in the democratic system — taking part, choosing, speaking and watching over public life — and by performing them he consolidates democracy, making it firm in his country. Give one real example from your own life in Bahrain, and name the lesson's key words: democracy, citizen, roles, participation. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Bahrain's own books teach civics by building the citizen from his own society outward — society and the modern state first, then living together, then the citizen facing the world and guarding his homeland. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
