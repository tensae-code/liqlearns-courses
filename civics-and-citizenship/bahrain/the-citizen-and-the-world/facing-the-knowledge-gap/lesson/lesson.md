# Facing the Knowledge Gap

## Do it first

Write one thing your country must learn or make for itself rather than always buy or borrow. Keep it; the lesson names the gap it answers

## What the book teaches

Countries are unequal in knowledge: some produce science and technology, others depend on them. That scientific and technological gap is the first world challenge the book sets before the citizen.

## The lesson in full

The citizen's answer is not complaint but learning: a generation that studies science and masters technology narrows the gap for its country. The challenge is set before the pupil because he is the one who will answer it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the citizen faces the world's challenges, first among them the scientific and technological gap between countries — the distance between those who produce knowledge and those who only consume it — and his answer is learning. Give one real example from your own life in Bahrain, and name the lesson's key words: knowledge, technology, gap, challenge. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Bahrain's own books teach civics by building the citizen from his own society outward — society and the modern state first, then living together, then the citizen facing the world and guarding his homeland. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
