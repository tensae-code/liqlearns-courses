# Dialogue of Civilisations

## Do it first

Write what two civilisations or cultures could misunderstand about each other if they never spoke, and what a real dialogue would need to succeed. Keep your answer; the lesson states the conditions

## What the book teaches

Civilisations are the great cultures of peoples, and they meet whether they choose to or not. Dialogue of civilisations is their meeting by speech and listening instead of by prejudice and clash.

## The lesson in full

The book teaches its importance and its conditions — real knowledge of the other, respect, and honest speech — because dialogue done properly prevents the misunderstanding from which conflicts grow. The Grade 8 lesson on dialogue returns here at the scale of the world.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: civilisations meet best in dialogue — knowing each other, speaking and listening across difference — whose importance and conditions the book teaches as the way to prevent misunderstanding and conflict. Give one real example from your own life in Bahrain, and name the lesson's key words: civilisations, dialogue, understanding, conflict. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
