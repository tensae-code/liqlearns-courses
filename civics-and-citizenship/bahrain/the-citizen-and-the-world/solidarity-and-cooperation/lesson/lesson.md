# Solidarity and International Cooperation

## Do it first

Name one hardship or problem no single country can solve alone, and one way countries help each other with it. Keep both; the lesson names the mechanisms built for exactly that

## What the book teaches

Solidarity is standing with others in their hardship as if it touched you; international cooperation is states working together on what none can do alone. The book teaches both as the world's way of answering shared problems.

## The lesson in full

It also teaches the mechanisms they created — the bodies and agreements through which solidarity and cooperation actually happen — so the pupil sees goodwill given an organised form.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: peoples and states practise solidarity and cooperation across borders — standing with others in hardship and working together on shared problems — and they have built mechanisms to carry that work. Give one real example from your own life in Bahrain, and name the lesson's key words: solidarity, cooperation, international, mechanisms. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Bahrain's own books teach civics by building the citizen from his own society outward — society and the modern state first, then living together, then the citizen facing the world and guarding his homeland. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
