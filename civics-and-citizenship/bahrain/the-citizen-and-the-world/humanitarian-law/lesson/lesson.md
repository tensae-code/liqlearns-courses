# International Humanitarian Law

## Do it first

Write two things that should still be forbidden even in a war — harming the wounded, attacking hospitals, and others you can name. Keep your list; the lesson gives it a name and a law

## What the book teaches

Even war has law. International humanitarian law is the body of law that limits the effects of armed conflict: protecting civilians, the wounded and prisoners, and restraining the means of fighting.

## The lesson in full

The book teaches commitment to it as part of the citizen's world education: humanity does not end where conflict begins, and the pupil learns that law's reach extends to the hardest place of all.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: international humanitarian law binds even war: it exists to limit the effects of wars — protecting those who do not fight and restraining how wars are fought — and committing to it is a duty the book teaches. Give one real example from your own life in Bahrain, and name the lesson's key words: humanitarian, law, war, protection. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Bahrain's own books teach civics by building the citizen from his own society outward — society and the modern state first, then living together, then the citizen facing the world and guarding his homeland. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
