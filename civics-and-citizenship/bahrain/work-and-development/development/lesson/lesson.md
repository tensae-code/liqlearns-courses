# Development

## Do it first

Choose one way life in Bahrain should be better in ten years, and write what would have to grow to make it so. Keep your answer; the lesson names that growth development

## What the book teaches

Development is more than building: it is the growth of a whole society's life — its people's learning, health, work and welfare rising together. The book teaches it after work because work is its engine.

## The lesson in full

Among the lesson's objectives the book names the sustainable development goals: development that lasts, meeting this generation's needs without stealing the next generation's share of land, sea and resources.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: development is the homeland's growth in people's lives — in learning, health, work and welfare — pursued so it lasts, including the sustainable development goals the book names among its objectives. Give one real example from your own life in Bahrain, and name the lesson's key words: development, sustainable, goals, growth. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Bahrain's own books teach civics by building the citizen from his own society outward — society and the modern state first, then living together, then the citizen facing the world and guarding his homeland. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
