# Reform and the Democratic March

## Do it first

Write down one change in Bahrain's public life that you have heard came from the King's reform project. Keep it; the dossier traces how far the echo reached

## What the book teaches

This part is a study dossier (ملف), not an ordinary lesson: it gathers how the reform, modernisation and democratic-march project echoed through Bahrain — in its institutions, its public life and its people.

## The lesson in full

The pupil studies the echo itself: a reform announced at the top becomes real only as it resounds through the society, and the dossier asks the citizen to hear where it reached and what it changed.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: King Hamad's reform and modernisation project, and the democratic march that followed it, resounded through the whole Kingdom — this study dossier traces that echo. Give one real example from your own life in Bahrain, and name the lesson's key words: reform, modernisation, democracy, dossier. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Bahrain's own books teach civics by building the citizen from his own society outward — society and the modern state first, then living together, then the citizen facing the world and guarding his homeland. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
