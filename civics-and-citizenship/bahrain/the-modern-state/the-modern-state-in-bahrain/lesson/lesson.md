# The Modern State in Bahrain

## Do it first

Name three institutions of the state you or your family have dealt with in Bahrain — a school, a ministry office, a court, a municipality. Keep your list; the lesson builds the state from them

## What the book teaches

The modern state is the form Bahrain takes as a country: a state standing on institutions rather than on persons, ruling by law, and providing the services and order a whole society needs.

## The lesson in full

The lesson shows how that modern state arose in Bahrain and what it consists of, so the pupil meets the state not as a distant power but as the organised form of his own society's life.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Bahrain is a modern state — a state with institutions, laws and a government that serves the whole country, and the pupil can say how that state arose and what it consists of. Give one real example from your own life in Bahrain, and name the lesson's key words: state, institutions, government, modern. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Bahrain's own books teach civics by building the citizen from his own society outward — society and the modern state first, then living together, then the citizen facing the world and guarding his homeland. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
