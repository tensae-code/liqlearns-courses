# Keeping Our Heritage

## Do it first

Name two pieces of heritage near you in Bahrain — a place, a craft, a custom — and one danger each faces. Keep your list; the lesson lays the responsibility on the citizen

## What the book teaches

Heritage is what a people inherits: its places, crafts, stories and ways. The book teaches preserving it as a responsibility, because heritage is part of identity — lose it, and the citizen loses a piece of who he is.

## The lesson in full

Heritage is also part of the environment the citizen lives in, so guarding it is daily work, not ceremony: respect it, learn it, hand it on whole.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: preserving heritage is a responsibility — heritage is part of the citizen's identity and of the environment he lives in, and each generation must guard what it inherited for the next. Give one real example from your own life in Bahrain, and name the lesson's key words: heritage, responsibility, identity, preserve. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Bahrain's own books teach civics by building the citizen from his own society outward — society and the modern state first, then living together, then the citizen facing the world and guarding his homeland. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
