# Civics and Citizenship — North Macedonia course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for North Macedonia inside the Civics and
Citizenship folder, organised by **levels, not grades**: a
learner climbs when they have proved the level, not when a
school calendar says so. The school grades the books come
from appear only in each lesson's sources (provenance),
never as the course's organisation. The path follows North
Macedonia's own order as its books lay it out, from the
filing's ladder sketch (`north-macedonia-placements.md` —
2 records / 2 distinct books: the Grade 9 Civic Education
student book and the gymnasium Ethics student book, both
Macedonian, both student books): the Grade 9 course moves
from the pupil's own civic identity, through cooperation,
difference and conflict in civil society, to the citizen
facing the state, and ends with the citizen and Europe; the
gymnasium Ethics course then climbs from what ethics and
morality are, through the building elements of ethics,
ethics in the religions and the modern teachings, out to
bioethics and social, economic and political ethics. The
filing stocks 66 placements into 66 lessons: the coined
lesson names were deduped before building — every coined
name was already distinct, no two placements feed the same
lesson — and every placement stocks into exactly one
lesson.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this
country's own Civic Education and Ethics books — except
the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people
making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the
books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book list and the
acceptance reckoning. **All 66 lessons stand on student
books; no lesson in this course stands on a teacher guide
or a programme.**

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **Civic Identity** — The Grade 9 Civic Education book opens with the pupil's own civic identity: developing my own traits, ethics, values and humanity as the citizen's inner compass, and professional and career development as part of a citizen's life plan. 3 lessons, in 1 title(s): `civic-identity/` (3 lessons).
2. **Living With Others in Civil Society** — Living with others in civil society: accepting and respecting differences, attitudes and viewpoints, conflicts and dealing with them, interculturalism; the citizen's social responsibility and the civic organisations and advocacy groups citizens act through; then culture and cultural identity, the role of religion in civil society, and the influence of social media on citizens. 9 lessons, in 3 title(s): `cooperation-in-civil-society/` (4 lessons); `the-citizen-and-civil-society/` (2 lessons); `culture-religion-and-media/` (3 lessons).
3. **The Citizen and the State** — The citizen facing the state: justice and the rule of law, the legal state (a state governed by law), elections and democratic processes in society, and state and public administration in the function of citizens. 4 lessons, in 1 title(s): `the-citizen-and-the-state/` (4 lessons).
4. **The Citizen and Europe** — The Grade 9 course closes outward, distinctively, with the citizen and Europe: the European Union — what it is and how it works — and the country's path into it. 2 lessons, in 1 title(s): `the-citizen-and-europe/` (2 lessons).
5. **What Ethics Is, the Value of Life and Its Building Elements** — The gymnasium Ethics course (Temkov) begins: what ethics is, what morality is and how it differs from ethics, ethics as the science of life's values, ethics in the contemporary era and ethical education; life and its value, the teachings about the goals of life, life as the highest value and a positive stance toward life; then the building elements of ethics — good and evil, autonomy and heteronomy, will, character, intention and deed, choice of values, the moral norm, ethical evaluation, conscience, reason in ethical reckoning, and building an autonomous personality. 20 lessons, in 3 title(s): `introduction-to-ethics/` (5 lessons); `the-value-of-life/` (4 lessons); `building-elements-of-ethics/` (11 lessons).
6. **Ethics in Religions, Modern Teachings and Today's Priorities** — Ethics widened: religion and ethics, the Ten Commandments, Christian ethics, the ethics of Islam and the ethics of the other religions; the contemporary teachings — utilitarianism, consequentialism and the ethics of care; and today's ethical priorities — the most important contemporary values, the essential positive values, responsibility and duty, and negative moral phenomena. 12 lessons, in 3 title(s): `ethics-and-religions/` (5 lessons); `contemporary-ethical-teachings/` (3 lessons); `ethical-priorities/` (4 lessons).
7. **Ethics Applied — Bioethics, Society, Work and Politics** — Ethics applied at the top of the ladder: bioethics as a new leading discipline, ecological ethics, the ethics of health, addictions and drugs; I and the others, ethics in the family, universal ethics, avoiding and resolving conflicts, and etiquette; work ethics and business ethics; and finally ethics and democracy, peace and human rights, closing with the course's own closing skill — how to write an ethical essay. 16 lessons, in 4 title(s): `bioethics/` (5 lessons); `social-ethics/` (5 lessons); `economic-ethics/` (2 lessons); `political-ethics/` (4 lessons).

**Totals: 66 lessons** (Level 1: 3 · Level 2: 9 · Level 3: 4 · Level 4: 2 · Level 5: 20 · Level 6: 12 · Level 7: 16 — from two read student books: *ГРАЃАНСКО ОБРАЗОВАНИЕ за 9 одделение / Civic Education, Grade 9* (Ministry of Education and Science of the Republic of North Macedonia, 2022, ISBN 978-608-273-028-8, Macedonian, COMPLETE) and *ЕТИКА за III година / Ethics for 3rd year, gymnasium education* (Просветно дело / Prosvetno Delo, Skopje, 2004, ISBN 9989-0-0401-3, Macedonian, COMPLETE by contents-and-samples image read); 66 placements stocking 66 lessons).
