# Why Cooperation Helps

## Do it first

Try to carry something heavy or awkward alone — then carry it with a partner. Say what changed: the weight, the speed, the feeling.

## Working together on purpose

Cooperation is not just being near other people; it is joining your effort with theirs on the same job. A wall one child cannot move, five children move together. A class project shared is finished sooner and better.

The lesson names the usefulness plainly: cooperating people share strength, share ideas, and finish with something no one of them could have made alone — and they end the job closer than they began it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: cooperation — working together on purpose — gets more done, better, than people working alone, and it makes the group stronger while it works. Give one real example from your own life in Albania, and name the lesson's key words: cooperation, together, group, shared work. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Albania's own book teaches civics by doing — family decisions, class rules, a mock campaign, a kiosk game. When a lesson asks you to practise something, really practise it with real people, even in a small way: the practising is the teaching.
