# Group Members and the Leader

## Do it first

Name one group you belong to. Name its leader and two members, and say the part each one plays — including your own part.

## Members, roles and the leader

A group is not a crowd. Its members each have a role — a part they are counted on to play — and together the roles make the group work. When a member does not play their part, the whole group feels the gap.

The leader is the member who guides: organising, deciding when the group must choose, and keeping everyone moving toward the group's purpose. The lesson teaches the leader as the group's guide and helper, chosen for the work — not a boss to fear.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: every group has members with parts to play and a leader who guides them — and a leader's job is to serve the group's purpose, not to rule over it. Give one real example from your own life in Albania, and name the lesson's key words: group, member, leader, role. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Albania's own book teaches civics by doing — family decisions, class rules, a mock campaign, a kiosk game. When a lesson asks you to practise something, really practise it with real people, even in a small way: the practising is the teaching.
