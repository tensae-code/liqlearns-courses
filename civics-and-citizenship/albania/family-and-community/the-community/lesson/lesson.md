# The Community

## Do it first

Draw a circle for your family and a bigger circle around it for your community. Write in the big circle five people or places that belong to it.

## The circle wider than family

Step outside your family's door and you are in the community: the neighbours on your street, the people at your school, the shopkeepers, the elders, the other children. A community is the people who share a place and a life together.

Belonging to a community means you are known there and you have a part there. The lesson asks you to find your own community: who is in it, where it meets, and what its people share.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: a community is the wider group of people who live and share life around you — neighbours, school, local people — and the child belongs to it too. Give one real example from your own life in Albania, and name the lesson's key words: community, neighbours, belonging, place. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Albania's own book teaches civics by doing — family decisions, class rules, a mock campaign, a kiosk game. When a lesson asks you to practise something, really practise it with real people, even in a small way: the practising is the teaching.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: a community is the wider group of people who live and share life around you — neighbours, school, local people — and the child belongs to it too.
3. Teach this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
