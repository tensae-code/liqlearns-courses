# Am I an Entrepreneur?

## Do it first

Name one need in your neighbourhood that nobody serves well. Say what an entrepreneur would start there, and one sign in yourself that you could do such a thing.

## Could it be you?

Enterprise begins with seeing: a thing people need that nobody is providing well — a service, a product, a small business. The entrepreneur is the one who does not only see it, but starts: organising, risking a little, serving real customers.

The lesson turns the question on the learner: Am I an entrepreneur? It looks for the signs in the child — noticing needs, enjoying making and organising, not fearing a first try — and treats enterprise as something learnable, not a gift for a lucky few.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: an entrepreneur is a person who sees a need, gathers what is needed, and starts something that serves people — and the chapter asks the child directly: could that person be you?. Give one real example from your own life in Albania, and name the lesson's key words: entrepreneur, enterprise, need, business. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Albania's own book teaches civics by doing — family decisions, class rules, a mock campaign, a kiosk game. When a lesson asks you to practise something, really practise it with real people, even in a small way: the practising is the teaching.
