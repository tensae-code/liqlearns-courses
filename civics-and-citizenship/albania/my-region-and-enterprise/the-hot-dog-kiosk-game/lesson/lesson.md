# The Hot-Dog Kiosk Game

## Do it first

Plan a kiosk on paper: list what you must buy to sell twenty hot dogs, and guess what each thing costs. Keep the list — the game will test it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Setting up the kiosk — buying what you need

The game opens a kiosk that sells hot dogs, and the first work is buying: bread, sausages, sauces — enough to sell, not so much that it spoils. Every purchase is a decision made with limited money, exactly as real sellers decide.

## Sub lesson 2 — Selling and serving customers

Then the kiosk opens. Customers come with wants and with money; you serve them well, take payment, give change, and keep the kiosk clean and quick. At the end you count: what came in, what went out, what is left — the game's honest score.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: running a kiosk — even in a game — teaches enterprise whole: you buy what you need, you sell to real customers, and the money left over tells you how you did. Give one real example from your own life in Albania, and name the lesson's key words: kiosk, buying, selling, customers. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Albania's own book teaches civics by doing — family decisions, class rules, a mock campaign, a kiosk game. When a lesson asks you to practise something, really practise it with real people, even in a small way: the practising is the teaching.
