# Election Campaign and Voting

## Do it first

Imagine your class must choose a representative. Say what a fair campaign would need: time to speak, promises stated, questions allowed. Then say what the vote itself needs to be fair.

## Campaign, then the vote

Before anyone votes, there is a campaign: candidates say who they are, what they promise, and why they should be chosen. Voters listen, compare and question — the campaign is the choosing made public.

Then comes voting: each voter marks their choice, the votes are counted, and the result stands as the people's decision. The book prepares this as a class activity, because a campaign and a vote are learned by doing them.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: an election campaign is how candidates present themselves and their plans so voters can choose — and voting is the act that turns the choice into a decision. Give one real example from your own life in Albania, and name the lesson's key words: election, campaign, voting, candidate. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Albania's own book teaches civics by doing — family decisions, class rules, a mock campaign, a kiosk game. When a lesson asks you to practise something, really practise it with real people, even in a small way: the practising is the teaching.
