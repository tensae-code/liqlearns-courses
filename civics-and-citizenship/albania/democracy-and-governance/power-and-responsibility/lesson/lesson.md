# Power and Responsibility

## Do it first

Name someone with a little power over your day — a class leader, an older sibling, a team captain. Say one decision they made and the answer they owed for it.

## Power owes an answer

Power means your decisions reach other people: a leader's choice changes the group's day. The lesson teaches that this reach is exactly why power must carry responsibility — an answer owed for what the power does.

Power without responsibility is the danger the chapter warns against at the child's scale: the class leader who favours friends, the older child who orders and never explains. Where power answers for itself, people can trust it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: power and responsibility belong together — power is the ability to decide and act for others, and it is only right when it carries its responsibility with it. Give one real example from your own life in Albania, and name the lesson's key words: power, responsibility, leadership, trust. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Albania's own book teaches civics by doing — family decisions, class rules, a mock campaign, a kiosk game. When a lesson asks you to practise something, really practise it with real people, even in a small way: the practising is the teaching.
