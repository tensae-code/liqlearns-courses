# The Community and Governance

## Do it first

Name three shared things in your place that somebody must decide about — a road, water, a playing field. For each, say who decides, if you know; mark the ones you must find out.

## Who governs your place?

Governance means the steering of a community: the decisions about shared things — roads, water, safety, schools — and the people and bodies who make and carry out those decisions.

The lesson asks the child to look at their own place with new eyes: which decisions here are governed, and by whom? A community that understands its own governance can take part in it; one that never looks cannot.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: governance is how a community is organised and led — who decides, by what right, and for whose good — and every community, from class to country, has some form of it. Give one real example from your own life in Albania, and name the lesson's key words: governance, community, decisions, leadership. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Albania's own book teaches civics by doing — family decisions, class rules, a mock campaign, a kiosk game. When a lesson asks you to practise something, really practise it with real people, even in a small way: the practising is the teaching.
