# Democracy Begins in the Family

## Do it first

Name one decision your family made together recently. Who spoke? Who listened? Was the decision shared fairly? Say it honestly.

## The first democracy is at home

Democracy is usually pictured in parliaments, but the book starts it at the kitchen table: a family where children are heard, where rules are fair, where decisions are explained — that family is teaching democracy every day.

The habits are the same ones a country needs: speaking and being listened to, accepting a shared decision you did not choose, treating each member as counting for one. Practised at home, they are ready when the citizen meets them in public.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: democracy begins in the family — where a child first gets a voice, is treated fairly, and shares in decisions, the habits of democracy are learned before any election. Give one real example from your own life in Albania, and name the lesson's key words: democracy, family, voice, fairness. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Albania's own book teaches civics by doing — family decisions, class rules, a mock campaign, a kiosk game. When a lesson asks you to practise something, really practise it with real people, even in a small way: the practising is the teaching.
