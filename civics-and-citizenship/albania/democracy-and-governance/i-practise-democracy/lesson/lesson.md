# I Practise Democracy

## Do it first

List the democratic acts you have already done this chapter — speaking in a family decision, keeping a class rule, voting, leading with an account. Mark the one you have not yet done.

## Doing democracy

The governance chapter closes by handing the whole subject to the learner: I practise democracy. Every piece of the chapter becomes an act — voice in the family, rights and rules in class, a campaign, a vote, power with responsibility.

Practising is how the habits set. A child who has run a fair class vote and accepted a result they disliked has learned more democracy than one who only read its definition.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: democracy is something you practise, not only something you study — the child exercises it in class and community: speaking, listening, voting, accepting decisions, answering for power. Give one real example from your own life in Albania, and name the lesson's key words: democracy, practice, voting, participation. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Albania's own book teaches civics by doing — family decisions, class rules, a mock campaign, a kiosk game. When a lesson asks you to practise something, really practise it with real people, even in a small way: the practising is the teaching.
