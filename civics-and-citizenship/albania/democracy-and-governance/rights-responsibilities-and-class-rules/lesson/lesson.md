# Rights, Responsibilities and Class Rules

## Do it first

Name one right you have in class and the responsibility that stands beside it. Then name one class rule and the right it protects.

## The classroom's first law

A right is what you may claim: to learn, to speak, to be safe, to be treated fairly. A responsibility is what the same world asks back: to let others learn, speak, be safe and be treated fairly too.

Class rules are where this becomes real. Agreed and kept by the class, they are the child's first law — not the teacher's weapon, but the class's own protection of everyone's rights, applying the same to all.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: in the classroom — the child's first public world — rights come with responsibilities, and class rules are the first law: they protect everyone's rights equally. Give one real example from your own life in Albania, and name the lesson's key words: rights, responsibilities, rules, class. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Albania's own book teaches civics by doing — family decisions, class rules, a mock campaign, a kiosk game. When a lesson asks you to practise something, really practise it with real people, even in a small way: the practising is the teaching.
