# Elections and Representation

## Do it first

Name one representative chosen by people you know — in school, community or country. Say whose voice they carry, and how those people could judge them.

## From votes to a voice

A community is too big for everyone to sit in every decision. So voters choose representatives: people who sit in the deciding bodies in the voters' place, carrying their interests and views.

Representation is the bridge the election builds. It holds only while the representative truly carries the voters' voice — and the next election is where the voters judge whether they did.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: elections are how the voters' choice becomes representation — the winners carry the people's voice into the bodies that decide, and answer for it. Give one real example from your own life in Albania, and name the lesson's key words: elections, representation, voters, representative. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Albania's own book teaches civics by doing — family decisions, class rules, a mock campaign, a kiosk game. When a lesson asks you to practise something, really practise it with real people, even in a small way: the practising is the teaching.
