# Civics and Citizenship — Albania course — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

Civics belongs to the country: this course is Albania's own —
built from Albania's Qytetaria (Civics) teacher guide for
Grade 5, *Libër Mësuesi Qytetaria 5* (Mediaprint, Albanian,
2019 first edition). Inside the course the organisation is
by **levels, not grades**: the titles below are the guide's
own chapters, and the lessons inside them climb the five
levels in path order — what the learner proves, step by
step. The grade the book comes from lives only in each
lesson's sources.

The path's own logic is the book's: its three-theme
programme — individuals, groups and society; power,
authority and governance; production, consumption and
distribution — taught through the child's own family, class
and community first (democracy literally "begins in the
family" and in class rules), then local self-government and
a mock election campaign, road citizenship, and finally the
home region and young entrepreneurship. The road chapter is
taught inside the governance theme in the pupil book's
order, as the placements file's ladder sketch lays it out.
That ladder IS the path order (recommended, never forced — a
learner can jump in anywhere; the "start here instead" hints
point down this ladder). Every lesson stands on the teacher
guide alone — marked TEACHER, acceptance 0; the pupil book
*Qytetari 5* is not in hand (see ABOUT.md).

## The titles

- `family-and-community/` — Level 1. The child's nearest
  circles, from the guide's community chapter (Kreu 1,
  lessons 1.1–1.6): the family as the first social group,
  its functions, the community, diversity and heritage,
  cooperation, groups and leaders. It sits first because the
  book itself starts there — belonging is learned at home
  before it is practised anywhere else.
- `taking-part/` — Level 2. The rest of the community
  chapter (lessons 1.7–1.12): belonging turned into action —
  active membership, self-restraint and compromise, conflict
  resolution, volunteering, community and school, and the
  dream of making a change. It sits second because the
  learner must know the community (Level 1) before the book
  asks them to act in it.
- `democracy-and-governance/` — Level 3. The governance
  chapter (Kreu 2, lessons 2.1–2.8): democracy starting in
  the family and in class rules, through community
  governance and local self-government, to campaigns,
  elections, representation and responsible power, closing
  with practising democracy. It sits third because the
  book's second theme — power, authority and governance —
  builds on the citizen formed in Levels 1–2.
- `road-citizenship/` — Level 4. The road chapter (Kreu 3,
  lessons 3.1–3.6): the citizen in public space — where I
  live, the road to school, transport, road conflicts,
  pedestrian and passenger duties, risk and signs. It sits
  fourth in the pupil book's own order, taught inside the
  governance theme: the governed community met on its
  streets.
- `my-region-and-enterprise/` — Level 5. The region chapter
  (Kreu 4): production and enterprise in the home region —
  the entrepreneur, regional resources, the kiosk game,
  problem-solving, and my region in the world. It sits last
  because the book's third theme — production, consumption
  and distribution — is the widest circle, ending where the
  whole course's widening circles end: the region standing
  in the world.
