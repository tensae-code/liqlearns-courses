# Conflicts on the Road

## Do it first

Name a road conflict you have seen or can picture — a quarrel at a crossing, a blocked path. Say how it ended, or how it should have ended by the lesson.

## Trouble on the road

The road squeezes strangers together at speed: the blocked pavement, the disputed crossing, the near miss. Tempers rise fastest exactly where calm matters most.

The lesson carries the community's conflict teaching onto the road: stop, stay calm, let the rules and courtesy decide, and give way where giving way prevents harm. No journey is worth an injury — and no traveller wins a fight with the road.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: conflicts happen on the road too — between travellers in a hurry and in each other's way — and they are resolved without harm: by calm, by rules, and by giving way where giving way is right. Give one real example from your own life in Albania, and name the lesson's key words: conflict, road, calm, safety. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Albania's own book teaches civics by doing — family decisions, class rules, a mock campaign, a kiosk game. When a lesson asks you to practise something, really practise it with real people, even in a small way: the practising is the teaching.
