# Risk and Information on the Road

## Do it first

Name three road signs or signals you pass on your way to school. Say what information each one gives, and the risk it guards against.

## Reading the road

Risk is danger before it happens: the fast corner, the hidden crossing, the place where vehicles turn. The citizen on the road learns to see risk early, while there is still time to choose.

Information is the road's own language: signs, signals, markings and lights that warn, direct and protect. Reading them is a skill like reading words — the chapter closes by making the child a reader of the road itself.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the road speaks through signs and information — reading them, and judging risk before it reaches you, is how a citizen stays safe in public space. Give one real example from your own life in Albania, and name the lesson's key words: risk, information, signs, safety. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Albania's own book teaches civics by doing — family decisions, class rules, a mock campaign, a kiosk game. When a lesson asks you to practise something, really practise it with real people, even in a small way: the practising is the teaching.
