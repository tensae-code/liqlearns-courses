# Where I Live

## Do it first

Draw your street or the streets around your home from memory. Mark your home, your school's direction, and the places where people must take care.

## Your place, known well

The road chapter opens at home: the place where you live. Its streets and pavements, its crossings and corners, its shared spaces — this is the ground where the child is first a citizen in public.

Knowing your place is the start: which streets lead where, where it is safe to cross, which spaces are shared and what rules keep them usable for everyone. The citizen of the road begins as a knower of their own ground.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the place where you live — its streets, spaces and shared rules — is your first public world, and a citizen knows it, uses it well and cares for it. Give one real example from your own life in Albania, and name the lesson's key words: place, streets, public space, rules. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Albania's own book teaches civics by doing — family decisions, class rules, a mock campaign, a kiosk game. When a lesson asks you to practise something, really practise it with real people, even in a small way: the practising is the teaching.
