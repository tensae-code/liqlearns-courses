# Pedestrian and Passenger

## Do it first

Say which role you were in last: pedestrian or passenger. Name one right you held in that role and one duty. Then do the same for the other role.

## Two roles, two sets of duties

On foot, you are a pedestrian: the most exposed traveller, with the right to safe passage — and the duty to cross with care, use the crossings, and keep yourself visible and predictable to others.

Riding, you are a passenger: with the right to a safe journey — and duties of your own: sit and behave so the driver can drive, respect the vehicle and the other passengers, and board and leave with care. The lesson makes the child master of both roles, because every traveller is both.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: as a pedestrian and as a passenger you hold two roles on the road, and each role has its own rights and its own duties. Give one real example from your own life in Albania, and name the lesson's key words: pedestrian, passenger, rights, duties. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Albania's own book teaches civics by doing — family decisions, class rules, a mock campaign, a kiosk game. When a lesson asks you to practise something, really practise it with real people, even in a small way: the practising is the teaching.
