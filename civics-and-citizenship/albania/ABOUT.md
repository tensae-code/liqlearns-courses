# About the Civics and Citizenship — Albania course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Qytetaria (Civics) book for Grade 5, in the form in hand:
its teacher guide, *Libër Mësuesi Qytetaria 5* (Mediaprint,
2019). The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box
is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the
lessons are the book's. **Guides-only footing, stated plainly
on this first page:** the whole course rests on that one
teacher guide alone — the pupil book it serves, *Qytetari 5*,
is not in hand — so every lesson stands as teacher-guide
evidence, acceptance 0, labelled as such in every lesson's
sources.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Albania inside the Civics and
Citizenship folder (one course per country). Inside it, the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place
is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the
ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced
gate. The school grade the book comes from (Grade 5) appears
only in each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

The course has **five stocked levels**, following the
guide's own order — its three-theme programme (individuals,
groups and society · power, authority and governance ·
production, consumption and distribution), with the road
chapter taught inside the governance theme in the pupil
book's order:

1. **Level 1 — Family and Community: the Child's Nearest
   Circles** (6 lessons, in the title
   `family-and-community/`). The family as the most
   important social group, what the family does, the
   community, cultural diversity and heritage, why
   cooperation helps, and group members and the leader.
2. **Level 2 — Taking Part in the Community** (6 lessons, in
   the title `taking-part/`). Being an active group member,
   self-restraint and compromise as marks of citizenship,
   solving conflicts in the community, voluntary work, the
   community and education, and the child's own dream of
   making the change — closing the guide's community chapter
   (Kreu 1).
3. **Level 3 — Democracy and Governance** (8 lessons, in the
   title `democracy-and-governance/`). Democracy begins in
   the family; rights, responsibilities and class rules;
   the community and governance; local self-government; the
   election campaign and voting, prepared as a class
   activity; elections and representation; power and
   responsibility; and "I practise democracy" — closing the
   governance chapter (Kreu 2).
4. **Level 4 — Road Citizenship: the Citizen in Public
   Space** (6 lessons, in the title `road-citizenship/`).
   Where I live, on the road to school, means of transport,
   conflicts on the road, myself as pedestrian and as
   passenger, and risk and information on the road (Kreu 3).
5. **Level 5 — My Region and Enterprise** (5 lessons, in the
   title `my-region-and-enterprise/`). "Am I an
   entrepreneur?", the home region's resources as the means
   for enterprise, the hot-dog kiosk game (a two-lesson
   simulation, built as one lesson with two sub lessons),
   entrepreneurs solve problems (a two-lesson project, built
   as one lesson with two sub lessons), and my region in the
   world — closing the book (Kreu 4).

**Albania's own way:** Grade 5 civics runs in three themes —
individuals, groups and society; power, authority and
governance; production, consumption and distribution —
taught through the child's own family, class and community
first (democracy literally "begins in the family" and in
class rules), then local self-government and a mock election
campaign, road citizenship, and finally the home region and
young entrepreneurship.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
book's own shape, walked in order):

- *From the family to the world:* "Start with the family —
  the first social group — and the community around it. Then
  take part: be active, compromise, solve conflicts,
  volunteer, and dream your change. Then democracy:
  beginning at home, through class rules, local
  self-government, a campaign and a vote, to power that
  answers for itself. Then the road — your place, your
  journey, your two roles as pedestrian and passenger. Last,
  your region: its resources, a kiosk game, problems solved,
  and your region standing in the world."
