# Solving Conflicts in the Community

## Do it first

Name a small conflict you have seen — at school, in the street, at home. Say what each side wanted. Then say a solution both sides could accept.

## Settling it without harm

Where people live together, conflicts come: over space, over noise, over turns, over words. The lesson teaches that a conflict is a problem to solve together, not a battle to win.

The way is the one the book practises: each side says what happened and what it needs, each side listens, and together they look for the solution both can accept. Solved this way, a conflict can leave neighbours closer than before.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: conflicts in a community are solved by talking, listening and finding a way both sides can accept — peacefully, without harm and without winners who crush losers. Give one real example from your own life in Albania, and name the lesson's key words: conflict, solution, listening, peace. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Albania's own book teaches civics by doing — family decisions, class rules, a mock campaign, a kiosk game. When a lesson asks you to practise something, really practise it with real people, even in a small way: the practising is the teaching.
