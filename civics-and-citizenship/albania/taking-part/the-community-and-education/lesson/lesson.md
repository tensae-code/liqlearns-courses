# The Community and Education

## Do it first

Name three ways your community supports your school — people, places, things. Then name one way your school gives back to the community.

## The school belongs to the community

A school does not stand alone. The community around it provides the children, the teachers, the building, the care — and expects the school to give back educated, capable young citizens.

The connection runs both ways, and the lesson makes the child part of it: how you learn and behave at school is the community's business, and the community's support — families valuing learning, elders encouraging pupils — is the school's strength.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the community and education hold each other up: the community builds and supports the school, and the school educates the community's children into its future citizens. Give one real example from your own life in Albania, and name the lesson's key words: community, education, school, support. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Albania's own book teaches civics by doing — family decisions, class rules, a mock campaign, a kiosk game. When a lesson asks you to practise something, really practise it with real people, even in a small way: the practising is the teaching.
