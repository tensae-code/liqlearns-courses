# Voluntary Work

## Do it first

Name one thing in your community that needs doing and nobody is paid to do. Say whether you could volunteer for part of it, and what part.

## Work you give freely

A volunteer is not hired and not forced: they see something the community needs — a clean park, help for an elder, a hand at an event — and they give their work freely.

The lesson counts what voluntary work gives back. The community gains the work itself. The volunteer gains practice, skills, and something money cannot buy: the knowledge that their place is better because they were in it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: voluntary work is work you give freely for the community — unpaid, chosen — and it gives back: a better place, new skills, and the trust of the people around you. Give one real example from your own life in Albania, and name the lesson's key words: voluntary work, volunteer, community, giving. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Albania's own book teaches civics by doing — family decisions, class rules, a mock campaign, a kiosk game. When a lesson asks you to practise something, really practise it with real people, even in a small way: the practising is the teaching.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: voluntary work is work you give freely for the community — unpaid, chosen — and it gives back: a better place, new skills, and the trust of the people around you.
3. Teach this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
