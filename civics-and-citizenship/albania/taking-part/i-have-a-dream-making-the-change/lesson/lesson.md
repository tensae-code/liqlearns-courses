# I Have a Dream — Making the Change

## Do it first

Finish this sentence out loud: 'In my community, I dream of changing…' Then name the very first step, small enough to start this week.

## Your dream for your place

The chapter on the community ends by turning to the learner: what would you change? A dream here is not a wish to escape — it is a picture of your own place made better, held clearly enough to work toward.

The lesson joins the dream to everything before it: you know your family, your community, its groups and leaders, its conflicts and its volunteers. Now name the change you want, and the first small step that is yours to take.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: a citizen can dream of a change their community needs and take the first steps toward it — the community chapter closes with the child's own dream of making a difference. Give one real example from your own life in Albania, and name the lesson's key words: dream, change, community, citizen. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Albania's own book teaches civics by doing — family decisions, class rules, a mock campaign, a kiosk game. When a lesson asks you to practise something, really practise it with real people, even in a small way: the practising is the teaching.
