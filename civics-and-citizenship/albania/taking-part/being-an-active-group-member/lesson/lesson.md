# Being an Active Group Member

## Do it first

Think of the last time your class or group did something together. Were you in the work or watching? Say one moment honestly, and what you could have done.

## Join in, don't stand by

There are two ways to belong to a group: on the list, or in the work. The active member is in the work — present, taking part, offering ideas, doing their share of the jobs the group needs.

The lesson is aimed at the bystander habit: watching others clean the street, organise the game, solve the problem. A community of bystanders stays weak; a community of active members can change itself.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: a citizen does not stand by and watch — being an active part of your group and community means joining in, speaking up and doing your share. Give one real example from your own life in Albania, and name the lesson's key words: active, participation, group, community. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Albania's own book teaches civics by doing — family decisions, class rules, a mock campaign, a kiosk game. When a lesson asks you to practise something, really practise it with real people, even in a small way: the practising is the teaching.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: a citizen does not stand by and watch — being an active part of your group and community means joining in, speaking up and doing your share.
3. Teach this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
