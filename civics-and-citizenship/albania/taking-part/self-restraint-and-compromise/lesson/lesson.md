# Self-Restraint and Compromise

## Do it first

Remember a disagreement where you wanted your own way completely. Say what a compromise could have looked like — what each side would give, and what each would keep.

## Holding back, meeting halfway

Self-restraint is strength used quietly: you want the last word, the biggest share, your own way — and you hold back, because the group matters too. The book names it a mark of citizenship, a sign that shows what kind of citizen you are.

Compromise is what self-restraint makes possible between people: each side gives a little, and the group reaches a decision everyone can live with. Without compromise, every disagreement becomes a fight somebody must lose completely.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: self-restraint — holding yourself back when you could push — and compromise — meeting in the middle — are marks of a citizen: the community works because its people can do both. Give one real example from your own life in Albania, and name the lesson's key words: self-restraint, compromise, citizenship, community. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
