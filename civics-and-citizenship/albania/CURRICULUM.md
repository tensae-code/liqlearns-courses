# Civics and Citizenship — Albania course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Albania inside the Civics and Citizenship
folder, organised by **levels, not grades**: a learner climbs
when they have proved the level, not when a school calendar
says so. The school grade the book comes from (Grade 5)
appears only in each lesson's sources (provenance), never as
the course's organisation. The path follows Albania's own
book's order — the guide's three-theme programme
(individuals, groups and society → power, authority and
governance → production, consumption and distribution):
the child's own family, class and community first, where
democracy literally "begins in the family" and in class
rules; then local self-government and a mock election
campaign; then road citizenship; and finally the home region
and young entrepreneurship, ending with the region in the
world. The road chapter is taught inside the governance
theme in the pupil book's order, exactly as the placements
file's ladder sketch lays it out.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Qytetaria (Civics) book for Grade 5, in the form in hand:
its teacher guide, *Libër Mësuesi Qytetaria 5* (Mediaprint,
2019) — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us,
the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are
the book's. **The whole course rests on that teacher guide
alone** — the pupil book it serves, *Qytetari 5*, is not in
hand — so every level stands as teacher-guide evidence,
acceptance 0. See ABOUT.md for the full book list and the
acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **Family and Community: the Child's Nearest Circles** —
   the family as the most important social group and what
   the family does; the community just wider than the
   family's door; cultural diversity and heritage respected;
   why cooperation helps; and group members and the leader
   (`family-and-community/`). 6 lessons. *(Teacher-guide
   evidence only — every lesson's sources say so.)*
2. **Taking Part in the Community** — being an active group
   member, not a bystander; self-restraint and compromise
   as marks of citizenship; solving conflicts in the
   community peacefully; voluntary work and what it gives
   back; the community and education holding each other up;
   and the child's own dream of making the change, closing
   the community chapter (`taking-part/`). 6 lessons.
3. **Democracy and Governance** — democracy begins in the
   family; rights, responsibilities and class rules as the
   child's first law; the community and governance; local
   self-government — Albania's local units and what they
   decide; the election campaign and voting, prepared as a
   class activity; elections and representation; power and
   responsibility belonging together; and "I practise
   democracy", closing the governance chapter
   (`democracy-and-governance/`). 8 lessons.
4. **Road Citizenship: the Citizen in Public Space** — the
   place where I live, its streets and shared rules; on the
   road to school; means of transport as the citizen's
   choices; conflicts on the road resolved without harm;
   myself as pedestrian and as passenger, two roles with
   their own rights and duties; and risk and information on
   the road — reading signs and judging danger
   (`road-citizenship/`). 6 lessons.
5. **My Region and Enterprise** — "Am I an entrepreneur?";
   the home region's resources as the means for enterprise;
   the hot-dog kiosk game (one lesson, two sub lessons:
   buying, then selling and serving); entrepreneurs solve
   problems (one lesson, two sub lessons: finding a real
   problem, then solving it); and my region in the world,
   closing the book (`my-region-and-enterprise/`).
   5 lessons.
