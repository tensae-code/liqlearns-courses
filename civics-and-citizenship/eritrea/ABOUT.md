# About the Civics and Citizenship — Eritrea course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Citizenship Education books. The one exception is the
**Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Eritrea inside the Civics and
Citizenship folder (one course per country). Inside it, the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place
is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the
ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced
gate. The school grades the books come from appear only in
each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

Eritrea's own way, preserved whole, as filed: the same
nine-unit spiral repeats in the Grade 6 and Grade 8 books —
character first, then school, culture, the Revolution and
the birth of the state, patriotism, symbols, rules/rights,
and finally the good/active citizen; teaching is through
stories, dialogues, poems, proverbs and group activities.
The Grade 8 pass deepens the Grade 6 spiral — self-control
and honesty replace general responsibility, world
revolutions set up the Eritrean one, and the course ends on
right/wrong consequences and the active citizen.

The course as built has **four stocked levels**, from the
filing for this build (`eritrea-placements.md` — 2 records /
2 distinct books, both grabbed via grab.py tree, byte size
verified against record, cover pages rendered and checked,
full text read page by page to the last teaching page; 60
placements in all, stocking 58 lessons — the coined lesson
names were deduped before building: two lessons are stocked
by two placements each, the Grade 6 and Grade 8 passes of
the same teaching — the national principles
(`the-state-of-eritrea/national-principles`) and the national
holidays (`national-symbols-and-holidays/national-holidays`)
— and every placement stocks into exactly one lesson):

1. **Level 1 — The Responsible Young Person and the Good
   Student (17 lessons).** Who am I, responsible behaviour,
   responsibility in a family, friendship; self-control,
   emotions, honesty; liking school, listening to teachers,
   reading well, why education matters and its power, respect
   and studying at school.
2. **Level 2 — Our Society and Culture (8 lessons).** Family
   and culture, doing good for others, the diversity of
   Eritrean society, religious harmony; cultural unity, first
   impressions, cuisine and etiquette, Eritrean literature.
3. **Level 3 — The National Story — Revolution, State,
   Patriotism and Symbols (23 lessons).** Honouring the
   revolution, its heroes and independence; oppression and
   revolutions around the world; liberation, the referendum,
   the provisional government, the state's national
   principles and its government; loving your country, young
   patriots, defending the country, our models; the national
   symbols, the flag and the national holidays.
4. **Level 4 — Rules, Rights and the Good, Active Citizen
   (10 lessons).** The rights and duties of Eritrean
   citizens; rules, rights paired with responsibilities,
   doing the right thing, wrongful acts; the qualities of a
   good citizen, respecting parents, learning to be good,
   and why active citizens are needed.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later;
the books' own shape, walked in order):

- *Eritrea's own order:* "First, the young person: who am I,
  how I behave responsibly at home, my duties in my family,
  my friends — and, deeper, self-control, my emotions, and
  honesty as the best policy. Then the student: loving your
  school, listening to your teachers, reading until you
  blossom, and the power of education to change a life and a
  country. Then the circle widens: your family and your
  culture, doing good for others, Eritrea's many peoples and
  religions living in harmony, one culture that unites. Then
  the national story: the revolution and its heroes,
  liberation, the referendum that chose independence, the
  state's principles and its government, loving and
  defending your country, and the symbols and holidays that
  carry it. And last, the citizen at work: rules, rights
  answered by duties, doing the right thing and the cost of
  wrongful acts — and the good, active citizen the country
  needs."

## Honesty notes (course level)

- **What the filings do and do not contain.** The filings
  contain 2 distinct read books (the Grade 6 and Grade 8
  Citizenship Education student books, in English), 60
  placements, and the books' own contents maps. The filings
  do not contain any other Eritrea book — no book for any
  other grade, no teacher guide, no programme, and no book
  in any language other than English. Nothing of that kind
  is in this course, and no lesson pretends to one. The
  ladder therefore covers exactly the two books' shared
  spiral, Grade 6 first and Grade 8 deepening it, as filed.
- **No PARTIAL, gap or wrong-file label is carried, because
  the filings carry none.** Both read books are filed
  COMPLETE, with cover/title pages verified against their
  record titles and byte sizes verified against their
  records, as filed. No placement in the filings carries a
  PARTIAL, gap or wrong-file label, so no lesson carries
  one.
- **One filed wording note, carried exactly as filed.**
  Book 2's section 9.1 is filed as "Learning to be Good",
  with the filing's own note that the printed heading in
  text is "Learning to be Kind". The lesson
  (`being-a-good-citizen/learning-to-be-good`) carries the
  filed section label in its sources, with that note in its
  teaching summary, exactly as filed.

  coined lesson names are each fed by two placements — the
  Grade 6 and Grade 8 passes of the same teaching:

  §7.2, printed p. 92; Book 2 §7.2, printed p. 100). Each is
