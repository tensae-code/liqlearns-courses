# Hands-on — Protection of the Law

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: Protection of the Law. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Papua New Guinea's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Criminal versus civil law, the rights engaged when police apprehend you (the Constitution's protection of the person, summarised by topic), apprehended/arrested/detained/charged distinguished, minor versus serious offences, steps when a wrong is done to you by a person, a company or the State, and a hypothetical human-rights case study of a police raid. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
