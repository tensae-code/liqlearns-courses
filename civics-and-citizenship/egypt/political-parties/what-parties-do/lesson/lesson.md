# What Parties Do

## Do it first

Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: what Parties Do. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Egypt's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

A party is known by its work. This lesson teaches the functions of political parties — what they actually do in the life of a state: gathering opinion, offering choices, forming the people who govern and the people who oppose.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The functions of political parties. Give one real example from your own life or from the Egypt you live in, and name the lesson's key words: what, parties. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: The functions of political parties.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
