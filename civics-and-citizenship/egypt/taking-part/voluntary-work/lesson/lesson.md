# Voluntary Work

## Do it first

Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: voluntary Work. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Egypt's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

Citizenship becomes real when it is done for others without pay. This lesson teaches voluntary work — what it means, the forms it takes, and why it is worth doing — and it teaches it the book's way: the pupils plan a real volunteer day of their own.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Voluntary work: its meaning, forms and worth — planned by pupils as a real volunteer day. Give one real example from your own life or from the Egypt you live in, and name the lesson's key words: voluntary, work. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: Voluntary work: its meaning, forms and worth — planned by pupils as a real volunteer day.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
