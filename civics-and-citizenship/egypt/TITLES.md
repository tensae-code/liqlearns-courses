# Civics and Citizenship — Egypt course — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

Civics belongs to the country: this course is Egypt's own —
built from Egypt's Citizenship and Human Rights book for the
Second Secondary year and its National Education book for
the Third Secondary year (Ministry of Education and
Technical Education, Arab Republic of Egypt, 2025–2026, in
Arabic). Inside the course the organisation is by **levels,
not grades**: the titles below ARE the levels' titles, in
path order — what the learner proves, step by step. The
grades the books come from live only in each lesson's
sources.

The path's own logic is Egypt's, from the filing's ladder
sketch, which is the books' own order. Level 1 is the
foundations, from the Grade 11 book: what citizenship is,
its duties, rights and dimensions, the citizen online, and
human rights from the old civilisations to Egypt's own
National Council. Citizenship is taught as a balance sheet —
duties and rights on facing pages — and then immediately
updated for the phone in the pupil's pocket: a full
digital-citizenship unit tied to the state's own
digital-transformation programme. Level 2 is citizenship
applied: the Egyptian woman's journey, era by era, and the
institutions that carry her claim, then voluntary work and
civil society — the Grade 11 book's chapters 3–4. Level 3 is
the machinery of the state, from the Grade 12 book: morals
first, then law, then the constitution — Egypt's 2014
constitution and its Constitutional Court. Level 4 is
taking part at the top of school: democracy's meaning and
history, parties in general and in Egypt, and the pupil's
own participation, ending in the electoral process and the
book's closing model. The final-year book is a single
argument — law and constitution first, then democracy, then
the parties, then the pupil's own participation — and every
general idea is immediately landed in Egypt. That ladder IS
the path order (recommended, never forced — a learner can
jump in higher; the "start here instead" hints point down
this ladder). All 55 lessons stand on student books.

## The titles

**Level 1 — citizenship, digital citizenship and human
rights** (the Grade 11 book, chapters 1–2 and its Digital
Citizenship section):

- `citizenship/` — Level 1. What Citizenship Is ·
  Citizenship and Civil Society · How Citizenship Is Gained
  · Duties and Rights of the Citizen · Dimensions of
  Citizenship · Institutions That Build Citizenship · Why
  Citizenship Must Grow. The course opens with citizenship
  itself, because the Grade 11 book opens there — the
  concept, the state it belongs to, and the balance of
  duties and rights everything later stands on.
- `digital-citizenship/` — Level 1. What Digital
  Citizenship Is · The Digital Citizen · Dimensions of
  Digital Citizenship · The State and Digital
  Transformation. Egypt's own update of citizenship for the
  phone in the pupil's pocket, tied to the state's Digital
  Egypt programme.
- `human-rights/` — Level 1. What Human Rights Are ·
  Rights Through History · Characteristics of Human Rights
  · Kinds of Rights · Rights in International Covenants ·
  Rights in Egyptian Society · NGOs and Human Rights. The
  rights side of the balance sheet, from the old
  civilisations to Egypt's own National Council for Human
  Rights.

**Level 2 — Egyptian women and taking part** (the Grade 11
book, chapters 3–4):

- `egyptian-women/` — Level 2. Women in Ancient Egypt ·
  Women in the Middle Ages · Women in the Modern Era · How
  Society Empowers Women · Leading Women · The National
  Council for Women · Women in International Conventions.
  One journey, era by era — "a journey of work and
  struggle", in the chapter's own subtitle — ending in the
  institutions and conventions that carry the claim.
- `taking-part/` — Level 2. Voluntary Work · Civil Society
  Organisations. Citizenship done, not just known: the
  pupil plans a real volunteer day and researches the
  organisations in their own area.

**Level 3 — law and the constitution** (the Grade 12 book,
chapter 1):

- `law-and-the-constitution/` — Level 3. Morals · Law ·
  Law, Morals and Religion · The Constitution · Egypt's
  2014 Constitution · The Constitutional Court · Why
  Constitution and Law Matter. The final-year book's first
  movement: conduct governed first by morals, then by law,
  then by the constitution above the law — landed at once
  in Egypt's own 2014 constitution and its court.

**Level 4 — democracy, parties and participation** (the
Grade 12 book, chapters 2–4):

- `democracy/` — Level 4. What Democracy Is · How
  Democracy Grew · Characteristics of Democracy · Why
  Democracy Matters · Democracy and Knowledge · Challenges
  to Democracy. Democracy defined against its neighbours,
  traced from Athens, and tested against its own obstacles.
- `political-parties/` — Level 4. What a Political Party
  Is · How Parties Arose · What Parties Do · Party
  Principles in Egypt · What Parties Aim For · Party
  Systems · Parties in Egypt. The channels between citizen
  and state — the general teaching landed, as always in
  this book, in Egypt's own party principles and history.
- `political-participation/` — Level 4. What Participation
  Is · Characteristics of Participation · The Role of
  Participation · Forms of Participation · Obstacles to
  Participation · Participation in Egyptian Society · The
  Electoral Process · A Model of Participation. The course
  ends where the book ends: with the pupil's own part,
  walked step by step through the electoral process and
  gathered into a model to follow.
