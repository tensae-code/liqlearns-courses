# Civics and Citizenship — Egypt course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Egypt inside the Civics and Citizenship
folder, organised by **levels, not grades**: a learner climbs
when they have proved the level, not when a school calendar
says so. The school grades the books come from appear only in
each lesson's sources (provenance), never as the course's
organisation. The path follows Egypt's own order as its books
lay it out — the Grade 11 book first: citizenship taught as a
balance sheet of duties and rights, then the citizen online,
then human rights from the old civilisations to Egypt's own
National Council; then citizenship applied — the Egyptian
woman's journey and the institutions that carry her claim,
and voluntary work and civil society; then the Grade 12 book:
morals, law and the constitution first, with Egypt's 2014
constitution and its Constitutional Court; and at the top
democracy, the parties, and the pupil's own participation,
ending in the electoral process and the book's closing model
of participation. The filing for this build
(`egypt-placements.md` — 3 records / 2 distinct books read;
55 placements in all, from two distinct student books read)
stocks 55 lessons: every placement names its own coined
lesson, the coined names were deduped before building (no
two placements shared a lesson), and every placement stocks
into exactly one lesson.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Citizenship and Human Rights and National Education
books — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us,
the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are
the books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book list and the
acceptance reckoning. **Every lesson in this course stands
on a student book; no lesson rests on a teacher guide or a
programme.** The third filed record — National Education,
Secondary 10 — is UNREAD (no copy could be obtained), so the
ladder honestly begins with the Grade 11 book's foundations,
as ABOUT.md states.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **Citizenship, Digital Citizenship and Human Rights** —
   the foundations, from the Grade 11 book: what citizenship
   is, its components, how it is gained, its duties and
   rights held together, its dimensions, the institutions
   that build it and why it must grow; then the citizen
   online — digital citizenship, the digital citizen, its
   dimensions, and the state's digital transformation; then
   human rights — what they are, their history, their
   characteristics and kinds, the international covenants,
   rights in Egyptian society, and NGOs. 18 lessons, in
   three titles: `citizenship/` (7 lessons),
   `digital-citizenship/` (4 lessons), `human-rights/`
   (7 lessons).
2. **Egyptian Women and Taking Part** — citizenship applied:
   the Egyptian woman's journey through the ancient,
   medieval and modern eras, how society empowers women, the
   pioneering models, the National Council for Women and
   women in the international conventions; then voluntary
   work and civil society organisations. 9 lessons, in two
   titles: `egyptian-women/` (7 lessons), `taking-part/`
   (2 lessons).
3. **Law and the Constitution** — the machinery of the
   state, from the Grade 12 book: morals before law, law and
   its branches, law with morals and religion, the
   constitution and its kinds, Egypt's 2014 constitution,
   the Supreme Constitutional Court, and why constitution
   and law matter. 7 lessons, in one title:
   `law-and-the-constitution/` (7 lessons).
4. **Democracy, Parties and Participation** — taking part
   at the top of school: democracy's meaning, history,
   characteristics and challenges, and its need for an
   informed citizenry; political parties — what they are,
   how they arose, what they do, their principles, goals
   and systems, and parties in Egypt; and political
   participation itself — its concept, characteristics,
   role, forms and obstacles, participation in Egyptian
   society, the electoral process step by step, and the
   book's closing model of participation. 21 lessons, in
   three titles: `democracy/` (6 lessons),
   `political-parties/` (7 lessons),
   `political-participation/` (8 lessons).

**Totals: 55 lessons** (Level 1: 18 · Level 2: 9 · Level 3:
7 · Level 4: 21 — from two read student books:
*Citizenship and Human Rights*, Second Secondary
(Ministry of Education and Technical Education, Arab
Republic of Egypt, 2025–2026, COMPLETE) and *National
Education — Political Participation in Egyptian Society*,
Third Secondary (Ministry of Education and Technical
Education, Arab Republic of Egypt, 2025–2026, COMPLETE);
55 placements stocking 55 lessons).
