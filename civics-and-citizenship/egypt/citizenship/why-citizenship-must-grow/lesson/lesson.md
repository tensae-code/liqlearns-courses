# Why Citizenship Must Grow

## Do it first

Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: why Citizenship Must Grow. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Egypt's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

Citizenship can be strong or weak in a society, and the difference matters. This lesson teaches why developing citizenship is important: a society whose citizens know their duties and rights, and act on them, holds together and moves forward in a way a society of bystanders cannot.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The importance of developing citizenship in society. Give one real example from your own life or from the Egypt you live in, and name the lesson's key words: why, citizenship. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: The importance of developing citizenship in society.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
