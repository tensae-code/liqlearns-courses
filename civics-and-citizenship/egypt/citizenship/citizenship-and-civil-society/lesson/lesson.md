# Citizenship and Civil Society

## Do it first

Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: citizenship and Civil Society. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Egypt's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

The book places citizenship next to civil society: the citizen belongs to the state, and also lives inside the web of associations and groups that make up civil society. It then takes citizenship apart into its components, set out as a diagram, so the learner can see the whole thing at once before the parts are taught one by one.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Citizenship set alongside civil society, and the components of citizenship, set out as a diagram. Give one real example from your own life or from the Egypt you live in, and name the lesson's key words: citizenship, and. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: Citizenship set alongside civil society, and the components of citizenship, set out as a diagram.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
