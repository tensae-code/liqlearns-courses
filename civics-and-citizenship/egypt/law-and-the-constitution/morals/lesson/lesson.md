# Morals

## Do it first

Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: morals. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Egypt's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

The final-year book begins before law, with morals. Morals are the rules of conduct a person carries inside — the sense of right and wrong that governs behaviour before any law is written or any court sits. This lesson teaches what morals are, because everything in the chapter after it stands on them.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: What morals are, and how they govern conduct before any law does. Give one real example from your own life or from the Egypt you live in, and name the lesson's key words: morals. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
