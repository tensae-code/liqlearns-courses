# Characteristics of Human Rights

## Do it first

Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: characteristics of Human Rights. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Egypt's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

What makes a human right a human right? The book names its marks. Human rights are universal — they belong to everyone. They are indivisible — you cannot keep some and drop others. And they cannot be waived — a person cannot sign them away. Those three marks are what this lesson fixes in the learner's mind.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The characteristics of human rights: universality, indivisibility, and that they cannot be waived. Give one real example from your own life or from the Egypt you live in, and name the lesson's key words: characteristics, of. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
