# About the Civics and Citizenship — Egypt course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Citizenship and Human Rights and National Education
books. The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a
box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours;
the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Egypt inside the Civics and
Citizenship folder (one course per country). Inside it, the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place
is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the
ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced
gate. The school grades the books come from appear only in
each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

Egypt's own way, preserved whole, as filed: the Grade 11
book teaches citizenship as a balance sheet — duties and
rights on facing pages — and then immediately updates it
for the phone in the pupil's pocket: a full
digital-citizenship unit tied to the state's own
digital-transformation programme. The Grade 12 book is a
single argument — law and constitution first, then
democracy, then the parties, then the pupil's own
participation — and every general idea is immediately
landed in Egypt: the 2014 constitution, the Constitutional
Court, Egypt's own party history. Both read books are
image-only scans and were read page by page from rendered
pages and contact sheets. The books' activities and
exam-model sections are assessment apparatus — named in the
book entries below, not placed. Constitutional material is
summarised by topic only, never transcribed.

The course as built has **four stocked levels**, from the
filing for this build (`egypt-placements.md` — 3 records /
2 distinct books read; 55 placements in all, stocking 55
lessons — the coined lesson names were deduped before
building, no two placements shared a lesson, and every
placement stocks into exactly one lesson):

1. **Level 1 — Citizenship, Digital Citizenship and Human
   Rights (18 lessons).** What citizenship is, its duties,
   rights and dimensions and the institutions that build
   it; the citizen online; and human rights from the old
   civilisations to Egypt's own National Council.
2. **Level 2 — Egyptian Women and Taking Part
   (9 lessons).** The Egyptian woman's journey era by era
   and the institutions that carry her claim, then
   voluntary work and civil society organisations.
3. **Level 3 — Law and the Constitution (7 lessons).**
   Morals, law, the constitution, Egypt's 2014 constitution
   and its Supreme Constitutional Court.
4. **Level 4 — Democracy, Parties and Participation
   (21 lessons).** Democracy's meaning and history, parties
   in general and in Egypt, and the pupil's own
   participation, ending in the electoral process and the
   book's closing model of participation.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later;
the books' own shape, walked in order):

- *Citizenship, in balance:* "First, citizenship itself:
  what it is, the state it belongs to, how it is gained —
  and its two sides held together, duties and rights, no
  right without a matching duty. Then the citizen online:
  digital citizenship, the digital citizen's traits and
  skills, and the state's own Digital Egypt. Then human
  rights: what they are, their long history, their kinds —
  and Egypt's own National Council for Human Rights. Then
  citizenship applied: the Egyptian woman's journey from
  the ancient era to 1919 and after, Hoda Sha'arawi and
  Nabaweya Mousa, the National Council for Women — and
  your own part: voluntary work and civil society. Then
  the machinery: morals, law, the constitution, Egypt's
  2014 constitution and the Constitutional Court. And
  last, taking part: democracy, the parties, and your own
  participation — step by step through the electoral
  process, to a model you can follow."
