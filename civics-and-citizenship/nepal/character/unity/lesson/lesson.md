# Unity

## Do it first

Try to snap one thin stick. Then try to snap a bundle of the same sticks tied together. Feel the difference unity makes.

## Stronger together

Unity means standing together as one. A single person can be pushed aside, tired out or frightened; people who are united hold each other up, and their strength is more than all their single strengths added together.

The lesson teaches unity as a part of character, not only a trick for hard times. A person of character chooses to stand with others — in the family, in the class, in the community — instead of standing alone for themselves only.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: unity is a strength of character — people who stand together can do what no one of them could do alone. Give one real example from your own life in Nepal, and name the lesson's key words: unity, together, strength, character. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Nepal's own books teach Moral Education through named people — a story first, then the behaviour it models, then discussion. When a lesson names a person, picture that person's day, not only their name: the story is the teaching.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: unity is a strength of character — people who stand together can do what no one of them could do alone.
3. Teach this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
