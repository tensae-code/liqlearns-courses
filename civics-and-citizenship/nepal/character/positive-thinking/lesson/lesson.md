# Positive Thinking

## Do it first

Take one hard thing in front of you today. Say how a positive thinker would name it — then name the first small act you will do about it.

## Think positively, learn and do

The book's lesson title is a three-step order: think positively, learn, and do. Positive thinking comes first because the way you name a difficulty decides whether you face it or flee from it.

But thinking alone is not the lesson. The positive thought must pass into learning — finding out, practising, asking — and learning must pass into doing. A positive thinker who never acts has stopped at the first step of three.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: positive thinking is not only a mood — it joins learning and doing: think positively, learn, and then act. Give one real example from your own life in Nepal, and name the lesson's key words: positive, thinking, learn, do. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Nepal's own books teach Moral Education through named people — a story first, then the behaviour it models, then discussion. When a lesson names a person, picture that person's day, not only their name: the story is the teaching.
