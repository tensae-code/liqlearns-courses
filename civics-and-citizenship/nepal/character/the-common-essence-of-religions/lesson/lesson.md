# The Common Essence of Religions

## Do it first

Name two ways people around you worship or believe. Then name one good way of treating people that both of them teach.

## One essence under many names

People follow different religions, with different names, prayers and customs. This lesson teaches that underneath those differences there is a common essence: every religion teaches its followers how to live well and how to treat other people — with honesty, kindness and care.

The lesson also joins two things that belong together: religious conduct and social conduct. What a person believes is not separate from how that person behaves in the family, the street and the community. Belief that never shows in conduct has missed the essence; conduct is where the essence is proved.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: under their different names and ways, religions share one essence about how a person should live and treat others — and religious conduct and social conduct belong together. Give one real example from your own life in Nepal, and name the lesson's key words: religions, essence, conduct, society. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Nepal's own books teach Moral Education through named people — a story first, then the behaviour it models, then discussion. When a lesson names a person, picture that person's day, not only their name: the story is the teaching.
