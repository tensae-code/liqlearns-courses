# Being a Good Example

## Do it first

Name one person younger than you who watches what you do. Name one act of yours they might copy — make sure it is a good one.

## Let's be a good example

The Class 7 book showed a role model to follow; this lesson turns the learner around: now you are the one being watched. Being a good example means making your own life a lesson others can learn from.

People learn more from what they see than from what they are told. A person who knows this lives carefully in public and in private alike — because a younger brother, a classmate or a neighbour is always learning from somebody, and that somebody may be you.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: make yourself an example for others — live so that people who watch you learn the good way from what you do. Give one real example from your own life in Nepal, and name the lesson's key words: example, others, watch, live. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Nepal's own books teach Moral Education through named people — a story first, then the behaviour it models, then discussion. When a lesson names a person, picture that person's day, not only their name: the story is the teaching.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: make yourself an example for others — live so that people who watch you learn the good way from what you do.
3. Teach this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
