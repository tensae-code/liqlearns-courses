# As You Sow, So You Reap

## Do it first

Think of one result in your life right now, good or bad. Trace it back: what act was the seed it grew from?

## As you sow, so you reap

The lesson teaches with a farmer's picture. A farmer who sows good seed and tends it reaps a good harvest; a farmer who sows weeds, or nothing, reaps accordingly. Actions work the same way: every act is a seed, and its consequence is the harvest.

This is not a threat — it is how life is built. The person who sows honesty reaps trust; the person who sows laziness reaps want. Knowing that your harvest grows from your own sowing puts your future in your own hands.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: actions bring their own consequences — as you sow, so you reap: good acts grow good results, and careless or harmful acts grow theirs. Give one real example from your own life in Nepal, and name the lesson's key words: sow, reap, actions, consequences. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Nepal's own books teach Moral Education through named people — a story first, then the behaviour it models, then discussion. When a lesson names a person, picture that person's day, not only their name: the story is the teaching.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: actions bring their own consequences — as you sow, so you reap: good acts grow good results, and careless or harmful acts grow theirs.
3. Teach this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
