# Regret and Learning

## Do it first

Think of one act you regret. Say what it taught you — and what you would do instead if it came again.

## Learning from regret

Regret is the pain a person feels after doing wrong. This lesson teaches that regret, honestly faced, is not only pain — it is a teacher. The person who regrets has seen their act clearly, and that clear sight is where learning begins.

Regret that only hurts is wasted; regret that teaches changes the next act. The lesson asks the learner to let regret do its proper work: name the wrong, learn its lesson, make what repair is possible, and turn.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: regret is a teacher — after wrongdoing, feeling regret honestly is how a person learns and turns. Give one real example from your own life in Nepal, and name the lesson's key words: regret, learning, wrongdoing, turn. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Nepal's own books teach Moral Education through named people — a story first, then the behaviour it models, then discussion. When a lesson names a person, picture that person's day, not only their name: the story is the teaching.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: regret is a teacher — after wrongdoing, feeling regret honestly is how a person learns and turns.
3. Teach this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
