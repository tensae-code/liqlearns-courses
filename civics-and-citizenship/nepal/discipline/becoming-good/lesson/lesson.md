# Becoming Good

## Do it first

Finish this sentence out loud, and mean it: 'I become good when I …' Name one real act, not a wish.

## I become good

The book's lesson speaks in the first person: I become good. This lesson teaches that goodness is not something poured into a person from outside — it begins with the person's own choice to become good.

A choice is only the start; it must be carried by acts. But without the choice, the acts are only obedience. The learner who says 'I become good' and means it has taken the first and hardest step: deciding, themselves, what kind of person to be.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: becoming good is a choice you make yourself — 'I become good' starts with your own decision, not with being forced. Give one real example from your own life in Nepal, and name the lesson's key words: becoming, good, choice, myself. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Nepal's own books teach Moral Education through named people — a story first, then the behaviour it models, then discussion. When a lesson names a person, picture that person's day, not only their name: the story is the teaching.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: becoming good is a choice you make yourself — 'I become good' starts with your own decision, not with being forced.
3. Teach this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
