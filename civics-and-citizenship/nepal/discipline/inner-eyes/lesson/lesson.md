# Inner Eyes

## Do it first

Close your eyes for a moment and look at your own day honestly. Name one thing you did well, and one thing you would change.

## Seeing with inner eyes

Outer eyes see the world; inner eyes see the self. This lesson teaches self-examination: turning your sight inward and looking at your own acts, motives and habits as honestly as you would look at another person's.

Self-examination is a discipline — it must be practised, because the inner eyes are easily flattered shut. The person who examines themselves daily catches small wrongs while they are small, and knows truly what in them is growing and what must change.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: see yourself honestly with your inner eyes — self-examination is how you know what in you must change. Give one real example from your own life in Nepal, and name the lesson's key words: inner eyes, honest, self, examination. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Nepal's own books teach Moral Education through named people — a story first, then the behaviour it models, then discussion. When a lesson names a person, picture that person's day, not only their name: the story is the teaching.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: see yourself honestly with your inner eyes — self-examination is how you know what in you must change.
3. Teach this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
