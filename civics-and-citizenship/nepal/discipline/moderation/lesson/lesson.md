# Moderation

## Do it first

Name one good thing that turns harmful when there is too much of it. Say where the right measure lies.

## Excess of anything is destructive

The book's lesson title is its whole teaching: excess of anything is destructive. Even good things — food, rest, play, speech, even work — turn harmful when they pass their measure. Moderation is the discipline of holding things at their right measure.

The moderate person is not a person of half-heartedness; they are a person of measure. They know how much is enough, and stop there — so the good things of life stay good, and nothing they love is destroyed by too much of itself.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: excess in anything destroys — moderation keeps even good things good, by holding them in measure. Give one real example from your own life in Nepal, and name the lesson's key words: moderation, excess, measure, destructive. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Nepal's own books teach Moral Education through named people — a story first, then the behaviour it models, then discussion. When a lesson names a person, picture that person's day, not only their name: the story is the teaching.
