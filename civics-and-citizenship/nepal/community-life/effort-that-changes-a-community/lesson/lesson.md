# Effort That Changes a Community

## Do it first

Name one thing in your community that is better because one person made an effort. Say who started it.

## Ganga's effort

This lesson's teacher is Ganga, whose effort improved the life of her community. The book holds her up to make one point plainly: communities do not only change by orders from above — they change when one person makes an effort and keeps going.

Effort is the bridge between wishing and change. Everyone can wish the path were swept, the water cleaner, the school repaired. Ganga's lesson is that the one who starts — and continues — is the one whose community actually changes.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: one person's effort can change a whole community — Ganga's effort shows that community life improves when someone starts and keeps going. Give one real example from your own life in Nepal, and name the lesson's key words: effort, Ganga, change, community. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Nepal's own books teach Moral Education through named people — a story first, then the behaviour it models, then discussion. When a lesson names a person, picture that person's day, not only their name: the story is the teaching.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: one person's effort can change a whole community — Ganga's effort shows that community life improves when someone starts and keeps going.
3. Teach this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
