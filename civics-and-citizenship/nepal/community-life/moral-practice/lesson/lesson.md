# Moral Practice

## Do it first

Choose one moral act — a true word, a helped hand, a kept promise — and do it on purpose before this lesson ends.

## Morality in daily practice

Knowing the good is not the same as doing it. This lesson is about moral practice: putting morality into daily practice, in the small acts of an ordinary day — the true word spoken, the help given, the promise kept.

Practice is how morality becomes habit. An act done once is an event; the same act done daily is character. The lesson asks the learner to stop treating morality as a subject to recite and start treating it as a thing to do, today and again tomorrow.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: morality is not only something you know — it is something you practise every day, in small acts, until it becomes your habit. Give one real example from your own life in Nepal, and name the lesson's key words: practice, daily, habit, morality. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Nepal's own books teach Moral Education through named people — a story first, then the behaviour it models, then discussion. When a lesson names a person, picture that person's day, not only their name: the story is the teaching.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: morality is not only something you know — it is something you practise every day, in small acts, until it becomes your habit.
3. Teach this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
