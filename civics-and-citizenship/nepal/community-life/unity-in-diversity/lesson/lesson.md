# Unity in Diversity

## Do it first

Name the members of one group you belong to — family, class or team. Say one way you differ, and one thing that holds you together.

## A story of five brothers

The book teaches this lesson as a story: five brothers, different members of one family. Separately each can be broken by life; together, as one family, they stand. It is the bundle-of-sticks teaching of the Class 7 book, grown into a story.

Unity in diversity means the members do not have to become the same to stand together. Their differences remain — it is their unity that is added. A family, a class and a country all live by this: many different members, one bond.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: different members can stand as one family — the story of five brothers teaches unity among people who are not the same. Give one real example from your own life in Nepal, and name the lesson's key words: unity, diversity, brothers, family. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Nepal's own books teach Moral Education through named people — a story first, then the behaviour it models, then discussion. When a lesson names a person, picture that person's day, not only their name: the story is the teaching.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: different members can stand as one family — the story of five brothers teaches unity among people who are not the same.
3. Teach this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
