# Peace and Friendship

## Do it first

Name one person different from you — in language, custom or belief — who is your friend, or could be. Say what keeps the friendship peaceful.

## Peace and friendship in a diverse community

Nepal's communities are diverse: many languages, customs and beliefs live side by side. This lesson teaches that the way such a community lives well is peace and friendship — difference without enmity.

Peace is not the absence of difference; it is difference handled with friendship. Friends across the lines of language and custom hold a community together more strongly than sameness ever could, because they prove daily that different people can choose each other.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: peace and friendship are how a diverse community lives — people who differ can still live as friends, in peace. Give one real example from your own life in Nepal, and name the lesson's key words: peace, friendship, diverse, community. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Nepal's own books teach Moral Education through named people — a story first, then the behaviour it models, then discussion. When a lesson names a person, picture that person's day, not only their name: the story is the teaching.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: peace and friendship are how a diverse community lives — people who differ can still live as friends, in peace.
3. Teach this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
