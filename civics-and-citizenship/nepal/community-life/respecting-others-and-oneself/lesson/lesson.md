# Respecting Others and Oneself

## Do it first

Say one respectful thing about another person, out loud. Then say one respectful thing about yourself, just as truly.

## Respecting others, respecting oneself

The book's lesson title joins two things with a comma, and this lesson teaches why they are joined: respecting others and respecting oneself are one habit, not two. A person who truly respects themselves does not trample others; a person who respects others does not belittle themselves.

Respect shows in small things: listening when another speaks, speaking of yourself without boasting or shame, treating every person — and yourself — as someone who counts. Practised both ways, respect becomes the air of a good community.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: respect for others and respect for yourself are one habit — you cannot truly have one without the other. Give one real example from your own life in Nepal, and name the lesson's key words: respect, others, oneself, habit. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Nepal's own books teach Moral Education through named people — a story first, then the behaviour it models, then discussion. When a lesson names a person, picture that person's day, not only their name: the story is the teaching.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: respect for others and respect for yourself are one habit — you cannot truly have one without the other.
3. Teach this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
