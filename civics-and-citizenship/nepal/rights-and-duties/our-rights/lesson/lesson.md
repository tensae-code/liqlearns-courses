# Our Rights

## Do it first

Name three things you believe every child should be able to do, learn or have. Those are rights you are naming.

## The rights a person holds

A right is something that belongs to a person simply because they are a person. This lesson opens the book's unit on civil duties and responsibilities by starting where it must start: with the rights each of us holds.

Rights are not gifts that others may take away when they please. They are ours — and because they are ours, other people must respect them, just as we must respect the same rights in them. The next lessons take up the other side of the coin: responsibility and duty.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: every person holds rights — things that are theirs by being a person, which others must respect. Give one real example from your own life in Nepal, and name the lesson's key words: rights, person, respect, ours. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Nepal's own books teach Moral Education through named people — a story first, then the behaviour it models, then discussion. When a lesson names a person, picture that person's day, not only their name: the story is the teaching.
