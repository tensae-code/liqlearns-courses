# Social Work

## Do it first

Name one act of social work you have seen in your own community — someone serving others without being paid. Say what it changed.

## Misrilal, a true social worker

Nepal's books teach through named people, and this lesson's teacher is Misrilal: a true social worker. His life is the picture of civic duty — a person who gives his time, strength and care to the community's good.

Social work is duty with its sleeves rolled up. The social worker does not wait for office or payment; he sees what the community needs and serves. Misrilal stands in the book so that learners can see the duty of citizens walking around in a real life.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Misrilal's life shows what civic duty looks like in a person — a true social worker spends himself for the good of the community. Give one real example from your own life in Nepal, and name the lesson's key words: social work, Misrilal, service, community. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Nepal's own books teach Moral Education through named people — a story first, then the behaviour it models, then discussion. When a lesson names a person, picture that person's day, not only their name: the story is the teaching.
