# Learning from Elders

## Do it first

Ask a parent or elder one question today: 'What is one duty you want me to learn?' Listen to the whole answer before you speak.

## A father's guidance

The book teaches this lesson through a father's guidance: the older generation handing the younger one its duties. Parents and elders have walked the road before; their guidance is experience turned into direction.

Taking guidance is itself a duty of the young. To listen carefully, to ask, and to follow good guidance is how a child learns what no book alone can teach — how duties look in a real life, in a real family.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: parents and elders guide us in our duties — listening to their guidance is part of how a young person learns to do right. Give one real example from your own life in Nepal, and name the lesson's key words: elders, guidance, parents, duties. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Nepal's own books teach Moral Education through named people — a story first, then the behaviour it models, then discussion. When a lesson names a person, picture that person's day, not only their name: the story is the teaching.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: parents and elders guide us in our duties — listening to their guidance is part of how a young person learns to do right.
3. Teach this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
