# My Duty

## Do it first

Write or say one sentence that begins 'My duty is…' — and name a duty you can actually do this week.

## Naming my duty

The lesson before this one named the duty of citizens in general. This lesson brings it home to one person: my duty. A duty that belongs to everybody in general can be dodged by anybody; a duty named as mine cannot.

Naming your duty means saying, in your own words, what you yourself owe — at home, at school, in the community — and owning it. The lesson asks the learner to stop speaking of duty as other people's business and take hold of their own share by name.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: duty becomes real when you name it as your own — 'my duty' is the duty you have taken hold of yourself. Give one real example from your own life in Nepal, and name the lesson's key words: duty, mine, own, naming. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Nepal's own books teach Moral Education through named people — a story first, then the behaviour it models, then discussion. When a lesson names a person, picture that person's day, not only their name: the story is the teaching.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: duty becomes real when you name it as your own — 'my duty' is the duty you have taken hold of yourself.
3. Teach this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
