# Moral Responsibility

## Do it first

Name one right you hold. Now name one responsibility that stands on its other side.

## The other side of rights

The lesson before this one taught the rights a person holds. This lesson turns the coin over: alongside every right stands a moral responsibility — the things a person ought to do, for others and for what is right.

Moral responsibility is not forced from outside; it is the inner ought. I ought to respect the rights I claim for myself when they belong to my neighbour. I ought to answer for my acts. A person who holds their rights and carries their responsibilities is a whole citizen in the making.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: rights come with a second side — moral responsibility: what you ought to do for others and for what is right. Give one real example from your own life in Nepal, and name the lesson's key words: responsibility, moral, rights, ought. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Nepal's own books teach Moral Education through named people — a story first, then the behaviour it models, then discussion. When a lesson names a person, picture that person's day, not only their name: the story is the teaching.
