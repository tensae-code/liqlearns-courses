# The Duty of Citizens

## Do it first

Name one duty you think every citizen of Nepal owes. Say who it helps when a citizen does it.

## What citizens owe

The Class 7 book taught rights first, then moral responsibility. The Class 8 book raises the civic weight: this lesson names the duty of citizens — what the members of a country owe to that country and to the community they share.

A citizen who only claims and never carries is a passenger, not a citizen. Duties — respecting the law and the rights of others, serving the community, answering when the country calls — are the citizen's share of the common life.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: citizens owe duties to their country and community — being a citizen is not only holding rights, it is carrying your share. Give one real example from your own life in Nepal, and name the lesson's key words: duty, citizens, country, community. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Nepal's own books teach Moral Education through named people — a story first, then the behaviour it models, then discussion. When a lesson names a person, picture that person's day, not only their name: the story is the teaching.
