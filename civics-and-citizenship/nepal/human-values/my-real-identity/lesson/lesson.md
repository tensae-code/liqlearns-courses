# My Real Identity

## Do it first

Say three things about yourself that no one could see just by looking at you. That is your real identity speaking.

## My real identity

Appearances are the first thing people see — face, clothes, size, wealth — but they are not who a person is. This lesson teaches the learner to know their real identity: the character, values and purposes that make them themselves.

Knowing your real identity steadies you. A person who knows who they truly are is not blown about by every opinion, and does not mistake a label for a self. Identity known honestly is the ground that good character is built on.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: your real identity is who you truly are — deeper than appearances, names or what others happen to see first. Give one real example from your own life in Nepal, and name the lesson's key words: identity, real, character, self. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Nepal's own books teach Moral Education through named people — a story first, then the behaviour it models, then discussion. When a lesson names a person, picture that person's day, not only their name: the story is the teaching.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: your real identity is who you truly are — deeper than appearances, names or what others happen to see first.
3. Teach this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
