# Universal Relationship

## Do it first

Name one person far outside your family and friends — in another village, town or country. Say one way their life touches yours.

## One relationship with all people

A person's closest relationships are with family and friends — but the lesson teaches that relationship does not stop at that circle. Each of us stands in a universal relationship: bound to all people, near and far, known and unknown.

What happens to distant people touches us, and what we do touches them — through trade, through the shared earth, through our common humanity. To know your universal relationship is to widen your care to the size of the world.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: you are related to all people, not only to your own circle — every human being stands in relationship with you. Give one real example from your own life in Nepal, and name the lesson's key words: universal, relationship, humanity, circle. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Nepal's own books teach Moral Education through named people — a story first, then the behaviour it models, then discussion. When a lesson names a person, picture that person's day, not only their name: the story is the teaching.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: you are related to all people, not only to your own circle — every human being stands in relationship with you.
3. Teach this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
