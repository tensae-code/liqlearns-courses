# Honesty

## Do it first

Think of one small moment today when you could have hidden the truth. Say out loud what the honest thing to say or do was.

## Honesty as a norm of life

A norm is the settled rule a life runs by. This lesson teaches honesty as exactly that: not a performance for the moments when someone is watching, but the normal way a person speaks and acts, every day.

An honest person tells the truth about what happened, owns what they did, and does not take what is not theirs. Because honesty is their norm, other people can trust their words — and trust is the ground every other good thing in this course stands on.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: honesty is a norm of life — tell the truth and act truly, not only when someone is watching, but as your settled way of living. Give one real example from your own life in Nepal, and name the lesson's key words: honesty, truth, norm, life. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Nepal's own books teach Moral Education through named people — a story first, then the behaviour it models, then discussion. When a lesson names a person, picture that person's day, not only their name: the story is the teaching.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: honesty is a norm of life — tell the truth and act truly, not only when someone is watching, but as your settled way of living.
3. Teach this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
