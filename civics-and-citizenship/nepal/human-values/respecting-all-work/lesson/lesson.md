# Respecting All Work

## Do it first

Name five different jobs people do in your community. For each one, say one thing that would go wrong if nobody did it.

## The dignity of various occupations

A community lives by many occupations: people grow food, teach children, build, carry, clean, heal, drive and mend. The lesson teaches that these various occupations all carry dignity, because each one serves a need that everyone shares.

Respecting all work means never looking down on a person for the job they do. The one who sweeps the street and the one who sits in an office both give their labour to the common life — and labour honestly done is worthy of respect, whoever does it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: every honest occupation has dignity — the farmer, the teacher, the cleaner and the driver all do work the community needs, and all deserve respect. Give one real example from your own life in Nepal, and name the lesson's key words: occupations, dignity, work, respect. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Nepal's own books teach Moral Education through named people — a story first, then the behaviour it models, then discussion. When a lesson names a person, picture that person's day, not only their name: the story is the teaching.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: every honest occupation has dignity — the farmer, the teacher, the cleaner and the driver all do work the community needs, and all deserve respect.
3. Teach this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
