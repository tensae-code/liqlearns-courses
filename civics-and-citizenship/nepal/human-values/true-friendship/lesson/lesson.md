# True Friendship

## Do it first

Name one true friend. Say one time they stood with you — and one time you stood with them.

## True soul mates

The book calls true friends 'true soul mates' — companions whose bond is deeper than convenience. This lesson teaches true companionship as a human value: a friendship that holds in hard times, not only in easy ones.

A true friend tells you the truth, stands beside you when others drift away, and rejoices at your good without envy. And the value runs both ways: to have a true friend, be one — standing with your friend is the same lesson from the other side.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: true companionship is a human value — a true friend stands with you in good times and hard ones, and you stand with them. Give one real example from your own life in Nepal, and name the lesson's key words: friendship, true, companion, stand. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Nepal's own books teach Moral Education through named people — a story first, then the behaviour it models, then discussion. When a lesson names a person, picture that person's day, not only their name: the story is the teaching.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: true companionship is a human value — a true friend stands with you in good times and hard ones, and you stand with them.
3. Teach this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
