# Helping Others

## Do it first

Look around your home or classroom. Find one person who could use a hand right now, and help them before you read on.

## Helping hands

Helping others is one of the human values: the worth a person shows by what they do for the people around them. The lesson's picture is in its name — helping hands. Help is not only a kind thought; it is hands that lift, carry, hold and work for someone else.

Help begins close by: a classmate who cannot carry, a neighbour who is struggling, a younger child who cannot reach. A person who has learned this value does not wait to be asked twice — they see the need and their hands are already moving.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: helping others is a human value you practise with your own hands — when someone near you needs help, giving it is what a person does. Give one real example from your own life in Nepal, and name the lesson's key words: helping, hands, others, value. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Nepal's own books teach Moral Education through named people — a story first, then the behaviour it models, then discussion. When a lesson names a person, picture that person's day, not only their name: the story is the teaching.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: helping others is a human value you practise with your own hands — when someone near you needs help, giving it is what a person does.
3. Teach this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
