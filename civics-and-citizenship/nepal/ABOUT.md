# About the Civics and Citizenship — Nepal course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Moral Education books (Classes 7 and 8, Curriculum
Development Centre). The one exception is the **Tip boxes**:
a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips
are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Nepal inside the Civics and
Citizenship folder (one course per country). Inside it, the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place
is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the
ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced
gate. The school grades the books come from (Classes 7 and 8)
appear only in each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as
provenance.

The course has **two stocked levels**, from the two books
filed — both books run the same five-unit frame (character
development · human values · civil rights and duties ·
community life style and diversity · discipline and positive
thinking), and the second book walks it one step up:

1. **Level 1 — Character, Values and First Civic Duties**
   (15 lessons, across the titles `character/`,
   `human-values/`, `rights-and-duties/`, `community-life/`
   and `discipline/`). The Class 7 book: character built
   through stories (Devi Ram, a path finder; the common
   essence of religions; unity), human values and norms
   (helping others, the dignity of various occupations,
   honesty), rights with moral responsibility (our rights,
   moral responsibility, a father's guidance), community
   life (the importance of education, moral practice,
   respecting others and oneself), and discipline with
   positive thinking (I become good, advantages of
   discipline, as you sow so you reap).
2. **Level 2 — The Citizen's Duty in the Community** (14 new
   lessons in the same five titles, plus Building Good
   Character deepened by its second sub lesson). The Class 8
   book keeps the frame and raises the civic content:
   character (Aaitaman, pride of the village; being a good
   example; think positively, learn and do), human values
   (true soul mates, my real identity, universal
   relationship), the duty of citizens (the duty of citizens,
   my duty, Misrilal the true social worker), community life
   (peace and friendship, Ganga's effort, a story of five
   brothers), and discipline (regret, inner eyes, excess of
   anything is destructive).

**Nepal's own way:** moral education is taught through
named-character stories (Devi Ram, and in Class 8 Aaitaman,
Misrilal, Ganga) — each lesson is a story, the expected
behaviours it models, and discussion, rather than rules
stated abstractly. Class 8 keeps the five-unit frame and
raises the civic content — from rights (Class 7) to the duty
of citizens, social work, and peace and friendship in a
diverse community.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
books' own shape, walked in order):

- *The five units, climbed twice:* "Start with character —
  follow Devi Ram, learn the essence the religions share,
  stand united. Then the values: helping hands, honest work,
  honesty itself. Then rights with responsibility, community
  life, and discipline. Then climb the same five again, one
  step up: Aaitaman's village, the duty of citizens,
  Misrilal's service, Ganga's effort — until regret teaches
  you, your inner eyes open, and you learn that excess of
  anything is destructive."
