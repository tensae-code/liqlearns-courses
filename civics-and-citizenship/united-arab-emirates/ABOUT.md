# About the Civics and Citizenship — United Arab Emirates course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Moral Education and Social Studies and National
Education books. The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a
tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips
are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for the United Arab Emirates inside the
Civics and Citizenship folder (one course per country).
Inside it, the organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a
learner's place is found by what they prove, and each level
stands on the ones before it as a recommended-first path —
never a forced gate. The school grades the books come from
appear only in each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as
provenance.

The United Arab Emirates' own way, preserved whole, as
filed: the Moral Education programme runs on four strands
(Character and Morals; the Individual and Society; Cultural
Studies; Civic Studies), and the youngest children's civics
is taught entirely through stories — Emirati heritage
itself arrives as tales told by grandparents, and honesty
is discovered through storytelling rather than stated as a
rule. National education for six-year-olds is one founding
story — Sheikh Zayed as father of the union — plus the days
and clothes and foods that make the heritage theirs.
Heritage is then handled, not just told: children describe
real old objects, ask where they came from, and finish by
making and showing work of their own. The Emirates' culture
is taught as something made by movement — caravans, camels,
ports and caravanserais — and peace is taught as a skill
practised at home and school, not a slogan. At the top of
primary school the pupil studies his own country as a case
of change — how a society became diverse — and proves
cultural competence by curating a museum exhibition; the
pupil book teaches tolerance from inside the pupil's own
classroom, and the Universal Declaration of Human Rights is
read as part of the lesson. The middle-school volume's
civics heart, in its printed body, is conflict: the pupil
learns to name a conflict inside and outside himself before
any institution is taught, and closes by planning and
running a real community project.

the Emirates Grew* lessons are each stocked by two
placements (the Grade 6 teacher guide, term 2, and the
Grade 6 student book, volume 2, teach the same unit); every
other coined name was already distinct, and every
placement stocks into exactly one lesson:

1. **Level 1 — Care, Honesty and the Work Around You
   (7 lessons).** Caring for ourselves, others and our
   school, being truthful and honesty in stories (Grade 1
   Moral Education, Unit 4); my grandfathers' professions
   and the job I want when I grow up (Grade 1 Social
   Studies and National Education, Unit 3).
2. **Level 2 — Belonging — Stories, Zayed and the Union,
   and Old Objects (13 lessons).** Storytelling and the
   UAE's heritage in stories (Grade 1 Moral Education,
   Unit 3); Zayed the father, days I love, I love my
   heritage (Grade 1 Social Studies and National Education,
   Unit 4); describing old objects, where they come from,
   what they were for, why they were made, and the
   archaeologists' work (Grade 2 Moral Education, Unit 3).
3. **Level 3 — Character in Action — Keeping Going and
   Good Character (10 lessons).** Working hard,
   perseverance, flexibility, my strengths, finishing and
   showing my project (Grade 2 Moral Education, Unit 4);
   what good character means, patience, forgiveness, words
   and deeds of a good person, growing into good character
   (Grade 5 Moral Education, Unit 4).
4. **Level 4 — The Widening World — Trade, Peace and
   Settlement (15 lessons).** Trade, the Emirates' trade
   routes, camels and desert trade, how trade changes
   culture, ports and cities (Grade 4 Moral Education,
   Unit 3); solving disagreements, talking and compromise,
   resolving conflict step by step, acting responsibly,
   responsibility to the community (Grade 4, Unit 4);
   change and temporary work, artefacts of settlement,
   settled and nomadic life, family history and oral
   tradition, pearls and oil (Grade 5, Unit 3).
5. **Level 5 — The Nation Itself — Health, a Diverse
   Society, Respect and Tolerance (15 lessons).** Looking
   after my body, healthy eating, obstacles to a healthy
   life, global health problems, a healthy life project
   (Grade 6 Moral Education, Unit 2); museums keep our
   culture, how Emirati culture changed, museum exhibits,
   preparing our exhibition, opening day (Grade 6, Unit 3 —
   teacher guide and student book together); tolerance and
   diversity, tolerance in society, exploring our cultures,
   recognising prejudice, a school for everyone (Grade 6
   student book, Unit 4).
6. **Level 6 — Conflict and Ethics in Action (6 lessons).**
   Internal conflict, managing internal conflict, external
   conflict, managing external conflict, resolving
   disagreements (Grade 8 Moral Education vol. 3, Unit 5);
   a project for others — Ethics in Action (Unit 6).

**The ladder reconciliation, stated plainly.** The first
filing's ladder sketch lays out Levels 1–5 from the Grades
1–6 books, and they stand unchanged. The second filing's
own sketch was drawn on its own: its L1 was the Grades 1–6
volumes (leads and census in that pass), its L2 the Grade 8
Volume 3 book it read, and its L3 the Grades 9–12 volumes
(leads only). In this one combined course the Grade 8 book
sits after the Grades 1–6 ladder, as **Level 6** — the
place both sketches agree it belongs: after primary school,
before the unread Grades 9–12 volumes.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later;
the books' own shape, walked in order):

- *The Emirates' own order:* "First, care: for yourself,
  for others, for your school — and honesty, found inside
  stories, with the work of the people around you. Then
  belonging: the tales your grandparents tell, Zayed and
  the founding of the union, and the old objects you can
  hold and describe. Then character in action: keep going,
  be flexible, and grow the virtues — patience, forgiveness,
  upright words and deeds. Then the widening world: the
  trade routes and caravans that made this culture, peace
  practised at home and school, and the families who
  settled this land from pearls to oil. Then the nation
  itself: your health as your responsibility, how the
  Emirates grew into a diverse society — shown in a museum
  exhibition you prepare yourself — and the respect and
  tolerance that hold it together. Last, conflict: the
  conflict inside you, the conflict outside you, and how
  disagreements are resolved — and you close by doing
  ethics, in a real project for others."
