# Civics and Citizenship — United Arab Emirates course — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

Civics belongs to the country: this course is the United
Arab Emirates' own — built from the UAE's Moral Education
books (Grades 1–6 and Grade 8) and its Grade 1 Social
Studies and National Education teacher guide, all in
Arabic. Inside the course the organisation is by **levels,
not grades**: the titles below ARE the levels' titles, in
path order — what the learner proves, step by step. The
grades the books come from live only in each lesson's
sources.

The path's own logic is the Emirates', from the two
filings' ladder sketches reconciled into one ladder: the
youngest child's civics is taught entirely through stories
— care for yourself, others and your school, honesty found
inside tales, the work people do, and one founding story,
Zayed and the union of the seven emirates on 2 December
1971. Heritage is then handled as well as told — real
artefacts described, sourced and explained — and character
is practised: perseverance and flexibility, then patience,
forgiveness and upright conduct. The circle widens to how
trade and travel made Emirati culture, how conflicts are
settled peacefully, and how families and tribes settled
the land from pearls to oil. At the top of primary school
the pupil studies the nation itself — his own health as a
responsibility, how the UAE became a diverse, inclusive
society, proved by curating a museum exhibition, and the
respect and tolerance that hold it together. The
middle-school volume closes the built ladder with conflict
named inside and outside oneself, and ethics put into a
real community project. That ladder IS the path order
(recommended, never forced — a learner can jump in higher;
the "start here instead" hints point down this ladder).
