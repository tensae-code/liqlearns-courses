# What Territory Means

## Do it first

Draw a rough map of the ground your day covers: home, the road, school, the shops. Now draw a line around the country on a map. This lesson asks what that line holds: what 'territory' means for a state and its people.

## Sub lesson 1 — Territory as the state's ground

A state stands on territory: the defined ground — land and the space that goes with it — within which the state exists.

Without territory there is no state, only an idea of one: the ground is one of the things a state is made of.

## Sub lesson 2 — Territory and its people

Territory is not empty: it is where the people live, work, travel and bury their dead — the shared ground of a common life.

That is why territory matters to citizens and not only to map-makers: it is the stage of everything the country does together.

## Sub lesson 3 — Authority over territory

Within its territory the state's authority runs: its laws apply, its institutions act, and it answers for what happens there.

The same ground, seen three ways — the state's base, the people's home, the reach of the law — is the one thing this lesson teaches.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: territory is the ground of the state — the land, and the space, where a people lives and where the state's authority runs. Give one real example from your own life or from the Chile you live in, and name the lesson's key words: territory, state, authority. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
