# Hands-on — What Territory Means

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw a rough map of the ground your day covers: home, the road, school, the shops. Now draw a line around the country on a map. This lesson asks what that line holds: what 'territory' means for a state and its people.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: territory is the ground of the state — the land, and the space, where a people lives and where the state's authority runs. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
