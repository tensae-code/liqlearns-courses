# What Democracy Is For

## Do it first

Think of three decisions that affect you — one at home, one at school, one in your town. For each one, write who decides it today. This lesson asks who should decide, and how democracy grew up answering that question.

## Sub lesson 1 — Democracy in Athens

In Athens, citizens met and decided the city's affairs together, face to face — democracy begins as the people ruling themselves.

That first democracy was small and direct, and it left many people out — but its big idea survives: the people are the source of power.

## Sub lesson 2 — How democracy has changed

Democracy has changed across the centuries: more people count as citizens, and in large countries citizens choose representatives to decide in their name.

Each change widened who takes part and how — the road from Athens to today is the road of a promise being extended to more and more people.

## Sub lesson 3 — What democracy is for

Democracy is for governing ourselves together: deciding shared affairs, protecting each person's dignity, and correcting mistakes without violence.

That purpose is the measure of the rest of this course: every later lesson tests Chile's shared life against what democracy is for.

## Say it to your companion

Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
