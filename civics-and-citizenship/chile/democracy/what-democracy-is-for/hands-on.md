# Hands-on — What Democracy Is For

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Think of three decisions that affect you — one at home, one at school, one in your town. For each one, write who decides it today. This lesson asks who should decide, and how democracy grew up answering that question.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: democracy has changed from Athens to today — and it exists so that the people themselves govern, as the road into what democracy is for. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
