# Lesson plan — Being a Citizen in a Democracy

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Chile · **Title:** Democracy · **Lesson:** Being a Citizen in a Democracy
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — Democracy (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** to be a citizen in a democracy is to hold rights, carry duties, and take part in the life of the country.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Who is a citizen** — A citizen is a full member of the country: the country recognises them, and they belong to its shared life, not as a guest but as one of its own.
2. **Rights and duties together** — The citizen holds rights the country must respect — and carries duties toward the country and toward other citizens.
3. **Taking part as a citizen** — Being a citizen is practised: informing yourself, giving your opinion, voting, and taking part in the community's life are how citizenship is lived.

3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## If the learner is stuck
- Lists only rights and no duties (or only duties and no rights) → a citizen holds both together; one side without the other is half a citizen.
- Go back to the one thing: to be a citizen in a democracy is to hold rights, carry duties, and take part in the life of the country. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
