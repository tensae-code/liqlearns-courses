# Hands-on — Being a Citizen in a Democracy

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write your name at the top of a page. Under it, list three things your country gives you — and three things you give your country. This lesson names both lists: rights and duties, the two sides of being a citizen.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: to be a citizen in a democracy is to hold rights, carry duties, and take part in the life of the country. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
