# About the Civics and Citizenship — Chile course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this
course comes from government textbooks — this country's own
Educación Ciudadana book for 3° y 4° Medio. The one exception is
the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Chile inside the Civics and Citizenship
folder (one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is
by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what
they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a
recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school grades
the books come from appear only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

The course as built has **two stocked levels**, both from the
one combined student volume in hand:

1. **Level 1 — what the democratic state is (8 lessons).** The
   3° Medio half of the book: the purpose of democracy and what
   it means to be a citizen in it; the state as defender of
   human rights (access to justice, and the pupil's own relation
   to rights — holder, witness and defender); state and
   territory; and citizens and the market.
2. **Level 2 — the graduating citizen acting in it (8
   lessons).** The 4° Medio half of the book: participation in
   Chile's democracy and the gaps to overcome; media and
   democracy; building a more inclusive democracy; and labour
   rights and development models.

Chile's own way, preserved whole: the whole course is built as
questions a citizen must answer — what democracy is for, how
the state defends human rights, what territory means, how
citizens and markets collaborate, why participation solves
social problems, how media and democracy interact, how to build
a more inclusive democracy, and how labour rights meet
development models. Each unit runs two lessons, a synthesis, a
unit project and an evaluation; the syntheses, projects and
evaluations are named in the contents map below, not placed.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
book's own shape, walked in order):

- *Questions a citizen must answer:* "First, what the
  democratic state is: what democracy is for, what a citizen
  is, how the state defends your rights, what territory means,
  and how citizens and the market work together. Then the
  graduating citizen acting in it: how to participate, what the
  media do to democracy, how to build a democracy that leaves
  no one out, and how labour rights meet the models a country
  grows by."

## The ways of the books (their own shapes, preserved)

**Educación Ciudadana, 3° y 4° Medio** — one combined volume
for the last two school years, built entirely as questions a
citizen must answer: each of its eight units is a question,
each unit runs two lessons, a synthesis, a unit project and an
evaluation, and the two years form one arc — first what the
democratic state is (democracy's purpose, citizenship, rights
defended by the state, territory, citizens and the market),
then the graduating citizen acting in it (participation, media,
inclusion, labour rights and development models). *(Student
book.)*
