# An Inclusive Territory

## Do it first

Walk one route you know well — to school, to the shops, to a public office — in your mind. At each step ask: who could not make this journey, or enter this place, or be heard in this decision? Write the names of the left-out. This lesson is about them, and about changing the route.

## Sub lesson 1 — Who is left out

Some people are left out of places and decisions: by distance, by cost, by steps they cannot climb, by language, by being treated as if they did not belong.

Naming the left-out is the first act of inclusion — a territory cannot include people it refuses to see.

## Sub lesson 2 — Why places exclude

Places and decisions exclude by design and by habit: a building, a timetable, a rule or a custom can shut a door without anyone deciding to shut it.

That is good news hidden in a hard truth: what was built excluding can be rebuilt including — exclusion is made, so it can be unmade.

## Sub lesson 3 — Changing toward inclusion

Advancing toward an inclusive territory means changing both: the places — so all can enter and move — and the decisions — so all are heard in them.

The test of the change is simple and strict: ask the person who was left out whether the door is open now.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: advancing toward an inclusive territory means finding who is left out of places and decisions — and changing the places and the decisions. Give one real example from your own life or from the Chile you live in, and name the lesson's key words: inclusive, left out, territory. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: advancing toward an inclusive territory means finding who is left out of places and decisions — and changing the places and the decisions.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
