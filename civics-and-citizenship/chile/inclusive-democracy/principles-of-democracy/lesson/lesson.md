# Principles of Democracy

## Do it first

Write the word 'democracy' in the middle of a page. Around it, write every rule you think a fair shared life needs — who decides, whose word counts, what no one may do to another. This lesson gathers those rules into their proper name: the principles that guide democracy.

## Sub lesson 1 — The people's rule

The first principle: power comes from the people — governments hold it in trust, for a time, under rules the people can change.

Everything else in this course hangs on this nail: authority that does not come from the people is not democracy, whatever it calls itself.

## Sub lesson 2 — Equality and freedom

Democracy's principles join equality — each citizen's word counting as one — with freedom — to speak, meet, organise and choose without fear.

Equality without freedom is a silent sameness; freedom without equality is a loud few and a quiet many — democracy needs both at once.

## Sub lesson 3 — Respect for rights

The third guide: rights stand above the count — even a majority may not vote away a person's human rights.

That is why this unit is about inclusion: a democracy is judged by whether its principles reach the last citizen, not only the first.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: democracy is guided by principles — the people's rule, equality, freedom, and respect for rights — and a democracy is more inclusive the more those principles reach everyone. Give one real example from your own life or from the Chile you live in, and name the lesson's key words: principle, equality, freedom. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
