# Lesson plan — Principles of Democracy

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Chile · **Title:** Inclusive Democracy · **Lesson:** Principles of Democracy
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — Inclusive Democracy (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** democracy is guided by principles — the people's rule, equality, freedom, and respect for rights — and a democracy is more inclusive the more those principles reach everyone.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The people's rule** — The first principle: power comes from the people — governments hold it in trust, for a time, under rules the people can change.
2. **Equality and freedom** — Democracy's principles join equality — each citizen's word counting as one — with freedom — to speak, meet, organise and choose without fear.
3. **Respect for rights** — The third guide: rights stand above the count — even a majority may not vote away a person's human rights.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: democracy is guided by principles — the people's rule, equality, freedom, and respect for rights — and a democracy is more inclusive the more those principles reach everyone.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or town — or, for this book's lessons, from the public life of Chile they live in.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks democracy is only a counting of votes → voting is the machinery; the principles — the people's rule, equality, freedom, rights — are what the machinery is for.
- Go back to the one thing: democracy is guided by principles — the people's rule, equality, freedom, and respect for rights — and a democracy is more inclusive the more those principles reach everyone. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
