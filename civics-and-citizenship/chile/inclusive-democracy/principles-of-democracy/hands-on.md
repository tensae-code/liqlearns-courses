# Hands-on — Principles of Democracy

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write the word 'democracy' in the middle of a page. Around it, write every rule you think a fair shared life needs — who decides, whose word counts, what no one may do to another. This lesson gathers those rules into their proper name: the principles that guide democracy.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: democracy is guided by principles — the people's rule, equality, freedom, and respect for rights — and a democracy is more inclusive the more those principles reach everyone. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
