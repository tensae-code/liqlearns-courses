# Hands-on — The Economy and Public Policy

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Name one rule or service your town lives by that costs public money — a school, a road, a subsidy, a tax. Write who pays, who gains, and what would change without it. This lesson names that whole circle: the economy and public policy.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: the economy and public policies shape each other: the state answers economic life with policies, and those policies change economic life in turn. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
