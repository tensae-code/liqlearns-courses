# How to Participate in Chile's Democracy

## Do it first

Name one problem in your street or school that bothers many people. Write who could act on it, and one channel — a vote, a meeting, an organisation, a petition — by which people like you could press for it to be solved. This lesson walks those channels.

## Sub lesson 1 — The channels of participation

Participation has channels: voting and elections, local organisations, public meetings, consultations and the community's own bodies — each is a door into shared decisions.

Knowing the doors is the first step: a citizen who cannot name the channels cannot use them, however much they care.

## Sub lesson 2 — Using a channel well

A channel works when it is used well: inform yourself, join with others, state the problem clearly, and follow the matter until it is answered.

One voice is easy to file away; organised citizens, with a clear ask and patience, are how problems actually move.

## Sub lesson 3 — Participation solves problems

The unit's own question stands behind this lesson: participation matters because social problems are solved by the people who take part, not by waiting.

Every later lesson in this level — the gaps, the media, inclusion, work — is a field where this same taking-part is practised.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: a citizen participates in Chile's democracy through real channels — and participation is how social problems get solved. Give one real example from your own life or from the Chile you live in, and name the lesson's key words: participation, channel, consultation. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
