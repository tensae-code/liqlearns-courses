# Hands-on — Gaps to Overcome to Participate

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Think of two people in your town: one who finds it easy to take part in shared decisions, and one who finds it hard. Write what makes the difference. This lesson names those differences: the gaps that must be overcome to participate.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: participation is unequal until its gaps are overcome — gaps of access, of information, and of voice. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
