# Gaps to Overcome to Participate

## Do it first

Think of two people in your town: one who finds it easy to take part in shared decisions, and one who finds it hard. Write what makes the difference. This lesson names those differences: the gaps that must be overcome to participate.

## Sub lesson 1 — Gaps of access

Some citizens cannot reach the channels: distance, cost, time, disability or paperwork shut doors that stand open for others.

A channel that cannot be reached is, for that citizen, not a channel at all — access is the first gap to close.

## Sub lesson 2 — Gaps of information

Some citizens do not know: that a decision is coming, that a channel exists, or how to use it — decisions pass them by in silence.

Information is participation's fuel: without it, even an open door is invisible, and the uninformed citizen is governed without a word.

## Sub lesson 3 — Gaps of voice

Some citizens are heard less: their word carries less weight because of poverty, origin, gender, age or where they live.

Closing the voice gap is democracy keeping its own promise — that each citizen's word counts, not only the words of the already-powerful.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: participation is unequal until its gaps are overcome — gaps of access, of information, and of voice. Give one real example from your own life or from the Chile you live in, and name the lesson's key words: gap, access, voice. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: participation is unequal until its gaps are overcome — gaps of access, of information, and of voice.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
