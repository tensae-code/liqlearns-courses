# Development Models and Daily Life

## Do it first

Write your day in five lines: waking, eating, travelling, working or studying, resting. Beside each line, write one thing that had to be produced, carried, or paid for to make that line possible. This lesson follows those lines upward: to the model of development your whole country lives inside, and what it does to daily life and the climate.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a development model is

A development model is the way a country chooses to grow: what it produces, how it works and trades, and what it treats as wealth and as cost.

Every country lives inside some model, chosen or drifted into — the citizen's first step is to be able to name the one they live in.

## Sub lesson 2 — Models in daily life

The model reaches the kitchen table: the work available, the prices charged, the journeys taken and the goods on the shelf are the model, lived daily.

So a debate about models is not distant from life — it is a debate about next month's work, food and rent, conducted in larger words.

## Sub lesson 3 — Models and climate change

Models also meet the climate: how a country produces, travels and consumes shapes what it emits and how exposed it stands to a changing climate.

Daily life and climate are the model's two mirrors — the course ends where it began, with the citizen judging the shared life they actually live.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: development models — the ways a country chooses to grow — reach into daily life and into the climate: no model is only an idea. Give one real example from your own life or from the Chile you live in, and name the lesson's key words: development model, daily life, climate change. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
