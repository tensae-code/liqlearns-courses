# Hands-on — Development Models and Daily Life

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write your day in five lines: waking, eating, travelling, working or studying, resting. Beside each line, write one thing that had to be produced, carried, or paid for to make that line possible. This lesson follows those lines upward: to the model of development your whole country lives inside, and what it does to daily life and the climate.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: development models — the ways a country chooses to grow — reach into daily life and into the climate: no model is only an idea. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
