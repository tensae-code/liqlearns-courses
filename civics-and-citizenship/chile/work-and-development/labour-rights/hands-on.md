# Hands-on — Labour Rights

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write one day of work you know well — your own, or a family member's — hour by hour. Beside each hour, write what could go wrong for the worker in it: pay, safety, rest, treatment. This lesson names the protections standing against that list: labour rights, and how they are protected.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: labour rights protect the person at work — and they are protected in turn by law, by organisation, and by the citizen's watchfulness. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
