# Labour Rights

## Do it first

Write one day of work you know well — your own, or a family member's — hour by hour. Beside each hour, write what could go wrong for the worker in it: pay, safety, rest, treatment. This lesson names the protections standing against that list: labour rights, and how they are protected.

## Sub lesson 1 — What labour rights are

Labour rights are the worker's human rights at work: fair pay, safe conditions, rest, and treatment with dignity — the person does not stop being a person when work begins.

They belong to the worker as rights, not as gifts from an employer: a gift can be withdrawn at pleasure; a right can be claimed.

## Sub lesson 2 — How labour rights are protected

Labour rights are protected by law that sets the floor, by inspection that checks it, and by workers organising together to claim what the law promises.

Each protection covers the others' weakness: law without inspection sleeps, inspection without organisation hears only one side.

## Sub lesson 3 — When rights are not respected

When a labour right is not respected, the worker is not without road: the claim, the organisation and the institutions exist so the wrong can be named and answered.

That road is the same one this course met at its start — access to justice — now standing at the workplace door.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: labour rights protect the person at work — and they are protected in turn by law, by organisation, and by the citizen's watchfulness. Give one real example from your own life or from the Chile you live in, and name the lesson's key words: labour rights, inspection, organising. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
