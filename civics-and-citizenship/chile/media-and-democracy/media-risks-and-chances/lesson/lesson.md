# Media Risks and Chances for Democracy

## Do it first

Write one true thing you learned from a screen this week, and one thing you saw on a screen that turned out to be false or twisted. Keep both in front of you: this lesson is about exactly those two columns — risks and opportunities.

## Sub lesson 1 — The risks media and ICT bring

The risks are real: falsehood travels fast, voices can be bought or drowned, echo rooms shut out other views, and private life can be exposed.

A risk is not a reason to abandon the tool — it is a reason to learn its handling, as with any powerful tool.

## Sub lesson 2 — The opportunities they bring

The opportunities are just as real: any citizen can inform themselves, publish, organise and be heard far beyond their own street.

What once needed a printing press now fits in a pocket — that widening of who can speak is a democratic chance without precedent.

## Sub lesson 3 — The citizen's handling

The citizen's task is double: take the opportunities — inform yourself, speak, organise — and guard against the risks — check before believing or sharing.

Checking is the citizen's brake and steering at once: source, date, and whether anyone independent says the same.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: media and ICT bring democracy both risks and opportunities — the citizen's task is to take the chances and guard against the risks. Give one real example from your own life or from the Chile you live in, and name the lesson's key words: ICT, risk, opportunity. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
