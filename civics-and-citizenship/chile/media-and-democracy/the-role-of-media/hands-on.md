# Hands-on — The Role of the Media

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write down where yesterday's news reached you — a screen, a radio, a paper, a person. Then write one thing you learned about your country's public life from it. This lesson asks what that whole system is for: the role of the media in a democracy.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: the media's role in a democracy is to inform the citizens, watch those who hold power, and carry the public conversation. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
