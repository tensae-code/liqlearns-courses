# How the State Defends Our Rights

## Do it first

Think of one unfair thing you have seen — someone cheated, hurt, or shut out. Write what happened, and then write where that person could turn for justice. This lesson follows that road: how access to justice is guaranteed.

## Sub lesson 1 — Rights need a defender

Human rights belong to every person — and the state is charged with defending them, not as a favour but as its duty.

When the state defends rights, it defends its own citizens against harm — including harm done by the powerful, and harm done in the state's own name.

## Sub lesson 2 — Access to justice

Access to justice means a real, open road to claim a right: to be heard, to present your case, and to have it decided fairly.

The road must be open to everyone — a road only the rich or the well-connected can use is not access to justice.

## Sub lesson 3 — Justice in practice

When a right is harmed, justice in practice is the claim being heard and answered: the wrong examined, decided, and repaired as far as it can be.

That is the state's defence of human rights where it counts — not in speeches, but in what happens to the person who was harmed.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the state defends our human rights in practice by guaranteeing access to justice — a real way to claim a right when it is harmed. Give one real example from your own life or from the Chile you live in, and name the lesson's key words: human rights, access to justice, the state. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
