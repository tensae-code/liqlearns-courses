# Video script — How the State Defends Our Rights

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
The state defends our human rights in practice by guaranteeing access to justice — a real way to claim a right when it is harmed.

Human rights belong to every person — and the state is charged with defending them, not as a favour but as its duty.

When the state defends rights, it defends its own citizens against harm — including harm done by the powerful, and harm done in the state's own name.

Access to justice means a real, open road to claim a right: to be heard, to present your case, and to have it decided fairly.

The road must be open to everyone — a road only the rich or the well-connected can use is not access to justice.

When a right is harmed, justice in practice is the claim being heard and answered: the wrong examined, decided, and repaired as far as it can be.

That is the state's defence of human rights where it counts — not in speeches, but in what happens to the person who was harmed.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
