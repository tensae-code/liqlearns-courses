# Me and Human Rights

## Do it first

Write three short lines about yourself: one right you hold, one time you saw someone else's right harmed or respected, and one thing you could do if you saw a right being harmed. This lesson gives those three lines their names: holder, witness, defender.

## Sub lesson 1 — I hold rights

Every person holds human rights simply by being human — including you: they are yours before anyone grants them, and no one may take them away.

Holding rights also means knowing them: a right you cannot name is a right you cannot claim when you need it.

## Sub lesson 2 — I witness rights

Each of us also witnesses other people's rights — respected or harmed — in the family, at school, in the street, every day.

The witness is not outside the story: what we see, and whether we look away, is part of how rights live or die around us.

## Sub lesson 3 — I defend rights

The third place is defender: speaking, helping, reporting, and standing with the person whose right is harmed, in the ways open to us.

Defending rights does not need a title or an office — it begins with refusing to treat another person's harm as none of our business.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: each person stands in three places at once with human rights: holder of their own, witness of others', and defender of both. Give one real example from your own life or from the Chile you live in, and name the lesson's key words: holder, witness, defender. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: each person stands in three places at once with human rights: holder of their own, witness of others', and defender of both.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
