# Hands-on — The Rule of Law

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Think of a rule you must obey this week. Write down: who made it, who checks it is fair, who applies it if it is broken — and where the people's vote stands behind it. This lesson names that whole arrangement: the rule of law.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: in France the rule of law (l'État de droit) means law is made by the people's national representation, guarded by the Conseil constitutionnel, applied by the courts, and founded on the popular will expressed by vote — and it is not simply a synonym of democracy. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
