# Lesson plan — The Rule of Law

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — France · **Title:** Rights, Freedoms and Responsibility · **Lesson:** The Rule of Law
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — Rights, Freedoms and Responsibility (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** in France the rule of law (l'État de droit) means law is made by the people's national representation, guarded by the Conseil constitutionnel, applied by the courts, and founded on the popular will expressed by vote — and it is not simply a synonym of democracy.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — Rights, Freedoms and Responsibility — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Law made by national representation** — In France, law is made by the national representation — the elected representatives of the people — not by one person's order.
2. **Guarded and applied** — The Conseil constitutionnel guards the law against the Constitution, and the courts apply the law to real cases.
3. **Founded on the vote — and not a synonym of democracy** — The whole arrangement rests on the popular will expressed by vote, yet État de droit is not simply a synonym of democracy.

Gate pool: the prove-it check for each sub lesson is at most 5 questions, mixed with earlier parts, picked as the hardest from the rank right under the wanted sub lesson — drawn from this lesson's question files (questions/).

- Thinks the rule of law is just another word for democracy → democracy names who holds power (the people); the rule of law names how power is held inside law — made by representation, guarded, and applied by courts.
