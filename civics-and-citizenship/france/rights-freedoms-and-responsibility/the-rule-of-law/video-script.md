This lesson is about one thing.
In france the rule of law (l'état de droit) means law is made by the people's national representation, guarded by the conseil constitutionnel, applied by the courts, and founded on the popular will expressed by vote — and it is not simply a synonym of democracy.

Law made by national representation. In France, law is made by the national representation — the elected representatives of the people — not by one person's order. That is the first mark of the État de droit: rules come from a lawful maker, chosen by the people.

Guarded and applied. The Conseil constitutionnel guards the law against the Constitution, and the courts apply the law to real cases. A rule that no one guards can be bent; a rule that no court applies stays words on paper.

Founded on the vote — and not a synonym of democracy. The whole arrangement rests on the popular will expressed by vote, yet État de droit is not simply a synonym of democracy. Democracy says the people rule; the rule of law says even the people's power moves inside law.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
