# Lesson plan — Environmental Rights

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — France · **Title:** Rights, Freedoms and Responsibility · **Lesson:** Environmental Rights
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — Rights, Freedoms and Responsibility (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the environment carries rights and duties: every person has the right to live in a balanced environment that respects health, and a responsibility to protect it.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — Rights, Freedoms and Responsibility — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **A right to a healthy environment** — Environmental rights recognise that living in a balanced, health-respecting environment is a right, not a favour.
2. **Responsibility matches the right** — Rights over the environment come with responsibility: each person and each power must take care not to damage what all depend on.
3. **Rights held for the future** — Environmental rights look forward: today's choices bind the conditions in which future citizens will live.

Gate pool: the prove-it check for each sub lesson is at most 5 questions, mixed with earlier parts, picked as the hardest from the rank right under the wanted sub lesson — drawn from this lesson's question files (questions/).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: the environment carries rights and duties: every person has the right to live in a balanced environment that respects health, and a responsibility to protect it.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or town — or, for this book's lessons, from the public life of France they live in.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the learner's own life in France and the book's own teaching, as the book itself works pearl by pearl from real texts, cases and questions.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the environment is only scenery → the manual treats it as a matter of rights: a healthy environment is something persons can claim, and damaging it is something persons answer for.
- Go back to the one thing: the environment carries rights and duties: every person has the right to live in a balanced environment that respects health, and a responsibility to protect it. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
