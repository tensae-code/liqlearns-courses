# Environmental Rights

## Do it first

Name one place near you — a river, a wood, a clean street — that your health depends on. Write who can damage it and who must protect it. This lesson names both sides: environmental rights and responsibility.

## Sub lesson 1 — A right to a healthy environment

Environmental rights recognise that living in a balanced, health-respecting environment is a right, not a favour.

France carries this in its Charter for the Environment, joined to the constitutional block.

## Sub lesson 2 — Responsibility matches the right

Rights over the environment come with responsibility: each person and each power must take care not to damage what all depend on.

Prevention comes before repair — damage to shared nature is often irreversible.

## Sub lesson 3 — Rights held for the future

Environmental rights look forward: today's choices bind the conditions in which future citizens will live.

That is why the environment stands in the axis of rights and responsibility together.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the environment carries rights and duties: every person has the right to live in a balanced environment that respects health, and a responsibility to protect it. Give one real example from your own life or from the France you live in, and name the lesson's key words: environmental rights, responsibility, environment. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** droits environnementaux — say the French word aloud once; in France's own course the French term is the name citizens actually use.

3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
