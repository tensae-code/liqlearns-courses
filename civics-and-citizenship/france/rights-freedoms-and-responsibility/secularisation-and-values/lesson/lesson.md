# Secularisation and the Hierarchy of Values

## Do it first

Name one rule of daily life that once came from religion and now comes from law or custom — school holidays, marriage, Sunday rest. This lesson studies that long change: secularisation.

## Sub lesson 1 — What secularisation is

Secularisation names the process by which religious authority over society's life weakens over time.

Beliefs remain, but they stop organising the whole of common life.

## Sub lesson 2 — Values re-ordered

As secularisation proceeds, the hierarchy of values — what a society puts first — is debated and decided socially, not handed down by one religion.

Different citizens rank values differently, and the ranking itself becomes a public question.

## Sub lesson 3 — Living plural

In a secularised, plural society, the shared frame must hold for believers of every religion and for non-believers alike.

That is why this chapter stands in the rights axis beside laïcité.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: secularisation is the long change by which religion's place in society shrinks and values once ordered by religion come to be ordered by society itself — raising the question of how a plural society ranks its values. Give one real example from your own life or from the France you live in, and name the lesson's key words: secularisation, hierarchy of values, plural society. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** sécularisation — say the French word aloud once; in France's own course the French term is the name citizens actually use.
