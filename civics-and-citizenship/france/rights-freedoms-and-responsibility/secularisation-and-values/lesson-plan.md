# Lesson plan — Secularisation and the Hierarchy of Values

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — France · **Title:** Rights, Freedoms and Responsibility · **Lesson:** Secularisation and the Hierarchy of Values
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — Rights, Freedoms and Responsibility (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** secularisation is the long change by which religion's place in society shrinks and values once ordered by religion come to be ordered by society itself — raising the question of how a plural society ranks its values.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — Rights, Freedoms and Responsibility — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What secularisation is** — Secularisation names the process by which religious authority over society's life weakens over time.
2. **Values re-ordered** — As secularisation proceeds, the hierarchy of values — what a society puts first — is debated and decided socially, not handed down by one religion.
3. **Living plural** — In a secularised, plural society, the shared frame must hold for believers of every religion and for non-believers alike.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks secularisation is the same word as laïcité → laïcité is France's legal principle of separation; secularisation is the wider social process — religion's declining hold over customs and values — that the principle answers.
- Go back to the one thing: secularisation is the long change by which religion's place in society shrinks and values once ordered by religion come to be ordered by society itself — raising the question of how a plural society ranks its values.
