# Freedom of Information and Pluralism

## Do it first

Take one news story and find it told in two different outlets. Write one thing each telling adds or leaves out. This lesson names what you just used: freedom of information and pluralism.

## Sub lesson 1 — Free information

Citizens must be able to seek and receive information freely — without that, every other freedom is blind.

Voting, debating and judging all depend on knowing.

## Sub lesson 2 — Pluralism of the media

Pluralism means a real diversity of media and viewpoints, so no single power owns the public's picture of the world.

Different outlets checking each other is part of how errors surface.

## Sub lesson 3 — A condition of democracy

The manual teaches free information and media pluralism as conditions of democracy, not decorations on it.

Where information is monopolised, democratic choice becomes theatre.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: freedom of information and a plurality of media are conditions of democracy — citizens can only choose freely if they can learn freely from more than one source. Give one real example from your own life or from the France you live in, and name the lesson's key words: information, pluralism, media, democracy. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** liberté d'information — say the French word aloud once; in France's own course the French term is the name citizens actually use.
