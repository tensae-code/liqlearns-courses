# Hands-on — Freedom of Information and Pluralism

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Take one news story and find it told in two different outlets. Write one thing each telling adds or leaves out. This lesson names what you just used: freedom of information and pluralism.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: freedom of information and a plurality of media are conditions of democracy — citizens can only choose freely if they can learn freely from more than one source. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
