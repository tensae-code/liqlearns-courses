The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

This lesson is about one thing.
Laïcité is france's democratic principle of separation between religions and the state — another name for tolerance — set by the 1905 separation law, written into the constitutional texts, and applied at school.

A democratic principle — another name for tolerance. Laïcité means the state recognises no religion and favours none, so every belief can exist freely and equally. The manual teaches it as a democratic principle: tolerance organised by law.

The 1905 law and the constitutional texts. The law of 1905 separated the churches and the state, and laïcité stands in France's constitutional texts as a principle of the Republic. Separation protects both sides: religions are free of the state, and the state is free of religions.

Laïcité at school, and religious signs. At school laïcité means a neutral space where pupils of every belief learn together; the question of religious signs is treated inside that frame. The school chapter of the manual works the principle where pupils actually meet it.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
