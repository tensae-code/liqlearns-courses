# Hands-on — Laïcité and Pluralism

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
List three beliefs held by people you know. Then write what the state may and may not do about any of them in France. This lesson names the principle that answers: laïcité.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: laïcité is France's democratic principle of separation between religions and the state — another name for tolerance — set by the 1905 separation law, written into the constitutional texts, and applied at school. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
