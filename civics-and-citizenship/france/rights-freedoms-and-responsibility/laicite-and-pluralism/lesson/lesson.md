# Laïcité and Pluralism

## Do it first

List three beliefs held by people you know. Then write what the state may and may not do about any of them in France. This lesson names the principle that answers: laïcité.

## Sub lesson 1 — A democratic principle — another name for tolerance

Laïcité means the state recognises no religion and favours none, so every belief can exist freely and equally.

The manual teaches it as a democratic principle: tolerance organised by law.

## Sub lesson 2 — The 1905 law and the constitutional texts

The law of 1905 separated the churches and the state, and laïcité stands in France's constitutional texts as a principle of the Republic.

Separation protects both sides: religions are free of the state, and the state is free of religions.

## Sub lesson 3 — Laïcité at school, and religious signs

At school laïcité means a neutral space where pupils of every belief learn together; the question of religious signs is treated inside that frame.

The school chapter of the manual works the principle where pupils actually meet it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: laïcité is France's democratic principle of separation between religions and the state — another name for tolerance — set by the 1905 separation law, written into the constitutional texts, and applied at school. Give one real example from your own life or from the France you live in, and name the lesson's key words: laïcité, pluralism, 1905 law, school, tolerance. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** laïcité — say the French word aloud once; in France's own course the French term is the name citizens actually use.
