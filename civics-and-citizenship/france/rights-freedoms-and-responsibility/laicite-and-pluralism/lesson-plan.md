# Lesson plan — Laïcité and Pluralism

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — France · **Title:** Rights, Freedoms and Responsibility · **Lesson:** Laïcité and Pluralism
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — Rights, Freedoms and Responsibility (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** laïcité is France's democratic principle of separation between religions and the state — another name for tolerance — set by the 1905 separation law, written into the constitutional texts, and applied at school.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **A democratic principle — another name for tolerance** — Laïcité means the state recognises no religion and favours none, so every belief can exist freely and equally.
2. **The 1905 law and the constitutional texts** — The law of 1905 separated the churches and the state, and laïcité stands in France's constitutional texts as a principle of the Republic.
3. **Laïcité at school, and religious signs** — At school laïcité means a neutral space where pupils of every belief learn together; the question of religious signs is treated inside that frame.

- Thinks laïcité means religion is banned → laïcité separates religions from the state so that all beliefs are equally free; the state is neutral, and at school that neutrality protects every pupil's freedom of conscience.
