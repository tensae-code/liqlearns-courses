# Development and Ecological Transition

## Do it first

List two things your town needs more of — housing, transport, jobs — and one environmental cost each could carry. This lesson asks how both columns are held together: development and transition.

## Sub lesson 1 — Development as a rights question

How a society develops decides who enjoys its rights — to work, to housing, to a healthy environment — and who is left out.

Development is therefore a civic question, not only an economic one.

## Sub lesson 2 — The ecological transition

Transition means changing energy, transport, building and production so development respects environmental limits.

It is a change of model, carried over years, not a single measure.

## Sub lesson 3 — Responsibility shared

Citizens, businesses and public powers each carry part of the transition — the axis's theme of responsibility applied to the planet.

The chapter closes the Seconde axis where it began: rights exercised responsibly.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: development must now answer an ecological question: how a society grows and organises its life without destroying the environment its citizens have rights in — the ecological transition. Give one real example from your own life or from the France you live in, and name the lesson's key words: development, ecological transition, responsibility. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** transition écologique — say the French word aloud once; in France's own course the French term is the name citizens actually use.
