# Lesson plan — Development and Ecological Transition

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — France · **Title:** Rights, Freedoms and Responsibility · **Lesson:** Development and Ecological Transition
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — Rights, Freedoms and Responsibility (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** development must now answer an ecological question: how a society grows and organises its life without destroying the environment its citizens have rights in — the ecological transition.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — Rights, Freedoms and Responsibility — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Development as a rights question** — How a society develops decides who enjoys its rights — to work, to housing, to a healthy environment — and who is left out.
2. **The ecological transition** — Transition means changing energy, transport, building and production so development respects environmental limits.
3. **Responsibility shared** — Citizens, businesses and public powers each carry part of the transition — the axis's theme of responsibility applied to the planet.

Gate pool: the prove-it check for each sub lesson is at most 5 questions, mixed with earlier parts, picked as the hardest from the rank right under the wanted sub lesson — drawn from this lesson's question files (questions/).

3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks development and ecology are simply enemies → the transition is precisely the work of joining them: developing differently, so that progress stops consuming the conditions of future life.
- Go back to the one thing: development must now answer an ecological question: how a society grows and organises its life without destroying the environment its citizens have rights in — the ecological transition. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
