# Public Order

## Do it first

Picture a busy street, a market and a demonstration sharing one town on one day. Write down what has to hold so that all three can happen safely. This lesson names that frame: public order.

## Sub lesson 1 — Freedoms need a frame

Each person's freedom ends where it would destroy another's — public order is the shared frame that keeps freedoms from colliding.

Without the frame, the loudest or strongest freedom would swallow the rest.

## Sub lesson 2 — Safety and calm as common goods

Public order protects the safety of people and property and the calm of shared places, so daily life can go on.

Streets, schools and gatherings can only be used freely when they are safe.

## Sub lesson 3 — Limits inside the frame

When a freedom is limited for public order, the limit itself must stay inside the law — the frame binds the authorities too.

Public order is a legal idea, not a blank permission to forbid.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: public order (l'ordre public) is the frame within which freedoms are exercised — the shared safety and calm that let everyone's freedom exist at the same time. Give one real example from your own life or from the France you live in, and name the lesson's key words: public order, ordre public, freedoms, frame. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** ordre public — say the French word aloud once; in France's own course the French term is the name citizens actually use.
