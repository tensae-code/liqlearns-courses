# Lesson plan — Freedom of Expression

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — France · **Title:** Rights, Freedoms and Responsibility · **Lesson:** Freedom of Expression
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — Rights, Freedoms and Responsibility (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** freedom of expression is the freedom to say, write and publish what one thinks — a foundation of democracy — and it has limits set by law.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — Rights, Freedoms and Responsibility — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The freedom and its foundations** — Freedom of expression lets every person speak, write and publish their thoughts — democracy cannot work if citizens cannot say what they think.
2. **Why democracy needs it** — Elections, debate and a free press all presuppose that opinions can be expressed and criticised in public.
3. **Its limits** — The law limits expression where it attacks others — incitement to hatred, defamation and similar harms are not protected by the freedom.

Gate pool: the prove-it check for each sub lesson is at most 5 questions, mixed with earlier parts, picked as the hardest from the rank right under the wanted sub lesson — drawn from this lesson's question files (questions/).

1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: freedom of expression is the freedom to say, write and publish what one thinks — a foundation of democracy — and it has limits set by law.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or town — or, for this book's lessons, from the public life of France they live in.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.
