# Hands-on — Freedom of Expression

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write one opinion you hold that others around you disagree with. Then write what would happen to public discussion if no one were allowed to say it. This lesson names the freedom that protects your sentence: freedom of expression.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: freedom of expression is the freedom to say, write and publish what one thinks — a foundation of democracy — and it has limits set by law. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
