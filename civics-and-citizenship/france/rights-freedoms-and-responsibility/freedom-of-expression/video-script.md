The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

This lesson is about one thing.
Freedom of expression is the freedom to say, write and publish what one thinks — a foundation of democracy — and it has limits set by law.

The freedom and its foundations. Freedom of expression lets every person speak, write and publish their thoughts — democracy cannot work if citizens cannot say what they think. It is founded on the idea that ideas must meet openly for a people to judge and choose.

Why democracy needs it. Elections, debate and a free press all presuppose that opinions can be expressed and criticised in public. Silence imposed from above is the first sign that citizens have become subjects.

Its limits. The law limits expression where it attacks others — incitement to hatred, defamation and similar harms are not protected by the freedom. A limit must itself be lawful and proportionate; it is the exception, argued case by case.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
