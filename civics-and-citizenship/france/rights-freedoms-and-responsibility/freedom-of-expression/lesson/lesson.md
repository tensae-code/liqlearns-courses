# Freedom of Expression

## Do it first

Write one opinion you hold that others around you disagree with. Then write what would happen to public discussion if no one were allowed to say it. This lesson names the freedom that protects your sentence: freedom of expression.

## Sub lesson 1 — The freedom and its foundations

Freedom of expression lets every person speak, write and publish their thoughts — democracy cannot work if citizens cannot say what they think.

It is founded on the idea that ideas must meet openly for a people to judge and choose.

## Sub lesson 2 — Why democracy needs it

Elections, debate and a free press all presuppose that opinions can be expressed and criticised in public.

Silence imposed from above is the first sign that citizens have become subjects.

## Sub lesson 3 — Its limits

The law limits expression where it attacks others — incitement to hatred, defamation and similar harms are not protected by the freedom.

A limit must itself be lawful and proportionate; it is the exception, argued case by case.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: freedom of expression is the freedom to say, write and publish what one thinks — a foundation of democracy — and it has limits set by law. Give one real example from your own life or from the France you live in, and name the lesson's key words: freedom of expression, liberté d'expression, limits, democracy. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** liberté d'expression — say the French word aloud once; in France's own course the French term is the name citizens actually use.
