# Fighting Discrimination

## Do it first

Think of a door — a job, a school, a club — that opens for some and not for others, for reasons that have nothing to do with ability. This lesson names that wrong and its answer: fighting discrimination, building an inclusive society.

## Sub lesson 1 — What discrimination is

Discrimination is unequal treatment based on who a person is — origin, sex, disability, beliefs and the other grounds the law names.

It denies equal citizenship in practice while promising it on paper.

## Sub lesson 2 — Forbidden by law

The law forbids discrimination and gives victims ways to answer it.

Naming the grounds in law is how a republic makes equality enforceable.

## Sub lesson 3 — An inclusive society

An inclusive society removes barriers — in school, work and public life — so every person can take part fully.

Inclusion is the positive side of the same chapter: not only refusing exclusion, but building access.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: discrimination — treating a person unequally because of who they are — is forbidden by law, and building an inclusive society means removing the barriers that keep people out. Give one real example from your own life or from the France you live in, and name the lesson's key words: discrimination, inclusive society, equality. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** discriminations — say the French word aloud once; in France's own course the French term is the name citizens actually use.
