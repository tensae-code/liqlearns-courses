# Lesson plan — Fighting Discrimination

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — France · **Title:** Cohesion and Diversity in a Democracy · **Lesson:** Fighting Discrimination
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — Cohesion and Diversity in a Democracy (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** discrimination — treating a person unequally because of who they are — is forbidden by law, and building an inclusive society means removing the barriers that keep people out.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — Cohesion and Diversity in a Democracy — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What discrimination is** — Discrimination is unequal treatment based on who a person is — origin, sex, disability, beliefs and the other grounds the law names.
2. **Forbidden by law** — The law forbids discrimination and gives victims ways to answer it.
3. **An inclusive society** — An inclusive society removes barriers — in school, work and public life — so every person can take part fully.

Gate pool: the prove-it check for each sub lesson is at most 5 questions, mixed with earlier parts, picked as the hardest from the rank right under the wanted sub lesson — drawn from this lesson's question files (questions/).

3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks discrimination is only a matter of bad manners → discrimination is a legal wrong: unequal treatment on forbidden grounds is prohibited, and inclusion requires changing practices, not only attitudes.
- Go back to the one thing: discrimination — treating a person unequally because of who they are — is forbidden by law, and building an inclusive society means removing the barriers that keep people out. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
