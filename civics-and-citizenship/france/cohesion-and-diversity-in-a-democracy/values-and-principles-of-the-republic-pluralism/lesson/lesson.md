# Pluralism

## Do it first

Name three opinions held in your class or street that cannot all be put into practice at once. Write how the group still lives together. This lesson names the principle that makes that possible: pluralism.

## Sub lesson 1 — Diversity as a fact and a principle

Opinions, beliefs and ways of life are genuinely diverse — pluralism takes that diversity as a principle to protect, not a problem to erase.

A republic of one opinion would be a republic of one person.

## Sub lesson 2 — No imposed opinion

The Republic imposes no official opinion or belief; public powers stay neutral between citizens' convictions.

Neutrality of the state is what keeps pluralism safe — the laïcité thread runs here too.

## Sub lesson 3 — Coexistence organised

Pluralism needs rules of coexistence: freedoms for each, limits where one freedom would crush another, and debate to settle the rest.

Diversity plus common rules — that pairing is the Première axis in one line.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: pluralism — the real diversity of opinions, beliefs and ways of life — is a principle of the Republic: the Republic does not impose one opinion, it organises the coexistence of many. Give one real example from your own life or from the France you live in, and name the lesson's key words: pluralism, Republic, opinions, beliefs, coexistence. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** pluralisme — say the French word aloud once; in France's own course the French term is the name citizens actually use.
