# Hands-on — Pluralism

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Name three opinions held in your class or street that cannot all be put into practice at once. Write how the group still lives together. This lesson names the principle that makes that possible: pluralism.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: pluralism — the real diversity of opinions, beliefs and ways of life — is a principle of the Republic: the Republic does not impose one opinion, it organises the coexistence of many. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
