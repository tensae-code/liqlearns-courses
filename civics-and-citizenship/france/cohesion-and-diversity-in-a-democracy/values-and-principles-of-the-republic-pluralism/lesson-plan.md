# Lesson plan — Pluralism

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — France · **Title:** Cohesion and Diversity in a Democracy · **Lesson:** Pluralism
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — Cohesion and Diversity in a Democracy (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** pluralism — the real diversity of opinions, beliefs and ways of life — is a principle of the Republic: the Republic does not impose one opinion, it organises the coexistence of many.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Diversity as a fact and a principle** — Opinions, beliefs and ways of life are genuinely diverse — pluralism takes that diversity as a principle to protect, not a problem to erase.
2. **No imposed opinion** — The Republic imposes no official opinion or belief; public powers stay neutral between citizens' convictions.
3. **Coexistence organised** — Pluralism needs rules of coexistence: freedoms for each, limits where one freedom would crush another, and debate to settle the rest.

Gate pool: the prove-it check for each sub lesson is at most 5 questions, mixed with earlier parts, picked as the hardest from the rank right under the wanted sub lesson — drawn from this lesson's question files (questions/).

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks pluralism means everyone must agree in the end → pluralism is the opposite: disagreement is permanent and legitimate, and the Republic's work is to organise it fairly, not to end it.
- Go back to the one thing: pluralism — the real diversity of opinions, beliefs and ways of life — is a principle of the Republic: the Republic does not impose one opinion, it organises the coexistence of many. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
