# Defence and National Security

## Do it first

Write down who protects your country, and what an ordinary citizen's part in that protection could be. This lesson treats defence as a citizen's subject, not only a soldier's: defence and national security.

## Sub lesson 1 — Why a republic defends itself

Defence protects the territory, the population and the Republic's independence — the conditions in which civic life exists at all.

Without security, every other right stands on sand.

## Sub lesson 2 — A shared responsibility

National security is shared: the armed forces, public institutions and citizens contribute, each in their place.

France marks this bond in the citizen's path — the defence and citizenship day is its best-known sign.

## Sub lesson 3 — Defence inside democracy

In a republic, defence stays under democratic authority and law — protection never suspends citizenship.

The chapter holds both ideas at once: strong protection, democratic control.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: national defence and security are a shared civic responsibility: the Republic protects its territory and people, and citizens — through national service and civic duties — take their part in that protection. Give one real example from your own life or from the France you live in, and name the lesson's key words: defence, national security, civic responsibility. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** défense et sécurité nationale — say the French word aloud once; in France's own course the French term is the name citizens actually use.
