# Video script — Nationality and Citizenship

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
French nationality is the legal bond to france — acquired by birth or by naturalisation — and citizenship is the civic life that flows from it: rights, duties, the possible loss of civic rights, and the debated question of what being french means.

Acquiring nationality. French nationality is acquired in set ways — by birth in the cases the law defines, and by naturalisation for those who ask and qualify. The law, not opinion, decides the bond.

Citizenship: rights, duties, and their loss. The citizen holds civic rights — voting first among them — and owes civic duties; civic rights can be lost in the cases the law provides. Rights and duties are the two faces of the same membership.

What being French means — a debated question. Beyond the legal bond, what being French means is debated: language, values, history, choice. The manual leaves the debate open — it is a question citizens argue, not a definition imposed.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
