# Hands-on — Nationality and Citizenship

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write down what makes someone French, as far as you know: a birthplace, a parent, a paper, a choice? This lesson separates the legal answer from the debated one: nationality and citizenship.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: French nationality is the legal bond to France — acquired by birth or by naturalisation — and citizenship is the civic life that flows from it: rights, duties, the possible loss of civic rights, and the debated question of what being French means. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
