# Decentralisation

## Do it first

Name one decision taken about your street by your town, and one taken in Paris. This lesson explains why both levels exist and how the nearer one works: decentralisation.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Republic close to citizens

Decentralisation brings public decisions closer to the people they concern.

A commune knows its streets in a way a distant ministry cannot.

## Sub lesson 2 — Communes, départements, régions

France is organised in territorial levels — the commune, the département and the région — each with its elected council and its own competences.

The levels stack; each does what it is best placed to do.

## Sub lesson 3 — Elected and responsible

Local powers are exercised by elected councils, answerable to local voters — decentralisation is democracy at walking distance.

It widens citizenship: the citizen meets public power first in their own town.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: decentralisation organises the Republic close to its citizens: communes, départements and régions hold real powers of their own, decided by elected local councils. Give one real example from your own life or from the France you live in, and name the lesson's key words: decentralisation, commune, département, région. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** décentralisation — say the French word aloud once; in France's own course the French term is the name citizens actually use.
