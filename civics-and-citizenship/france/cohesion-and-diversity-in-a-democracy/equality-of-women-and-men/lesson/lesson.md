# Equality of Women and Men

## Do it first

List three jobs and three public offices, and ask who usually holds them around you. This lesson compares that picture with the Republic's promise: equality of women and men.

## Sub lesson 1 — Equality in law

Women and men hold the same rights in French law — the principle is constitutional and complete on paper.

The chapter starts from that proclaimed equality.

## Sub lesson 2 — Equality in work

In working life the facts lag: pay gaps and unequal careers persist, and law and policy push against them.

Measuring the gap is the first step the chapter teaches.

## Sub lesson 3 — Equality in political life

Political equality advanced by deliberate rules — parity in elections is France's named instrument for sharing seats.

Representation is where the Première axis's cohesion is tested in public.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: equality between women and men is a principle of the Republic that must hold in law, in work and in political life — and the distance between the principle and the facts is the chapter's subject. Give one real example from your own life or from the France you live in, and name the lesson's key words: equality, women and men, parity, work. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** égalité femmes-hommes — say the French word aloud once; in France's own course the French term is the name citizens actually use.
