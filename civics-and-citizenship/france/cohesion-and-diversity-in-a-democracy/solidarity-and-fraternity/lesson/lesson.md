# Solidarity and Fraternity

## Do it first

Name one time this month when someone carried a cost that was not theirs — a shared meal, a collection, a tax-funded service. This lesson names that bond: solidarity and fraternity.

## Sub lesson 1 — Fraternity, the motto's third word

Liberty and equality are joined by fraternity: citizens are not strangers but members of one another's lives.

The motto's three words stand or fall together.

## Sub lesson 2 — Solidarity organised

Public solidarity — social protection, public services, help in hardship — is fraternity organised by law and funded in common.

It turns a value into a system that holds when goodwill alone would fail.

## Sub lesson 3 — Solidarity practised

Everyday solidarity — associations, mutual help, volunteering — is the same value in citizens' own hands.

The chapter closes the Première axis: cohesion is fraternity at work.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: solidarity and fraternity — the third word of the Republic's motto — mean citizens carry one another: through public solidarity organised by law and through the everyday fraternity of people who treat each other as equals. Give one real example from your own life or from the France you live in, and name the lesson's key words: solidarity, fraternity, motto, cohesion. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** solidarité et fraternité — say the French word aloud once; in France's own course the French term is the name citizens actually use.
