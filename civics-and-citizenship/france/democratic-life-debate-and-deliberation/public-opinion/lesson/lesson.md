# Public Opinion

## Do it first

Ask ten people one question and announce the result as 'what everyone thinks'. This lesson asks what you actually measured: public opinion.

## Sub lesson 1 — Defining public opinion

Public opinion is the shared judgement attributed to a public on the questions of common life.

It is a real force — governments watch it — yet no one has ever seen it directly.

## Sub lesson 2 — Polls: thermometers or distorting mirrors

Polls measure opinion and can distort it: wording, samples and timing bend the result.

The critical citizen reads a poll the way they read an argument — asking how it was made.

## Sub lesson 3 — Networks, conformism, and a world opinion

Social networks now factory-make opinion at speed; conformism pressures each person to think with the crowd; and opinion increasingly forms at world scale.

The chapter ends asking whether a world public opinion is being born.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: public opinion is what a society is taken to think — measured by polls that can mirror or distort it, increasingly made on social networks, and always shadowed by conformism. Give one real example from your own life or from the France you live in, and name the lesson's key words: public opinion, polls, social networks, conformism. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** opinion publique — say the French word aloud once; in France's own course the French term is the name citizens actually use.
