# The Digital Society

## Do it first

Take one striking claim you saw online this week. Before believing it, write three checks you could run on it. This lesson is about the world that claim travels in: the digital society.

## Sub lesson 1 — Debate moves online

Public discussion now happens largely online: fast, wide, and open to everyone — its strengths and its dangers come from the same openness.

The Terminale axis's discussion culture must now survive in this arena.

## Sub lesson 2 — Disinformation and conspiracy thinking

Disinformation is false content spread deliberately; conspiracy thinking reads every event as a hidden plot's proof.

Both exploit the wish for a simple story; both corrode shared facts.

## Sub lesson 3 — Keeping critical distance

The defence is method: check the source, compare outlets, wait before sharing — critical distance as a daily habit.

A democracy can debate anything except a world where facts themselves dissolve.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the digital society moves public debate online — where disinformation and conspiracy thinking spread fast, and the citizen's defence is critical distance: checking sources before believing or sharing. Give one real example from your own life or from the France you live in, and name the lesson's key words: digital society, disinformation, conspiracy, critical distance. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** société numérique — say the French word aloud once; in France's own course the French term is the name citizens actually use.
