# Lesson plan — International Law

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — France · **Title:** Democratic Life: Debate and Deliberation · **Lesson:** International Law
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — Democratic Life: Debate and Deliberation (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** international law is the frame within which states deliberate and bind themselves — treaties, organisations and rules that no single state commands, yet all can invoke.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — Democratic Life: Debate and Deliberation — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **A frame between states** — International law organises relations between states: treaties, diplomacy and international organisations.
2. **Made by consent, kept by interest** — States make international law by agreeing to it, and keep it because breaking faith isolates and costs them.
3. **The citizen's horizon widens** — Rights and protections also live at this level — the international texts stand behind many national rights.

Gate pool: the prove-it check for each sub lesson is at most 5 questions, mixed with earlier parts, picked as the hardest from the rank right under the wanted sub lesson — drawn from this lesson's question files (questions/).

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks international law is weak because no world police enforces it → its force is different: states bind themselves by treaty, judge each other by shared rules, and pay real costs for breaking them.
- Go back to the one thing: international law is the frame within which states deliberate and bind themselves — treaties, organisations and rules that no single state commands, yet all can invoke. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
