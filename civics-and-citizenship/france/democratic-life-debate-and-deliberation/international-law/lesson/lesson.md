# International Law

## Do it first

Imagine two countries disputing a border with no law above either of them. Write what could settle it besides force. This lesson names that frame: international law.

## Sub lesson 1 — A frame between states

International law organises relations between states: treaties, diplomacy and international organisations.

It is deliberation continued by legal means — this axis's theme at world scale.

## Sub lesson 2 — Made by consent, kept by interest

States make international law by agreeing to it, and keep it because breaking faith isolates and costs them.

Its authority is consensual, which is both its limit and its legitimacy.

## Sub lesson 3 — The citizen's horizon widens

Rights and protections also live at this level — the international texts stand behind many national rights.

The course's last legal layer: from the commune to the world, law frames deliberation at every level.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: international law is the frame within which states deliberate and bind themselves — treaties, organisations and rules that no single state commands, yet all can invoke. Give one real example from your own life or from the France you live in, and name the lesson's key words: international law, treaties, states, deliberation. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** droit international — say the French word aloud once; in France's own course the French term is the name citizens actually use.
