# The Culture of Organised Discussion

## Do it first

Take one question your group disagrees on. Write the rules that would let you discuss it without shouting or splitting. This lesson is about exactly those rules: the culture of organised discussion.

## Sub lesson 1 — What discussing is, and its ethics

Discussion is the exchange of reasons under shared rules: each speaks, each listens, arguments answer arguments.

Its ethics — honesty, listening, no force but reasons — is what separates debate from quarrel.

## Sub lesson 2 — Pluralism, parties and the duty to vote

Discussion lives inside pluralism: political parties, named in the 1958 Constitution, organise the choices; the citizen's part ends in the duty to vote.

Parties turn millions of opinions into decidable options.

## Sub lesson 3 — Debating the hardest questions

The manual practises discussion on real hard cases — the death penalty, the ecological question — where values collide.

If discussion can hold there, it can hold anywhere in democratic life.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: democracy decides by organised discussion: citizens who know what discussing is, keep the ethics of discussion, hear plural views, and vote — debating even the hardest questions, from the death penalty to the ecological question. Give one real example from your own life or from the France you live in, and name the lesson's key words: organised discussion, debate ethics, parties, vote. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** discussion organisée — say the French word aloud once; in France's own course the French term is the name citizens actually use.
