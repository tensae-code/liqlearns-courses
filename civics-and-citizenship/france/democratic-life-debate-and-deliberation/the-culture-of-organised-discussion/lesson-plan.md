# Lesson plan — The Culture of Organised Discussion

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — France · **Title:** Democratic Life: Debate and Deliberation · **Lesson:** The Culture of Organised Discussion
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — Democratic Life: Debate and Deliberation (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** democracy decides by organised discussion: citizens who know what discussing is, keep the ethics of discussion, hear plural views, and vote — debating even the hardest questions, from the death penalty to the ecological question.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What discussing is, and its ethics** — Discussion is the exchange of reasons under shared rules: each speaks, each listens, arguments answer arguments.
2. **Pluralism, parties and the duty to vote** — Discussion lives inside pluralism: political parties, named in the 1958 Constitution, organise the choices; the citizen's part ends in the duty to vote.
3. **Debating the hardest questions** — The manual practises discussion on real hard cases — the death penalty, the ecological question — where values collide.

Gate pool: the prove-it check for each sub lesson is at most 5 questions, mixed with earlier parts, picked as the hardest from the rank right under the wanted sub lesson — drawn from this lesson's question files (questions/).

- Thinks discussion is just talking until someone wins → organised discussion has an ethics: listen, answer the argument not the person, accept the decision procedure — debate is a discipline, not a contest of volume.
