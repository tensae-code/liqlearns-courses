# Active Citizenship

## Do it first

Name one public office held by someone from a background like yours, and one you could imagine holding. This lesson is about the step from voter to actor: active citizenship.

## Sub lesson 1 — Engagement as a real activity

Political engagement — joining, campaigning, standing, serving — is citizenship in its active form.

Voting is its minimum, not its whole.

## Sub lesson 2 — Knowing the offices

The active citizen knows the offices and what each does: mayor, deputy, senator, minister.

You cannot aim at, judge or question an office you cannot name.

## Sub lesson 3 — Open to all, against professionalisation

Engagement tends to professionalise — a political class forms — and democracy answers by keeping responsibilities open to all citizens.

The chapter's closing question: who gets to be active, and how the door stays open.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: active citizenship is citizenship practised: engaging in political life, knowing the offices citizens hold — mayor, deputy, senator, minister — and keeping those responsibilities open to all, even as engagement professionalises. Give one real example from your own life or from the France you live in, and name the lesson's key words: active citizenship, engagement, mayor, deputy, senator, minister. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** citoyenneté active — say the French word aloud once; in France's own course the French term is the name citizens actually use.
