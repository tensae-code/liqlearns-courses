# Lesson plan — Legitimacy, Consensus and Majority

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — France · **Title:** Democratic Life: Debate and Deliberation · **Lesson:** Legitimacy, Consensus and Majority
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — Democratic Life: Debate and Deliberation (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a democratic decision binds everyone when it is legitimate — taken by the right procedure — and majority rule, the usual procedure, works only when the minority accepts it and consensus is sought where it can be found.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Legitimacy: why decisions bind** — A decision is legitimate when it comes from the authority and procedure citizens recognise as rightful.
2. **Majority rule and the minority** — Democracy decides by majority — and protects the minority, whose turn to persuade and win may come at the next vote.
3. **Consensus where it can be found** — Some decisions seek consensus — the widest agreement possible — because near-unanimous decisions hold longest.

Gate pool: the prove-it check for each sub lesson is at most 5 questions, mixed with earlier parts, picked as the hardest from the rank right under the wanted sub lesson — drawn from this lesson's question files (questions/).

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks majority rule means the majority is always right → majority is a decision procedure, not a truth machine: it binds because it is legitimate — fair procedure, minority rights protected, decision open to revision.
