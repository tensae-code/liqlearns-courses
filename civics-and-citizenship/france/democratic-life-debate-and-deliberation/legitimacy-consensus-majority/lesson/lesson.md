# Legitimacy, Consensus and Majority

## Do it first

Your group votes 6–4 on a plan you voted against. Write why you would — or would not — accept the result as binding. This lesson names the ideas inside your answer: legitimacy, consensus and majority.

## Sub lesson 1 — Legitimacy: why decisions bind

A decision is legitimate when it comes from the authority and procedure citizens recognise as rightful.

Legitimacy is what turns a vote count into an obligation.

## Sub lesson 2 — Majority rule and the minority

Democracy decides by majority — and protects the minority, whose turn to persuade and win may come at the next vote.

A majority that crushes the minority destroys the procedure that made it.

## Sub lesson 3 — Consensus where it can be found

Some decisions seek consensus — the widest agreement possible — because near-unanimous decisions hold longest.

The chapter weighs the two: majority for decision, consensus for depth.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: a democratic decision binds everyone when it is legitimate — taken by the right procedure — and majority rule, the usual procedure, works only when the minority accepts it and consensus is sought where it can be found. Give one real example from your own life or from the France you live in, and name the lesson's key words: legitimacy, consensus, majority, decision. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** légitimité, consensus, majorité — say the French word aloud once; in France's own course the French term is the name citizens actually use.
