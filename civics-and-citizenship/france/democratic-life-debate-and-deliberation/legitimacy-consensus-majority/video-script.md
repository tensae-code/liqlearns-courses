# Video script — Legitimacy, Consensus and Majority

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

This lesson is about one thing.
A democratic decision binds everyone when it is legitimate — taken by the right procedure — and majority rule, the usual procedure, works only when the minority accepts it and consensus is sought where it can be found.

Legitimacy: why decisions bind. A decision is legitimate when it comes from the authority and procedure citizens recognise as rightful. Legitimacy is what turns a vote count into an obligation.

Majority rule and the minority. Democracy decides by majority — and protects the minority, whose turn to persuade and win may come at the next vote. A majority that crushes the minority destroys the procedure that made it.

Consensus where it can be found. Some decisions seek consensus — the widest agreement possible — because near-unanimous decisions hold longest. The chapter weighs the two: majority for decision, consensus for depth.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
