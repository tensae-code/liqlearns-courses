# Hands-on — Legitimacy, Consensus and Majority

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Your group votes 6–4 on a plan you voted against. Write why you would — or would not — accept the result as binding. This lesson names the ideas inside your answer: legitimacy, consensus and majority.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: a democratic decision binds everyone when it is legitimate — taken by the right procedure — and majority rule, the usual procedure, works only when the minority accepts it and consensus is sought where it can be found. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
