# Hands-on — European Law

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Name one rule in your daily life — food labels, phone roaming, travel without a passport check — that was decided beyond your own country. This lesson names that layer: European law.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: European law is the law of the European Union — built by treaties between its member states, standing above national law in its fields, and raising the debated question of what Europe is for. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
