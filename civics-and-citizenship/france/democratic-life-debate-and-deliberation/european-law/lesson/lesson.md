# European Law

## Do it first

Name one rule in your daily life — food labels, phone roaming, travel without a passport check — that was decided beyond your own country. This lesson names that layer: European law.

## Sub lesson 1 — Law built by treaties

European law rests on the treaties the member states signed together, creating common institutions and common rules.

France is an author of this law, not only its subject.

## Sub lesson 2 — Sovereignty debated

How much sovereignty states pool in Europe is a standing democratic debate — the chapter teaches the debate, not a verdict.

Every treaty step re-opens the question of who decides.

## Sub lesson 3 — What Europe is for

Peace between its peoples, shared prosperity, common rules for a shared market and shared problems — the chapter asks what Europe is for.

The citizen of Terminale is also a European citizen, with rights at that level too.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: European law is the law of the European Union — built by treaties between its member states, standing above national law in its fields, and raising the debated question of what Europe is for. Give one real example from your own life or from the France you live in, and name the lesson's key words: European law, European Union, treaties, sovereignty. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** droit européen — say the French word aloud once; in France's own course the French term is the name citizens actually use.
