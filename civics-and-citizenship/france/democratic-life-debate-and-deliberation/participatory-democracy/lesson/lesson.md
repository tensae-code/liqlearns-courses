# Participatory Democracy

## Do it first

Your town must choose between two plans for a public space. Voting day is years away. Write two ways residents could still shape the choice now. This lesson names them: participatory democracy.

## Sub lesson 1 — Beyond the vote

Citizens can take part between elections: public debates, consultations, participatory budgets and citizens' assemblies.

Participation widens the discussion culture of this axis into institutions.

## Sub lesson 2 — What participation adds

Decisions gain local knowledge, legitimacy and acceptance when those affected help shape them.

People support what they helped build — participation is cohesion applied to decisions.

## Sub lesson 3 — Its place in the Republic

Participatory democracy completes the representative system: representatives decide, citizens inform, test and enrich the decision.

The course ends where citizenship becomes a practice, not a status.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: participatory democracy takes citizens into decisions beyond the vote — consulted, associated and heard between elections — completing representative democracy rather than replacing it. Give one real example from your own life or from the France you live in, and name the lesson's key words: participatory democracy, participation, consultation, citizens. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** démocratie participative — say the French word aloud once; in France's own course the French term is the name citizens actually use.
