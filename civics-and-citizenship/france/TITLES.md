# Civics and Citizenship — France course — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

Civics belongs to the country: this course is France's own —
built from France's *Manuel Libre d'Enseignement moral et
civique* for the Lycée (student book, French). Inside the course
the organisation is by **levels, not grades**: the titles below
ARE the levels, in path order — what the learner proves, step by
step. The grades the book comes from (2de, 1re, Terminale) live
only in each lesson's sources.

The path's own logic is the book's — the official programme's
three axes: the citizen's legal frame first (rights, freedoms,
responsibility — with laïcité as the distinctively French
thread), then what holds a diverse society together (cohesion
and diversity), then how democratic decisions are actually
debated and taken (deliberation). That ladder IS the path order
(recommended, never forced — a learner can jump in higher; the
"start here instead" hints point down this ladder).

## The titles

- `rights-freedoms-and-responsibility/` — Level 1. The rule of
  law (law made by national representation, guarded by the
  Conseil constitutionnel, applied by courts, founded on the
  vote) · public order (the frame within which freedoms are
  exercised) · freedom of expression (its foundations and its
  limits) · laïcité and pluralism (the 1905 separation law,
  constitutional texts, religious signs, laïcité at school) ·
  freedom of information and pluralism (media pluralism as a
  condition of democracy) · secularisation and the hierarchy of
  values · environmental rights · development and ecological
  transition.
- `cohesion-and-diversity-in-a-democracy/` — Level 2.
  Nationality and citizenship (acquiring nationality, civic
  rights and duties, loss of civic rights, what being French
  means) · pluralism (opinions, beliefs and ways of life as a
  republican principle) · decentralisation (communes,
  départements, régions) · defence and national security ·
  fighting discrimination (and building an inclusive society) ·
  equality of women and men (in law, work and political life) ·
  figures of social hatred (racism, antisemitism and related
  hatreds) · solidarity and fraternity.
- `democratic-life-debate-and-deliberation/` — Level 3. The
  culture of organised discussion (the ethics of discussion,
  parties in the 1958 Constitution, the duty to vote, debating
  the death penalty and the ecological question) · public opinion
  (polls as thermometers or distorting mirrors, social networks,
  conformism, a world public opinion) · the digital society
  (disinformation, conspiracy thinking, critical distance) ·
  European law (EU treaties, sovereignty, what Europe is for) ·
  international law · legitimacy, consensus and majority ·
  active citizenship (engagement, the offices — mayor, deputy,
  senator, minister) · participatory democracy.
