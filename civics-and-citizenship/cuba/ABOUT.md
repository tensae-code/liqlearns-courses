# About the Civics and Citizenship — Cuba course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Educación Ciudadana, Educación Moral y Ciudadana and
Cultura Política
books.
The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is
from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the
lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Cuba inside the Civics and
Citizenship folder (one course per country). Inside it, the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place
is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the
ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced
gate. The school grades the books come from appear only in
each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

Cuba's own way, preserved whole, as filed: citizenship
starts from the child's own family and pioneer organisation
and is read straight out of the 2019 Constitution — family
duties, equality and the mass organisations are all taught
from constitutional text, and the state is presented as the
people's own power, with delegates answerable to their
electors. The secondary course opens by defining the Cuban
citizen's morality through national history and Martí's
ethics, then reads the 2019 Constitution as a values
document — humanist ideal first, articles after — and
closes basic secondary with the politically organised
society itself. At pre-university the subject becomes
Cultura Política, taught from primary documents — pupils
read Marx, Engels, Lenin, Martí, Bolívar and Fidel in the
original, with biographical notes first, rather than from a
textbook's summary of them — and the course closes by
applying everything to Cuba's own recent history.

The course as built has **nine stocked levels**, from the
two filings for this build (`cuba-placements.md` — 15
records / 15 distinct books, read as noted per book; and
`cuba-placements-b.md` — the Grade 6 Educación Ciudadana
