# Civil Society and the Media

## Do it first

Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: civil Society and the Media. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Vanuatu's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Sub lesson 1 — Civil society

Civil society is the organised life of citizens outside government — NGOs, churches, women's, youth and disability groups, business — and it holds its own place in Vanuatu's democracy.

## Sub lesson 2 — The media — and telling true from false

The media watch government and inform citizens, social media included; the citizen's skill is telling misinformation and disinformation from reliable sources, and checking information before passing it on.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Between the citizen and the government stand civil society — NGOs, churches, women's, youth and disability groups and business — and the media, which watch the government and inform citizens; both must be read with care, because misinformation and disinformation travel the same channels. Give one real example from your own life or from the Vanuatu you live in, and name the lesson's key words: civil, society. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
