# Lesson plan — Transparency and Accountability

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Vanuatu · **Title:** Taking Part · **Lesson:** Transparency and Accountability
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — Elections, Voting and Watching Government (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Transparency means government works in the open; accountability means it must answer for what it does — and civil society does the checking, on schools, transport, health and jobs, so democracy is exercised by both sides. This lesson stands on a student book.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What the two words mean** — Transparency: decisions and spending can be seen. Accountability: those who decide must explain themselves and face the result.
2. **Civil society does the checking** — Citizens acting together check the government on the things daily life depends on — schools, transport, health and jobs — because democracy is exercised by both sides, not by government alone.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
