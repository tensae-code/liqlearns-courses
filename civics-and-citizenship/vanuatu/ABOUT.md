# About the Civics and Citizenship — Vanuatu course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own civic education books. The one exception is the **Tip
boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Vanuatu inside the Civics and
Citizenship folder (one course per country). Inside it, the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place
is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the
ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced
gate. The school grades the books come from appear only in
each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

Vanuatu's own way, preserved whole, as filed: civics taught
as one short, direct story for every citizen — democracy,
the three arms, Parliament and local councils (including
the Malvatumauri National Council of Chiefs), then the
whole act of voting step by step, in plain English with
Bislama titles ("Vot blong yu") and real Ni-Vanuatu voices.
The teacher manual turns the booklet into participatory
lessons — debates, role-play, values clarification and a
full mock class election — and adds the character strand
(what makes a good citizen, what makes a good leader) the
booklet assumes. The student workbook is the pupil
companion to the same course, built around one threshold —
turning 18 and voting for the first time — so citizenship
is taught as preparation for a real first election, in the
pupil's own country and languages. The French stream has
its own Social Citizenship course with its own teacher
guides, parallel to the English stream's Electoral Office
course — taught as a cross-curricular citizenship,
integrated into other subjects, not only as its own
lessons. And the Pacific-regional ESD guide contributes
the Pacific way of belonging: identity tied to ocean and
land, knowledge held by elders, and the community deciding
and acting together on a shared issue.

The course as built has **four stocked levels**, from the
two filings for this build (`vanuatu-placements.md` — 3
records / 3 distinct books, all read in place at
phase3-staging, 32 placements; and
`vanuatu-placements-b.md` — 14 link leads triaged, 2
distinct new books read, 18 placements; 50 placements in
all, stocking 27 lessons — the coined lesson names were
deduped before building, parallel placements for the same
lesson stocking into that one lesson as its parts, and
every placement stocks into exactly one lesson):

1. **Level 1 — The Citizen and Belonging (8 lessons).**
   What citizenship is and what makes a good citizen;
   Pacific belonging — place and community, the sea, the
   land and elders (the ESD readers, on teacher-guide
   evidence); rights and responsibilities, human rights,
   and all citizens equal under the Constitution.
2. **Level 2 — Democracy and How Vanuatu Is Governed
   (8 lessons).** What democracy is; independence, national
   sovereignty and the three arms; Parliament, the work of
   an MP, local government with the Malvatumauri, and the
   whole system of government; and the French stream's two
   method-side lessons, on teacher-guide evidence — the
   active citizen at school, and teaching social
   citizenship across the curriculum.
3. **Level 3 — Elections, Voting and Watching Government
   (6 lessons).** Civil society and the media, transparency
   and accountability, community action on a shared issue;
   what an election is; your vote is your voice; and seven
   Ni-Vanuatu citizens saying why they vote.
4. **Level 4 — Fair Elections, Inclusion and Leading
   (5 lessons).** Candidates, programmes and campaigns;
   electoral offences; including everyone in a democracy;
   what makes a good leader; and the class election, the
   course's practical capstone.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later;
the books' own shape, walked in order):

- *Vot blong yu — your vote:* "First, the citizen: what
  citizenship is, what makes a good citizen, and where a
  Pacific citizen belongs — in a community, by the sea, on
  the land of the elders. Then the rights that come with
  the citizen, and the responsibilities that travel with
  them, and the Constitution's promise that all citizens
  are equal. Then democracy itself, and the country it
  built in 1980: three arms of government, a Parliament of
  52 members, councils in every province — and the chiefs
  of the Malvatumauri advising them all. Then the watching:
  civil society and the media, transparency and
  accountability, a community acting together. Then the
  vote: what an election is, how you register at eighteen,
  the secret ballot behind the screen — and seven citizens
  telling you, in their own words, why they vote. And last,
  the rules that keep it fair, the door opened to everyone,
  the marks of a good leader — and an election you run
  yourselves."

## Honesty notes (course level)

- **What the filings do and do not contain.** The filings
  contain 5 distinct read books (two student books — one
  of them a public voter-education booklet filed at Grade
  6 — two teacher guides/manuals read as TEACHER evidence,
  and one Pacific-regional teacher guide read PARTIAL,
  civics parts only), 50 placements, and the books' own
  contents maps. The filings do not contain any Vanuatu
