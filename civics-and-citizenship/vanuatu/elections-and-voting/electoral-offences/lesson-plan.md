# Lesson plan — Electoral Offences

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Vanuatu · **Title:** Elections and Voting · **Lesson:** Electoral Offences
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — Fair Elections, Inclusion and Leading (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Elections stay fair because breaking their rules is an offence: double voting, impersonation, bribery, treating, false statements, destroying ballot papers and breaking the ballot's secrecy are punished by fines or imprisonment — and a disputed election goes by petition to the Supreme Court within 21 days. ## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The offences** — The main electoral offences: voting twice, impersonating another voter, bribery, treating, false statements, destroying ballot papers, and breaking the secrecy of the ballot.
2. **What happens — and why voting still matters** — Offences are punished with fines or imprisonment; complaints go through the courts, and an election petition reaches the Supreme Court within 21 days — the rules protect the vote, which is why voting matters.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
