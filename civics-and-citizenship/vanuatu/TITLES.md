# Civics and Citizenship — Vanuatu course — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

Civics belongs to the country: this course is Vanuatu's own —
built from Vanuatu's civic education books (the Electoral
Office and Curriculum Development "Vanuatu Blong Yu" course —
its student booklet, its teacher manual and its student
workbook — the Pacific-regional ESD readers teacher guide,
and the French stream's *Citoyenneté Sociale* teacher guide
for Years 7–10). Inside the course the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: the titles below ARE the levels'
titles, in path order — what the learner proves, step by
step. The grades the books come from live only in each
lesson's sources.

The path's own logic is Vanuatu's, reconciled from the two
filings' ladder sketches: the first filing sketches four
rungs from the "Vanuatu Blong Yu" course's own order — the
citizen and their rights first in the manual's opening
lessons, the machinery of democracy in the middle, and
voting, fairness and participation as the climb's top. The
second filing sketches its whole set at one rung — the
citizen at the threshold of voting, the workbook's thirteen
lessons and the French Years 7–10 guide's strands — with the
French stream's first rung (its Years 1–6 guide) sketched
below it, unread. The reconciliation built here: the
workbook is the pupil companion to the same thirteen-lesson
course the manual teaches, so its placements stock into the
same lessons as the manual's and the booklet's, at the
levels the first filing's ladder gives those lessons; the
French guide's strands stock into the same lessons where
they teach the same thing (citizenship, human rights,
including everyone), and its two method-side strands —
the active citizen at school, and teaching social
citizenship itself — keep the second filing's own level,
Level 2. So: Level 1 is the citizen and belonging (the
manual's opening lessons, the ESD readers' Pacific
belonging, rights and equality). Level 2 is democracy and
the machinery of government (booklet and workbook), with
the French method lessons beside it. Level 3 is elections,
voting and watching government. Level 4 is fairness,
inclusion, leadership and practice — closing with the class
election. That ladder IS the path order (recommended, never
forced — a learner can jump in higher; the "start here
instead" hints point down this ladder). 20 lessons stand on
student books; 7 stand on teacher guides — evidence only,
acceptance 0, labelled in every lesson's sources.

## The titles

**Level 1 — the citizen and belonging** (the teacher
manual's opening lessons, the ESD readers, and the
booklet's equality chapter):

- `belonging-and-citizenship/` — Level 1. What Is
  Citizenship? · What Makes a Good Citizen? · Our Place
  and Community (TEACHER) · People and the Sea (TEACHER)
  · People and the Land (TEACHER). The course opens with
  the citizen, because Vanuatu's manual opens there — and
  the Pacific readers ground that citizen in ocean, land
  and elders.
- `rights-and-equality/` — Level 1. Rights and
  Responsibilities in a Democracy · Human Rights · All
  Citizens Are Equal. Rights come with the citizen, before
  any machinery: the manual teaches them in its fourth and
  fifth lessons, and the booklet crowns them with the
  Constitution's equality.

**Level 2 — democracy and how Vanuatu is governed** (the
booklet's opening chapters and the workbook's course, with
the French stream's method lessons):

- `democracy-and-the-constitution/` — Level 2. What Is
  Democracy? · Three Arms of Government. The booklet's own
  opening: democracy first, then the state it builds.
- `parliament-and-government/` — Level 2. What Is
  Parliament? · The Work of a Member of Parliament · Local
  Government · Our System of Government. The machinery,
  piece by piece, then whole — councils and the
  Malvatumauri included, as Vanuatu's book includes them.
- `belonging-and-citizenship/` — Level 2. Teaching Social
  Citizenship (TEACHER). The French stream's framing
  lesson: citizenship woven through the whole curriculum.
  It sits at this level by its own filing's ladder.
- `taking-part/` — Level 2. The Active Citizen at School
  (TEACHER). The French guide's third strand: citizenship
  practised inside one's own school, at its filing's level.

**Level 3 — elections, voting and watching government**
(the booklet's middle chapters and the manual's and
workbook's matching lessons):

- `taking-part/` — Level 3. Civil Society and the Media ·
  Transparency and Accountability · Community Action for
  a Shared Issue (TEACHER) · Why I Vote. The watchers and
  the actors: civil society, the media, the checking of
  government, a community acting together, and citizens'
  own voices on why they vote.
- `elections-and-voting/` — Level 3. What Is an Election?
  · Your Vote Is Your Voice. The act at the course's
  centre, taught whole: choosing representatives, then
  registering and voting, step by step.

**Level 4 — fair elections, inclusion and leading** (the
course's top, as both filings close it):

- `elections-and-voting/` — Level 4. Candidates,
  Programmes and Campaigns · Electoral Offences. The rules
  that keep elections fair: who may stand, how campaigns
  run, and what is punished when the rules are broken.
- `rights-and-equality/` — Level 4. Including Everyone in
  a Democracy. Level 1's equality, climbed again at the
  top: inclusion as the finished democracy's working part.
- `taking-part/` — Level 4. What Makes a Good Leader? ·
  The Class Election (TEACHER). The climb ends with
  judging leaders — and then running an election
  yourselves, the manual's capstone.
