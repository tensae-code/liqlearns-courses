# Telling Our Story with the Map

## Do it first

Spread out an imaginary map in front of you. Put your finger on one place, then travel it to another. At each stop, ask: what story belongs to this place? Hold that game in mind as you read.

## A journey that is told, place by place

The handbook's last teaching unit is a didactic game: pupils travel across the map of Slovenia, and wherever the journey stops, a story is told — the story of that place.

The map is the board and the country is the journey. A place is not just a name and a dot: it is somewhere a tale happened, somewhere people remember something.

Telling the story is the pupil's part. The game turns the map from something you look at into something you can tell — place by place, story by story.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: telling our story with the map — travelling the map of Slovenia in a game, and telling the story of each place you reach. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: map, story, journey, place, didactic game. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
