# Who We Are

## Do it first

Look around your class or your group. Name the people you learn with, one by one. Now name the groups you belong to — your class, your family, your place. Hold this in mind as you read: this lesson is about who we are, together.

## We, together

A class is a community: a group of children and their teacher who learn and live part of each day together. Each person in it is themselves — and each person also belongs to the group.

Groups join us to each other. Your class joins you to your classmates. Your family joins you to the people at home. Your place joins you to the people who live where you live.

The handbook's first unit starts here, with feelings and belonging: before a young pupil learns anything else about the homeland, they learn to say who we are — the people I am with, and the bigger home we share.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: who we are — we belong to a class and to the groups that join us, and together we are part of Slovenia. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: we, class, community, group, belonging. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: who we are — we belong to a class and to the groups that join us, and together we are part of Slovenia.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
