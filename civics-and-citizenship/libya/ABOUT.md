# About the Civics and Citizenship — Libya course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own National Education books, أنا المواطن (I Am the
Citizen). The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a
box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours;
the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Libya inside the Civics and
Citizenship folder (one course per country). Inside it, the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place
is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the
ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced
gate. The school grades the books come from appear only in
each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

Libya's own way, preserved whole, as filed: all six books
are أنا المواطن (I Am the Citizen), the National Education
(التربية الوطنية) activity books for Grades 4–9 of Basic
Education, prepared by a specialist committee of the Centre
for Educational Curricula and Pedagogical Research, Ministry
of Education, State of Libya, school year 1440–1441 AH /
2019–2020, in Arabic. The books are image-only and were read
visually page by page from renders; each book opens with the
same citizen's creed ("I am the citizen, a social being… I
claim my rights and commit to my duties…"), summarised in
the filing's headers, never transcribed. The books' activity
and assessment pages are the lessons' own working parts and
are folded into each lesson's placement; no separate
assessment section exists. Citizenship is built as widening
"I" statements — I am a human, I and my family, I and my
neighbourhood — inside an activity book where every lesson
ends in the child's own conclusion (أستنتج). The fifth year
turns from the self to the homeland and then to the values
that let a divided country live together — equality,
justice, solidarity, tolerance, dialogue. The sixth year
names the citizen outright — rights against duties, freedom
with responsibility — then re-grounds them in family,
school and quarter, and closes by repeating the Grade 4
health lessons at a citizen's depth. The seventh year is a
rights year built directly on the 1989 Convention on the
Rights of the Child (UNICEF-sourced text, summarised), then
turns the coin over: the same child's duties to family and
society. The eighth year takes the single word "citizenship"
apart into five dimensions — belonging, social,
administrative, legal, political — each taught through a
case (a late civil registration, a boy driving at fourteen).
The basic stage closes on democracy as practice — the
course ends not with a test but with two workshops in which
the class actually elects its representative and runs a
vote.

The course as built has **eight stocked levels**, from the
filing for this build (`libya-placements.md` — 6 records /
6 distinct books, all read in full; 53 placements in all,
stocking 49 lessons — the coined lesson names were deduped
before building, the Grade 6 book's repeats of the Grade 4
health and neighbourhood lessons stocking into those same
lessons as their deeper parts, and every placement stocks
into exactly one lesson):

1. **Level 1 — Myself and My Health (4 lessons).** I am a
   human; my cleanliness, my nutrition and my bodily
   safety — the last three stocked at two depths, the
   Grade 4 teaching and the Grade 6 return.
2. **Level 2 — My Family, School and Neighbourhood
   (5 lessons).** Family, school and neighbourhood, and
   the Grade 6 revisits: responsibilities in the family
   and relations at school; the neighbourhood lesson is
   stocked at two depths.
3. **Level 3 — My Environment (2 lessons).** What the
   environment is, and protecting it.
4. **Level 4 — My Country (2 lessons).** My country Libya,
   and Libya for all its regions and people.
5. **Level 5 — Values for Living Together (7 lessons).**
   Equality, justice, cooperation and solidarity,
   tolerance, accepting the other, the culture of dialogue,
   and difference and agreement.
6. **Level 6 — Rights and Duties (11 lessons).** My rights
   and my duties and responsible freedom, then the child's
   rights under the 1989 convention — identity, health,
   education, protection — and the duties that answer
   them, in the family and in society, closing with
   equality and non-discrimination.
7. **Level 7 — Citizenship (10 lessons).** The homeland,
   citizenship itself, and its social, administrative,
   legal and political dimensions, with freedom, voluntary
   work, participation in cultural life and responsibility
   toward the natural environment.
8. **Level 8 — Democracy (8 lessons).** Democracy,
   participation in public life, peaceful transfer of
   power, civil society, women's participation, elections,
   and the two workshops: the class representative charter
   and running a vote.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later;
the books' own shape, walked in order):

- *I am the citizen:* "First, you yourself: I am a human —
  your name, your talents, your strengths — and your
  health: cleanliness, nutrition, safety. Then your first
  circles: your family, society's basic group; your
  school, a small society with its own rules; your
  neighbourhood, a fabric of warm human relations. Then
  the world you live in: the environment — mountain, sea,
  forest, desert and oasis — and how you protect it. Then
  your homeland: Libya, its wealth and customs and cities,
  one country for all its regions. Then the values that
  let people live together: equality, justice, solidarity,
  tolerance, dialogue. Then your rights and your duties,
  named outright — and the child's rights under the 1989
  convention: identity, health, education, protection.
  Then citizenship itself, taken apart: social,
  administrative, legal, political. And last, democracy
  in practice: taking part, peaceful transfer of power,
  civil society, elections — and a vote you run
  yourselves."

  lessons.** Every lesson in this course stands on a
  student book (Books 1–6, all STUDENT as filed) and
  carries the acceptance line "taught in 1 country

  count.
- **Teacher-guide evidence only, counted nowhere:** none.
  The filings hold no teacher guide and no programme for
  Libya: all six books are student books, so no lesson in
  this course stands at acceptance 0, and no level rests
  on guides. Teacher guides and programmes are evidence
  only — that rule is stated here because the folder's
  reckoning requires it, and it changes nothing in this
  course, there being no such book in the filings.
- Every lesson's `lesson/sources.md` carries its own
  acceptance line in exactly these terms.

## Honesty notes (course level)

- **What the filings do and do not contain.** The filings
  contain 6 distinct read books (all student books — the
  National Education activity books for Grades 4–9 of
  Basic Education, in Arabic), 53 placements, and the
  books' own contents maps. The filings do not contain
  any Libya National Education book for Grades 1–3, any
  book for the secondary stage beyond Grade 9, any
  teacher guide or programme, or any book in any language
  other than Arabic. Nothing of that kind is in this
  course, and no lesson pretends to one.
- **Phantom pushes, carried exactly as filed.** Every
  record was marked pushed but its file was absent from
  the GitHub tree (grab 404) — phantom pushes, library
  debts named in the group log. The recorded source host
  (liasinstitute.com) was unreachable from the filing
  machine, so each book was fetched from the Wayback
  Machine's snapshot of its recorded source URL and staged
  under the push-don't-delete rule. That label is carried
  in every book entry above and in every lesson's sources
  header, exactly as filed. No book was downloaded or
  staged for this build (DISK HOLD in force); everything
  was built from the placements filing alone.
- **Staged-pending files, as filed.** The six books stand
  staged under the push-don't-delete rule, as the filing
  records ("staged (see group log)"). Their securing is
  the library's own work, outside this build; this course
  rests on the filing's full visual read of them.
- **No PARTIAL label is carried, because the filing
  carries none.** All six books are filed COMPLETE. Where
  a contents map rests on content verified at a named
  folio rather than a contents-page folio, the book
  entries above say so in the filing's own terms.
- **No UNREAD book is carried, because the filing carries
  none.** The filing records 6 records / 6 distinct books,
  all read in full. No book is named as not yet in the
  course.
- **No wrong-file label is carried, because the filing
  carries none for these books.** The filing states no
  wrong-file or wrong-grade-body label for any of the six
  books; nothing of that kind has been added here. Each
  book's cover was verified against its record title, as
  filed.
- **The convention's text, as filed.** The Grade 7 book is
  built directly on the 1989 Convention on the Rights of
  the Child (UNICEF-sourced text); as filed, its articles
  are named by topic and summarised, never transcribed —
  and the same holds in this course: the lessons teach the
  filing's summaries in our own words, and the only quoted
  fragment carried is the book's own short phrase for the
  child, "the seed of the future society", in the Child
  Protection lesson's filing summary.

  at a citizen's depth. Those four lessons each hold two

  as their parts; they are counted once each in the 49
  lessons and twice each in the 53 placements. No
  placement was dropped, and every lesson's sources carry
  each of its placements' own book and printed page
  exactly as filed.
