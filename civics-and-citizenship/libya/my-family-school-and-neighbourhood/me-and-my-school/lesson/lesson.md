# Me and My School

## Do it first

Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: me and My School. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Libya's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

The school is the second environment after home and a small society with its own rules — and basic education is the child’s right, free and compulsory, as the child-rights convention’s article on education provides, in summary.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The school is the second environment after home and a small society with its own rules — and basic education is the child’s right, free and compulsory, as the child-rights convention’s article on education provides, in summary. Give one real example from your own life or from the Libya you live in, and name the lesson's key words: me, school. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: The school is the second environment after home and a small society with its own rules — and basic education is the child’s right, free and compulsory, as the child-rights convention’s article on education provides, in summary.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
