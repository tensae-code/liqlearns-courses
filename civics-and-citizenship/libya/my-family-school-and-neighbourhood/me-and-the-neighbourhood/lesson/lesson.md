# Me and the Neighbourhood

## Do it first

Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: me and the Neighbourhood. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Libya's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Sub lesson 1 — Responsibility in the quarter

Helping the elderly, the disabled and the poor, respecting neighbours’ privacy, not disturbing them — the neighbourhood as a fabric of warm human relations, not just a place.

## Sub lesson 2 — Behaviour in the quarter, revisited

The child’s behaviour in the quarter — playing without harming, keeping it clean, respecting its people.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Responsibility in your quarter: helping the elderly, the disabled and the poor, respecting neighbours’ privacy and not disturbing them — the neighbourhood as a fabric of warm human relations, not just a place. Give one real example from your own life or from the Libya you live in, and name the lesson's key words: me, neighbourhood. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: Responsibility in your quarter: helping the elderly, the disabled and the poor, respecting neighbours’ privacy and not disturbing them — the neighbourhood as a fabric of warm human relations, not just a place.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
