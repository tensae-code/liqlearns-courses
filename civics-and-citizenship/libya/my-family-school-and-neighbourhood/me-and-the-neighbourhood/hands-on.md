# Hands-on — Me and the Neighbourhood

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: me and the Neighbourhood. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Libya's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Responsibility in your quarter: helping the elderly, the disabled and the poor, respecting neighbours’ privacy and not disturbing them — the neighbourhood as a fabric of warm human relations, not just a place. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
