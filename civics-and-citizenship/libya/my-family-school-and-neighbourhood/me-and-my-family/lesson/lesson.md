# Me and My Family

## Do it first

Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: me and My Family. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Libya's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Sub lesson 1 — The family, society’s basic group

The family as society’s basic group, bound by kinship.

## Sub lesson 2 — What the family does and teaches

The family’s functions are care, upbringing, passing on values and meeting material needs; in it the child learns solidarity, cooperation, equality, rights and duties.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The family is society’s basic group, bound by kinship: it cares, brings up, passes on values and meets material needs — and in it the child learns solidarity, cooperation, equality, rights and duties. Give one real example from your own life or from the Libya you live in, and name the lesson's key words: me, family. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: The family is society’s basic group, bound by kinship: it cares, brings up, passes on values and meets material needs — and in it the child learns solidarity, cooperation, equality, rights and duties.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
