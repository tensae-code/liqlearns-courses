# My Cleanliness

## Do it first

Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: my Cleanliness. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Libya's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Sub lesson 1 — Cleanliness and its value

What cleanliness is and its value — ending in the book’s own point that not throwing litter in the street spares the sanitation worker’s back.

## Sub lesson 2 — Cleanliness at a citizen’s depth

The fields of cleanliness, cleanliness behaviour at home and school, bearing responsibility for it — and the causes when it is neglected.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: What cleanliness is and why it matters — at home, at school and in the street — and the responsibility each person bears for it. Give one real example from your own life or from the Libya you live in, and name the lesson's key words: cleanliness. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: What cleanliness is and why it matters — at home, at school and in the street — and the responsibility each person bears for it.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
