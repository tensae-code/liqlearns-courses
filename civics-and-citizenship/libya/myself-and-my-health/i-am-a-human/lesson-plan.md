# Lesson plan — I Am a Human

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Libya · **Title:** Myself and My Health · **Lesson:** I Am a Human
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — Myself and My Health (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Knowing yourself: your name, your family, your talents and your strengths — and that each person shares some traits with others and differs in others. This lesson stands on a student book.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Knowing yourself** — Your name, your family, your talents and your strengths — the things that make you you.
2. **Alike and different** — Each person shares some traits with others and differs in others; both are part of being human.

Gate pool: at most 5 questions for the prove-it check, mixed with earlier parts, picked as the hardest from the rank right under the wanted sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- This is the first lesson of the course — start here.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Knowing yourself: your name, your family, your talents and your strengths — and that each person shares some traits with others and differs in others.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, school or town — or, for this book's lessons, from the public life of Libya they live in.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the learner's own life in Libya and the book's own teaching, as the book itself works.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner does the opening act of lesson/lesson.md before any explanation.
2. **The explanation** — the lesson's parts in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of i Am a Human in Libya's public life — the scene at the centre of the lesson, with citizens in it — the student names the parts aloud and says what each one does.
