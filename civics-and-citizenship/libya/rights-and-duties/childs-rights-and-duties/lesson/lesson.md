# The Child's Rights and Duties

## Do it first

Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: the Child's Rights and Duties. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Libya's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Sub lesson 1 — Right and duty

A right as the power to do or not do a thing, and a duty as what must be done.

## Sub lesson 2 — The 1989 convention

The 1989 convention as the first binding instrument joining civil, political, cultural, social and economic rights.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: A right is the power to do or not do a thing and a duty is what must be done; the 1989 Convention on the Rights of the Child is the first binding instrument joining civil, political, cultural, social and economic rights. Give one real example from your own life or from the Libya you live in, and name the lesson's key words: child, s. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
