# Civics and Citizenship — Libya course — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

Civics belongs to the country: this course is Libya's own —
built from Libya's National Education books, أنا المواطن
(I Am the Citizen), the activity books for Grades 4–9 of
Basic Education (Centre for Educational Curricula and
Pedagogical Research, Ministry of Education, State of
Libya, 2019–2020, in Arabic). Inside the course the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: the titles below
ARE the levels' titles, in path order — what the learner
proves, step by step. The grades the books come from live
only in each lesson's sources.

The path's own logic is Libya's, from the filing's ladder
sketch: citizenship is built as widening "I" statements —
I am a human, I and my family, I and my neighbourhood —
inside activity books where every lesson ends in the
child's own conclusion. Level 1 is the child's own person
and health, with the Grade 6 book's deeper return to the
same three health lessons stocked as those lessons' second
parts. Level 2 is the first circles — family, school,
neighbourhood — revisited as responsibilities and
relations. Level 3 is the natural world. Level 4 turns to
the homeland. Level 5 is the values that let a divided
country live together. Level 6 names the citizen outright —
rights against duties, freedom with responsibility — then
spends a full year on the child's rights under the 1989
convention and the duties that answer them. Level 7 takes
the single word "citizenship" apart into five dimensions —
belonging, social, administrative, legal, political — each
taught through a case. Level 8 closes the basic stage with
democracy as practice, ending not with a test but with two
workshops in which the class actually elects its
representative and runs a vote. That ladder IS the path
order (recommended, never forced — a learner can jump in
higher; the "start here instead" hints point down this
ladder). All 49 lessons stand on student books.

## The titles

**Level 1 — myself and my health** (the Grade 4 book's
first unit, with the Grade 6 book's health lessons stocked
as deeper parts):

- `myself-and-my-health/` — Level 1. I Am a Human · My
  Cleanliness · My Nutrition · My Bodily Safety. The
  course opens with the child's own person, because
  Libya's books open there — knowing oneself first, then
  the health the citizen's body needs.

**Level 2 — my family, school and neighbourhood** (the
Grade 4 book's second unit, revisited in Grade 6 as
responsibilities and relations):

- `my-family-school-and-neighbourhood/` — Level 2. Me and
  My Family · Me and My School · Me and the Neighbourhood
  · My Responsibilities in My Family · My Relations at
  School. The first circles widen from the self: the
  family as society's basic group, the school as a small
  society, the quarter as a fabric of human relations.

**Level 3 — my environment** (the Grade 4 book's third
unit):

- `my-environment/` — Level 3. Me and the Environment · I
  Protect the Environment. The natural world the child
  lives in — its components and kinds — and the child's
  own hand in protecting it.

**Level 4 — my country** (the Grade 5 book's first unit):

- `my-country/` — Level 4. My Country Libya · Libya for
  All. The fifth year turns from the self to the homeland:
  its wealth, customs and cities, and its regions and
  products as one homeland belonging to everyone.

**Level 5 — values for living together** (the Grade 5
book's values lessons and its Living Together unit,
closing in Grade 7 with difference and agreement):

- `values-for-living-together/` — Level 5. Equality ·
  Justice · Cooperation and Solidarity · Tolerance ·
  Accepting the Other · Culture of Dialogue · Difference
  and Agreement. The values that let people live together,
  each taught through a story or a classroom practice.

**Level 6 — rights and duties** (the Grade 6 book's I Am
a Citizen unit, then the Grade 7 book's rights year):

- `rights-and-duties/` — Level 6. My Rights and My Duties
  · My Freedom Is Responsible · The Child's Rights and
  Duties · The Right to Identity · The Right to Health ·
  The Child's Right to Education · Child Protection · My
  Duties in the Family · My Responsibility toward Society
  · My Duties within Society · Equality and
  Non-Discrimination. Rights named and bounded, built
  directly on the 1989 Convention on the Rights of the
  Child, then the coin turned over: the same child's
  duties to family and society.

**Level 7 — citizenship** (the Grade 8 book, whole):

- `citizenship/` — Level 7. My Homeland · Citizenship ·
  Citizenship — the Social Dimension · Citizenship — the
  Administrative Dimension · Citizenship — the Legal
  Dimension · Citizenship — the Political Dimension ·
  Freedom · Voluntary Work · Participation in Cultural
  Life · Responsibility toward the Natural Environment.
  One word taken apart into its dimensions, each through
  a case — a late civil registration, a boy driving at
  fourteen.

**Level 8 — democracy** (the Grade 9 book, whole):

- `democracy/` — Level 8. Democracy · Participation in
  Public Life · Peaceful Transfer of Power · Civil
  Society · Women's Participation in Society · Elections
  · The Class Representative Charter · Running a Vote.
  Democracy as practice: the course ends not with a test
  but with two workshops in which the class drafts its
  charter, elects its representative and runs a vote.
