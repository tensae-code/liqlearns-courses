# The Notion of Human Rights

## Do it first

Name one thing no one may rightly do to you, even if they are stronger. Now ask where that protection is written down, and who watches that it is kept. This lesson answers: human rights, their texts, and their guardians.

## Sub lesson 1 — What human rights are

Human rights are the basic claims of every human being — to life, freedom, fairness and respect — held by all, everywhere.

They grow out of dignity: because every person has worth, every person holds rights.

## Sub lesson 2 — The texts that contain them

Human rights are written down in known texts — declarations and laws that pupils can name and point to.

A right written in a text can be quoted back to power; that is why the texts matter and why the book teaches them.

## Sub lesson 3 — Abuse — and the institutions that watch

Rights are abused when they are violated — and named institutions monitor respect for human rights and receive complaints.

Knowing who watches is part of holding a right: a violation reported to the right institution can be stopped.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: human rights are the claims every person holds by being human — they are written in known texts, they are abused in known ways, and named institutions watch over respect for them. Give one real example from your own life in Cameroon, and name the lesson's key words: human rights, texts, institutions, abuse. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in your own language as well as in English — Cameroon's own book teaches citizenship for life at home and in the village, not only for the classroom.
