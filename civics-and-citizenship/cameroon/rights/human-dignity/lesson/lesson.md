# The Dignity of the Human Being

## Do it first

Look at the youngest child you know, and at the oldest elder. Ask what makes each of them worth respect before they have done anything at all. This lesson gives that worth its name: dignity.

## Sub lesson 1 — What human dignity is

Human dignity is the worth every person has simply by being human — it is not earned, bought or granted.

Because dignity comes with being human, it belongs to the child exactly as it belongs to the elder and the chief.

## Sub lesson 2 — Dignified and undignified behaviour

Behaviour is dignified when it respects the worth of oneself and others, and undignified when it shames, degrades or treats a person as a thing.

The lesson teaches pupils to judge conduct — their own first — by this measure.

## Sub lesson 3 — The child's fundamental rights

Because the child is a human being, the child holds fundamental rights that flow from dignity itself.

Rights are not gifts from adults; they are dignity written as claims the child can stand on.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: every human being has dignity — worth that no one gives and no one may take away — shown in dignified behaviour, refused by undignified behaviour, and standing behind the child's fundamental rights. Give one real example from your own life in Cameroon, and name the lesson's key words: dignity, human being, fundamental rights. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in your own language as well as in English — Cameroon's own book teaches citizenship for life at home and in the village, not only for the classroom.
