# The Village

## Do it first

Picture the village you know best, or one you have visited. Name its houses, its centre, its paths. This lesson teaches what a village is — its types, its building materials, and how Cameroonian villages differ.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a village is

A village is a small settled community — homes, families and shared life in one place, under its own order.

The village is the first community beyond the family in Cameroon's book: Book 2 opens where Book 1's family widens.

## Sub lesson 2 — Village types and building materials

Villages take types — by their layout, their people's work and their place — and they are built from the materials their land provides.

Roof, wall and fence tell you where you are: materials are the land's own signature on the village.

## Sub lesson 3 — Telling Cameroonian villages apart

Cameroon's village types can be told apart by those signs — layout, work, materials and setting.

A pupil who can read those signs can arrive in an unknown village and already understand half of what he sees.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: a village is a settled community smaller than a town, with its own life and order — villages come in types, are built from the materials of their place, and Cameroon's village types can be told apart. Give one real example from your own life in Cameroon, and name the lesson's key words: village, types, building materials. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in your own language as well as in English — Cameroon's own book teaches citizenship for life at home and in the village, not only for the classroom.
