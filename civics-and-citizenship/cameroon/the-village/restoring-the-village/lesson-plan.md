# Lesson plan — The Restoration of the Village

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Cameroon · **Title:** The Village · **Lesson:** The Restoration of the Village
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — The Village (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the village is worth restoring: developing its traditional culture and keeping its place in the country's life, so that what the village holds is not lost as the country changes.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — The Village — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Developing traditional culture** — Restoring the village means developing its traditional culture — its crafts, festivals, skills and stories — as living wealth, not museum pieces.
2. **The village's importance** — The village matters to the whole country — as producer, as keeper of culture, and as the home most citizens' belonging starts from.

Gate pool: the prove-it check for each sub lesson is at most 5 questions, mixed with earlier parts, picked as the hardest from the rank right under the wanted sub lesson — drawn from this lesson's question files (questions/).

## Before this lesson
- Start with the lesson before this one in path order — The Village in the Economic Life of the Country (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: the village is worth restoring: developing its traditional culture and keeping its place in the country's life, so that what the village holds is not lost as the country changes.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street, school or village in Cameroon.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the learner's own life in Cameroon and the book's own teaching, as Cameroon's Citizenship Education books work chapter by chapter from the family outward.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks restoration means freezing the village in the past → it means developing its culture and life so the village has a future, not embalming it.
- Go back to the one thing: the village is worth restoring: developing its traditional culture and keeping its place in the country's life, so that what the village holds is not lost as the country changes. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
