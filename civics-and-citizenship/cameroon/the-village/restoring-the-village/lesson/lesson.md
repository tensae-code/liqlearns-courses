# The Restoration of the Village

## Do it first

Imagine a village whose young people have all left and whose crafts no one practises. What exactly has been lost? This lesson teaches the restoration of the village — why it matters and what it restores.

## Sub lesson 1 — Developing traditional culture

Restoring the village means developing its traditional culture — its crafts, festivals, skills and stories — as living wealth, not museum pieces.

A culture that is practised and improved keeps its people rooted while the country moves.

## Sub lesson 2 — The village's importance

The village matters to the whole country — as producer, as keeper of culture, and as the home most citizens' belonging starts from.

Book 2's village section closes here: the village restored is the foundation the council and the region are built on.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the village is worth restoring: developing its traditional culture and keeping its place in the country's life, so that what the village holds is not lost as the country changes. Give one real example from your own life in Cameroon, and name the lesson's key words: restoration, traditional culture, village. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in your own language as well as in English — Cameroon's own book teaches citizenship for life at home and in the village, not only for the classroom.
