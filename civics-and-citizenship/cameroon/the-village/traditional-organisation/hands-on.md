# Hands-on — The Traditional Organisation of the Village

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
In a village, when two families dispute a boundary, who settles it? When a feast is called, who calls it? This lesson maps that order: the traditional organisation of the village.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: the village is governed traditionally by a hierarchy of institutions — chiefs and councils with defined competence — and traditional authorities hold real powers that the book names. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in the village. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
