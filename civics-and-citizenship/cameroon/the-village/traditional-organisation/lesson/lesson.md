# The Traditional Organisation of the Village

## Do it first

In a village, when two families dispute a boundary, who settles it? When a feast is called, who calls it? This lesson maps that order: the traditional organisation of the village.

## Sub lesson 1 — The village's hierarchical institutions

The village is organised in a hierarchy of traditional institutions — from the family head up through quarter and village authorities.

Hierarchy here means order, not vanity: each level settles what the level below cannot.

## Sub lesson 2 — Their competence and the powers of traditional authorities

Each institution has its competence — the matters it may judge and decide — and traditional authorities hold named powers within it.

A power with a known limit is authority; a power without one is mere force. The book teaches the limits with the powers.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the village is governed traditionally by a hierarchy of institutions — chiefs and councils with defined competence — and traditional authorities hold real powers that the book names. Give one real example from your own life in Cameroon, and name the lesson's key words: traditional organisation, chief, competence, authority. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in your own language as well as in English — Cameroon's own book teaches citizenship for life at home and in the village, not only for the classroom.
