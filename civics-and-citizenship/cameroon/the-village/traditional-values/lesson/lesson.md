# Traditional Social Values

## Do it first

Name one custom your elders praise — how guests are received, how elders are greeted, how neighbours help at harvest. This lesson weighs such values: what they give, where they can harm, and how to keep the good.

## Sub lesson 1 — What traditional values are

Traditional social values are the standards of conduct a community hands down — respect for elders, solidarity, hospitality, honesty and the rest.

They are the village's unwritten curriculum: taught at home and in gatherings long before any school lesson.

## Sub lesson 2 — Their advantages and disadvantages

Traditional values bind a community and guide the young — but a value can also carry a disadvantage when it is kept past its usefulness or applied unjustly.

The book does not worship or discard the values; it weighs them, which is the pupil's own work too.

## Sub lesson 3 — Enhancing the positive ones

The positive values can be deliberately enhanced — practised, taught and honoured — so the community keeps their strength and sheds their harm.

A value that is practised on purpose survives; a value left to habit alone fades or twists.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: a community lives by traditional social values — the customs of respect, solidarity and honesty handed down — which carry advantages and disadvantages, and whose positive side can be deliberately strengthened. Give one real example from your own life in Cameroon, and name the lesson's key words: traditional values, advantages, disadvantages. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in your own language as well as in English — Cameroon's own book teaches citizenship for life at home and in the village, not only for the classroom.
