# Divorce

## Do it first

Think of a family that has split apart, if you know one. Ask: what broke, who was hurt most, and what could have kept the peace? This lesson teaches divorce honestly — its meaning, its causes, and its cost.

## Sub lesson 1 — What divorce means — and its causes

Divorce is the recognised ending of a marriage, and it comes from causes the book names plainly — broken duties, quarrels that never settle, faithlessness and neglect.

A marriage rarely breaks in one day; the causes grow where the spouses' duties were left undone.

## Sub lesson 2 — Consequences for family and child

Divorce scatters the household and falls hardest on the child, who can lose care, schooling and the daily presence of a parent.

That is why the book teaches divorce as a grave matter: the adults end a union, but the child pays the largest share.

## Sub lesson 3 — The child's part in family peace

The child has a part in family peace — respect, honesty and conduct that does not feed quarrels at home.

A child cannot hold a marriage together alone, but a child's behaviour can guard the peace of the house day by day.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: divorce is the legal ending of a marriage — it has causes, it carries consequences for the family and above all for the child, and the child's own part is to work for peace in the family. Give one real example from your own life in Cameroon, and name the lesson's key words: divorce, causes, consequences, family peace. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in your own language as well as in English — Cameroon's own book teaches citizenship for life at home and in the village, not only for the classroom.
