# Hands-on — The Rights and Duties of a Child in the Family

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
List two things you may rightly ask for at home — food, schooling, protection. Then list two things home rightly asks of you. Hold both lists together: this lesson is about why both lists are true at once.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: a child in the family holds real rights — and carries real duties: the child matters greatly in an African family, and the parent–child relationship works when rights are respected and duties are done. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in the village. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
