# Marriage

## Do it first

Think of a married couple you know. Ask yourself: how do people know they are married — what paper proves it, and what promises stand behind it? This lesson answers those questions.

## Sub lesson 1 — What marriage is and its forms

Marriage is the union of two spouses recognised by law and custom, and it comes in more than one form.

Cameroon's book teaches the forms side by side, so a pupil can tell them apart and know what each one means.

## Sub lesson 2 — The certificate and the conditions

A marriage certificate is the written proof of the marriage, and a marriage is recognised only when its conditions are fulfilled.

Without the conditions met and the proof kept, a union can be disputed — the certificate protects both spouses and the children.

## Sub lesson 3 — The spouses' duties — and failed marriage

Married spouses owe each other faithfulness, help and respect; when those duties collapse, a marriage can fail.

The duties are the daily content of the promise: a marriage stands or falls on how the spouses treat each other.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: marriage is the recognised union of spouses — it has forms, it is proved by a marriage certificate, it holds only when its conditions are met, and it gives the spouses duties to each other. Give one real example from your own life in Cameroon, and name the lesson's key words: marriage, spouses, marriage certificate. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in your own language as well as in English — Cameroon's own book teaches citizenship for life at home and in the village, not only for the classroom.
