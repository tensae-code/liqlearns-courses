This lesson is about one thing.
Marriage is the recognised union of spouses — it has forms, it is proved by a marriage certificate, it holds only when its conditions are met, and it gives the spouses duties to each other.

What marriage is and its forms. Marriage is the union of two spouses recognised by law and custom, and it comes in more than one form. Cameroon's book teaches the forms side by side, so a pupil can tell them apart and know what each one means.

The certificate and the conditions. A marriage certificate is the written proof of the marriage, and a marriage is recognised only when its conditions are fulfilled. Without the conditions met and the proof kept, a union can be disputed — the certificate protects both spouses and the children.

The spouses' duties — and failed marriage. Married spouses owe each other faithfulness, help and respect; when those duties collapse, a marriage can fail. The duties are the daily content of the promise: a marriage stands or falls on how the spouses treat each other.

Now you. Say it back, in your own words. Then go and do it — today, at home, at school, in your street.
