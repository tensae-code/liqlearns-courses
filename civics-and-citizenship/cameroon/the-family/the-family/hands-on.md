# Hands-on — The Family

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Name the people who live in your home or care for you every day. Say one thing each person does for the family, and one thing you do back. That circle of people, and what you owe each other, is this lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: a family is the first group a child belongs to — people joined together who owe each other care and support, and whose unity helps the child while their quarrels harm the child. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in the village. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
