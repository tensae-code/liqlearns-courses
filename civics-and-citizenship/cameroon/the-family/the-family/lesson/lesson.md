# The Family

## Do it first

Name the people who live in your home or care for you every day. Say one thing each person does for the family, and one thing you do back. That circle of people, and what you owe each other, is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a family is

A family is a group of people joined by birth, marriage or care, living as one household and bound to each other.

It is the first society a child ever meets: before school, before the village, the child learns belonging inside the family.

## Sub lesson 2 — The types of family

Families take different forms — a small household of parents and children, or a wide household of many relatives together.

The form can differ from house to house; what makes it a family is the bond and the duties, not the size.

## Sub lesson 3 — What unites or harms it — and our duties

Love, respect and shared work unite a family; neglect and quarrels harm it — and the child and the family owe each other duties both ways.

The child owes respect and help; the family owes the child care and protection. Each side keeps the other strong.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: a family is the first group a child belongs to — people joined together who owe each other care and support, and whose unity helps the child while their quarrels harm the child. Give one real example from your own life in Cameroon, and name the lesson's key words: family, household, duties. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in your own language as well as in English — Cameroon's own book teaches citizenship for life at home and in the village, not only for the classroom.
