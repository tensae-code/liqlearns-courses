# The Responsibilities of Parents

## Do it first

Watch a parent or guardian for one morning. Count the things they do before anyone thanks them: feeding, preparing, teaching, correcting. This lesson names that work — the responsibilities of parents.

## Sub lesson 1 — Responsible parenthood

Responsible parenthood means having children you can care for, and then truly caring for them — feeding, clothing, schooling and guiding them.

Parenthood in the book is a duty before it is a joy: the child did not choose to come, so the parents owe the care.

## Sub lesson 2 — The father's and the mother's duties

Father and mother each carry duties in the home, and the mother stands as the child's first educator — the first teacher of words, manners and values.

Long before school, the child learns by watching the parents; what the home teaches first, the school builds on.

## Sub lesson 3 — Parent–child communication

Parents and children must truly talk and listen to each other — communication is how duties are explained and trust is kept.

A home where no one listens raises children who stop speaking; the book teaches talk as a parental duty, not a favour.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: parents carry the family's first responsibilities: responsible parenthood, the father's duties and the mother's duties — the mother as the child's first educator — held together by real parent–child communication. Give one real example from your own life in Cameroon, and name the lesson's key words: parents, responsible parenthood, educator, communication. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in your own language as well as in English — Cameroon's own book teaches citizenship for life at home and in the village, not only for the classroom.
