# About the Civics and Citizenship — Cameroon course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this
course comes from government textbooks — this country's own
Citizenship Education books (Books 1–2, the ANUCAM volume). The
one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us,
the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the
books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Cameroon inside the Civics and
Citizenship folder (one course per country). Inside it, the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place is
found by what they prove, and each level stands on the ones
before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced gate.
The school grades the book comes from (secondary Books 1–2)
appear only in each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

The course has **four levels**, which are the book's own order:

1. **Level 1 — The Family** (Book 1 opening). Family, marriage,
   the child's rights and duties, parents' responsibilities,
   divorce, family protection — in the book's own order.
   6 lessons, in the title `the-family/`.
2. **Level 2 — Belonging, Rights and School Life** (Book 1
   rest). The extension of the family (clan, tribe, ethnic
   groups, tribalism), then human dignity and human rights,
   then school life whole — the school day, the road, the
   school as institution, and guidance. 9 lessons, in the
   titles `belonging/`, `rights/` and `school-life/`.
3. **Level 3 — The Village** (Book 2 opening). The village, its
   traditional organisation and values, its economy and its
   restoration. 5 lessons, in the title `the-village/`.
4. **Level 4 — Local Government** (Book 2 rest). Councils,
   their management, resources and budget, their services, and
   Cameroon's regional organisation: the ladder ends at the
   citizen's first state, as the book lays it out. 6 lessons,
   in the title `local-government/`.

**Cameroon's own way:** the course starts inside the family —
marriage, divorce and parenthood taught head-on — moves through
clan and tribe to human dignity and rights, then to school as
an institution, and in Book 2 to the village, its traditional
authorities, and the council as the citizen's first state. Each
chapter of the book closes with a Civic and Moral Education
section (named in the contents map below; it is part of its
chapter and is not stocked as separate lessons).

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
book's own shape, walked in order):

- *From the family to the region:* "Start at home: the family,
  marriage, your rights and duties as a child, your parents'
  responsibilities. Then widen: clan, tribe, dignity, rights —
  and school, the first institution. Then the village and its
  chiefs and values. Last, the council and the region — the
  citizen's first state. That is Cameroon's Citizenship
  Education: home first, the state last, every step yours."

## The way of the book (its own shape, preserved)

**Citizenship Education, Books 1–2 (ANUCAM)** — a secondary
citizenship course in two books bound as one volume. Book 1
works from the inside out: the family first (its forms,
marriage, the child's and parents' reciprocal duties, divorce,
protection), then the family's extension into clan, tribe and
ethnic group with tribalism named as the danger, then the human
being's dignity and rights, then the school treated whole — the
day, the road, the institution, its working, its environment,
and guidance into a calling. Book 2 works from the village
upward: the village and its traditional organisation and
values, its economic weight and its restoration, then the
council — its types, management, resources, budget and daily
services — and finally the regions. Every chapter closes with a
Civic and Moral Education section. This course preserves that
way: each lesson is one chapter of the book, taught from its
contents entry, in our own words. *(Student book — read at
contents level only: PARTIAL.)*
