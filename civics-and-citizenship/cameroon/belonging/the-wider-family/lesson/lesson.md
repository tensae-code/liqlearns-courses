# The Extension of the Family

## Do it first

Start with your family, then name the wider group your family belongs to, then one group different from yours that you know. This lesson walks that widening — clan, tribe, ethnic group — and names its danger.

## Sub lesson 1 — Clan, tribe and ethnic group

Beyond the household stand the clan (families joined by a common ancestor), the tribe, and the ethnic group — each wider circle of belonging.

A Cameroonian belongs to all these circles at once; none of them cancels the family, each one extends it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Cameroon's major ethnic groups

Cameroon holds many major ethnic groups, each with its own language, customs and homeland inside one country.

The book teaches the pupil to know the country's groups as neighbours in one nation, not as strangers.

## Sub lesson 3 — The dangers of tribalism

Tribalism — favouring your own group against others and against the nation — divides the country and breeds injustice and conflict.

Belonging is a gift; tribalism is belonging turned into a weapon. The lesson keeps the first and refuses the second.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the family widens into clan, tribe and ethnic group — Cameroon's people belong to many groups, each with its own heritage — and tribalism, putting your group against the nation, is the danger that widening must refuse. Give one real example from your own life in Cameroon, and name the lesson's key words: clan, tribe, ethnic group, tribalism. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in your own language as well as in English — Cameroon's own book teaches citizenship for life at home and in the village, not only for the classroom.
