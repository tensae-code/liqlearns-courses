# The Day-to-Day Life of the Council and the Quality of Life of the Population

## Do it first

List everything your household used today that a council touches: the road, the market, the water point, a birth certificate. This lesson counts those services — the day-to-day life of the council.

## Sub lesson 1 — The services a council provides — and their purpose

Day by day the council provides services — each one existing for a purpose in the population's life, from markets and roads to civil records.

A service is the council made visible: citizens judge the council not by its speeches but by its water, roads and records.

## Sub lesson 2 — The importance of the council area

The council area — the community the council serves — matters because it is the scale at which services meet real lives.

Quality of life is decided locally: the area where a citizen lives is where the state first succeeds or fails for him.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the council proves itself daily: the services it provides — water, markets, roads, records and the rest — are its purpose, and they are what raise the quality of life in its area. Give one real example from your own life in Cameroon, and name the lesson's key words: services, quality of life, council area. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in your own language as well as in English — Cameroon's own book teaches citizenship for life at home and in the village, not only for the classroom.
