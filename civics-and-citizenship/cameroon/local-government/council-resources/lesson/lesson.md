# Resources of the Council

## Do it first

A council promises a road, a market, clean water. Ask the hard question: with what? This lesson follows the money in — the resources of the council and where they come from.

## Sub lesson 1 — Council resources and their sources

A council's resources are the money and means it may use, and they come from known sources — its own revenues, grants and the taxes of its area.

A source that is known can be checked; the book teaches citizens to know where their council's money should come from.

## Sub lesson 2 — Why every citizen must pay council taxes

Council taxes are every citizen's share in the services all enjoy — paying them is a duty of citizenship, because without them the council can do nothing.

The tax is the lesson's circle closing: citizens fund the council, and the council serves the citizens. Break either side and both fail.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: a council can only serve with resources: they come from known sources — above all the taxes of its own citizens — which is why every citizen must pay council taxes honestly. Give one real example from your own life in Cameroon, and name the lesson's key words: resources, taxes, duty. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in your own language as well as in English — Cameroon's own book teaches citizenship for life at home and in the village, not only for the classroom.
