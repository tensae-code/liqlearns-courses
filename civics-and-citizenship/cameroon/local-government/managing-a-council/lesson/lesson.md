# The Management of a Council

## Do it first

Take the council of the last lesson and open its doors. Who sits where, who signs what, and how did each person get there? This lesson is the management of a council.

## Sub lesson 1 — The organisation chart and officials' duties

A council works from an organisation chart — its services and posts in order — and each official, from the mayor down, carries defined duties.

The chart is the citizen's map: it shows who answers for what, so nothing is everyone's job and therefore no one's.

## Sub lesson 2 — Composition — and how positions are gained

The municipal council is composed of members in a known number and manner, and its positions are gained through election by the people of the area.

Election is the root of the council's right to act: its authority rises from the voters, and returns to them.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: a council is managed in the open: it has an organisation chart, its officials carry defined duties, the municipal council is composed in a known way, and its positions are gained by election, not by favour. Give one real example from your own life in Cameroon, and name the lesson's key words: management, organisation chart, mayor, election. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in your own language as well as in English — Cameroon's own book teaches citizenship for life at home and in the village, not only for the classroom.
