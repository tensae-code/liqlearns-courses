# The Council Budget

## Do it first

Give a household one month of money on one page: what comes in, what each thing costs, what is left. A council does the same at the scale of a town. This lesson is the council budget.

## Sub lesson 1 — What the council budget is — and its use

The budget is the council's planned statement of its resources and its spending for a period — the tool that turns money into services in an order citizens can follow.

A budget is a promise in numbers: each line says what the community will get for its money.

## Sub lesson 2 — Civic spirit — and managing the budget well

Civic spirit is the citizen's care for the common good — and it is why the budget must be managed well, spent on what it promised, and accounted for.

Good management is not a favour from officials; it is what the citizens' own civic spirit demands and watches.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the council budget is the council's money planned in the open — what comes in, what it is used for — and civic spirit is what makes citizens care that it is managed well. Give one real example from your own life in Cameroon, and name the lesson's key words: budget, civic spirit, management. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in your own language as well as in English — Cameroon's own book teaches citizenship for life at home and in the village, not only for the classroom.
