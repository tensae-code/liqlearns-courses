# From the Home to the School

## Do it first

Walk your road to school in your mind. Name its parts, its crossings, its dangers. This lesson teaches that road — its signs, its responsible use, and why accidents happen.

## Sub lesson 1 — The parts of the road and its signs

A road has parts — carriageway, pavement or verge, crossings — and signs that speak to everyone who uses it.

A pupil who can read the road's parts and signs can be read by the road's other users: drivers see a child who behaves predictably.

## Sub lesson 2 — Responsible road use

Responsible road use means walking facing care, crossing where and how one should, and never playing on the carriageway.

Most road rules for children are one rule in many shapes: be seen, be predictable, stay out of the vehicles' path.

## Sub lesson 3 — Causes and damage of road accidents

Road accidents come from named causes — speed, carelessness, disobeyed signs — and their damage falls on bodies, families and goods.

Teaching the causes is the prevention: an accident with a known cause is an accident a careful pupil can help avoid.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the road to school must be used responsibly: the pupil knows the road's parts and signs, crosses and walks with care, and knows that road accidents come from causes that care can prevent. Give one real example from your own life in Cameroon, and name the lesson's key words: road, signs, accidents, road use. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in your own language as well as in English — Cameroon's own book teaches citizenship for life at home and in the village, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: the road to school must be used responsibly: the pupil knows the road's parts and signs, crosses and walks with care, and knows that road accidents come from causes that care can prevent.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
