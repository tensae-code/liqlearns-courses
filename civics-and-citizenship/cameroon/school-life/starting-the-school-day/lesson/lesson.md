# How to Start a School Day

## Do it first

Think of your last rushed morning — the forgotten book, the late arrival. Now think of a calm one. This lesson teaches the difference: a school day is started the eve before, not at the gate.

## Sub lesson 1 — The eve of a school day

On the evening before, the pupil prepares: lessons reviewed, books and materials packed, clothes made ready.

Half the morning's peace is bought the night before; preparation is the pupil's first responsibility.

## Sub lesson 2 — The morning of a school day

In the morning the pupil rises in time, washes, eats, dresses and leaves early enough to arrive without hurry.

Each morning act has its order and its hour; a pupil who keeps them starts learning before the first lesson begins.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: a school day starts the evening before: the pupil prepares at night and acts in order in the morning, so that the day's learning begins on time and unhurried. Give one real example from your own life in Cameroon, and name the lesson's key words: school day, preparation, responsibilities. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in your own language as well as in English — Cameroon's own book teaches citizenship for life at home and in the village, not only for the classroom.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: a school day starts the evening before: the pupil prepares at night and acts in order in the morning, so that the day's learning begins on time and unhurried.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
