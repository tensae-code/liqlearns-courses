# The School Institution

## Do it first

Name the adults at your school and what each one does. Then name the paper that proves you are a pupil there. This lesson puts those pieces together: the school as an institution.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a school institution is — and its types

A school institution is an organised body for teaching, with a structure and a purpose — and schools come in types, by level and by kind.

Calling the school an institution teaches the pupil that it runs on roles and rules, not on whoever shouts loudest.

## Sub lesson 2 — Staff and the student's documents

Known staff run the school — head, teachers and others, each with a role — and the student holds documents, such as his school identity, that prove his place.

Documents protect the pupil: his record and identity card say who he is and what he has done at school.

## Sub lesson 3 — Protecting the school environment

The school's environment — its buildings, grounds and materials — belongs to all its pupils, present and future, and must be protected.

A pupil who damages his school damages his own place of learning and the place of those who come after him.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: a school is an institution, not just a building: it has types, it is run by known staff, the student holds documents that prove his place in it, and its environment must be protected. Give one real example from your own life in Cameroon, and name the lesson's key words: school institution, staff, documents, environment. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in your own language as well as in English — Cameroon's own book teaches citizenship for life at home and in the village, not only for the classroom.
