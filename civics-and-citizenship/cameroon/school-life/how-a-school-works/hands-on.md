# Hands-on — How a School Institution Works

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Yesterday at school, something worked — a lesson happened, a problem was solved, a meal or a record appeared. Ask whose duty each part was. This lesson maps how a school institution actually works.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: a school works through people doing defined duties: its staff each carry a role, its councils decide together, its support services help the pupils, and its internal rules hold the whole together. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, in the village. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
