# How a School Institution Works

## Do it first

Yesterday at school, something worked — a lesson happened, a problem was solved, a meal or a record appeared. Ask whose duty each part was. This lesson maps how a school institution actually works.

## Sub lesson 1 — Staff duties

Each member of staff — head, teachers, and the rest — carries defined duties, and the school works when each duty is done.

Defined duties mean a pupil always knows who to ask, and a failure can always be traced and repaired.

## Sub lesson 2 — School councils and support services

The school's councils bring its members together to decide, and its support services — health, guidance and others — help pupils beyond the classroom.

A school is not run by orders alone; its councils deliberate and its services catch the pupils the classroom cannot reach.

## Sub lesson 3 — Internal rules

The school's internal rules state what is expected of everyone inside it, and they bind staff and pupils alike.

Rules known by all and applied to all are what make the institution fair instead of arbitrary.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: a school works through people doing defined duties: its staff each carry a role, its councils decide together, its support services help the pupils, and its internal rules hold the whole together. Give one real example from your own life in Cameroon, and name the lesson's key words: staff duties, school councils, support services, internal rules. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Say the lesson's key words aloud once in your own language as well as in English — Cameroon's own book teaches citizenship for life at home and in the village, not only for the classroom.
