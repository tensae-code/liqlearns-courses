# Lesson plan — Facing Problems and What Low Self-Worth Costs

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Myself · **Lesson:** Facing Problems and What Low Self-Worth Costs
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — Myself (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Problems come to everyone.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the lessons before it in Level 1 — Myself — and on the learner's own daily life at home, at school and in the community. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **“a picture of …” in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Facing problems** — Problems come to everyone.
2. **What low self-worth costs** — A low view of yourself does damage.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Level 1 — Myself. Recommended, never forced.
- The learner's own experience of the lesson's subject in daily life is the real doorway; the Do it first opens there.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say and show what “Facing problems” teaches, in their own words, and do the practice named for it.
2. Say and show what “What low self-worth costs” teaches, in their own words, and do the practice named for it.
3. Carry the lesson's practice into the week: naming the next small step through a problem instead of fleeing it.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — every activity uses the learner's own home, school and street.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Think of one small problem you have been avoiding. Name it honestly. Then name the very next small step through it — the stepping stone, not the whole journey. Take that step today if you can.
2. **The explanation** — the lesson's parts, in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 28204]: a picture of a child at a fork — one path around a puddle-sized problem, one straight through with a stepping stone — the student studies the picture and names what it shows about the lesson before reading on.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the lesson's key sentences aloud — the student listens once, then reads them aloud in the same voice.
3. **Say it to your companion** — as written in lesson/lesson.md.
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the student watches and then does the Say it to your companion with a real companion.
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-or-false, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the numbered doing block at the end of lesson/lesson.md.

## If the learner is stuck
- Cannot say what the lesson teaches → go back to the Do it first and the picture; name what the picture shows, in one sentence, then read the first part again slowly.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → hold the one thing: Problems come to everyone.
- Finds the practice hard → shrink it to the smallest real version and do that today; small and done beats grand and promised.
