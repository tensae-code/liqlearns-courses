# Facing Problems and What Low Self-Worth Costs

## Do it first

Think of one small problem you have been avoiding. Name it honestly. Then name the very next small step through it — the stepping stone, not the whole journey. Take that step today if you can.

## Sub lesson 1 — Facing problems

Problems come to everyone. Facing a problem means meeting it rather than fleeing it: look at what the problem really is, take the next small step through it, and ask for help where help is truly needed. Running away does not shrink a problem — it usually lets the problem grow while you lose practice at solving.

## Sub lesson 2 — What low self-worth costs

A low view of yourself does damage. A person who believes they are worth little attempts little, flees problems, and lets chances pass; over time the damage spreads — less learning, weaker friendships, and a shrinking life. That damage is exactly what the lessons before this one exist to prevent. Facing problems is one of the ways the damage is turned back.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: what facing a problem means, and one cost of low self-worth. Then tell them the small problem you chose and your next step through it.

## Do It Yourself

1. Choose one avoided problem and take the first small step through it today.
2. Write down one cost of low self-worth you have seen or felt.
3. When you want to flee a problem this week, pause and ask: what is the next small step?
