# Lesson plan — Keeping Myself Safe

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Myself · **Lesson:** Keeping Myself Safe
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — Myself (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** keeping yourself safe — from harmful acts to body and feelings, and at school: safe routes, safety rules, and telling a trusted adult.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the lessons before it in this title — the learner already knows the earlier circles of this course (myself, then family, neighbours, school, community and culture) as far as this title. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Keeping safe from harmful acts** — Keeping yourself safe is a skill to be shown, not just named
2. **Staying safe at school** — Know the routes you travel to and from school — and an alternative route if a road is blocked.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Respecting Myself — What It Is, Why It Matters, How It Grows (the previous lesson in this title). Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Keeping yourself safe is a skill to be shown, not just named.
2. The practice has three steps.
3. Harm to feelings counts too.
4. Know the routes you travel to and from school — and an alternative route if a road is blocked.
5. Know and follow the school's safety rules, the way the adults around you follow them.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson's scenes are the learner's own home, school and neighbourhood.

## If the learner is stuck
- Believes that “if you feel unsafe at school, the right thing is to tell no one and keep it secret” → go back to the lesson's own words: keeping yourself safe — from harmful acts to body and feelings, and at school: safe routes, safety rules, and telling a trusted adult. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
- Gives the words but no example → ask for the lesson happening once in their own home, school or street today; the example is the proof.
