# Keeping Myself Safe

## Do it first

Think of your way to school. Name the safest road you use. Now name what you would do if that road were blocked one morning. This lesson makes that plan strong — and adds what to do when anything feels unsafe.

## Sub lesson 1 — Keeping safe from harmful acts

Keeping yourself safe is a skill to be shown, not just named: useful practices for protecting your own safety from physically and emotionally harmful acts.

The practice has three steps: stop, move away, tell an adult.

Harm to feelings counts too: acts that frighten, shame or hurt you inside are harmful acts, and the same three steps protect you.

## Sub lesson 2 — Staying safe at school

Know the routes you travel to and from school — and an alternative route if a road is blocked.

Know and follow the school's safety rules, the way the adults around you follow them: they are there to protect everyone.

If you feel unsafe: find a teacher, reach a trusted neighbour, and know how to reach your own family. The school-safety reading prints the police number 995.

Tell school staff what they need to protect you — a health condition, a food allergy, or bullying — so they can help.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: keeping yourself safe — from harmful acts to body and feelings, and at school: safe routes, safety rules, and telling a trusted adult. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: trusted adult, alternative route, safety rules. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: protecting your own safety, at home and at school.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
