# Self-Control — What It Is and How a Self-Controlled Child Acts

## Do it first

Think of one moment today when you wanted to do something you knew was wrong — to shout, to snatch, to skip a duty. What did you do? Hold that moment in mind; this lesson is about the strength that guides such moments.

## What self-control is

Self-control is the right moral behaviour a child develops and keeps.

It is learned: children try hard to learn and understand the rules of good conduct, and practise them every day.

A self-controlled child has the courage to ask when facing a moral problem, instead of hiding it.

## How a self-controlled child acts

Such children respect their parents.

They keep themselves clean, and they eat properly.

They help their parents and others grow in ability.

They play with friends safely and honestly.

They do not wait to be told before doing a good thing.

They are peaceful and hardworking — in the neighbourhood, at school and in the family.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: self-control is the right moral behaviour a child grows and keeps — shown in clean, kind, honest, hardworking daily acts. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: self-control, moral problem, good conduct. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
