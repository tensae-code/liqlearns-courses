# When Self-Control Is Missing — What Follows

## Do it first

Think of a quarrel you have seen that began with one uncontrolled act — a snatched toy, an angry shout. Act out in your mind what happened next, step by step. This lesson follows that chain.

## Finding the results

The chapter closes by having pupils identify the results of not controlling oneself.

The book's own way of learning them is to act them out: pupils show the negative outcomes of a lack of self-control in a short drama.

The chain is plain: an act done without control — snatching, shouting, striking, refusing every duty — brings quarrels, hurt feelings, broken trust and trouble at home and at school.

## Choosing the other road

Seeing the results clearly is itself a protection: a child who knows where the uncontrolled road leads can stop at its start.

The stop is the skill of the earlier lessons: pause, ask about the moral problem, and do the good thing without waiting to be told.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: when a child does not control himself, unhappy results follow — for the child and for the people around him. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: lack of self-control, outcome. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: the results that follow when self-control is missing.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
