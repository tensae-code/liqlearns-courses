# Self-Confidence and Self-Control

## Do it first

Practise both now. First, confidence: volunteer an answer or an idea out loud, even if you are not fully sure. Then, control: think of the last time you wanted to snatch, shout or rush — replay it slowly, and pause and breathe at the moment of choice.

## Sub lesson 1 — Self-confidence

Self-confidence is trusting that you can do a thing — and then attempting it. A confident child raises a hand to answer even when the answer might be wrong, because trying is how ability grows. Confidence is not knowing you will succeed every time; it is believing the attempt is worth making.

## Sub lesson 2 — Self-control

Self-control is holding back your impulses and governing your own behaviour. The impulse says: snatch it, shout it, hit back now. Self-control says: pause, breathe, choose. First learn to recognise self-control when you see it — in yourself and in others — then practise it in the small moments, because the small moments are where it is built.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: what self-confidence is, and what self-control is. Give one example of each from real life — yours or someone you have seen.

## Do It Yourself

1. Raise your hand or speak up once today, even if your answer may be wrong.
2. When an impulse comes today — to snatch, shout or rush — pause, breathe, then choose.
3. Tonight, name one moment where you controlled yourself well.
