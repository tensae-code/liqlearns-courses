# Moral Integrity — Inside and Outside in Agreement

## Do it first

Think of one thing you believe is right. Now remember the last time your actions matched that belief — and one time they did not. Hold both in mind. This lesson is about closing the gap between the two.

## What integrity is

Moral integrity is lasting agreement between your inner thought and your outer action. What you hold inside — your values, your judgement of right and wrong — is what your hands and words actually do outside, not just today but as a pattern of life. A person of integrity is one person, the same inside and outside.

## What integrity is made of

Living by your basic values is part of integrity: the answers name its components — steadfastness in thought, basic values, honesty, self-confidence and faith. Steadfastness means your thought does not blow about with every wind; honesty means the agreement is truthful; self-confidence means you dare to act on what you hold.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what integrity means in one sentence: inside and outside in agreement. Then name its components as far as you remember, and give one example of thought and action matching.

## Do It Yourself

1. Catch one moment today where your action matched your belief — note it.
2. Catch one mismatch, and repair it if you can: apologise, redo, or tell the truth.
3. Choose one basic value you hold, and write one action that would show it this week.
