# Peace — Positive and Negative

## Do it first

Two classrooms after a quarrel. In one, the teacher has silenced everyone and the air is heavy; nobody speaks, nobody has forgiven. In the other, the quarrel was heard out, wrongs were named and mended, and the noise is back — work noise, laughing noise. Which room is at peace? Write your answer and hold it against the book's pair of words.

## The contrast pair

**Positive peace** is true, lasting, sustainable peace built on justice for all: the presence of the attitudes, institutions and structures that create and sustain peaceful societies. It works at eliminating the root causes of war, violence and injustice — meeting citizens' needs, reducing grievances, and resolving disagreements without violence. Its efforts run through non-violent dispute resolution, social equality and justice, economic equity, ecological balance, protection from attack, basic-needs provision, constitutional means of settlement, elimination of indirect violence, and conflict resolution as the foundation of interpersonal relationships.

**Negative peace** is peace without justice — a false calm bought at justice's cost: the mere absence of direct violence or fear of it, with tension boiling beneath an unreconciled surface. Its efforts manage and contain conflict, limit war through crisis management, deterrence and arms control — addressing symptoms rather than causes.

The book's **contrast table** fixes the pair in memory. Negative peace is the column of **absence**: war, fear, repression, evil — gone, and nothing put in their place. Positive peace is the column of **presence**: tranquillity, harmony and wellbeing, strengthened human bonds, shared human values, shared feelings of humanity. When you judge any peace, ask which column you are looking at.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: the two definitions, then the table's two columns — absence against presence. Finish with the two classrooms from Do it first: which column was each?

## Do It Yourself

1. Take one real ceasefire or settlement you know of. Judge it: positive or negative peace? Give three pieces of evidence from the lesson's lists.
2. Write the contrast table from memory in two columns. Then add a third column: your school. Fill it honestly.
3. Find one symptom-treating effort and one cause-removing effort in your locality's peace work. Name both, and say which kind each is.
