# Indigenous Institutions and Peace

## Do it first

A settlement has ended — hands shaken, compensation agreed. Is the work finished? The two families must still share the well, the market, the path, for the rest of their lives. Write what could still go wrong between them. This lesson names the five roles by which indigenous institutions guard the peace after the settlement.

## Sub lesson 1 — Cohesion and restoration

The first role is **building community cohesion**: keeping community values alive; building common ground, togetherness and collective spirit that enhance solidarity and self-governance and can be harnessed for community-led development. Its marks: wide participation in the judicial process; a consensus process letting accuser and accused argue without interruption until one side persuades; and the virtues of peace, solidarity, harmonious living, respect and honesty promoted — with sanctions guaranteeing future conduct. 

[Picture 28938 — A whole village eating together after a settlement, the two reconciled families at one table]



The second role is **restoration of order and relationships** — aimed at the **future, not punishment for the past**: re-establishing the basis for reconciliation, and reintegrating deviant members rather than harming the group a second time, since the ultimate aim of sustainable peace is restored relationships.

## Sub lesson 2 — Healing, inclusion, access

The third role addresses the **psycho-social and spiritual dimensions**: dealing not only with material issues, reason and talk, but with feelings, non-verbal communication and spirit — purification and healing by customary healers, priests, sheiks and other spiritual authorities, as mental and spiritual rehabilitation of victims and perpetrators alike, at least as important as material reconstruction. 

[Picture 28939 — A healer blessing two reconciled men while their extended families watch in one circle]



The fourth is **inclusion and participation**: all parties and every member responsible for both conflict and solution; consensus producing win-win outcomes covering honour, prestige and face, not only material interests; and the supplementary activities around negotiation valued, since custom does not isolate political events from their whole context.

The fifth is **accessibility**: immediate, community-near settlement before conflicts protract and endanger harmony; deep knowledge of conflicts' backgrounds and past relationships; and reconciliation treated as a long-term process — not a quick-fix military solution. The guide's picture is an institution house with its door open to the path, people of both sides entering. 

[Picture 28940 — A village institution house with its door open to the path, people of both sides entering (teacher guide — evidence)]

 Nearness is itself one of the roles.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: the five roles in the book's order. Then answer: a settlement is signed — which roles are still working the next morning?

## Do It Yourself

1. Take the reconciliation you know best. Score it against the five roles: which did the institution truly perform? Evidence per role.
2. Write why restoration 'reintegrates deviant members rather than harming the group a second time' — in your own words, with one example.
3. Accessibility is nearness. Measure your locality: how far is the nearest customary settlement, and how far the nearest formal court — in time, cost and welcome? Write the comparison.
