# Lesson plan — Indigenous Institutions and Peace

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Taking Part, Conflict and Peace · **Lesson:** Indigenous Institutions and Peace
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 20 — Taking Part, Conflict and Peace (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** indigenous institutions keep peace in five roles — building cohesion, restoring relationships, healing the inner wounds, including everyone, and standing near enough to reach.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the home-knowledge level, where settling conflicts was first met at home — this level is its Grade 10 depth: what peace is, what its absence costs, and how Ethiopia's own institutions build it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Cohesion and restoration** — The first two roles: cohesion built before conflict, and relationships restored after it — future-facing, not punitive.
2. **Healing, inclusion, access** — The last three roles: healing inside and out, everyone included in conflict and solution, and justice that stands near.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Indigenous Conflict Resolution in Ethiopia, just before this lesson in this level. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the first two roles: cohesion and restoration.
2. Name the last three: healing, inclusion, access.
3. Say how restoration aims at the future, not punishment for the past.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.
