# Lesson plan — Indigenous Conflict Resolution in Ethiopia

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Taking Part, Conflict and Peace · **Lesson:** Indigenous Conflict Resolution in Ethiopia
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 20 — Taking Part, Conflict and Peace (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** with more than eighty ethnic groups, Ethiopia holds many indigenous systems — Makabanna, Shimagille, Bayto, Jarsumma, Sera, Xeer, Korefinie, Ye Joka — each society's own way, led by its elders.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the home-knowledge level, where settling conflicts was first met at home — this level is its Grade 10 depth: what peace is, what its absence costs, and how Ethiopia's own institutions build it.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the eight systems and their peoples, in this book's spellings.
2. Say what range of conflicts these systems answer.
3. Say where their legitimacy comes from, and who leads the process.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Reads the eight as the whole count → the book names eight as its worked list inside a country of more than eighty groups and numerous mechanisms; the eight are the named systems, not the total.
- Assumes elders rule by office → their standing is age, cultural knowledge, lineage or influence, and their legitimacy is the community's norms and values.
