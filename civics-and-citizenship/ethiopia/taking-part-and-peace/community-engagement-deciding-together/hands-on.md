# Hands-on — Community engagement — deciding together

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
A circle of parents, teachers and officials around a school plan. Arrows run both ways around the circle — nobody only talks, nobody only listens. Before reading, say why both-way arrows matter more than a loudspeaker.

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — a community is a group sharing place or purpose; engagement is its long-term, two-way partnership for deciding what affects it — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
