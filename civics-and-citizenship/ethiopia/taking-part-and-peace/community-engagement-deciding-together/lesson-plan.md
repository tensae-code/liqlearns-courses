# Lesson plan — Community engagement — deciding together

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Taking Part, Conflict and Peace · **Lesson:** Community engagement — deciding together
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 20 — Taking Part, Conflict and Peace (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a community is a group sharing place or purpose; engagement is its long-term, two-way partnership for deciding what affects it.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the levels before it in this course's path order (recommended, never forced); inside its own title it stands on the lessons before it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
1. **Community** — **Community** comes from the Latin *communis* — common, shared.
2. **Engagement** — **Engagement** shifts the focus from the individual to the collective: the process of working with community members on what affects their wellbeing.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the Human Rights level. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define community both ways.
2. Define engagement as a two-way partnership.
3. Give school examples of engagement.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — A circle of parents, teachers and officials around a school plan. Arrows run both ways around the circle — nobody only talks, nobody only listens. Before reading, say why both-way arrows matter more than a loudspeaker.
2. **The explanation** — the lesson's parts in order (lesson/lesson.md).
