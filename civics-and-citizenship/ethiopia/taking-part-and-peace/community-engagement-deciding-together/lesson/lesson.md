# Community engagement — deciding together

## Do it first

A circle of parents, teachers and officials around a school plan. Arrows run both ways around the circle — nobody only talks, nobody only listens. Before reading, say why both-way arrows matter more than a loudspeaker.

## Sub lesson 1 — Community

**Community** comes from the Latin *communis* — common, shared. A community is a social unit sharing place, norms, religion, values, customs or identity.

[Picture 28718 — a circle of parents, teachers and officials around a school plan]

It comes in two kinds. **Geographic:** the people of a village or neighbourhood. **Formed by needs, interests and roles:** a school's pupils, teachers and staff are one community, though they sleep in different streets. If a group shares something real and knows it, it is a community.

## Sub lesson 2 — Engagement

**Engagement** shifts the focus from the individual to the collective: the process of working with community members on what affects their wellbeing.

[Picture 28719 — a village map beside a circle linked by shared interest]

It is a **long-term, two-way partnership** of residents, officials and providers — **not one-way information handed down**. In a school it looks like parents' notes, meetings and home visits: the school speaks, the families answer, and both change the plan.
