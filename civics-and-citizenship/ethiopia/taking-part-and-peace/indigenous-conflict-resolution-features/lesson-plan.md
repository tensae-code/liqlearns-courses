# Lesson plan — Indigenous Conflict Resolution — Features

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Taking Part, Conflict and Peace · **Lesson:** Indigenous Conflict Resolution — Features
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 20 — Taking Part, Conflict and Peace (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Ethiopia's indigenous ways of settling conflict share seven features in the student book — voluntary, flexible, negotiating, dynamic, restorative, forgiving — a set the teacher guide counts as ten.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the home-knowledge level, where settling conflicts was first met at home — this level is its Grade 10 depth: what peace is, what its absence costs, and how Ethiopia's own institutions build it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Peace Building, just before this lesson in this level. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define indigenous conflict resolution in the book's terms.
2. Name the student book's seven shared features.
3. State the guide's ten as the evidence layer — and keep the two counts separate, as the design directs.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner names why both sides accepted a real customary settlement, before the features are listed (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the definition; the seven shared features in order; the guide's ten-feature evidence layer and its video walk, kept separate from the seven (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 28935]: a picture of an elders' circle with two disputants both rising and shaking hands, a compensation calf led between them — the student names the features visible in the scene — voluntariness, negotiation, restoration, compensation.

4. **Questions** — from questions/: fill-in-the-blank, matching, multiple-choice, short-answer, true-or-false — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.

## If the learner is stuck
- Merges the lists into one set of ten taught features → the student book teaches seven; the guide's ten are the evidence layer, kept separate and labelled, as the design directs.
