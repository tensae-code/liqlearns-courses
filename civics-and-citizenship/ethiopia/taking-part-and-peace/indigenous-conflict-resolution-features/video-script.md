# Video script — Indigenous Conflict Resolution — Features

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

[The companion stands where the elders sit. Camera slow.]
Ten features walk past, one by one — the teacher guide's full count, each with its example.
One: context-specific. Every society shapes its own way — as many approaches as there are peoples.
Two: inclusive. The same door opens for a petty offence and for the gravest wrong — homicide, even war.
Three: voluntary and consensual. No one is dragged; the community's pressure, not the policeman's hand, enforces.
Four: locally circumscribed. The settlement belongs to its place and its people, near home.
Five: accepted and flexible norms. Known rules, honestly bent to fit the case at hand.
Six: group-based responsibility. The family and clan answer with the person, not the person alone.
Seven: negotiation and compromise. Both sides yield something; there is no winner and no loser.
Eight: dynamic. The old way is not frozen — it grows, generation by generation, and learns from the formal system beside it.
Nine: restoration of peaceful coexistence. The end is not a verdict — it is neighbours living together again.
Ten: forgiveness and compensation. The wrongdoer asks pardon and restores what was broken — instead of a cell.
Seven of these the student book teaches as its own list. The guide counts ten. This course keeps both counts, labelled — and merges neither into the other.
