# Indigenous conflict resolution — elders, forgiveness, compensation

## Do it first

Elders sit in a crescent under a huge tree. Two former opponents shake hands before them. Nobody is going to prison — yet the conflict is ending. Before reading, ask: what is being restored, if not order through punishment?

## Sub lesson 1 — The shared pattern, and Jaarsumma and Shimgilina

Indigenous mechanisms are **grassroots**: built on **forgiveness**, the elders' **symbolic authority** to enforce decisions, and **compensation** transferred to restore relations — **reconciling families rather than punishing offenders**, through local actors and community judicial custom.

[Picture 28738 — elders in a crescent under a huge odda tree, opponents shaking hands]

**Jaarsumma** (Oromia): reconciliation of individuals and clans by Jaarsaa elders, linked to the Gadaa system, held under the great **odda tree** — the council chosen from a **peacemaking clan not party to the conflict**. **Shimgilina** (Amhara), with **Erq**: mediation and conciliation by the **Yehager Shimagille** — elders, religious fathers and prominent figures — minimising differences toward amicable settlement, used even for disagreements threatening whole communities' peace.

## Sub lesson 2 — Xeer, Yajoka, Mada'a

Somali **Xeer**: an **unwritten, orally transmitted** code letting travel, trade and marriage run safely; elders act as mediator-judges on precedent and custom, **compensation replacing imprisonment** — **Xeer Guud** (criminal and civil, for all clans) against **Xeer Gaar** (one community or clan).

[Picture 28739 — three panels: clan assembly, Gurage council, Sultan with elders]

Gurage **Yajoka**: the Sebat-bet-wide institution preventing and resolving conflict under Gurage customary law, the **Kitchha**, which governs all human relations. Afar **Mada'a**: the traditional legal system standing as **foundation and constitution** for other administration, working through elder reconciliation (Shimglena) and arbitration by tribal leaders including the Sultan and religious leaders.

## Sub lesson 3 — Fieldwork

The lesson leaves the classroom. Pupils **interview parents and elders** about their own community's institution: how it hears, how it decides, how it reconciles.

[Picture 28740 — students with notebooks before village elders under a tree]

The guide sends the class to its **own locality** rather than naming one national model — because the institution that matters most to a learner is the one that would actually sit for their family's dispute.
