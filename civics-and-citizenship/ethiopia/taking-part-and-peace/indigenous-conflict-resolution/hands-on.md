# Hands-on — Indigenous conflict resolution — elders, forgiveness, compensation

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
Elders sit in a crescent under a huge tree. Two former opponents shake hands before them. Nobody is going to prison — yet the conflict is ending. Before reading, ask: what is being restored, if not order through punishment?

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — Ethiopia's own institutions resolve conflict by reconciliation — forgiveness, elders' authority and compensation restoring relations — not by punishment — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
