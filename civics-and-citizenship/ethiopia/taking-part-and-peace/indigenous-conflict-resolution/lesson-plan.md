# Lesson plan — Indigenous conflict resolution — elders, forgiveness, compensation

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Taking Part, Conflict and Peace · **Lesson:** Indigenous conflict resolution — elders, forgiveness, compensation
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 20 — Taking Part, Conflict and Peace (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Ethiopia's own institutions resolve conflict by reconciliation — forgiveness, elders' authority and compensation restoring relations — not by punishment.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the levels before it in this course's path order (recommended, never forced); inside its own title it stands on the lessons before it.

## Sub lessons
1. **The shared pattern, and Jaarsumma and Shimgilina** — Indigenous mechanisms are **grassroots**: built on **forgiveness**, the elders' **symbolic authority** to enforce decisions, and **compensation** transferred to restore relations — **reconciling families rather than punishing offenders**, through local actors and community judicial custom.
2. **Xeer, Yajoka, Mada'a** — Somali **Xeer**: an **unwritten, orally transmitted** code letting travel, trade and marriage run safely; elders act as mediator-judges on precedent and custom, **compensation replacing imprisonment** — **Xeer Guud** (criminal and civil, for all clans) against **Xeer Gaar** (one community or clan).
3. **Fieldwork** — The lesson leaves the classroom.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Elders sit in a crescent under a huge tree. Two former opponents shake hands before them. Nobody is going to prison — yet the conflict is ending. Before reading, ask: what is being restored, if not order through punishment?
