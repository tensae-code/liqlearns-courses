# Conflict resolution — negotiation and the win-win

## Do it first

Two pupils across one table. Each arrives with a full speech bubble. Watch the bubbles as they talk: parts empty out, and a new shared bubble grows between them. Before reading, decide what each pupil must give up — and what neither should have to.

## Talk first, together

The premise: **ignoring conflict aggravates it**. Resolution means the parties **collaborating** to an outcome satisfactory to **both** — a **win-win**, not a winner and a loser.

[Picture 28733 — two pupils across a table, a shared agreement bubble growing]

Of the four peaceful mechanisms — negotiation, mediation, arbitration, litigation — **negotiation** is taught here in depth. The parties face each other **directly**, state feelings, wants and refusals, and build the outcome themselves, civilly and fairly, with **no third party**.

Two tools inside it are distinguished as the book distinguishes them. **Compromise:** each side gives something up. **Collaboration:** the parties work together toward an outcome that serves both fully. Compromise splits the loss; collaboration grows the gain.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson's one thing to your companion in your own words: negotiation resolves conflict face to face: the parties state feelings and wants, and build compromise and collaboration toward a win-win. Use the lesson's own case or example, not the book's sentences. Your companion asks one hard question; answer it from the lesson, or say honestly what you would check.
