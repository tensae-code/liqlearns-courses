# Hands-on — Conflict and peace — what they really are

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
A cafeteria queue. A pupil is bumped from behind, collides with the boy ahead, and shoves are exchanged. Both end up before the vice-principal. Before reading: who is to blame — only the first bumper? Only the shovers? Hold your verdict.

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — conflict is disagreement from opposing views, interests or goals; peace is more than war's absence — it is the presence of justice — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
