# Lesson plan — Conflict and peace — what they really are

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Taking Part, Conflict and Peace · **Lesson:** Conflict and peace — what they really are
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 20 — Taking Part, Conflict and Peace (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** conflict is disagreement from opposing views, interests or goals; peace is more than war's absence — it is the presence of justice.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the levels before it in this course's path order (recommended, never forced); inside its own title it stands on the lessons before it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
1. **Conflict** — **Conflict** is disagreement from opposing viewpoints, ideas, interests or goals.
2. **Peace** — **Peace** is more than war's absence: it is the **presence of justice, law and order**.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Community engagement — the social, political and economic sides. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define conflict and its inevitability.
2. Judge the Nemera and Bayu case.
3. Tell negative peace from positive peace.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — A cafeteria queue. A pupil is bumped from behind, collides with the boy ahead, and shoves are exchanged. Both end up before the vice-principal. Before reading: who is to blame — only the first bumper? Only the shovers? Hold your verdict.
2. **The explanation** — the lesson's parts in order (lesson/lesson.md).
