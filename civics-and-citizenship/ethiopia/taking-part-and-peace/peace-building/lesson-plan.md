# Lesson plan — Peace Building

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Taking Part, Conflict and Peace · **Lesson:** Peace Building
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 20 — Taking Part, Conflict and Peace (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** stopping the fighting does not end the violence — peace building is the long work after: parties talking, relationships repaired, institutions rebuilt to address root causes.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the home-knowledge level, where settling conflicts was first met at home — this level is its Grade 10 depth: what peace is, what its absence costs, and how Ethiopia's own institutions build it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: When Peace Is Absent, just before this lesson in this level. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say why simply stopping fighting is not enough.
2. Name peace building's works: talk, understanding, repaired relationships.
3. Name what successful peace building creates.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Equates peace building with a ceasefire → stopping fighting is where peace building starts, not where it ends; relationships, institutions and root causes are its work.
- Reads the success list as decoration → each item answers a crack from the previous lesson; match them and the list becomes a map.
