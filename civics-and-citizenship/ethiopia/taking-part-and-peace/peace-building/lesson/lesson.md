# Peace Building

## Do it first

Watch the GIF at the top of this lesson before you read: a broken rope, re-tied knot by knot. Count the knots as they are tied. A ceasefire cuts the rope's pulling — the re-tying is something else, slower. This lesson is about the re-tying.

## More than stopping the fighting

Simply stopping fighting does not permanently end violence. **Peace building** is the persistent, long-term work that follows the silence of the guns. 

[Picture 28932 — Former opponents jointly raising the first wall of a shared building]



Its works: encouraging conflicting parties to talk; bringing them together to understand each other's views; and repairing broken relationships. Supporting viable political, socio-economic and cultural institutions able to address root causes and sustain peace and stability. Transforming the institutions and discourse that reproduce cultures of violence. And integrating competing or alienated groups into mainstream society through equitable access to political decision-making, social networks, economic resources and information — in all phases of conflict.



[GIF 28933 — A broken rope being re-tied knot by knot between two hands]

 The guide's evidence picture is quieter than a wall: two former opponents replanting a boundary hedge together — the line between their fields, grown again by both hands. 

[Picture 28934 — Two former opponents replanting a boundary hedge together (teacher guide — evidence)]



Successful peace building creates environments where differences are resolved peacefully and future violence is prevented: reconciling opponents, building rule-of-law mechanisms, increasing tolerance and coexistence, protecting human rights, improving socio-economic development, reforming justice and security institutions, promoting cultures of justice, truth and reconciliation, and addressing underlying structural issues. Count the GIF's knots again: talk, understanding, repair, institutions, integration — the rope holds because every knot was tied, not because the pulling stopped.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: why is a ceasefire not yet peace building? Walk the rope: name one knot for each work of peace building you remember.

## Do It Yourself

1. Take a conflict you know — school, neighbourhood, or news. Write what 'stopping the fighting' would look like, and then what peace building would still have to do afterwards.
2. Choose one item from the success list — rule-of-law mechanisms, truth and reconciliation, structural issues, or another. Write what it would concretely mean in the conflict you chose.
3. The hedge picture: write what a 'boundary hedge' would be in a school conflict — the shared thing both sides must replant together.
