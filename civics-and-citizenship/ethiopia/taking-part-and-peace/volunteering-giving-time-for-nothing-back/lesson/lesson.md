# Volunteering — giving time for nothing back

## Do it first

A woman opens her door to two thin, drought-stricken children. Behind her, the house is already full of children she has taken in. She is not paid. Nobody ordered her. Before reading, ask: what is she doing called — and what, if anything, does she get?

## Sub lesson 1 — Story: the Selfless Mother

The story, in summary — and in this lesson she stays **unnamed**, exactly as this book leaves her, asking its readers to discover whose story it is.

[Picture 28722 — a woman opening her door to two drought-thin children]

She lost her father young and fled a forced marriage at eleven. She built a settled life in Addis Ababa. Then, on a **1972 pilgrimage to Gishen Mariam**, she met two children dying in drought — and took in **21 children within a year**, despite family opposition. She left her marital home to raise them, and founded a children's care association serving **thousands**. Honours and gifts came to her; she never used them for herself. Jimma University gave her an honorary doctorate in **2010**. She died on **27 June 2021**, unrewarded in money to the end.

**Volunteering** is what she did at scale: the unpaid contribution of time, effort and talent to a need or cause — distinct from required or contracted work. A volunteer expects nothing back.

## Sub lesson 2 — What the giver gains

And yet the giver gains. **Connection** — meeting people of every age, ethnicity and walk of life, some becoming lifelong friends. **Self-confidence** — accomplishment that carries back into ordinary life, especially for the shy. **Purpose** — meaning and direction at any age; youth who volunteer stay closer to community, do better at school and avoid risky behaviour. **Career ground** — practised communication, cooperation, problem-solving, planning and organisation, which employers read as self-motivation.

[Picture 28723 — a pupil volunteer in four small scenes]

[Picture 28724 — a woman welcoming children at the door of a modest home]

Nothing back was the promise. These gains are not the payment — they are the surprise.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson's one thing to your companion in your own words: volunteering is the unpaid gift of time, effort and talent — and it returns connection, confidence, purpose and career ground to the giver. Use the lesson's own case or example, not the book's sentences. Your companion asks one hard question; answer it from the lesson, or say honestly what you would check.
