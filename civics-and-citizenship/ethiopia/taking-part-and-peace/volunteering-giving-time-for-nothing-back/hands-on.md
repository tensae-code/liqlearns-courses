# Hands-on — Volunteering — giving time for nothing back

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
A woman opens her door to two thin, drought-stricken children. Behind her, the house is already full of children she has taken in. She is not paid. Nobody ordered her. Before reading, ask: what is she doing called — and what, if anything, does she get?

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — volunteering is the unpaid gift of time, effort and talent — and it returns connection, confidence, purpose and career ground to the giver — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
