# Lesson plan — Peace — Inner and Outer

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Taking Part, Conflict and Peace · **Lesson:** Peace — Inner and Outer
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 20 — Taking Part, Conflict and Peace (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** peace is of two kinds that hold each other up — inner peace, the calm of a mind free of worry and ill-will, and outer peace, a society with harmony, justice and respect for rights.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the home-knowledge level, where settling conflicts was first met at home — this level is its Grade 10 depth: what peace is, what its absence costs, and how Ethiopia's own institutions build it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. ## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the home-knowledge level — settling conflicts the Ethiopian way. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define peace from daily life, as the book does.
2. Describe inner peace and why the book calls it true peace and the foundation.
3. Describe outer peace in both its halves — evils absent, virtues present.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Defines peace as only the absence of war → outer peace is also the presence of harmony, justice, equality, friendship and rights-respect; and inner peace is its foundation.
- Treats inner peace as private and unconnected → the two are interrelated and interdependent; the worried person is always ready to fight.
