# When Peace Is Absent

## Do it first

Break a clay pot in your mind — you cannot break only one part of it. The cracks run. This lesson is the book's map of the cracks: what happens, in five directions, when peace is absent from a society.

## Sub lesson 1 — Security, harmony, diversity

The first crack is **security**: direct and structural violence and destruction — including exclusion built into structures, oppression through police or judiciary denying participation — breeding painful government–society relations and a **chain of violent conflict**, as each side seeks the top and payback. 

[Picture 28929 — A cracked communal water pot with three labelled cracks]



The second is **continuous social and political disharmony**: long-term erosion of cohesion and unity; helplessness and enmity; suspicion and hatred; in-groups against out-groups; accusation and counter-accusation; escalation through bloody feud to civil war and internal factionalism.

The third is **failure to accommodate cultural diversity**: groups competing for power and resource control, leaders and officials becoming more tribal than national, inter-tribal oppression common, antagonism between groupings entrenched. The guide counts the cracks on one village: unsafe homes, broken friendships, a closed market, an empty school, a fleeing family — that village is this whole lesson in one picture, and it returns in the second sub lesson.

## Sub lesson 2 — Livelihoods and rights

The fourth impact is **undermined socio-economic development**: resources diverted from health, education and investment to military spending; weapons accumulation feeding violent crime; trauma, social stress, eroded trust, prejudice and injustice; trade, farming, fishing and investment declining amid fear of confiscation and blocked movement; displacement and migration as people cannot meet basic needs, bringing social alienation and fragmentation; tax bases destroyed while military spending rises. 

[Picture 28930 — An empty market street with a closed school and clinic behind shuttered doors]



The fifth is **grave human-rights violations**: weak institutions unable to manage power struggles or protect rights; women and children at high risk of sexual and gender-based violence, abuse and displacement; people generally deprived of dignity, development, schooling and health services, and impoverished.

The guide's one village carries all five cracks at once. 

[Picture 28931 — A village with five cracks — unsafe homes, broken friendships, a closed market, an empty school, a fleeing family (teacher guide — evidence)]

 Read it slowly: every crack in that picture is a heading of this lesson, and every heading is a crack in somebody's real village.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: the five impacts in the book's order. Then tell the guide's village as a story — five cracks, one village — matching each crack to its impact.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw the cracked pot: five cracks lettered with the five impacts. Between two cracks, draw the arrow that joins them and write how one feeds the other.
2. Take the development list. Which three mechanisms could you verify from news or local knowledge this year? Write the mechanism and the evidence.
3. Women and children stand at highest risk in the fifth impact. Write what a community that took this seriously would do differently before conflict, not after.
