# Lesson plan — When Peace Is Absent

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Taking Part, Conflict and Peace · **Lesson:** When Peace Is Absent
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 20 — Taking Part, Conflict and Peace (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** where peace is absent, the damage runs in five directions — security, harmony, diversity, development and rights — each one feeding the others.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the home-knowledge level, where settling conflicts was first met at home — this level is its Grade 10 depth: what peace is, what its absence costs, and how Ethiopia's own institutions build it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Security, harmony, diversity** — The society cracking: violence and its chain, disharmony deepening, diversity turned into competition.
2. **Livelihoods and rights** — The living destroyed: development diverted and undermined, and rights gravely violated — women and children at highest risk.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Peace — Positive and Negative, just before this lesson in this level. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the three impacts of the first sub lesson: security, harmony, diversity.
2. Name the two of the second: development undermined, grave rights violations.
3. Explain one chain: how one impact breeds the next.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

1. **Do it first** — the learner pictures a cracked pot and predicts where the cracks run, before the five impacts are taught (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — first the society cracking — security impacts, disharmony, diversity unaccommodated; then the livelihoods — development undermined and grave human-rights violations (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Lists impacts as separate disasters → the book's chain joins them: violence erodes harmony, disharmony weaponises diversity, and all of it diverts development and breaks rights.
- Skips who is at highest risk → the rights impact names women and children at high risk of sexual and gender-based violence, abuse and displacement. It is taught as written.
