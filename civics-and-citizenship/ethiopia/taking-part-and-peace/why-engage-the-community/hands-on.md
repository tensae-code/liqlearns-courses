# Hands-on — Why engage the community — knowledge, trust, power, solutions

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
Four panels: a terraced hillside; a handshake between an official and an elder; residents raising hands; a problem being mapped on paper. Before reading, match each panel to a gain: knowledge, trust, power, or skill.

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — engagement pays four ways: it draws on local knowledge, builds trust, empowers citizens, and builds problem-solving skill — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
