# Hands-on — Conflict resolution — mediation, arbitration, litigation

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
Three doors of rising size. Behind the first, a helper sits between two chairs. Behind the second, one seated figure listens, then decides. Behind the third, a courtroom bench. Before reading: which door should two friends with one textbook try first — and why does the book insist on the order?

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — when talk alone fails: a mediator helps the parties agree, an arbitrator decides bindingly, and a court litigates — in that order of cost and formality — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
