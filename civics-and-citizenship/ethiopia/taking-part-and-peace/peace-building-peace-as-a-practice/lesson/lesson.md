# Peace building — peace as a practice

## Do it first

A broken bridge, being rebuilt plank by plank — by both villages together, meeting in the middle. Before reading: is the bridge only wood? Name everything else being rebuilt as the planks go down.

## Sub lesson 1 — Before, during and after

**Peace building** is a **continuous process** of establishing deeper, lasting peace **before, during and after** violent outbreaks — not a ceremony after a war, but a practice that never clocks off.

[Picture 28736 — many peoples in national dress celebrating in one square]

It is **everyone's work**: citizens, religious leaders, civil societies, political parties and trade unions, each fulfilling civic responsibilities. And its soil is **tolerance** — of race, language, religion, ideas and political belief. Guide depth adds its inner work: repairing relations, rebuilding trust, and addressing causes so the conflict does not return.

## Sub lesson 2 — Why it matters

Peace building **prevents escalation** — and prevents conflict itself where none exists. It creates **security and stability**, and **safeguards life and property**. It fosters **unity and democratic living**. And it **saves the cost** of restoring what conflict destroys — the cheapest bridge is the one never burned.

[Picture 28737 — a broken bridge rebuilt plank by plank by both villages]

**Nations and Nationalities Day** is the book's illustration: many peoples, in their own dress, celebrating together in one square — peace practised in public, on purpose.
