# Lesson plan — Community engagement — the social, political and economic sides

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Taking Part, Conflict and Peace · **Lesson:** Community engagement — the social, political and economic sides
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 20 — Taking Part, Conflict and Peace (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** engagement has three aspects: social (meeting social needs together), political (shaping who rules and what policy does), and economic (communities inside development projects).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the levels before it in this course's path order (recommended, never forced); inside its own title it stands on the lessons before it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
1. **The social side** — **Social engagement** is people's collective, conscious, voluntary action to meet a community's social needs and improve public services' quality and fit — above all in **old age care and education**.
2. **The political side** — **Political engagement** is voluntary activity shaping the political sphere — directly or indirectly joining in choosing rulers and forming public policy: voting, seeking information, meetings, party contributions, strikes and demonstrations, contacting legislators.
3. **The economic side** — **Development engagement** means including all kinds of community members **continuously** in identifying and carrying out projects — a core duty of funding and implementing agencies.
