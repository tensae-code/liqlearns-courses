# Lesson plan — Good Values at Home — the Principles a Family Lives By

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** My Family and Respect · **Lesson:** Good Values at Home — the Principles a Family Lives By
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — My Family and Respect (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a family lives by moral values and principles — and a child shows them to the family in daily acts, even in play.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the lessons before it in this title — the learner already knows the earlier circles of this course (myself, then family, neighbours, school, community and culture) as far as this title. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: My Family — Who Is in It and Why Each Person Matters (the previous lesson in this title). Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Every family has its own moral values and principles — the good rules of life it holds and lives by.
2. They are named as their own topic of the chapter.
3. A child shows good moral values to the family members — and practises them.
4. The practising happens everywhere, including in play by yourself.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson's scenes are the learner's own home, school and neighbourhood.

## If the learner is stuck
- Believes that “a family's values only matter when guests are watching; in play alone they do not count” → go back to the lesson's own words: a family lives by moral values and principles — and a child shows them to the family in daily acts, even in play. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
- Gives the words but no example → ask for the lesson happening once in their own home, school or street today; the example is the proof.
