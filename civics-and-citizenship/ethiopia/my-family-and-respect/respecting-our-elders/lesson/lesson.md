# Respecting Our Elders and Religious Fathers

## Do it first

Sit with an elder — a grandparent, an old neighbour — and ask one question about their life. Then listen all the way through without interrupting. You have just done this lesson's first part.

## Respecting elders

Be humble and gentle when you meet your elders.

Listen carefully when they counsel you.

Do not interrupt while they speak.

Each community also has its own customary moral principles of elder-respect — pupils name the ones found in their own society.

## Respecting religious fathers

Religious fathers are respected too, with their own things to consider.

Among them the answers name reconciliation — peace-making (እርቅ): the work of bringing people who quarrelled back together.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: respecting elders — humbly, gently, listening without interrupting — and respecting religious fathers, with peace-making at the heart. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: elder, counsel, reconciliation (እርቅ). Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: how we respect elders and religious fathers.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
