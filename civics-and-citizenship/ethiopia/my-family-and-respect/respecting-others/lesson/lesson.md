# Respecting Others — Every Person Is Free and Has Dignity

## Do it first

Today, greet three different people properly — a friend, an elder, someone who works at your school. Notice: each one is a whole person, free and standing on their own. This lesson says why that matters.

## Who 'others' are

“Others” means anyone besides the speaker — friends, brothers and sisters, mother and father, neighbours, and so on.

Respecting others begins from recognising that the other person is self-standing, special and free.

[Picture 28018 — greeting each person in turn]

## What respect does

Respecting a person confirms the person's human dignity — it is giving value to people's human dignity.

Respect benefits both sides: the one who respects and the one who is respected.

Pupils show in role-play how they respect each other, and hold a positive attitude to others.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: respecting others means recognising that every person is self-standing, special and free — and giving value to their human dignity. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: others, self-standing, human dignity. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: every person is free and has dignity — so we respect them.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
