# Building Good Morals

## Do it first

Climb five imaginary stairs, one slow step at a time. On each step, you will name one way of building good morals as you read. By the top, you should be able to name all five without looking.

## The five ways

Good morals are built, and the ways are named — at least five of them: self-control; respecting the law; accepting and following the community's values and principles; respecting the school's rules; and respecting the community's principles. Two of the five — respecting the law and the school's rules — are this lesson's civics part: morals grow inside the rules a community lives by, not outside them.

## Self-control, defined and justified

Self-control is restraining yourself from needless thoughts and acts. It is justified plainly: it saves you from harm, and it makes a balanced life. In the guide's case of a disruptive pupil named Thomas — a case the guide works for advice, given in summary only — the advice is this: count what your acts do to yourself and to others before you act. That counting is self-control at work.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the five ways of building good morals, in order. Then define self-control and give its two justifications. Mention the Thomas case only as the guide gives it: a disruptive pupil, advised to count what his acts do to himself and others.

## Do It Yourself

1. Pick one of the five ways and practise it deliberately all day.
2. Before one act today, count what it would do to yourself and to others — then decide.
3. Respect one school rule today with extra care, especially when nobody is watching.
