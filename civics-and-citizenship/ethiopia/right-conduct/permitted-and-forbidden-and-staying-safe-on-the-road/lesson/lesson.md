# Permitted and Forbidden and Staying Safe on the Road

## Do it first

Practise the crossing sequence right where you are: stop, look left, look right, look left again, then walk across an imaginary road in a straight line. Do it twice, slowly. Your body is learning the rule.

## Sub lesson 1 — Permitted and forbidden

Every social life has things permitted and things forbidden: what may be done, and what may not. Learning which is which is not about fear — it is how a community keeps its shared life safe and fair. When you are unsure, ask before you act; the asking itself is good conduct.

## Sub lesson 2 — Staying safe on the road

The road has rules, and they are ethics you can be hurt by breaking. Watch the crossing in the picture and the moving image: stop, look both ways, cross on the marked crossing, and walk — never run blindly — across. Road rules protect your body the way other rules protect the community's life; breaking either kind brings harm that cannot be undone.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the difference between permitted and forbidden, then walk them through the safe crossing sequence step by step.

## Do It Yourself

1. Use the marked crossing and the full look-both-ways sequence every time you cross this week.
2. Teach a younger child the crossing sequence.
3. Name one permitted thing and one forbidden thing in your school's shared life, and say why each is so.
