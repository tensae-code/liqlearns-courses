# Ethics in What We Do Together and Using Free Time Properly

## Do it first

Look at your last free hour. Sort what you did into two piles in your mind: time used well, and time that did harm or wasted the trust. No shame — just accuracy. That sorting is the first step of this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ethics in social activities

A community's shared activities — its gatherings, games, work and celebrations — need ethics inside them, or they break. Ethics in social activities means the rules of right conduct travel into the group's life: fairness in the game, honesty in the shared work, respect for everyone taking part. Shared life runs on the conduct of the people sharing it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Using free time properly

Rest and free time are part of life, and how you use them is an ethical act. Time is a trust: free time used well — resting truly, reading, helping at home, playing fairly — builds you and harms no one. Free time used badly — idle mischief that damages things or people — spends the trust on harm. Nobody gives you your free time back; spend it like it matters, because it does.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion why ethics matters inside shared activities. Then tell them how you plan to use your next free hour well.

## Do It Yourself

1. Spend one free hour this week on reading or helping at home, on purpose.
2. In one group activity, be the person who keeps it fair.
3. At the end of a day, sort your free time honestly: well used, or trust wasted.
