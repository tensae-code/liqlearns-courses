# Lesson plan — Ethics — The Rules of Right Conduct

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Right Conduct With Others · **Lesson:** Ethics — The Rules of Right Conduct
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 8 — Right Conduct With Others (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Ethics — ሥነ-ምግባር (sine-migbar) — is the rules of right conduct: the settled rules a person carries into everything they do with others.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the lessons before it in Level 8 — Right Conduct With Others — and on the learner's own daily life at home, at school and in the community. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **“a picture of …” in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Level 8 — Right Conduct With Others. Recommended, never forced.
- The learner's own experience of the lesson's subject in daily life is the real doorway; the Do it first opens there.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say and show what “What ethics means” teaches, in their own words, and do the practice named for it.
2. Say and show what “Rules you carry” teaches, in their own words, and do the practice named for it.
3. Carry the lesson's practice into the week: pausing at doorways to choose your conduct before you enter.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — every activity uses the learner's own home, school and street.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Pause at the next doorway you pass today — a real one. In the pause, ask: when I go through, how will I treat the people on the other side? That small question, asked all day long, is where this lesson lives.
2. **The explanation** — the lesson's parts, in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 28216]: a picture of a child pausing at a doorway, a thought bubble showing right and wrong paths — the student studies the picture and names what it shows about the lesson before reading on.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the lesson's key sentences aloud — the student listens once, then reads them aloud in the same voice.
3. **Say it to your companion** — as written in lesson/lesson.md.
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the student watches and then does the Say it to your companion with a real companion.
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-or-false, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the numbered doing block at the end of lesson/lesson.md.

## If the learner is stuck
- Cannot say what the lesson teaches → go back to the Do it first and the picture; name what the picture shows, in one sentence, then read the first part again slowly.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → hold the one thing: Ethics — ሥነ-ምግባር (sine-migbar) — is the rules of right conduct: the settled rules a person carries into everything they do with others.
- Finds the practice hard → shrink it to the smallest real version and do that today; small and done beats grand and promised.
