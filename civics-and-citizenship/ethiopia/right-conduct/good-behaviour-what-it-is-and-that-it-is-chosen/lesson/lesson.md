# Good Behaviour — What It Is and That It Is Chosen

## Do it first

Think of two people you watch often — one whose behaviour you admire, one whose behaviour troubles you. Notice: you are learning from both, whether you choose to or not. This lesson says the choosing is yours to take hold of.

## What good behaviour is

Behaviour is the indicator of how we treat family, friends, teachers and the community — it shows, outwardly, what a person is like inwardly. Good behaviour is feeling well toward others and doing good for them as for yourself. Its marks are named: humanity, generosity, compassion, sharing others' feelings, thoughtfulness, friendliness and humility.

## Behaviour is chosen — and learned from what you watch

Behaviour is a result of choices. It is shaped by what children see at home and among friends: a child who watches cheating learns to think cheating, and a child who watches honesty learns that instead. You cannot choose everything you see — but you can choose which examples you copy, and every choice trains the next one.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what behaviour indicates, name the marks of good behaviour, and then say which example — honest or cheating — a watching child learns from, and why the choice of what to copy matters.

## Do It Yourself

1. Copy one good example you saw today — do the same good act yourself.
2. When you see cheating or cruelty, name it silently for what it is, and do not copy it.
3. Do one thoughtful, friendly act for a family member today.
