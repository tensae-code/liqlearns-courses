# Diversity Management in Ethiopia — And the Balance

## Do it first

A young teacher is posted far from home, to a community of another faith. His relatives warn him: 'You will not be welcomed there.' If he goes and they are wrong, what exactly will have managed the diversity? Keep his bag packed; the lesson tells his story.

## Sub lesson 1 — Why diversity management matters in Ethiopia

Ethiopia holds **over 80 ethnic groups**, each with tribes and sub-tribes, its own cultural practices and its own language. In such a country, diversity is not a topic; it is the terrain. The adopted solution: building **unity in diversity through a multicultural state and a federal system** — and managing it means **accommodating diversity while maintaining unity and avoiding fragmentation**, weighing historical, political and socio-cultural factors as you go. 

[Picture 28867 — A teacher arriving with a bag to a village welcome]



Now the case, as a summary. **Abdella**, a new Muslim graduate teacher, was posted to a Christian-majority community. His relatives warned he would not be welcomed because of his religion, and urged him not to go. He went. He was **warmly welcomed** — and found a community that celebrates both Christian and Muslim holidays together, and is proud of its diversity.

Read what the case proves and what it does not. It does not prove every posting ends so. It proves the warnings are not knowledge: a managed, lived diversity can be standing there already, ahead of the fear.

## Sub lesson 2 — The balance — unity built by respecting difference

Here is the balance itself, the second half of the teaching. **Recognising and respecting differences** — of language, religion, identity, cultural values and historical background — is **how** national integration and a common national identity are created. Not the obstacle to integration: the means of it. 

[Picture 28868 — A balance — 'our differences, respected' against 'one nation', level]



Appreciation of diversity is therefore not only a remedy for diversity's challenges; it is the engine of the other pan. **National unity** is an awareness of common identity and national feeling — and when unity and harmony are in people's hearts, common citizenship and loyalty to the nation follow. 

[Picture 28869 — A gardener tending many different crops in one field (teacher guide — evidence)]



So the scales stand level only when both pans are filled: our differences, respected — and one nation. Empty the difference pan in the name of unity, and watch the scale tip into the very fragmentation it feared; empty the unity pan, and difference curdles into separation. For Ethiopia — a complex pattern of ethnic, linguistic and religious groups — making a united Ethiopia and promoting its diverse differences are declared **equally important**. Neither pan is the sacrifice. Both are the policy.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion Abdella's story in four sentences — then the balance, in one.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write your own 'warning and welcome': a time you expected rejection across a difference and found welcome instead. Four sentences, honestly.
2. List the diversities of your own region under the lesson's five heads: language, religion, identity, cultural values, historical background.
3. Draw the scale and label both pans. Under it, write the sentence that keeps it level.
