# Unity in Diversity

## Do it first

A choir: twenty voices, all different, singing one song. If every voice sang the same note, would it be more united — or just poorer? Hold the choir in mind; this whole lesson is inside it.

## Oneness without sameness

**Unity in diversity** is the feeling of being one, over common destinies, among a group's members — where diversity is the existence of varied characteristics in that group: oneness among peoples of many cultures, religions, ethnicities and languages who **acknowledge and respect the rich diversity among them** and agree to live together as **one undivided nation**. 

[Picture 28861 — Differently dressed nations in one line, holding one rope]



The formulas fix it, and they are the lesson's core, to be learned by heart: **not uniformity, but harmony between dissimilar people** — and **commonality without uniformity, and diversity without fragmentation**. 

[Picture 28862 — Many rivers of different colours joining one dam lake (teacher guide — evidence)]



Run the choir test on the formulas. Uniformity would make every voice one note: common, and worthless. Fragmentation would make every voice a solo: diverse, and no choir at all. Unity in diversity is the third thing — many notes, one song, each voice necessary.

The working exercise is a table: list what needs **unity** against what is **difference** in today's Ethiopia. The evidence layer keys its own examples — support for the Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam stands on the Unity side; ethnic identity and differing historical backgrounds stand on the Diversity side. Neither column is the enemy of the other; the table is the lesson, done by hand.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the definition — then both formulas, by heart, without the book.

## Do It Yourself

1. Build the table for your own school or town: five entries under Unity, five under Diversity, all real.
2. Write the two formulas on a card and keep it where you will see it daily for a week.
3. Find one 'diversity' fact that someone treats as a unity problem. Write which column it truly belongs in, and why.
