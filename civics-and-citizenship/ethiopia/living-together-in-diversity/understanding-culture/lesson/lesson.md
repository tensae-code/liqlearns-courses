# Understanding Culture

## Do it first

Describe 'a proper meal', 'proper dress' and 'a proper greeting' — as your home does them. Now notice: you did not describe food, cloth and hands. You described judgements — proper and improper. Where did those judgements come from? Open the lesson.

## Mirror and lens

Culture is the shared **language, beliefs, values, norms, behaviours and material objects** passed from generation to generation — rituals, customs, celebrations, dress, and ways of relating. In its fuller form: a multifaceted whole of knowledge, belief, art, morals, law, custom and habit, acquired as a member of society. 

[Picture 28852 — A coffee ceremony drawn three regional ways]



Culture works in two directions at once. It is a **mirror**: members identify themselves by it — this is us, this is how we are. And it is a **lens**: members judge everything through it — proper or improper, normal or abnormal. 

[Picture 28853 — A mirror woven from language, dress, food and festival threads (teacher guide — evidence)]



The lens is exactly where other cultures get **misjudged**. What is strange through your lens gets stamped improper — not because it failed any fair test, but because your lens was never ground for it. That is why learning culture teaches recognition of difference **without stereotype**.

Ethiopia's variety is the standing example — with the coffee ceremony practised as the pride of it — presented as beauty to be maintained by the new generation. And diversity itself is given its reception in four verbs that the next lessons unpack one by one: to be **accepted, respected, tolerated and appreciated**.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the definition, then explain mirror and lens with one example of each from your own life.
