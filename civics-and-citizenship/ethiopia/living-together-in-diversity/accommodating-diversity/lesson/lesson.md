# Accommodating Diversity

## Do it first

A new family arrives whose language, food and feast days differ from the street's. Three streets answer: 'Become exactly like us, or leave.' 'Keep your ways at home; outside, be like us.' 'Your ways have room here — in the open, with ours.' Name which street you would move to. The lesson names all three.

## Making room

**Accommodation** is presented as the contemporary strategy for cultural diversity, set against national integration. Accommodation **recognises more than one** ethnic, linguistic, national or religious community in the state, and **secures their coexistence**. 

[Picture 28865 — Two roads — figures flattened uniform, and figures distinct under one flag]



**Integration**, by contrast, aims at equal citizenship with differences **privatised** — kept behind doors. And the rejected pole is named plainly: **assimilation**, the strategy that ignores, eliminates and excludes diversity. Three streets, three answers: make room, blend in, disappear. 

[Picture 28866 — A house with many rooms beside a single melting pot (teacher guide — evidence)]



The working process that runs accommodation is **diversity management**: creating and maintaining an environment where individuals' similarities and differences are **valued** — aimed at cultural accommodation and national integration together, the two held in one policy, not played against each other.

And accommodation is *achieved* — not declared — when a society's components mutually accept differences and respect their consequences; it is also defined as the steps ensuring everyone can participate fully, regardless of physical ability, religion or gender. A society accommodates when difference walks in public without asking permission.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the three strategies in ascending order of room made — and the test that tells whether accommodation is real.

## Do It Yourself

1. Take one institution you know — a school, an office, a team — and judge it: assimilation, integration or accommodation? Evidence, not vibes.
2. Write the diversity-management definition from memory; check; restore 'valued' if you lost it — it is the load-bearing word.
3. Name one step that would let everyone near you participate fully regardless of ability, religion or gender. Cost it honestly.
