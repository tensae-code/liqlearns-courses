# Forms of Diversity

## Do it first

Sort these into kinds of difference: the language a pupil dreams in; the prayer her family keeps; the work a culture assigns to daughters; the wheelchair at the classroom door. Four piles or two? This lesson sorts them its own way — check yours against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ethnicity and religion

**Ethnicity**: groups identify themselves on cultural grounds — language, origins, common descent or intermarriage, shared culture or history. Identifying yourself ethnically is natural in a multicultural society, and no threat to it. The danger has its own name: ethnicity hardened into **ethnocentrism** becomes a potential threat to multiculturalism. 

[Picture 28858 — A church and a mosque on one street, neighbours greeting between]



**Religion**: organised belief and worship, central to human lives. In religiously diverse societies, tolerance lets faiths live in harmony — and the standing example is home-grown: Ethiopia's Christians and Muslims have lived together for a long period in mutual respect and tolerance, neighbours before they are categories.

## Sub lesson 2 — Gender and disability

**Gender** is the array of socially constructed roles, relationships, traits, attitudes, behaviours, values and relative power that a culture ascribes differently to the two sexes. Mark the definition's spine: gender is a **social construct** — it defines appropriate roles, rights and responsibilities for women and men; it is not biology itself. 

[Picture 28859 — A pupil using a wheelchair leading a group presentation]



**Disability** is a physical or mental impairment limiting one or more major life activities — and it is itself a fundamental aspect of human diversity, not an exception to it. 

[Picture 28860 — A market crowd with four callouts (teacher guide — evidence)]



So this sub lesson lifts the aim past the third pillar: beyond simple tolerance, to **embracing and celebrating** the dimensions of diversity inside each individual — and it counts self-awareness and knowledge of the world among the gains of doing so.

The evidence layer sorts diversity differently — ethnic, cultural, linguistic and religious. Both sorts are kept, each under its own book's name; they are maps of the same country drawn by different hands, and they are not blended into one.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the four forms in the lesson's pairing — and the one-line spine of each definition.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the four definitions from memory, then check the spines: cultural grounds; organised belief; social construct; dimension of diversity.
2. Interview one person whose daily life runs through one of these forms. Write what a classroom, a market or a bus could do better for them.
3. Find one place where embracing, not merely tolerating, already happens near you. Describe it.
