# Lesson plan — Forms of Diversity

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Living Together in Diversity · **Lesson:** Forms of Diversity
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 24 — Living Together in Diversity (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** diversity takes named forms: ethnicity and religion, and gender and disability — each a real dimension of human difference, and each asking more than tolerance.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the citizenship levels before it — the learner knows one constitution over one country of many peoples; this band teaches how those peoples live together inside it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Ethnicity and religion** — ethnicity defined and its hardening named; religion and Ethiopia's long coexistence
2. **Gender and disability** — gender as social construct; disability as a dimension of diversity; the aim raised to embracing

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Pillars of a Multicultural Society. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define ethnicity, and name its hardening danger.
2. Say how Ethiopia's Christians and Muslims stand as the religion example.
3. Define gender as a social construct, and disability as a dimension of diversity.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks ethnic identity itself threatens multiculturalism → identity is natural and no threat; *ethnocentrism* is the hardening that threatens.
- Thinks gender is biology → the definition's spine is social construction: ascribed roles, rights and relative power.
