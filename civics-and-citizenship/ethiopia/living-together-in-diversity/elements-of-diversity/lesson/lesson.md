# Elements of Diversity

## Do it first

Count, roughly, on your fingers: how many languages could you hear in one busy Ethiopian market day? How many faiths' houses stand in your nearest town? The lesson you are entering inventories exactly that wealth — in three elements.

## Three elements

Diversified societies are characterised by multiculturalism, political pluralism and religious diversity — and Ethiopia's own elements are worked in three parts. 

[Picture 28863 — Three overlapping circles — Culture, Religion, Language — over a map]



**Cultural diversity** — different cultures, religions, languages and ethnic backgrounds brought together, with **no culture superior or inferior to any other**: all seen equally. That equality clause is the element's spine; a diversity that ranks its cultures has already stopped being this lesson's subject.

**Religious diversity** — significant differences in belief and practice, to be respected as culture itself is respected. Ethiopia is named a land of peace and religious tolerance, where churches and mosques often stand close and relations are peaceful — alongside the indigenous belief systems that were here before either. 

[Picture 28864 — A patchwork map of Ethiopia (teacher guide — evidence)]



**Linguistic diversity** — many languages in one country, giving **literary wealth** and carrying different cultural entities, since language is among culture's most important aspects. A language is not a label on a culture; it is one of the rooms the culture lives in.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the three elements — and the equality clause that keeps the first one honest.

## Do It Yourself

1. Inventory your own area by the three elements: cultures present, faiths present, languages heard in one week. Real counts, honestly come by.
2. Learn to greet in two languages of Ethiopia not your own, from speakers of them, and use the greetings this week.
3. Write what would be lost if one language of Ethiopia vanished: name three things only it carries.
