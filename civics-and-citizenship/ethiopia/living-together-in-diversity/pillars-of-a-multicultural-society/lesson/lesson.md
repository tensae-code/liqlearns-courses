# Pillars of a Multicultural Society

## Do it first

Four neighbours, four attitudes to the new family's strange festival next door: 'Fine, it exists.' 'Their ways deserve regard.' 'I dislike it, but I will not interfere.' 'Tell me about it — I want to understand.' Rank those four from weakest to strongest. The lesson's pillars are waiting for your ranking.

## Four pillars

Diversity in a multicultural society is handled by four pillars, and each is defined, not merely named. 

[Picture 28856 — Four pillars under one pediment — Accept, Respect, Tolerate, Appreciate]



**Accepting differences** — understanding how we are alike and different, and treating everyone with respect regardless. This is the right way to accommodate diversity, and it produces peaceful coexistence. **Respecting differences** — supporting each other across gender, culture, religion, ethnicity and socio-economic background, and respecting *what those differences bring* — the gifts arrive with the difference, not despite it.

**Tolerating differences** — the willingness to accept the existence of differences one **dislikes or disagrees with**. Mark that: tolerance is not for the differences you enjoy; it is the pillar that stands precisely where your liking ends. It is called the bedrock of multiculturalism — openness of mind. 

[Picture 28857 — Five pillars holding one roof labelled Ethiopia (teacher guide — evidence)]



**Appreciating differences** — actively seeking to understand and learn from another culture, and to connect across cultures. And keep the caution that guards this pillar: appreciation is **not appropriation** — not taking a piece of another's culture for one's own interest. To honour a thing and to help yourself to it are opposite acts wearing similar clothes.

The evidence layer climbs the same wall by a five-step stair — acknowledge and value, accept, respect, tolerate and appreciate, accommodate — and names Ethiopia's arrangement multicultural federalism. The lesson stands on the student book's four pillars; the stair is the guide's way up the same building, kept as evidence and not merged into a fabricated five.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the four pillars in order — and the sentence that separates appreciation from appropriation.

## Do It Yourself

1. Take the four neighbours from Do it first and label each with their pillar. Did your ranking match the lesson's order?
2. Write one difference you merely tolerate — honestly — and what tolerating it costs you and buys the town.
3. Find one piece of another culture you appreciate. Write how you honour it without taking it.
