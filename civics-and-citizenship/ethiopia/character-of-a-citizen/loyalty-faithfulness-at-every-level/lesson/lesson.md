# Loyalty — Faithfulness at Every Level

## Do it first

Think of the people who count on you — and the people you count on — and the moment one
of those threads was tested. Loyalty is what the thread is made of. This lesson follows
the thread from two friends all the way to a soldier at a post.

## Faithfulness at every level

**Loyalty** is truthfulness and faithfulness to a person, a society, an institution or
the country. The book gives its seat: loyalty is primarily a **matter of conscience** —
it holds when no contract is being checked and no watcher is near, because the watcher
is inside.

[Picture 28535 — three professionals at their posts]

It is needed between **friends**, who must be faithful when faithfulness costs; between
**spouses**; and between **lender and debtor**, where trust is quite literally the
currency. At work it widens: **leaders** must serve everyone equally and use public
property fairly — loyalty to the public, not to favourites. And **professionals** —
teachers, doctors, accountants, soldiers, engineers are named — must serve their
institution and the public loyally, each at their own post: the teacher at the desk, the
doctor at the clinic, the soldier at the post, under one shared caption: faithful
service.

The lesson leaves the classroom. Its field activity sends you to a nearby government
office to ask employees how loyal they are, and what loyalty gains. Ask respectfully,
listen fully, and bring the answers back: loyalty sounds different described by the
people who practise it daily than it does in a definition.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what loyalty is, why it is a matter of conscience, and three places
it is needed — one personal, one between lender and debtor, one professional.

## Do It Yourself

1. Plan the field activity: write three respectful questions for a government-office
employee about loyalty and its gains.
2. Write one paragraph on a leader's loyalty: serving everyone equally and using public
property fairly — what it looks like, what its opposite looks like.
3. Choose your own post — the place where people count on you — and write the faithful
act it asks of you this week.
