# Video script — Civic virtues — tax, information, participation

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md,
add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form
until then.

---

**Civic virtues — tax, information, participation.**

The moral virtues turned public — a citizen's reasoned commitment to the state, shown in
three ways: paying fair taxes, staying informed, and participating in public government.

A civic virtue is the reasoned commitment every citizen owes the state.

It is real only when the moral virtues are internalised — honesty, fairness and the rest
living inside the person first.

Its first public form is paying fair taxes.

A fair tax is proportional to income: the share fits what a person earns, and it funds
every government function — the roads, schools, clinics and courts everyone uses.

A progressive tax carries the principle further: it takes a greater share from higher
incomes, according to ability to pay — those who have more carry more.

Against the fair tax stand its enemies: tax evasion and contraband smuggling, which
steal from every honest citizen at once.

The second form is staying informed.

A citizen who does not know what is happening cannot do their duties or judge their
government.

The lesson's channels are the everyday ones: newspaper, radio and television.

Information is a virtue's raw material.

Now you. Tell your companion the three public forms of civic virtue, and explain a
progressive tax correctly: a greater share from higher incomes, according to ability to
pay.
