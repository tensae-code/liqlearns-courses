# Lesson plan — Civic virtues — tax, information, participation

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Character of a Citizen ·
**Lesson:** Civic virtues — tax, information, participation
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 14 — Character of a Citizen (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the moral virtues turned public — a citizen's
reasoned commitment to the state, shown in three ways: paying fair taxes, staying
informed, and participating in public government.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands inside Level 14 — Character of a Citizen; the
learner needs the world of home, school and community, which they already have, and
nothing else is forced before it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked
below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every
slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the
student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while
the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot
and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule.
GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Paying fair taxes** — part 1 of this lesson, in the order the books teach it.
2. **Staying informed** — part 2 of this lesson, in the order the books teach it.
3. **Participating in public government** — part 3 of this lesson, in the order the
books teach it.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in
questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the seven virtue lessons before this one — civic virtue is real
only when the moral virtues are internalised.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define a civic virtue: the reasoned commitment every citizen owes the state, real
when the moral virtues live inside.
2. Explain fair tax correctly: a tax proportional to income, and a progressive tax
taking a greater share from higher incomes, according to ability to pay.
3. Name how a citizen stays informed: newspaper, radio and television.
4. Name ways of participating: meetings, debate, associations, voting, writing on
issues, service.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- If tax is heard as theft by the state, walk the funding line: taxes proportional to
income fund every government function everyone uses. If the progressive definition is
reversed by a learner, correct it on the spot with the scale picture — greater share
from higher incomes.
