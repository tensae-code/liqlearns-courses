**Making an ethical decision — five steps.**

A five-step method for deciding ethically — and the virtue-ethics question to carry
through every step.

Ethical decision-making runs in five inseparable steps.

They are one method, practised whole — learn them as a staircase you climb, not as five
separate lessons.

Step one: identify the central ethical problem, through investigation.

Look before you judge.

What exactly is wrong, or at risk, here?

Step two: identify the stakeholders and their interests.

Who is affected — you, your family, your school, a stranger, the whole community?

What does each of them stand to gain or lose?

Step three: develop possible solutions.

More than one.

Now you. Walk your companion up the five steps with your own example. Name each step as
you climb, and at the top, ask the virtue question out loud: do I want to be a generous,
honest person?
