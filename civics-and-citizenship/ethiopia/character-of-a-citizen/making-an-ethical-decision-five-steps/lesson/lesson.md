# Making an ethical decision — five steps

## Do it first

Bring one real small problem to mind — a found item, a shared cost, a hard truth to
tell. Do not solve it yet. Just hold it. This lesson gives you five steps; you will walk
your problem up them before the lesson ends.

## The five steps, practised whole

Ethical decision-making runs in five inseparable steps. They are one method, practised
whole — learn them as a staircase you climb, not as five separate lessons.

[Picture 28506 — five steps to an ethical decision]

**Step one: identify the central ethical problem**, through investigation. Look before
you judge. What exactly is wrong, or at risk, here?

**Step two: identify the stakeholders and their interests.** Who is affected — you, your
family, your school, a stranger, the whole community? What does each of them stand to
gain or lose?

**Step three: develop possible solutions.** More than one. A decision with only one
option in view is usually a decision not yet thought through.

**Step four: evaluate each solution's strengths and weaknesses.** Test them honestly —
what each would really do, for each stakeholder.

**Step five: select and justify the best.** Choose, and be able to say why. A decision
you cannot justify aloud is not finished.

Through every step, carry the virtue-ethics colouring that makes this method this
course's own. Faced with giving to charity or telling the truth, the question is not
only 'what is my duty?' It is: **'do I want to be a generous, honest person?'** The
steps find the act; the question finds the person you are becoming by doing it. Now take
the problem you held at the start, and walk it up all five steps — then apply the same
staircase to a real ethical problem in your locality.

## Say it to your companion

Walk your companion up the five steps with your own example. Name each step as you
climb, and at the top, ask the virtue question out loud: do I want to be a generous,
honest person?

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the five steps on a card, in order, and keep it where you do your homework.
2. Take one ethical problem from your locality — littering at a shared water point,
cheating in a market measure, a rumour spreading — and work it through all five steps in
writing.
3. At step five, write your justification in two sentences a stranger could follow.
