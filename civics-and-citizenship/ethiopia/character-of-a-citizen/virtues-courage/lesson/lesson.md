# Virtues — courage

## Do it first

Think of something you tried, failed at, and quietly stopped trying. Hold it in mind —
honestly, without shame. This lesson is about the next attempt: what it takes to climb
again after slipping.

## Try again until it holds

**Courage** faces what is difficult or painful instead of avoiding it. Its mark is
persistence without a shortcut — never losing heart after failure. Courage is not the
absence of fear or defeat; it is the next attempt after them.

[Picture 28508 — a spider climbing a cave wall, a king watching]

The general point is carried by an inventor: Edison made many failed attempts before the
light bulb of 1879 worked. The failures were not the opposite of his success; they were
its road.

[Picture 28509 — the spider's seventh attempt]

The story at the lesson's heart is told in summary. Scotland's King Robert Bruce,
repeatedly defeated by the English, hid in a cave. There he watched a spider try to
climb the wall on its own thread — fail, climb, fail, climb again — until it succeeded.
The king took heart, fought again, and succeeded on his seventh attempt. The spider did
not give the king an army. It gave him the one thing defeat had taken: the will to try
once more.

[GIF 28510 — a spider climbing, slipping, climbing again]

Courage also turns outward, toward other people. The lesson's role plays practise it in
ordinary places: defending a friend who is being mocked, and challenging someone who
litters. No cave, no crown — the same virtue, at school size.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the spider story in your own words, and finish with the number: the
king succeeded on his seventh attempt. Then name one outward act of courage from the
role plays.

## Do It Yourself

1. Choose one thing you stopped trying and make the next attempt this week — one
attempt, honestly made.
2. Write Edison's lesson in one line: failures are the road, not the opposite, of
success.
3. Defend someone being mocked, or challenge a litterer, once this week — kindly and
bravely.
