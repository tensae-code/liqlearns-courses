# Video script — Virtues — courage

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md,
add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form
until then.

---

**Virtues — courage.**

Courage — facing what is difficult or painful instead of avoiding it, and trying again
until it holds.

Courage faces what is difficult or painful instead of avoiding it.

Its mark is persistence without a shortcut — never losing heart after failure.

Courage is not the absence of fear or defeat; it is the next attempt after them.

The general point is carried by an inventor: Edison made many failed attempts before the
light bulb of 1879 worked.

The failures were not the opposite of his success; they were its road.

The story at the lesson's heart is told in summary.

Scotland's King Robert Bruce, repeatedly defeated by the English, hid in a cave.

There he watched a spider try to climb the wall on its own thread — fail, climb, fail,
climb again — until it succeeded.

The king took heart, fought again, and succeeded on his seventh attempt.

The spider did not give the king an army.

Now you. Tell your companion the spider story in your own words, and finish with the
number: the king succeeded on his seventh attempt. Then name one outward act of courage
from the role plays.
