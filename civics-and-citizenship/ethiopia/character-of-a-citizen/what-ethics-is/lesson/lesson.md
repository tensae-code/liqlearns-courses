# What Ethics Is

## Do it first

An exam room. Your best friend leans over: copy the top student's answers and text them
to me. Your phone is in your hand. Everything in this lesson is inside your next ten
seconds. Hold the moment — the lesson begins there.

## Right and wrong, studied and practised

**Ethics** — from the Greek *ethos*, custom — is the discipline concerned with right and
wrong, good and bad, acceptable and unacceptable conduct. **Morality** names the
standards themselves; **ethics** is the reflective study and practice of them. One is
the map; the other is learning to read it, and then walking by it.

[Picture 28530 — three pupils, a phone refused]

Ethics obliges. It obliges a person to refrain from acts such as stealing, fraud,
assault and slander. And it binds a person in virtues such as honesty, compassion and
loyalty, and in rights such as life, privacy and freedom from injury. Teachers add the
distinctions that keep the idea clean: ethics is not the same as law, which can be
obeyed from fear; not the same as custom, which can simply be old; and not the same as
habit, which can be thoughtless. Ethics is conduct *reflected on* — and then chosen.

The opening case puts it in an exam room, in summary. Three classmates: X, the top
student; Y; and Z. Z asks his best friend Y to copy X's answers in the final exam and
text them to him. Y refuses — angrily. He calls cheating serious academic dishonesty,
warns that the friendship itself will end, and Z repents, apologises, and promises to
work hard instead. Judge the acts yourself: whose do you appreciate, and why? Notice
what Y protected — not a rule in a book, but the meaning of his own results, his
friend's future, and the trust between them.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the difference between morality and ethics, and the whole X, Y and Z
case in four sentences. Finish by saying whose act you appreciate, and why.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the definition of ethics and the ethos root where you can find it again.
2. List two acts ethics obliges you to refrain from, and two virtues it binds you in.
3. Write your own judgement of Y and of Z, in three sentences each.
