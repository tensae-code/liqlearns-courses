# Lesson plan — Why good citizenship and virtue ethics matter

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Character of a Citizen ·
**Lesson:** Why good citizenship and virtue ethics matter
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 14 — Character of a Citizen (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** why it all matters — good citizenship holds a
society together, and virtue ethics makes its people whole, its laws workable and its
officials trustworthy.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands inside Level 14 — Character of a Citizen; the
learner needs the world of home, school and community, which they already have, and
nothing else is forced before it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked
below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every
slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the
student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while
the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot
and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule.
GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Why good citizenship matters** — part 1 of this lesson, in the order the books
teach it.
2. **Why virtue ethics matters** — part 2 of this lesson, in the order the books teach
it.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in
questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: all the lessons of this title before this one — this lesson gathers
their reasons.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name what good citizenship brings: a moral code, a safe society, constructive debate,
respect, common ground, wider views, attention to injustice, resistance to extremism.
2. Name challenging injustice — reporting a theft or a drug dealer — as good
citizenship's everyday form.
3. Recite the five importances of virtue ethics.
4. Give Roosevelt's warning as the book gives it: educating mind without morals educates
a menace.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- If the lists feel abstract, return to scale: one reported theft, one swept road, one
orderly queue. The lesson's whole argument is that large goods are small acts multiplied
— and that character is what multiplies them.
