# Lesson plan — Commitment to Excellence — Responsibility Through Hardship

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Character of a Citizen ·
**Lesson:** Commitment to Excellence — Responsibility Through Hardship
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 14 — Character of a Citizen (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** commitment to excellence — dedication that keeps
building inner strength through hardship — and Omod, who shone shoes and ranked first.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands inside Level 14 — Character of a Citizen; the
learner needs the world of home, school and community, which they already have, and
nothing else is forced before it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked
below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every
slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the
student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while
the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot
and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule.
GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson;
his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Abiding by the Law — Rules, Then Laws, the value before this one.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define commitment (dedication) and excellence (continuously building inner strength
to face challenges).
2. Say what they mean together: full responsibility for your behaviour, decisions and
actions in every situation.
3. Tell Omod's story in summary, from shoe-shining to Ethiopia's mega-projects.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- If excellence is heard as a prize for the gifted, use the definition: continuously
building inner strength — building is work anyone can do, and Omod's work is
deliberately ordinary: shoes, labour, study, kept up.
