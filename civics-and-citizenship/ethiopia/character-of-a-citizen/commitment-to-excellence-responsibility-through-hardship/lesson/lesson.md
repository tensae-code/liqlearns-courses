# Commitment to Excellence — Responsibility Through Hardship

## Do it first

Picture a boy polishing shoes on a street, schoolbooks stacked beside him where he can
see them while he works. Most people in the picture are looking at the shoes. This
lesson is about what he was looking at.

## Responsibility through hardship

**Commitment** is dedication. **Excellence** is continuously building inner strength to
face challenges. Together — **commitment to excellence** — they mean taking full
responsibility for your behaviour, your decisions and your actions, in **every**
situation. The book is honest about the path: it is frankly **challenging and painful**.
No shortcut is promised, and none is needed.

[Picture 28539 — shoe-shining, with books beside]

The case, in summary, is Omod. From a very poor family, he shone shoes and worked as a
daily labourer outside school — and never gave up. He ranked **first throughout**,
scored **straight As in the Grade 12 national examination**, won a **Japanese government
engineering scholarship** against East African competition, graduated with **distinction
and a gold medal**, took his **MSc and PhD in Germany**, and now works on **Ethiopia's
mega-projects**.

Read the story the lesson's way: not as luck, and not as talent falling from the sky.
Every line of it is responsibility taken in a hard situation — work and study carried
together, year after year. The hardship was not the obstacle to the excellence; carried
responsibly, it was its training ground.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion both definitions, and Omod's whole road in six beats: poverty, shoe-
shining, first rank, straight As, scholarship, gold medal — and where he works now.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write your own two definitions and pin them over your study place.
2. Take full responsibility in one situation this week where blaming would be easier —
and write what you did instead.
3. Set one excellence practice: a daily building of inner strength — study kept, promise
kept, hardship carried without complaint — for seven days.
