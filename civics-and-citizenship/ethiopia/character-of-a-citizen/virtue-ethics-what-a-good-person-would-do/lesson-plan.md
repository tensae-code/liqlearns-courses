# Lesson plan — Virtue ethics — what a good person would do

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Character of a Citizen ·
**Lesson:** Virtue ethics — what a good person would do
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 14 — Character of a Citizen (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** virtue ethics — judging an action by asking what
a virtuous person would do, because character comes first.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands inside Level 14 — Character of a Citizen; the
learner needs the world of home, school and community, which they already have, and
nothing else is forced before it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked
below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every
slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the
student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while
the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot
and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule.
GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Character judges the act** — part 1 of this lesson, in the order the books teach
it.
2. **Virtues practised into habit** — part 2 of this lesson, in the order the books
teach it.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in
questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the two good-citizenship lessons before this one — they name the
citizen; this lesson names the character inside the citizen.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a virtue and a vice are, with an example pair (honesty/dishonesty,
courage/cowardice).
2. Explain that virtue is acquired by practice and by imitating exceptionally virtuous
people — it is not inborn.
3. State the virtue-ethics question: what would a virtuous person do here?
4. Place virtue ethics among ethics' branches and against judging by rules alone or
results alone.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- If virtue sounds like a talent some people are born with, return to the acquisition
point: practice and imitation, until habit. If the three ways of judging blur, use one
case — a found wallet — and judge it three ways: by character, by rule, by result.
