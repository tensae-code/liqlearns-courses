**Virtue ethics — what a good person would do.**

Virtue ethics — judging an action by asking what a virtuous person would do, because
character comes first.

Virtues are dispositions — habits of character — that serve a community's healthy
functioning and its common good.

Vices are their opposites.

Honesty stands against dishonesty; courage stands against cowardice.

A virtue is not one good action.

It is the settled habit of acting that way.

Virtue is not inborn.

It is acquired — by practice, doing the honest or brave thing again and again until it
becomes habit, and by imitating exceptionally virtuous people, watching how they act and
following their pattern.

Ethics is the study of good and bad ends and of right and wrong conduct, with standards
for deciding between them.

Virtue ethics — the branch traced to Aristotle and the ancient Greeks — judges an action
by asking: what would a virtuous agent do?

Character comes first, before rules and duties, and before counting consequences.

Now you. Tell your companion the virtue-ethics question — what would a virtuous person
do? Then explain, in your own words, how a virtue is acquired: practice, and imitating
virtuous people, until it becomes habit.
