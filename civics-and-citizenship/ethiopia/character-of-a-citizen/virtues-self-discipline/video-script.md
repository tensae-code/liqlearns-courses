---

**Virtues — self-discipline.**

Self-discipline — following good rules freely, from inside, with no one making you: the
virtue that governs yourself first.

Self-discipline is following acceptable rules and standards freely, on one's own
initiative, with no external control.

The disciplined person is not the one who obeys because a watcher stands near.

It is the one who obeys when the watcher has gone home — because the rule has moved
inside.

Its portrait is a swap: denying yourself an extravagant pleasure for a harder,
charitable deed.

The pleasure was allowed; the deed was better; you chose the deed yourself.

That choosing, unprompted, is the virtue at work.

What does it keep?

It keeps a person focused on goals — study finished before play, saving continued before
spending — and master of their reactions: anger answered slowly, disappointment carried
without breaking things or people.

Teachers say it plainly: self-discipline governs your own appetites and time before
anyone else can — it is the virtue that makes study, saving and promise-keeping
possible.

The lesson's exercise is an audit: list where self-discipline is present in your life,
and what its absence costs, at home, in class and in the community.

Now you. Tell your companion what self-discipline is — freely, on your own initiative,
no external control — and name one goal it helps you keep and one reaction it helps you
master.
