# Virtues — self-discipline

## Do it first

Picture your desk at study time. On it: your open book — and beside it, a football
calling you outside and sweets calling you to stop. Nobody is watching. Which voice
wins, and who decides? This lesson is about the governor inside who decides.

## Governed from inside

**Self-discipline** is following acceptable rules and standards freely, on one's own
initiative, with no external control. The disciplined person is not the one who obeys
because a watcher stands near. It is the one who obeys when the watcher has gone home —
because the rule has moved inside.

[Picture 28518 — studying, with football and sweets set aside]

Its portrait is a swap: denying yourself an extravagant pleasure for a harder,
charitable deed. The pleasure was allowed; the deed was better; you chose the deed
yourself. That choosing, unprompted, is the virtue at work.

[Picture 28519 — clock, closed game, open book — in that order]

What does it keep? It keeps a person **focused on goals** — study finished before play,
saving continued before spending — and **master of their reactions**: anger answered
slowly, disappointment carried without breaking things or people.

Teachers say it plainly: self-discipline governs your own appetites and time **before
anyone else can** — it is the virtue that makes study, saving and promise-keeping
possible. The lesson's exercise is an audit: list where self-discipline is present in
your life, and what its absence costs, at home, in class and in the community. An alarm
clock set by your own hand, a game box closed by your own hand, a book opened by your
own hand — in that order, that is the whole lesson on one desk.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what self-discipline is — freely, on your own initiative, no
external control — and name one goal it helps you keep and one reaction it helps you
master.

## Do It Yourself

1. Set tomorrow's alarm yourself, close the game or phone before study, open the book
first — in that order, for three days.
2. Write your audit: self-discipline present, and its cost when missing — at home, in
class, in the community.
3. Deny yourself one extravagant pleasure this week and do a harder, charitable deed
instead.
