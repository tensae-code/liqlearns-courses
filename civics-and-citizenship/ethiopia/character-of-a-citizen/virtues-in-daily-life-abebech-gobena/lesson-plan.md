# Lesson plan — Virtues in daily life — Abebech Gobena

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Character of a Citizen ·
**Lesson:** Virtues in daily life — Abebech Gobena
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 14 — Character of a Citizen (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the virtues in one real life — Abebech Gobena,
who took in abandoned children until her care served thousands — and the virtues her
life displays.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands inside Level 14 — Character of a Citizen; the
learner needs the world of home, school and community, which they already have, and
nothing else is forced before it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked
below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every
slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the
student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while
the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot
and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule.
GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson;
his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the whole virtue walk of this title. This lesson is where the
virtues leave the list and enter a life.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Tell Abebech Gobena's story in summary: the two babies, 21 children within a year,
later 200, licensed in 1988, six branches serving over 12,000 children.
2. Name what she gave: her gold ornaments sold, her own dresses cut up and remade for
the children, her life lived with them around the clock.
3. Extract the virtues on display: compassion, generosity, self-discipline.
4. Carry the virtues home — to clubs and community — in role plays of your own settings.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- If the story is heard as unreachable heroism, return to its start: one baby, found on
one journey, taken home. The thousands came one door-opening at a time — the virtues are
the same size at the start as in the classroom role plays.
