# Virtues in daily life — Abebech Gobena

## Do it first

Close your eyes and picture a door. A woman opens it, again and again, year after year —
and every time, a child with nowhere to go comes in. By the end of this lesson you will
know her name, her numbers, and which virtues were standing at that door.

## One life, read whole

On a journey to Wollo, a woman found an abandoned baby and brought her home. Then she
found a second baby, lying beside his dead father, and brought him home too. Within a
year she had taken in **21 children**, raising them on her own money — until the
children numbered **200**.

[Picture 28528 — a woman gathering children at her door]

Licensed in **1988**, her Children's Care and Development organisation grew to **six
branches serving over 12,000 children**. The numbers matter, but the cost matters more,
because the cost is where the virtues live. She sold her **gold ornaments**. She **cut
up and remade her own dresses** to fit the children. She lived with them **around the
clock**. Her own words carry the whole lesson: *'Commitment is everything to me.'*

[Picture 28529 — a growing circle of children, virtues around]

Now do the lesson's work: extract the virtues on display. **Compassion** — she felt with
abandoned children and acted. **Generosity** — she gave what cost her: gold, dresses,
money, years. **Self-discipline** — around-the-clock care, kept up for decades, is
appetite and comfort governed from inside, daily. Teachers call her, in their phrase,
'Africa's Mother Teresa' — the name is theirs; the virtues are hers, and they are the
same virtues this title has walked through, one by one.

The unit closes by carrying the virtues home — to clubs and community — and so does this
lesson: role-play compassion, generosity and self-discipline in your own settings. A
virtue that stays in a list is a word. In a life, it feeds thousands.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story with its numbers — 21 within a year, later 200, licensed
1988, six branches, over 12,000 children — and name the three virtues her life displays.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the virtues extraction yourself: for compassion, generosity and self-
discipline, one act of hers each.
2. Role-play one of the virtues in your own setting this week — home, club or community
— and write what happened.
3. Write her sentence where you will see it: 'Commitment is everything to me.' Under it,
write one commitment of yours.
