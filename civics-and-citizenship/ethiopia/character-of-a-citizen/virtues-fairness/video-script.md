# Video script — Virtues — fairness

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md,
add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form
until then.

This lesson's script is a read-aloud of its story or case, in plot summary only.

---

**Virtues — fairness.**

Fairness — judging without favour: like situations alike, on proper criteria — and why
fairness is not sameness in every case.

Fairness treats people without letting personal feeling bend decisions.

It is free of bias, and it gives everyone in a group a real chance to benefit.

A reward is unfair if it turns on the giver's opinion of a person rather than on equal
ability and time — the same task, done as well, in the same time, must meet the same
reward.

But hear the lesson's own qualification, because it protects fairness from a shallow
copy: fairness is not sameness in every case.

People are treated equitably — on merit and ability.

Like situations are handled alike, and consistently; different situations may rightly be
handled differently.

What must never enter is favouritism or prejudice: the decision must rest on proper
criteria, whoever stands in front of you.

The virtue is learned by doing it.

The class runs a jury role play: a paper-slip 'crime' is drawn from a hat, a classroom
courtroom hears it, the jury sentences — and then the class audits its own fairness.

Did the verdict follow the evidence and the criteria, or did liking and disliking sneak
in?

Now you. Tell your companion the difference between fairness and sameness, and the two
things that must never bend a decision: favouritism and prejudice.
