# Virtues — fairness

## Do it first

Imagine two pupils did the same task, equally well, in the same time — and only one was
rewarded, because the teacher liked her more. Feel the wrongness of that. This lesson
names it, and builds the discipline that prevents it.

## Judging the case, not the person

**Fairness** treats people without letting personal feeling bend decisions. It is free
of bias, and it gives everyone in a group a real chance to benefit. A reward is unfair
if it turns on the giver's opinion of a person rather than on equal ability and time —
the same task, done as well, in the same time, must meet the same reward.

[Picture 28516 — scales: identical tasks, identical rewards]

But hear the lesson's own qualification, because it protects fairness from a shallow
copy: **fairness is not sameness in every case.** People are treated equitably — on
merit and ability. Like situations are handled alike, and consistently; different
situations may rightly be handled differently. What must never enter is favouritism or
prejudice: the decision must rest on proper criteria, whoever stands in front of you.

[Picture 28517 — a classroom courtroom]

The virtue is learned by doing it. The class runs a jury role play: a paper-slip 'crime'
is drawn from a hat, a classroom courtroom hears it, the jury sentences — and then the
class audits its own fairness. Did the verdict follow the evidence and the criteria, or
did liking and disliking sneak in? Teachers may invite a real judge to the class, so
learners hear the discipline from someone who practises it daily.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the difference between fairness and sameness, and the two things
that must never bend a decision: favouritism and prejudice.

## Do It Yourself

1. Take one decision you made this week — sharing, choosing, judging — and audit it:
what criteria did I actually use?
2. Write the scales sentence: the same task, done as well, in the same time, meets the
same reward.
3. In the next group game or task, volunteer to keep the fairness audit: note each
decision and its criterion.
