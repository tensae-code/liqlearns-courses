# Why Ethical Values Matter

## Do it first

Picture two streets. On one, neighbours lock everything, trust no one, and walk fast
with their eyes down. On the other, people greet, lend, queue fairly and leave a
shopkeeper's change counted honestly. The laws on both streets are identical. This
lesson is about the difference the laws did not make.

## From the citizen to the state

The argument runs from the citizen to the state. Ethics is called the **single factor
that determines a person's real value**, and a **builder of character**. A society of
such citizens becomes **peaceful and harmonious**; **mutual trust strengthens**;
**conflict reduces**. And values govern where laws do not reach — the spaces no rule
covers are exactly where character decides. The example given is the **care of ageing
parents**: no law can schedule a child's kindness, yet whole households stand or fall on
it.

[Picture 28540 — one street shown twice]

The lesson opens by naming the problem the values answer: the present erosion of moral
integrity in the country. Against it, picture the country of ethical citizens as the
lesson pictures it: **sustainable peace, social harmony, rapid socio-economic
transformation, no corruption, the rule of law, justice, equality and national unity**.
Read the list slowly. Not one item on it can be delivered by a ministry alone. Every one
of them is the sum of citizens' conduct — which is why this band taught the values one
by one before asking why they matter.

[Picture 28541 — lit streets and dark lanes]

Teachers press the same point from the other side: on a map, the lit streets are where
law reaches; the dark lanes are where it does not — and a single figure still walks
honestly through the dark. That figure is the lesson. Laws light the streets they can;
values walk everywhere.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the ageing-parents example, and then the whole pictured country in
one breath: peace, harmony, transformation, no corruption, rule of law, justice,
equality, unity.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the two-streets paragraph in your own words — same laws, different values,
different street.
2. Name one dark-lane place in your own life — where no rule reaches — and the value
that must walk there with you.
3. Write one line for each item of the pictured country saying what citizens, not
ministries, contribute to it.
