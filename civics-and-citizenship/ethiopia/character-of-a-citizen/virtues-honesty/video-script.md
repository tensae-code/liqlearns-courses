# Video script — Virtues — honesty

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md,
add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form
until then.

This lesson's script is a read-aloud of its story or case, in plot summary only.

---

**Virtues — honesty.**

Honesty — truthful in word and deed — and the fable that shows what dishonesty leaves
behind: nothing decidable.

Honesty means truthfulness, trustworthiness, loyalty, fairness and sincerity.

The honest person never tells a lie, never accepts what is not theirs, never steals and
never cheats.

Honesty is owed everywhere a person lives: at home, among friends, in the community and
at work.

It is repaid with the best compliment society gives — being reputed honest.

A person known for honesty is trusted with things, with work and with people.

Dishonesty has an address in public life too.

Abuse of public property and corruption are dishonesty in office, and the book names
them plainly: a disaster for development, and a fight every responsible citizen must
join.

Here is the fable, in summary.

Truth, Falsehood, Water and Fire divided four cattle equally between them.

Then Falsehood began his tricks: he tricked Water into quenching Fire, and tricked Truth
into hiding the cattle uphill, away from Water — then laughed at his own deceit.

Now you. Tell your companion the fable in your own words — four cattle, two tricks, a
judge who could not judge. Then say what honesty is repaid with: a reputation for
honesty.
