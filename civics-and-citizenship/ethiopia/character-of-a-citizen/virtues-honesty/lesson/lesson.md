# Virtues — honesty

## Do it first

Remember a wallet, a phone or a purse you once saw lost — or imagine finding one now,
with money inside and nobody watching. Hold the moment. This lesson is about what the
honest person does in exactly that unwatched moment.

## Sub lesson 1 — Honesty: truthful in word and deed

**Honesty** means truthfulness, trustworthiness, loyalty, fairness and sincerity. The
honest person never tells a lie, never accepts what is not theirs, never steals and
never cheats. Honesty is owed everywhere a person lives: at home, among friends, in the
community and at work. It is repaid with the best compliment society gives — being
reputed honest. A person known for honesty is trusted with things, with work and with
people.

[Picture 28507 — a lost wallet handed back]

Dishonesty has an address in public life too. Abuse of public property and corruption
are dishonesty in office, and the book names them plainly: a disaster for development,
and a fight every responsible citizen must join.

## Sub lesson 2 — Story: Truth and Falsehood

Here is the fable, in summary. Truth, Falsehood, Water and Fire divided four cattle
equally between them. Then Falsehood began his tricks: he tricked Water into quenching
Fire, and tricked Truth into hiding the cattle uphill, away from Water — then laughed at
his own deceit.

Truth fought him. Wind was called to judge who owned the cattle — and Wind could not
settle the ownership. That is the fable's sting: where falsehood has worked, nothing is
decidable any more. No judge, not even the wind, can rebuild the truth out of tricks.
Honesty keeps a world in which questions can still be answered.

Remember the lost wallet from the start of the lesson. The honest act is small and
quick: hand it back. The dishonest world is the fable's world — one where nothing can be
settled and nobody can be trusted.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the fable in your own words — four cattle, two tricks, a judge who
could not judge. Then say what honesty is repaid with: a reputation for honesty.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the five words inside honesty — truthfulness, trustworthiness, loyalty,
fairness, sincerity — and one honest act for each.
2. Practise the unwatched test: return one small thing this week that is not yours,
without being asked.
3. Write two sentences on why corruption is dishonesty, not just bad management.
