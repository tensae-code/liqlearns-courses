**Applied Ethics — Professional and Environmental.**

Ethics applied — the shared traits of honest professional work, and our moral obligation
to the environment that keeps us alive.

Applied ethics is the branch that reflects on moral dilemmas inside particular social
practices.

Its fields include social and political ethics, computer, medical, bio-, environmental
and business ethics, and the professional ethics forms.

A profession is work needing advanced expertise, self-regulation and service to the
public good, carrying a social contract of competence, integrity, altruism and
accountability.

Professional ethics is the norms guiding that service.

For teachers it is worked as smooth relations with pupils, colleagues, employers and
parents — and across professions, the shared traits are eight: honesty, integrity,
transparency, confidentiality, impartiality, punctuality, respectfulness, and obedience
to the law.

Teachers add the future tense: our obligations run also to those who come after us.

The lesson's task sends you out: interview three teachers about their own professional
ethics, and listen for the eight traits in their answers.

The environment is the surrounding natural world of living and non-living things, and
the foundation of all survival.

Environmental ethics is the moral obligation to protect and maintain its well-being —
the values and beliefs between humans and that world, including our obligations to
future generations.

The argument is direct: a safe environment means a healthy life; a damaged one damages
us.

Now you. Tell your companion the eight professional traits, then the survival sentence
exactly: care is not a matter of choice, rather a matter of survival.
