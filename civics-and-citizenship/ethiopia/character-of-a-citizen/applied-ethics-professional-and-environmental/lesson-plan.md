# Lesson plan — Applied Ethics — Professional and Environmental

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Character of a Citizen ·
**Lesson:** Applied Ethics — Professional and Environmental
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 14 — Character of a Citizen (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** ethics applied — the shared traits of honest
professional work, and our moral obligation to the environment that keeps us alive.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands inside Level 14 — Character of a Citizen; the
learner needs the world of home, school and community, which they already have, and
nothing else is forced before it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked
below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every
slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the
student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while
the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot
and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule.
GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Professional ethics — the eight shared traits** — part 1 of this lesson, in the
order the books teach it.
2. **Environmental ethics (doing lesson) — protection as survival** — part 2 of this
lesson, in the order the books teach it.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in
questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Why Ethical Values Matter, the lesson before this one. Values
argued for; now values at work.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what applied ethics is: reflection on moral dilemmas inside particular social
practices.
2. Define a profession: work needing advanced expertise, self-regulation and service to
the public good, under a social contract.
3. Recite the eight shared professional traits.
4. State the environmental claim: care is not a matter of choice, rather a matter of
survival — and do the photograph–clean–photograph task.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- If professional ethics sounds like politeness, use the social contract: competence,
integrity, altruism, accountability — owed to the public, not performed for it. If
environmental care sounds optional, the lesson's sentence answers: survival, not choice.
