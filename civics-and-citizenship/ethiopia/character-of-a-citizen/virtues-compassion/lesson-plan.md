# Lesson plan — Virtues — compassion

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Character of a Citizen ·
**Lesson:** Virtues — compassion
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 14 — Character of a Citizen (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** compassion — feeling with another person, then
acting for them; pity that does nothing is not compassion.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands inside Level 14 — Character of a Citizen; the
learner needs the world of home, school and community, which they already have, and
nothing else is forced before it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked
below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every
slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the
student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while
the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot
and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule.
GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Compassion: feeling with, then acting for** — part 1 of this lesson, in the order
the books teach it.
2. **Story: the Fox in the Well** — part 2 of this lesson, in the order the books teach
it.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in
questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Virtues — courage, the virtue before this one.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define compassion: empathic understanding joined to an unselfish desire to act.
2. Tell the two-sisters spring case and name which sister showed compassion.
3. Tell the Fox in the Well story in summary and judge the wolf's pity.
4. Name what a society with more compassion would know less of: hunger, conflict,
homelessness, unhappiness.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- If compassion and pity are used as the same word, the wolf separates them: pity talks,
compassion acts. If the elder sister's refusal is defended by the princess's rank,
return to the case — the drink went to the poor old woman who asked, and the lesson asks
pupils to name the compassionate sister themselves.
