# Good citizenship — tolerance and patriotism

## Do it first

Think of someone whose language, religion or opinions differ from yours, whom you live
beside peacefully. Write one sentence about what you each allow the other to be. Then
think of a runner raising a flag abroad. Keep both pictures in mind — this lesson says
both belong to good citizenship.

## Living with difference, loving your country

**Tolerance** is the willingness to live peacefully with opinions, religions, cultures
and languages different from your own. It does not ask you to agree with everyone. It
asks you to let difference exist beside you without turning it into a fight. A tolerant
citizen shares ideas across those lines and lets others share theirs.

[Picture 28502 — tolerance and patriotism in two panels]

**Patriotism** is genuine love of country, shown by a willingness to sacrifice for it.
Ethiopia's heroes showed it in battle, and that sacrifice is honoured. But the lesson is
wider than the battlefield: a patriot also serves through diplomacy, through education,
through medicine, through farming, through trade and through sport.

That is why athletes who raise the Ethiopian flag abroad are named as patriots: Haile
Gebrselassie and Derartu Tulu carried their country with them every time they ran, and
the whole country felt carried too.

Tolerance and patriotism belong together. Love of country that cannot tolerate the
country's own differences is not the love this lesson teaches — the country is its
people, in all their languages, faiths and opinions.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what tolerance means, and then name three ways — not counting battle
— that a person can show patriotism. Finish with the two athletes this lesson names.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write one sentence for each of the six peaceful ways of serving a country —
diplomacy, education, medicine, farming, trade, sport — saying what the server gives.
2. Find one real example of tolerance in your school or street this week and write what
happened.
3. Write the names Haile Gebrselassie and Derartu Tulu and one line on why this lesson
calls them patriots.
