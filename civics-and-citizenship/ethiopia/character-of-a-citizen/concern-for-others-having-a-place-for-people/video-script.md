# Video script — Concern for Others — Having a Place for People

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md,
add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form
until then.

---

**Concern for Others — Having a Place for People.**

Concern for others — holding space for people: listening to their needs, letting them
share in decisions, and supporting them in hardship.

Concern for others means holding space for people: listening to and weighing the needs
and feelings of others, letting affected people share in the decisions that touch them,
and supporting people in hardship.

It is not a feeling that visits; it is a place kept.

The book names the people a community must keep a place for: people with disabilities,
children, elders, women, and people living with HIV/AIDS.

Naming matters — concern that stays vague usually stays undone.

And the value is grounded where learners already live: in home practice.

Help given at weddings, at births, in mourning, in disaster and in sickness — and in the
cooperative work parties, Debo/Jigi, where neighbours rebuild together what one
household cannot: carrying materials together to raise a damaged house again is this
lesson's picture because it is this lesson's practice.

The lesson's own work is a survey: find out what support actually exists in your
community for the named groups — and where it does not exist, propose remedies.

Concern ends in a proposal, not a sigh.

Now you. Tell your companion the definition, the five named groups, and two home
practices where concern already lives. Finish with one remedy your community needs.
