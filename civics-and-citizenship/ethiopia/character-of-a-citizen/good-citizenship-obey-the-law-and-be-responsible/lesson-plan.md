# Lesson plan — Good citizenship — obey the law and be responsible

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Character of a Citizen ·
**Lesson:** Good citizenship — obey the law and be responsible
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 14 — Character of a Citizen (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** two features of a good citizen — obeying the law,
and being responsible — and that good citizenship is learned, not born.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands inside Level 14 — Character of a Citizen; the
learner needs the world of home, school and community, which they already have, and
nothing else is forced before it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked
below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every
slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the
student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while
the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot
and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule.
GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson;

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: nothing in this title — this is the title's opening lesson. The
learner's own home, school and street are all it stands on.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say where good citizenship is learned: family, community, friends and teachers.
2. Explain obeying the law as a good citizen does it — following just laws and rules,
paying taxes, protecting the environment.
3. Explain responsibility in a pupil's own life: learning, homework, respect, and
reporting wrongdoing such as tax fraud and smuggling.
4. Tell the Parents' Day model pupil case and the anti-corruption club case apart, and
name the features each one shows.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- If the learner thinks good citizens are born special, return to the lesson's first
line: it is learned, and the four teachers are named. If obeying the law sounds like
fear, use the tax and environment examples — the law here is a shared protection, not a
threat.
