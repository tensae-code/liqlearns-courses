# Respect for Others — Dignity for Being Human

## Do it first

Someone holds a door for an elder and greets them warmly. It takes four seconds. But the
elder walks through it standing taller. This lesson unpacks those four seconds — what
was given, why it was owed, and what it costs when it is withheld.

## Dignity for being human

**Respect** is positive regard, and treatment with dignity, owed to a person
**regardless of** ethnicity, religion, language, colour, sex, economic background,
disability or age. Its ground is short enough to say in one breath: dignity for the mere
fact of being human. Nothing must be earned first; nothing can remove it.

[Picture 28537 — holding a door for an elder]

Respect is learned first **in the family** — toward parents, elders, teachers, friends
and neighbours — and then carried everywhere. Its practice is a set of small, repeatable
acts: **kind words when upset; please and thank you; waiting one's turn; accepting
difference or disagreeing kindly; care for places and things; a quiet voice in public;
taking only what one needs of shared resources.** None of them is large. Together they
are the climate other people live in around you.

And disrespect has a price, charged to the one who pays it out: **exclusion** from the
community's warmth, **no respect or love returned**, and **no support in hardship** when
hardship comes — as it comes to everyone. Respect is not softness; it is the membership
fee of a supported life, paid in four-second acts.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the ground of respect — dignity for being human — and four
respectful acts. Finish with the three prices of disrespect.

## Do It Yourself

1. Practise the acts deliberately for a day: kind words when upset, please and thank
you, waiting your turn, a quiet voice in public. Mark each one you manage.
2. Write the disregard list — the things respect ignores: ethnicity, religion, language,
colour, sex, economic background, disability, age — and check your own week against it.
3. Take only what you need of one shared resource this week, and notice who else it
serves.
