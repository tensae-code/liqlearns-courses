**Respect for Others — Dignity for Being Human.**

Respect for others — positive regard and treatment with dignity, owed to every person
for the mere fact of being human.

Respect is positive regard, and treatment with dignity, owed to a person regardless of
ethnicity, religion, language, colour, sex, economic background, disability or age.

Its ground is short enough to say in one breath: dignity for the mere fact of being
human.

Nothing must be earned first; nothing can remove it.

Respect is learned first in the family — toward parents, elders, teachers, friends and
neighbours — and then carried everywhere.

Its practice is a set of small, repeatable acts: kind words when upset; please and thank
you; waiting one's turn; accepting difference or disagreeing kindly; care for places and
things; a quiet voice in public; taking only what one needs of shared resources.

None of them is large.

Together they are the climate other people live in around you.

And disrespect has a price, charged to the one who pays it out: exclusion from the
community's warmth, no respect or love returned, and no support in hardship when
hardship comes — as it comes to everyone.

Respect is not softness; it is the membership fee of a supported life, paid in four-
second acts.

Now you. Tell your companion the ground of respect — dignity for being human — and four
respectful acts. Finish with the three prices of disrespect.
