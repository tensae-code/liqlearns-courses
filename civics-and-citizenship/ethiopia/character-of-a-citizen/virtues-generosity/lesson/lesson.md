# Virtues — generosity

## Do it first

Think of the last thing you gave away — time, food, help, a possession. Did it cost you
anything? Would you have missed it? Keep your honest answer. This lesson measures
generosity by exactly that measure.

## Giving that costs something

**Generosity** is the disposition to expend time, effort and resources for others —
kind, understanding, selfless giving with good intention. Notice the breadth: not money
only. Time given, effort spent and resources shared all count.

[Picture 28514 — pupils sharing books and food]

Generosity rewards the giver too. It brings good feeling and confidence, freedom from
self-hatred, and it pulls people closer — building the sense of connectedness that makes
a community a community. The generous person is not poorer in the ways that matter.

[Picture 28515 — a farmer sharing seed grain]

Teachers add the honest edge, and it matters. Generosity is giving **beyond what duty
requires** — of goods, of time, of attention. It is not showing off: giving done to be
seen is a performance, not a virtue. And it is not giving what costs the giver nothing —
the thing you would have thrown away, handed over with ceremony. Real generosity costs
something real, and is glad of the cost.

Pupils demonstrate it by classroom role play — sharing books and food across a table, a
farmer sharing seed grain from his own store with a neighbour whose store ran short.
Seed grain is next year's life; sharing it is generosity with the cost left visible.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what generosity is, what it gives back to the giver, and the two
false kinds: showing off, and giving what costs nothing.

## Do It Yourself

1. Give something this week that costs you something real — time, effort or a resource —
without telling anyone you did it.
2. Write one sentence each: generosity is…; showing off is…; the difference is…
3. Share seed-grain style: help someone with the very thing you are short of yourself —
an hour when your time is tight, notes when your own exam is near.
