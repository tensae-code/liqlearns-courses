# Virtues — prudence

## Do it first

You stand at a fork in a path. One way crosses a river with no bridge. The other, a
little longer, crosses by a bridge. You are in a hurry. Before you choose — stop. That
pause, and what happens inside it, is this entire lesson.

## Think before you act

**Prudence** is practical wisdom: care, caution, good judgement and foresight. The
prudent person pauses to examine consequences before acting — avoiding needless risk,
and the later regret that follows unexamined action.

[Picture 28520 — pausing at a fork: two crossings]

Three habits make the pause fruitful. The prudent person **thinks before deciding**.
They **ask what the right and kind thing is** — not only what is fastest or easiest. And
they **learn from past mistakes**, so yesterday's regret becomes today's signpost.
Prudence plans for short and long goals alike: the bridge path serves today's crossing
and teaches tomorrow's route-finding too.

[Picture 28521 — testing the rope bridge plank by plank]

The lesson checks itself. A six-item self-check table has pupils grade their own
prudence — honestly, item by item — and role plays practise wise decisions before real
ones arrive. Teachers give the same picture in another dress: a traveller testing a rope
bridge plank by plank before crossing. The testing is not fear. It is how the crossing
gets made at all.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the three habits inside the prudent pause: think before deciding;
ask what is right and kind; learn from past mistakes. Then describe the traveller on the
rope bridge.

## Do It Yourself

1. Grade yourself with a six-item prudence check you write from this lesson — honestly,
no inflation.
2. Before one real decision this week, write the consequences you examined, both paths,
before choosing.
3. Write one past mistake and the signpost it has become for you.
