# Video script — Virtues — prudence

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md,
add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form
until then.

---

**Virtues — prudence.**

Prudence — practical wisdom: pausing to examine consequences before you act, so you act
once and do not regret it.

Prudence is practical wisdom: care, caution, good judgement and foresight.

The prudent person pauses to examine consequences before acting — avoiding needless
risk, and the later regret that follows unexamined action.

Three habits make the pause fruitful.

The prudent person thinks before deciding.

They ask what the right and kind thing is — not only what is fastest or easiest.

And they learn from past mistakes, so yesterday's regret becomes today's signpost.

Prudence plans for short and long goals alike: the bridge path serves today's crossing
and teaches tomorrow's route-finding too.

The lesson checks itself.

A six-item self-check table has pupils grade their own prudence — honestly, item by item
— and role plays practise wise decisions before real ones arrive.

Teachers give the same picture in another dress: a traveller testing a rope bridge plank
by plank before crossing.

Now you. Tell your companion the three habits inside the prudent pause: think before
deciding; ask what is right and kind; learn from past mistakes. Then describe the
traveller on the rope bridge.
