# Lesson plan — Abiding by the Law — Rules, Then Laws

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Character of a Citizen ·
**Lesson:** Abiding by the Law — Rules, Then Laws
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 14 — Character of a Citizen (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** abiding by the law — the willingness to obey,
told apart cleanly: rules guide conduct in a society or organisation; laws are written
standards, endorsed by authority, binding on all.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands inside Level 14 — Character of a Citizen; the
learner needs the world of home, school and community, which they already have, and
nothing else is forced before it.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define abiding by the law: the willingness, commitment and responsibility to obey
rules and regulations.
2. Tell rules from laws: rules are prescribed standards guiding conduct in a society or
organisation; laws are written standards endorsed by authority and binding on all.
3. Name what rule-breaking brings: punishment, condemnation or exclusion.
4. Work the school example: uniform, punctuality, respect for teachers, no exam cheating
— the school's 'laws', with stated consequences.

## What you need

## If the learner is stuck
- If rules and laws blur, use the binding test: a rule guides conduct inside its society
or organisation; a law is written, authority-endorsed and binds everyone. The school
example is called its 'laws' in the book's own loose usage — the distinction still
stands and the lesson teaches both.
