**Abiding by the Law — Rules, Then Laws.**

Abiding by the law — the willingness to obey, told apart cleanly: rules guide conduct in
a society or organisation; laws are written standards, endorsed by authority, binding on
all.

Abiding by the law is the willingness, commitment and responsibility to obey rules and
regulations.

Willingness comes first in the definition — this value is not obedience extracted by
fear, but obedience owned.

Keep the two words apart.

Rules are prescribed standards guiding conduct in a society or organisation; break them,
and what follows is punishment, condemnation or exclusion.

The duties not to steal, cheat or corrupt live here.

Laws are written standards, endorsed by authority and binding on all — no one is above
them, and they do not depend on which organisation you happen to stand in.

The school is the worked example.

Its 'laws', as the book calls them: uniform, punctuality, respect for teachers, and no
exam cheating — each with stated consequences.

A pupil arriving on time in uniform at the school gate is not merely tidy; they are
rehearsing citizenship in miniature.

The gate is small.

Now you. Tell your companion the difference between a rule and a law, and the school's
four 'laws'. Finish with the first word of the definition: willingness.
