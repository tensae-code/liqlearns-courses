# Peace — A Settled, Secure Community

## Do it first

Look at the picture of the market in your mind: full stalls, unhurried people, children walking to school. Ask yourself: what makes this scene possible, and whose work keeps it that way? Read on with that question open.

## Peace, three ways

Peace can be defined three ways, and all three are true together. Peace is a quiet, settled state. Peace is the presence of security and order in a society. And peace is the absence of war and conflict. A community can be quiet because it is afraid — that is not the whole of peace. Real peace joins all three: settled hearts, security and order, and no war.

## Your own role in keeping peace

Pupils are members of the community, and members share in solving its problems. The community's problems named for pupils to share in solving include early marriage and violence — harms that break peace from inside. And the cost of peace's absence stands at the end of the lesson as a warning: conflict, war, and loss of freedom.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the three definitions of peace. Then name one community problem pupils share in solving, and the three costs of peace's absence.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the three definitions of peace in your own words.
2. Name one thing in your community that keeps security and order — and one thing that threatens them.
3. Do one act this week that adds to your community's peace.
