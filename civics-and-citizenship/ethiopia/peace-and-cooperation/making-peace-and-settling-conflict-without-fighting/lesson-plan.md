# Lesson plan — Making Peace and Settling Conflict Without Fighting

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Peace and Cooperation · **Lesson:** Making Peace and Settling Conflict Without Fighting
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 7 — Peace and Cooperation (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Peace does not only happen — it is built and kept.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the lessons before it in Level 7 — Peace and Cooperation — and on the learner's own daily life at home, at school and in the community. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **“a picture of …” in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Bringing peace** — Peace does not only happen — it is built and kept.
2. **Settling conflict peacefully** — When conflict comes, there is a way through that is not fighting: talking and listening.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Level 7 — Peace and Cooperation. Recommended, never forced.
- The learner's own experience of the lesson's subject in daily life is the real doorway; the Do it first opens there.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say and show what “Bringing peace” teaches, in their own words, and do the practice named for it.
2. Say and show what “Settling conflict peacefully” teaches, in their own words, and do the practice named for it.
3. Carry the lesson's practice into the week: talking and listening until a settlement both sides can accept appears.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — every activity uses the learner's own home, school and street.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Think of a small conflict you know — over a game, a place, a thing. Do not pick sides. Now walk it through the peaceful way in your mind: what would each side say, what would each side need to hear, and what settlement could both accept?
2. **The explanation** — the lesson's parts, in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 28211]: a picture of two children in dispute with a third helping them talk, hands open — the student studies the picture and names what it shows about the lesson before reading on.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the lesson's key sentences aloud — the student listens once, then reads them aloud in the same voice.
3. **Say it to your companion** — as written in lesson/lesson.md.
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the student watches and then does the Say it to your companion with a real companion.
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-or-false, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the numbered doing block at the end of lesson/lesson.md.

## If the learner is stuck
- Cannot say what the lesson teaches → go back to the Do it first and the picture; name what the picture shows, in one sentence, then read the first part again slowly.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → hold the one thing: Peace does not only happen — it is built and kept.
- Finds the practice hard → shrink it to the smallest real version and do that today; small and done beats grand and promised.
