# Making Peace and Settling Conflict Without Fighting

## Do it first

Think of a small conflict you know — over a game, a place, a thing. Do not pick sides. Now walk it through the peaceful way in your mind: what would each side say, what would each side need to hear, and what settlement could both accept?

## Sub lesson 1 — Bringing peace

Peace does not only happen — it is built and kept. The ways are plain: speak before anger grows, treat people fairly so grievances do not pile up, apologise when you are wrong, and help others settle their troubles instead of stirring them. A peacemaker is not a bystander; peacemaking is work done on purpose.

## Sub lesson 2 — Settling conflict peacefully

When conflict comes, there is a way through that is not fighting: talking and listening. Each side says what happened and what they need; each side truly listens; then together they find the settlement both sides can accept. A settlement both sides accept lasts. A victory one side forces lasts only until the loser can fight again.

## Say it to your companion

Act it out with your companion: one of you brings a small dispute, the other helps you talk it through — each side speaks, each side listens, and together you find a settlement both can accept.

## Do It Yourself

1. Use talking and listening on one real small disagreement this week.
2. Apologise once, promptly, where you are wrong.
3. When you see a quarrel starting, help the two sides talk instead of cheering the fight.
