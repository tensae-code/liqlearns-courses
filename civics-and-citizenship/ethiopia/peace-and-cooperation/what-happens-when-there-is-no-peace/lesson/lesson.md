# What Happens When There Is No Peace

## Do it first

Picture the village from the peace lesson — the trading, the playing, the unafraid people. Now take the peace away, piece by piece: the market first, then the school, then the evenings. What is left? Keep that picture in mind as you read.

## The effects of peace's absence

When peace is absent, the damage spreads through a whole life. On people: fear takes the settled mind's place, and ordinary days are lived braced for trouble. On learning: schools empty or close, and even where they stand, frightened pupils cannot study. On a community's life: trading stops, work is abandoned, neighbours stop trusting and helping each other, and the shared life of the place thins until it breaks.

## Why this lesson stands here

This lesson is the reason the lessons before it matter. Peace is built by small acts — fairness, listening, apology — precisely because its absence costs so much. Knowing the cost is what makes a person guard the peace while it is still there to guard.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion three effects of the absence of peace: one on people, one on learning, one on a community's life.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write one sentence for each: what no peace does to people, to learning, to a community.
2. Name one small act you do that guards peace, knowing its cost.
3. Tell a younger child one reason peace is worth keeping.
