# Cooperation — Working Together

## Do it first

Try to picture lifting a long, heavy bench alone. Now picture four people lifting it together, one at each part. Feel the difference in your shoulders. This lesson is about that difference — and about much more than benches.

## What cooperation is and what it gives

Cooperation is people working together on something they share. It makes tasks easier — the bench is lighter in four hands — and more enjoyable, and it strengthens the bonds between people: working together knits people together. A group that cooperates gets the task done and becomes more of a group by doing it.

## Cooperation in the classroom

Its classroom forms are named plainly: studying together, solving problems together, and sharing ideas. And one more thing belongs here: pupils should take part in decisions that affect them. Participation grows understanding and problem-solving skill, and it strengthens pupils' ties with family and teachers.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what cooperation gives — to a task and to the people doing it. Then name the three classroom forms of cooperation.

## Do It Yourself

1. Study one hard thing together with a classmate this week.
2. Share one idea in a group instead of keeping it back.
3. When a decision affects you, take part in it respectfully — ask, listen, suggest.
