# The Rule of Justice — Same Work, Same Treatment

## Do it first

Two children carry the same load the same distance. Should one be paid more because of who they are? Decide before you read — then test your answer against the rule in this lesson.

## The principle

The main principle of justice: equals are treated equally, and unequals unequally.

In today's form: people are treated equally unless they differ in a way that is relevant to the treatment.

[Picture 28030 — same work — same pay]

## The worked example — Jack and Jill

Jack and Jill do the same work equally, so under justice they must be paid equally.

Paying Jack more because he is white, or because of sex, is an unjust difference — colour and sex are not relevant to the work.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the rule of justice: equals are treated equally, and a difference in treatment is just only when it rests on a difference that is relevant. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: relevant, principle, equal treatment. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
