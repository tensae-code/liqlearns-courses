# Lesson plan — The Rule of Justice — Same Work, Same Treatment

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Honesty, Justice and Fairness · **Lesson:** The Rule of Justice — Same Work, Same Treatment
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — Honesty, Justice and Fairness (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the rule of justice: equals are treated equally, and a difference in treatment is just only when it rests on a difference that is relevant.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the lessons before it in this title — the learner already knows the earlier circles of this course (myself, then family, neighbours, school, community and culture) as far as this title. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. ## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Justice — Giving Each Person What Is Due (the previous lesson in this title). Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. The main principle of justice.
2. In today's form.
3. Jack and Jill do the same work equally, so under justice they must be paid equally.
4. Paying Jack more because he is white, or because of sex, is an unjust difference — colour and sex are not relevant to the work.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson's scenes are the learner's own home, school and neighbourhood.

## If the learner is stuck
- Believes that “it is just to pay a worker more because of his colour, even when the work is the same” → go back to the lesson's own words: the rule of justice: equals are treated equally, and a difference in treatment is just only when it rests on a difference that is relevant. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
- Gives the words but no example → ask for the lesson happening once in their own home, school or street today; the example is the proof.
