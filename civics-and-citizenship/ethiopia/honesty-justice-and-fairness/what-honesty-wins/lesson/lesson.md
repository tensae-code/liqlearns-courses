# What Honesty Wins, What Dishonesty Costs

## Do it first

Think of someone everyone trusts with important things. Now think of someone no one trusts with anything. What did each one build, act by act? This lesson names the two roads.

## What honesty wins

The chapter closes with the importance of truthfulness and honesty, and the benefits of being a truthful and honest person.

The first benefit is trust: honest people are believed, relied on, and given responsibility.

[Picture 28028 — two roads — trust and broken trust]

## What dishonesty costs

Against the benefits stand the damages of honesty's absence — pupils must identify the harms of dishonesty as a chapter outcome.

And the proof is conduct: pupils must show the marks of a truthful person in their own behaviour.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: truthfulness and honesty win trust and honour — and their absence brings real harms; a truthful person shows the marks in daily conduct. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: benefit, damage, mark (of a truthful person). Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: the benefits of honesty, and the harms of its absence.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
