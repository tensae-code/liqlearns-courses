# Standing Against What Is Unfair

## Do it first

Recall a time someone was treated unfairly near you — a turn stolen, a blame wrongly given. What did you do? What could a fair person do? Bring your answer into this lesson.

## The fair person's character

The fair person's character must be shown in the pupil's own behaviour — fairness is proved in acts, not claimed in words.

One activity studies pictures of individuals who stood for justice for their people and their government.

[Picture 28032 — speaking up — fairly and calmly]

## Struggling against injustice

Pupils explain the need to struggle against what is unjust in the school and in society.

Standing against unfairness is done fairly: speak up calmly, name the wrong, and seek the rule that protects everyone.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: a fair person shows fairness in their own behaviour — and speaks up, calmly and fairly, against what is unjust at school and in society. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: unjust, injustice. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: showing fairness, and standing against injustice.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
