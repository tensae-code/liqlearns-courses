# Hands-on — Becoming a citizen — birth, descent, naturalisation

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
Ask three people where they were born and who their parents are. For some people the two answers point to the same country; for others they point to different ones. Countries need clear rules for who belongs. This lesson teaches the three main doors in.

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — people become citizens by place of birth, by descent from citizen parents, or by naturalisation — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
