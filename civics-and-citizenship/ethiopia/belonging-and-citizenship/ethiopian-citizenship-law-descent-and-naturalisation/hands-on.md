# Hands-on — Ethiopian citizenship law — descent and naturalisation

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
A baby is born in France. Her mother is Ethiopian. Her father is American. Which country — or countries — can claim her, and which can she claim? Do not guess yet. This lesson gives you Ethiopia's rule, then you work the case.

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — Ethiopia gives citizenship by descent, and a foreigner can become Ethiopian by meeting the Proclamation's requirements — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
