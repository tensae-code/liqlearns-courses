# Ethiopian citizenship law — descent and naturalisation

## Do it first

A baby is born in France. Her mother is Ethiopian. Her father is American. Which country — or countries — can claim her, and which can she claim? Do not guess yet. This lesson gives you Ethiopia's rule, then you work the case.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Ethiopian rule: descent

Ethiopia's first written citizenship law came in 1930; before it, custom governed who belonged. Today the rule stands in **Article 6 of the 1995 FDRE Constitution**: anyone with one or both Ethiopian parents is an Ethiopian citizen.

[Picture 28607 — the Proclamation cover beside an eight-item checklist]

So Ethiopia's door is **descent**, not birthplace. Being born on Ethiopian soil does not by itself make a foreigner's child Ethiopian, and being born abroad does not take citizenship away from an Ethiopian parent's child. The detail of the law is carried in **Proclamation No. 378/2003**.

## Sub lesson 2 — How a foreigner becomes Ethiopian

A foreigner who wants to become Ethiopian must meet the Proclamation's requirements — all of them: be an **adult**; have **lived in Ethiopia at least four years**; **speak a language** of the country's nations and nationalities; have **lawful, sufficient income**; be of **good character, with no criminal record**; show **release, or coming release, from the previous nationality — or be stateless**; and take the **oath of allegiance**.

[Picture 28608 — a family at an office desk with three flags]

There is one more door, rarely opened: citizenship may be granted exceptionally for an **outstanding contribution** to the country.

## Sub lesson 3 — Worked problem: the child of an Ethiopian mother and American father born in France

Now work the opening case with the rule. Mother Ethiopian, father American, baby born in France.

By Ethiopia's rule of descent, the baby is **Ethiopian** — one Ethiopian parent is enough. By descent from her father she may also claim American citizenship, and France may claim her by birthplace. But Ethiopia does not allow dual citizenship: the family holds the choice until the child is 18, and then she must choose.

See how the rule does the work: descent first, then the conditions. That is how every citizenship problem is solved — find the rule, then apply it.
