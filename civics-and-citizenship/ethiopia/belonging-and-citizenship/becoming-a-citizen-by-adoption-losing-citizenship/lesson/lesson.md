# Becoming a citizen by adoption; losing citizenship

## Do it first

Imagine two people handing back a citizenship paper. One smiles: 'I choose this.' The other protests while an official stamps the paper. Both lose citizenship — but are the two losses the same thing? Judge that as you read.

## Sub lesson 1 — Citizenship by adoption

Adoption can carry citizenship. Parents who are citizens — whether citizens by birth or by naturalisation — may adopt a child under 18 who comes from another state, and the child gains citizenship through them.

[Picture 28605 — a family with an adopted child beside two exit doors]

It is not informal. There are set steps to follow, because the state must be sure the adoption is real and the child's place in the new family is secure. When the steps are followed, the adopted child stands in the family — and in the state — as a citizen.

## Sub lesson 2 — Losing citizenship: voluntary and involuntary

Citizenship can also end, in two very different ways.

[Picture 28606 — a passport handed back, and one stamped cancelled]

**Voluntary loss:** the person freely renounces citizenship, giving it up under their own state's rules, by their own choice.

**Involuntary loss:** the state revokes citizenship through a process, for failure to meet requirements. The person does not choose it; the state acts.

Keep the two apart. One is a citizen's own door out. The other is the state closing the door — which is why it needs a process, and why some countries, Ethiopia among them, do not permit it at all. That protected position is the last Belonging lesson.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson's one thing to your companion in your own words: an adopted child can gain citizenship through citizen parents, and citizenship can be lost freely or taken through a process. Use the lesson's own case or example, not the book's sentences. Your companion asks one hard question; answer it from the lesson, or say honestly what you would check.
