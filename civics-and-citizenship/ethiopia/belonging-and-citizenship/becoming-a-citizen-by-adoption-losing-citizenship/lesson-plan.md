# Lesson plan — Becoming a citizen by adoption; losing citizenship

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Belonging and Citizenship · **Lesson:** Becoming a citizen by adoption; losing citizenship
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 15 — Belonging and Citizenship (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** an adopted child can gain citizenship through citizen parents, and citizenship can be lost freely or taken through a process.

## Sub lessons
1. **Citizenship by adoption** — Adoption can carry citizenship.
2. **Losing citizenship: voluntary and involuntary** — Citizenship can also end, in two very different ways.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Becoming a citizen — birth, descent, naturalisation. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain how adoption can carry citizenship.
2. Tell voluntary loss from involuntary loss.
3. Say why the two ways of losing are not the same.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Imagine two people handing back a citizenship paper. One smiles: 'I choose this.' The other protests while an official stamps the paper. Both lose citizenship — but are the two losses the same thing? Judge that as you read.
2. **The explanation** — the lesson's parts in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 28605]: a picture of a family with an adopted child beside two exit doors marked freely given up / taken away — the student matches each door to voluntary and involuntary loss.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 28606]: a picture of a passport being handed back at a counter, and a second one stamped cancelled by an official — the student says which picture shows a free choice.
   - ADD AUDIO: none named in the placements for this lesson — no audio slot is built.
3. **Say it to your companion** — explain the one thing in your own words, with the lesson's own case (lesson/lesson.md).
