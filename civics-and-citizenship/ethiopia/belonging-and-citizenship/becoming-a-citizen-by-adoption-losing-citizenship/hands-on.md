# Hands-on — Becoming a citizen by adoption; losing citizenship

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
Imagine two people handing back a citizenship paper. One smiles: 'I choose this.' The other protests while an official stamps the paper. Both lose citizenship — but are the two losses the same thing? Judge that as you read.

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — an adopted child can gain citizenship through citizen parents, and citizenship can be lost freely or taken through a process — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
