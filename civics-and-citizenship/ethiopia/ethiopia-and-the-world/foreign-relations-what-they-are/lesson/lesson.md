# Foreign Relations — What They Are

## Do it first

Could you live entirely alone — grow, build, heal, defend, celebrate, all yourself? Name the first thing that defeats you. Now scale the question to a country of over a hundred million people: what defeats *its* aloneness first? Begin.

## No state lives alone

From Aristotle's saying that **man is a social animal**, the argument runs by analogy: as no person is self-sufficient, **no state can exist or sustain itself in isolation**. A state's external relations with the world's countries, undertaken to fulfil and ensure its **national interests**, are its **foreign relations**. 

[Picture 28882 — Ethiopia at the centre of a web of handshakes with its neighbours]



Ethiopia's interests are itemised in the evidence layer as three: **peace and security; socio-economic development; and global influence** — the three chairs at the country's table, around which the neighbours' flags stand. 

[Picture 28883 — A country's table with three chairs — peace, development, influence (teacher guide — evidence)]



And Ethiopia's relations are not improvised; they are **anchored in Article 86** of the 1995 FDRE Constitution, whose heads are taught as given: protection of national interests and sovereignty; mutual benefit and equality of states; observance of international agreements; non-interference in other states' internal affairs; and an emphasis on mutual economic development, peaceful coexistence and strong public relations with neighbours and other African states.

Hold the shape of the whole band from here: relations (this lesson), the interest they serve (next), the policy that steers them, the instruments that work them, and Ethiopia's region where all of it is practised.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the analogy and the definition — then the three interests and two of Article 86's anchors.
