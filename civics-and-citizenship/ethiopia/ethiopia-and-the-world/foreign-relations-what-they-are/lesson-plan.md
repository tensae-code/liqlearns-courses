# Lesson plan — Foreign Relations — What They Are

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Ethiopia and the World · **Lesson:** Foreign Relations — What They Are
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 25 — Ethiopia and the World (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** no state lives alone: a state's external relations with the world's countries, undertaken to fulfil and ensure its national interests, are its foreign relations — and Ethiopia's are anchored in Article 86 of its constitution.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on all the citizenship levels before it — the learner knows the state from inside; this band turns the state outward, to its relations with neighbours and the world.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Argue the no-state-alone claim from the person-alone analogy.
2. Define foreign relations.
3. Name Article 86's anchors for Ethiopia.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks foreign relations is only ambassadors → it is the state's whole external relations, undertaken for its national interests.
- Thinks a big state could live alone → the analogy's claim is existence itself: no state can exist or sustain itself in isolation.
