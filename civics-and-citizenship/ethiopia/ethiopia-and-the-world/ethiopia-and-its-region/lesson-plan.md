# Lesson plan — Ethiopia and Its Region

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Ethiopia and the World · **Lesson:** Ethiopia and Its Region
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 25 — Ethiopia and the World (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** in its region, Ethiopia's foreign policy works three fields: cooperation over shared rivers and through IGAD, peace and security against terror and civil war, and the trade, oil and power flows that bind the Horn's economies together.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on all the citizenship levels before it — the learner knows the state from inside; this band turns the state outward, to its relations with neighbours and the world.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Regional cooperation** — shared rivers, the GERD disagreement as live negotiation, IGAD, and the two stated interests
2. **Peace and security** — peace as precondition; Ethiopia as regional power and peacekeeper against terror and civil war
3. **Trade, oil and power** — the Djibouti bedrock, Sudan oil savings, and power exports to three neighbours

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Instruments of Foreign Policy. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the Horn's states as the books list them.
2. Tell the cooperation story: rivers, the GERD negotiation, IGAD.
3. Give the three economic flows: Djibouti port, Sudan oil, power exports.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

1. **Do it first** — the learner sketches the Horn and marks a river, a port and a fragile border (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the three fields of regional policy in the book's order — cooperation, peace and security, socio-economic development (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Reverses a flow → oil flows *from* Sudan *to* Ethiopia (savings); power flows *from* Ethiopia *to* Djibouti, Sudan and Kenya (earnings).
- Thinks landlocked means cut off → Ethiopia is the largest portless country in the Horn, and precisely therefore its regional policy is a lifeline, worked daily.
