# Global Issues — Work, Disease, Terror

## Do it first

One road, three signposts: a factory gate chained shut; a clinic chart climbing; a street cordoned off. Before reading, walk the road in your head and write what each signpost costs a society — not in money. The book's costs are dignity, years of life, and the meaning of a word everyone uses and few define.

## Three signposts on one road

**Unemployment**: looking for work and not finding it. Its costs: people locked in poverty, skills wasted, inequality perpetuated as wealth concentrates — and societies destabilised, high youth unemployment standing behind conflicts, uprisings and unrest. The jobless are socially excluded into anxiety and hopelessness; crime, drug trafficking and use, juvenile delinquency, family breakdown, domestic violence and migration follow in the book's accounting. Against it, the lesson sets the positive: meaningful employment gives dignity, a place in the community, character, and developed human potential.

**Disease**: travel and migration spreading HIV/AIDS and Covid-19 worldwide — with origins as the book states them, recorded at the disadvantages lesson — AIDS named a poor person's disease, reversing life-expectancy gains.

**Terrorism**: hard to define, inherently political — one side's terrorism is the actor's independence, liberation, struggle, jihad, self-defence or revolution; the word is rarely applied to one's own governments. The book's definition, held exactly: **violence against non-combatants, civilians or other normally illegitimate targets, to attract attention to a political cause, force the aloof to join, or intimidate opponents into concessions** — a global security problem of hostage-taking, bombing, hijacking and indiscriminate attacks on civilian targets.

Tell your companion: the three signposts and their costs. Finish with the definition — terrorism, in the book's exact terms — and why the book says the word is so hard to hold.
