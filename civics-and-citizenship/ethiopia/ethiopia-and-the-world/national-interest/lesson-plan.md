# Lesson plan — National Interest

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Ethiopia and the World · **Lesson:** National Interest
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 25 — Ethiopia and the World (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** national interest is what every state is protecting: its goals, claims and demands among nations — and at its core, survival: sovereignty, security, political independence and territorial integrity.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on all the citizenship levels before it — the learner knows the state from inside; this band turns the state outward, to its relations with neighbours and the world.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Foreign Relations — What They Are. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define national interest, core sense first.
2. Name what heads every state's list.
3. Give the two interlock examples: Somalia's crisis, Djibouti's port.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks national interest means selfishness → its core is survival — a universally accepted right — and interests interlock, so neighbours' states are partly one's own business.
- Forgets the core sense → goals and claims sit on a floor: sovereignty protected from intervention or aggression.
