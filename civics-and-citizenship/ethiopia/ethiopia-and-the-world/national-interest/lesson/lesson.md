# National Interest

## Do it first

What would your family protect first if everything could not be protected — the house, the land title, the children's safety, the family name? Rank them under pressure. A state's ranking, under the same pressure, is this lesson.

## What every state is protecting

**National interest** is the goals, claims and demands a state always seeks to achieve, preserve and secure among nations — political, socio-economic, cultural and security interests. In its **core sense** it is plainer and harder: **survival** — protection of sovereignty from external intervention or aggression, a universally accepted right that governs all state behaviour. 

[Picture 28884 — A shield labelled 'sovereignty' over a port of Ethiopian goods at Djibouti]



So the heads of every state's list are fixed: **security, political independence, and territorial integrity**. Before trade, before prestige, before friendship — the state must *continue*, and everything else is built on that floor.

And interests **interlock**; no state's list is private. On the dark side: Somalia's long security crisis is Ethiopia's concern, and the world's — insecurity exports itself. On the bright side: about **90 per cent of Ethiopia's imports arrive through Djibouti's port** — one state's development riding on another's harbour. The shield in the picture stands over that port for a reason: sovereignty and dependence, held in one lesson, without flinching from either.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the definition — core sense first — then the three heads of every list, then the port figure.
