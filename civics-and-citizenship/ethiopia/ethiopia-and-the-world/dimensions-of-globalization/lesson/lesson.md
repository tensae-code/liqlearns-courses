# Dimensions of Globalization

## Do it first

One house, four windows. Through one: a parliament debating a policy copied from abroad. Through another: a port. Through a third: a family video-calling across an ocean. Through the fourth: a festival where two cultures' songs trade verses. Same house — the connected world. Before reading, guess the four names this lesson gives the windows.

## Four windows, one house

**Political globalization**: the intensification and expansion of political interrelations across the globe — the worldwide diffusion of political ideas and culture producing similar political policies, ideologies, cultures and institutions across countries. **Economic globalization**: the intensification of economic interdependence — expanding mutual integration of world markets through cross-border trade in commodities and services, international capital flow and rapid, wide diffusion of technology. 

[Picture 28985 — Four windows of one house — a parliament, a port, a family chat screen, a festival]



**Social globalization**: the sharing of ideas and information between and within countries, transmitted via internet, social media and similar means — bearing on human lives and work, on families and societies: employment and working conditions, income, social protection and security, culture and identity, social inclusion and family cohesion. **Cultural globalization**: the circulation and diffusion of ideas, meanings and values — seen as increasing social contact: people themselves being connected, not only money and commodities.

Keep the four apart by their cargo: policies travel through the first, goods and capital through the second, information and life-patterns through the third, meanings and values through the fourth. One event can load all four ships at once — the sorting in the doing below is where the dimensions prove themselves.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: the four dimensions and each one's cargo. Then take one event from this week's talk and load all four ships with it — or say honestly which ship it does not board.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the four dimensions as four windows. In each, draw or describe one scene from your own town that belongs there — and only there.
2. Take coffee: write its journey four times, once per dimension. Four short paragraphs, four different stories about the same bean.
3. Find one example that refuses sorting — an event loading all four ships. Write why the dimensions still help, even when they overlap.
