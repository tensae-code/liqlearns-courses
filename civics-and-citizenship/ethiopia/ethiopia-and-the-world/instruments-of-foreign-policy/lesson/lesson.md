# Instruments of Foreign Policy

## Do it first

A dispute with a neighbour must be settled. Your toolbox holds a conversation, a trade deal, and a weapon. A wise hand reaches for them *in an order* — and the order itself is a moral fact. Guess the order and defend it before the lesson fixes it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Diplomacy

States implement foreign policy through instruments, and **diplomacy** heads the list: the ways states manage sovereign relations with other mutually recognised states — the **art and practice of conducting negotiations between nations**, the skill of handling affairs without arousing hostility, and the machinery and personnel by which foreign policy is executed. 

[Picture 28886 — Two negotiators across a table, a signed paper between them]



Its maxim is kept, in paraphrase: diplomacy is **not an end but a means; not a purpose but a method** — seeking by reason, conciliation and exchange of interests to prevent major conflicts between sovereign states. Diplomacy does not replace the national interest; it is how the interest travels without burning the road behind it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Economic means and military force

Economic methods arise from **unequal development**. States stand at very different levels — the USA, European states, China, Japan and India named at one pole; many African states, including Ethiopia and its neighbours, at the other — and all depend on each other to meet economic interests. That **interdependence is itself an instrument**: trade, aid, ports and pipelines persuade where speeches cannot. 

[Picture 28887 — A scale — trade goods one side, a sheathed sword laid last]

 

[Picture 28888 — A handshake, a trade ship and a sheathed sword, in that order (teacher guide — evidence)]



**Military force is the last resort** — used when national interests cannot be fulfilled peacefully. It is violent imposition, and bargaining power follows military strength and technology, with the USA, Russia and China named as holding great power in the arena.

The doing task is the argument itself, and it is yours to make, not to recite: **explain why force is held to last.** Reach for the costs — what force destroys that diplomacy builds, what it does to the interests it claims to serve, and what remains possible the day after it is used.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the three instruments in order — then your why-force-is-last argument, in under a minute.

## Do It Yourself

2. Find one current example of each instrument in the news you can access. Label them 1, 2, 3 by the lesson's order.
3. Write diplomacy's maxim from memory and one sentence on what it forbids diplomats to become.
