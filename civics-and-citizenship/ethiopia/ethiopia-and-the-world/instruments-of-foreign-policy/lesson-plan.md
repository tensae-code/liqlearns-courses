# Lesson plan — Instruments of Foreign Policy

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Ethiopia and the World · **Lesson:** Instruments of Foreign Policy
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 25 — Ethiopia and the World (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** states work their foreign policy through instruments in a fixed order: diplomacy first, economic means second, and military force last — the resort when peaceful fulfilment fails.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on all the citizenship levels before it — the learner knows the state from inside; this band turns the state outward, to its relations with neighbours and the world.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Diplomacy** — negotiation between recognised states; reason, conciliation and exchange of interests
2. **Economic means and military force** — interdependence as an instrument; force as violent last resort; the why-last argument as a doing task

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Foreign Policy. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the three instruments in order.
2. Define diplomacy with its maxim.
3. Argue why force is held to last.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks diplomacy is the goal → its own maxim refuses: a means, a method — the interest is the goal.
- Thinks force's place is about weakness → force is last because of what it costs and destroys, in a world where bargaining power still follows strength.
