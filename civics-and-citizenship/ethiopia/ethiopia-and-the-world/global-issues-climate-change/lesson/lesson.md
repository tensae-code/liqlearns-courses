## The problem family and its three causes

Climate change and its family of problems stand among the greatest challenges humanity faces. The book's accounting is blunt: economic growth and innovation raised wealth while degrading natural resources — producing supply constraints: greenhouse-gas emissions, overexploited resources, soil erosion, overfishing — food production cut and food prices raised.

The problem family, as the book lists it: **acid rain, forest destruction, falling biodiversity, desertification, contaminated oceans, ozone destruction** — and the biggest, **global warming**: rising seas submerging island and coastal towns, more drought, declining agriculture and fishing, extreme weather (storms, floods, droughts, heat waves), spreading infectious disease, desertification. Three human causes drive it. **Massive industrialisation**: surging demand for energy and resources, emitting harmful gases and solid particles. **Rapid population growth**: competition for limited resources — overgrazing, over-cultivation, deforestation for farmland, pressure on food production. **Rapid urbanisation**: pollution and congestion, urban garbage and waste, wood energy and construction driving deforestation, automobiles polluting the air. The guide's evidence answers support this lesson from its concept lesson — rising seas, droughts, storms, desertification and spreading disease — labelled as such in sources.
