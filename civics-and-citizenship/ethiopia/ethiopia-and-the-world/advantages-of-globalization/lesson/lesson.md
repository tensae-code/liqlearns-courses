# Advantages of Globalization

## Do it first

A crane lifts Ethiopian coffee onto a ship. Somewhere, a conference table fills with flags. A counter clerk hands a family money sent from a relative abroad. Three scenes, one web, all gains. Before reading, write the gains you can already see — the book's ledger is longer, and it is arranged in five columns.

## Sub lesson 1 — Economy and politics

**Economic advantages**: sharply increased trade and exchange generating strong global growth, rapid industrial development, and rapidly expanding technologies and commodities — a global common market based on free exchange of goods and capital. Larger, diverse markets raise revenues and domestic growth; advances in transport and information technology leap productivity. And countries hold **environmental comparative advantages**: natural capital of economic value — forests, fisheries, tourist attractions; ecological advantages like carbon sinks; and aesthetic, historical and scientific value. 

[Picture 28986 — A port crane loading Ethiopian coffee beside a conference table of many flags]



**Political advantages**: people learn how others are governed and what freedoms they hold — universal awareness fixing the principles of democratic governance (good governance, transparency, accountability) as standards. Participation in international treaties protects human rights and multiplies global and regional standards — copyright, patent and world-trade principles named. Regional and international integration deepens security cooperation; and a common political culture circulates around peace, justice, equality, freedom, democracy, competition and free elections — even where many countries fail to practise what circulates.

## Sub lesson 2 — Society, culture, money

**Social advantages**: transformation of social development — wider, faster spread of material and spiritual values across states and societies; education turned market-oriented with labour mobility, pressing states toward proper educational policies; the dispersion of values, ideas, knowledge and beliefs through travel and migration, improving health, education and ecological practice; technology simplifying daily life.

**Cultural advantages**: exchange driving migration and travel, leading to multiculturalism and cross-cultural contact, and a consciousness of world culture — the worked example is **coffee, said by the book to originate in Ethiopia**, drunk now on every continent. **Financial advantages**: simplified regulations, eliminated mediators and broken barriers between financial centres — **remittances arriving via MoneyGram** the book's worked example — and worldwide markets giving better access to external financing. 

[Picture 28987 — A remittance counter beside a coffee cup lettered with three cities' names]

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: the five ledgers, one gain each, minimum. Then the two worked examples — coffee and MoneyGram — and what each proves.

## Do It Yourself

1. Build the five-column ledger on one page. Fill each column from the lesson; star the gain you judge largest for Ethiopia, and defend the star in three sentences.
2. Write coffee's advantage-story: origin (as the book states it), journey, and what returns to Ethiopia besides money.
3. Interview the remittance: ask a family that receives one what changed — access, speed, cost. Write the answers under the financial column.
