# Disadvantages of Globalization

## Do it first

Opposite the traditional clothes stall, a fast-food sign lights up — brighter, cheaper, everywhere the same. Something is gained on that street and something is lost. Before reading, name one of each. This lesson fills the loss side of the balance — and ends by handing the balance to governments.

## Sub lesson 1 — Culture and politics

**Cultural loss and insecurity**: homogenisation — local languages and cultural practices disappearing, imported cultures replacing local ones by hybridisation and assimilation; Westernised or Americanised music and film reshaping societies far away. The book's Ethiopian evidence is pointed: young people preferring Western fast food and clothes over local products, mixing English into local languages, even giving children foreign names — identity itself being given up. 

[Picture 28989 — A traditional clothes stall closing while a fast-food sign lights up opposite]



**Political harms**: pressure on developing countries' leaders to adopt political and social models opposite to their peoples' feelings, reinforcing authoritarian regimes — counter-terrorism measures carried out at external powers' behest named as the case. And **fragmentation**: economies, polities, societies and cultures fragmenting, national consciousness and cohesion weakening — divisiveness and instability following, facilitating authoritarian rule — while conflicts take new characteristics from global weapons traffic, blurred political and business interests, and new actors.

Over the whole ledger stands the book's governing frame: **advantages or disadvantages follow how different governments manage globalization.** The harms are real; they are not weather. They are what unmanaged connection does.

## Sub lesson 2 — Economy and security

**Economic harms**: growth that undermines economic and social fabrics, violates markets and destroys the environment; income inequality and disproportionate benefit; developing economies **marginalised** — dependent on a few primary goods whose demand and prices are set externally — accentuating poverty and inequality. Brain-drain carries skills away. Imported capital-intensive production suppresses indigenous technology, worsening unemployment and poverty.

**Social insecurity in four forms** — the book's own figure, redrawn below. **Personal**: criminals harvesting the deregulated web — illicit trade in drugs, women and weapons; laundered money; internet trafficking networks; crime groups criminalising politics, business and police. **Job and income**: restructuring and dismantled protection leaving precarious work under flexible labour. **Health**: travel and migration spreading disease — HIV/AIDS and Covid-19, which the book says first emerged in America and China respectively; AIDS a poor person's disease, reversing life-expectancy gains. **Environmental**: transport emissions, warming, air pollution; resource depletion, deforestation, ecosystem and biodiversity destruction; garbage and plastic pollution; accelerated climate change. 

[Picture 28990 — Four warning signs — a factory gate closed, an empty wallet, a clinic queue, a smokestack — over one globe]



Read the two sub lessons together and the balance from the last lesson is complete: the gains were real, and so is this. What a government — and a citizen — does with the web is the next lesson.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: the harms in their two sets, then the four insecurities as four warning signs. Finish with the frame — whose hands hold the balance?

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the Ethiopian cultural evidence as a field report: three observations from your own street matching the book's three (food and clothes, language mixing, names). Honest matching only.
2. Take marginalisation apart: choose one primary good Ethiopia exports. Trace who sets its price, and what 'dependent on a few primary goods' means for a farmer at the chain's start.
3. Write the management frame as a law in one sentence. Beneath, list three management acts a government could perform — you will need them next lesson.
