# Fighting Globalization's Harms

## Do it first

Farmers are clearing an irrigation channel together, the way their grandparents did, while a school class watches and writes it down. An outside agency offered to replace the channel with a design nobody local could repair. Before reading: which offer do you take — and what does your choice say about fighting the web's harms?

## Sub lesson 1 — Home knowledge and community

The stance first: ignoring globalization's real costs is neither possible nor defensible — and the only way forward is **better management at national and international levels**. Not retreat: management.

**Promoting indigenous knowledge**: against the Western knowledge carried by the IMF and World Bank and counted as the only legitimate knowledge — and against education systems copied from the West — the counter-measure disseminates indigenous knowledge through the education system itself: the creativity of generations in agriculture, health care and medicine, technology, food preparation, conservation and natural-resource management. The young learn to value their own creative efforts, motivated by forebears' knowledge appreciated and carried into the future.

**Valuing community development**: the community as pillar of the whole structure. The worked example: **collectively maintained irrigation channels** distributing water resources fairly — and local knowledge knowing where a well should go. What local people invest in planning and execution sustains; what outsiders' unsuited designs build, fails to sustain.

## Sub lesson 2 — Protection and cooperation

**Indigenous social welfare and protection**: safety nets reducing dependence on conditional external aid — Ethiopia's traditional, religious and community mechanisms standing as the named first line of defence. Protection comes in three kinds. **Protective**: basic transfers stopping the vulnerable starving — public employment schemes, school feeding, regular cash transfers. **Preventive**: stopping falls into destitution — contributory social security, savings and insurance, universal child allowances. **Promotive**: raising real incomes and capabilities through livelihood interventions. 

[Picture 28992 — Three shields lettered Protect, Prevent, Promote standing over a village, neighbouring flags joined behind]



**Regional cooperation**: nations of comparable status intensifying political and economic cooperation — trade blocs, common policies, pooling values across governments, NGOs and private sectors of three or more countries — fostering transformation and industrialisation, answering Western domination functionally rather than rhetorically.

And the **innovation fund**: prizes rewarding researchers for the value of their innovations to developing countries — big rewards, the book argues, for cures for incurable, widespread, socially costly diseases — redirecting research incentives and cutting technology-import costs. The guide's evidence picture gathers the lesson: a village channel kept by its own people, a regional map, and a medal for a local inventor.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: the stance in one sentence. Then the five counter-measures — knowledge, community, protection (all three kinds), cooperation, innovation fund.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the stance as a poster line: 'We cannot go forward by ___' — complete it from the lesson, then defend the completion in a paragraph.
2. Sort twelve welfare instruments into protective, preventive, promotive. Any instrument that fits two kinds gets an honest split note.
3. Design your parish or kebele's channel: one collectively maintained thing, its fairness rule, and how a school class records its knowledge before it is lost.
