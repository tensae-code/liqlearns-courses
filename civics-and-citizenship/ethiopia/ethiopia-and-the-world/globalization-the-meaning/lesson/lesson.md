# Globalization — The Meaning

## Do it first

A stall in a village market: coffee from here, shoes from across one ocean, a phone charged by a panel from across another. Strings run from that stall to the whole map. Before reading, pull one string: what happened, somewhere far away, that put that object on this stall? This lesson names the web you just touched.

## The web and its name

**Globalization** is the multiplicity of linkages and interconnections between the states and societies that make up the modern world system — a process by which events, decisions and activities in one part of the world can come to have **significant consequences** for individuals and communities in quite distant parts of the globe. 

[Picture 28983 — A village market stall with goods lettered from four continents, string lines running from the stall to a world map]



The word is often used for its economic face: the **integration of national economies** through trade, foreign direct investment, capital flows, migration and the spread of technology. But the book's definition is wider than money — it is the whole web of states and societies, decisions and consequences.

What drives it: **revolutions in information technology, transportation and communications**, easing the movement of goods, services, capital and knowledge across borders — the interactions and interdependencies defining modern international relations. The guide's evidence picture draws the web itself: a globe threaded with routes in the four colours of the four dimensions the next lesson names.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: the definition, the face the word usually names, and the three revolutions driving the web. End at your market stall: one string, pulled, fully traced.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the definition from memory. Circle the phrase about distant consequences — the heart of the word.
2. Take three objects near you now. Trace each one's string: where made, by what route, decided by whom. One page, three journeys.
3. Write the economic face in one sentence, then the wider definition in one sentence. Say which one the word 'globalization' usually means in newspapers — and what that habit hides.
