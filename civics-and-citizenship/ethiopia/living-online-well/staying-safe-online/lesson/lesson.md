# Staying Safe Online

## Do it first

Look at the picture in your mind: a smiling avatar mask — and behind it, a shadow you cannot name. Hold that two-layer picture. Everything in this lesson hangs on the difference between the mask and what stands behind it.

## The dangers, named plainly

The dangers of unethical and careless use are named plainly: violated privacy; physical and information insecurity; strangers using anonymity against the young; bullying; and adults who befriend children online to exploit them. Naming them plainly is the protection's first layer: a child who can name a danger can report it, refuse it, and warn a friend about it.

## Sara's story — the secrecy itself

The chapter's closing story is Sara's, given in summary only: a girl keeps a 'Facebook friend' secret from her parents, and the class works out what the secrecy itself endangers. Notice where the lesson puts its point — not on any single event, but on the secrecy itself. A secret kept from parents cuts the child off from the very people who could protect her. The chapter closes with a yes/no self-rating review: check yourself, honestly, item by item.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the dangers named in this lesson. Then tell Sara's story exactly as the summary gives it — no added events — and say where its point lies: the secrecy itself.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the yes/no self-rating on your own online life today, honestly.
2. If anything online is currently secret from your parents, end the secrecy — tell them today.
3. Learn the danger list by heart and teach it to one friend.
