# Lesson plan — What Social Media Is Good For

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Living Online Well · **Lesson:** What Social Media Is Good For
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 11 — Living Online Well (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** What social media is good for: connection — people far apart staying close; learning — knowledge and teaching travelling to where the learner is; sharing — news, work, art and help passed hand to hand; and keeping in touch — the ordinary thread of family and friendship, maintained daily instead of lost to distance.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the lessons before it in Level 11 — Living Online Well — and on the learner's own daily life at home, at school and in the community. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **“a picture of …” in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say and show what “The goods, named” teaches, in their own words, and do the practice named for it.
2. Say and show what “A tool, not a threat — first” teaches, in their own words, and do the practice named for it.
3. Carry the lesson's practice into the week: using the tool for its goods — connect, learn, share, keep in touch.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — every activity uses the learner's own home, school and street.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Picture a grandparent in a far village and a child in town, faces on one small screen, laughing. The distance between them has not moved — but it has been crossed. Before reading, name two more goods a tool like that can do.
2. **The explanation** — the lesson's parts, in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 28248]: a picture of a child video-calling a grandparent far away — the student studies the picture and names what it shows about the lesson before reading on.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the lesson's key sentences aloud — the student listens once, then reads them aloud in the same voice.
3. **Say it to your companion** — as written in lesson/lesson.md.
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the student watches and then does the Say it to your companion with a real companion.
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-or-false, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.

## If the learner is stuck
- Cannot say what the lesson teaches → go back to the Do it first and the picture; name what the picture shows, in one sentence, then read the first part again slowly.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → hold the one thing: What social media is good for: connection — people far apart staying close; learning — knowledge and teaching travelling to where the learner is; sharing — news, work, art and help passed hand to hand; and keeping in touch — the ordinary thread of family and friendship, maintained daily instead of lost to distance.
- Finds the practice hard → shrink it to the smallest real version and do that today; small and done beats grand and promised.
