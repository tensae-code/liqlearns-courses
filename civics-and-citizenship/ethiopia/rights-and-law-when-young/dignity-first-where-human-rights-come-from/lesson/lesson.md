# Dignity First — Where Human Rights Come From

## Do it first

Think of a classmate who needed help — with food, water, clothing or money. Did helping them feel like giving them something extra, or like treating them as they deserve? Hold your answer. This lesson shows why it is the second one.

## Sub lesson 1 — Inherited dignity — the dignity you are born with

Every human being has dignity simply by being a member of the human race. You need no qualification beyond being human — no exam, no wealth, no rank. This inherited dignity can never be earned and never be taken away.

## Sub lesson 2 — Respecting dignity is the basis of human rights

**Respecting dignity is the basis of human rights.** Respecting human dignity is the foundation for human rights. Human rights cannot be seen apart from human dignity: because every person has dignity, laws exist to protect every person's rights. The reasons for respecting dignity are these: it respects human rights, it moves us to give humanitarian aid, and it is the ground on which human laws are framed.

**Helping, and the customs we stop.** Respecting dignity shows in daily acts: if your friend needs your help, help — with money, clothing, food, water and the like. Respecting another person's dignity is also an Ethiopian custom by which your own dignity is respected in return. Some old customs do the opposite — they injure people's bodies and rights: female circumcision, under-age marriage, cutting the face in mourning, cutting the body with blades, wastefulness and abduction (ጠለፋ). Practices that injure bodies and rights are not kept; they are stopped.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: where do human rights come from? Say it in one sentence, then give one helping act that respects dignity, and name one harmful practice that must stop.
