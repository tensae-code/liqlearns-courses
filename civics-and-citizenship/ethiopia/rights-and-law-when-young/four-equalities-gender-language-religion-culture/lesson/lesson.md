# Four Equalities — Gender, Language, Religion, Culture

## Do it first

Name the languages spoken by children you know. Is any one of them worth less than another? This lesson works through four equalities, one by one.

## Sub lesson 1 — Gender equality

Women and men, girls and boys, are equal in rights and benefit — in housework, education, farm work, family responsibility and trade. Women can do what men do, and men can do what women do. In past times women lacked not ability, but the chance to participate.

## Sub lesson 2 — Language equality

In Ethiopia all languages are equal and must be respected equally. People have the right to use their language for learning, for creative writing and literature, and to express themselves easily in their mother tongue. Languages named are Afar, Amharic, Somali, Oromo and Tigrinya, among others.

## Sub lesson 3 — Religion equality

Every citizen has the right to follow the faith he or she chooses, and to teach or preach its teachings. Ethiopia's faiths — Christianity, Islam and indigenous faiths (Waaqeffanna) among them — live with tolerance and mutual respect.

## Sub lesson 4 — Culture equality

No culture is greater or lesser than another. Cultures can change as social and economic life develops, and every culture is practised with pride. Practise all four equalities in the classroom and the neighbourhood.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the four equalities, with one example for each.
