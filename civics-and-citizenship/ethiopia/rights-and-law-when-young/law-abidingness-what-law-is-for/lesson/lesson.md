# Law-Abidingness — What Law Is For

## Do it first

Open a rule-book in your mind. Why does a country write laws down at all? This lesson gives law its three purposes — and the duties of a citizen who lives by them.

## Sub lesson 1 — What law-abidingness is

Law-abidingness is readiness and commitment to obey your country's laws. Law itself has three purposes: to make citizens know their rights and duties; to direct behaviour; and to secure citizens' equality. Paying tax honestly belongs here too: tax is the payment individuals and organisations make to government from their income, a legal duty everyone owes, because without tax the common services — education, health, defence — cannot be had.

## Sub lesson 2 — A law-abiding citizen's duties

The law-abiding citizen is ready to obey, knows the country's laws, and settles problems peacefully. At school the same shape holds: know the school's rules, complete teachers' tasks, treat peers with respect — respecting the law and respecting the school's rules are one habit, practised in two places.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion law's three purposes, and three duties of a law-abiding citizen — one of them at school.
