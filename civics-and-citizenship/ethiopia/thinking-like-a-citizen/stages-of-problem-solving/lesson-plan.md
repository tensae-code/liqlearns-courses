# Lesson plan — Stages of Problem-Solving

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Thinking Like a Citizen · **Lesson:** Stages of Problem-Solving
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 21 — Thinking Like a Citizen (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** problem-solving climbs four stages in order: define the problem, brainstorm, decide on the solution, and implement — and even the best solution fails through ineffective implementation.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the citizenship levels before it — the learner can already think critically; this band turns that thinking onto problems themselves, as a citizen's working skill.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Define the problem** — root causes and nature, by questioning and many angles, until clearly understood
2. **Brainstorm** — dig deeper, gather facts from those connected, creativity balanced with logic
3. **Decide on the solution** — organise, break into pieces, link options, select and commit
4. **Implement** — an action plan, shared with the right people, followed through — with the stage's warning

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks brainstorming is deciding quickly → brainstorming gathers facts and options; deciding is the *next* stage, with analysis and commitment.
- Thinks a good decision guarantees success → stage four carries the warning: the best solution fails through ineffective implementation.
