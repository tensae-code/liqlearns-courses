# Stages of Problem-Solving

## Do it first

Your school problem, chosen now: pick a real one — small, present, yours. A broken tap, a quarrelsome queue, a dark corridor. You will climb the staircase with it in this lesson, one stage per sub lesson, and you will not be allowed to skip a step.

## Sub lesson 1 — Define the problem

Stage one: **define the problem** — find its root causes and its nature by questioning, and by viewing it from different angles, until it is clearly understood. 

[Picture 28878 — A four-step staircase with one worked school problem climbing it]



Work it on the staircase's own problem: the school tap drips all day and the yard floods. The blurry problem is 'the tap'. The defined problem, after questioning: *the washer inside the tap has perished, no one owns the repair, and the puddle blocks the path to the latrines.* Root cause, ownership, consequence — that is a definition you can climb from.

## Sub lesson 2 — Brainstorm

Stage two: **brainstorm** — dig deeper and gather as many facts as possible, asking those connected to the problem, balancing creativity with logic.

At the tap: Who last repaired it? What does a washer cost? Who keeps keys and tools? Could the path be re-routed while repair waits? Facts first, wild ideas welcome, judgement postponed — brainstorming is gathering, not grading.

## Sub lesson 3 — Decide on the solution

Stage three: **decide on the solution** — organise and analyse the gathered information, break the big problem into smaller pieces, build links between options, then select the best strategy and **commit to it**.

At the tap: replace the washer (small piece), assign the repair to a named person with a named day (second piece), re-route the path for one week (third piece). The pieces join into one strategy — and deciding means committing, not admiring the options. The guide's pairing runs as handrails along this staircase: **creative thinking** gives more options on the way up; **critical thinking** gives better judgement among them.

## Sub lesson 4 — Implement

Stage four: **implement** — draw up an action plan, share it with the appropriate people, and follow through. 

[GIF 28879 — A figure climbing the four stages, ticking each one off]



And carry the warning fixed to this stage like a maker's plate: **even the best solution fails through ineffective implementation.** Watch the marker climb the staircase and tick each stage as it is honestly passed — the climb, not the view from the top, is the skill. The next lesson takes up the two handrails — critical and creative thinking — and trains them directly.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the four stages in order — then climb your own problem up them aloud, one stage at a time, without skipping.

## Do It Yourself

1. Finish the climb: your carried problem, four stages, written out in full — definition, facts, decision, plan.
2. Name the stage you personally rush. Write the discipline that would slow you there.
3. Find a failed good solution in your world — one that died at stage four. Write its autopsy in five lines.
