# Getting better at critical thinking — strategies that work

## Do it first

A maze. One thinker measures before entering. One traces the path backward from the exit. One stands frozen at the entrance, thinking and thinking, never stepping in. Before reading: all three are 'thinking hard'. Only two are strategies.

## Sub lesson 1 — The ten strategies

The book's warning opens the set: **children are not born critical thinkers, and most people never learn**. The ten teachable strategies: **trial and error**; being a **lifelong, continual learner**; **understanding the barriers**; creating **mental images**; seeking **patterns**; using **manipulatives** — moving objects to organise a problem; considering **unusual opinions**; avoiding **information overload and analysis paralysis** by holding the big picture; identifying **multiple solutions**; and **monitoring a solution** instead of rushing to an answer.

## Sub lesson 2 — The guide's three core strategies in depth

The guide's evidence goes deep on three. The **guesstimate**: estimate before calculating, so the real answer has something to be checked against. **Working backward**: start from the goal and trace the path in reverse. **Breaking the problem down**: smaller pieces, each solvable.

[Picture 28765 — a maze solved three ways, one thinker frozen at the entrance]

And its warning matches the student book's: **analysis paralysis** — thinking so long that nothing is decided — is a failure of thinking, not an excess of it.
