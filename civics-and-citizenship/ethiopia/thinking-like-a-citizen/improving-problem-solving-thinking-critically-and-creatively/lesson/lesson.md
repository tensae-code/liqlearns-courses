# Improving Problem-Solving — Thinking Critically and Creatively

## Do it first

One gear turns and drives nothing. Add a second gear, meshed with the first, and the machine moves. Which gear are you — the one that invents options, or the one that tests them? And which gear do you neglect? The lesson machines both.

## Two gears, one machine

**Creative problem-solving** identifies unique solutions through problem identification and resolution planning. Its effectiveness depends on the approach and strategy chosen, and the skill needs **constant improvement** — building, around the solver, an environment of consistent innovation. In one line: problem-solving as **taking control of one's surroundings**, instead of being carried by them. 

[Picture 28880 — Two gears meshed — Analyse and Invent — turning one solution cog]



Its two engines are contrasted, and the contrast is the lesson. **Critical thinking** is reflective, independent, clear and rational thought: it understands logical connections, weighs facts, and evaluates information scientifically — held by open-minded, fair and reasonable thinkers.

**Creative thinking** is thinking outside the box: generating new methods and solutions. Creativity **generates** ideas, while critical thinking **analyses and evaluates** them — and the creative solver needs both, meshed: invention without judgement floods the desk with glitter; judgement without invention starves it to a single grey option.

Recall the staircase: brainstorming is the creative gear spinning freely; deciding is the critical gear engaging. Improvement means oiling both — deliberately, constantly, and on real problems, not in the abstract.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion which gear generates and which evaluates — then name the gear you personally neglect.

## Do It Yourself

1. Take yesterday's solved problem and re-run it with the neglected gear: if you invented, now evaluate ruthlessly; if you judged, now invent five more options.
2. Write one critical question and one creative question about the same local problem. Label each gear.
3. For one week, log one use of each engine daily. Two lines a day; the log is the improvement environment in miniature.
