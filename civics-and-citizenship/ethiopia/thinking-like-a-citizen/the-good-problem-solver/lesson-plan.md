# Lesson plan — The Good Problem Solver

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Thinking Like a Citizen · **Lesson:** The Good Problem Solver
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 21 — Thinking Like a Citizen (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** good problem solvers share six characteristics: they define clearly, stay open-minded yet sceptical, hunt the opportunity inside the problem, treat problems as challenges to learn from, prevent more than they intervene, and keep reasonable expectations.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the citizenship levels before it — the learner can already think critically; this band turns that thinking onto problems themselves, as a citizen's working skill.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the six characteristics.
2. Explain 'prevention more than intervention'.
3. Measure yourself honestly against the portrait.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks open-minded means believing everything → the mark pairs the two: open-minded *yet logically sceptical*.
- Thinks good solvers are just fast interveners → mark five reverses it: prevention over intervention.
