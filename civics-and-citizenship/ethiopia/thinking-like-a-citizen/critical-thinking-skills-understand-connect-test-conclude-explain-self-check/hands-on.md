# Hands-on — Critical thinking skills — understand, connect, test, conclude, explain, self-check

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
Six tools on a workbench, each with a label. A thinker picks them up one at a time to work on a single claim. Before reading, guess the tools: what must a mind be able to DO with an idea before it may trust it?

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — six trainable skills make critical thinking: comprehending meaning, identifying relationships, assessing credibility, drawing conclusions, explanation, and self-regulation — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
