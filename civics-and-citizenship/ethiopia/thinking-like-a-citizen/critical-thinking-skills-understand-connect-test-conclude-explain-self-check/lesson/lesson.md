# Critical thinking skills — understand, connect, test, conclude, explain, self-check

## Do it first

Six tools on a workbench, each with a label. A thinker picks them up one at a time to work on a single claim. Before reading, guess the tools: what must a mind be able to DO with an idea before it may trust it?

## Six trainable cognitive acts

**One — comprehending and expressing meaning:** understanding the meaning of experiences, events, judgements and beliefs, and expressing it. **Two — identifying relationships:** seeing the intended and actual inferential links between statements, reasons and opinions. **Three — assessing credibility:** judging the truth of accounts of perception, experience and belief, and the logical strength of the inferences drawn.

**Four — drawing reasonable conclusions:** securing the elements, speculating responsibly and tracing consequences. **Five — explanation:** stating one's reasoning and justifying it on evidence, concepts, method and context, as a cogent argument. **Six — self-regulation:** monitoring one's own thinking, applying analysis and evaluation to one's own judgements, to confirm or correct them.

These are trainable — practised, not bestowed. The guide's evidence set names five (analysis, evaluation, inference, explanation, self-regulation) and practises them on its Marshet and Mohammed broken-classroom case — the same pair you will meet again in the next lesson.
