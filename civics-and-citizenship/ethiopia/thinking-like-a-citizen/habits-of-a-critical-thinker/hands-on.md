# Hands-on — Habits of a critical thinker

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
A broken classroom: overcrowded, dirty, desks snapped. Two pupils refuse to call it normal. Across the country, a teacher nods at three contradictory headlines in one morning and asks no questions. Before reading: which of the two scenes is a habit at work — and which is a habit missing?

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — skills must harden into habits: evidence used impartially, judgement suspended without it, ignorance admitted, curiosity kept, and one's own views questioned — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
