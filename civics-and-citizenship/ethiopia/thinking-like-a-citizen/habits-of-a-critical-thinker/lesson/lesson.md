# Habits of a critical thinker

## Do it first

A broken classroom: overcrowded, dirty, desks snapped. Two pupils refuse to call it normal. Across the country, a teacher nods at three contradictory headlines in one morning and asks no questions. Before reading: which of the two scenes is a habit at work — and which is a habit missing?

## Sub lesson 1 — The habits

The book's list of habits: using **evidence skilfully and impartially**; keeping thought **organised and articulated**; **suspending judgement** without sufficient evidence; **admitting ignorance**; keeping **curiosity** alive; judging by **evidence, not preference**; **questioning one's own views**; recognising one's **bias and fallibility**; seeking and offering **clear reasons**; staying **informed and open-minded**; and **seeking the truth**.

[Picture 28762 — a broken classroom measured and questioned by two pupils, a habits checklist]

A skill used once is an exercise. The same skill used unthinkingly, daily, is a habit — and habits are what a thinker actually is.

## Sub lesson 2 — Two classrooms

The proving pair, both in summary. **Marshet and Mohammed** refused their overcrowded, filthy, broken classroom as "normal": they escalated questions until committees and a fundraising campaign rebuilt it into a model school. The habits, worked in public, changed a building.

[Picture 28763 — a man nodding at three contradictory headlines, question marks unasked]

The counter-portrait: teacher **Obong** in Gambela — certain he knows more than he does, impatient with complexity, judging by first impressions, deaf to others, ruled by emotion. Every habit on the list, missing in one person. The two classrooms are the same lesson read from opposite ends.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson's one thing to your companion in your own words: skills must harden into habits: evidence used impartially, judgement suspended without it, ignorance admitted, curiosity kept, and one's own views questioned. Use the lesson's own case or example, not the book's sentences. Your companion asks one hard question; answer it from the lesson, or say honestly what you would check.
