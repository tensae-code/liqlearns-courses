# Lesson plan — Habits of a critical thinker

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Thinking Like a Citizen · **Lesson:** Habits of a critical thinker
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 21 — Thinking Like a Citizen (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** skills must harden into habits: evidence used impartially, judgement suspended without it, ignorance admitted, curiosity kept, and one's own views questioned.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the levels before it in this course's path order (recommended, never forced); inside its own title it stands on the lessons before it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
1. **The habits** — The book's list of habits: using **evidence skilfully and impartially**; keeping thought **organised and articulated**; **suspending judgement** without sufficient evidence; **admitting ignorance**; keeping **curiosity** alive; judging by **evidence, not preference**; **questioning one's own views**; recognising one's **bias and fallibility**; seeking and offering **clear reasons**; staying **informed and open-minded**; and **seeking the truth**.
2. **Two classrooms** — The proving pair, both in summary.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Critical thinking skills — understand, connect, test, conclude, explain, self-check. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. List the habits.
2. Retell the two classrooms in summary.
3. Name the counter-portrait's failures.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — A broken classroom: overcrowded, dirty, desks snapped. Two pupils refuse to call it normal. Across the country, a teacher nods at three contradictory headlines in one morning and asks no questions. Before reading: which of the two scenes is a habit at work — and which is a habit missing?
2. **The explanation** — the lesson's parts in order (lesson/lesson.md).
