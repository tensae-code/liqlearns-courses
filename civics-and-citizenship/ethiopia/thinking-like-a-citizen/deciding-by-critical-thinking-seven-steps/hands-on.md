# Hands-on — Deciding by critical thinking — seven steps

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
A principal's desk. Report files. Warning letters. A scale for weighing them. An unsigned decision stamp. Before reading: two pupils face dismissal. What must happen on that desk before the stamp may fall — and what would make the decision a critical one rather than an angry one?

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — decisions are made critically in seven steps: identify the decision, gather information, identify alternatives, weigh the evidence, choose, act, and review — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
