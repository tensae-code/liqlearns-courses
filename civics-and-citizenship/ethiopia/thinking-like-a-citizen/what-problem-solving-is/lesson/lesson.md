# What Problem-Solving Is

## Do it first

Your bag — phone and money inside — is on a bus rolling away in a strange city, and the relatives you came to visit cannot be called. Stand in that bus stop for a moment. What exactly is 'the problem' here — one problem, or several wearing one coat? The lesson begins with your answer.

## The gap and the bridge

A **problem** is an unpleasant situation that prevents people from achieving what they want. **Problem-solving** is the act of **defining** the problem, **determining its cause**, **identifying and selecting alternatives**, and **implementing** the solution — a process, using steps. 

[Picture 28871 — A traveller at a bus stop, thought-bubbles of the four parts]



Defining the problem properly is stressed as the **critical start**, and for a hard reason: problems differ in difficulty, and some exceed one's capacity alone. A problem wrongly defined gets solved wrongly — brilliantly, expensively, and to no effect. 

[Picture 28872 — A gap between two cliffs, a bridge half-built (teacher guide — evidence)]



Now a distinction citizens blur daily. **Decision-making** is choosing the right option among alternatives. **Problem-solving** is finding answers to complicated issues — and tactful solving **contains deciding inside it**: you will decide, more than once, on the way through a problem, but deciding is a room inside the house, not the house.

Return to the bus stop with the definitions loaded. The bag is gone — that is the situation. The problem, defined properly, might be: *I cannot reach my hosts or pay for help in a city I do not know.* Different definition, different alternatives, different first step. That is why the defining comes first, and why it is a skill and not a talent.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion both definitions — then define the bus-stop problem properly, in one sentence, and say what changed when you did.

## Do It Yourself

1. Take one live problem of yours. Write it twice: the blurry version you first say, and the properly defined version. Compare what each invites you to do.
2. Catch one decision this week that was really a room inside a bigger problem. Name the house it belonged to.
3. Ask someone older: tell me about a problem that turned out to be wrongly defined. Write their story in five lines.
