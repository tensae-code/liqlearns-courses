# Lesson plan — Problem-Solving in Practice — Mending a Friendship

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Thinking Like a Citizen · **Lesson:** Problem-Solving in Practice — Mending a Friendship
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 21 — Thinking Like a Citizen (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a case with no model answer: two close friends fall out over a borrowed reference book one denies holding — and you, friend to both, must act to restore the friendship.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the citizenship levels before it — the learner can already think critically; this band turns that thinking onto problems themselves, as a citizen's working skill.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the case exactly as given.
2. Apply the four parts to the case, in order.
3. Name the specific methods you would use — and defend them.

## If the learner is stuck
- Demands the official answer → there is none, by design; the skill is building and defending a solution, and that is what is practised.
- Starts by deciding who is lying → the case gives no verdict; defining and cause-finding come before any judgement.
