# Critical thinking skills — analysis

## Do it first

Two advice slips under a magnifier. One says: respect everyone. The other says: respect only your family. Before reading, examine both: which survives examination, and can you say exactly why?

## Examine it from every side

**Analysis** is examining something closely to judge its worth.

[Picture 28745 — a magnifier over two advice slips, one ticked, one crossed]

[Picture 28746 — a football team in a huddle over a tactics board]

The two-advice test works it. *Respect everyone* survives examination — it holds in every case you test it against. *Respect only your family* fails — because it excludes, and the excluded cases break it.

The second case is action, not advice. Two pupils fight; classmates watch approvingly; others urge peaceful settlement. Analysing the **consequences** — injury, disrupted learning — shows that helping them stop and talk is the sound decision. And analysis is not finished until the analyst can **explain why** to the class.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson's one thing to your companion in your own words: analysis is examining something closely from every side to judge its worth — and being able to explain the judgement. Use the lesson's own case or example, not the book's sentences. Your companion asks one hard question; answer it from the lesson, or say honestly what you would check.

## Do It Yourself

1. Practise this until you can do it without looking: define analysis.
2. Practise this until you can do it without looking: work the two-advice test.
3. Practise this until you can do it without looking: apply analysis to the fighting-pupils case.
4. Write the lesson's one thing in one sentence of your own: analysis is examining something closely from every side to judge its worth — and being able to explain the judgement. Keep the sentence; it is your proof you were here.
