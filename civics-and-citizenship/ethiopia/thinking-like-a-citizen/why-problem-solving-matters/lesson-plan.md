# Lesson plan — Why Problem-Solving Matters

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Thinking Like a Citizen · **Lesson:** Why Problem-Solving Matters
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 21 — Thinking Like a Citizen (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** problem-solving skill buys six gains — good decisions, brainstorming, self-awareness and creativity, an analytical mind, emotional intelligence, and power over complex challenges — and it lives in everyone, trained or not.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the citizenship levels before it — the learner can already think critically; this band turns that thinking onto problems themselves, as a citizen's working skill.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the six gains.
2. Describe the skilled person's cycle in order.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the skill is a rare talent → it is a soft skill everyone holds in some degree; training deepens what life already planted.
- Stops the cycle at 'apply' → the cycle ends at receiving and responding to the response; that listening is a beat, not an extra.
