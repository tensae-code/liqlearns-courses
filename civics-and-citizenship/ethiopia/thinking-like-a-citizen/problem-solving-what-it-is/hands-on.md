# Hands-on — Problem solving — what it is

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
Two close friends are fighting, and your shared study circle has collapsed. Your own results are falling with it. Before reading: what exactly is the problem — the fight, the circle, or your results? Problem solvers earn their name on questions like that.

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — problem solving is overcoming the barriers between you and a goal — applying old knowledge to a new situation when the solution is not obvious — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
