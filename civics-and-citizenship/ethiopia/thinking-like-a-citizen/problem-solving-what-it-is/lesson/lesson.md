# Problem solving — what it is

## Do it first

Two close friends are fighting, and your shared study circle has collapsed. Your own results are falling with it. Before reading: what exactly is the problem — the fight, the circle, or your results? Problem solvers earn their name on questions like that.

## Sub lesson 1 — Clear the obstacle

**Problem solving** is overcoming the barriers standing between you and success — needed daily, at school and beyond.

[Picture 28751 — two pupils back to back, a third drawing a bridge of steps]

The worked situation runs at friend scale. Two close friends fight; the shared study circle collapses; your own results suffer. The method given: **understand first** — listen to both fully; **stay objective** — you are their mutual friend, not a partisan; **facilitate**; and **propose a settlement both can accept**. Obstacle named, obstacle cleared, goal recovered.

## Sub lesson 2 — Old knowledge meeting a new situation

The deeper telling defines it formally: reaching a goal when the solution is **not obvious** — applying previously acquired knowledge, skills and understanding to a **new situation**, through gathering information, creating new knowledge and deciding on it.

[Picture 28752 — a dry field turning panel by panel to trees and green grass]

[Picture 28753 — a dry hillside before and after: seedlings in rows, grass returning]

[GIF 28754 — a dry field greening across a year]

Its case, in summary, runs at community scale. During drought — grazing gone, water scarce, trees felled — two elected farmers, **Hotesa and Zeberga**, convene community figures every three days. Four remedies are proposed: community tree-planting, drought-resistant grass, resting the grazing land, reducing cattle. The first two are chosen and finished **within a month** by public effort — and **within a year**, water, grass, cattle health and harvests recover.

The formal six-step method built on this same case is the next lesson. Here, only see the shape: a goal, a barrier, old knowledge meeting a new situation.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson's one thing to your companion in your own words: problem solving is overcoming the barriers between you and a goal — applying old knowledge to a new situation when the solution is not obvious. Use the lesson's own case or example, not the book's sentences. Your companion asks one hard question; answer it from the lesson, or say honestly what you would check.
