# Lesson plan — Problem solving — what it is

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Thinking Like a Citizen · **Lesson:** Problem solving — what it is
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 21 — Thinking Like a Citizen (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** problem solving is overcoming the barriers between you and a goal — applying old knowledge to a new situation when the solution is not obvious.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the levels before it in this course's path order (recommended, never forced); inside its own title it stands on the lessons before it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
1. **Clear the obstacle** — **Problem solving** is overcoming the barriers standing between you and success — needed daily, at school and beyond.
2. **Old knowledge meeting a new situation** — The deeper telling defines it formally: reaching a goal when the solution is **not obvious** — applying previously acquired knowledge, skills and understanding to a **new situation**, through gathering information, creating new knowledge and deciding on it.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Critical thinking skills — creativity. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define problem solving at friend scale and community scale.
2. Work the two-friends method.
3. Retell the drought case in summary.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Two close friends are fighting, and your shared study circle has collapsed. Your own results are falling with it. Before reading: what exactly is the problem — the fight, the circle, or your results? Problem solvers earn their name on questions like that.
2. **The explanation** — the lesson's parts in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 28751]: a picture of two pupils back to back and a third drawing a bridge of steps between them: listen, understand, propose — the student walks the bridge in order.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 28752]: a picture of a dry grazing field turning, panel by panel, to planted trees and green grass with healthy cattle — the student reads the panels as a year passing.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 28753]: a picture of a dry hillside before and after: seedlings planted in rows, grass returning — the student names the two chosen remedies from the picture.
   - ADD GIF [GIF 28754]: a GIF of a dry field greening across a year — the student watches the loop and names the two remedies whose work it shows.
   - ADD AUDIO: none named in the placements for this lesson — no audio slot is built.
3. **Say it to your companion** — explain the one thing in your own words, with the lesson's own case (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, matching, fill-in-the-blank — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the closing doing block (lesson/lesson.md).
