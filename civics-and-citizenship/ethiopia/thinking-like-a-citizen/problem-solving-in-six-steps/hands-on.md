# Hands-on — Problem solving in six steps

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
Six footprints cross a cracked field to a water trough. Each footprint has a word on it. Before reading, arrange your own guess of the six words: what must come first — choosing a solution, or knowing what the problem actually is?

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — the six steps: define the problem, find its root cause, develop alternatives, select the best fit, implement it, evaluate the outcome — in order, none skipped — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
