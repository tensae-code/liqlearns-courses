# Problem solving in six steps

## Do it first

Six footprints cross a cracked field to a water trough. Each footprint has a word on it. Before reading, arrange your own guess of the six words: what must come first — choosing a solution, or knowing what the problem actually is?

## One method, practised whole

The six steps, formalised on the farmers' case from the previous lesson.

[Picture 28756 — six stepping stones across a stream, each labelled with a step]

[GIF 28757 — a figure stepping stone to stone across the stream]

**1. Define the problem** — context, background, symptoms. **2. Determine the root cause** — by public discussion, not by symptom-chasing. **3. Develop alternative solutions** — weighing each one's benefits and drawbacks. **4. Select the solution that best fits** — more benefits than drawbacks. **5. Implement it** — action, not sitting and thinking. **6. Evaluate the outcome** — monitor whether the fix worked, and improve it.

The book's own warning rides with the method: steps are **not to be skipped or jumped**. The sequence removes bias, steadies decisions and justifies the result. The drought case fits it exactly — symptom (dead grazing) separated from cause, four alternatives weighed, two selected, work done within a month, recovery evaluated within the year.
