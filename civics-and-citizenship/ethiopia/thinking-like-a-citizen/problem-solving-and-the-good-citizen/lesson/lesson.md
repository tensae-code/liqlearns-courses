# Problem-Solving and the Good Citizen

## Do it first

The band closes by folding the skill back into the citizen. Ask it plainly of yourself: what has your community gained, this year, from your solving — and what has it lost from your unsolved, avoided, or badly solved problems? The lesson will now name the citizen it has been building.

## The citizen the skill builds

The binding claim stands first: solving problems **with available information, using soft social skills**, is what makes a **competent, responsible citizen** — and a good problem solver must carry a good citizen's attributes. Two are developed. 

[Picture 28881 — Two former disputants shaking hands over a shared desk]



**Conflict-resolution skill.** Conflict in relationships is inevitable; healthy handling turns it into growth that strengthens bonds. Its marks: recognising and responding to **what matters** to the parties; readiness to **forgive and forget**; seeking **compromise** and avoiding punishment; and **believing resolution can serve both parties' interests and needs** — all four, held together, against the winner-takes-all instinct.

**Critical-thinking skill.** The rational, clear analysis whose holder is **open-minded and logical**, **willing to reassess information**, and considers **a wide range of perspectives** before concluding. The citizen does not merely hold opinions about public problems; the citizen can *work* them.

That closes the band's circle: the character levels taught what a citizen is; this band taught what a citizen can *do* — define, gather, decide, implement, mend friendships, resolve conflicts, and think. Competence, practised, becomes character.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the binding claim — then the four marks of healthy conflict handling, without the book.

## Do It Yourself

1. Take one live conflict within your reach. Write its handling plan using all four marks, one line each.
2. Reassess one public opinion you hold: write the strongest perspective against it, fairly, before you re-conclude.
3. Write your citizen's account from Do it first in full: one gain your solving gave your community, one loss your avoidance cost it. Keep both.
