# Hands-on — Why critical thinking matters

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
Two paths fork. Down one, a cheat sheet; at its end, a clinic where a nurse who qualified by cheating treats patients. Down the other, an evidence file; at its end, a ballot box. Before reading: which path did each traveller's thinking choose for them?

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — decisions built on false information go wrong — critical thinking protects patients, voters and whole communities — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
