# Hands-on — Critical thinking in problem solving — evidence before omens

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
A woman sets out with a water pot on an urgent journey. A bird sings — she turns back. She sets out again, stumbles — turns back. A passer-by in a black coat — turns back. The water never gets fetched. Before reading: what is she reading, and what should she be reading instead?

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — critical thinking is what makes problem solving more than luck — and it refuses to read danger into birdsong, stumbles and strangers' coats — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
