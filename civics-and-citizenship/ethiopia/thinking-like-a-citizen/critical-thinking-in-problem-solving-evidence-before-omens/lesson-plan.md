# Lesson plan — Critical thinking in problem solving — evidence before omens

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Thinking Like a Citizen · **Lesson:** Critical thinking in problem solving — evidence before omens
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 21 — Thinking Like a Citizen (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** critical thinking is what makes problem solving more than luck — and it refuses to read danger into birdsong, stumbles and strangers' coats.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the levels before it in this course's path order (recommended, never forced); inside its own title it stands on the lessons before it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
1. **Reason over omen** — **Critical thinking is what makes problem solving more than luck**: better definitions of the problem, better options, better decisions.
2. **Story: Zerfe and the omens** — The story, in summary.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Getting better at critical thinking. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State what critical thinking adds to problem solving.
2. Retell the Zerfe story in summary.
3. List the gains of critical thinking.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — A woman sets out with a water pot on an urgent journey. A bird sings — she turns back. She sets out again, stumbles — turns back. A passer-by in a black coat — turns back. The water never gets fetched. Before reading: what is she reading, and what should she be reading instead?
2. **The explanation** — the lesson's parts in order (lesson/lesson.md).
