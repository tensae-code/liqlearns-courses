# Critical thinking — analyse, triangulate, decide

## Do it first

A claim lands on your phone: sensational, shocking, shareable. Before reading, list every way you could check it before passing it on. Count your ways. This lesson's method is called triangulation — and most people use none of them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Check before you believe

**Critical thinking** is analysing information objectively and reaching a reasoned decision. Its working habit is **triangulation**: checking a claim across several sources — friends, teachers, radio, press, books, film, social media — because not all information is equal.

[Picture 28741 — one claim on a phone, checked against book, teacher and newspaper]

And learn its boundary: critical thinking is **not** collecting facts or having a good memory. It is drawing consequences, solving problems and finding relevant sources.

The classroom debate carries the method: one group defends colonialism for its roads and bridges; the other shows the roads served exploitation, rights were denied and peoples oppressed. The second argument persuades **on evidence** — and Ethiopia's never-colonised history closes the case.

## Sub lesson 2 — Case: Obang and Facebook

The counter-portrait, in summary. **Obang**, a university student, takes everything on Facebook as true and important, never checking elsewhere — amid fake accounts and fake news, he is the book's portrait of the **non-critical thinker**.

[Picture 28742 — a phone with a sensational headline under a magnifying glass]

Notice the failure is not ignorance. Obang has information in flood. What he lacks is the check: one source, believed whole, passed on whole. Triangulation is the whole difference between Obang and a critical thinker.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson's one thing to your companion in your own words: critical thinking is analysing information objectively and reaching a reasoned decision — checking a claim across several sources before believing it. Use the lesson's own case or example, not the book's sentences. Your companion asks one hard question; answer it from the lesson, or say honestly what you would check.
