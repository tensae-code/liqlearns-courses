# Hands-on — Critical thinking — analyse, triangulate, decide

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
A claim lands on your phone: sensational, shocking, shareable. Before reading, list every way you could check it before passing it on. Count your ways. This lesson's method is called triangulation — and most people use none of them.

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — critical thinking is analysing information objectively and reaching a reasoned decision — checking a claim across several sources before believing it — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
