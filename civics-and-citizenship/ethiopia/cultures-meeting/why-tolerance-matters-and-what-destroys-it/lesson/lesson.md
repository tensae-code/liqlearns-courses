# Why Tolerance Matters and What Destroys It

## Do it first

Picture the shared table again, all places set. Now watch one place being pushed away from the table, slowly. Who loses? (Not only the pushed one — think before you answer.) Keep asking that question through both sub lessons.

## Sub lesson 1 — Why tolerance matters

A tolerant culture matters because it is the condition in which different people can actually live one shared life: learning together, working together, settling troubles with words. Where tolerance stands, difference becomes wealth — many languages, many skills, many views. Where it falls, the same differences become fuel for conflict. Tolerance is therefore not decoration on a community; it is load-bearing.

## Sub lesson 2 — What destroys tolerance

Its enemies are named: discrimination — treating people unequally for their difference; exclusion — shutting people out; blind hatred — hatred that refuses to see the person; feelings of superiority — one group counting itself above others; and division — the splitting of the community into camps. The picture shows the first two at one table: a place set for everyone, and one place being pushed away. Learn the five enemies by name; a named enemy can be refused.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion why tolerance matters, then name its five enemies one by one and say what each one does.

## Do It Yourself

1. Refuse one enemy this week in your own conduct — no exclusion at your table.
2. Include one person who is usually left out of a game, group or table.
3. Write the five enemies from memory and check your list.
