# Lesson plan — Why Tolerance Matters and What Destroys It

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Tolerance and Cultures Meeting · **Lesson:** Why Tolerance Matters and What Destroys It
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 12 — Tolerance and Cultures Meeting (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A tolerant culture matters because it is the condition in which different people can actually live one shared life: learning together, working together, settling troubles with words.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the lessons before it in Level 12 — Tolerance and Cultures Meeting — and on the learner's own daily life at home, at school and in the community. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Why tolerance matters** — A tolerant culture matters because it is the condition in which different people can actually live one shared life: learning together, working together, settling troubles with words.
2. **What destroys tolerance** — Its enemies are named: discrimination — treating people unequally for their difference; exclusion — shutting people out; blind hatred — hatred that refuses to see the person; feelings of superiority — one group counting itself above others; and division — the splitting of the community into camps.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say and show what “Why tolerance matters” teaches, in their own words, and do the practice named for it.
2. Say and show what “What destroys tolerance” teaches, in their own words, and do the practice named for it.
3. Carry the lesson's practice into the week: keeping every place at the table — refusing exclusion in your own conduct.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — every activity uses the learner's own home, school and street.

1. **Do it first** — Picture the shared table again, all places set. Now watch one place being pushed away from the table, slowly. Who loses? (Not only the pushed one — think before you answer.) Keep asking that question through both sub lessons.

## If the learner is stuck
- Cannot say what the lesson teaches → go back to the Do it first and the picture; name what the picture shows, in one sentence, then read the first part again slowly.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → hold the one thing: A tolerant culture matters because it is the condition in which different people can actually live one shared life: learning together, working together, settling troubles with words.
- Finds the practice hard → shrink it to the smallest real version and do that today; small and done beats grand and promised.
