# Signs That Cultures Are Truly Linked

## Do it first

You are a tester now. A visitor claims two cultures are 'linked'. You carry a checklist of three signposts. Before reading, guess what evidence you would demand — then check your guesses against the lesson's three signs.

## The three signs

How can you tell linkage is really happening? By three signs: effective communication — people across cultures actually understand each other, not just exchange noise; intercultural linkage in practice — exchange happening in real life, in markets, schools and celebrations, not only in speeches; and active communication behaviour — people taking part, initiating, responding, joining in. Where all three stand, linkage is real.

## The chapter's test of itself

This lesson is the chapter testing itself. Definitions can be memorised and festivals praised while nothing actually links. The signs give you the examiner's questions: Do they understand each other? Does exchange happen in practice? Do people actively join in? Ask them of your own school and neighbourhood — and let the answers guide what you build next.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the three signs of true linkage, and apply them once: pick one place you know and judge it against all three.

## Do It Yourself

1. Test your school against the three signposts this week — write one honest line per sign.
2. Practise active communication behaviour: start one cross-culture conversation yourself.
3. Where one sign is missing around you, do one act that supplies it.
