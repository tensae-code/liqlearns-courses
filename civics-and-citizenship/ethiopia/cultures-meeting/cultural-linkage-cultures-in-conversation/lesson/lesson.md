# Cultural Linkage — Cultures in Conversation

## Do it first

Picture two festival crowds on a bridge, walking toward each other with food and instruments. Half the lesson is whether they pass in silence or stop and exchange. Watch what your picture does — then read the definition.

## What cultural linkage means

Cultural linkage is the connection and exchange between cultures: people of different cultures in real communication, not merely side by side. Side-by-side is geography; linkage is conversation. Food crosses, music crosses, words and ideas cross — and both sides are changed a little by the crossing, and enriched.

## From room to exchange

Linkage is accommodation become active. Making room lets another culture sit beside you; linkage talks with it, trades with it, celebrates with it. This lesson is the bridge between the tolerance lessons and the chapter's close: room, then conversation, then the signs that the conversation is real.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what cultural linkage is, and the sentence that separates it from mere side-by-side living.

## Do It Yourself

1. Exchange one thing across cultures this week: a food, a song, a word, a story — given and received.
2. Ask one real question of someone from another culture and listen to the whole answer.
3. Learn how to say 'thank you' in a language new to you, and use it.
