# Cultures Meeting — How Contact Grew

## Do it first

Mime it: first write a slow letter and wait weeks for its reply. Then lift a phone. Then tap a screen and get an answer in seconds. Your own lifetime holds that whole acceleration. This chapter opens by noticing it.

## Contact in your own lifetime

Cultural linkage is widening in pupils' own lifetimes: phone, fax, email and the internet have made contact between cultures constant. What was once a rare traveller's tale is now a daily message. Cultures that never met now meet every day — in pockets, on screens, in markets and classrooms.

## Noticing comes first

The chapter opens by noticing: before defining linkage or judging it, see how much contact has grown. A pupil who notices the growth understands the rest of the chapter as their own world's description, not as faraway theory.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the line of tools the lesson gives, in order, and how each one changed contact between cultures.

## Do It Yourself

1. Ask an elder how a message travelled when they were your age; compare it with today.
2. Use one tool this week to learn one true thing about a culture not your own.
3. Count today's cross-culture contacts — messages, songs, foods, words — and be surprised honestly.
