# Tolerance — What It Is and What It Is Made Of

## Do it first

Count the five scenes in the picture on your fingers: two languages speaking, a vote, a court, two places of worship, a shared meal. In each scene ask one question: is everyone treated equally here? That question, asked five ways, is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Tolerance — the concept

A culture of tolerance is a community's settled way of letting different people live fully alongside each other — different languages, beliefs, customs and views, without any group being crushed for its difference. Tolerance is a culture, not a mood: it is practised by a whole community, in ordinary days, not only announced on special ones.

## Sub lesson 2 — What tolerance is made of

What is tolerance made of? Equality, practised across the fields the guide names: language, politics, law, religion and social life. That is the deep definition — tolerance is not mere patience while feeling superior inside. Tolerance is equal treatment, lived across every field where people meet.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what a culture of tolerance is. Then name the five fields where equality must be practised, and explain why tolerance is more than mere patience.

## Do It Yourself

1. In one field today — language, or social life — practise deliberate equal treatment.
2. Share one meal or game this week with someone different from you in language, belief or custom.
3. Catch one superior-feeling thought this week and answer it with the equality definition.
