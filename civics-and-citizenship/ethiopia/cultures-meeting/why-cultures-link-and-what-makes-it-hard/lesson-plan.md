# Lesson plan — Why Cultures Link and What Makes It Hard

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Tolerance and Cultures Meeting · **Lesson:** Why Cultures Link and What Makes It Hard
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 12 — Tolerance and Cultures Meeting (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Cultural linkage matters because exchange enriches: each culture gains what the other carries — knowledge, craft, food, music, friendship — and understanding grows where contact is real.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the lessons before it in Level 12 — Tolerance and Cultures Meeting — and on the learner's own daily life at home, at school and in the community. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **“a picture of …” in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Why linkage matters** — Cultural linkage matters because exchange enriches: each culture gains what the other carries — knowledge, craft, food, music, friendship — and understanding grows where contact is real.
2. **What makes linkage hard** — But linkage has difficulties inside it, and the lesson names the second half honestly: difference can be met with suspicion instead of curiosity, contact can be unequal, and old habits of standing apart pull against new bridges.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say and show what “Why linkage matters” teaches, in their own words, and do the practice named for it.
2. Say and show what “What makes linkage hard” teaches, in their own words, and do the practice named for it.
3. Carry the lesson's practice into the week: meeting difference with curiosity instead of suspicion.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — every activity uses the learner's own home, school and street.

## If the learner is stuck
- Cannot say what the lesson teaches → go back to the Do it first and the picture; name what the picture shows, in one sentence, then read the first part again slowly.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → hold the one thing: Cultural linkage matters because exchange enriches: each culture gains what the other carries — knowledge, craft, food, music, friendship — and understanding grows where contact is real.
- Finds the practice hard → shrink it to the smallest real version and do that today; small and done beats grand and promised.
