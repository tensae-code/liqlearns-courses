# Making Room for Other Cultures

## Do it first

Picture the schoolyard: different traditional dresses, one game, everyone playing. Nobody had to change their dress to join. Before reading, ask: what did the yard have to do — and not do — for that game to happen?

## Accommodating cultural diversity

To accommodate cultural diversity is to make room for other cultures: receiving other people's languages, customs and celebrations as part of the community's wealth rather than as intrusions. The word matters — room. A guest culture is not squeezed into a corner or merely endured at the edge; space is genuinely made, the way you move along a bench to make a real seat.

## Room, practised

Making room shows in ordinary welcomes: greeting a neighbour in their language, honouring a celebration that is not your own, learning a custom instead of mocking it. Each act says the same sentence: there is room for you here — not despite your difference, but with it counted as wealth.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what accommodating cultural diversity means, using the bench picture: moving along to make a real seat. Give two ordinary welcomes that make room.

## Do It Yourself

1. Greet one person this week in their own language, as well as you can.
2. Honour one celebration or custom that is not your own — attend, help, or ask about it respectfully.
3. Never mock a custom this week; ask one question about it instead.
