# Watching the government — participation every day, and the 2008 campaign case

## Do it first

Democracy is not a one-day event with a ballot paper. Jefferson said the citizen participates 'not merely at an election one day in the year but everyday'. What would everyday participation look like in your town — and what stops it?

## The teaching

Participation is democracy's daily work. Jefferson's 1816 words set the standard: the citizen as participator 'not merely at an election one day in the year but everyday'. The enemies are named plainly: apathy, where everything seems fine; fear, in one-party states; and simple ignorance of how to begin — Ethiopia's own risk as a new democracy. The instruments are demonstrations (cross-referred to Unit 1, Lesson 3), framed public agendas on common interests, and election campaigns watched beyond their speeches.

[Picture 29041 — citizens at a public budget hearing]

The case is Barack Obama's 2008 presidential primary campaign — treated in this book's present tense, his early office watched for the change his speeches promised. The era tense is itself part of the evidence: the book was written in 2008/09, and the lesson keeps that date honestly. The teaching point survives the date: citizens should expect, and check, after the speeches end.
