# Ethiopia in the world — League of Nations, OAU to AU, and its foreign policy

## Do it first

Ethiopia hosts the headquarters of the African Union. Why Addis Ababa? Write your guess — then read how a country that was itself invaded while the world watched became the founder and host of Africa's own organisations.

## The teaching

Ethiopia is a founder, twice over. It joined the League of Nations in 1923, admitted against British opposition — and then learned the League's weakness: when Italy invaded, the League failed to act despite its own covenant. The lesson Africa drew became the Organisation of African Unity: Haile Selassie helped engineer its 1963 charter, and Addis Ababa won the seat. In 2000 the African Union replaced the OAU, with a new right under Article 4 to intervene in a member state in cases of war crimes, genocide and crimes against humanity — the answer to 1936, written into law.

[Picture 29005 — the AU building in Addis Ababa and a 1923 League card]

Addis Ababa also hosts the UN Economic Commission for Africa (since 1958) and joined in founding IGAD (1996) for the region. Ethiopia's foreign policy sets out from three embarking points: development and building a democratic system at home; national honour; and globalisation accepted as the century's reality — not resisted, but engaged.
