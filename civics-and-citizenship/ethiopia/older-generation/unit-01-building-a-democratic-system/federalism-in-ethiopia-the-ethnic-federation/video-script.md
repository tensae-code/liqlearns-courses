One lesson from the older course.

Ethiopia's answer after 1991 is ethnic federalism. The scholar Turton judged the transformation 'radical and pioneering': self-determination for federated units inside what had been a centralised state, with ethnicity as the organising principle taken further than anywhere else in Africa. Merera Gudina's history traces the national question behind it — from Tewodros II, through the student struggle of the 1960s and the Derg years, to the victory of the ethnic-based movements in 1991.
