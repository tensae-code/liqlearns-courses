# Features of democracy — transparency and accountability

## Do it first

Think of your school. Could a student find out how the school's money is spent? Who would you ask, and would they have to answer? That pair of questions — can you see it, and must they answer — is this whole lesson.

## The teaching

Two working features make a democracy real between elections: **transparency** — decisions and money are visible to the public — and **accountability** — officials must explain and answer for what they did. The 'transparent officials' case shows an organisation that exposed corrupt officials through posters and telephone hotlines, and earned respect because its delivery satisfied its users. The lesson's activity turns the lens home: audit your own school — what is posted, what is hidden, who answers questions?

The readings survey African democracy's rough road. Uganda's Movement system set multi-party competition aside. Botswana's traditional public meetings are a real democratic habit, yet carry a gender risk — who gets heard in the meeting? Kenya under Kenyatta and Moi shows the cost of the missing features: corruption, assassinations, and the rigged election of 1988. Where citizens cannot see and cannot question, power decays.
