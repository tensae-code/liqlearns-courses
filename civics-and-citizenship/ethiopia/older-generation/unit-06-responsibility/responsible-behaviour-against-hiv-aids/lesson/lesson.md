# Responsible behaviour against HIV/AIDS — orphans, women and the Dawn of Hope

## Do it first

An epidemic is not only a medical fact; it is a test of responsibility — to the sick, to orphans, to women blamed first. Read the ledger this lesson keeps, then meet the people who answered it by organising.

## The teaching

The epidemic's human ledger (UNESCO 2004, as the guide keys it): over 18 million AIDS orphans projected in sub-Saharan Africa by 2010; widows disinherited and made homeless; affected children leaving school — in Addis Ababa, over three-quarters of interviewed child domestic labourers were orphaned, 80% unable to leave their jobs, many working over 11 hours a day, 7 days a week. Women are first tested and first blamed, burdened as caregivers and driven to the street. The 2004 strategy for orphans and vulnerable children is the policy answer summarised in the lesson.

[Picture 29028 — an association meeting, a banner of hope]

The human answer is the case study: Berehane Kelkay and the Dawn of Hope — an association founded by eleven HIV-positive people, grown to over 10,000 members with branches across Ethiopia including Awassa — demanding that the rights of people living with HIV be respected. Responsible behaviour means prevention, care, and refusing stigma.
