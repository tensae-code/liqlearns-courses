# Public property — care, cost and the school itself

## Do it first

Look at your desk. Carved names? Ink stains? That desk was bought with your parents' taxes and is meant to serve students after you. This short lesson is about carelessness as a civic failing — and a plan to end it.

## The teaching

Carelessness is a civic failing. Graffiti and dirty fingers shorten the life of desks and books that parents' taxes bought; a torn page may be an accident, but accidents can be avoided, and resources cared for 'should last for many years'. The case is the school itself — the typical public building whose users own nothing directly and pay for nothing at the desk — which is exactly why public property needs deliberate care: what belongs to everyone is looked after by no one unless citizens choose to.

[Picture 29027 — a school desk before and after]

The lesson's product is practical: learners must produce a plan to protect school property, and persuade other students to respect it. Responsibility here is not a lecture; it is a campaign.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words — the one thing, its strongest case, and the figure or date you remember best. Your companion asks one hard question; answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the lesson's one thing in two sentences of your own, using one of its named cases.
2. Find the same issue in your own school or town this week; write what the lesson's teaching would say about it.
3. Teach the lesson to someone at home in five minutes; write down the question they asked that was hardest to answer.
