# Obligations in society — participation, negotiation, compromise

## Do it first

Two farmers claim the same strip of land. Voices rise. Before anyone wins, someone must do four things in the right order — this lesson's sub lessons are those four stages. As you read, keep asking: what happens if a stage is skipped?

Political participation, negotiation and compromise are core obligations of a citizen, not hobbies. The negotiator's craft is spelled out plainly: separate the problem from the personalities; avoid premature judgement; judge by objective criteria; communicate. Ethiopia has practised this for centuries — elders settling disputes, with Sidama elders held to standards of probity, integrity, honesty and truthfulness. The craft is old; the lesson makes it deliberate.

## Sub lesson 1 — Describe the dispute clearly

Describe the dispute clearly. A Senegalese land-dispute case (from Meyer-Bisch) gives the stages their order. First, each side must be able to say what the dispute actually is — land, boundary, inheritance — before feelings rename it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Calm and defuse, and each party listens and understands

Calm and defuse, and each party listens and understands. Heat first falls; then each side hears the other's account whole.

## Sub lesson 3 — Find the basic cause, and bring mediators when direct talks fail

Find the basic cause, and bring mediators when direct talks fail. Causes sit under quarrels; when the parties cannot reach them alone, a mediator both trust — an elder in the Ethiopian pattern — is brought in.

## Sub lesson 4 — Seek solutions serving both sides

Seek solutions serving both sides. A settlement one side hates is a pause, not a peace; the craft ends in an outcome both can live with.
