# Consequences — Liberia's warning about promises and power

## Do it first

A leader promises everything. The people cheer — and stop watching. What do they own when it goes wrong? Liberia lived this story twice in ten years. Read it as a warning addressed to citizens, not only to presidents.

Sub lesson 1 — Tolbert: the fortune, the conference hall and the rice. President Tolbert of Liberia amassed a corrupt fortune and spent half the national budget on a conference hall, while his cousin's rice imports rose 50% and ordinary people queued. Troops fired on protesters; in 1980 Tolbert was murdered. Sub lesson 2 — Doe: the coup's promise, the rigged re-count and civil war. Samuel Doe's coup promised liberation and delivered a dictatorship as brutal as what it replaced — 'same taxi, different driver'. Doe amassed a $300 million fortune, and his 1985 re-election rested on a rigged re-count giving him 50.9%. Liberia descended into civil war.

The case study's moral is addressed to citizens: promises unexamined and power unwatched are also choices, and their consequences also belong, in part, to those who chose not to participate and not to stand by principles. Responsibility for consequences is this unit's theme — and it does not stop at presidents.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words — the one thing, its strongest case, and the figure or date you remember best. Your companion asks one hard question; answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the lesson's one thing in two sentences of your own, using one of its named cases.
2. Find the same issue in your own school or town this week; write what the lesson's teaching would say about it.
3. Teach the lesson to someone at home in five minutes; write down the question they asked that was hardest to answer.
