# Development — measuring it, and Ethiopia's potential

## Do it first

Is Ethiopia developing? Before you answer with a feeling, answer with numbers: how many children die before five, how many girls sit in class, how many children never enter a school at all? This lesson measures first — then audits what Ethiopia has to build with.

## The teaching

Development is measured, not felt. UNICEF's Millennium Development Goal evidence, as the guide keys it, is sobering: child mortality 'seriously off-track', though Ethiopia's under-five rate was falling about 1% a year in 2004; gender parity in education set to be missed by 54 countries in 2015; 75 million children out of primary school, 70% of them in sub-Saharan Africa; 29,000 under-fives dying daily of largely preventable causes.

[Picture 29021 — a river past a turbine, granary and school, with a gauge]

Against the ledger stands Ethiopia's potential: agricultural land and livestock, and the rivers — 'the water tower of Africa', the Abbay flowing to the Nile, with a Nile Basin framework negotiated for over ten years and hoped for signature in 2009 by all but Egypt and Sudan. And against the potential stand the reasons poverty persists: population growth, an undereducated workforce, and governance. Development is the race between the second list and the third.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words — the one thing, its strongest case, and the figure or date you remember best. Your companion asks one hard question; answer it from the lesson.
