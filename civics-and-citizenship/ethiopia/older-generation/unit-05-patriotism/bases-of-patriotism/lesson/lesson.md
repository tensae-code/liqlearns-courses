# Bases of patriotism — true history, the flag, tolerance

## Do it first

Can you love your country and still ask hard questions about its history? This lesson says patriotism stands on three bases — and the first one is not pride. It is truth. Bring your scepticism; you will need it.

## The teaching

Patriotism stands on three bases. First, **objective history**: teachers are warned that sources lie in both directions — a Haile Selassie-era schoolbook's reverent myth-making ('the best of kings') against a Cuban account praising Mengistu, written during the Red Terror. The discipline is to check references and use at least two sources. Second, **the flag**: flags carry cultural identity — waved in pride, burned in anger. Huntington's scene makes it concrete: Sarajevo, 18 April 1994, two thousand people waving Saudi and Turkish flags to tell the world who their real friends were. Third, **tolerance**: Locke's 1689 *Letter Concerning Toleration* argued that belief cannot be coerced, that the fallible find truth only when free, and that persecution, not diversity, breeds instability — with Luper's caveat that Locke's own tolerance excluded atheists and Catholics.

The activities survey how the school perceives the national and regional flags, and discuss quotations on loyalty and free speech — Voltaire's among them: defending to the death another's right to say what you disagree with.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words — the one thing, its strongest case, and the figure or date you remember best. Your companion asks one hard question; answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the lesson's one thing in two sentences of your own, using one of its named cases.
2. Find the same issue in your own school or town this week; write what the lesson's teaching would say about it.
3. Teach the lesson to someone at home in five minutes; write down the question they asked that was hardest to answer.
