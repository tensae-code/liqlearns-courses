# A patriot's responsibilities — the nurse, the whistle-blower and the drunk driver

## Do it first

Three small scenes, three hard choices. A nurse is offered money to move a patient up the queue. A worker sees her boss's wrongdoing — and fears exposure herself. A traffic officer stops a drunk driver and finds his own friend. What does love of country require in each? Decide all three before reading.

## The teaching

Patriotism is wider than guarding the border. It is working for the public good, respecting the Constitution, and fighting corruption ethically — in ordinary jobs, on ordinary days. Case one: Chaltu, a nurse, is offered money to jump a patient queue. It is unethical — if every nurse did it, the hospital would collapse into bribery, and the sickest would wait behind the richest. Case two: Aster, harassed by her boss, must weigh exposing him against the exposure she herself would face. The guide's answer: the aggrieved should stand together — whistle-blowing protects everyone, including future victims. Case three: the drunk driver is the traffic officer's own friend. The officer's duty is to arrest him — a car in drunk hands is a lethal weapon, and feelings must not displace duty.

Notice what the three cases share: in each, the patriotic act costs something — money refused, safety risked, a friendship strained. Patriotism that costs nothing is only a flag on a wall.
