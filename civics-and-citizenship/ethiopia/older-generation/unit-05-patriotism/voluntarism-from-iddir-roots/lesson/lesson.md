# Voluntarism — from Iddir roots to the 2009 Charities Proclamation

## Do it first

When a neighbour dies, who pays for the funeral? In Ethiopia, often the Iddir — neighbours organised to carry each other. Voluntary action is not imported; it is old. Trace it with this lesson from the village to the law books.

## The teaching

Voluntary action has Ethiopian roots as old as society itself: the extended family, the Church, and indigenous organisations carrying the needy. The NGO era came later — most organisations arriving after 1970, food aid after the famines of 1973/4 and 1984/5. The guide is honest about weaknesses: minimal giving by citizens, firms and public bodies; a brain drain since the late 1970s depleting the voluntary sector; a civil society 'less developed, less active and much fewer' than in other African countries. The law then organised the field: Proclamation No. 621/2009 sorts organisations into four classes — Ethiopian Charities/Societies, Ethiopian Residents Charities/Societies (over 10% foreign funds), Foreign Charities, and Mass-Based Societies.

Two cases show voluntarism alive: Tolosa, whose initiative — including help from the Diaspora — improved facilities at home; and heart surgery: Ethiopia's first Cardiac Center, inaugurated on 12 February 2009 by Dr. Belay, who recalls being a Grade 9 student without shoes.
