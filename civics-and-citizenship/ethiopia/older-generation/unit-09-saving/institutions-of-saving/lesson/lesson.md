# Institutions of saving — equb, Iddir, banks and insurers, from salt bars to policies

## Do it first

Your grandmother's equb and a modern bank do the same job with different tools. This lesson walks the whole road in order — from salt bars in a cave treasury to insurance policies — and asks where the old institutions still beat the new.

Sub lesson 1 — The old money: salt bars, the treasury cave and the 1678 ban. Saving's Ethiopian history, from Pankhurst: Gondar's money-lending banned by the church council of 1678 while usury flourished with foreign trade; Sahla Sellase's treasury cave north of Ankobar, with near three hundred jars of 5,000–6,000 dollars each; interest reckoned at one bar of salt per Maria Theresa dollar. Sub lesson 2 — The traditional institutions: equb and Iddir, and the Metebaber Meredaja debate. The equb — members contributing in turn, the pot rotating — and the Iddir — neighbours organised for funerals and support — stand beside the modern institutions, not beneath them. A modern Iddir, Metebaber Meredaja, is debated: should an Iddir serve only funerals, or also medication and members' economic life?

Banks begin with the Menelik II–National Bank of Egypt agreement; insurance from 1905; nationalisation into the Ethiopian Insurance Corporation in 1974; private insurers again after 1991 — nine companies and eleven brokerages by 2000.
