# New thinking in saving — buy local, waste nothing, the leather proof

## Do it first

Every imported shoe is birr leaving the country for something Ethiopian hands can make. Saving, this lesson argues, is not only a household habit — it is national economics. But a famous sceptic disagrees about how easy that is. Read both.

## The teaching

Saving is reframed as national economics: buy local and avoid wastage, so the country stops importing what it can make. The guide honestly supplies the cynical counter — de Rivero: transnational corporations invest only where technical capacity, infrastructure, market size, legal security and stability already promise profit. New thinking must build those conditions, not just chant 'buy local'.

[Picture 29036 — an export-standard shoe and an Ethiopian tannery]

The proof case is Ethiopia's leather industry: export-standard shoes, exports up 13% in 2008 under the engineering capacity building programme, a European glove manufacturer setting up to buy from local tanneries, and a German company planting itself in Ethiopia. The new thinking can work — where capacity is built, it demonstrably did.
