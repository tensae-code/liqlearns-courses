# Saving becomes investment — banks, the World Bank and Ethiopia's banking story

## Do it first

Your deposit does not sleep in a vault. It becomes someone's loan, a bridge, a power line. This lesson follows the money — and tells the biography of the banks that move it, a story with invasions, liquidations and rebirths.

## The teaching

Economists argue the direction both ways: saving as the engine of growth, against growth as the cause of saving. The machinery, either way, is the banks — Ethiopian banks' savings, investment and loans — and, internationally, borrowing through the World Bank and IMF under strict tender procedures against corruption: the Bank's HIV/AIDS support of over $1.6 billion to more than 30 sub-Saharan African countries since 2000, and its lighting-by-2030, broadband and trade-corridor aims, show the scale.

[Picture 29039 — a savings book growing into a bridge, school and power line]

The case is the banking system's own biography: the Bank of Abyssinia (1906), liquidated in 1931; the Bank of Ethiopia; the Italian invasion banks; the State Bank of 1943; the National Bank (1963) and the Commercial Bank (1964, founded with 20 million Birr); nationalisation in 1974; and the National Bank re-established in 1994 as a judicial entity separate from government. Saving becomes investment only where institutions survive to carry it — Ethiopia's had to be rebuilt three times.
