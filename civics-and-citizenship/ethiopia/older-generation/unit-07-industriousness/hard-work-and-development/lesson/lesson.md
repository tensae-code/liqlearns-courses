# Hard work and development — can Ethiopia copy the take-off?

## Do it first

South Korea was poor within living memory; now it builds the world's ships. Could Ethiopia simply copy that? Two camps argue opposite answers in this lesson. Pick your side as you read — and notice what both sides agree Ethiopia must do.

## The teaching

Development's pessimists and optimists argue. De Rivero's case: no new industrialised countries are likely to emerge — the last ones (South Korea, Taiwan, Singapore, Hong Kong) are already through, and liberalising alone 'will never suffice' without technological modernisation and solid democratic institutions. Against him, the IMF and World Bank keep faith that growth can be copied — their language of 'take-offs' and 'economic miracles'.

[Picture 29031 — a foreign crew's factory, an Ethiopian crew building next]

Ethiopia's own task is set against the argument, and both camps' lists overlap: work hard, and cut what keeps the country poor — population growth, poor education, lack of health care. The cases are Chinese development visible across the country, and Ethiopia's current poverty statistics: evidence brought home.
