# Ethiopia's development strategies — ADLI, industry, capacity, and globalisation weighed

## Do it first

A country chooses its road in documents most citizens never read. This lesson opens four of them. Notice the number — four, not three; the guide itself corrects the student text on exactly that point.

## The teaching

Ethiopia's chosen instruments are four strategy documents: **ADLI** — Agricultural Development-Led Industrialisation, the rural development strategy centred on agriculture, which carries over 80% of the population: agriculture-led growth assures accelerated growth, lets the people benefit, frees the country from dependence and builds a market economy. Then the Industrial Development Strategy; Issues of Building a Democratic System (the capacity-building document); and the FDRE Foreign Relations and Security Policy as the fourth.

[Picture 29032 — four strategy documents over farmland and a factory]

Globalisation is then weighed whole. The UNDP's 1999 definition: 'shrinking space, shrinking time and disappearing borders'. The effects stand in two columns — wealth creation, technology spread and women's rights against exploitation, pollution, overwhelmed cultures and suppressed unions. The lesson does not cheer or boo; it weighs.
