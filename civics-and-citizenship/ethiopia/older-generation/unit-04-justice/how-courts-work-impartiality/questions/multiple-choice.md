# How courts work — impartiality, nepotism and stereotyping — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1.** A serving judge may not —
a) compete for political office  b) read case files  c) retire
**Answer: a) compete for political office**

**2.** Judges are removable —
a) every year  b) only conditionally, with retirement fixed by law  c) at any minister's wish
**Answer: b) only conditionally, with retirement fixed by law**

**3.** Nepotism means —
a) working overtime  b) favouring family, as in Ato Worku's case  c) judging by evidence
**Answer: b) favouring family, as in Ato Worku's case**

**4.** A judge deciding a case may not consider —
a) who is in power  b) the evidence  c) the law
**Answer: a) who is in power**

**5.** The 1991 looting followed —
a) the Derg's fall  b) an election  c) a harvest failure
**Answer: a) the Derg's fall**

**6.** Chief targets of the looting were —
a) service cooperatives  b) schools  c) churches
**Answer: a) service cooperatives**

**7.** Stereotyping in court is —
a) never a lawful way to judge  b) sometimes required  c) the judge's right
**Answer: a) never a lawful way to judge**

**8.** Impartiality is protected by —
a) secrecy  b) structure — rules about office, removal and retirement  c) hoping judges are angels
**Answer: b) structure — rules about office, removal and retirement**

**9.** A 'relative's application' pushed at a judge should be —
a) refused — jobs are not family gifts  b) signed at once  c) hidden
**Answer: a) refused — jobs are not family gifts**

**10.** When lawful order collapses, the lesson shows —
a) looting and arson fill the space  b) fairness improves  c) taxes fall
**Answer: a) looting and arson fill the space**
