One lesson from the older course.

Courts are kept impartial by structure, not by hoping judges are angels. A judge may not consider who is in power when deciding; may not compete for political office while serving; and can be removed only conditionally, with retirement fixed by law — so neither favour nor fear can reach the bench. The Ato Worku case shows the rot the structure guards against: corruption through nepotism, a boss engineering a job for his family. The second case is the looting that followed the Derg's fall in 1991, when resented service cooperatives were chief targets of arson and destruction — what fills the space when lawful order collapses.
