# Fairness — benefits and burdens, and who pays

## Do it first

School is free; the clinic is cheap; the road is built. Who paid? Fairness is not only what you get — it is also what you carry. Before reading, guess: in a fair country, should everyone pay the same amount, or the same share of what they have?

## The teaching

Fairness is equity of benefits and burdens. Citizens receive: education free to the end of the first secondary cycle, and partly subsidised health care. Citizens carry: taxation above all. Other countries balance the pans differently — the United Kingdom's health care is fully free (its 2000/01 budget spent about £109 billion on social security, £72 billion on health, £50 billion on education, at £1 = 15 Birr as the guide converts it), while the United States leaves health largely to private insurance. Neither balance is automatic; each is a choice about fairness.

Two cases put the principle to work: Teshome's share — a worker treated unfairly by Ato Gadissa, and how Abdi should respond; and the benefits principle at the fuel pump, where a duty decision on fuel moves every transport price in the country, and those who benefit from the roads help pay for them.
