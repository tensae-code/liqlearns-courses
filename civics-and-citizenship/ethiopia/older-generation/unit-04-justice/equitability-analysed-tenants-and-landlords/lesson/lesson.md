# Equitability analysed — tenants, landlords and Bloody Sunday

## Do it first

A farmer grows four sacks of grain on rented land. The landlord takes three — plus the farmer's free labour. The farmer keeps one sack for his family. Write one word for this arrangement. Then read how two countries lived it.

## The teaching

Inequitable distribution, examined historically. Pre-revolutionary Russia is the mirror: three-quarters of the population poor peasants, serfs treated near livestock even after abolition in 1861, and Bloody Sunday 1905 — hymn-singing petitioners fired on, about 95 killed. Ethiopia's own case is landlords and tenants under the 1967 Civil Code: tenants owed 75 percent of their produce plus free labour, transport, firewood, domestic service and granary building, living 'in perpetual fear of eviction', as Meredith writes.

The additional activity turns analysis into empathy: a role-play debate among a disabled job-seeker, a shoe-shine street child, an abandoned pregnant girl, a self-made businessperson, an HIV-clinic doctor and a rural teacher — each arguing what a fair share would mean from where they stand.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words — the one thing, its strongest case, and the figure or date you remember best. Your companion asks one hard question; answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the lesson's one thing in two sentences of your own, using one of its named cases.
2. Find the same issue in your own school or town this week; write what the lesson's teaching would say about it.
3. Teach the lesson to someone at home in five minutes; write down the question they asked that was hardest to answer.
