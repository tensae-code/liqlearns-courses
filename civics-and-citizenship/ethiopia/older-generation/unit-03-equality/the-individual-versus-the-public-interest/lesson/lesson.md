# The individual versus the public interest — dams and resettlement

## Do it first

A dam will light a whole city — but its lake will drown one village's farms. The villagers must move. Is that fair? Who decides, and what do the villagers deserve? Argue it in your head before the lesson stages the real debate.

## The teaching

Sometimes one person's rights must yield to everyone's benefit — but 'sometimes' is the whole argument. The lesson teaches it through hydro-electric power: the Tekeze plant case, and the Omo Valley scheme, where a March 2009 press report (power reaching only 25% of the population, five large schemes under construction, export revenue from neighbours) is set against the opposition's case — displacement along the Omo and Lake Turkana, damage to the environment, and food security for the people who live there now.

[Picture 29011 — a dam wall, a village, a lit city, a balance]

The lesson closes by staging the argument it has built: a four-party resettlement debate — residents, environmentalists, electricity users, and government — each side required to answer the others. The individual does not simply vanish into 'the public interest'; the public interest must justify itself to the individual, face to face.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words — the one thing, its strongest case, and the figure or date you remember best. Your companion asks one hard question; answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the lesson's one thing in two sentences of your own, using one of its named cases.
2. Find the same issue in your own school or town this week; write what the lesson's teaching would say about it.
3. Teach the lesson to someone at home in five minutes; write down the question they asked that was hardest to answer.
