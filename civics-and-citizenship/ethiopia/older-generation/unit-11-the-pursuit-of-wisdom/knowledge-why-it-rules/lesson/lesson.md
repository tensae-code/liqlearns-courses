# Knowledge — why it rules, and the eclipse that proves it can be tested

## Do it first

On one special day, the moon covers the sun exactly — not almost, exactly. Coincidence? Arithmetic? This lesson argues that knowledge is democracy's precondition and poverty's conqueror — then proves knowledge can be tested, in the sky.

## The teaching

Knowledge rules because of what it does. Mayor demands that education promote 'the successes and accomplishments of everybody', not select an elite — with dark counter-examples of elites marked for destruction: Burundi in 1972, Cambodia, China's Red Guards. Ethiopia's own commitment is the 70% rule on higher-education provision for science and technology: knowledge aimed at the whole people's capacity, weighted toward the sciences that build.

The case study tests knowledge itself: the solar eclipse. The sun is 400 times the moon's width and 400 times further away, so the two discs fit exactly in our sky — a fact anyone can verify at the next total eclipse, and not before: the moon is receding 3.8 cm a year, so in millions of years total eclipses will end. The passage is adapted from *New Scientist* (31.1.2009), as the guide credits. Knowledge that can be tested can be trusted; that is why it rules.
