One lesson from the older course.

A myth is believed but unprovable, created for purposes — among which politics is one. El Dorado, the golden city that never was; the biblical leviathan; and, handled plainly, Ethiopia's own Queen of Sheba and Solomon story, the birth of Menelik — 'taken by historians as myth', though embroiled in the country's history. Yet myths are not pure invention: Greek myths carry real people and places, so archaeology keeps proving parts of them. The skill is separation, not dismissal.

Remember it with its case. Now — say it back, in your own words.
