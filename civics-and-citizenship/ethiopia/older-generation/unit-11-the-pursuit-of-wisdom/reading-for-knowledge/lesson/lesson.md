# Reading for knowledge — literacy, women and Galileo's banned book

## Do it first

A banned book that changed the universe. A girl under a tree whose reading will educate a whole family she has not yet had. This lesson connects literacy's numbers to one man's telescope — and asks why reading frightens the powerful.

## The teaching

Reading is the citizen's self-education. UNESCO's figures, as the guide keys them: 95% of the world's adult illiterates live in developing countries; Ethiopia's literacy in 2000 stood at 47% of men and 31% of women — up from 37% and 20% in 1990, but below Kenya's and Sudan's. Mayor's chain explains why the women's figure matters most: educating a woman educates an entire family, making children's education 'a living reality from the day they are born'.

[Picture 29044 — a girl reading under a tree, a telescope behind her]

The case is Galileo: the telescope of 1609, the earth demoted to a planet among planets, condemnation by the Catholic Church, and his book placed on the 1616 Index of Forbidden Books. The ban failed — the printing press carried the discovery past it, and conceptions of the universe changed anyway. Books outlive bans; readers outlast censors.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words — the one thing, its strongest case, and the figure or date you remember best. Your companion asks one hard question; answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the lesson's one thing in two sentences of your own, using one of its named cases.
2. Find the same issue in your own school or town this week; write what the lesson's teaching would say about it.
3. Teach the lesson to someone at home in five minutes; write down the question they asked that was hardest to answer.
