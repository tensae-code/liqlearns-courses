# Fighting corruption — Watergate and the Ethics and Anti-Corruption Commission

## Do it first

If a president himself breaks the law, who can touch him? Write your honest answer. Then read how two young reporters, a secret source, and a set of tapes answered that question in America — and what Ethiopia built for the same fight.

## The teaching

Corruption is an 'old phenomenon', not an African invention — a 1994 East and Central African seminar's papers name Italy, Japan and Brazil alongside African cases. Its scale is the scandal: sums stolen and hidden in foreign accounts exceed, in some countries, the whole public debt. Corruption is development's major obstacle and democracy's enemy, and its most devastating form is electoral fraud, because it steals the people's voice itself.

[Picture 29009 — a president's office door, headline pinned to it]

The Watergate case proves accountability can reach the top. Two young reporters followed a burglary story; a secret source, 'Deep Throat', guided them; and a president who had been re-elected in a landslide in November 1972 was forced to resign in 1974 when his own tapes surfaced. Ethiopia's institution for this fight, named in the unit's review answers, is the Ethics and Anti-Corruption Commission.
