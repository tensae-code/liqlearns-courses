# Why rule of law is necessary — when it breaks down — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1.** In Moi's Kenya, corruption became —
a) impossible  b) a school subject  c) institutionalised
**Answer: c) institutionalised**

**2.** Gitobu Imanyara was —
a) a judge  b) a journalist arrested after exposing corruption  c) a president
**Answer: b) a journalist arrested after exposing corruption**

**3.** Rule by decree in Ghana by 1960 means —
a) the ruler's orders replaced law  b) courts grew stronger  c) elections multiplied
**Answer: a) the ruler's orders replaced law**

**4.** The unpublished anti-corruption findings show —
a) a printing error  b) a healthy system  c) accountability was only pretend
**Answer: c) accountability was only pretend**

**5.** The South Omo case is about —
a) youth peace-building across cultures  b) a dam  c) a tax dispute
**Answer: a) youth peace-building across cultures**

**6.** Tolerance between communities helps rule of law because —
a) law rules best where neighbours refuse to become enemies  b) courts are unnecessary  c) laws are written in gardens
**Answer: a) law rules best where neighbours refuse to become enemies**

**7.** A president 'above the law' contradicts —
a) only etiquette  b) nothing important  c) the rule of law itself
**Answer: c) the rule of law itself**

**8.** Arresting journalists who expose corruption —
a) silences the checking that rule of law needs  b) strengthens the courts  c) is required by constitutions
**Answer: a) silences the checking that rule of law needs**

**9.** The lesson teaches the necessity of rule of law by —
a) showing what breaks without it  b) listing fines  c) memorising articles
**Answer: a) showing what breaks without it**

**10.** Rule by decree replaced law in —
a) Botswana's meetings  b) South Omo  c) Nkrumah's Ghana
**Answer: c) Nkrumah's Ghana**
