# Limited versus unlimited government — Soweto and Biko

## Do it first

Can a government do anything it likes, or are there lines it may not cross? Name one line. This lesson compares governments that accept limits with those that refuse them — and tells, soberly, what refusal cost in South Africa.

## The teaching

A **limited government** accepts that a constitution stands above it: Britain, the United States, and Ethiopia since 1991, building separation of powers under a supreme constitution. An **unlimited government** refuses the line: Ethiopia's military regime of 1974–91, and Franco's Spain, where a republic was overthrown into civil war and thirty-five years of military rule followed.

[Picture 29008 — school students marching in Soweto, 1976]

The case study is apartheid South Africa. On 16 June 1976, school students in Soweto rose against being forced to learn in Afrikaans; thirteen-year-old Hector Peterson was shot dead. Steve Biko, the Black Consciousness leader, was banned, harassed, tortured — and died in police custody in September 1977. An unlimited government does not only break laws; it breaks people, nearly twenty years before apartheid finally fell.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words — the one thing, its strongest case, and the figure or date you remember best. Your companion asks one hard question; answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the lesson's one thing in two sentences of your own, using one of its named cases.
2. Find the same issue in your own school or town this week; write what the lesson's teaching would say about it.
3. Teach the lesson to someone at home in five minutes; write down the question they asked that was hardest to answer.
