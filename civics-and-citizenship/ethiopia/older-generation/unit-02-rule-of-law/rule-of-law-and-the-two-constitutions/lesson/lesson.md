# Rule of law and the two constitutions — federal and regional

## Do it first

Ethiopia does not have one constitution — it has a federal constitution and a constitution for each region. In pairs, imagine one of you reads only the federal one and the other only a regional one. What would each of you know, and what would you miss?

## The teaching

Rule of law begins with a written rulebook that stands above everyone — and in a federation there are two levels of rulebook. The lesson's method is comparison: the class splits in two, one side reading the federal constitution, the other a regional one, then teaching each other. Neither book alone is the whole law of the land; a citizen lives under both.

[Picture 29006 — two constitution books open side by side]

The readings show federalism's working problems through SNNPR, the region of greatest ethno-cultural diversity. The hardest problem is the language of education. Vaughan found that peasants welcomed mother-tongue schooling, while the educated feared it would disadvantage school leavers. For civics itself, English was selected as the language of learning from Grade 5 — a practical compromise inside a principled federation.
