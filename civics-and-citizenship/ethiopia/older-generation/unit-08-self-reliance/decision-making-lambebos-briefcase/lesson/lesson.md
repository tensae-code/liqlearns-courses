# Decision-making — Lambebo's briefcase and the SWOT habit

## Do it first

You find a briefcase. It is heavy, locked, and nobody saw you pick it up. Four roads open: keep it, report it, return it whole, return it light and lie. Decide now — then learn the method this lesson teaches for deciding everything.

Sub lesson 1 — The good decision-maker: attributes and accurate information. Decision-making is self-reliance in action. The good decision-maker is courageous, social-minded, innovative, confident, aware, self-respecting, and shoulders responsibility — and works from accurate information, not wishes. Sub lesson 2 — The SWOT habit: Shmebo's ambition analysed. Shmebo's ambition is worked as a full SWOT analysis — Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats — the honest four-box habit that turns a hope into a plan. Sub lesson 3 — Amendable and unamendable decisions: Lambebo's briefcase decided.

Some wrong decisions can be amended — where to live, which course to take. Others cannot easily — marrying too young, a job taken only for money. Lambebo finds a briefcase and must choose: keep it, report it, return it whole, or return it light and lie. The method's closing virtue is flexibility: a good decision-maker can amend what can be amended — and thinks twice before what cannot.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words — the one thing, its strongest case, and the figure or date you remember best. Your companion asks one hard question; answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the lesson's one thing in two sentences of your own, using one of its named cases.
2. Find the same issue in your own school or town this week; write what the lesson's teaching would say about it.
3. Teach the lesson to someone at home in five minutes; write down the question they asked that was hardest to answer.
