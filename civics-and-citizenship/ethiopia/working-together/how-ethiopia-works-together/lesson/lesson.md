# How Ethiopia Works Together — Debo, Idir, Equb

## Do it first

Ask at home: does your family belong to an idir or an equb? Has your neighbourhood held a debo? Bring one true answer into this lesson — it is about your own people's way of working together.

## Debo — the work party

Debo (the placement prints the Amharic gloss ጂጊ beside it) is the communal work party: neighbours gather to do one household's heavy work together.

[Picture 28062 — debo, idir, equb — three panels]

## Idir and equb

Idir is the mutual-aid association: members support each other, above all in times of loss and burial.

Equb is the rotating savings association: members pay in together and the gathered sum goes to each member in turn.

## And at school

At school, the same culture lives as group work.

Pupils identify the cooperation and teamwork culture in their own community and school.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Ethiopia's own culture of cooperation: debo, the communal work party; idir, the mutual-aid association; equb, the rotating savings association — and group work at school. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: debo (ጂጊ), idir, equb. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
