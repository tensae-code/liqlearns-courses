# Public Property Is Ours — Use It Properly, Keep It for Tomorrow

## Do it first

Choose one shared thing you use — a school book, a water point, a bench. Inspect it: is it treated as everyone's treasure or as nobody's? This lesson is the book's answer.

## What public property is

Public wealth is the natural and man-made materials, services and other things that contribute to human benefit.

Using it properly means handling and guarding it carefully, responsibly, lawfully, scientifically and morally.

Proper use saves it from destruction, theft, shortage and unfair distribution, creates an equal chance for the community to use it, and keeps it for the coming generation.

## What is expected of the public

Use resources responsibly and economically, and build a saving culture.

Know what correct use is, and prevent acts that destroy resources.

Be willing to share, maintain and protect what is for common use.

Fight selfishness, waste, unnecessary pollution and over-use.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: public property is the people's wealth: use it carefully, responsibly, lawfully and morally — so it is saved from destruction and kept for the coming generation. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: public property, saving culture, over-use. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: using public property properly, keeping it for tomorrow.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
