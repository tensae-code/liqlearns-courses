# What Blocks the Common Good

## Do it first

Visit one shared place — a park, a water point, a school yard. List everything there that blocks its good use. Compare your list with the chapter's blockers.

## The obstacles named

The chapter closes with the obstacles that keep common goods from being worked for and used properly.

Pupils identify the factors that influence common goods as a chapter outcome.

From the chapter's own contrasts, the blockers are: selfishness, waste, destruction, theft, unfair distribution and pollution.

[Picture 28059 — a shared park spoiled by dumping]

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the common good is blocked by selfishness, waste, destruction, theft, unfair distribution and pollution — naming the blockers is the first defence. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: obstacle, unfair distribution, pollution. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: the obstacles to the common good.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
