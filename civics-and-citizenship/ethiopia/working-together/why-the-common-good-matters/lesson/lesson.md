# Why the Common Good Matters — Safety, Law and Each Other

## Do it first

Try to name one good thing in your life that needs no other person at all. It is harder than it sounds. This lesson shows why the common good stands at the centre.

## Each other

No one can live a good life separated alone — our private wants are often the same as others'.

Shared values create unity, a common feeling, satisfaction and happiness.

Standing for the common good needs participation, cooperation, mutual support and citizens who take responsibility — striving for it is called a patriotic virtue of citizens.

## Safety, law and each other

Without police, courts and a defence force there could be no peace, no rule of law and no respect for human rights — instead disorder, social turmoil and destruction would spread.

The common good has a world scope too: the peoples of the world must cooperate for world peace, a clean environment, equality and justice.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: no good life is lived alone: shared values make unity, standing for the common good needs participation and responsibility — and peace, law and rights stand or fall together. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: patriotic virtue, rule of law, mutual support. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
