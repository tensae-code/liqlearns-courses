# Lesson plan — Cooperation — Together for a Common Benefit

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Working Together and the Common Good · **Lesson:** Cooperation — Together for a Common Benefit
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 5 — Working Together and the Common Good (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** cooperation is a group of people working together in unity for a common benefit — sharing new ideas and making hard work easy.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the lessons before it in this title — the learner already knows the earlier circles of this course (myself, then family, neighbours, school, community and culture) as far as this title. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: What Blocks the Common Good (the previous lesson in this title). Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Cooperation is a group of people working together in unity for a common benefit, formed of two or more people.
2. Working together brings sharing new ideas, and hard work made easy.
3. Working alone keeps its own uses — studying for an exam, doing simple tasks.
4. The wise person knows which work asks for hands together and which asks for quiet alone.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson's scenes are the learner's own home, school and neighbourhood.

## If the learner is stuck
- Believes that “cooperation needs only one person working alone” → go back to the lesson's own words: cooperation is a group of people working together in unity for a common benefit — sharing new ideas and making hard work easy. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
- Gives the words but no example → ask for the lesson happening once in their own home, school or street today; the example is the proof.
