# Indigenous Knowledge — What It Is

## Do it first

Ask yourself where the knowledge lives in your own community: who knows when the rains will fail, how to settle a feud, which leaf stops a fever? Was any of it learned from a book? This lesson names the library that has no shelves.

## The library with no shelves

Indigenous knowledge is knowledge **developed by a local people through long histories of social life and interaction with their environment**. It is based on the community's culture, traditions, social interaction, rituals, values and belief systems — and therefore it is **different from society to society**: each people's knowledge is fitted to its own land and life. 

[Picture 28839 — Pottery wheels, with elders settling a dispute behind]



Ethiopia is rich in it. The fields named: architecture, pottery, medicine, agriculture, cottage industry, conflict resolution and environmental conservation — knowledge held not in departments but in grandmothers, smiths, elders and farmers. 

[Picture 28840 — An elder telling a proverb to children (teacher guide — evidence)]



Here is the everyday example that shows it working. When a marriage falls into conflict, the couple is taken first **not to a court, but to local elders**, for a win-win settlement. That is the popular indigenous conflict-resolution pattern across most of Ethiopia — the subject of the next lesson, and a knowledge system older than the state that now studies it.

Your own first errand in this band: inventory what is practised in your community. The list you bring back is this lesson, proved at home.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the definition — and three kinds of indigenous knowledge practised within walking distance of where you stand.

## Do It Yourself

1. Make the inventory: five items of indigenous knowledge in your community, each with its living carrier named (a role, not a private name, unless they agree).
2. Ask an elder one 'how do we know…?' question and write the answer exactly as given.
3. For one field — medicine, farming, building — write what the indigenous method is and what the imported method is. Where do they agree?
