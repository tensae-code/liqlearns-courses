# Lesson plan — Indigenous Knowledge — What It Is

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Home Knowledge and Its Institutions · **Lesson:** Indigenous Knowledge — What It Is
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 23 — Home Knowledge and Its Institutions (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** indigenous knowledge is the knowledge a local people builds through long histories of living with its environment — carried in its culture, rituals, values and beliefs, and different from society to society.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the citizenship levels before it — the learner knows the state's written frame; this band teaches the older knowledge that was governing home ground long before it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. ## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define indigenous knowledge fully.
2. Name fields where Ethiopia is rich in it.
3. Give the everyday example: marital conflict taken first to elders.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks indigenous knowledge means old-fashioned guesses → it is knowledge *developed through long histories* of tested living — experience, systematised by generations.
- Thinks it is one Ethiopian system → it differs from society to society; each people's is fitted to its own land.
