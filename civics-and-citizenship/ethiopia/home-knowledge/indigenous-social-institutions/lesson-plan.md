# Lesson plan — Indigenous Social Institutions

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Home Knowledge and Its Institutions · **Lesson:** Indigenous Social Institutions
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 23 — Home Knowledge and Its Institutions (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Ethiopia's indigenous social institutions organise care itself: Siinqee guards women's rights, Idir buries and Iqub saves, Mahber feasts and Debo harvests — voluntary, trust-based, and built by neighbours.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the citizenship levels before it — the learner knows the state's written frame; this band teaches the older knowledge that was governing home ground long before it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Siinqee — women's endorsed rights on a stick** — the Oromo women's institution — endorsed rights, protection, and mediation by raised sticks
2. **Idir and Iqub — burying together, saving together** — Idir's mutual aid around burial and sudden risks; Iqub's rotating pot, paid by lottery
3. **Mahber/Senbete and Debo/Jigie — eating together, working together** — feast-and-care associations, and labour shared at ploughing, harvest and building

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the rest of this band — what indigenous knowledge is and the citizens it makes. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Tell what Siinqee is and what its stick commands.
2. Contrast Idir and Iqub.
3. Contrast Mahber/Senbete with Debo/Jigie.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes Idir and Iqub → Idir answers calamity (burial first); Iqub rotates savings by lottery. Both are trust, differently aimed.
- Thinks Siinqee is ceremonial dress → it is endorsed *rights* with practical work: protection, respect, and stopping fights.
