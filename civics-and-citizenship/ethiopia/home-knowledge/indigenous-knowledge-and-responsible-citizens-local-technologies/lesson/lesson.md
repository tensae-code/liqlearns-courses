# Indigenous Knowledge and Responsible Citizens — Local Technologies

## Do it first

Look at the objects nearest you that were made by hand near here — a pot, a cloth, a tool. Then look at what your hands are doing as you answer: would you greet the maker of that pot as an expert, or look down on them? The lesson will ask you that to your face.

## The crafts at hand

Active participation in socio-economic life is a citizen's duty, and one road into it is **indigenous technology**: local products at reasonable cost and better accessibility than pricey, scarce imports — reducing dependence on outside supplies. What your own place can make, your own place cannot be held hostage for. 

[Picture 28843 — Three workshop panels — a loom, a forge, a carver's bench]



The long-practised crafts named: **pottery, iron-smithing, jewellery, weaving, wood carving and leather work**. Watch the weaving in motion: the shuttle crosses, the cloth grows, the pattern holds — skill you can set your watch by. 

[GIF 28844 — A shuttle crossing a loom, back and forth]



Now the lesson turns its lens on the pupils. The craftspeople who preserved these knowledges did so **against deep-rooted negative social attitudes** — and their determination, and their valuing of their own wisdom, are exactly the marks of a responsible citizen.

And the contradiction is confronted head-on, as an activity, not a whisper: a society **loving the products while looking down on the producers**. Take the weekend observation visit the lesson sets — go and watch a local craft being worked — and let your eyes correct your attitudes.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the six crafts and the three advantages of local technology — then say the contradiction out loud, plainly.

## Do It Yourself

1. Make the weekend visit: watch one local craft for an hour. Write what the maker's hands knew that yours do not.
2. Price one imported item and its local equivalent. Write both prices and both makers' distances from you.
3. Find one object in your home made by a craft this lesson names. Write its maker's defence in three lines.
