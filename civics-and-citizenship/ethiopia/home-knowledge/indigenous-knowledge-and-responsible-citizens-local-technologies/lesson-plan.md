# Lesson plan — Indigenous Knowledge and Responsible Citizens — Local Technologies

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Home Knowledge and Its Institutions · **Lesson:** Indigenous Knowledge and Responsible Citizens — Local Technologies
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 23 — Home Knowledge and Its Institutions (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** indigenous technologies — pottery, smithing, jewellery, weaving, carving, leather work — are local, cheaper and at hand; and the craftspeople who kept them alive against contempt teach what a responsible citizen looks like.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the citizenship levels before it — the learner knows the state's written frame; this band teaches the older knowledge that was governing home ground long before it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the long-practised crafts.
2. Give the three advantages of local technology: cost, access, independence.
3. State the contradiction the lesson confronts, and its answer.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks local means inferior → the lesson's measures are cost, accessibility and independence — on those, local wins at home.
- Thinks the contradiction is about politeness → it is about citizenship: valuing your own people's wisdom is the responsible citizen's mark.
