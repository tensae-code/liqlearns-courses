# Voluntary Work — Giving Time and Skill Freely

## Do it first

Think of one hour you own this week. Now imagine giving it away — no payment, no prize — to something your community needs. What would you give it to? Hold your answer; the lesson will test it against the definition.

## What volunteering is

Voluntary work is giving your time, energy and knowledge freely for the community's benefit, without seeking profit. It is social service as free service to where you live — done alone or together. The three gifts are time, energy and knowledge; the mark of the thing is that it is free: benefit flows to the community, not payment to the giver.

## Its named forms

The forms named in the answers: helping people who need help, planting seedlings, cleaning the surroundings, and farm work. A pupil's volunteering is real volunteering — seedlings planted by children shade the school for years. Do not wait to be grown before you give; the definition has no age in it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what voluntary work is — the three gifts and the one mark. Then name its four forms.

## Do It Yourself

1. Give one hour this week freely: help, plant, clean, or work a field with someone.
2. Offer a skill you have — reading, carrying, digging — where it is needed, without payment.
3. Invite one friend to volunteer with you; together counts too.
