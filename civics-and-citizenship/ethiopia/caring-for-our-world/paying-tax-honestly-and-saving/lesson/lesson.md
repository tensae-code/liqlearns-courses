# Paying Tax Honestly and Saving

## Do it first

Imagine a market stall at closing time. The seller counts the day: some coins paid over as tax with a receipt, some coins dropped in a savings box, the rest for tomorrow's goods. Three piles, three duties. Which pile is most often forgotten? Read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — Trade and market

Trade is buying, selling and exchanging goods and services. A market is where goods are bought and sold. These two definitions come first because the lesson's scene is the market: it is in buying and selling, honestly done, that tax and saving meet daily life.

## Sub lesson 2 — Paying tax honestly

Tax is the payment individuals and organisations make to government from their income. Everyone has a legal duty to pay it — because without tax the common services cannot be had: education, health and defence are named. Paying honestly is therefore not a favour to the government; it is how a citizen funds the services everyone stands on. This part is the civics part of the lesson.

## Sub lesson 3 — Saving

Saving is putting aside money or time for the future instead of consuming it all. Banks and insurance companies are named as modern saving institutions — places where putting aside is organised and kept. Saving and honest tax belong in one lesson because both are the same character in two directions: honesty toward the community's fund, and care toward your own future.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: what trade is, what a market is, what tax is and why it must be paid, and what saving is. Name the three common services tax makes possible.

## Do It Yourself

1. If you handle money this week, practise the three piles in small: spend, save, and what is owed.
2. Put one coin or note aside today as saving — future money, untouched.
3. Ask an adult trader one respectful question about how tax receipts work at their stall.
