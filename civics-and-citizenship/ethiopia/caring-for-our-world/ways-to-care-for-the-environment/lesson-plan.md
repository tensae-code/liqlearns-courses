# Lesson plan — Ways to Care for the Environment

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Caring for Our Shared World · **Lesson:** Ways to Care for the Environment
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 10 — Caring for Our Shared World (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The ways to care are habits of repair and restraint: closing taps so water is not wasted; fixing leaks instead of walking past them; and the chapter's other named habits of the same kind — repairing what drips, leaks and wastes, and restraining your own use to what is needed.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the lessons before it in Level 10 — Caring for Our Shared World — and on the learner's own daily life at home, at school and in the community. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **“a picture of …” in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of Level 10 — Caring for Our Shared World. Recommended, never forced.
- The learner's own experience of the lesson's subject in daily life is the real doorway; the Do it first opens there.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say and show what “The practices” teaches, in their own words, and do the practice named for it.
2. Say and show what “Repair and restraint” teaches, in their own words, and do the practice named for it.
3. Carry the lesson's practice into the week: repair what wastes, restrain what you take.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — every activity uses the learner's own home, school and street.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Listen — really listen — for a drip anywhere near you: a tap, a pipe, a roof edge after rain. Each drip is water leaving. This lesson is a doing-lesson: its practices are things your hands can do today.
2. **The explanation** — the lesson's parts, in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 28241]: a picture of a dripping tap being tightened, the drip stopping — the student studies the picture and names what it shows about the lesson before reading on.

   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the lesson's key sentences aloud — the student listens once, then reads them aloud in the same voice.
3. **Say it to your companion** — as written in lesson/lesson.md.
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the student watches and then does the Say it to your companion with a real companion.
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-or-false, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.

## If the learner is stuck
- Cannot say what the lesson teaches → go back to the Do it first and the picture; name what the picture shows, in one sentence, then read the first part again slowly.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → hold the one thing: The ways to care are habits of repair and restraint: closing taps so water is not wasted; fixing leaks instead of walking past them; and the chapter's other named habits of the same kind — repairing what drips, leaks and wastes, and restraining your own use to what is needed.
- Finds the practice hard → shrink it to the smallest real version and do that today; small and done beats grand and promised.
