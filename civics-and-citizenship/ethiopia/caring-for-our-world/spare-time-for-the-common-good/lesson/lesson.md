# Spare Time for the Common Good

## Do it first

Picture your next holiday afternoon divided down the middle like the picture: one half bright with play, the other half carrying water with an elder. The lesson does not ask you to give up the first half. Read carefully what it does ask.

## Spare time and the common good

Spare time is time outside regular work and duties. The common good is what serves all members of the community. Put the two together and a resource appears: pupils' holidays and breaks — time that is truly yours — can be shared, so that part of it serves everyone. Play is not the enemy here; rest and play belong in spare time. The lesson's claim is only that spare time can also be a gift.

[Picture 28245 — half play, half helping]

## A resource the community can share

Your breaks are a resource the community can share in — help given in holidays reaches people that school-hours help cannot. Even a part of spare time, given regularly, becomes a pattern a community can rely on: the elder's water carried every holiday is a small institution built from spare hours.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what spare time is and what the common good is. Then say how much of your next holiday you could share, and for what.

## Do It Yourself

1. Give one part of your next break to the common good — help an elder, clean a shared place.
2. Keep the rest of the break for true rest and play, without guilt.
3. Make it a pattern: the same small help on every holiday for a month.
