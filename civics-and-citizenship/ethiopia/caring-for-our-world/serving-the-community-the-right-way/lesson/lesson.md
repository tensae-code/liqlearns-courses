# Serving the Community the Right Way

## Do it first

Think of one act of service you could do this week. Now check it against the three principles before you do it: Does it respect their rights? Keep their confidences? Have their agreement? Only then is it service the right way.

## Sub lesson 1 — Serving the community

Serving the community is helping, done outward. The guide's own examples: donating clothes, helping neighbours, planting trees, and cleaning the classroom after lessons. Notice the size of the list — service is not only grand projects. A cleaned classroom is service; donated clothes are service; an hour given to a neighbour is service.

## Sub lesson 2 — The right way to serve

Service has principles, and they are this lesson's civics frame: basic rights, confidentiality and consent. You may not help people in ways that trample their rights. You may not repeat what someone told you in trust — confidentiality. And you may not act for people without their agreement — consent. Help that breaks these principles stops being service, however busy it looks. The neighbour in the picture watches and nods: agreement is part of the scene.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the guide's four examples of service, then the three principles of serving the right way — and what each principle forbids.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do one act of service this week: donate, help, plant, or clean.
2. Ask before you help in someone's private matter — get agreement first.
3. Keep one confidence connected with helping: what you were told in trust stays with you.
