# When We Do Not Care for the Environment

## Do it first

Picture the queue at the dry water point: empty jerrycans, cracked ground, people waiting for water that is not coming. Before you read the chain of consequences, ask: how many steps of not-caring stand behind this one scene?

## The chain of consequences

Not caring has consequences, and they come as a chain: water shortage; pollution; food shortage; loss of biodiversity — the variety of living things; health problems; flooding and storms; and acid rain. Read the chain slowly. Each link was once preventable, and each link pulls the next: spoiled water sickens people, lost variety weakens the land, damaged air and water return as damaged weather.

## Why the chain is taught

The chain is not taught to frighten you into doing nothing. It is taught backwards: every link names a place where care — the small acts, the repairs, the restraint of the lessons before — would have held. Knowing the chain is knowing exactly what your care prevents.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the chain of consequences, link by link, in order. Then pick one link and say what care would have prevented it.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the chain of consequences from memory and check it.
2. Do one act of care today aimed at the first link: water.
3. Teach the chain to someone younger, ending with what care prevents.
