# What Makes a Decision Moral

## Do it first

Picture a small accident: someone has fallen; a crowd is gathering; you are near. Before you move, your mind works. What does it do in those seconds? This lesson names the three things it must do for your decision to be a moral one.

## The three marks

What makes a decision moral? Three marks: thoughtfulness — the decision is actually thought about, not rushed from impulse; reasonableness — it can be explained with reasons that hold up; and balanced judgement — the sides are weighed fairly, not tipped by panic, favour or pressure. A decision missing any one mark is weakened: impulse without thought, reasons without fairness, fairness without thought.

## The first-aid test

The marks are tested in the answers against a first-aid case — a case where the moral choice must also be the sensible one. That is the deep point: a moral decision is not goodness instead of sense. In real trouble, the moral choice and the sensible choice must be found together, by a mind that thinks, reasons and weighs.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the three marks of a moral decision. Then explain the first-aid point: why must the moral choice also be the sensible one?

## Do It Yourself

1. Before one decision today, run the three marks aloud: Have I thought? Can I give reasons? Have I weighed fairly?
2. When you see a small trouble today, pause and think before you act — then act.
3. Write the three marks where you will see them this week.
