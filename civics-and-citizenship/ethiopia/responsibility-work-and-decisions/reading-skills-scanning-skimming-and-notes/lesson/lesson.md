# Reading Skills — Scanning, Skimming and Notes

## Do it first

Take any page of text near you. First let your eyes skim the whole page quickly — do not read, just fly over it. Then slow down and read one line closely. You have just used two of this lesson's tools. Read on to collect the rest.

## The toolkit

Reading well is a toolkit, not one trick: reading with a purpose — know why you are reading before you start; scanning — hunting fast for one thing; skimming — flying over to get the gist; noting key words; reading phrase by phrase; using marks as you read; taking notes; and shared reading — reading with others. The strategies stand side by side: reach for the one the task needs.

## A line the answers draw

This lesson's answers also draw a line every reader should know: the line between child work and child labour exploitation. A child's fair share of work is one thing; work that exploits a child — crushing their schooling, health or childhood — is another, and the two must never be confused.

## Say it to your companion

Name the reading strategies to your companion, one by one. Then demonstrate: skim a page aloud-ish — eyes only — and say the gist; then read one line closely and say what changed.

## Do It Yourself

1. Read one page three ways today: skim for gist, scan for one fact, read closely one paragraph.
2. Take notes on one text this week using key words only.
3. Read one short text together with a friend — shared reading — and compare what you each noticed.
