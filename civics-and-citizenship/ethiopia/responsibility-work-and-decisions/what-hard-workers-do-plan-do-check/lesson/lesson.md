# What Hard Workers Do — Plan, Do, Check

## Do it first

Take one real task you must do today. Before reading, say how you would normally begin it. Then read the rhythm of hard workers and compare: which of their four moves do you usually skip?

## The four-part rhythm

The marks of hard workers form one working rhythm: they use time well; they plan before they act; they carry the plan out; and they evaluate what they did. Four moves, in a loop — the evaluation of the last task becomes the wisdom of the next plan. It is a rhythm to copy whole, not four separate tricks.

## Why each move matters

Using time well gives the work room to happen. Planning first means the hands do not wander. Carrying out is where plans become real — a plan never carried out is only a wish. Evaluating closes the loop: looking back honestly is how a worker gets better instead of only older.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the four moves of hard workers in order. Then take one task and walk it through all four moves out loud.

## Do It Yourself

1. Plan one task on paper today before you touch it.
2. Carry today's plan out fully, then evaluate it in two honest sentences.
3. Use one block of time today with no drifting — guard it like a worker.
