# What Makes Deciding Hard — Pressure and Pulls

## Do it first

Stand still and imagine five hands pressing on you from five sides at once — not hurting, just pushing. It is hard to walk straight like that. Name the pressures that push on your decisions before you read the lesson's five.

## The five pressures

Five named pressures push against a good decision: low self-esteem — not trusting your own judgement; other commitments — duties pulling at the same hour; peer pressure — friends pushing toward their choice; parents' wishes; and the community's wishes. Parents' and community's wishes are not enemies — they often carry wisdom — but they are pressures: pulls that must be weighed with the three marks and the five steps, not obeyed or defied blindly.

## Holding against pressure

The five steps are the frame that holds against the arrows. Identify the problem clearly, list options beyond the pushed one, weigh outcomes for everyone — and the pressure becomes one thing weighed among others, instead of the thing that decides. Naming a pressure already weakens it: an unnamed pull pushes in the dark.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the five pressures that make deciding hard. Then name the pressure that pushes you most, and how the five steps help you hold.

## Do It Yourself

1. Name today's strongest pressure on one of your decisions — say it aloud.
2. Run one pressured decision through the five steps this week.
3. When peers push, buy thinking time: 'Let me think' is a full sentence.
