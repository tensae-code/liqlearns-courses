# Kinds of Responsibility — Self, Family and Country

## Do it first

Draw three circles in the air with your finger — small, medium, large, one inside the other. In each circle, point and name one duty that lives there. Keep the picture: three circles, one you standing in the middle.

## Sub lesson 1 — Responsibility to yourself

The innermost circle: responsibility to yourself. Your learning, your health, your conduct and your promises to yourself are yours to keep. A person who cannot be trusted with their own duties will struggle to carry anyone else's — the first circle is where responsibility is practised daily.

[Picture 28225 — three nested circles]

## Sub lesson 2 — Responsibility to your family and society

The middle circle: responsibility to your family and society. The work of the home, care for its members, and your share in the life of the community around you — these duties widen the first circle without replacing it. What you practised on yourself, you now owe outward.

## Sub lesson 3 — Responsibility to your country

The widest circle: responsibility to your country — the duty owed to the nation as a member of it. This is the civics part of the lesson: the same owning and answering, stretched to the largest circle you belong to. The circles are nested: country duties rest on family duties, and family duties rest on the duties you keep to yourself.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the three kinds of responsibility, with one duty in each circle. Explain why the circles are nested, not separate.

## Do It Yourself

1. Keep one duty in each circle today: one to yourself, one to your family, one wider.
2. Name one country duty a pupil of your age can really carry.
3. Draw the three circles on paper and write one duty inside each.
