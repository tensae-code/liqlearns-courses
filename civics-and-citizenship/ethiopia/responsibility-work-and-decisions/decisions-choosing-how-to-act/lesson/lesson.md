# Decisions — Choosing How to Act

## Do it first

Stand at an imaginary crossroads. One path sign points left, one right. Every day you stand at such crossroads — small ones mostly. Name three crossroads you stood at this week before you read on.

## What a decision is

A decision is choosing between ways to act. Where two or more paths stand open and you must take one, a decision is happening — whether you notice it or not. Noticing matters: a choice made by drifting is still a choice, but it is rarely a good one.

## A skill, not a talent

Deciding opens this chapter as a skill that can be learned, not a talent some people are born with. That is hopeful news: skills grow with teaching and practice. The lessons after this one give the marks of a moral decision, the five steps, the reasons it matters, and the pressures that make it hard — a whole craft, learnable piece by piece.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what a decision is, and why it matters that deciding is a learnable skill, not a talent.

## Do It Yourself

1. Name three decisions you made today — including one you made by drifting.
2. Choose one small thing deliberately today instead of drifting into it.
3. Tell someone at home: deciding is a skill — and one way you are practising it.
