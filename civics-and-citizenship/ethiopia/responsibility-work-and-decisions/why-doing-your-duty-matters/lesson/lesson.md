# Why Doing Your Duty Matters

## Do it first

Two pictures, side by side. In one, a boy runs from a broken window. In the other, a boy knocks at a door to apologise. Stand between the two pictures in your mind and ask: which boy can sleep well tonight? Remember your answer as you read.

## The two cases

The guide teaches this lesson through two cases, given in its answers as a summary. Ali broke a neighbour's window and fled. Faris apologised. The class judges both boys — and the judging is the lesson: fleeing leaves the damage unowned, the neighbour wronged and the boy carrying a secret; apologising owns the act, begins the repair, and lets the boy stand straight.

## Why fulfilling responsibility matters

Doing what you are responsible for matters because duties are the threads other people hang their lives on: the neighbour's window, the family's work, the class's trust. And when you fail — everyone fails sometimes — owning the failure is itself part of responsibility. The apology is not the opposite of duty; it is duty, continued after a fall.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the two cases as they are given — Ali fled, Faris apologised — and judge both, with reasons. Say only what the summary gives; the lesson is in the judging, not in invented detail.

## Do It Yourself

1. If anything of yours is unowned right now — a breakage, a missed duty — own it today: say it, apologise, begin the repair.
2. Do one duty today before you are asked.
3. Thank one person who owns their failures honestly, and tell them you noticed.
