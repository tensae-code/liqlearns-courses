# Our Moral Responsibilities

## Do it first

Count the duties on your fingers as you read them in this lesson — there are seven. By the end, hold up your hand and name them finger by finger. The traffic light in the moving picture will test one of them.

## The seven responsibilities

Our moral responsibilities, taught as one list: keep promises; help others; use resources well; take an active part in the community; pay tax; respect rules — including road safety; and care for the environment. It is a list to live from, not a sequence to climb: each duty stands on its own, and the list is practised with a traffic-light card activity — red, amber, green — judging acts by the duties.

## The civics part inside the list

Two items on the list are the civics part: paying tax, and respecting rules including road safety. They sit inside the moral list because that is where a child first meets them — as duties owed, like promises kept. The citizenship levels of this course take tax and rules up again in their own right; here, carry them as responsibilities you own.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion all seven moral responsibilities from memory. Then say which two are the civics part, and why they sit inside a moral list.

## Do It Yourself

1. Keep one promise today, exactly as given.
2. Use one resource well today — water, light, food, or materials — wasting nothing.
3. Play the traffic-light judgement at home: name three acts and judge each red, amber or green against the duties.
