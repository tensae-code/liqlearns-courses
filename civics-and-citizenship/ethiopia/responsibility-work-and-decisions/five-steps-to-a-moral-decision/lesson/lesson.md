# Five Steps to a Moral Decision

## Do it first

Choose one small real decision you face this week. Walk it across the five stones now, in your head, slowly: problem, options, outcomes, decide, and — later — review. Read each sub lesson standing on its stone.

## Sub lesson 1 — Identify the problem

Step one: identify the problem. Say clearly what the trouble is — not the noise around it, the problem itself. A decision aimed at the wrong problem solves nothing, however bravely it is carried out.

## Sub lesson 2 — Think of the options

Step two: think of the options. There are almost always more paths than the first one you saw. List them before judging any of them — an option never thought of can never be chosen.

## Sub lesson 3 — Identify each option's likely outcomes

Step three: for each option, identify its likely positive and negative outcomes. Look both ways from this stone, as the moving picture shows: what good might follow, what harm might follow, for you and for everyone the decision touches.

## Sub lesson 4 — Decide and follow through

Step four: decide — and follow through. Choosing is half the step; carrying the choice into action is the other half. A decision not followed through is a wish with a name.

## Sub lesson 5 — Review and evaluate what happened

Step five: review and evaluate what happened. Look back honestly: did the outcomes match the forecast? What does this teach the next decision? The fifth stone is where a decider becomes a better decider.

## Say it to your companion

Walk your companion through the five steps using one real small decision. Name each step as you cross to it.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the five steps on a card and keep it where you decide things.
2. Use all five steps on one real decision this week — including step five afterwards.
3. Teach the five steps to a friend using a decision of theirs.
