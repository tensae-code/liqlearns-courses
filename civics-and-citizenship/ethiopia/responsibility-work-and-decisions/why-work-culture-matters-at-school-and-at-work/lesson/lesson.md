# Why Work Culture Matters — At School and at Work

## Do it first

Put your school desk and a future work bench side by side in your mind. Draw one habit — good or bad — as a line running across both. Which habits of yours do you want on that line, and which do you want to break before it crosses?

## Sub lesson 1 — The work culture of a school

A school has a work culture: how lessons are met, how tasks are finished, how time is kept, how pupils treat shared work. It matters now — learning itself depends on it — and it matters later, because it is training. Every habit you build at the desk is a habit you are carrying toward the bench.

## Sub lesson 2 — The work culture of the world of work

The world of work has the same need: work valued, shown up for, finished properly. Habits formed at school carry into work — the pupil who finishes tasks properly becomes the worker who finishes jobs properly, and the pupil who drifts becomes the worker who drifts. That is why work culture matters in both fields: they are one road, walked at different ages.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion why work culture matters at school and at work, and the one sentence that joins them: habits formed in one carry into the other.

## Do It Yourself

1. Finish one school task today exactly the way a good worker would finish a job.
2. Keep one time promise at school today — arrive, start, finish on time.
3. Name one desk habit you intend to carry to the bench, and one you intend to leave behind.
