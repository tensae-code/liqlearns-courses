# Hands-on — Human rights classified — liberty, equality, solidarity

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
Three steps ascending. On the first, a speaker speaking freely. On the second, a pupil and a patient. On the third, a clean river valley everyone shares. Before reading: why would rights come in generations at all — like a family growing?

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — human rights fall into three generations: civil and political, economic social and cultural, and solidarity rights — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
