# Human rights instruments — world, Africa, nation

## Do it first

Three documents stacked as steps. The top one is for the whole world, written in 1948. The middle one is Africa's, from 1981. The bottom one is Ethiopia's own constitution. Before reading, decide: which step protects you most directly — and can any step stand without the others?

## Sub lesson 1 — International and regional

**Instruments** are the treaties, declarations, conventions and protocols that serve as legal sources for rights.

[Picture 28703 — three stacked documents as descending steps]

At the international level stands the **Universal Declaration of Human Rights, 1948 — 30 articles**, a common standard for all peoples and nations. At the regional level stands the **African Charter on Human and Peoples' Rights, 1981 — close to 68 articles**, carrying universal standards in African traditions' values.

National instruments complete the ladder — Ethiopia's floor is the next sub lesson.

## Sub lesson 2 — The dated ladder and the national floor

The full dated ladder, as evidence depth: **ICERD 1965; ICCPR and ICESCR 1966; CEDAW 1979; CAT 1984; CRC 1989; ICMW 1990; CPED and CRPD 2006** — and Africa's own **Charter on the Rights and Welfare of the Child, 1990**.

[Picture 28704 — a timeline wall from 1948 to 2006 with dated plaques]

At home, the floor is **Chapter 3, Articles 14–44, of the FDRE Constitution** — Ethiopia's own catalogue of rights, standing under the world and regional steps and giving them force in Ethiopian courts and offices.
