# Hands-on — Human rights instruments — world, Africa, nation

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
Three documents stacked as steps. The top one is for the whole world, written in 1948. The middle one is Africa's, from 1981. The bottom one is Ethiopia's own constitution. Before reading, decide: which step protects you most directly — and can any step stand without the others?

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — human rights are written down in instruments at three levels: international, regional and national — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
