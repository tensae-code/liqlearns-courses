# Lesson plan — Human rights challenges in Ethiopia — traditions, poverty, flight

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Human Rights · **Lesson:** Human rights challenges in Ethiopia — traditions, poverty, flight
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 19 — Human Rights (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** rights in Ethiopia are challenged by harmful traditional practices, by poverty and child labour, and by displacement, weak institutions and missing democracy.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the levels before it in this course's path order (recommended, never forced); inside its own title it stands on the lessons before it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
1. **Harmful traditional practices** — Practices held for generations persist even while violating rights, because they **look moral to those who practise them**.
2. **Poverty, inequality and child labour** — Persistent poverty itself violates rights: **hunger is a rights violation**, and a hungry person cannot easily respect others' rights.
3. **Displacement, weak institutions, missing democracy** — Goal-incompatible conflicts — ethnic, religious, ideological — plus violence, disasters and development projects **drive people from their homes**, violating rights in the flight itself.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. List the harmful practices as the book lists them.
2. State hunger's place in rights language.
3. Explain why rights and democracy need each other.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.
