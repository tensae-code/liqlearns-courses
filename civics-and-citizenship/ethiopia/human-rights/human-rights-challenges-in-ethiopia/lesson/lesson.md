# Human rights challenges in Ethiopia — traditions, poverty, flight

## Do it first

Three photographs. An elder, a mother and a health worker talking, a clinic door open behind them. A child at a loom, an empty school desk through a doorway. A family carrying bundles away from smoke. Three challenges to everything this level has taught. Name them as you read.

## Sub lesson 1 — Harmful traditional practices

Practices held for generations persist even while violating rights, because they **look moral to those who practise them**. Against women: **female genital mutilation, early marriage, marriage by abduction, forced marriage, wife inheritance, polygamy**. The book's abduction role play ends pointedly: elders try to legitimise the act, and the book insists **a court decision must finally govern**.

[Picture 28714 — an elder, a mother and a health worker in discussion]

Against bodies: **uvulotomy, milk-teeth extraction, food taboos** withholding white foods — milk, fatty meat, porridge, banana, colostrum, fruit — from pregnant women and children, **eyelid incision**, and **cauterisation ("tattate")** for infections, headache, tuberculosis and fractures.

## Sub lesson 2 — Poverty, inequality and child labour

Persistent poverty itself violates rights: **hunger is a rights violation**, and a hungry person cannot easily respect others' rights. Inequality benches half the society: women's inferior assignment is named, against **Article 35/8**'s guarantee of equality in employment, promotion, pay and pension transfer.

[Picture 28715 — a child at a loom with an empty school desk through a doorway]

**Child labour** causes physical and mental harm and death, can become slavery and sexual or economic exploitation, and steals schooling and health care — against **Article 36**'s protection of children from exploitative and hazardous work. Evidence depth anchors the child-rights part in the **UN Convention on the Rights of the Child, 1989**, and states plainly that hunger by itself is a violation of human rights.

## Sub lesson 3 — Displacement, weak institutions, missing democracy

Goal-incompatible conflicts — ethnic, religious, ideological — plus violence, disasters and development projects **drive people from their homes**, violating rights in the flight itself.

[Picture 28716 — a family carrying bundles away from smoke]

[Picture 28717 — a child carrying water past a closed school gate]

Rights also need **investigators**: the Commission, the Ombudsman, public defenders and their kin. Where institutions are weak, violations go unanswered. And rights and democracy are **mutually constitutive**: only a democracy that accommodates diversity, promotes equality and protects liberty reliably hedges against concentrated power and its abuses — so Ethiopia's democratic shortfall is named a daily barrier to rights.
