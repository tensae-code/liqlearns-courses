# Hands-on — Human rights challenges in Ethiopia — traditions, poverty, flight

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
Three photographs. An elder, a mother and a health worker talking, a clinic door open behind them. A child at a loom, an empty school desk through a doorway. A family carrying bundles away from smoke. Three challenges to everything this level has taught. Name them as you read.

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — rights in Ethiopia are challenged by harmful traditional practices, by poverty and child labour, and by displacement, weak institutions and missing democracy — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
