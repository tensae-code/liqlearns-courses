# Hands-on — Why human rights must be respected

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
One town scene. A school with every child inside. A clinic queue treated strictly in order. Two faiths' buildings standing peacefully side by side. Nothing in the scene is rich or grand — so why does it feel like a good town? This lesson answers.

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — respecting human rights builds the society people want — securing democracy, peace, basic needs and equal opportunity — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
