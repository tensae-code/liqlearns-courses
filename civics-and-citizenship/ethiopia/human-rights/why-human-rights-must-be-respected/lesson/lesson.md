# Why human rights must be respected

## Do it first

One town scene. A school with every child inside. A clinic queue treated strictly in order. Two faiths' buildings standing peacefully side by side. Nothing in the scene is rich or grand — so why does it feel like a good town? This lesson answers.

## What respect builds

Respect for human rights builds the society people want, at family, community, school, workplace, political and world levels. It **secures democracy, justice and growth**. It is a **prerequisite of lasting peace and development**. It **ensures basic needs** are met. It **protects vulnerable groups**, such as women and children. It **frees religious practice**. It **opens equal work opportunity and education**. And it gives a **universal standard** by which governments are held accountable.

The negative is stated just as plainly: without equality — with citizens discriminated against by ethnicity — lasting peace is unthinkable. In Ethiopia specifically, respect is framed as a national asset: dignity at home, peace between communities, and the country's standing in the world.
