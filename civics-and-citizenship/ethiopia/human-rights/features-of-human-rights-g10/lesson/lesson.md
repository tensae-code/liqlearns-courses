# Features of Human Rights

## Do it first

Six rings, linked in a chain so each passes through the others. Try to remove one ring in your mind without opening the rest. You cannot — and the book's picture of human rights' features is exactly that chain. Before reading, guess: what six marks could make rights impossible to take apart?

## The six marks

Six salient features distinguish human rights from other rights. **Universalism**: applying equally to all people everywhere, without time limit and without distinction — and expressly **not uniformity**: diversity requires a world where everyone is equal and equally respected, and each state is free to set higher standards. **Eternality**: enjoyed until natural death; in principle eternal. 

[Picture 28957 — Six interlocking rings lettered with the six features, no ring removable alone]



**Inalienability**: they cannot be lost, being linked to human existence itself — though the book keeps its qualification honestly: some rights may be **suspended or restricted** in particular circumstances, as a convicted person's liberty, or derogated in a publicly declared emergency, such as a curfew. **Indivisibility and interdependence**: rights intrinsically connected, none more important; one set not fully enjoyable without the other — progress in civil and political rights easing economic, social and cultural ones, and vice versa.

**Inviolability**: by nature not to be violated or even restricted by anyone — a logic some consolidated democracies extend even to crises. **Irrevocability**: they cannot be taken away by any power or authority, originating in man's social nature, akin to moral rights. Six rings, one chain: the picture is the argument.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: the six marks in order, with the qualification inside inalienability spoken aloud — not hidden. Then say why the chain cannot lose one ring.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the six features on six cards and link them in a drawn chain. Under each, one line: what breaks in the chain if this ring is cut?
2. Universalism is not uniformity. Write two sentences proving you understand the difference, using school standards as your example.
3. Find the qualification: a convicted person's liberty; a curfew in a declared emergency. Write why the book can hold inalienability AND these, without contradiction.
