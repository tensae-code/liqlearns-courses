# Lesson plan — Human rights under Ethiopia's regional constitutions

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Human Rights · **Lesson:** Human rights under Ethiopia's regional constitutions
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 19 — Human Rights (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** every regional constitution copies the federal rights catalogue in substance — no region may fall below the federal floor.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the levels before it in this course's path order (recommended, never forced); inside its own title it stands on the lessons before it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
1. **One floor for all** — Regional states, empowered by **FDRE Article 52/2b** and bound by **Article 9(1)** supremacy, adopted their own constitutions — **most in 1995; Benishangul-Gumuz and Gambella in 1996; Afar in 1998; Sidama in 2020** (the guide's keying names Oromia, Tigray, SNNPRS, Somali, Harar and Amhara in 1995).
2. **Case audit: Ato Tona** — The case, in summary.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The right to equality. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the federal floor rule and its articles.
2. Give the adoption years.
3. Audit the Ato Tona case article by article.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Nine regional constitutions stand under one federal constitution. In each, one chapter glows the same colour as the federal rights chapter. Before reading: may a region write fewer rights than the federal floor? May it write more?
2. **The explanation** — the lesson's parts in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 28711]: a picture of a federal constitution above nine regional constitutions, a single rights chapter glowing identically in each — the student says what may vary and what may never fall.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 28712]: a picture of a map of Ethiopia's regions, each stamped with its constitution's year — the student finds 1995, 1996, 1998 and 2020 on the map.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 28713]: a picture of a citizen standing between a police station and a courthouse, both doors marked with the rights at stake — the student names two rights on the doors.
   - ADD AUDIO: none named in the placements for this lesson — no audio slot is built.
3. **Say it to your companion** — explain the one thing in your own words, with the lesson's own case (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, matching, fill-in-the-blank — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the closing doing block (lesson/lesson.md).
