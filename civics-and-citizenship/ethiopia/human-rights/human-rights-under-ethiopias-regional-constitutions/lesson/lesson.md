# Human rights under Ethiopia's regional constitutions

## Do it first

Nine regional constitutions stand under one federal constitution. In each, one chapter glows the same colour as the federal rights chapter. Before reading: may a region write fewer rights than the federal floor? May it write more?

## Sub lesson 1 — One floor for all

Regional states, empowered by **FDRE Article 52/2b** and bound by **Article 9(1)** supremacy, adopted their own constitutions — **most in 1995; Benishangul-Gumuz and Gambella in 1996; Afar in 1998; Sidama in 2020** (the guide's keying names Oromia, Tigray, SNNPRS, Somali, Harar and Amhara in 1995).

[Picture 28711 — a federal constitution above nine regional constitutions]

Their **third chapters copy the federal rights catalogue in substance**: life, security, liberty, freedom from inhuman treatment, the rights of arrested, accused and detained persons, non-retroactivity, no double jeopardy, honour, equality, privacy, religion and conscience, crimes against humanity — all three rights generations included. Some regions protect **further** — broader movement rights, and a right to regional public office everywhere except Afar and Somali; Afar and Somali tailor economic and social provisions to regional life.

But one rule binds all: **no region may fall below the federal floor**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Case audit: Ato Tona

The case, in summary. **Ato Tona**, a respected 78-year-old Wolaita elder, is attacked on his way home. Then the protection fails from the other side: he is detained long without court, beaten unseen, denied family and counsel, and tried in private — while his attackers are freed. He is bailed only after community leaders intervene.

Audit it article by article, as the book does: which rights in the catalogue — liberty, freedom from inhuman treatment, access to court, family and counsel, public trial — were violated, one by one? That audit is this lesson's real examination.
