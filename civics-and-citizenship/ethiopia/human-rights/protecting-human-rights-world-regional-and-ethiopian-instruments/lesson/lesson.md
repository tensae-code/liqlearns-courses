# Protecting Human Rights — World, Regional and Ethiopian Instruments

## Do it first

A promise written on paper protects no one by itself. It needs layers: a declaration the world agreed, treaties states signed, regional shields, and at home a constitution with institutions that have doors and desks. Count the layers you can name before reading; this lesson builds all of them in order.

## Sub lesson 1 — The UDHR and the treaties

The **Universal Declaration of Human Rights** was adopted by the UN General Assembly on **10 December 1948**. It is **not legally binding** — hold that precisely — but it defines the 'human rights' and 'fundamental freedoms' of UN Charter Article 55(c) that all member states are obliged to observe, and it endures and influences: its principles are incorporated into many national constitutions, and it founded later core treaties, regional treaties and national laws. 

[Picture 28964 — One declaration scroll at the centre with treaty booklets radiating from it by year]



The treaty family the book names — more than ten core instruments — includes the Genocide Convention (1948), the Refugee Convention (1951), the Slavery Convention (1926, amended 1953), the **ICCPR** and the **ICESCR** (both 1966), CAT, ICERD, CEDAW, the CRC and the Migrant Workers Convention. The ICCPR stands in that list **once**, dated 1966 — the book prints it twice, and sources record the correction.

## Sub lesson 2 — Regional systems and Ethiopia's own

Beside the UN regime stand **regional human-rights systems for Africa, the Americas and Europe** — both layers aiming to make states fulfil their obligations to promote, protect, provide and fulfil. 

[Picture 28965 — Three shields lettered Africa, Americas, Europe above an Ethiopian courthouse and commission building]



Ethiopia's foundation is the **FDRE Constitution** — supreme law and source of legality — which classifies human rights among its five fundamental principles, and by **Articles 9(4) and 13**, as the book gives them, makes ratified international agreements integral to the law of the land and requires constitutional rights to be interpreted in conformity with the UDHR, the International Covenants and instruments Ethiopia has adopted.

And the constitution requires the institutions that give the words doors and desks: a **national human rights commission**, the **attorney general's office** and the **ombudsman** — with an independent, resourced judiciary. One dating guard: the book's passage calls the constitution 'ratified in 1994'; this course teaches it as the **1995 FDRE Constitution** — adopted December 1994 — and the book's wording is named in sources only.
