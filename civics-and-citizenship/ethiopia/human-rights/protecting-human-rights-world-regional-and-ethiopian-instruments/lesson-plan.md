# Lesson plan — Protecting Human Rights — World, Regional and Ethiopian Instruments

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Human Rights · **Lesson:** Protecting Human Rights — World, Regional and Ethiopian Instruments
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 19 — Human Rights (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** rights are protected by instruments in three layers — the world's: the UDHR of 1948 and its treaty family; the regions': Africa, the Americas, Europe; and Ethiopia's own: the FDRE Constitution and the institutions it requires.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the constitution level — the learner knows the frame that carries rights; this level teaches the rights themselves: what they are, their marks, their generations, their obligations and their protection.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The UDHR and the treaties** — The world layer: the Declaration that defined the words, and the treaties that followed it.
2. **Regional systems and Ethiopia's own** — The regional shields, and home ground: the constitution as foundation, Articles 9(4) and 13, and the required institutions.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Obligations for Human Rights — Citizens and the State, just before this lesson in this level. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what the UDHR is, its date, and its legal character — precisely.
2. Name the treaty family the book lists.
3. Name the three regional systems, Ethiopia's foundation, and the institutions the constitution requires.

## If the learner is stuck
- Calls the UDHR legally binding → the book is precise: not legally binding, but defining the Charter's terms that member states are obliged to observe, and founding the treaties that followed.
- Lists the ICCPR twice, copying the book → it is listed once, dated 1966; the book's double printing is recorded in sources, as the design directs.
