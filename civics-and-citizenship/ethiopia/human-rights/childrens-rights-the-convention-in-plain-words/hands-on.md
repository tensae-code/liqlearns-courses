# Hands-on — Children's rights — the Convention in plain words

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
A child falls asleep in a house where she has been neglected and hurt. In the night, something magical happens: one by one, the rights that should have protected her come to show themselves. Before reading, list what a child needs protection for — then compare your list with the Convention's.

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — the Convention on the Rights of the Child sets out, article by article, the rights that protect every child — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
