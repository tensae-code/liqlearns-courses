# Protecting rights at home — institutions and the state's three duties

## Do it first

Two office doors in one corridor: 'Human Rights Commission' and 'Ombudsman'. A citizen with a complaint walks toward them. Before reading: what are those offices for — and what must the state itself do, beyond keeping offices open?

## Sub lesson 1 — Institutions at home

A **third of Ethiopia's 1995 Constitution** is devoted to rights and freedoms, and it is to be read consistently with the international instruments Ethiopia has adopted.

[Picture 28705 — two office doors beside three verbs]

Two national institutions bridge citizens, civil society and government: the **Ethiopian Human Rights Commission** and the **Ethiopian Institution of the Ombudsman**. They receive complaints, investigate, and press for rights to be honoured where the citizen alone might not be heard.

## Sub lesson 2 — The state's three duties: respect, protect, fulfil

The state itself owes three duties. **Respect:** take no action against treaty rights — not stopping speech, leaving worship alone. **Protect:** act so no one is denied rights — a prisoner must not be beaten; citizens are defended against terrorist attack. **Fulfil:** take positive steps beyond prevention — rights education campaigns, free or affordable schooling where citizens cannot reach it themselves; schools, hospitals and business licensing are rights in daily dress.

[Picture 28706 — three panels: stepping back, shielding, building a clinic]

Respect is the state stepping back. Protect is the state standing guard. Fulfil is the state building. A state that does only the first has done a third of its job.
