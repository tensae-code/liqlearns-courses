# Hands-on — Protecting rights at home — institutions and the state's three duties

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
Two office doors in one corridor: 'Human Rights Commission' and 'Ombudsman'. A citizen with a complaint walks toward them. Before reading: what are those offices for — and what must the state itself do, beyond keeping offices open?

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — at home, rights are guarded by the Human Rights Commission and the Ombudsman, and by the state's three duties: respect, protect, fulfil — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
