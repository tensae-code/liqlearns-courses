# The right to equality

## Do it first

A principal's office. People are served strictly in the order they arrived — no pushing in for the important, no waiting longer for the unknown. Before reading: is strict order the whole of equality? This lesson says it is the start, not the whole.

## Sub lesson 1 — The same service, in the same order

**Equality** is equal treatment and service from government and institutions, and equal opportunity to make the most of one's life and talents, regardless of birth, belief or disability.

[Picture 28709 — a service counter queue served strictly in turn]

**FDRE Article 25** guarantees equality before the law **without discrimination** — the forbidden grounds are listed in full: gender, colour, creed, social status, parentage, nationality, race, language, religion, political or other opinion, wealth and other status.

The principal's-office queue is the picture: same service, same order, for everyone.

## Sub lesson 2 — The two affirmative duties

But sameness alone can freeze an unfair starting line in place. So the constitution adds two affirmative duties. **Special consideration for women**, so they can compete equally — **Article 35/2**. And **special assistance to the least-favoured nations, nationalities and peoples** — **Article 89/4**.

[Picture 28710 — very different people standing on one level line marked Article 25]

The rule first; then the tempering duties that make the rule real for those who start furthest back.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson's one thing to your companion in your own words: equality means the same treatment and the same opportunity — tempered by special consideration for women and special assistance to the least-favoured. Use the lesson's own case or example, not the book's sentences. Your companion asks one hard question; answer it from the lesson, or say honestly what you would check.
