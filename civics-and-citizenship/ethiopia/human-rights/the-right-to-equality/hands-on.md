# Hands-on — The right to equality

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
A principal's office. People are served strictly in the order they arrived — no pushing in for the important, no waiting longer for the unknown. Before reading: is strict order the whole of equality? This lesson says it is the start, not the whole.

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — equality means the same treatment and the same opportunity — tempered by special consideration for women and special assistance to the least-favoured — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
