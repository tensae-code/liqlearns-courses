# Lesson plan — Generations of Human Rights

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Human Rights · **Lesson:** Generations of Human Rights
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 19 — Human Rights (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** rights grew in three generations — first, civil and political; second, economic, social and cultural; third, solidarity — three trees of increasing breadth, planted in 1789, 1917 and 1970 soil.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the constitution level — the learner knows the frame that carries rights; this level teaches the rights themselves: what they are, their marks, their generations, their obligations and their protection.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Features of Human Rights — indivisibility will be needed to read the generations rightly. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the three generations in historical order, with their contents.
2. Give each generation's roots — revolutions, socialist traditions, the late twentieth century.
3. State the UDHR article ranges the book assigns to the first two generations.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

2. **The explanation** — first generation — civil and political, UDHR 2–21; second — economic, social, cultural, UDHR 22–27; third — solidarity rights, still debated; the guide's evidence portrait (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Memorises the guide's labels instead of the book's → the generations are taught corrected, matching the student book: first civil and political; second economic, social, cultural; third solidarity. The guide's mislabels live in sources only.
- Ranks the generations by importance → arrival order is history, not rank; indivisibility (previous lesson) forbids the ranking.
