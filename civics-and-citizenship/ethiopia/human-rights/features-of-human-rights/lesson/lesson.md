# Features of human rights — for all, inborn, untakeable, joined

## Do it first

Four rings, linked in one chain. Pull any one ring out and the chain breaks. Before reading, guess what the four rings are: what must be true of a right for it to protect a stranger, a prisoner, and you, equally?

## The four features as one set

**Universal** — held by all, everywhere, without discrimination of race, colour, sex, origin, religion, language, nationality, age or disability.

**Inherent** — not granted, bought, earned or inherited. Rights are rooted in human dignity, so no government gives you your free speech; you are born holding it.

**Inalienable** — no one may take them away, and you cannot surrender them. The one exception is the book's own: liberty may be limited through **due process** when a court finds a person guilty.

**Indivisible and interdependent** — each right leans on others. Life needs food and a decent standard; denied education blocks access to justice and public life. Cut one right, and its neighbours bleed.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson's one thing to your companion in your own words: human rights are universal, inherent, inalienable, and indivisible and interdependent. Use the lesson's own case or example, not the book's sentences. Your companion asks one hard question; answer it from the lesson, or say honestly what you would check.

## Do It Yourself

1. Practise this until you can do it without looking: define each of the four features.
2. Practise this until you can do it without looking: give the one exception to inalienability.
3. Practise this until you can do it without looking: show how rights lean on each other.
4. Write the lesson's one thing in one sentence of your own: human rights are universal, inherent, inalienable, and indivisible and interdependent. Keep the sentence; it is your proof you were here.
