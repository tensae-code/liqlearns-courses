## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define each of the four features.
2. Give the one exception to inalienability.
3. Show how rights lean on each other.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Four rings, linked in one chain. Pull any one ring out and the chain breaks. Before reading, guess what the four rings are: what must be true of a right for it to protect a stranger, a prisoner, and you, equally?
2. **The explanation** — the lesson's parts in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 28696]: a picture of four linked rings labelled universal, inherent, inalienable, indivisible, none removable without breaking the chain — the student names each ring and its meaning.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 28697]: a picture of four rings linked in one chain over a crowd of every age and place — the student says why the crowd is drawn under the chain.
   - ADD AUDIO: none named in the placements for this lesson — no audio slot is built.
3. **Say it to your companion** — explain the one thing in your own words, with the lesson's own case (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
