# Obligations for Human Rights — Citizens and the State

## Do it first

A seesaw: on one seat, 'my rights'; on the other, 'my duties'. Tip it either way in your mind and watch what falls off. The book teaches that the constitution which entitles also obliges — and that the state sits on a seesaw of its own, with three weights to carry. This lesson loads both.

## Sub lesson 1 — Citizens' obligations

Human rights involve **responsibility toward other people and the community**: individuals must exercise their rights with due regard for others' rights. The obligation arises from the right enjoyed — **the constitution that entitles also obliges**. 

[Picture 28961 — A seesaw balanced with 'my rights' and 'my duties' at equal weight]



The citizen's named obligations: respect for **religious equality**; respect for **gender equality**; respect for the **rights of others**; and respect for the **national flag**. Note the pattern: three of the four are respect paid outward, to other people's equality and rights — the duty-side of living among rights-holders.

One honesty guard travels with this sub lesson, stated fully in sources: both books illustrate the respect-duty with disability-convention examples. The duty itself is taught **generally**, as the design directs — the examples stay examples.

## Sub lesson 2 — State obligations — respect, protect, fulfil

The state carries the **prime responsibility** to protect, promote and implement all human rights, in three obligations. **Respect**: refraining from interfering with the enjoyment of rights. **Protect**: shielding individuals against abuses by non-state actors, foreign agents, or state agents acting outside their capacity — preventively and remedially, by legislation, action on known threats, and impartial legal remedies. **Fulfil**: positive action creating the legal, institutional and procedural conditions for full enjoyment — its extent varying with the right and available resources. 

[Picture 28962 — Three concentric guards around one child at a school desk lettered Respect, Protect, Fulfil]



The book works protect and fulfil through **education**: education protected from interference and indoctrination by third parties — including parents, teachers, religions, sects, clans and firms — is protect's worked example; and for fulfilment in education: free compulsory primary schooling, free secondary and higher education, vocational and adult education, enough schools and paid teachers.

The guide's evidence picture draws the three as doors the state must walk through, a citizen holding the last door open. 

[Picture 28963 — Three verbs as three doors a state must walk through, a citizen holding the last door open (teacher guide — evidence)]

 The citizen's obligations and the state's meet in that doorway.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: your four obligations as a citizen; the state's three as a state. Then open the guide's doorway: what does the citizen holding the last door mean?

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the four citizen obligations as four promises, in the first person, each with one concrete act it requires of you this month.
2. Sort six state actions (you invent them) into respect, protect, fulfil. Defend the two hardest aloud.
3. Take fulfil in education: free compulsory primary schooling; free secondary and higher education; vocational and adult education; enough schools and paid teachers. Score your locality on each, with evidence.
