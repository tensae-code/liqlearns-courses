# Lesson plan — Obligations for Human Rights — Citizens and the State

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Human Rights · **Lesson:** Obligations for Human Rights — Citizens and the State
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 19 — Human Rights (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** rights arrive with duties on both sides — citizens owe respect: for religious and gender equality, for others' rights, for the flag; the state owes three obligations: respect, protect, fulfil.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the constitution level — the learner knows the frame that carries rights; this level teaches the rights themselves: what they are, their marks, their generations, their obligations and their protection.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Citizens' obligations** — The citizen's side: obligations arising from rights, the four named respects.
2. **State obligations — respect, protect, fulfil** — The state's side: prime responsibility in three obligations — respect (hands off), protect (shield), fulfil (build the conditions).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Human Rights in Ethiopia's Constitution, just before this lesson in this level. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name citizens' four named obligations.
2. Name the state's three obligations and define each.
3. Give the book's worked example of protect — education defended from interference.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Teaches citizens' duties but skips the state's prime responsibility → the state carries the prime responsibility, in three obligations; citizens' duties do not replace them.
- Makes the disability examples the whole content of the respect-duty → the duty is taught generally; both books' disability-convention examples remain examples, as the design directs and sources record.
