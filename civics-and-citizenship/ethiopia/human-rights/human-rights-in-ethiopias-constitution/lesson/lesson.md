# Human Rights in Ethiopia's Constitution

## Do it first

Open a big book at its table of contents in your mind. One whole chapter — a third of everything — is given to a single subject. What does that proportion announce before a single article is read? This lesson opens Ethiopia's constitution at exactly that chapter.

## Chapter Three

A distinguishing feature of the FDRE Constitution is its emphasis on internationally recognised norms. **Article 10** declares human rights and freedoms, **emanating from the nature of mankind, inviolable and inalienable** — the same words the features lesson taught, now standing in a constitution. 

[Picture 28960 — The constitution opened at Chapter Three with the sampled rights as a contents column]



**Chapter Three** carries the long list — **one-third of the constitution's provisions** devoted to human rights and democratic rights. Articles **13–44** cover 'Fundamental Rights and Freedoms': Articles **14–28** named **'Human Rights'**, and Articles **29–44** named **'Democratic Rights'**.

The book's table samples the human-rights side: rights to life; security of person; liberty; rights of persons in custody and of convicted prisoners; rights of arrested and accused persons; prohibition of inhuman treatment; non-retroactivity of criminal law; honour and reputation; prohibition of double jeopardy; equality; freedom of religion, belief and opinion; privacy; and crimes against humanity. Read the sample slowly — it is a map of what the constitution refuses to let happen to a person.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: the chapter, the range, the proportion, and the two halves with their ranges. Then sample five rights from the table without looking.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw the constitution as a pie: shade the third that is Chapter Three. Inside the shaded part, draw the dividing line between Arts. 14–28 and 29–44.
2. Take the sampled table. For each right, write in plain words the bad event it forbids. Keep the list — it is the chapter read backwards.
3. Article 10 uses 'inviolable and inalienable'. Find both words in the features lesson and write what each adds here, in a constitution, that it did not have in a definition.
