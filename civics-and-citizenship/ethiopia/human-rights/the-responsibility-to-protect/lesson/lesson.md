# The responsibility to protect — state first, world behind it

## Do it first

Two shields, one inside the other. The inner shield is a village's own state. The outer shield is the world. Before reading, answer honestly: if the inner shield itself turns on the village, may the outer shield act — or is that nobody else's business?

## State first, world behind it

For a long time, rights were treated as each state's internal affair — even amid the gravest violations. The **Responsibility to Protect** replaced that view. It was approved **unanimously by UN heads of state and government on 16 September 2005**.

[Picture 28701 — a village inside two concentric shields]

[Picture 28702 — a shield held jointly by village, government and nations]

Its rule: each state must protect its own people — Ethiopia included — from four crimes: **genocide, war crimes, ethnic cleansing and crimes against humanity**.

And if a state fails its own people, the international community bears the moral responsibility to act **collectively, in time and decisively, through the Security Council**. State first. World behind it. No people left shieldless.
