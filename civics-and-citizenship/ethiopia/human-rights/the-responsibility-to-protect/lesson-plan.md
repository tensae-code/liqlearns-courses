# Lesson plan — The responsibility to protect — state first, world behind it

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Human Rights · **Lesson:** The responsibility to protect — state first, world behind it
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 19 — Human Rights (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** each state must protect its own people from four crimes; if it fails, the world must act collectively through the Security Council.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Why human rights must be respected. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the four crimes.
2. State the date and the unanimous adoption.
3. Explain who acts when a state fails.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Two shields, one inside the other. The inner shield is a village's own state. The outer shield is the world. Before reading, answer honestly: if the inner shield itself turns on the village, may the outer shield act — or is that nobody else's business?
2. **The explanation** — the lesson's parts in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 28701]: a picture of a village inside two concentric shields, the inner labelled the state, the outer the United Nations — the student says which shield acts first, and when the outer one may act.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 28702]: a picture of a shield held jointly by a village, a government and a ring of nations — the student names the three holders in order of duty.
   - ADD AUDIO: none named in the placements for this lesson — no audio slot is built.
