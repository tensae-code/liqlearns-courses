# Hands-on — The responsibility to protect — state first, world behind it

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
Two shields, one inside the other. The inner shield is a village's own state. The outer shield is the world. Before reading, answer honestly: if the inner shield itself turns on the village, may the outer shield act — or is that nobody else's business?

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — each state must protect its own people from four crimes; if it fails, the world must act collectively through the Security Council — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
