# Lesson plan — Opportunities for Young Citizens Online

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** The Digital Citizen · **Lesson:** Opportunities for Young Citizens Online
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 22 — The Digital Citizen (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** for young citizens, digital technology opens five opportunities: information, connectivity, learning, work, and innovation.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the citizenship levels before it — the learner already knows what a citizen owes and is owed; this level teaches the same citizen how to live in the digital world.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the gains-and-harms lessons of this level — the doors open widest for those who know the harms too. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the five opportunities.
2. Explain why innovation grows out of the other four.
3. Plan one real use of one opportunity this month.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner pictures the five doors before they are named (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the five opportunities in order, and how the fifth grows out of the other four (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 28822]: a picture of a young person at one desk reaching toward five doors lettered Information, People, Learning, Work, Invention — the student opens each door aloud with one real example.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 28823]: a picture of a young person at a phone with five open doors around them: a library, friends, a class, a workshop, a market — the student matches each door to its opportunity.
3. **Say it to your companion** — explain the lesson's one thing in your own words (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the lesson retold in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: fill-in-the-blank, matching, multiple-choice, short-answer, true-or-false, writing — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the doing block at the end of lesson/lesson.md.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the opportunities are only for city youth → the doors are digital; distance is exactly what they abolish.
- Thinks innovation needs genius first → the lesson grows it from information, connectivity and learning — ground any young citizen can stand on.
