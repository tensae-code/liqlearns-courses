# Opportunities for Young Citizens Online

## Do it first

Imagine five doors in front of you, lettered Information, People, Learning, Work, Invention. Behind each stands something a young person in a village once had to migrate to find. Open them one by one as this lesson names them.

## Five doors

**Information access.** The young citizen gets a window on their own country and the whole world; open access enables collaboration, and makes the user part of the world's people, not a spectator of them. 

[Picture 28822 — One desk, five doors — Information, People, Learning, Work, Invention]



**Social connectivity.** Connection across time and space — and more than company: empowerment to participate fully in the digital world as an informed citizen. **Learning opportunities.** A huge share of the world's knowledge; virtual lessons and courses; learning directly from sources, foreign events and languages without travel; short trainings; skills built by video, audio and search.

**Versatile working opportunities.** Work itself is transformed: remote working from hundreds or thousands of miles away; searching and applying for jobs online. **Innovation and creative opportunities.** And here the five join: information, connectivity and digital learning are the fertile ground — the exposure — from which young citizens innovate. 

[Picture 28823 — Five open doors from one phone (teacher guide — evidence)]



Read the list as a ladder, not a heap. Information feeds connection; connection feeds learning; learning feeds work; and all four together feed the fifth — the young person who stops only consuming the digital world and starts adding to it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the five opportunities in order — then say which door stands most open to you today, and which you must push.

## Do It Yourself

1. Behind each door, write one concrete thing available to you this month: a source, a community, a course, a job board, a making-tool.
2. Spend one hour this week inside the Learning door on a skill you chose. Note what you can do now that you could not do before.
3. Name one thing you could *make* — a page, a recording, a service — with the other four doors' help. Write its first step.
