# Culture and Digital Technology

## Do it first

Think of one thing from your own culture — a song, a dress, a dish, a holiday — that you have seen on a phone screen. Did the screen honour it or cheapen it? Hold that picture in mind; this lesson is about the two forces meeting.

## Two forces on each other

Culture and technology are two equal forces, and each shapes the other. They shape our outlooks, our attitudes, and our judgements of good and bad. Cultural values underlie civilisation itself — and every technical advance too. And every new technology that arrives is received by a culture, positively or negatively: welcomed, resisted, or bent out of shape. 

[Picture 28802 — A cultural festival filmed and shared with pride]



Culture lives by communication. A culture is learned, transmitted and preserved through people talking, singing, showing and telling — and that is exactly what digital media does at great speed. So digital media can carry a culture to the whole world.

Here is the invitation: Ethiopia's nations and nationalities can promote their language, dress, food, arts, historic sites and holiday celebrations through YouTube, Facebook, Twitter, Telegram and IMO — responsibly and ethically, so the world meets the real culture, carried by its own people. 

[Picture 28803 — A family table where two phones have silenced the meal]



But the warning stands beside the invitation. Deviating use — use that breaks away from the culture's own values — dissolves indigenous values. Picture a couple at a meal, both absorbed in social media, ignoring their own child at the table with them: the screens are full, and the family is empty.

So the rule is drawn plainly: digital culture must stay in line with the society's moral, social and cultural values. The technology is the guest; the culture is the house.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion one way digital media could carry your culture to the world — and the rule that keeps the carrying honest.

## Do It Yourself

1. Choose one item of your local culture — a song, a dish, a dress, a site. Write how you would present it online so its own people would be proud.
2. For one meal, note every time a screen pulls someone at the table away. Count honestly; the lesson's warning is about exactly this.
3. List two values of your community. For each, write one digital habit that honours it and one that breaks it.
