# Lesson plan — Culture and Digital Technology

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** The Digital Citizen · **Lesson:** Culture and Digital Technology
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 22 — The Digital Citizen (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** culture and digital technology shape each other — so a people's digital life must stay in line with its own moral, social and cultural values.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the citizenship levels before it — the learner already knows what a citizen owes and is owed; this level teaches the same citizen how to live in the digital world.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Digital Technology — What It Is, just before this lesson in this level. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say how culture and technology act on each other.
2. Name what Ethiopia's peoples can carry to the world through digital media.
3. State the rule: digital culture stays in line with society's values.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner recalls a piece of their own culture seen on a screen and judges how the screen treated it (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — culture and technology as two forces on each other; carrying Ethiopia's cultures to the world; the danger of deviating use; the standing rule (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 28802]: a picture of a cultural festival with dancers in local dress, one person filming it proudly on a phone — the student says what the film carries to the world and who it honours.

4. **Questions** — from questions/: fill-in-the-blank, matching, multiple-choice, short-answer, true-or-false, writing — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks culture and technology are enemies → they are two forces shaping each other; the lesson's rule is alignment, not rejection.
- Cannot name what digital media can carry → language, dress, food, arts, historic sites, holidays.
