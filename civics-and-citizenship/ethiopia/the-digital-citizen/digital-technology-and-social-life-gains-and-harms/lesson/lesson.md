# Digital Technology and Social Life — Gains and Harms

## Do it first

List the errands your family no longer walks to do — paying, booking, asking, sending. Each one moved onto a screen. Now ask: what did the street lose when the errands left it? Begin with both lists open.

## Sub lesson 1 — The gains — seven

**Increased social connectivity** — platforms and phones make new connections and keep users informed from the local to the international. **Speedy communication** — broadband moves large data; real-time audio and video, anywhere. **Advancing education** — academic information shared, virtual laboratories, learning directly from sources. 

[Picture 28818 — Seven panels across one town street]



**Facilitating transportation** — online timetables and booking; digital-chip passports speeding check-in and customs; vehicles bookable from a chair. **Advancing banking and finance** — online and mobile banking, remote transfers and bill payment, dealing across borders. **Improving health care** — diagnostic tools, appointment and medication reminders, complete patient histories, vital-signs monitoring, specialists reachable anywhere in the world.

**Promoting economic growth** — innovation and investment, agricultural gains from satellite and mobile information, automation raising output. Seven gains, and a town that uses them well is visibly richer in time, reach and care.

## Sub lesson 2 — The harms — six, and three cases

**Social alienation** — digital socialising and contactless shopping and banking breed disconnectedness, while platforms amplify prejudice, hate speech, misinformation and echo chambers. **Eroding privacy** — data can be stolen or sold; anyone's photos can be posted; employers screen online lives; cameras record public movement. 

[Picture 28819 — A shuttered telephone kiosk beside teenagers with mobile phones]



**Job insecurity** — remote work lets low-wage workers undercut, and automation abolishes jobs; driving and delivery are named as next. **Data insecurity** — vast stored private data, and one breach delivers it to criminals, terrorists, rivals or foreign adversaries. **Aggravated crime and terrorism** — promotion by terrorists on social media; the dark-web drug trade. **Physical-health effects** — eyestrain, blurred vision and dry eyes; head, neck and shoulder pain; poor posture building into lasting musculoskeletal problems.

Three cases make the harms personal — all summaries, as they are given. **Kobalo** restricted his friendships into isolation through online-gaming addiction. **Helen** found her Facebook photos taken as other people's profile pictures, used to friend-request her friends — even after she deleted them. And **Mr. Ojulu** watched his public telephone centre die as his customers moved to mobile phones.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the seven gains quickly — then slow down for the six harms, and end with Helen's story.

## Do It Yourself

1. Walk your street and list every service that now also lives on a phone. Mark which street-place it emptied.
2. Check your own photos online: could a stranger take one as their profile picture? Tighten one setting.
3. Stand up. Roll your shoulders, look far away for twenty seconds. Your body is part of social life too — note the lesson's sixth harm.
