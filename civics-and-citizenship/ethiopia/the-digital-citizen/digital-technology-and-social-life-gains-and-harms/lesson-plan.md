# Lesson plan — Digital Technology and Social Life — Gains and Harms

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** The Digital Citizen · **Lesson:** Digital Technology and Social Life — Gains and Harms
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 22 — The Digital Citizen (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** on social life, digital technology gives seven gains — connection, speed, education, transport, banking, health, growth — and six harms, from alienation to damaged eyes.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the citizenship levels before it — the learner already knows what a citizen owes and is owed; this level teaches the same citizen how to live in the digital world.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The gains — seven** — connectivity, speed, education, transport, banking and finance, health care, economic growth
2. **The harms — six, and three cases** — alienation, privacy erosion, job insecurity, data insecurity, crime and terrorism, physical health — with the Kobalo, Helen and Ojulu cases

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Digital Technology and Emotions — Gains and Harms, the field before this one. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the seven social gains.
2. Name the six social harms.
3. Retell one of the three cases — Kobalo, Helen, Mr. Ojulu — and name its harm.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner lists the errands that left the street for the screen, and what the street lost (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the seven gains across one town's life, then the six harms, with the three in-lesson cases as the harms made personal (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 28818]: a picture of seven small panels across one town street: a classroom screen, a train booking, a mobile bank, a clinic tablet and the other gains — the student names the gain each panel shows.

3. **Say it to your companion** — explain the lesson's one thing in your own words (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the lesson retold in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: fill-in-the-blank, matching, multiple-choice, short-answer, true-or-false, writing — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the doing block at the end of lesson/lesson.md.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks job insecurity only means laziness → it is structural: remote undercutting and automation abolishing whole jobs.
- Thinks deleting a photo ends its life → Helen deleted hers; the copies went on working.
