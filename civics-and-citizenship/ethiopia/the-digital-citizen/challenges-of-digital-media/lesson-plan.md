# Lesson plan — Challenges of Digital Media

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** The Digital Citizen · **Lesson:** Challenges of Digital Media
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 22 — The Digital Citizen (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** digital media carries three challenges: disregard for ethical values, fake news, and threats to privacy and security.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the citizenship levels before it — the learner already knows what a citizen owes and is owed; this level teaches the same citizen how to live in the digital world.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Codes of Conduct Online — the rules this lesson's challenges break. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the three challenges.
2. Explain why fake news spreads faster than its correction.
3. Say how a private post stops being private.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner recalls a false story's head start over its correction (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the three challenges one by one; the GIF shows the speed gap between a false post and its correction (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 28812]: a picture of a false headline leaping quickly across three phones while a small correction crawls behind — the student points to the gap between the lie and the correction and says why it exists.
   - ADD GIF [GIF 28813]: a GIF of one false post passing from phone to phone, moving faster with each pass — the student watches the acceleration and says why speed is the danger.
3. **Say it to your companion** — explain the lesson's one thing in your own words (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the lesson retold in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: fill-in-the-blank, matching, multiple-choice, short-answer, true-or-false, writing — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the doing block at the end of lesson/lesson.md.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks fake news is only about politics → it is also made for commercial gain and advertising clicks.
- Thinks deleting a post ends it → reshares and copies outlive the original; platforms are public places.
