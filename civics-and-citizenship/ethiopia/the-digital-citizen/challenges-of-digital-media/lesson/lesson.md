# Challenges of Digital Media

## Do it first

Remember a shocking story that raced through phones you know — and later turned out false. How far did it get before the truth caught up? Measure that gap in your mind; this lesson explains it.

## Three challenges

**The first challenge: disregard for ethical values.** Information on digital media is transmitted uncontrolled — nobody checks it at the gate. So its accuracy, and its impact on society, are a standing worry. The remedy taught is ethical use: the codes from the lesson before this one are not decoration; they are the answer to this challenge.

**The second challenge: fake news.** Reports, images and video are fabricated to spread misinformation — to manipulate opinion for political or commercial gain, and to harvest clicks for advertising income. And fake news has a runner's advantage: sharing is so easy that a falsehood laps the world while the correction is still tying its shoes.

Watch the spread itself: one tap, and the post is on the next phone; another, and it is moving faster still. Speed is the whole danger — by the time checking starts, believing has finished.

**The third challenge: privacy and security.** Social platforms are public places wearing private clothes. A post you think of as private can be reshared to anyone — uploads to Facebook and YouTube are the standing example. And the same insecurity climbs: what begins as one person's exposed photo ends as a national-security concern.

The running harms stand behind all three: falling school performance, harm at work, and damaged personal relationships. So the lesson's own exercise is a balance sheet: tabulate social media's advantages and disadvantages for learning — honestly, both columns filled.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the three challenges, and why the second one always seems to win the race.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw the balance sheet: advantages and disadvantages of social media for your own learning, at least four honest entries each side.
2. Next false-looking story you receive: before anything else, write down two checks you could make. Keep the list on your phone case or notebook.
3. Check one of your own old posts: who could see it now, if it were reshared? Adjust one privacy setting.
