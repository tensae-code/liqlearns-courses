# Lesson plan — Digital Technology and Ethical Values — Gains and Harms

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** The Digital Citizen · **Lesson:** Digital Technology and Ethical Values — Gains and Harms
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 22 — The Digital Citizen (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** on ethical values, good use builds critical thinking, universal moral values and self-learned good habits — and bad use brings six harms, with Ali's confusion as the warning case.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the citizenship levels before it — the learner already knows what a citizen owes and is owed; this level teaches the same citizen how to live in the digital world.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The gains — three** — critical thinking; universal moral values; self-learning of good habits
2. **The harms — six, and Ali** — ethical dilemma, uncertainty, unhappiness, untruthfulness, eroded freedom, negative emotions — and Ali's case

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Digital Technology and Social Life — Gains and Harms. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the three ethical gains.
2. Name the six ethical harms.
3. Tell Ali's case and the harm it carries.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks many sources automatically make wisdom → Ali had many sources; the gains come from *examining* them.
- Thinks universal values mean forced sameness → they are good norms that win open debate and become shared guides.
