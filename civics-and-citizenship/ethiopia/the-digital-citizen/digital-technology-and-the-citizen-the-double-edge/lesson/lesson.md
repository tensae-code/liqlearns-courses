# Digital Technology and the Citizen — The Double Edge

## Do it first

Hold one of your own phone habits up to the light: the first check in the morning, the last scroll at night. Honestly — does the habit serve you, or do you serve it? This lesson gives the rule that decides.

## The double edge

Digital technologies have advanced faster than any earlier technology, and they reach much of the world. They create positive and negative impacts on citizens — on their **emotions**, their **social life**, and their **ethical values**. They can also threaten privacy, erode ethical and cultural values and security, and fuel inequality. 

[Picture 28814 — A balance — a phone at a desk against a phone all night in bed]



So which way does the technology cut? The lesson's governing rule stands at its head, and everything after it is commentary: **good and moderate use leads to positive effects, while extremely excessive and addictive use leads to negative effects.** 

[Picture 28815 — A phone splitting one scene in two (teacher guide — evidence)]



Picture a balance. On one pan, a phone used briefly, at a desk, for a purpose — it weighs light. On the other, a phone used all night, in bed, without purpose or end — it drags the scale down. Same phone. Different dose. Different citizen.

The next three lessons work the rule field by field: emotions first, then social life, then ethical values — gains and harms in each, with cases. Keep the balance in your hand through all three: the question is never only *what does the technology do?* but always also *how much, and how, is it used?*

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the governing rule, word for word if you can — then name the three fields it runs through.

## Do It Yourself

1. Weigh three of your own habits on the balance: brief and purposeful, or all-night and endless? Write the verdicts.
2. Set one dose limit for this week — a time, a place, or a purpose — and keep it. Note what changes.
3. Interview one person whose phone use you admire. What is their dose rule?
