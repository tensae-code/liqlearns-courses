# Unethical Use — Cheating, Plagiarism, Piracy

## Do it first

Two pupils hand in the same beautiful assignment. One spent four days in a library; the other spent four minutes copying. If both receive the same mark, what has the school just taught the whole class? Begin there.

## Theft in three forms

**Cheating** is copying another's work, letting your own work be copied, submitting another's work as your own, fabricating data, or using unauthorised help or aids in examinations. **Plagiarism** is presenting other people's ideas, words or intellectual property as your own. 

[Picture 28824 — Two assignment pages — one copied and stamped, one earned]



The damage is not only unfairness. Copiers make themselves **dependent and unanalytical** — the copy produces no thinker. And authors' morale erodes: academics who watch their work stolen leave research, and future generations are starved of the books, articles and research those people would have made.

Now the case, as a summary. Mrs. Zebenay, a citizenship teacher, set library-based individual work over four days. Many pupils copied straight from internet sources; some worked properly from books. She punished the copiers and rewarded the workers. Judge her — the lesson asks whether the decision was right, and any honest class finds the theft in it: the copiers were stealing the workers' four days. 

[Picture 28825 — An evidence tray (teacher guide — evidence)]



**Digital media piracy** is the third theft: the illegal circulation and distribution of music, movies, books and other intellectual property. It violates creators' property rights, harms the production industries, and undermines the young's own commitment to hard work — musicians, academics and filmmakers lose what is theirs without payment, and the pirate learns that taking beats making.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the difference between cheating and plagiarism — then your verdict on Mrs. Zebenay, with your reason.

## Do It Yourself

1. Take one past assignment of yours. Mark honestly: every sentence truly yours? Every borrowed idea credited? Fix what can be fixed.
2. List three creators — a musician, a writer, a filmmaker — whose work you enjoy. Beside each: what does piracy take from them?
3. Write your own one-line honour code for schoolwork and sign it. Keep it in your exercise book.
