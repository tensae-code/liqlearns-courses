# Communicating Well Through Digital Media

## Do it first

Read this message, imagined on your phone: “Come. Bring it. Tomorrow maybe.” What is missing? Who is coming, what is “it”, and is tomorrow sure? Feel how a few missing words breed confusion — then begin.

## Clear words across distance

Communication is informing and influencing individuals and large audiences by scientific and ethical methods — and it decides success and failure, for a person and for an organisation alike. Today digital media is its key strategy. 

[Picture 28810 — A farmer checking a price message at a market stall]



Four improvements built this power. **Cellular and satellite** links raised the speed and ease of connection. **The internet** made long-distance exchange easy, and far-off web access possible. **Computers** improved storage, presentation and teaching. And **mobile phones** enabled e-commerce and transactions anywhere — the market now sits in a pocket.

Now meet the lesson's proof in a person. Megarsa Tullu is a farmer in Dimtu village. He uses his mobile phone to follow market prices for farm produce; he keeps himself informed about the whole country; and he passes the prices on to other farmers. 

[Picture 28811 — Two chat windows — one confused, one calm (teacher guide — evidence)]



See what good communication did there: one farmer, informed, becomes a service to every farmer around him. The phone did not only carry words — it carried a fair price. @@M2@@

So communicate well: be clear about who, what and when; remember there is no face to soften your words, so choose the tone deliberately; and use the channel to inform others, the way Megarsa did — not merely to fill silence.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the four improvements in order, then retell Megarsa's story in three sentences.

## Do It Yourself

1. Rewrite the vague message from Do it first into a clear one: who, what, when, where — all present.
2. Find one price, timetable or announcement that reaches your area by phone. Note who sends it and who it serves.
3. Send one genuinely useful message today — a price, a time, a warning — to someone it helps.
