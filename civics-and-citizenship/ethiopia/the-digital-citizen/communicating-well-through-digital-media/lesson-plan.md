# Lesson plan — Communicating Well Through Digital Media

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** The Digital Citizen · **Lesson:** Communicating Well Through Digital Media
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 22 — The Digital Citizen (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** good digital communication is clear, ethical and effective — and four inventions made it possible: cellular links, the internet, computers, and mobile phones.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the citizenship levels before it — the learner already knows what a citizen owes and is owed; this level teaches the same citizen how to live in the digital world.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Codes of Conduct Online. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what communication is and why it decides success and failure.
2. Name the four improvements that made digital communication what it is.
3. Tell the Megarsa Tullu case and what it proves.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks communication is just talking → it is informing *and* influencing, by scientific and ethical methods — method matters.
- Cannot see why a farmer's prices belong in a citizenship lesson → informed citizens serve each other; that is the lesson's proof.
