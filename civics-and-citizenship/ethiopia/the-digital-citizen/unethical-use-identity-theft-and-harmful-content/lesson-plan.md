# Lesson plan — Unethical Use — Identity Theft and Harmful Content

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** The Digital Citizen · **Lesson:** Unethical Use — Identity Theft and Harmful Content
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 22 — The Digital Citizen (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** behind a hidden identity people do what they would never do face to face — identity theft steals a person's very name, and harmful content passed hand to hand breaks friendships, learning and families.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the citizenship levels before it — the learner already knows what a citizen owes and is owed; this level teaches the same citizen how to live in the digital world.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Unethical Use — Cheating, Plagiarism, Piracy, just before this lesson. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what identity theft is.
2. Explain why expecting no consequences changes behaviour.
3. State the harms of irresponsibly shared harmful content, in the lesson's own framing.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

1. **Do it first** — the learner pictures a stranger borrowing under their own name before the term is defined (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — identity theft and the anonymity that feeds antisocial behaviour; then the lesson's second harm — harmful content shared irresponsibly — taught strictly in the book's own framing (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks identity theft is only about money → it steals the *personality* — name and standing — and the victim carries the consequences.
- Thinks forwarding is harmless because they did not create the content → the lesson counts sharing and forwarding as the harm's vehicle.
