# Lesson plan — Codes of Conduct Online

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** The Digital Citizen · **Lesson:** Codes of Conduct Online
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 22 — The Digital Citizen (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** online life has a code: the Grade 9 rule list first, then the six ethical codes — etiquette, responsibility, safety, security, confidentiality, accuracy.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the citizenship levels before it — the learner already knows what a citizen owes and is owed; this level teaches the same citizen how to live in the digital world.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The Grade 9 code list** — the rule list for everyday use — media for good, privacy, no fake news, no defamation, correct mistakes, no addiction
2. **The six ethical codes — the depth** — etiquette, responsibility, safety, security, confidentiality, accuracy — each with its violation example; horizon: digital citizenship

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: What Digital Technology Is For. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Recite the heart of the Grade 9 code list.
2. Name the six ethical codes and what each one guards.
3. Judge a real online act against the codes and name the one it breaks.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Confuses safety with security → safety guards the *body* (eyes, posture, wrists); security guards *data and people* (viruses, firewalls, updates).
- Thinks the codes are only for children → they bind every user; the lesson's horizon is citizenship itself.
