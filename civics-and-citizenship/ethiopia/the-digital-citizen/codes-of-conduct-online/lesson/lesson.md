# Codes of Conduct Online

## Do it first

Think of the last message, post or forward that troubled you — something cruel, false, or private made public. Which rule would have stopped it, if everyone lived by that rule? This lesson holds two sets of those rules.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Grade 9 code list

Social-media content is broad and uncensored — nobody stands at the gate. So the use of digital technology must be governed by ethical codes, fitted to each platform's purpose. Here is the rule list to live by. 

[Picture 28807 — The code list as a phone-shaped poster]



Use digital media **for good** — for example, solving community problems by raising awareness. **Turn what you learn into knowledge and skill.** **Ignore content that provokes** political, religious or sexual violence. **Treat others as you wish to be treated.** **Respect privacy and keep confidentiality.**

**Fight fake news**: do not post, share or follow it — expose it with facts. **No defamation**, and no posts that degrade anyone's dignity. **Acknowledge your mistakes and correct them promptly.** **Do not waste excessive time.** **Do not let your use damage** family and friendship relations, or study and work time. And **free yourself from addiction** before the tool owns you. 

[Picture 28808 — A phone screen with seven rules being ticked (teacher guide — evidence)]



## Sub lesson 2 — The six ethical codes — the depth

Now the same conduct, organised as six codes. Each code guards something, and each is taught with the act that breaks it. 

[Picture 28809 — Six labelled shields around a phone]



**Etiquette** — your digital behaviour affects others, and everyone online is a role model. Broken by: pupils texting non-class messages in class. **Responsibility** — think twice about what is appropriate, ethical and legal to download. Broken by: copying internet material for a project without credit, or downloading illegal material. **Safety** — the physical dangers are real: carpal tunnel syndrome, eyestrain, poor posture. Broken by: working long in poorly lit rooms.

**Security** — protect stored data and people: virus protection, firewalls, offsite storage, updated software. Broken by: exposing your identity on email or chat, leaving data unprotected, letting updates lapse. **Confidentiality** — personal information leaks online; the classic break is telling others your email or Facebook password. **Accuracy and trustworthiness** — no agency monitors online truth, so teachers must warn and users must check: inaccurate information causes social confusion, and medical and legal harm.

The horizon of all six codes has a name: **digital citizenship** — active, engaged, respectful young users. And note the honest warning at the end: young citizens will meet online risks whatever their skill level. The codes do not remove the risks; they teach you to walk among them awake.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the six codes in order, then confess — honestly — which one your own online life breaks most often.

## Do It Yourself

1. Audit yourself against the Grade 9 list: write kept / broken beside each rule, no excuses.
2. Change one setting or habit this week for each of the Safety and Security codes. Write what you changed.
3. Find one false or degrading post. Using the codes, write the correction you would post — facts, no insults.
