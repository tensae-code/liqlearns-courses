# Lesson plan — Digital Technology and Emotions — Gains and Harms

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** The Digital Citizen · **Lesson:** Digital Technology and Emotions — Gains and Harms
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 22 — The Digital Citizen (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** on emotions, digital technology gives seven gains — from self-control to security — and five harms, from isolation to addiction; the dose decides which list you live in.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the citizenship levels before it — the learner already knows what a citizen owes and is owed; this level teaches the same citizen how to live in the digital world.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The gains — seven positive effects** — self-control, socialisation, balance, happiness, regulation, security, satisfaction
2. **The harms — five negative effects** — isolation, secondhand living, depression and anxiety, anonymity and fake personality, addiction

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Digital Technology and the Citizen — The Double Edge, which states the rule this lesson works. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name at least five of the seven emotional gains.
2. Name the five emotional harms.
3. Explain 'secondhand living' in your own words.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner recalls one lifting and one lowering screen-day from their own life (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the seven gains first, in the book's order, then the five harms — a paired balance sheet for the emotions (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 28816]: a picture of a grandmother smiling into a video call, a phone glowing warmly beside her tea — the student names two emotional gains this picture holds.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 28817]: a picture of a pupil alone at a full family table, face lit only by a phone screen while the seats around stand empty of talk — the student names the harm in the picture and its irony.

   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the lesson retold in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: fill-in-the-blank, matching, multiple-choice, short-answer, true-or-false, writing — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the doing block at the end of lesson/lesson.md.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks isolation means being alone in a room → it is a *feeling* of disconnection that can grow while the screen is full of people.
- Confuses the gains list with permission → the same lesson carries the harms; the dose decides.
