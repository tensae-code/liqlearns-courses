# What Digital Technology Is For

## Do it first

Take the phone list from your day again. Sort everything it did into piles: connecting people, sending something fast, learning something, buying or selling something. If your piles come out at four, you have already found this lesson.

## The four purposes

A tool is judged by what it is for. Digital technology has four purposes, and using it well means using it for these. 

[Picture 28805 — Four panels — video call, fast message, online lesson, online shop]



**First: social connection.** The technology keeps family, relatives and work-teams far away in real contact — by words, by video calls and virtual conferences, and by shared media. Distance stops deciding who is close to you.

**Second: communication speed.** Vast amounts of information are compressed onto small devices and exchanged in fractions of a second. Email is the worked example: a letter that once took weeks now arrives before you have stood up from the chair.

**Third: online learning.** Courses, training, books, journals and interactive media can be reached in seconds, without travelling to a learning centre. The library comes to the learner.

**Fourth: digital marketing.** Goods and services are promoted and sold through websites, micro-sites, apps and social platforms — so customers can buy while the shop is closed, and staff time is freed for other work. 

[Picture 28806 — Four doors from one phone (teacher guide — evidence)]



People debate whether modern life is even possible without social media. Whatever your answer, the four purposes are the measure: connection, speed, learning, trade — a use that serves none of them is the technology wasting you, instead of you using it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the four purposes in order, with one example of each from your own street or family.

## Do It Yourself

1. For one day, tally your digital uses under the four purpose headings. Which purpose do you use most? Which least?
2. Find one person who sells something through a phone or a page. Ask what it saves them.
3. Name one thing you could learn online this week that you cannot learn within walking distance.
