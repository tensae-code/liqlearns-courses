# Values of democracy — tolerance, liberty, justice, equality, civility, diversity

## Do it first

Herders move with their cattle for pasture and water. Their children cannot attend a fixed school. If the school will not move, are those children treated justly? Hold that case — it carries this lesson's idea of justice.

## Sub lesson 1 — Tolerance, liberty, justice

Principles are democracy's skeleton; **values** are its lived culture. **Tolerance:** natural differences of ethnicity, language, culture, religion and wealth make coexistence impossible without it — and intolerance, in Gandhi's words as the book quotes them, is a form of violence blocking the democratic spirit. **Liberty:** freedom within society from oppressive restriction — belief, friendship, ideas and their public expression, assembly, work and business. **Justice:** fairness and impartiality with equal protection under law.

[Picture 28660 — a mobile classroom tent beside herded cattle]

Justice is carried by a case: **mobile schools** follow Afar herder children, whose families move for pasture and water, because a fixed school would exclude them. Three friends of different languages who defend other pupils' rights open the section — values are practised by people, not posters.

## Sub lesson 2 — Equality, civility, valuing diversity

**Equality:** equal access to political, social and economic opportunity — wealth, ethnicity and religion are external to equal humanity. **Civility:** the moral duty owed to one another — respect, honouring difference, listening for common ground, discourse, manners and non-discrimination — pictured in drivers yielding to pedestrians with no traffic officer watching. **Valuing diversity:** communities that prize their differences and similarities together, guaranteeing equal treatment and access regardless of race, ethnicity, orientation or disability — **unity in diversity**.

A deeper note, from evidence: equality sometimes needs **distributive justice** — disadvantaged regions such as Afar may need **more**, not merely equal, support to stand truly equal — as a water pipe reaching a dry distant village pictures it.
