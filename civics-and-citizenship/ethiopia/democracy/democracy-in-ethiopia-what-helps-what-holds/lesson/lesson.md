# Democracy in Ethiopia — what helps it, what holds it

## Do it first

A balance. On one pan: new schools, roads being built, institutions being reformed. On the other: empty institutions, media the public cannot reach, hunger. Which pan is heavier today — and what would you add to the lighter one?

## Opportunities against challenges

**What helps.** Reforming democratic institutions — the Human Rights Commission and the National Electoral Board among them — against their fragility. Fast **education expansion**, raising consciousness. **Infrastructure investment** knitting society together. And agricultural, water, industrial and tourism investment widening work and change.

[Picture 28669 — a road under construction splitting around obstacles]

**What holds it back.** No strong institutions, so administration follows leaders' preferences. Parties that do not complement or check one another with alternative ideas. An **uninformed society**. Media the public cannot access and which do not independently analyse. Weak political participation, and an unfinished separation of government from politics. And **poverty itself** — hunger puts basic rights in question.

Both sides are taught whole, as the books state them. A citizen who sees only one pan cannot work on either.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson's one thing to your companion in your own words: Ethiopia's democracy is helped by reform, education and connection — and held back by weak institutions, silence and poverty. Use the lesson's own case or example, not the book's sentences. Your companion asks one hard question; answer it from the lesson, or say honestly what you would check.
