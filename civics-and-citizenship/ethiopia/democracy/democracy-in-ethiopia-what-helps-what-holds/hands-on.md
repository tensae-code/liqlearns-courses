# Hands-on — Democracy in Ethiopia — what helps it, what holds it

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
A balance. On one pan: new schools, roads being built, institutions being reformed. On the other: empty institutions, media the public cannot reach, hunger. Which pan is heavier today — and what would you add to the lighter one?

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — Ethiopia's democracy is helped by reform, education and connection — and held back by weak institutions, silence and poverty — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
