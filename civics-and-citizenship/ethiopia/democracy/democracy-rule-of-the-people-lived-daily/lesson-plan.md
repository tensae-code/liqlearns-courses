# Lesson plan — Democracy — rule of the people, lived daily

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Democracy · **Lesson:** Democracy — rule of the people, lived daily
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 17 — Democracy (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** democracy is supreme power held by the people — a system of rule, and a daily way of life.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the levels before it in this course's path order (recommended, never forced); inside its own title it stands on the lessons before it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
1. **Rule of the people** — **Democracy** comes from the Greek *demos* — the people — and *kratos* — rule: supreme power held by the people, exercised directly or through elected representatives.
2. **A system and a way of life** — Democracy is also more than elections.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the State and Government level. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Unpack demos + kratos and Lincoln's formula.
2. Work the class-election case.
3. Tell democracy the system from democracy the way of life.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Thirty-five pupils must choose two class representatives — one male, one female. No teacher appoints them. How should the choosing be done so that it is fair, and so the losers accept it? Your answer is already the seed of this lesson.
2. **The explanation** — the lesson's parts in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 28642]: a picture of a class election with ballot slips going into a box and two representatives standing — the student names the two representatives' job.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 28643]: a picture of a crowd at a ballot box with the Greek roots demos and kratos lettered above — the student reads the two roots and says the whole word they make.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 28644]: a picture of hands raised in a class vote beside the same class cooperating, listening and including — the student points to system in one half and way of life in the other.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 28645]: a picture of a school office door: on one side a single chair deciding, on the other a council table — the student says which side misuses the word democracy.
   - ADD AUDIO: none named in the placements for this lesson — no audio slot is built.
3. **Say it to your companion** — explain the one thing in your own words, with the lesson's own case (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, matching, fill-in-the-blank — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the closing doing block (lesson/lesson.md).
