# Hands-on — Democracy — rule of the people, lived daily

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
Thirty-five pupils must choose two class representatives — one male, one female. No teacher appoints them. How should the choosing be done so that it is fair, and so the losers accept it? Your answer is already the seed of this lesson.

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — democracy is supreme power held by the people — a system of rule, and a daily way of life — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
