# Hands-on — Democracy begins — Athens

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
Draw a circle. Inside it, men raising hands to vote on everything themselves. Outside it stand women, slaves and foreigners who live in the same city. The world's first democracy is both of those pictures at once. Judge it whole.

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — democracy began in Athens, which gave direct participation and equality among citizens — and excluded most of the people — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
