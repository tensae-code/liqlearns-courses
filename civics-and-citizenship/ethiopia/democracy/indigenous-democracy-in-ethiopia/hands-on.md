# Hands-on — Indigenous democracy in Ethiopia — councils, consensus, elders

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
Under a great tree, elders sit in a circle. No ballot box, no parliament building — yet decisions are debated openly, agreed by consensus, and disputes are settled. Is this democracy? This lesson says: look at what it does, not what it is called.

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — Ethiopia practised democracy in its own institutions — councils, consensus and elders — long before the word arrived — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
