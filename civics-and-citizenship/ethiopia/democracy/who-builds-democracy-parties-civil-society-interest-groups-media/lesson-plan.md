# Lesson plan — Who Builds Democracy — Parties, Civil Society, Interest Groups, Media

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Democracy · **Lesson:** Who Builds Democracy — Parties, Civil Society, Interest Groups, Media
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 17 — Democracy (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** democracy is built by four actors — political parties, civil society, interest groups and the media — each carrying a different part of the work.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the constitution level before it — the learner already knows the frame of the state; this level teaches democracy itself: what it is, who builds it, and the values it runs on.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Political parties and civil society** — The first two builders: parties that seek power by election, and civil society that defends interests and rights without seeking to govern.
2. **Interest groups and media** — The second two builders: interest groups carrying citizens' wants to government, and a free media measured by quality, not quantity.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Democracy and Democratization, just before this lesson in this level. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what political parties do as vectors of democracy.
2. Say how civil society differs from parties in its final goal, and what work they share.
3. Say what interest groups transmit, and what the media must be to do its work.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes up parties and civil society → parties seek power through elections; civil society defends interests and rights and does not seek to govern.
- Counts media by outlets → the book measures media by the quality of information given to the public, not by how many outlets exist.
