# Lesson plan — Democratic Institutions

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Democracy · **Lesson:** Democratic Institutions
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 17 — Democracy (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** democracy lasts when it is institutionalised — an electoral commission, parties and pressure groups, each with named functions, doing the work whoever is in power.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the constitution level before it — the learner already knows the frame of the state; this level teaches democracy itself: what it is, who builds it, and the values it runs on.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The electoral commission — nine functions** — What an institution is, the guide's three branches, and the electoral commission's nine functions.
2. **Political parties as institutions — nine functions** — Parties in their second picture: not campaigners but standing institutions with nine functions.
3. **Pressure groups — eight functions** — Pressure groups as institutions: eight functions, from advice to watchdog to manpower bank.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Who Builds Democracy — the same parties and groups appear again here, now as institutions. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. List the electoral commission's nine functions.
2. List parties' nine functions as institutions, and say how this differs from their actor role.
3. List pressure groups' eight functions.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Recites functions without the frame → institutions exist to serve the whole population with equality under the law; that is the test autocratic governments fail.
- Confuses parties-as-actors with parties-as-institutions → same parties, two pictures the book itself keeps: campaigning vectors in the last lesson, standing institutions here.
