# Procedural and substantive democracy — the vote, and what follows it

## Do it first

An election is held. The voting is free and fair. Five years later: no new school, no clean water, no change. Was that democracy? One measure says yes. Another says not yet. Learn both measures in this lesson.

## Process, or results?

**Procedural democracy** judges by **how decisions are made**: universal participation, political equality, majority rule, free elections properly run. Ethiopia's regular elections are its face. By this measure, a country with clean processes is democratic.

**Substantive democracy** — also called functional democracy — judges by **what government actually delivers**: elections must issue in real change — political equality, prosperity, the people's interests served — and the people must have a real, continuing say, not merely a vote.

Neither measure is the enemy of the other. The first guards the road; the second asks where the road went. A mature citizen applies both, to every government, including their own.
