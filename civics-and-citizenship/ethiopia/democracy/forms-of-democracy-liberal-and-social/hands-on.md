# Hands-on — Forms of democracy — liberal and social

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
Two town squares. In one, the state runs only the courts and the roads, and the market runs itself. In the other, the state also runs the clinic, the school and the housing. Both squares vote. Both are democracies. What differs?

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — liberal democracy keeps the state's economic hand light; social democracy uses the state to pursue equality — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
