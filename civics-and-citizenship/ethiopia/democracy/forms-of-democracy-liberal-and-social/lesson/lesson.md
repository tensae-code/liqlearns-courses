# Forms of democracy — liberal and social

## Do it first

Two town squares. In one, the state runs only the courts and the roads, and the market runs itself. In the other, the state also runs the clinic, the school and the housing. Both squares vote. Both are democracies. What differs?

## A contrast of the state's reach

The two forms are sorted by **how far government enters economic life**.

[Picture 28648 — a balance: market stall against school, clinic, houses]

[Picture 28649 — two town squares with different state roles]

**Liberal democracy** keeps the state's economic role minimal — order and basic coordination — and lets market forces lead. It secures individual political and legal equality; it does not secure economic equality. It is also called capitalist democracy, and is practised by most Western countries.

**Social democracy** has government intervene for equality, in a classless direction, through reform within capitalism: social security, housing, free education and health care, justice and empowerment for the poor.

Both hold elections and rights. They differ over one question: how much should the winners of the market be balanced by the state?
