# Lesson plan — Democracy and Social Transformation

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Democracy · **Lesson:** Democracy and Social Transformation
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 17 — Democracy (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** democracy transforms society — through social change and civic engagement first, then through dialogue, social capital and community development.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the constitution level before it — the learner already knows the frame of the state; this level teaches democracy itself: what it is, who builds it, and the values it runs on.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Change and engagement** — The two senses of transformation, and democracy's first two channels: social change and civic engagement.
2. **Dialogue, social capital, community development** — The deeper channels: dialogue that suspends judgement, capital made of trust, and development a community does itself.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Democratic Institutions, just before this lesson in this level. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Keep the two senses of social transformation apart.
2. Say what civic engagement includes.
3. Explain bonding and bridging social capital, and what community development weighs.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Merges the two senses of transformation → one is a single life changing its inherited status; the other is a whole society restructuring. Keep them apart, as the book does.
- Thinks social capital is money → it is the value stored in networks: trust, reciprocity, information, cooperation.
