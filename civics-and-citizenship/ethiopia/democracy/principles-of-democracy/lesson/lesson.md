# Principles of democracy — sovereignty, constitution, participation, checks, openness, choice, law

## Do it first

A town meets under a tree — 240 residents, women and men equally, a woman in the chair — to tackle drugs, traffic, early marriage and tax. Before reading, name what makes that meeting democratic. This lesson's nine principles are the checklist your answer needs.

## Sub lesson 1 — The people, the constitution, participation

Three principles open the set. **Popular sovereignty:** consent of the governed, given through regularly elected representatives, is the only legitimate source of authority — and constitutional change itself needs the people's approval. **Supremacy of the constitution:** the constitution stands above civil, criminal and every other law, and anything contravening it is void — **FDRE Article 9/1**. **Citizen participation:** a duty as well as a right — voting, standing for office, staying informed, debating, attending meetings, joining voluntary bodies, and engaging on tax, gender equality, child trafficking, road accidents and the environment.

## Sub lesson 2 — Checks, openness, choice, law

The remaining six. **Separation of powers:** legislature makes, executive implements, judiciary applies, each checking the others. **Secularism:** state and religion separate, no state religion — **FDRE Article 11** — lest a majority faith capture state power. **Accountability and transparency:** the public's right to information, petition and impartial redress. **Free, fair and periodic elections:** universal suffrage, secret ballot, votes of equal weight accurately counted, against intimidation and corruption. **Respect for human rights**, from life to food, education, work, health and liberty. **A multi-party system:** real choice — one party means dictatorship. And the **rule of law:** no one, king or president, above it.
