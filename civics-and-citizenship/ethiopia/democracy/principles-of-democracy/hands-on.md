# Hands-on — Principles of democracy — sovereignty, constitution, participation, checks, openness, choice, law

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
A town meets under a tree — 240 residents, women and men equally, a woman in the chair — to tackle drugs, traffic, early marriage and tax. Before reading, name what makes that meeting democratic. This lesson's nine principles are the checklist your answer needs.

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — democracy stands on nine principles, from popular sovereignty to the rule of law — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
