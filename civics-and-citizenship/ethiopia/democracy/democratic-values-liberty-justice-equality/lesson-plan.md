# Lesson plan — Democratic Values — Liberty, Justice, Equality

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Democracy · **Lesson:** Democratic Values — Liberty, Justice, Equality
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 17 — Democracy (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** democracy runs on three values — liberty, justice and equality — and each of the three is itself threefold.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the constitution level before it — the learner already knows the frame of the state; this level teaches democracy itself: what it is, who builds it, and the values it runs on.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Democracy and Democratization in this level — the values fill out its definition. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name liberty's three parts and one example of each.
2. Name justice's three parts and tell them apart.
3. Name equality's three parts, including 'one adult, one vote, one value'.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner writes what each value would look like on their street, before the threefold parts are taught (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — liberty — personal, political, economic; justice — distributive, corrective, procedural; equality — political, social, economic; the three as one set (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Lists freedoms of speech and assembly here → those belong to the political aspect in the next lesson; this lesson's liberty is personal, political (election participation), and economic.
- Mixes distributive and corrective justice → distributive shares out benefits and burdens fairly; corrective answers a wrong already done, proportionally.
