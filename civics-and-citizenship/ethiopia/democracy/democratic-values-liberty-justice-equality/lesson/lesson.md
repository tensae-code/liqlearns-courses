# Democratic Values — Liberty, Justice, Equality

## Do it first

Three words: liberty, justice, equality. Write one sentence for each — what it would look like on your own street if it were real, and one sentence for its absence. This lesson takes each word apart into three named parts.

## Three values, each threefold

Three core values carry democracy, and the book teaches each one in three parts. Learn them as a single building with three columns. 

[Picture 28906 — Three columns lettered Liberty, Justice, Equality, each split into its three named parts]



**Liberty** is threefold. *Personal* freedom: freedom from arbitrary arrest and detention, and homes and property secure from unreasonable search and seizure. *Political* freedom: participation in elections without arrest, harassment, or electoral corruption such as vote-buying, intimidation and obstruction. *Economic* freedom: acquiring, using, transferring and disposing of private property without unreasonable interference; seeking and changing employment; joining lawful unions and businesses.

**Justice** is threefold. *Distributive* justice shares benefits and burdens by agreed standards of fairness. *Corrective* justice answers wrongs and injuries with a proportional response. *Procedural* justice keeps the processes impartial and open — the gathering of information and the deciding itself.

**Equality** is threefold. *Political* equality: one adult, one vote, one value. *Social* equality: no hierarchy or discrimination. *Economic* equality: equal and fair access to national resources and services. The three values hold each other up: liberty without equality is a privilege, and equality without liberty is a cage.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: recite the three values with their three parts each — nine in all. Then give one street-level example of the part you find hardest to keep.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the nine parts on nine cards. Sort them under their three values without looking at the lesson; then check.
2. Find one news story or local event for each value: one where liberty was protected or harmed, one for justice, one for equality. Name the exact part involved.
3. Complete the sentence three ways: 'One adult, one vote, one value' protects… / fails when… / is guarded by…
