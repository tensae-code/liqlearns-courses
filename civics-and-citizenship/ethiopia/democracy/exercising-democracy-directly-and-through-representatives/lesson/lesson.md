# Exercising democracy — directly and through representatives

## Do it first

Your zone could become a region. Who should decide — every adult voting on that one question, or representatives you elected earlier deciding for you? Both are democracy at work, but they are not the same work. This lesson separates them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Direct democracy and the referendum

In **direct democracy**, all adult citizens decide themselves, without elected officials in between: they attend, debate, vote, accept the majority decision, and anyone may stand for office.

[Picture 28650 — a referendum ballot with two boxes and a queue]

It cannot run a whole modern country — a whole people cannot gather — but it supplements democracy powerfully in local committees, schools and associations. Its national instrument is the **referendum**: a direct vote of all citizens on a set question, at local, regional or national level. The book's role play has a class vote, under National Electoral Board rules, on promoting their zone to a region.

## Sub lesson 2 — Representative democracy

In **representative democracy**, citizens elect people to decide for them. Ethiopians elect parliamentarians **every five years** to the House of People's Representatives, which debates and passes the laws.

[Picture 28651 — voters at a polling station and the parliament they fill]

[Picture 28652 — a referendum queue at a school polling station]

[GIF 28653 — a hand dropping a ballot into a box]

The people's control does not sleep between elections. Each member represents **all** citizens, not only those who voted for them; members must stay in contact with their voters; and a member, or a party, that fails can be **removed at the next election**. The vote is a leash, not a gift.
