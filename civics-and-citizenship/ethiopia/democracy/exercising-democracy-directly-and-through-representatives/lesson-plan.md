# Lesson plan — Exercising democracy — directly and through representatives

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Democracy · **Lesson:** Exercising democracy — directly and through representatives
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 17 — Democracy (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** citizens exercise democracy directly — deciding themselves, as in a referendum — or indirectly, through elected representatives.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the levels before it in this course's path order (recommended, never forced); inside its own title it stands on the lessons before it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
1. **Direct democracy and the referendum** — In **direct democracy**, all adult citizens decide themselves, without elected officials in between: they attend, debate, vote, accept the majority decision, and anyone may stand for office.
2. **Representative democracy** — In **representative democracy**, citizens elect people to decide for them.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Forms of democracy — liberal and social. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Describe direct democracy and the referendum.
2. Describe representative democracy and the five-year term.
3. Say how the people's control continues between elections.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Your zone could become a region. Who should decide — every adult voting on that one question, or representatives you elected earlier deciding for you? Both are democracy at work, but they are not the same work. This lesson separates them.
