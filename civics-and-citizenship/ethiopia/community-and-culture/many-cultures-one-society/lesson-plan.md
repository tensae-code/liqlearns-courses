# Lesson plan — Many Cultures, One Society — What Multiculturalism Means

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Community, Culture and Many Cultures · **Lesson:** Many Cultures, One Society — What Multiculturalism Means
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 6 — Community, Culture and Many Cultures (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** multiculturalism is the presence of many cultures together in one society — and because every culture has its own social life, diversity gives individuals meaningful choices.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the lessons before it in this title — the learner already knows the earlier circles of this course (myself, then family, neighbours, school, community and culture) as far as this title. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. ## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Multiculturalism is the presence of many cultures together.
2. Every culture has its own social life and organisation — so cultural diversity gives individuals the chance to have meaningful choices.
3. Pupils discuss the concept in pairs and bring living examples from their own society.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson's scenes are the learner's own home, school and neighbourhood.

## If the learner is stuck
- Believes that “multiculturalism means one culture replacing all the others” → go back to the lesson's own words: multiculturalism is the presence of many cultures together in one society — and because every culture has its own social life, diversity gives individuals meaningful choices. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
- Gives the words but no example → ask for the lesson happening once in their own home, school or street today; the example is the proof.
