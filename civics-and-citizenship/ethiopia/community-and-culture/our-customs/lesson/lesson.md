# Our Customs — the Good We Keep, the Harmful We Stop

## Do it first

List your people's customs in two columns in your mind: customs that help people, and customs that harm people. This lesson does that sorting for real — and keeps only one column.

## The good we keep

Good practices are a basis for the country's development and change.

They are: respecting elders; idir and equb — the community's mutual-aid and rotating-savings associations; helping the needy; working together; and hospitality.

[Picture 28079 — the keep side — idir and equb]

## The harmful we stop

The harmful practices are named plainly, in words only: female circumcision, under-age marriage, cutting the face in mourning, cutting the body with blades, wastefulness, and abduction (ጠለፋ).

These are practices that injure people's bodies and rights — that is why they are stopped, not kept.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: a people's customs are sorted by equality: keep the good — respecting elders, idir, equb, helping the needy, working together, hospitality — and stop the harmful practices that injure bodies and rights. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: custom, abduction (ጠለፋ), harmful practice. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: good customs we keep, harmful practices we stop.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
