# Kinds of Heritage — Things We Can Touch and Things We Carry in Song

## Do it first

Sort five things from your people's past into two piles in your mind: things you could touch, and things you can only hear, sing or celebrate. That sorting is this lesson.

## Tangible heritage

Tangible heritage is what can be seen and touched.

Movable heritage can be carried from place to place: coins, writings, clothes and means of transport.

Immovable heritage's base is tied to the land: monuments, castles, graves, mosques, churches, palaces and bridges.

## Intangible heritage

Intangible heritage is glossed in the key words as songs, oral literature and religious festivals.

One activity has pupils research two intangible heritages from foods, drinks, dress styles and festival-day practices.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: heritage is tangible or intangible: tangible divides into movable (coins, writings, clothes, transport) and immovable (monuments, churches, bridges); intangible lives in songs, oral literature and religious festivals. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: tangible, movable heritage, immovable heritage. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: tangible and intangible heritage; movable and immovable.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
