# Cultural Heritage — What Our Grandparents Hand Us

## Do it first

Name one thing — a skill, a cloth, a song, a story — that came to you from a grandparent's generation. You are holding heritage. This lesson defines what you hold.

## Culture — the whole

Culture is the complex whole holding knowledge, belief, art, morality, custom and the other things a society wants.

It is not one person's: it is a people's shared way of living together and their way of relating to the natural environment.

Culture has four characteristics: it is symbolic (expressed in language, art and image); it is shared by nature; it is learned and passed from generation to generation; and it can be adapted as people find fitting.

## Heritage — the handed part

Heritage is the part of culture one generation receives and hands to the future as wealth.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: heritage is the part of culture one generation receives and hands to the future as wealth — and culture itself is symbolic, shared, learned and passed on, and adaptable. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: heritage, symbolic, adaptable. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
