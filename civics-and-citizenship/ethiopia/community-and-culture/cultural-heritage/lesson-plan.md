# Lesson plan — Cultural Heritage — What Our Grandparents Hand Us

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Community, Culture and Many Cultures · **Lesson:** Cultural Heritage — What Our Grandparents Hand Us
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 6 — Community, Culture and Many Cultures (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** heritage is the part of culture one generation receives and hands to the future as wealth — and culture itself is symbolic, shared, learned and passed on, and adaptable.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the lessons before it in this title — the learner already knows the earlier circles of this course (myself, then family, neighbours, school, community and culture) as far as this title. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Cultural Values — the Principles a Community Stands On (the previous lesson in this title). Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Culture is the complex whole holding knowledge, belief, art, morality, custom and the other things a society wants.
2. It is not one person's.
3. Culture has four characteristics.
4. Heritage is the part of culture one generation receives and hands to the future as wealth.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson's scenes are the learner's own home, school and neighbourhood.

## If the learner is stuck
- Believes that “heritage is a private possession that each person keeps from the next generation” → go back to the lesson's own words: heritage is the part of culture one generation receives and hands to the future as wealth — and culture itself is symbolic, shared, learned and passed on, and adaptable. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
- Gives the words but no example → ask for the lesson happening once in their own home, school or street today; the example is the proof.
