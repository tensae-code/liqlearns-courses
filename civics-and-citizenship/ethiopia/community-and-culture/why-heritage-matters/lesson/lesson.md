# Why Heritage Matters — Who We Are, and Work for Our People

## Do it first

Find a 100-birr note if you can, and look at its pictures. Each one is heritage at work — earning, teaching, uniting. This lesson explains those three jobs.

## History and image

Heritage gives factual information about our history, civilisation and development, and builds Ethiopia's image worldwide.

Through it, the book teaches, the country is known as the origin of mankind, a civilisation more than 5,000 years old, owner of an ancient civilisation with its own alphabet and calendar, and the one African country never colonised.

Knowing our past teaches our shared values, unity and differences, successes and failures.

## Work and pride

Economically: heritage attracts visitors and brings the country income, lifts hotels and transport, creates jobs for guides, drivers and museum workers, and draws foreign investors.

Socially: it gives the people a feeling of ownership and pride, promotes culture and creates work.

A classroom activity compares the heritages pictured on the new Ethiopian 100-birr note.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: heritage matters politically, economically and socially: it tells our history and builds the country's image, brings visitors, income and jobs, and gives the people ownership and pride. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: civilisation, investor. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
