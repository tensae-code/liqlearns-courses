# Lesson plan — Why Heritage Matters — Who We Are, and Work for Our People

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Community, Culture and Many Cultures · **Lesson:** Why Heritage Matters — Who We Are, and Work for Our People
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 6 — Community, Culture and Many Cultures (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** heritage matters politically, economically and socially: it tells our history and builds the country's image, brings visitors, income and jobs, and gives the people ownership and pride.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the lessons before it in this title — the learner already knows the earlier circles of this course (myself, then family, neighbours, school, community and culture) as far as this title. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Heritage gives factual information about our history, civilisation and development, and builds Ethiopia's image worldwide.
2. Through it, the book teaches, the country is known as the origin of mankind, a civilisation more than 5,000 years old, owner of an ancient civilisation with its own alphabet and calendar, and the one African country never colonised.
3. Knowing our past teaches our shared values, unity and differences, successes and failures.
4. Economically.
5. Socially.
