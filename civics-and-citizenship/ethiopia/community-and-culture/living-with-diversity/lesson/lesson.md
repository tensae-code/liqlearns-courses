# Living With Diversity — Respect, Not Just Tolerance

## Do it first

Think of one custom of another culture near you. Move it through three stations in your mind: tolerate it, value it, respect it. Feel the difference between the stations. This lesson walks all the way to the last one.

## The ways, in full

Recognise multiculturalism.

Remove the tendency to count your own culture superior or inferior.

Respect difference, and remove counting an individual's or group's way of life as backward.

Recognise the correctness and contributions of different cultural expressions, and value what other cultures give.

Be ready to admire other cultures, and encourage different groups' contributions.

Encourage people, freed from partiality, to develop their potential for themselves and others.

And the list's own climax: to bring unity in diversity, respect differences rather than merely tolerating them.

## In your own community

Pupils' own communities' ways of accommodating diversity are the discussion matter.

The pictures of the lesson are people celebrating, listening, respecting and getting to know each other together.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: living with diversity is done in named ways — recognising it, refusing to rank cultures, respecting difference — and its climax is unity in diversity: respect differences rather than merely tolerating them. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: unity in diversity, partiality, potential. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: the ways of accommodating multiculturalism.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
