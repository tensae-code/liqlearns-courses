# Keeping Heritage Safe — Teach, Record, Watch, Protect by Law, Belong

## Do it first

Choose one heritage item near you. Decide which of the five shields in this lesson it already has — and which it lacks. That decision is the lesson in your hands.

## What protection means

Protection means keeping a thing as it is — guarding its safety, original identity, size, shape, colour, character and meaning.

[Picture 28077 — five shields around a monument]

## The five ways

First, awareness: teach the public what heritage is worth, what can harm it and how its safety is kept — including putting heritage into the school curriculum and teaching through radio and television.

Second, records: keep information on each heritage item, in digital and paper archives — what it holds, when and from what and why and by whom it was made.

Third, continuing and periodic monitoring of the heritage and its site's history and condition.

Fourth, legal frameworks that protect it.

Fifth, let local people visit for a small fee, so they feel ownership of the heritage.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: heritage is protected by keeping it as it is — through five ways in the book's order: awareness, records, monitoring, legal frameworks, and local people visiting so they feel ownership. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: protection (of heritage), monitoring, archive. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
