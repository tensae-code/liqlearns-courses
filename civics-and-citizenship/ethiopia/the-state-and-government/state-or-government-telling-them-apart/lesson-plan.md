# Lesson plan — State or government? — telling them apart

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** The State and Government · **Lesson:** State or government? — telling them apart
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 16 — The State and Government (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the state endures and holds everyone; a government is its temporary working part.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the levels before it in this course's path order (recommended, never forced); inside its own title it stands on the lessons before it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Before this lesson

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain how state and government are related.
2. Give the contrasts between them.
3. Explain the permanence contrast with an example.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Ethiopia has existed for a very long time. Its governments, in just the last fifty years, have been an empire, a military regime and a republic. So which changed — the state, or its governments? Keep that question open through this lesson.
2. **The explanation** — the lesson's parts in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 28620]: a picture of the six contrasts as a two-column chart with a lasting map outline on one side and changing office-holders on the other — the student reads one contrast aloud from each column.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 28621]: a picture of a parliament building standing unchanged while two different cabinets walk in and out of its door — the student says what stays and what changes.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 28622]: a picture of a lasting outline of Ethiopia with a series of changing government seals rotating above it — the student names the contrast the picture teaches.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 28623]: a picture of a stone building (the state) with a changing row of office nameplates by its door (governments) — the student explains the picture to a partner.
   - ADD GIF [GIF 28624]: a GIF of cabinets/seals changing above an unchanging map/building — the student watches the loop and says what never moves.
   - ADD AUDIO: none named in the placements for this lesson — no audio slot is built.
3. **Say it to your companion** — explain the one thing in your own words, with the lesson's own case (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
