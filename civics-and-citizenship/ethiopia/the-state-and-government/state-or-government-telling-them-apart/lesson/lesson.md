# State or government? — telling them apart

## Do it first

Ethiopia has existed for a very long time. Its governments, in just the last fifty years, have been an empire, a military regime and a republic. So which changed — the state, or its governments? Keep that question open through this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Related: no state without a government

State and government are related before they are different. There is **no state without a government**: government is one of the four state elements named in the Montevideo Convention, and the most important of them — the state's **brain**, expressing and implementing its collective will. And the traffic runs both ways: the **state legitimises the government**, giving its acts their authority. Everyone in a state lives under its government, but only a few people govern.

## Sub lesson 2 — Different: the contrasts

Now the contrasts. The state is the **wider concept**; government is one part of it. The state is **abstract** — unseen, an idea held in law; government is **concrete** — named people in structured offices. The state holds **everyone**; government holds only the few elected or appointed. State membership is **automatic and compulsory**; government membership must be sought and won. Every state has the **same four elements**, while governments vary — parliamentary, presidential, democratic, monarchic. The state holds **original power**; government **derives** its power from the state. And above all: the **state endures** while governments come and go by election or revolution.

The deeper telling adds five more: government is **intrinsic** to human beings — even stateless societies govern themselves — while the state is extrinsic, built around them; the state **represents power**, the government **materialises** it through its occupants; and the state is near-permanent while a government is temporary by design.
