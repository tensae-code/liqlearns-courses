# Government systems — presidential

## Do it first

One polling day, two ballot boxes. Into one, voters choose a president. Into the other, a congress. Neither can simply dismiss the other. Before reading, decide: is that stability, or deadlock waiting to happen?

## Separate, fixed, checked

In the **presidential system**, the president is both **head of state and head of government** in one person, directly elected by the people — in the United States, through the Electoral College — for a **fixed term**: four years, at most twice. The president is not accountable to the legislature and cannot be dismissed by a simple vote of no confidence.

The system's marks: the president's superior position; **strict separation of powers**, with checks and balances among the branches; and **impeachment** — the legislature's removal weapon, reserved for oath-breaking. Examples: the United States, France, Brazil, Argentina, Chile, Mexico and Kenya.
