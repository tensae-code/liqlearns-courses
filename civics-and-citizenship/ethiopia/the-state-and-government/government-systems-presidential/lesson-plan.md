# Lesson plan — Government systems — presidential

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** The State and Government · **Lesson:** Government systems — presidential
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 16 — The State and Government (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** in the presidential system a separately elected president is both head of state and head of government for a fixed term.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the levels before it in this course's path order (recommended, never forced); inside its own title it stands on the lessons before it.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Government systems — parliamentary. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Describe the presidential system.
2. Name its marks: separation, fixed term, impeachment.
3. Contrast it with the parliamentary system.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — One polling day, two ballot boxes. Into one, voters choose a president. Into the other, a congress. Neither can simply dismiss the other. Before reading, decide: is that stability, or deadlock waiting to happen?
2. **The explanation** — the lesson's parts in order (lesson/lesson.md).

   - ADD AUDIO: none named in the placements for this lesson — no audio slot is built.
