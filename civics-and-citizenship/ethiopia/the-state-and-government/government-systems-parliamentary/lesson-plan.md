# Lesson plan — Government systems — parliamentary

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** The State and Government · **Lesson:** Government systems — parliamentary
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 16 — The State and Government (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** in the parliamentary system the majority in parliament forms the government, and the prime minister answers to parliament.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the levels before it in this course's path order (recommended, never forced); inside its own title it stands on the lessons before it.

## Sub lessons
1. **The executive question** — Before the two systems, one question grounds both: **how are executives formed, and how are they held?** Who chooses the people who run the government day to day — and who can remove them, how, and how fast?

Every government system is an answer to that question.
2. **The parliamentary system** — In the **parliamentary system**, the majority party or coalition in parliament forms the government.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: State structures — the confederation. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain how a parliamentary government is formed and falls.
2. Tell the real head from the nominal head in Ethiopia.
3. Name fusion of powers and collective responsibility.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — A council of seven runs a city block. It elects its own chair, and the chair must keep the council's trust or fall. Now scale that block up to a whole country. That scaling is this lesson.
