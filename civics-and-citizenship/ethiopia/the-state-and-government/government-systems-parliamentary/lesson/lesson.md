# Government systems — parliamentary

## Do it first

A council of seven runs a city block. It elects its own chair, and the chair must keep the council's trust or fall. Now scale that block up to a whole country. That scaling is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — The executive question

Before the two systems, one question grounds both: **how are executives formed, and how are they held?** Who chooses the people who run the government day to day — and who can remove them, how, and how fast?

[Picture 28633 — a parliament chamber with the prime minister's bench]

Every government system is an answer to that question. The parliamentary system answers: the legislature forms the executive, and the legislature can dismiss it. The presidential system, next lesson, answers almost oppositely. Hold the question; judge both systems by it.

## Sub lesson 2 — The parliamentary system

In the **parliamentary system**, the majority party or coalition in parliament forms the government. The **prime minister** is drawn from parliament and is accountable to it: lose parliament's confidence, and the government falls. A kebele case models it closely — Block 13's seven-person council electing a chair accountable to council and residents.

[Picture 28634 — a parliament chamber and a presidential palace side by side]

[Picture 28635 — two chairs: a ceremonial throne and a working desk]

Ethiopia runs a variant: under **Article 74/2**, ministers may be nominated from outside parliament. And the head of state is **nominal** — ceremonial. Compare the articles: **Article 71** gives the President the ceremonial role — opening parliament, receiving diplomats, awarding honours; **Article 74** gives the Prime Minister the real executive power, including command of the armed forces. Two more marks: **collective responsibility** — the cabinet stands or falls together — and **fusion of powers** — the executive sits inside the legislature rather than apart from it. The system's examples run from its origin, the United Kingdom, to Ethiopia, Germany, India and Japan.
