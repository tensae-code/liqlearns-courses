# Hands-on — The state — an organised political community

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
Look at a map of the world. It is divided into coloured shapes, each with a name, a flag and a government. Why do those shapes exist at all? What would happen to shared life inside one shape if there were no laws to settle disputes? Start there.

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — a state is a formally established, organised political community under one government — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
