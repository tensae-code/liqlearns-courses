# State structures — the unitary state

## Do it first

Picture a pyramid. At the top, one capital. Every arrow points down. Lower offices exist — but only because the top allows them, and only with the power the top lends. That is this lesson's state.

## One centre

In a **unitary state**, all executive, legislative and judicial power concentrates in the central government. A horizontal separation among the three branches may exist, but there is **no vertical division of power**: any power a sub-unit holds is delegated by the centre's will, not guaranteed by law, and the centre is legally supreme.

[Picture 28628 — a pyramid with one capital at the top]

The examples given are China, Sri Lanka, the United Kingdom, France, Finland, Italy, Kenya, South Africa and Djibouti. States adopt it for simplicity, equal resource distribution, administrative efficiency and no duplication.

Its balance sheet, as printed: **for** — uniformity of administration, equal access to services, and no centre-versus-units conflict over authority. **Against** — concentration and misuse of power, and non-recognition of local diversity. Homogeneous societies are said to prefer it.
