# Hands-on — State structures — the unitary state

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
Picture a pyramid. At the top, one capital. Every arrow points down. Lower offices exist — but only because the top allows them, and only with the power the top lends. That is this lesson's state.

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — in a unitary state all power concentrates in the central government; lower units hold only what the centre delegates — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
