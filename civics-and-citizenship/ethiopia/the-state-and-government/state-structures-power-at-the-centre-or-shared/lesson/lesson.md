# State structures — power at the centre or shared

## Do it first

Two families. In household Z, the father decides everything alone. In household Y, written family rules divide decisions among father, mother and children. Which house is more democratic? Careful — this lesson's answer is: you cannot tell from the shape alone. Read on.

## The map of the next three lessons

**State structure** is the distribution of power among a state's parts: whether power is constitutionally shared down to lower units — as Ethiopia shares it with its regions — or monopolised at the centre, as in the book's contrasting example, Kenya.

[Picture 28625 — two family tables: one deciding alone, one sharing rules]

[Picture 28626 — three diagrams of power]

Three structures exist: **unitary, federal, confederal**. The next three lessons take one each.

One warning before the map is used as a judgement: **no structure is democratic or undemocratic by itself**. Some unitary states out-democratise federal ones. Whether a country is democratic turns on its government's commitment, not on the shape of its power map. Households Z and Y frame the whole unit: a shape can invite good or bad rule, but it does not guarantee either.
