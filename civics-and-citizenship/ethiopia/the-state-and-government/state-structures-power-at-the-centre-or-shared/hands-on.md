# Hands-on — State structures — power at the centre or shared

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
Two families. In household Z, the father decides everything alone. In household Y, written family rules divide decisions among father, mother and children. Which house is more democratic? Careful — this lesson's answer is: you cannot tell from the shape alone. Read on.

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — state structure is how power is distributed: monopolised at the centre, or constitutionally shared with lower units — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
