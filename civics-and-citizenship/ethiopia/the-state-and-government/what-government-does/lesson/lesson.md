# What government does — order, services, security, the economy

## Do it first

Walk, in your mind, through one ordinary day. A road you travel on, a health post, safety on the street, the price of bread. Behind each of these stands work a government does. This lesson sorts that work into four functions.

## Sub lesson 1 — Law and order, and public services

**Law and order:** government makes laws governing everyone's activity, and keeps courts and mechanisms that settle conflicts in time and protect life, rights and property. Order is not silence — it is the confidence that a dispute will be settled by law, not by strength.

[Picture 28615 — community policing and a health post side by side]

**Public services:** government supplies directly, or funds, what life requires for all — fire protection, policing, environmental protection, public housing and transport among them. These services are tied to basic human rights: they are how rights become things you can actually use.

## Sub lesson 2 — National security and economic decisions

**National security:** government guards the country against external and internal threats through defence forces and peacekeeping, and keeps good foreign relations so danger is met with friends, not alone.

[Picture 28616 — a guard post beside the Renaissance Dam]

[Picture 28617 — four panels of the four functions]

**Economic decisions:** no country can meet every want, so government steers the economy — controlling inflation, stimulating trade, regulating natural resources and setting binding economic rules. The Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam, pictured in the book, is economic decision-making built in concrete: a national resource turned toward national development.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson's one thing to your companion in your own words: governments do four common things: keep law and order, provide public services, guard national security, and steer the economy. Use the lesson's own case or example, not the book's sentences. Your companion asks one hard question; answer it from the lesson, or say honestly what you would check.
