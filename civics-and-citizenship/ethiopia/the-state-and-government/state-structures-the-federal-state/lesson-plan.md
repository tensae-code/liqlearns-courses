# Lesson plan — State structures — the federal state

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** The State and Government · **Lesson:** State structures — the federal state
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 16 — The State and Government (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a federal state divides power by constitution between a centre and regions, each supreme in its own sphere.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the levels before it in this course's path order (recommended, never forced); inside its own title it stands on the lessons before it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
1. **Two levels, one constitution** — A **federal state** keeps at least two levels of government, each with powers divided by the constitution itself — not lent by the centre, but guaranteed.
2. **Ethiopia's division: Articles 51 and 52** — Ethiopia works the division out in two articles.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: State structures — the unitary state. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Describe the federal division of powers.
2. Work Articles 51 and 52 item by item.
3. Judge the closing case against federal principle.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — A two-storey house. The top floor handles defence and foreign visitors. The ground floor handles the daily life of the people who live there. Neither floor may simply order the other out of its own rooms — the house rules, written down, divide them. Work out who holds which room as you read.
2. **The explanation** — the lesson's parts in order (lesson/lesson.md).
