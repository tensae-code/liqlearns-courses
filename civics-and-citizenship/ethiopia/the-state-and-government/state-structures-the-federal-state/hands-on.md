# Hands-on — State structures — the federal state

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
A two-storey house. The top floor handles defence and foreign visitors. The ground floor handles the daily life of the people who live there. Neither floor may simply order the other out of its own rooms — the house rules, written down, divide them. Work out who holds which room as you read.

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — a federal state divides power by constitution between a centre and regions, each supreme in its own sphere — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
