# Hands-on — Judging Ethiopia's governments — what each built, what each broke

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
Three columns on a page. In each column, a building on one side and a crack on the other. No era in this lesson is all building or all crack. Your job is to weigh, not to cheer or condemn.

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — each of Ethiopia's three eras is judged by the same measures: what it built, and what it broke — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
