# Hands-on — State structures — the confederation

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
Several flags stand in a loose circle. In the middle is one small shared table, and the threads joining flags to table are thin. Any flag can ignore the table. Before reading: is that circle one state, or several states cooperating?

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — a confederation is an alliance of sovereign states delegating a little power to a weak shared centre — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
