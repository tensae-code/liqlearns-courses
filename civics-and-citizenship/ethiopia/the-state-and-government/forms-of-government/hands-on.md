# Hands-on — Forms of government — one, few, force, the people

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
Four rooms. In the first, one crowned person decides. In the second, a small circle decides. In the third, an armed person decides and nobody may object. In the fourth, everyone decides together. Walk through all four before reading on.

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — governments are sorted by who holds sovereign power: one, a few, one by force, or the people — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
