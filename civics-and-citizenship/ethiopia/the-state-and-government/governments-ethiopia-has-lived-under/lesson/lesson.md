# Governments Ethiopia has lived under

## Do it first

A timeline with three seals: a crown, a star, a ballot box. Two dates cut it: 1974 and 1991. Before reading, place what you already know of Ethiopia's history along that line.

## Sub lesson 1 — The imperial monarchy (until 1974)

Until 1974 Ethiopia was an **imperial monarchy**: absolutist and centralised, with sovereignty in the Emperor under the 1931 Constitution — the throne hereditary, under its Article 4. The Emperor was lawmaker, enforcer and part-judge in one person. Parliament had two chambers, but he nominated its Senate. The people were subjects, not citizens with a voice in who ruled.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Derg military regime (1974–91)

The **Derg** replaced the monarchy in 1974: a dictatorial, unitary military regime that replaced feudalism and monarchy with socialism and a presidential system, and ended hereditary succession. Sovereignty was claimed for the working class, ruled through the unicameral National Shengo; the president was head of state and commander-in-chief.

[Picture 28639 — a timeline 1974–1991–today with three seats of power]

The word itself: **Derg is an Amharic word meaning committee or council** — the name of the committee of soldiers that took power.

## Sub lesson 3 — The post-1991 republic

Since 1991 Ethiopia has been a **parliamentary republic**: rule by citizens' representatives through free, fair, periodic elections, in a federal order.

The book also records the complaints made against this order — one-party dominance, rigging and corruption — and this lesson teaches them as the book states them. Judging all three eras, strength by strength and break by break, is the next lesson.
