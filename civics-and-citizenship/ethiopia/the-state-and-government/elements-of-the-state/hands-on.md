# Hands-on — Elements of the state — people, land, government, sovereignty

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
Take the definition from the last lesson apart. A state was called a people, on land, under a government, free from outside control. Four pieces. Before reading, guess: must the people be huge? Must the land be vast? The answers may surprise you.

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — a state stands on four elements: population, territory, government and sovereignty — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
