# Elements of the state — people, land, government, sovereignty

## Do it first

Take the definition from the last lesson apart. A state was called a people, on land, under a government, free from outside control. Four pieces. Before reading, guess: must the people be huge? Must the land be vast? The answers may surprise you.

## Sub lesson 1 — Population and territory

**Population:** no fixed size is required. A state's people may be few or many, and they need not share one race, religion, language or culture — the modern state reconciles many groups, and Ethiopia's diversity is the standing example.

[Picture 28612 — Ethiopia's map with its six neighbours labelled]

**Territory:** no state exists without fixed land, but the land also has no fixed size — Vatican City covers about 110 hectares, against Russia's vast area. Borders mark the land and regulate the movement of people, money and goods. Ethiopia's six neighbours stand around it, named by direction.

A case closes this half: two neighbours in a land dispute that even the elders cannot settle. Some questions need a final authority above both sides. That need is why the next two elements exist.

## Sub lesson 2 — Government and sovereignty

**Government** is the state's working arm: it makes, enforces and interprets laws and carries out the state's authority. Without a government, there is no state.

[Picture 28613 — flag and anthem above two sovereignty panels]

[Picture 28614 — a four-part diagram of the elements]

**Sovereignty** is the state's supreme power, and it has two faces. **Internal sovereignty:** supreme command inside the state. **External sovereignty:** independence from every foreign will — the state deals with other states as an equal, freely.

Ethiopia's flag and anthem mark its sovereignty, and its sovereignty was defended across history by its heroes' sacrifice — remembered in Victory Day.
