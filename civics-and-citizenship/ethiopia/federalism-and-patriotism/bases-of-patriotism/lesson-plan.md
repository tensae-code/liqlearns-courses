# Lesson plan — Bases of Patriotism

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Federalism and Patriotism · **Lesson:** Bases of Patriotism
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 27 — Federalism and Patriotism (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** patriotism stands on named bases — equality, tolerance and loyalty first; then concern, sacrifice, the constitution, duty and defence — with Adwa, 1 March 1896, as the worked example of the second set.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the federalism lessons just before it in this level — the learner knows the house the federation built; this part teaches the heart that keeps it: patriotism, its types, bases, worth, traits and duties.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. ## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Equality, tolerance, loyalty** — The first course of stones: equal rights defended, differences borne as obligation, loyalty owed to constitution and community.
2. **Concern, sacrifice, the constitution, duty, defence** — The second course of stones — with Adwa as its worked example, dated 1896.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Types of Patriotism — Six Types, just before this lesson in this level. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define the first three bases: equality, tolerance, loyalty.
2. Define the second set: concern, sacrifice, the constitution, duty, defence.
3. Tell Adwa's place in the bases — with its true date, 1896.

Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Bases confused with types → types are the forms love takes (six); bases are what it stands on (eight, in two sets). A type without bases is noise.
- Repeats the book's printed 1996 for Adwa → the true date is 1 March 1896, taught corrected everywhere; the misprint is named in sources only.
