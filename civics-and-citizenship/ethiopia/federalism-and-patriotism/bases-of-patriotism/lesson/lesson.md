# Bases of Patriotism

## Do it first

A house stands on stones you cannot see once the walls are up. Patriotism, the book says, also stands on stones — eight of them, in two courses of stonework. Before reading, name three things you think love of country must rest on, or it falls into mere noise.

## Sub lesson 1 — Equality, tolerance, loyalty

**Equality**: constitutional patriotism rests on respect and acceptance of constitutional democracy and human rights — every citizen protecting others' constitutional rights and engaging in no practice violating the constitution. In undemocratic systems, the book notes, citizens do not hold equal rights and responsibilities; Ethiopia's present basis is citizens' rights and responsibilities — rather than praise of geography, minerals, mountains, rivers and soil.

**Tolerance**: in a multi-ethnic, multilingual, multi-religious Ethiopia, tolerance gives every group common ground and pride in its own legacy. Historically, only a few could take pride when differences went unrecognised; in the democratic constitutional system, tolerance is **not a gift given to others but a constitutional and civil obligation** — accepting differences as normal and natural for peaceful coexistence.

**Loyalty**: constitutional loyalty — to the constitution, the people, the elected government and the community; care for others' property and wealth; and performing one's assigned duties and responsibilities.

## Sub lesson 2 — Concern, sacrifice, the constitution, duty, defence

**Concern for the well-being of one's country**: patriotic citizens ready to defend the country, in Ethiopia's long tradition of enormous sacrifices for independence and territory. The worked example is **Adwa**: the patriotic deed of nations, nationalities and peoples who came from all corners in agreement against a common enemy — with a moral obligation to honour those heroes. Adwa is dated **1 March 1896** — taught corrected, here and everywhere in this course. 

[Picture 28974 — Many differently dressed figures converging from a map's corners toward one hill labelled Adwa 1896]



**Sacrificing personal desires and interests for the common good**: true patriots subordinate their own wants to love, devotion and commitment to country. **Protecting the constitution**: the supreme law, voiding any contrary law, custom or official decision — every citizen owing unreserved support for its protection and implementation, shown also by contributing to respect for individual and group rights.

**Carrying out personal and civic duties**: true patriots perform duties without enforcement, are not excessively self-centred, and know their success depends on healthy relationships with others. **Defending the country in external attack**: readiness when security is in danger — as Ethiopians of all nations and nationalities stood at Adwa. The guide's evidence picture plants the whole lesson: a flagpole standing on the first three stones.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: the two courses of stones, all eight bases. Finish at the hill: what happened at Adwa, and on what date — exactly?

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the eight bases as two rows of stones. Under each, one sentence of what a citizen does when that stone is truly under them.
2. Tolerance is an obligation, not a gift. Rewrite a real sentence you have heard — 'we tolerate them' — into the book's grammar, and say what changed.
3. Prepare the Adwa paragraph you would read to a younger class: the deed, the peoples, the date — 1 March 1896 — and the obligation to honour. Three sentences, no more.
