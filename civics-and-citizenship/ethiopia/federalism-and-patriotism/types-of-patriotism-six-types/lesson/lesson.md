# Types of Patriotism — Six Types

## Do it first

Watch the flag rise at dawn — the GIF above this lesson. While it climbs, ask: how many different things are the watchers loving? A constitution? A memory? The flag itself? Their fields? This lesson counts the loves: six types, in the book's own pairings.

## Sub lesson 1 — Constitutional and traditional

**Constitutional patriotism**: strong commitment and alignment with democracy and human rights — devotion to fundamental values and principles, standing guard for citizens' rights and interests, acknowledging people of different language, colour, sex and religion. It implies coexistence, mutual understanding, tolerance, peaceful conflict resolution and togetherness.

**Traditional patriotism**: readiness to give one's life for the motherland — a particularistic belonging to nation or country, with no attempt to align love of country with democratic principles. 

[Picture 28968 — Two figures — one holding a constitution, one holding a sword — both facing the same flag]



Hold the pair against each other: both are loves of country. One binds its love to principles anyone can share; the other binds it to belonging itself, at life's price.

## Sub lesson 2 — Icon and symbolic

**Icon patriotism**: showing love of country through actions of positive impact. Icon patriots are heroic, willing to risk their lives to defend the homeland — and the heroic values living in the younger generation's memory are to be carried by the present generation into building an independent nation in all aspects. 

[Picture 28969 — A nurse, a teacher and an athlete under one flag-raising scene]

 The guide's dawn picture belongs here: a flag being raised while the watchers stand in that carried memory. 

[GIF 28970 — A flag being raised at dawn (teacher guide — evidence)]



**Symbolic patriotism**: patriotism at the level of national symbols — respect for flag, national anthem and other symbols evoking the spirit of love of country; an abstract, affective attachment to the nation and its core values, in a trend of loyalty that also professes love toward other nations.

## Sub lesson 3 — Capital and environmental

**Capital patriotism**: patriotism expressed through developing the national economy — individuals who count their economic involvement as their patriotic contribution, raising the country's wellbeing, honour and image, and indirectly igniting national pride and identity.

**Environmental patriotism**: love of country shown toward its environment — the environment as source and foundation of life, every human's responsibility to protect. Patriotism, then, is not only defending the country but improving quality of life and environment. 

[Picture 28971 — A market trader and a tree-planter shaking hands over a seedling in a sack of grain]



The guide gathers all six under one flag in six panels. 

[Picture 28972 — Six panels under one flag — a constitution, a battlefield grave, a builder, a flag-raising, a factory, a tree planter (teacher guide — evidence)]

 Count them there — and remember the count: **six types**. The student book's own Unit 7 summary claims 'seven types' and names only four — an error, named in this lesson's sources and never taught.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: the six types in their pairings, one definition each. Then answer the trap: how many types are there, and what does the book's summary wrongly claim?

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the six types as six panels of your own, each with a one-line portrait: a person, a place, an act.
2. Take three people you know or know of. Assign each a dominant type with evidence. One person may carry two — say which pairing.
3. The count check: write 'six types' at the top of a page. Beneath, note where the false 'seven' lives (the unit summary) and that it names only four. File the page in your notebook as your sources habit.
