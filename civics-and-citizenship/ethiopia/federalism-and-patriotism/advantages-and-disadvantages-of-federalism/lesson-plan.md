# Lesson plan — Advantages and Disadvantages of Federalism

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Federalism and Patriotism · **Lesson:** Advantages and Disadvantages of Federalism
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 27 — Federalism and Patriotism (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** federalism's case is argued in the open — nine advantages in the student book, from divided power to grassroots participation, against five disadvantages, from conflict of authority to blocked national policies.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the constitution level and the diversity level — the learner knows the state's frame and the peoples it holds; this level teaches the federal answer: power divided, unity kept.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Advantages — the book's nine** — The praise list: nine advantages, the first five restating the features as benefits.
2. **Disadvantages — the book's five** — The price list: five disadvantages — and the guide's fuller table kept as a labelled extension.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Features of Federalism, just before this lesson in this level. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. List the book's nine advantages.
2. List the book's five disadvantages.
3. State how the guide's fuller table extends the lists — as evidence, not as a substitute.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Recites advantages as the features re-worded and cries repetition → the book restates some features as benefits by design; the lesson keeps its design and says so.
- Quotes the guide's extra items as the student book's → the fuller table is the evidence extension, labelled; the taught lists are nine and five.
