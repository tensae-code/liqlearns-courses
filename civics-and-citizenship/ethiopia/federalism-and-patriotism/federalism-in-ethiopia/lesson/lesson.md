# Federalism in Ethiopia

## Do it first

A country of many peoples tried one language, one government, one centre for almost a century — and it did not hold them together. Write what a different design would have to promise each people, and promise the whole country at the same time. This lesson is Ethiopia's actual answer.

## Sub lesson 1 — Purpose and powers

The rationale first: Ethiopian federalism exists for **reconciling ethnic diversity with political unity** — the culmination of a long search for a constitutional formula to unite and contain Ethiopia's peoples, after almost a century of highly centralised unitary state that sidelined diversity under slogans of one nation, one government, one language and one religion. A centralised, unified state proved ineffective; federalism offers the promising formula.

Its first peculiar feature is the **power division**: the constitution explicitly lists **federal powers**, **state powers** and **concurrent powers**, leaving **residual power to the states** — whatever is not listed belongs to the states, not the centre. And it requires mutual respect and **non-intervention**: as the book paraphrases Article 50(8), federal and state powers are defined by the constitution, each level respecting the other's, both confined to constitutionally limited jurisdiction.

## Sub lesson 2 — The two houses

Ethiopia's legislature is **bi-cameral** — two houses under one roof. 

[Picture 28953 — Two assembly halls — one of population representatives, one of nations' representatives — under one roof]



The **House of Peoples' Representatives** is where the highest authority of the federal government rests: legislating in all matters enumerated to the federal government; acting with the House of Federation on human-rights violations no regional state can control; and empowered to question the Prime Minister and federal officials and, on one-third of members' request, to discuss any executive matter and take decisions.

The **House of Federation** is the representative organ of **nations, nationalities and peoples**: each nationality represented by **at least one member, plus one more per additional million of population** — members elected directly by the people or indirectly by state councils, for **five-year terms**. The guide's evidence picture seats it above the map of the regions it represents. 

[Picture 28954 — A map of Ethiopia's regions held in one frame, the House of the Federation seated above it (teacher guide — evidence)]



One honesty rule governs this sub lesson: institutions and functions are taught as the book gives them — the book's article numbers for the houses do not line up cleanly with the constitution's own numbering and are **not** taught as fact; they are named in sources only.
