# Lesson plan — Federalism in Ethiopia

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Federalism and Patriotism · **Lesson:** Federalism in Ethiopia
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 27 — Federalism and Patriotism (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Ethiopia's federation was built to reconcile ethnic diversity with political unity — powers listed for both levels, the rest left to the states, and two houses: one of the people's representatives, one of the nations themselves.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the constitution level and the diversity level — the learner knows the state's frame and the peoples it holds; this level teaches the federal answer: power divided, unity kept.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Purpose and powers** — Why Ethiopia chose federation, and how its powers are listed — with the remainder to the states.
2. **The two houses** — The two houses: the people's representatives holding federal authority, and the nations' house composed by nationality and population.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Advantages and Disadvantages of Federalism, just before this lesson in this level. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the rationale: diversity reconciled with unity, after a century of centralisation.
2. Describe the power division — federal, state, concurrent, residual — and mutual non-intervention.
3. Describe the two houses and how the House of Federation is composed.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Gives residual power to the centre → the book is explicit: the constitution lists federal, state and concurrent powers, and the residue belongs to the states.
- Recites article numbers for the houses as fact → the book's numbers do not line up cleanly with the constitution's own; institutions and functions are taught, the shaky numbers are named in sources only.
