# Lesson plan — Features of Federalism

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Federalism and Patriotism · **Lesson:** Features of Federalism
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 27 — Federalism and Patriotism (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a federation is known by its features — power constitutionally divided, and a written, supreme constitution above both levels — with the guide completing the set: rigidity, and an umpire.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the constitution level and the diversity level — the learner knows the state's frame and the peoples it holds; this level teaches the federal answer: power divided, unity kept.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Division of power — and what decentralisation is not** — The first feature worked in these pages: power divided by the constitution — and why decentralisation alone is not federation.
2. **A written, supreme constitution** — The second feature: the bargain written down and set above both levels — with the guide's five completed as evidence.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Types of Federalism — Dual and Cooperative, just before this lesson in this level. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain division of power, and what decentralisation is not.
2. Explain why the constitution must be written and supreme.
3. Name the guide's completing features — a rigid constitution and an umpire — as the evidence layer.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Calls any decentralised state a federation → apply the test: can the centre revoke the local powers unilaterally, by statute? If yes, unitary — however generous.
- Merges the books' feature counts → student book works two features in these pages; the guide keys five. Both reported as their own book's list, never merged.
