# Traits of Patriots — And the Nurse Offered 500 Birr

## Do it first

A nurse at a crowded hospital. A patient at the very bottom of a long waiting list offers her 500 birr to be treated first. Before reading: what should she do — and what makes your answer a patriot's answer, rather than merely a polite one? This lesson gives the four traits, then hands you the case.

## The four traits — then the case

Four traits mark the patriot. **Awareness of rights and duties**: taking no undue advantage of rights, fulfilling duties, guarding the balance whose disturbance unsettles everyone's freedom. **Fighting for a better nation**: not wrangling with citizens, but naming the country's drawbacks as citizens' responsibility, and voicing opinions on wrongdoing and injustice to free the country of them.

**Eagerness to make the country better**: constant effort — career choices, social work, awareness-raising, saving resources — taking initiative rather than waiting for others' first step. **Not taking undue advantage of privileges**: refusing special benefits got by position, knowing others suffer for them; ordinary citizens choosing to behave in the best manner, their patriotism contagious.

 The fourth trait is taught **corrected** — the guide's list drops its negative; the student book's wording governs, and sources record it.

Now the case, as a summary only. **Roza**, a nurse at a referral hospital with long waiting lists of chronic patients — diabetes, cancer — waiting days, is offered **500 birr** by a patient at the bottom of the list to be treated first. Judge it as the book asks: judge the offer; evaluate refusal as a patriotic trait; and project what happens if many nurses accept. The guide's evidence picture shows her sister-image: a nurse at a hospital desk turning away an envelope, the queue behind her treated in order.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: the four traits. Then sit in Roza's chair: give your judgement of the offer, name the trait that decides it, and finish the projection — if many nurses accept, then…

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the four traits on four cards. Under the fourth, write the correction note: whose wording governs, and what the guide's list drops.
2. Write Roza's case in five summary sentences — no invented dialogue. Then list which traits the patient is asking her to break, in order of weight.
3. Project honestly: if many nurses accept, trace three consequences — for the queue, for the poor, for trust in the hospital.
