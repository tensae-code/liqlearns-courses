# Lesson plan — Types of Federalism — Dual and Cooperative

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Federalism and Patriotism · **Lesson:** Types of Federalism — Dual and Cooperative
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 27 — Federalism and Patriotism (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** federations come in two types — dual, the layer cake, where each level keeps to its own layer; and cooperative, the marble cake, where the colours swirl through both.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the constitution level and the diversity level — the learner knows the state's frame and the peoples it holds; this level teaches the federal answer: power divided, unity kept.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Dual federalism — the layer cake** — The separated type: authority divided cleanly, each level final in its layer — with its costs.
2. **Cooperative federalism — the marble cake** — The swirled type: shared power on overlapping functions, the highways example, and the book's recommendation task.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Federalism — The Meaning, just before this lesson in this level. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Classify federations by how the centre executes its laws, as the book opens.
2. Describe dual federalism with its advantages and disadvantages.
3. Describe cooperative federalism with its worked example — and answer the book's task: recommend a system for Ethiopia.

## If the learner is stuck
- Swaps the cakes → layer cake = dual = separated; marble cake = cooperative = swirled through both. The knife that cannot cross belongs to the layer cake.
- Makes cooperative mean merged → both levels keep their own powers and responsibilities inside cooperation; that is why participation survives.
