# Federalism — The Meaning

## Do it first

One house, two storeys, one roof. The upper floor cannot order the lower floor out of existence; the lower floor cannot saw off the roof. Write who holds power in that house, and what stops each floor from swallowing the other. This lesson names that house.

## What federalism is

**Federalism** is a system where power is divided between federal and regional governments. Power is devolved — transferred downward — so that **neither level is supreme**: power is coordinated between centre and region, avoiding monopoly of power. 

[Picture 28941 — Two storeys of one house lettered Federal and Regional sharing one roof labelled 'the constitution']



The book gives three scholarly definitions in its own selection. First: government activities divided so that each level makes **final decisions** on some activities. Second: a multi-tiered government combining **shared-rule and regional self-rule** — neither level constitutionally subordinate, each deriving its sovereign powers from the constitution, each dealing directly with citizens and directly elected. Third: an institutional arrangement dividing public authority, with a **high federal court** adjudicating federalism disputes.

Then the case, as a summary only: when the Omo River flooded the Lower Omo Basin — villages and farms under water, livestock dead, thousands homeless — the disaster exceeded the regional state's capacity. The federal government stepped in; Ethiopians of all backgrounds, religious institutions and other regional governments mobilised help. The guide reads the same case the same way: the units interdependent, never mutually exclusive. 

[Picture 28942 — Flood relief boats from several regions converging on one flooded village (teacher guide — evidence)]



The guide also sets the world frame, as evidence: **25 countries, over 40% of the world's people, are federal**. Ethiopia's choice is a world pattern, not an experiment of one.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: the definition, then the three scholarly voices in your own words. Finish with the flood: what did it prove that a definition cannot?

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the three scholarly definitions on three cards. Under each, underline the phrase that does the real work — final decisions, shared-rule and self-rule, the adjudicating court.
2. Take the flood case. List what the regional state could do alone, what it could not, and what the federation added. That list is interdependence, itemised.
3. Find one matter in your locality handled federally and one handled regionally. Write the evidence you used to tell which was which.
