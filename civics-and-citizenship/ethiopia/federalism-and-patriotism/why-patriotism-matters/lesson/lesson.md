# Why Patriotism Matters

## Do it first

A stadium when the flag rises: strangers stand together, and for one minute a whole crowd breathes as one body. What just happened — and is it worth anything the next morning? Write your answer. This lesson gives the book's three reasons why it matters.

## The three headline importances

The book argues from the family outward: children raised with patriotic sentiment tend to a better sense of self, and love of country takes many forms — freedom of speech for one person, free self-expression for another. On that ground stand three headline importances. 

[Picture 28976 — A stadium crowd of all ages under one waving flag]



**Bringing people together**: one nation motivating citizens toward making the country better, relating them to one another in unity and togetherness with direction and purpose — the Ethiopian flag waving at events giving belonging and good feeling.

**Building a stronger nation**: belief in patriotic values motivating each individual to work for the country's betterment — the whole nation working together, reminded to be hardworking and honest and to support each other's endeavours.

**Patriotic spirit as an admirable quality**: citizens who know their beliefs and values, stick to their virtues and openly display loyalty and affection, are stronger and more attractive people. The three interlock: togetherness gives the nation its workers; the work gives togetherness its proof; and the quality makes both visible in a single life.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: the three importances in order, and how they interlock. Then answer honestly: which of the three does the flag at an event actually deliver?

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the three importances as three headlines for a school wall newspaper. Under each, one paragraph of your own reporting from real life.
2. Take the family-outward argument: find its first step in your own home — one patriotic sentiment you were raised with, named honestly, and its effect on your sense of self.
3. Argue against the lesson for one paragraph — 'patriotism's importance is overstated because…' — then answer yourself from the book. Both voices labelled.
