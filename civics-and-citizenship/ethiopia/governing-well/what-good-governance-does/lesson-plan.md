# Lesson plan — What Good Governance Does

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Governing Well and Fighting Corruption · **Lesson:** What Good Governance Does
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 26 — Governing Well and Fighting Corruption (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** good governance is called the most important factor in development — it builds economies and living standards, and it holds a society together in cohesion, peace, justice, performance and rights.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the democracy level before it — the learner knows who builds democracy and its institutions; this level asks how governing is done well, and what it costs when it is done badly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Economic and social development** — The development roles: governance as the most important factor in economic growth, and social development itemised.
2. **Cohesion, peace, justice, performance, rights** — The holding-together roles: cohesion, peace, justice, performance and human rights.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Elements of Good Governance, just before this lesson in this level. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say how good governance promotes economic development.
2. List what improving social development means, item by item.
3. Name the five further roles: cohesion, peace, justice, performance, rights.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Reads 'most important factor' as boasting → it is the book's argued claim, with channels named: rule of law, accountable sectors, fair allocation, protected property, decentralised responsibility.
- Treats development as money only → the book itemises social development: living conditions, livelihood, health and sanitation, literacy, participation.
