# What Good Governance Does

## Do it first

Picture two towns with the same land, the same rain, the same people — but in one, offices work and courts are trusted; in the other, nothing is decided honestly. In ten years, which town eats better? Learns more? Sleeps safer? Write your answers; this lesson gives the book's reasons.

## Sub lesson 1 — Economic and social development

The book's claim is strong: good governance is called **the most important factor** in economic development. It works through rule of law; efficient and accountable public and private sectors; better allocation and distribution of resources; community access to institutions and markets; growth whose outcomes are redistributed to all; impartial institutions; protected property rights; equitable law enforcement; and decentralised development in which citizens are **subjects taking responsibility for their lives, not objects of development**. 

[Picture 28915 — One town with a clinic, a school and a market all reached by one good road]



**Social development** is development as sustainable improvement in living standards and wellbeing, itemised by the book: better living conditions; the ability to feed, clothe and shelter oneself from a livelihood; longer life through health facilities and disease prevention by sanitation; literacy and understanding through education; and meaningful participation in public affairs.

The guide's picture sets the same claim in stone: a road, a clinic and a market all rising from one foundation stone lettered *good governance*. 

[Picture 28916 — A road, a clinic and a market rising from a foundation stone labelled good governance (teacher guide — evidence)]



## Sub lesson 2 — Cohesion, peace, justice, performance, rights

Five further roles hold a society together. **Social cohesion and unity**: everyone holding a stake, the poorest and most vulnerable heard in allocating development resources, diversified opinions mediated into consensus — served through democratic principles, participation, human rights, rule of law and transparent administration, and helping peaceful transformation and conflict resolution. 

[Picture 28917 — Five hands of different ages holding one rope level and untangled]



**Peace**: pathways for citizens to feel represented, equitable receipt of benefits, and mechanisms to voice grievances and protect rights — with exclusion under non-participatory governance named as a driver of instability. **Social justice**: an environment of unreserved opportunity for all-round development, where people can rely on correct application of law and equal treatment by courts, police and civil services.

**Government performance**: capacity, efficiency and effectiveness — hallmarked by accountability, transparency and responsiveness. And **respect for human rights**: without rule of law, courts and institutions, enforcement and respect for rights would be impossible. Five hands, one rope, held level: pull governance out and every role sags.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: why does the book call good governance the most important factor in development? Then name the five holding-together roles and the mechanism inside one.

## Do It Yourself

1. Take the foundation-stone picture in words: road, clinic, market on one stone. Draw it for your own woreda — what are your three buildings, and what is your stone made of?
2. Find one local example of exclusion breeding instability, or of inclusion preventing it. Write it against the peace role, in the book's terms.
3. List social development's five items. Score your own locality out of five on each, with one fact per score.
