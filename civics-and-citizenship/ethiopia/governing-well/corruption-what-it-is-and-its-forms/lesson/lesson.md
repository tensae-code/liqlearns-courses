# Corruption — What It Is and Its Forms

## Do it first

Someone at a public desk hints that your paper will move faster with 'something for tea'. Name what has just happened — not the hint, the act. This lesson defines that act, names the soil it grows in, and opens its five doors one by one.

## The definition, the soil, the five forms

**Corruption is the misuse of public office or power for private gain or benefit** — and, in the book's second half, the impairment of integrity, virtue or moral principle at every level of society's power structure. It erodes trust, weakens democracy, hampers economic development and exacerbates inequality, poverty, division and instability.

It flourishes where administration lacks independence and professionalism; where legal frameworks and enforcement are weak; where institutions are weak; where accountability and transparency are absent; and where procedures are unpredictable and bureaucratically complex. Remember this soil — the next lesson's fight is waged exactly on it.

The five forms, off one corridor: **bribery** — offering or providing undue payment, gift or advantage to an official against legal duty, to obtain an undue favour. **Embezzlement** — theft or misappropriation of public resources by officials. **Fraud** — trickery, swindle or deceit; the book's example is teaching on a licence obtained on false grounds. **Nepotism** — appointment favouritism toward one's own relatives. **Extortion** — extracting money or resources by coercion, violence or threats.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: the definition in both halves, then the five forms with one example each — and finish with the soil: the five conditions corruption flourishes in.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write five short cases of your own invention, one per form, each two sentences. Exchange with a partner: they must name the form and the clue that proves it.
2. Take the five flourishing conditions. Score your local administration honestly against each: present, partly, absent — with a reason.
3. The fraud example is a licence obtained on false grounds. Write two more examples of fraud in public life, and what each one steals from the public.
