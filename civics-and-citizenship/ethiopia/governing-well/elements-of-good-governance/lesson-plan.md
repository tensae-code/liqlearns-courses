# Lesson plan — Elements of Good Governance

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Governing Well and Fighting Corruption · **Lesson:** Elements of Good Governance
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 26 — Governing Well and Fighting Corruption (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** good governance has eight elements — accountability, participation, rule of law, transparency, equity and inclusiveness, responsiveness, consensus orientation, and effectiveness and efficiency — and they hold together or fall together.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the democracy level before it — the learner knows who builds democracy and its institutions; this level asks how governing is done well, and what it costs when it is done badly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Governance and Good Governance — The Concepts, just before this lesson in this level. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the eight elements and define each briefly.
2. Read Mr. Alemu's case and name which elements he realises.
3. Carry out the book's field evaluation: one local office against the eight elements.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Learns the elements as eight separate virtues → the book declares them interrelated: failure in one compromises the others.
- Skips the case as a story → Mr. Alemu is the elements made visible; every question about him is a question about the set.
