# When Governance Fails

## Do it first

Walk a street in your mind where governance has failed. The clinic shelf is empty. The court file has dust on it. The office window is dark at working hours. Write what each closed door does to the family standing in front of it. This lesson lists the seven impacts — your street already shows three.

## The seven impacts

The book lists seven impacts of the lack of good governance, and this lesson teaches them as one list — learn the set whole. 

[Picture 28920 — A clinic shelf empty where medicine boxes should stand, with a 'closed — come tomorrow' note on the door]



**Political instability** — decisions driven by the interests of those in power, without public participation. **Inability of rule of law** — rights to life, liberty, property and pursuit of happiness unsecured, and leaders tempted to manipulate and break laws touching security, property, life and money. **Inability to secure justice** — unfair distribution of essential resources, and promised deliverables undelivered.

**Lack of transparency** — government performance unknowable until disgrace surfaces. **Lack of empowerment** — people given no voice in policy, through government unwillingness or public unawareness. **Inefficient service delivery** — in education and health: lost standards, information, incentives and accountability; clinic medicines and equipment sold off privately; court delays. **Administrative irregularities** — data not properly managed when offices do not work as they should.

The guide compresses the lesson into one street with three failures: a clinic shelf emptied, a court file gathering dust, a dark office window. 

[Picture 28921 — A clinic shelf emptied, a court file gathering dust, a dark office window — one street, three failures (teacher guide — evidence)]

 When you can read that street, you can read any town's governance without a report.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: the seven impacts in order. Then take the guide's street — shelf, file, window — and match each failure to its impact by name.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the seven impacts on seven cards. For each, write the mechanism in one line — not the name, the working of it.
2. Audit your own locality quietly: which of the seven impacts can you evidence this year? Write the impact and the evidence; leave names out.
3. Pick the impact you judge most dangerous and argue why, against a partner who picks a different one. Both must use mechanisms, not slogans.
