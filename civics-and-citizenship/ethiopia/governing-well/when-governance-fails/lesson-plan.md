# Lesson plan — When Governance Fails

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Governing Well and Fighting Corruption · **Lesson:** When Governance Fails
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 26 — Governing Well and Fighting Corruption (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** when governance fails, seven impacts follow — instability, law and justice out of reach, secrecy, voicelessness, services that do not serve, and offices that cannot keep their own records straight.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the democracy level before it — the learner knows who builds democracy and its institutions; this level asks how governing is done well, and what it costs when it is done badly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Challenges to Good Governance, just before this lesson in this level. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. List the seven impacts of lack of good governance.
2. Explain two impacts deeply — how each one works.
3. Recognise the impacts in a real street: the guide's one street with three failures.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Memorises names without workings → each impact has a mechanism in the book: instability from decisions without participation; secrecy until disgrace surfaces; services failing through lost standards, information, incentives and accountability.
- Thinks failure means no government at all → offices exist, shelves exist, files exist — what fails is the governing: law, justice, transparency, empowerment, service, records.
