# Fighting Corruption

## Sub lesson 1 — People and the civil service

The first front is people and the service they staff. **Creating public awareness**: media, campaigns, training and workshops teaching the public what is and is not allowed in officials' duties, the minimum legal standards, and citizens' own rights — shaping society's attitudes to corruption's harm. 

[Picture 28923 — Citizens at a budget hearing with the town's spending drawn as a pie on a board]



**Citizen participation**: pathways and tools for citizens to engage — identifying problems, prioritising needs, controlling and evaluating government commitments, reporting corruption. The worked example is the **participatory budget hearing**, where ending bribery visibly frees resources for essential services.

A **strengthened civil service**: depoliticised and professionalised, with attractive career paths and qualified officials; more accountable and transparent through accessible, available and simplified information, processes and regulations. And **simplifying administrative procedures** themselves — standardised procedures improving coordination, removing duplicated duties and cutting unjustified **discretion**, since complexity and unpredictability breed corruption. Every mechanism on this front attacks the soil named in the last lesson.

## Sub lesson 2 — Media and courts

The second front is light and law. A **free, independent media** with professional investigative capability fights corruption four ways: raising public awareness of causes, consequences and remedies, and fostering a culture of integrity; informing and educating people on corruption's effects — successful action depends on the knowledge media deliver; detecting, investigating, exposing and reporting incidents, bringing cases into the public sphere and instigating judicial involvement; and enhancing accountability by disseminating information on governmental, administrative and business activity so the public can hold power to account. 

[Picture 28924 — A newspaper front page beside a courtroom scales, one beam of light joining them]



The **judiciary closes the chain**: reliable, independent, effective courts make laws actually enforced — contributing by visible independence in dispensing justice, with professional judges and investigating authorities enjoying the greatest degree of independence.

The guide's picture gathers both fronts in one image: five hands — citizen, teacher, journalist, clerk, judge — lifting one stone lettered *corruption*. 

[Picture 28925 — Five hands — citizen, teacher, journalist, clerk, judge — lifting one stone labelled corruption (teacher guide — evidence)]

 No single hand lifts it alone.

## Do It Yourself

1. Design a one-page awareness poster for your school or kebele: what is and is not allowed at one named public desk, and what a citizen's rights are there.
2. Take one real administrative procedure you know — a licence, a registration, a permit. Rewrite it simplified: fewer steps, standard forms, posted fees. Mark where discretion was cut.
3. Write the media's four ways as a checklist. Take one corruption story from the news and check which of the four the reporting actually did.
