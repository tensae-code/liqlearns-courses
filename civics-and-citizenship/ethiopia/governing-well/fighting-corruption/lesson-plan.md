# Lesson plan — Fighting Corruption

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Governing Well and Fighting Corruption · **Lesson:** Fighting Corruption
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 26 — Governing Well and Fighting Corruption (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** corruption is fought on two fronts — people and the civil service first: awareness, participation, a professional service, simpler procedures — then the media's four ways and the independent judiciary that closes the chain.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the democracy level before it — the learner knows who builds democracy and its institutions; this level asks how governing is done well, and what it costs when it is done badly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **People and the civil service** — The first front: awareness, participation, a professional depoliticised civil service, and simpler procedures that cut discretion.
2. **Media and courts** — The second front: the media's four ways of fighting, and the independent judiciary closing the chain.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Corruption — What It Is and Its Forms, just before this lesson in this level. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the first front's four mechanisms.
2. Say what simplifying procedures removes, and why that fights corruption.
3. Give the media's four ways, and the judiciary's closing role.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.
