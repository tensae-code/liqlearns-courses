# Joining In — Clubs and Co-curricular Activities at School

## Do it first

Name the clubs and activities your school offers. Circle one you have never tried. This lesson is the book's case for joining in — actively, not watching.

## Active participation

The chapter's last content line values active participation in the school's accompanying (co-curricular) activities — the clubs and activities beyond the lesson timetable.

[Picture 28041 — three clubs — sport, gardening, singing]

## What joining in grows

Physical and mental strength.

A sense of responsibility and self-confidence.

Special skills.

And meeting new tasks — readiness for things you have not done before.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: taking an active part in the school's co-curricular activities grows strength, responsibility, confidence, skills — and readiness for new tasks. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: co-curricular activities, participation, self-confidence. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
