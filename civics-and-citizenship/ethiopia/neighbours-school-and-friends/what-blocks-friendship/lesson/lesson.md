# What Blocks Friendship — and the Greedy Dog

## Do it first

Name one friendship you have seen cool or break. What blocked it — a misunderstanding? mistrust? greed? Compare your answer with the book's three blocks.

## The obstacles

The chapter closes with the obstacles to positive social interaction, identified in pair and group work from a table of examples, notes and demonstrations.

The activity answers point to three: misunderstanding, mistrust, and the failure to build lasting friendship.

[Picture 28045 — the greedy dog — alone with too much]

## The greedy dog

The chapter's story anchor is the tale of the greedy (selfish) dog, which pupils read and draw the lesson from.

Greed is friendship's enemy in picture form: wanting everything for yourself leaves you alone with more than you can use, while others go without.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: friendship is blocked by misunderstanding, mistrust and the failure to build lasting friendship — the greedy dog is the book's warning picture. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: misunderstanding, mistrust, greedy. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: the obstacles to friendship — and the greedy dog.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
