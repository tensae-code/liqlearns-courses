# What Blocks Friendship — and the Greedy Dog — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1.** What does this lesson teach?
a) the obstacles to friendship — and the greedy dog  b) culture: a people's way of life, handed on  c) who is in a family, and why each person matters  d) cultural heritage: what one generation hands to the next
**Answer: a) the obstacles to friendship — and the greedy dog** — That is the one thing this lesson teaches.

**2.** What does “misunderstanding” mean?
a) not believing another is honest or safe to rely on  b) wanting more than your share, only for yourself  c) It is best to shut other people out and think only of yourself.  d) taking another's meaning wrongly, so the bond is strained
**Answer: d) taking another's meaning wrongly, so the bond is strained** — “misunderstanding” means: taking another's meaning wrongly, so the bond is strained.

**3.** What does “mistrust” mean?
a) wanting more than your share, only for yourself  b) Rules and values do not matter if nobody is watching.  c) not believing another is honest or safe to rely on  d) taking another's meaning wrongly, so the bond is strained
**Answer: c) not believing another is honest or safe to rely on** — “mistrust” means: not believing another is honest or safe to rely on.

**4.** What does “greedy” mean?
a) The strongest person may take whatever they want.  b) wanting more than your share, only for yourself  c) taking another's meaning wrongly, so the bond is strained  d) not believing another is honest or safe to rely on
**Answer: b) wanting more than your share, only for yourself** — “greedy” means: wanting more than your share, only for yourself.

**5.** Which of these belongs to this lesson?
a) The chapter's story anchor is the tale of the greedy (selfish) dog, which pupils read and draw the lesson from  b) It is best to shut other people out and think only of yourself.  c) Rules and values do not matter if nobody is watching.  d) The strongest person may take whatever they want.
**Answer: a) The chapter's story anchor is the tale of the greedy (selfish) dog, which pupils read and draw the lesson from** — It is one of this lesson's own teachings.
