# Growing Good Friendships — Sharing, Trust, Keeping Confidences

## Do it first

Keep one confidence today: something a friend told you in trust stays with you. Notice how it feels to be a safe person. This lesson lists the traits that make friends safe for each other.

## The marks of a good friendship

A shared interest or idea in particular.

Readiness to give and to receive — sharing thoughts, feelings, experiences and materials.

From the activity answers, the acceptable traits: sociability, openness, keeping respect, trust, caring for others, keeping confidences and team spirit.

## Growing them on purpose

The chapter teaches ways of increasing positive social interaction at school and among the public — friendship is grown on purpose, in pairs and groups.

One activity turns on what friends like and dislike, and on speaking about hidden wants inside a friendship — openness practised, not just named.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: good friendships grow by named traits: sociability, openness, respect, trust, caring, keeping confidences and team spirit — ready to give and to receive. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: confidence (kept), sociability, openness. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: the traits that grow good friendships.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
