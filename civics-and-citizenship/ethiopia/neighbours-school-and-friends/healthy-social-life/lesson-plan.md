# Lesson plan — Healthy Social Life — Relationships That Keep Us Well

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Neighbours, School and Friends · **Lesson:** Healthy Social Life — Relationships That Keep Us Well
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — Neighbours, School and Friends (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a healthy social life is made of relationships that fit us comfortably into social life — and they touch our mental health, our physical health and our whole lives.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the lessons before it in this title — the learner already knows the earlier circles of this course (myself, then family, neighbours, school, community and culture) as far as this title. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: What Blocks Friendship — and the Greedy Dog (the previous lesson in this title). Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Social relationships connect with how comfortably we fit into social situations.
2. They affect our mental health, our physical health and our whole lives.
3. Pupils start from the earlier equality lesson, give real examples from their own surroundings, and define a healthy social relationship.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson's scenes are the learner's own home, school and neighbourhood.

## If the learner is stuck
- Believes that “social relationships have no effect on health or on life as a whole” → go back to the lesson's own words: a healthy social life is made of relationships that fit us comfortably into social life — and they touch our mental health, our physical health and our whole lives. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
- Gives the words but no example → ask for the lesson happening once in their own home, school or street today; the example is the proof.
