# Lesson plan — Principles of a Constitution

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Law and the Constitution · **Lesson:** Principles of a Constitution
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 18 — Law and the Constitution (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** six fundamental principles live inside a constitution: popular sovereignty, limited government, separation of powers, rule of law, respect for human rights, and transparency and accountability.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the citizenship levels before it — the learner knows the state and its people; this band teaches the rulebook the state itself must obey.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Kinds of Constitution. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the six principles.
2. Explain each in one sentence.
3. Spot which principle a real public act honours or breaks.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner counts what feels wrong in a mayor's lawless order before the principles are named (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the six principles as one set of pillars, each defined, then the mayor's act judged against all six (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Merges limited government and rule of law → limited government confines power to its grant; rule of law makes the law bind the powerful themselves. Near, but distinct.
- Thinks the principles are decorations → they are tests: count real acts against them, as the lesson does with the mayor.
