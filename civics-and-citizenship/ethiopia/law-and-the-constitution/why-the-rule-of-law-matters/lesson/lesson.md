# Why the rule of law matters

## Do it first

A father is driving his son, late for school. Empty road. Red light. No officer anywhere. The father wants to go through. His wife says wait. You already know the Zeberega version of this story — now judge the question underneath it: do we obey only when watched?

## The story, then the four importances

The opening story, in summary: a father, his son late for school and no officer in sight, wants to run a red light; his wife urges waiting for green. The question it leaves — **do we obey only when watched?** — is the doorway into the four importances.

[Picture 28675 — a car waiting at a red light on an empty road]

[Picture 28676 — a market where contracts are honoured]

**One:** the rule of law is a **precondition of democratic government** — equality before the law regardless of religion, ethnicity, language, colour or gender. **Two:** it frees the **judiciary to decide by law alone**. **Three:** it enhances **individual freedom** — opposing views and a free media (radio, television, press, social media) can exist. **Four:** it **prevents arbitrariness and dictatorship**, whose tools are force, fear and intimidation.

A red light on an empty road is the smallest courtroom in the world: you, the law, and nobody watching.
