# Hands-on — Why the rule of law matters

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
A father is driving his son, late for school. Empty road. Red light. No officer anywhere. The father wants to go through. His wife says wait. You already know the Zeberega version of this story — now judge the question underneath it: do we obey only when watched?

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — the rule of law matters four ways: it grounds democracy, frees judges, protects freedom, and blocks dictatorship — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
