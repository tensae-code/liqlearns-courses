# Constitutionalism, peace and stability

## Do it first

Two versions of the same street. In one, an officer helps a child cross; people plan their week without fear. In the other, the street is empty — everyone indoors, unsure what tomorrow's rule will be. The difference between the streets is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Where constitutionalism holds

Where constitutionalism holds, **individual and group rights are safe** from interference. **Life is predictable and secure** — you can plan, because the rules tomorrow are the rules today. **Police prevent crime** rather than prey on people. And relations — between people, and between people and government — are **governed**: known, lawful, answerable.

[Picture 28693 — a calm kebele street beside the same street emptied by fear]

## Sub lesson 2 — What its absence brings

Its absence brings three fruits. **Lawlessness** — chaos, robbery, violated rights. **Arbitrary rule** — action by personal will, nothing predictable. And **absence of certainty** — fear of today, tomorrow and each other; inequality unprotected; corruption prevailing.

[Picture 28694 — two disputing parties shaking hands across an open constitution]

Close with the book's exercise: imagine a principal, or a police officer, who may beat and torture with no court to appeal to. Name the provisions such power violates. If you can name them, you hold the lesson.
