# Lesson plan — Constitutionalism — Limited Government in Practice

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Law and the Constitution · **Lesson:** Constitutionalism — Limited Government in Practice
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 18 — Law and the Constitution (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** constitutionalism is a constitution actually obeyed — limited government in real practice, where rights are realised, power is checked, and the rule of law stands against arbitrary judgement.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the citizenship levels before it — the learner knows the state and its people; this band teaches the rulebook the state itself must obey.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say the difference between having a constitution and constitutionalism.
2. Give the three parts of constitutionalism's importance.
3. Answer the homework question: does a constitution alone imply constitutionalism?

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks constitutionalism is a kind of constitution → it is a *practice*: government acting according to the constitution, rights actually realised.
- Thinks the paper tiger is about printing quality → it is about enforcement: unenforced, the fiercest text frightens nobody.
