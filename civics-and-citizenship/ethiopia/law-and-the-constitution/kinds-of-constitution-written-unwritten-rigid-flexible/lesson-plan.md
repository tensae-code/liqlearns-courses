# Lesson plan — Kinds of constitution — written, unwritten, rigid, flexible

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Law and the Constitution · **Lesson:** Kinds of constitution — written, unwritten, rigid, flexible
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 18 — Law and the Constitution (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** constitutions are classified by form — written or unwritten — and by how hard they are to change — rigid or flexible.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the levels before it in this course's path order (recommended, never forced); inside its own title it stands on the lessons before it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
1. **Written or not** — **Written constitutions** are gathered in one document: the United States, Canada, Nigeria and Ethiopia are the book's examples.
2. **Easy or hard to change** — The second axis is amendment.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Importance of constitutionalism. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Tell written from unwritten with examples.
2. Tell rigid from flexible.
3. Define amendment.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Two books. One is printed and bound, heavy, with a lock on its spine. The other is not a book at all — it lives in customs, old acts and court decisions, carried in people's practice. Both are constitutions. Sort them as you read.
2. **The explanation** — the lesson's parts in order (lesson/lesson.md).

   - ADD AUDIO: none named in the placements for this lesson — no audio slot is built.
3. **Say it to your companion** — explain the one thing in your own words, with the lesson's own case (lesson/lesson.md).
