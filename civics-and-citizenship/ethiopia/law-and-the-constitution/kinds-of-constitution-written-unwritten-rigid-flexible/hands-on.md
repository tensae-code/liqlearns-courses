# Hands-on — Kinds of constitution — written, unwritten, rigid, flexible

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
Two books. One is printed and bound, heavy, with a lock on its spine. The other is not a book at all — it lives in customs, old acts and court decisions, carried in people's practice. Both are constitutions. Sort them as you read.

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — constitutions are classified by form — written or unwritten — and by how hard they are to change — rigid or flexible — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
