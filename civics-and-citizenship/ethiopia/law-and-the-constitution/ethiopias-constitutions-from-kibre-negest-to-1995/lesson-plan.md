# Lesson plan — Ethiopia's Constitutions — From Kibre Negest to 1995

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Law and the Constitution · **Lesson:** Ethiopia's Constitutions — From Kibre Negest to 1995
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 18 — Law and the Constitution (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Ethiopia's constitutional line runs from the Kibre Negest through 1931, 1955, 1987 and the 1991 Charter to the 1995 FDRE Constitution — a long history of constitutions, judged not a good history of constitutionalism.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the citizenship levels before it — the learner knows the state and its people; this band teaches the rulebook the state itself must obey.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The traditional order** — Kibre Negest, Fetha Negest, Sirate Mengist — myth legitimising monarchs, not securing citizens
2. **1931 and 1955** — the granted charter of 1931 and the revised charter of 1955 — imperial power unlimited in both
3. **1987 and the 1991 Transitional Charter** — the 1974–1987 vacuum, the PDRE book, and the Charter that leaned toward the citizen
4. **The 1995 FDRE Constitution** — federal, parliamentary, two houses, rights guaranteed — and the five principles, Arts. 8–12

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Constitutionalism — Limited Government in Practice; this lesson is its Ethiopian evidence. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Walk the line in order: traditional order, 1931, 1955, 1987, 1991, 1995.
2. Say what each stage secured — and for whom.
3. Name the five principles of the 1995 Constitution with their articles.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes the vacuum's dates → the monarchy fell in 1974; the PDRE book came in 1987. Thirteen years of decrees between them.
- Thinks 1955's size (131 articles) means stronger rights → imperial power stayed unlimited and rights unguaranteed; size is not the measure.
