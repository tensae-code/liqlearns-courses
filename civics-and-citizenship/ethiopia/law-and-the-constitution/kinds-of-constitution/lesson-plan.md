# Lesson plan — Kinds of Constitution

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Law and the Constitution · **Lesson:** Kinds of Constitution
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 18 — Law and the Constitution (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** constitutions are sorted two ways: written or unwritten, and rigid or flexible — and also by where power sits, federal or unitary.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the citizenship levels before it — the learner knows the state and its people; this band teaches the rulebook the state itself must obey.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Written and unwritten** — one codified document against customs and conventions — with the Chapter Three doing task
2. **Rigid and flexible; federal and unitary** — by amendment procedure (rigid/flexible) and by distribution of power (federal/unitary)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: What a Constitution Is, just before this lesson. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Contrast written and unwritten constitutions, with examples of each.
2. Contrast rigid and flexible, and federal and unitary.
3. Carry out the doing task: find citizens' rights in Chapter Three of the 1995 Constitution.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks unwritten means 'no constitution' → it lives in customs and conventions; it exists, but cannot be opened and read.
- Confuses rigid with written → different axes: one is about the document, the other about how hard change is.
