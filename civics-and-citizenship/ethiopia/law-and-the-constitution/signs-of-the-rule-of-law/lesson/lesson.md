# Signs of the rule of law — independent courts, equal law, peace

## Do it first

How would you know, walking through a town, whether the rule of law lives there? Not by its law books. This lesson gives you three signposts to look for — check any real place against them.

## Three signposts

**Sign one: an independent judiciary.** Judges are free of party, private interest and popular pressure; they answer to the law, not to the crowd or the powerful.

**Sign two: equal application of laws.** Like cases are treated alike, so people can plan their lives by the law. Inside this sign stand its protections: everyone is **presumed innocent until a court finds guilt**; there is **no arbitrary detention, imprisonment or seizure**; and punishment is set by a court and **proportionate** to the offence.

**Sign three: prevailing peace and order.** When security fails, faith in government fails with it, and people take the law into their own hands — the surest sign the rule of law has left the street.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson's one thing to your companion in your own words: you can see the rule of law by three signs: independent courts, equal application of law, and prevailing peace and order. Use the lesson's own case or example, not the book's sentences. Your companion asks one hard question; answer it from the lesson, or say honestly what you would check.
