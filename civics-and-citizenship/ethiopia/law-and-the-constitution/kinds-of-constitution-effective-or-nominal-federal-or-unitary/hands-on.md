# Hands-on — Kinds of constitution — effective or nominal, federal or unitary

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
Two crowns. One is solid metal, worn daily, obeyed. The other is paper — impressive in a photograph, weightless in the hand. Both are called crowns. This lesson's first pair is exactly that difference, applied to constitutions.

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — constitutions are also classified by performance — effective or nominal — and by structure — federal or unitary — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
