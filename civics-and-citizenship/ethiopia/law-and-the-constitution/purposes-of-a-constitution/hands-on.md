# Hands-on — Purposes of a constitution

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
Draw a house frame: beams, rooms, a flag on the roof. A constitution is drawn the same way in this lesson — a frame within which a whole country lives. Guess its jobs before reading: what must a frame do?

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — a constitution's core purpose is limiting government power while securing citizens' rights — and it does six more things besides — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
