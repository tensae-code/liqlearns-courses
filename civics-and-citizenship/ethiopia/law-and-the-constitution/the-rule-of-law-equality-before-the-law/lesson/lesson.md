# The rule of law — equality before the law

## Do it first

A school guard catches two boys fighting over a rope. He knows the principal's son started it. He punishes the other boy — the innocent one. Before reading: which rule has been broken, even if no written school rule names it?

## Sub lesson 1 — No one above the law

Laws are binding rules protecting safety and rights against everyone — **including government itself**. The **rule of law** means, first, the **absence of arbitrary government power**: no official may act on mere will. And second, **equality before the law**: no legal person holds benefits unavailable to all, and no one escapes legal penalty.

[Picture 28670 — scales level between a principal's son and an ordinary pupil]

Judge the opening case by those marks. The guard punished the innocent pupil and spared the principal's son. Penalty followed connection, not conduct. That is exactly what equality before the law forbids — whoever your father is.

## Sub lesson 2 — Justice beneath it

Beneath the rule stands **justice**: moral rightness and fairness. A law that discriminates by gender, colour, race or status is **unjust**, even if it is applied evenly — the rule of law needs just laws, not only equal application. And justice must be **unbiased**: the pupil who respectfully protests a teacher who only ever calls on the same two pupils is asking for exactly this.

Use the lesson's own case or example, not the book's sentences. Your companion asks one hard question; answer it from the lesson, or say honestly what you would check.
