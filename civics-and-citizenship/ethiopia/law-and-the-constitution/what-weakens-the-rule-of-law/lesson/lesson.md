# What weakens the rule of law

## Do it first

A courthouse wall with four cracks. Each crack has a name. Before reading, guess the four: what eats a legal system from inside? Then meet one bakery worker who patched the wall instead of cracking it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The four factors

Four factors weaken the rule of law, each traced to its damage and its remedy.

[Picture 28681 — four cracks in a courthouse wall, a citizen telephoning police]

**Corruption** — public authority used for private gain — is the **chief impediment**, fought by ending rent-seeking and building hatred of malpractice. **Illiteracy** — citizens who cannot read the law stay silent when government acts above it. **Poor leadership** — undemocratic leaders resent the law's limits and rule for themselves. **Lack of patriotism** — the unpatriotic break the law for gain instead of fighting malpractice.

Notice the pattern: three of the four are failures of people, not of paper. Laws do not defend themselves.

## Sub lesson 2 — Case: the bakery worker who strengthened it

The closing case, in summary. A bakery worker discovers that injera is being mixed with harmful substances. Reporting it carries risk — the offenders are her employers. She reports it anyway. The police gather evidence, and the court imprisons and fines the offenders.

[Picture 28682 — a courthouse wall with four labelled cracks]

One report, made despite the risk, set the whole machinery of the rule of law moving: report, evidence, court, judgement. The four factors weaken the wall; citizens like this worker are how it is strengthened.
