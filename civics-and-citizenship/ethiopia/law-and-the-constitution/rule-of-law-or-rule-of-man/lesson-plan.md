# Lesson plan — Rule of law or rule of man?

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Law and the Constitution · **Lesson:** Rule of law or rule of man?
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 18 — Law and the Constitution (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** under rule of man the ruler's will is the law; under rule of law the law stands above every individual will, the ruler's included.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the levels before it in this course's path order (recommended, never forced); inside its own title it stands on the lessons before it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. ## Sub lessons

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Signs of the rule of law. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Describe rule of man from history.
2. State rule of law as its opposite.
3. Judge a ruler's act by the contrast.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Two panels. In the first, a ruler raises one finger and a written law changes. In the second, the same ruler bows to a law book larger than himself. Which panel would you rather live in — and what exactly differs between them?
