# Hands-on — Rule of law or rule of man?

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
Two panels. In the first, a ruler raises one finger and a written law changes. In the second, the same ruler bows to a law book larger than himself. Which panel would you rather live in — and what exactly differs between them?

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — under rule of man the ruler's will is the law; under rule of law the law stands above every individual will, the ruler's included — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
