# Rule of law or rule of man?

## Do it first

Two panels. In the first, a ruler raises one finger and a written law changes. In the second, the same ruler bows to a law book larger than himself. Which panel would you rather live in — and what exactly differs between them?

## The contrast

For most of history, ruler and law were one. That is **rule of man**: people imprisoned, property seized, torture used — often with no formal charge — because the law was the ruler's tool. It governed the citizens; it never governed the state, meaning it never governed the ruler.

[Picture 28679 — two panels: a finger changing a law; a ruler bowing to a law book]

[Picture 28680 — two panels: a book above a king; a king above a small book]

**Rule of law** is its opposite. Law, made by the land's supreme lawmaking authority, stands **above every individual will** — and even the ruler must rule through legal means.

The question in the lesson's title is therefore not academic. Every act of power answers it: was that done by law, or by a person's will? Learn to ask it automatically.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson's one thing to your companion in your own words: under rule of man the ruler's will is the law; under rule of law the law stands above every individual will, the ruler's included. Use the lesson's own case or example, not the book's sentences. Your companion asks one hard question; answer it from the lesson, or say honestly what you would check.

1. Practise this until you can do it without looking: describe rule of man from history.

3. Practise this until you can do it without looking: judge a ruler's act by the contrast.
