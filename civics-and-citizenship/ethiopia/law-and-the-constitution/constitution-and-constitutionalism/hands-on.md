# Hands-on — Constitution and constitutionalism — the rulebook, and obeying it

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
Your class must write its own one-page constitution, starting 'We the students of this class…' What three things must it contain? And a harder question: if the class then ignores its own page, does it have constitutionalism — or only a constitution?

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — a constitution is the supreme law on paper; constitutionalism is government actually conducted within its bounds — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
