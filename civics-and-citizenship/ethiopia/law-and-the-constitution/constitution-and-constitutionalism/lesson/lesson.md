# Constitution and constitutionalism — the rulebook, and obeying it

## Do it first

Your class must write its own one-page constitution, starting 'We the students of this class…' What three things must it contain? And a harder question: if the class then ignores its own page, does it have constitutionalism — or only a constitution?

## Sub lesson 1 — The constitution

A **constitution** is the supreme, fundamental law: the document holding a nation's **basic principles**, its **government's organisation and powers**, and its **citizens' rights**. It is also a reflection of the nation's aspirations — the country it means to be.

[Picture 28683 — a constitution book beside a building shaped by its outline]

Every other law stands under it. A law that contradicts the constitution is not merely a bad law; it is void.

## Sub lesson 2 — Constitutionalism

**Constitutionalism** is different, and the difference is the lesson. It is government **actually conducted within the constitution's bounds**: power limited, and authority conditional on observing the limits. It is a **political culture** more than a text — constitutions followed and enforced by government and citizens alike.

[Picture 28684 — a class writing its one-page constitution on the board]

So a country can hold a constitution and still be ruled arbitrarily. The classroom activity anchors both ideas: drafting class rules, with a sample preamble — "We the students of 8A class…" — is writing a constitution; obeying it on a bad day is constitutionalism.
