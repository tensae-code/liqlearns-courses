# Lesson plan — Constitution and constitutionalism — the rulebook, and obeying it

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Law and the Constitution · **Lesson:** Constitution and constitutionalism — the rulebook, and obeying it
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 18 — Law and the Constitution (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a constitution is the supreme law on paper; constitutionalism is government actually conducted within its bounds.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the levels before it in this course's path order (recommended, never forced); inside its own title it stands on the lessons before it.

## Sub lessons
1. **The constitution** — A **constitution** is the supreme, fundamental law: the document holding a nation's **basic principles**, its **government's organisation and powers**, and its **citizens' rights**.
2. **Constitutionalism** — **Constitutionalism** is different, and the difference is the lesson.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson

## What the learner can do when it ends

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Your class must write its own one-page constitution, starting 'We the students of this class…' What three things must it contain? And a harder question: if the class then ignores its own page, does it have constitutionalism — or only a constitution?
2. **The explanation** — the lesson's parts in order (lesson/lesson.md).
