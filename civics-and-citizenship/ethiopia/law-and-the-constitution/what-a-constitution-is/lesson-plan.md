# Lesson plan — What a Constitution Is

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ethiopia · **Title:** Law and the Constitution · **Lesson:** What a Constitution Is
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 18 — Law and the Constitution (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a constitution is the fundamental, supreme law of a state — the whole frame of laws, principles and rules by which a government works and citizens hold their rights and duties.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the citizenship levels before it — the learner knows the state and its people; this band teaches the rulebook the state itself must obey.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. ## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define a constitution in one full sentence.
2. Say what a constitution defines: government, its functions, citizens' rights and duties.
3. Explain why every other law stands under it.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks a constitution is just a list of laws → it is the *aggregate frame* — laws, customs, conventions, principles — and it defines the government itself.
- Thinks parliament stands above it → parliament works *inside* it; that is what 'supreme' means.
