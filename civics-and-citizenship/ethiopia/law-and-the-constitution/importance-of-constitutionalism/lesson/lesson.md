# Importance of constitutionalism — limited power, real rights

## Do it first

An official must hand an account book to watching citizens outside parliament: here is what I did with your power and your money. That handing-over is this whole lesson in one picture. Read for the six reasons it matters.

## The six importances

**One:** constitutionalism **enhances democracy** — guiding it with rights, limited government and checks and balances, in letter and spirit. **Two:** it **promotes equality before the law** — no twisting the law for rich or poor, king or servant, with equal access to courts, hearing and advice. **Three:** it provides **limited, accountable government** — officials are trustees, replaceable at elections when they fail the majority.

**Four:** it **respects fundamental human rights in practice**, with court redress for violations. **Five:** it ensures **popular sovereignty** — the people's mandate, the vote, and recall of failed representatives. **Six:** it **promotes the common good** — social interest above personal interest.

The student book's preamble-writing activity for the classroom rules applies all six at desk scale: write the rules, then live inside them.
