# Hands-on — Sources of law — constitution, custom, belief, legislation

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening again, for real
Four springs feed one river. One spring is largest. Before reading, guess the four: where can a rule come from before anyone writes it in a law book? A people's long habit? A holy teaching? A parliament? A founding document?

## Sort it on the table
On paper, write the lesson's key pairs from its matching questions — one term per slip. Shuffle the slips, then sort and match them again from memory, saying each meaning aloud as you place it. Any pair you cannot explain goes back into the lesson for one more read.

## Take it home
Find this lesson where you live: one real example of its one thing — law flows from four sources: the constitution, custom, religion and morality, and legislation — in your family, your school or your street. Write two sentences about it: what happened, and what the lesson says about it. Bring the sentences back and read them to your companion.
