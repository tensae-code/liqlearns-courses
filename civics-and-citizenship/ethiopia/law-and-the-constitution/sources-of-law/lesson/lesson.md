# Sources of law — constitution, custom, belief, legislation

## Do it first

Four springs feed one river. One spring is largest. Before reading, guess the four: where can a rule come from before anyone writes it in a law book? A people's long habit? A holy teaching? A parliament? A founding document?

## Four springs, one river

Law's **sources** are its origins — where law comes from. There are four, taught in the book's order.

[Picture 28673 — four springs feeding one river labelled law]

[Picture 28674 — a river drawn from four springs joining into one stream]

**The constitution** — the power map setting government's structure, limits and citizens' rights: the **fundamental** source. **Custom** — long practice grown binding; once it was the way disputes were settled, and it is the seed from which most state law grew, as customs were written into authoritative rules. **Religion and morality** — codes of conduct societies held as higher law, much of it later enacted by the state. **Legislation** — the legislature enacting definite rules: **now the primary source**, illustrated by a school code of conduct and classroom rules.

Hold the distinction: the constitution is fundamental — deepest. Legislation is primary — the busiest spring today.
