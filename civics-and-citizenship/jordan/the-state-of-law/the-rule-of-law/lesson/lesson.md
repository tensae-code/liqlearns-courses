# The Rule of Law

## Do it first

A strong official breaks a rule that an ordinary person must obey. Say what the rule of law requires to happen — and what a state without it would look like.

## A state ruled by law

The Grade 9 book's third unit is named for its subject: the State of Law. Its first lesson teaches what it means that the state is ruled by law — law is not a tool the powerful pick up and put down; it binds the rulers as it binds the ruled, and no one stands above it.

## Why it binds the citizen's life together

The rule of law is what makes the rest of the course trustworthy: rights hold because law holds, elections count because law counts them, and officials answer because law questions them. The next lesson — accountability — is this one put to work.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the rule of law means the state itself is ruled by law: law stands above persons, and every act of power must answer to it. Give one real example from your own life in Jordan, and name the lesson's key words: rule of law, law, state, justice. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Jordan's own books teach civics by starting from Jordanian society itself — its make-up, its culture, its live issues — and only then widening to participation, law and heritage. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
