# Jordan of the Future

## Do it first

Write three sentences about the Jordan you want to live in as an adult — one about its land and resources, one about its place among the Arabs, one about its people's life.

## The challenges, tied to the land and the role

The Grade 10 first term closes looking forward: Jordan of the future, and the challenges Jordan faces. The book ties those challenges to real grounds — Jordan's geography, its Arab belonging and its national role — so the future is read from the homeland's actual position, not from wishes.

## The pupils' own future

A future lesson in a civics book is a charge to its readers: the Jordan of the future is the one today's pupils will run. Everything before this lesson — identity, citizenship, participation, constitution — is the equipment they carry into building it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Jordan of the future is the homeland the pupils will inherit and build — its challenges read honestly, tied to its geography, its Arab belonging and its national role. Give one real example from your own life in Jordan, and name the lesson's key words: future, challenges, geography, national role. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Jordan's own books teach civics by starting from Jordanian society itself — its make-up, its culture, its live issues — and only then widening to participation, law and heritage. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
