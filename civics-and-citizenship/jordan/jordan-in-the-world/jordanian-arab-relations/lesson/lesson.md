# Jordanian–Arab Relations

## Do it first

Name three Arab countries and one thing that joins Jordan to each — language, history, family, trade, a shared cause.

## The nearest circle outward

The second term turns outward. Jordan does not stand alone in the world: its first circle is the Arab world — the peoples who share its language, much of its history, and its belonging. The lesson teaches Jordan's relations with the Arab world as that nearest circle: what joins them and how the relations are carried.

## Belonging beyond the border

Arab belonging was already part of the pupil's identity in Level 1; here it becomes policy and relation — the homeland acting among its sister states. The pupil learns to read Jordan's place on the map as a member of a family of nations, before the next lesson widens the view to the whole world.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Jordan's relations with the Arab world are the homeland's nearest circle outward — ties of language, history and belonging joining Jordan to its Arab sister states. Give one real example from your own life in Jordan, and name the lesson's key words: Arab relations, Arab world, belonging, neighbours. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Jordan's own books teach civics by starting from Jordanian society itself — its make-up, its culture, its live issues — and only then widening to participation, law and heritage. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
