# Civil Society Organisations

## Do it first

Name one organisation in Jordan that is not part of the government — a charity, a society, a club or association. Say what it does and who it gathers.

## Between citizen and state

Public life is not only the citizen alone and the state above. Between them stand civil society organisations: the associations, societies and institutions citizens form by their own choice to work on what concerns them together — welfare, culture, profession, neighbourhood and cause.

## Their place in participation

These organisations are a school and an instrument of participation: inside them citizens practise organising, deciding and serving together, and through them the citizen's voice reaches further than one voice can. That is why the book places them inside the unit on political participation.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: civil society organisations are the bodies citizens form between themselves and the state — associations and institutions through which people act together on their shared concerns. Give one real example from your own life in Jordan, and name the lesson's key words: civil society, organisations, associations, participation. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Jordan's own books teach civics by starting from Jordanian society itself — its make-up, its culture, its live issues — and only then widening to participation, law and heritage. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
