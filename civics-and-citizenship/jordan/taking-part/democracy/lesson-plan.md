## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Democracy — the door into participation** — Democracy means the people govern themselves: the decisions that rule the community are made with the people's share in them. The Grade 9 book teaches it first, as the door into political participation — elections, organisations and volunteering are ways through that door.
2. **Democracy — deepened** — The Grade 10 book returns to democracy and deepens it: with citizenship and identity already in hand, the pupil meets democracy again as part of the bond between the Jordanian and his state — the form of public life the whole course builds toward.

Gate pool: the prove-it check for each sub lesson is at most 5 questions, mixed with earlier parts, picked as the hardest from the rank right under the wanted sub lesson — drawn from this lesson's question files (questions/).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: democracy is the people's rule of themselves — the door into political participation, where citizens share in deciding how they are governed.

3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the learner's own life in Jordan and the book's own teaching, as Jordan's National and Civic Education works from Jordanian society itself, then participation, law, heritage, and the citizen's own integrity.
