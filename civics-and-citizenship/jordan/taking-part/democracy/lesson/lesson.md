# Democracy

## Do it first

Your class must choose one of two activities. Describe two ways the class could decide — one where one person decides for all, one where all share in deciding. Which is democracy?

## Sub lesson 1 — Democracy — the door into participation

Democracy means the people govern themselves: the decisions that rule the community are made with the people's share in them. The Grade 9 book teaches it first, as the door into political participation — elections, organisations and volunteering are ways through that door.

## Sub lesson 2 — Democracy — deepened

The Grade 10 book returns to democracy and deepens it: with citizenship and identity already in hand, the pupil meets democracy again as part of the bond between the Jordanian and his state — the form of public life the whole course builds toward.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: democracy is the people's rule of themselves — the door into political participation, where citizens share in deciding how they are governed. Give one real example from your own life in Jordan, and name the lesson's key words: democracy, people, participation, decision. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Jordan's own books teach civics by starting from Jordanian society itself — its make-up, its culture, its live issues — and only then widening to participation, law and heritage. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
