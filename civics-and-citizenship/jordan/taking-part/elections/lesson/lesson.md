# Elections

## Do it first

Imagine your school elects a pupil representative. List what must happen for the election to be fair — who may vote, how votes are counted, how the result is known.

## Sub lesson 1 — Elections — the citizen's instrument

An election is how a democracy turns the people's will into chosen representatives. The Grade 9 lesson teaches elections as the citizen's instrument: the tool by which an ordinary citizen, with one vote, shares in deciding who governs.

## Sub lesson 2 — I and the ballot box

The Grade 10 lesson defines elections, weighs their importance, and puts the pupil in front of the ballot box itself: the moment where citizenship becomes an act. The pupil is placed where a voter stands.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: elections are the citizen's instrument: the act by which citizens choose who represents them — defined, weighed, and finally faced at the ballot box itself. Give one real example from your own life in Jordan, and name the lesson's key words: elections, vote, ballot box, representatives. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Jordan's own books teach civics by starting from Jordanian society itself — its make-up, its culture, its live issues — and only then widening to participation, law and heritage. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
