# Participation in Public Life

## Do it first

List three ways a citizen takes part in public life — at school, in the neighbourhood, in the country. Mark which are open to you now and which come later.

## Not achieved without it

The Grade 10 book states the lesson's core plainly: active citizenship is not achieved without participation in public life. A citizen who only holds a status but never takes part has the name of citizenship without its life.

## A right and a duty

Participation is taught as two things at once: a right — no one may bar the citizen from public life — and a duty — the citizen owes public life their share. It is also a civilised behaviour: taking part peaceably, in the open, by the forms the community lives by.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: participation in public life is active citizenship in act — a civilised behaviour, and both a right and a duty of every citizen, without which active citizenship is not achieved. Give one real example from your own life in Jordan, and name the lesson's key words: participation, public life, active citizenship, duty. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Jordan's own books teach civics by starting from Jordanian society itself — its make-up, its culture, its live issues — and only then widening to participation, law and heritage. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
