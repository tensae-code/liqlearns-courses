# Voluntary Work

## Do it first

Name one thing in your school or neighbourhood that needs doing and nobody is paid to do. Say what part of it you could volunteer for.

## Participation you can practise now

Most of this unit's instruments — voting, parties — wait for the pupil's majority. Voluntary work does not wait: it is participation open to the pupil today, work chosen and given freely for the community's good, without pay.

## What volunteering builds

The volunteer gives work and receives formation: habits of service, knowledge of the community's real needs, and the standing of someone the community can rely on. The book closes its participation unit with volunteering because it is where the pupil's participation stops being a topic and becomes a practice.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: voluntary work is participation a school pupil can already practise — work given freely, unpaid, for the good of the community. Give one real example from your own life in Jordan, and name the lesson's key words: voluntary work, volunteer, service, community. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Jordan's own books teach civics by starting from Jordanian society itself — its make-up, its culture, its live issues — and only then widening to participation, law and heritage. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: voluntary work is participation a school pupil can already practise — work given freely, unpaid, for the good of the community.
3. Teach this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
