# Civics and Citizenship — Jordan course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Jordan inside the Civics and Citizenship
folder, organised by **levels, not grades**: a learner
climbs when they have proved the level, not when a school
calendar says so. The school grades the books come from
(Grades 9 and 10) appear only in each lesson's sources
(provenance), never as the course's organisation. The path
follows Jordan's own books' order: the course starts from
Jordanian society itself — its make-up, its culture, its
live issues — and only then widens to participation, law
and heritage; the Grade 10 books build citizenship as an
identity before a mechanism, turn outward to Jordan among
the Arabs and the world and the heritage it shares with
humanity, and end inward, with the citizen's own integrity
and conduct — national integrity taught as the personal
form of the rule of law. That ladder IS the placements
file's ladder sketch, followed as filed.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this
country's own التربية الوطنية والمدنية (National and Civic
Education) student books (National Centre for Curriculum
Development, Jordan, 2024, adopted from 2024/2025) — except
the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people
making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the
books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book list and the
acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **My Society and My Jordanian Identity** — Jordanian
   society and its characteristics; Jordanian national
   culture; the live issues of my society examined as a
   citizen's business; then what national and civic
   education is (aimed at a society of justice, human
   dignity, freedoms and the rule of law), my Jordanian
   identity, citizenship as the bond between the Jordanian
   and his state, and serving the flag
   (`my-society/`, `citizenship-and-identity/`). 7 lessons.
2. **Taking Part** — democracy as the door into political
   participation (one lesson, two sub lessons: the Grade 9
   treatment, then the Grade 10 deepening); elections (one
   lesson, two sub lessons: the citizen's instrument, then
   "I and the Ballot Box"); civil society organisations
   between citizen and state; voluntary work, the
   participation a pupil can already practise;
   participation in public life as a right and a duty; and
   political parties, with Jordan's party history from
   1927 (`taking-part/`). 6 lessons.
3. **The State of Law, the Constitution and Human
   Rights** — the rule of law (the state itself ruled by
   law); accountability (المساءلة والمحاسبة — officials
   answer for what they do); the Jordanian constitution
   and the rule of law (an independent Arab state,
   sovereignty belonging to the nation); the international
   bill of human rights (all people born free and equal in
   dignity and rights); and human rights in Jordan, held
   and protected at home (`the-state-of-law/`,
   `the-constitution/`, `human-rights/`). 5 lessons.
4. **Heritage and Jordan in the World** — Jordanian
   national heritage and what it consists of; chosen
   models of it; the role of museums in preserving it;
   national heritage taken whole; world heritage and
   UNESCO, with Damascus as a worked example; Jordan's
   relations with the Arab world; Jordan's international
   relations beyond it; and Jordan of the future — its
   challenges tied to its geography, its Arab belonging
   and its national role (`heritage/`,
   `jordan-in-the-world/`). 8 lessons.
5. **Integrity and Life Skills** — national integrity
   (النزاهة) as a national value and the personal form of
   the rule of law; fighting corruption and favouritism
   (الفساد والمحسوبية); reading media content critically;
   and behaviour — the citizen's own conduct, where the
   course lands (`integrity/`, `life-skills/`). 4 lessons.

**Totals: 30 lessons, 32 placements stocked** (Level 1: 7 ·
Level 2: 6 · Level 3: 5 · Level 4: 8 · Level 5: 4 — all from
three student books: *National and Civic Education* Grades
9 and 10, Terms 1–2 (National Centre for Curriculum
Development, Arabic, 2024, all COMPLETE as filed); see
ABOUT.md).

*Standing note on the count: the three books' 32 placements
coin 30 distinct lesson names — two names are coined twice.
Democracy (Grade 9, printed p. 26; Grade 10 Term 1, printed
p. 27) and Elections (Grade 9, printed p. 31; Grade 10
Term 1, printed p. 60) each stock into ONE lesson as its
two sub lessons, in ladder order. 32 placements, 30
lessons, every placement stocked exactly once.*
