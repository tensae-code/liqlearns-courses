# The Jordanian Constitution and the Rule of Law

## Do it first

Say what a country would need written down before any other laws could be made — who holds power, where it comes from, what no law may break. You are describing a constitution's work.

## The constitution and the state it founds

The Grade 10 book joins the constitution and the rule of law in one lesson. The constitution is the law above laws: it establishes the state — Jordan an independent Arab state — and declares where sovereignty lies: sovereignty belongs to the nation, the source from which all authority in the state proceeds.

## The sovereignty of law

From that foundation the lesson draws the sovereignty of law: because the constitution stands highest, every authority in the state acts under law, and the citizen's rights and the state's powers are both read from the same written source. The constitution is thus not a ceremony text but the working frame of the citizen's whole public life.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Jordan's constitution is the state's highest law: it founds an independent Arab state whose sovereignty belongs to the nation, and it sets the sovereignty of law over the whole of public life. Give one real example from your own life in Jordan, and name the lesson's key words: constitution, sovereignty, nation, law. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Jordan's own books teach civics by starting from Jordanian society itself — its make-up, its culture, its live issues — and only then widening to participation, law and heritage. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
