# Human Rights in Jordan

## Do it first

Take one right from the international bill — to learn, to be safe, to speak. Say how that right shows itself in your daily life in Jordan, and what protects it.

## Rights at home

After the world's bill, the homeland's own account: human rights in Jordan — how the rights every person is born with are held in Jordan's own order, in its constitution and laws, and in the life of its society.

## How they are protected

A right on paper needs protection in fact. The lesson traces how rights are protected in Jordan — the standing of the person before the law, and the bodies and forms through which a right can be claimed when it is pressed. The citizen who knows both the right and its protection can actually hold the right.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: human rights in Jordan are the same universal rights as they are held and protected in the homeland — the pupil learns how Jordan holds them and what protects them. Give one real example from your own life in Jordan, and name the lesson's key words: human rights, Jordan, protection, rights. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Jordan's own books teach civics by starting from Jordanian society itself — its make-up, its culture, its live issues — and only then widening to participation, law and heritage. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
