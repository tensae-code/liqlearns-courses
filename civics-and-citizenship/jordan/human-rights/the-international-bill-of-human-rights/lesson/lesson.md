# The International Bill of Human Rights

## Do it first

Say in your own words what it means that a person is born free and equal in dignity and rights — before any government gives them anything.

## Born free and equal

The Grade 10 second term opens its human-rights unit with the international bill: the set of world instruments that declare the rights of every human being. It opens where the declaration itself opens — with the first principle that all people are born free and equal in dignity and rights: rights begin at birth, not at a government's gift.

## A bill for the whole world

The bill is international: it speaks across borders, to every state and about every person. The pupil meets it as the widest frame of rights — the frame inside which the next lesson, human rights in Jordan, sets the homeland's own holding and protection of those rights.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the international bill of human rights is the world's own written promise about every person's rights — opening with the principle that all people are born free and equal in dignity and rights. Give one real example from your own life in Jordan, and name the lesson's key words: human rights, international bill, dignity, equality. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Jordan's own books teach civics by starting from Jordanian society itself — its make-up, its culture, its live issues — and only then widening to participation, law and heritage. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
