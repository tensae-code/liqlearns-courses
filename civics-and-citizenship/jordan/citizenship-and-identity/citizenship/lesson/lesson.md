# Citizenship

## Do it first

Say what you think a citizen has that a visitor does not — and what a citizen owes that a visitor does not owe.

## The bond between citizen and state

Citizenship names the legal and lived bond between a person and their state. For the Jordanian, it is the standing of belonging to Jordan: the state recognises the citizen as its own, and the citizen belongs to the state — a two-way bond, not a one-way label.

## Rights and duties in one bond

The bond carries both directions at once: the citizen holds rights the state protects, and the citizen owes duties the state and society rely on. The lessons after this one — participation, law, rights — are this one bond opened out part by part.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: citizenship is the bond between the Jordanian and his state — the standing that joins a person to the homeland with rights on one side and duties on the other. Give one real example from your own life in Jordan, and name the lesson's key words: citizenship, rights, duties, state. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Jordan's own books teach civics by starting from Jordanian society itself — its make-up, its culture, its live issues — and only then widening to participation, law and heritage. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
