# What National and Civic Education Is

## Do it first

Say in one sentence what you think a person must learn in order to be a good citizen of Jordan. Keep it; the lesson gives the book's own answer.

## The concept and its meanings

The Grade 10 course opens by defining its own subject: national and civic education is the education that builds the citizen — knowledge of the homeland, belonging to it, and the conduct of a member of its society. Its meanings reach from loving the homeland to knowing one's rights and duties inside it.

## What it aims at

The book aims this education at a named goal: a society of justice, human dignity, freedoms and the rule of law. Those four aims are the measure of everything the course teaches afterwards — identity, citizenship, participation and rights are all taught as roads toward that society.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: national and civic education is the learning that forms a citizen — aimed, in Jordan's book's own framing, at a society of justice, human dignity, freedoms and the rule of law. Give one real example from your own life in Jordan, and name the lesson's key words: national education, civic education, citizen, dignity. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Jordan's own books teach civics by starting from Jordanian society itself — its make-up, its culture, its live issues — and only then widening to participation, law and heritage. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
