# Media Content

## Do it first

Take one claim you saw or heard this week from a screen or a speaker. List three questions you could ask before believing it or passing it on.

## Content that must be read, not swallowed

The citizen's day is full of media content — news, posts, clips and claims arriving faster than they can be checked. The lesson teaches the pupil to read that content critically: to ask who made it, on what evidence, and to what end, before believing it.

## Belief and passing-on are acts

To pass content on is to vouch for it. The critical reader therefore protects two things at once: their own judgement, and their community's information. In a course that began with society itself, this closing skill guards the society's mind.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: reading media content critically is a life skill of the citizen: not everything shown, posted or broadcast deserves belief, and the citizen learns to test content before trusting or passing it on. Give one real example from your own life in Jordan, and name the lesson's key words: media, critical reading, content, claims. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Jordan's own books teach civics by starting from Jordanian society itself — its make-up, its culture, its live issues — and only then widening to participation, law and heritage. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
