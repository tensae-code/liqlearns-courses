# About the Civics and Citizenship — Jordan course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own التربية الوطنية والمدنية (National and Civic Education)
student books of the Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan, published
by the National Centre for Curriculum Development and
adopted by the Ministry of Education for all schools of
Jordan from 2024/2025. The one exception is the **Tip
boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Jordan inside the Civics and
Citizenship folder (one course per country). Inside it, the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place
is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the
ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced
gate. The school grades the books come from (Grades 9 and
10) appear only in each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as
provenance.

The course has **five stocked levels**, following Jordan's
own books' order — the course starts from Jordanian society
itself (its make-up, its culture, its live issues) and only
then widens to participation, law and heritage; the pupil's
own community is the textbook's first text:

1. **Level 1 — My Society and My Jordanian Identity** (7
   lessons, in the titles `my-society/` and
   `citizenship-and-identity/`). Jordanian society and its
   characteristics, Jordanian national culture, the issues
   of my society; then what national and civic education
   is, my Jordanian identity, citizenship itself, and
   serving the flag.
2. **Level 2 — Taking Part** (6 lessons, in the title
   `taking-part/`). Democracy, elections, civil society
   organisations, voluntary work, participation in public
   life, and political parties.
3. **Level 3 — The State of Law, the Constitution and
   Human Rights** (5 lessons, in the titles
   `the-state-of-law/`, `the-constitution/` and
   `human-rights/`). The rule of law, accountability, the
   Jordanian constitution and the rule of law, the
   international bill of human rights, and human rights in
   Jordan.
4. **Level 4 — Heritage and Jordan in the World** (8
   lessons, in the titles `heritage/` and
   `jordan-in-the-world/`). Jordanian national heritage,
   models of Jordanian heritage, museums keep heritage,
   national heritage, world heritage and UNESCO;
   Jordanian–Arab relations, Jordan's international
   relations, and Jordan of the future.
5. **Level 5 — Integrity and Life Skills** (4 lessons, in
   the titles `integrity/` and `life-skills/`). National
   integrity, fighting corruption and favouritism, media
   content, and behaviour — the citizen's own conduct at
   the top of the ladder: national integrity is taught as
   the personal form of the rule of law.

**Jordan's own way:** the homeland is introduced, in the
Grade 10 unit's own framing line, as what gathers and does
not divide — it unites people in loving it, belonging to it
and defending it — and citizenship is built as an identity
before it is built as a mechanism. The second term turns
outward and then inward: Jordan among the Arabs and the
world, the heritage it shares with humanity, and finally
the citizen's own integrity.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
books' own shape, walked in order):

- *From my society to my own conduct:* "Start with your own
  society — what Jordanian society is, its culture, its live
  issues — and with who you are: your Jordanian identity,
  your citizenship, your service of the flag. Then take
  part: democracy, elections, civil society, volunteering,
  public life, parties. Then the frame that binds: the rule
  of law, accountability, the constitution, your rights at
  home and in the world's bill. Then Jordan placed: its
  heritage and museums, its Arab and world relations, its
  future. Last, yourself: integrity, the fight against
  corruption and favouritism, a critical eye for media, and
  your own behaviour."
