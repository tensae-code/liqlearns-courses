# National Integrity

## Do it first

A shopkeeper gives you too much change. Say what integrity requires — and why a whole country's integrity is built out of small moments like that one.

## Integrity as a national value

The second term's sixth unit turns from the state back to the citizen's own conduct — at its highest. Integrity (النزاهة) is taught as a national value the citizen practises: straight dealing, clean hands, and refusal of crooked advantage, in private acts and public office alike.

## The personal form of the rule of law

The filing names the lesson's place in the course exactly: national integrity is the personal form of the rule of law. Law binds the state from outside; integrity binds the citizen from inside. A country of law needs both bindings, and this level teaches the inner one.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: national integrity — النزاهة — is the citizen's own honesty raised to a national value: public life kept clean because the people in it refuse crooked dealing. Give one real example from your own life in Jordan, and name the lesson's key words: integrity, honesty, values, citizen. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Jordan's own books teach civics by starting from Jordanian society itself — its make-up, its culture, its live issues — and only then widening to participation, law and heritage. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
