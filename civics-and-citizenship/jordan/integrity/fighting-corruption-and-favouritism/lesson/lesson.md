# Fighting Corruption and Favouritism

## Do it first

Two pupils deserve one school prize; one is the teacher's nephew. Say what favouritism would do, what fairness requires, and why the whole school is harmed by the crooked choice.

## Naming the enemies

Integrity's lesson named the value; this lesson names its enemies: corruption (الفساد) — public trust turned to private gain — and favouritism (المحسوبية) — rights, jobs and chances handed out by connection rather than by merit. Both steal the same thing: what belongs to all, or to the deserving, given to the connected.

## The citizen's part in the fight

Fighting them is not only inspectors' work. The citizen fights corruption by refusing to pay or take the crooked shortcut, by insisting on merit and right, and by holding the standard in the small offices of daily life — school, desk and counter — where favouritism first learns to walk.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: fighting corruption and favouritism — الفساد والمحسوبية — is integrity at war: refusing the bribe, the stolen public right, and the job or favour given by connection instead of by merit. Give one real example from your own life in Jordan, and name the lesson's key words: corruption, favouritism, merit, integrity. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Jordan's own books teach civics by starting from Jordanian society itself — its make-up, its culture, its live issues — and only then widening to participation, law and heritage. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
