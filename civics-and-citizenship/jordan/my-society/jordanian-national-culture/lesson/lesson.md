# Jordanian National Culture

## Do it first

Name one custom, one food, one song or celebration and one craft that people in Jordan would recognise as their own. Say where you have seen each one.

## What national culture is

Culture is the whole way of life a people builds together — its language and stories, its customs and celebrations, its food, dress, crafts and arts. A national culture is that way of life at the scale of the homeland: the things Jordanians recognise as theirs because they live them together.

## What carries it

The lesson traces what carries Jordanian national culture: the people who practise it daily, the occasions that gather them, and the handing-on from one generation to the next. Culture stays alive by being lived — every pupil who learns a custom and practises it is one of its carriers.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: a nation's culture is what its people create, practise and hand on together — and Jordanian national culture is the shared way of life that carries Jordan's identity. Give one real example from your own life in Jordan, and name the lesson's key words: culture, customs, heritage, identity. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Jordan's own books teach civics by starting from Jordanian society itself — its make-up, its culture, its live issues — and only then widening to participation, law and heritage. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
