# Jordanian Society and Its Characteristics

## Do it first

Name three things the people of your own town or neighbourhood in Jordan share — a language, customs, occasions, a way of helping each other. Keep your list; the lesson names what you found.

## What Jordanian society is

The course starts where Jordan's book starts: with Jordanian society itself. A society is the people who live together in one homeland, bound by a shared life — and Jordanian society is that shared life as Jordanians live it, in its cities, villages and camps, its families and its neighbourhoods.

## The characteristics that mark it

The lesson names the characteristics that mark Jordanian society: what its people hold in common, the ties between its members, and the values its life runs on. To know your society's characteristics is to be able to say what makes its people one society and not a crowd of strangers.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Jordanian society is the society the learner already lives in — and it has characteristics that can be named: who composes it, what its people share, and what holds it together. Give one real example from your own life in Jordan, and name the lesson's key words: society, characteristics, community, homeland. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Jordan's own books teach civics by starting from Jordanian society itself — its make-up, its culture, its live issues — and only then widening to participation, law and heritage. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
