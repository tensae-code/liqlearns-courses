# Museums Keep Heritage

## Do it first

Imagine your town must keep one object that tells its story for people a hundred years from now. Choose the object and say how it must be guarded and shown.

## The museum's role

Things perish and scatter; a people's memory with them. The museum is the institution a society builds against that loss: it collects the works and objects of the past, guards them, studies them, and sets them where everyone can meet them. The lesson teaches the museum's role in preserving national heritage.

## Preservation as a citizen's concern

Preservation is not the museum staff's affair alone. Heritage survives because citizens value it, report it, refuse its destruction and bring their children to meet it. The Grade 9 course thus ends where it began — with the pupil's own society — now seen as something the pupil helps to keep.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: museums keep heritage: they collect, guard, study and show the nation's inheritance so that what time would scatter, the whole people can still meet. Give one real example from your own life in Jordan, and name the lesson's key words: museums, preservation, heritage, memory. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Jordan's own books teach civics by starting from Jordanian society itself — its make-up, its culture, its live issues — and only then widening to participation, law and heritage. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
