# Jordanian National Heritage

## Do it first

Name three things in Jordan that are older than your grandparents — a place, an object, a custom. Say how each one has reached you.

## What heritage is

Heritage is the inheritance of a whole people: what past generations made, built, practised and remembered, handed down to the present one. The Grade 9 book closes with it: Jordanian national heritage, and what it consists of — the homeland's tangible remains and its living traditions together.

## An inheritance held in trust

What is inherited is held in trust: the present generation did not make it and may not waste it; it receives heritage, guards it, and hands it on enlarged by its own care. That trust is why heritage stands in a civics book and not only in a history book.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Jordanian national heritage is what the homeland has inherited — the places, works, customs and memory handed down — and knowing what it consists of is the first step to keeping it. Give one real example from your own life in Jordan, and name the lesson's key words: heritage, inheritance, traditions, trust. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Jordan's own books teach civics by starting from Jordanian society itself — its make-up, its culture, its live issues — and only then widening to participation, law and heritage. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
