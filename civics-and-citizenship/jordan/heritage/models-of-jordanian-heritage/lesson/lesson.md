# Models of Jordanian Heritage

## Do it first

Choose one Jordanian heritage place or work you know of. Describe it as if to a visitor who has never seen it — what it is, how old, why it matters.

## Learning heritage from its models

A definition tells what heritage is; a model shows it. This lesson works through chosen models of Jordanian national heritage — real instances the pupil can picture, visit or point to — so that the inheritance of the previous lesson gains faces and places.

## What the models teach together

Taken together, the models teach the range of the inheritance: built and made things, places and practices, across the homeland's regions and ages. The pupil who has met the models can recognise heritage when they meet it unnamed — in their own town first of all.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: models of Jordanian heritage are the chosen examples through which the pupil meets the homeland's inheritance face to face — heritage learned from real instances, not from a definition alone. Give one real example from your own life in Jordan, and name the lesson's key words: models, heritage, examples, places. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Jordan's own books teach civics by starting from Jordanian society itself — its make-up, its culture, its live issues — and only then widening to participation, law and heritage. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
