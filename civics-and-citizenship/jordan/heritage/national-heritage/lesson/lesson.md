# National Heritage

## Do it first

List the kinds of things that belong to a nation's heritage — not single objects, but kinds: kinds of places, kinds of works, kinds of practices.

## The inheritance as one trust

The Grade 10 second term returns to heritage in its own unit. Where the Grade 9 lessons introduced Jordan's heritage, its models and its museums, this lesson takes national heritage whole: the complete inheritance — places, works, customs and memory — considered as one trust held by the nation.

## From national to world heritage

The lesson also sets the step the next one takes: a nation's heritage is its own, yet some of it belongs to the story of all humanity. Holding both truths — ours to guard, humanity's to honour — is the balance the world-heritage lesson builds on.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: national heritage, taken up again in the second term, is the homeland's whole inheritance considered as one trust — the ground on which the lesson of world heritage then stands. Give one real example from your own life in Jordan, and name the lesson's key words: national heritage, trust, inheritance. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Jordan's own books teach civics by starting from Jordanian society itself — its make-up, its culture, its live issues — and only then widening to participation, law and heritage. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
