# World Heritage and UNESCO

## Do it first

Name one place anywhere in the world that you think all humanity should help protect. Say why it belongs to everyone's story, not only its own country's.

## Heritage of all humanity

Some inheritances outgrow their borders: a city, a monument or a landscape whose loss would impoverish every people's story. The lesson teaches world heritage — that shared inheritance — through worked examples such as the heritage city of Damascus, where the pupil sees what makes a place the world's concern.

## UNESCO's role

UNESCO is the world body charged with this protection: it names and lists world heritage, and joins the peoples of the world in guarding it. The pupil's own national heritage from the previous lesson now stands inside a wider duty — Jordan guards its inheritance, and humanity stands behind the guarding of what is humanity's own.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: world heritage is the part of peoples' inheritance that belongs to all humanity's story — and UNESCO is the world body charged with helping to protect it, with heritage cities such as Damascus as worked examples. Give one real example from your own life in Jordan, and name the lesson's key words: world heritage, UNESCO, Damascus, humanity. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Jordan's own books teach civics by starting from Jordanian society itself — its make-up, its culture, its live issues — and only then widening to participation, law and heritage. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
