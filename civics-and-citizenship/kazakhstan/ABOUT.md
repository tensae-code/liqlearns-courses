# About the Civics and Citizenship — Kazakhstan course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Samopoznanie (Self-Knowledge) and Fundamentals of Law
books. The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip
in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are
ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Kazakhstan inside the Civics and
Citizenship folder (one course per country). Inside it, the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place
is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the
ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced
gate. The school grades the books come from appear only in
each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

**Kazakhstan's own way, as filed:** Samopoznanie teaches
universal human values through paired lessons built on
stories, poems, songs and "wise words", closing each lesson
"from heart to heart"; the homeland, native nature and
family are the child's first civic world. The programme
author of Samopoznanie is S. A. Nazarbayeva and every volume
is recommended by the Ministry of Education and Science of
the Republic of Kazakhstan. All Samopoznanie books are
image-only scans (okulyk.kz copies of the Bobek editions)
read from page renders and their printed contents. At Grade
9 the country's one school law course, Fundamentals of Law,
gives the pupil the whole of Kazakhstan's own legal system
branch by branch, built on the 1995 Constitution and the
republic's codes; its preface tells pupils they will soon be
full citizens of the Republic of Kazakhstan with all the
rights and duties in its Constitution.

**Parallel editions, as filed:** the Samopoznanie textbooks
and the Grade 9 Fundamentals of Law textbook were each
issued in parallel Russian and Kazakh editions (Grade 9 Law
also in Uyghur). Per the filing's parallel-edition rule, one
edition of each book was read in full and the parallel
spot-checked (cover + contents + spreads) and named in the
header; placements are filed once per distinct book. The
Kazakh edition of the Grade 9 law book exists as two records
— duplicate scans of the same edition, as filed.

The course as built has **ten stocked levels** (Levels
1–10), from the filing for this build
(`kazakhstan-placements.md` — 36 records / 17 distinct
books: 11 student textbooks and 6 pupil's workbooks, all
student footing). 197 placements in all, stocking 192 lessons
— the coined lesson names were deduped before building
(see CURRICULUM.md, "Where the build stands"): every
placement stocks into exactly one lesson, and every
placement's provenance row is in that lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`.
