# About the Civics and Citizenship — Malaysia course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Pendidikan Moral
books.
The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is
from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the
lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Malaysia inside the Civics and
Citizenship folder (one course per country). Inside it, the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place
is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the
ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced
gate. The school grades and forms the books come from appear
only in each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

Malaysia's civics-type course is Pendidikan Moral (Moral
Education), the subject taken by non-Muslim pupils and the
closest Malaysian equivalent of a civics course: it teaches
the Rukun Negara, citizens' rights and duties, Malaysia's
institutions and laws, and the values of its plural society.

Malaysia's own way, preserved whole, as filed: at primary
level the moral course is one theme per year, taught through
stories, songs, poems and comics about named child neighbours
of every Malaysian community — at Tahun 4 the whole of civics
is the neighbourhood: the child's first society is the jiran
(neighbour). The secondary course is organised in widening
circles (self → family and friends → community and society →
morals, rules and law), every unit opening with a KONSEP
exposition and closing with reinforcement and enrichment
activities; it opens by defining moral itself (from Latin
mores) against religion, community norms, rules and law at
once, teaches the Rukun Negara as a moral source alongside
the scriptures of Malaysia's religions, and closes its first
year with children's rights under the Child Act 2001 and
corruption as the nation's enemy under the SPRM Act 2009.
Tingkatan 3 turns the earlier circles outward — moral choice
becomes the pupil's own — and ends beyond Malaysia's borders
with international humanitarian law. The senior course
(Tingkatan 4, Bidang 5–7 following Bidang 1–4 of Tingkatan
1–3) turns from personal virtue to the state: citizens'
rights read from the Federal Constitution, unity, ethical
spending, Malaysia's diversity as national wealth, and
sovereignty as a shared citizen duty. The final year
(Tingkatan 5, the same Bidang 5–7 at the global scale)
turns the whole course outward: the global society and
its norms, the glokal person, spirituality, trustworthy
information, integrity in the organisation and the
nation, humane conduct for global wellbeing, community
programmes, global cooperation, ethical finance, and
Malaysia's place in the world.
