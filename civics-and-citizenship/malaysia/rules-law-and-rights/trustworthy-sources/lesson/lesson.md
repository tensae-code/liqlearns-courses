# Trustworthy Sources

## Do it first

Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: Trustworthy Sources. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Malaysia's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

Which sources and information are trustworthy for the wellbeing of all — research reports, encyclopaedias, government circulars, scholarly books and recognised websites and people in authority — and that false information harms society if it is not stopped.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Which sources and information are trustworthy for the wellbeing of all — research reports, encyclopaedias, government circulars, scholarly books and recognised websites and people in authority — and that false information harms society if it is not stopped. Give one real example from your own life or from the Malaysia you live in, and name the lesson's key words: trustworthy, sources. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
