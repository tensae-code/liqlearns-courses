# Civics and Citizenship — El Salvador course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for El Salvador inside the Civics and
Citizenship folder, organised by **levels, not grades**: a
learner climbs when they have proved the level, not when a
school calendar says so. The school grades the books come
from appear only in each lesson's sources (provenance),
never as the course's organisation. The path follows El
Salvador's own order as its books lay it out, from the
filing's ladder sketch (`el-salvador-placements-b.md` —
0 census records / 8 link leads; 6 distinct books read, all
student books, all Spanish: the Ministry of Education's
new *Ciudadanía y Valores* textbooks for Grades 5, 6, 7,
8, 9 and 1.° año de bachillerato): the series' own grade
order is the ladder, and every book repeats the same arc —
territory and history first, citizenship and rights last —
at rising depth. Only the citizenship, rights,
participation, values and governance teaching is placed;
the books' geography and history units are named in each
book's contents map in ABOUT.md, not placed (except, as
filed, the Grade 8 women's-rights history weeks and the
Grade 9 human-rights history week, which teach rights
directly). The filing stocks 74 placements into 73
lessons: the coined lesson names were deduped before
building — one coined name,
`digital-and-environmental-citizenship/environmental-citizenship`,
is used by two books (Grade 8 and Grade 9) and stocks ONE
lesson with one sub lesson per placement; every other
coined name was already distinct, and every placement
stocks into exactly one lesson.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this
country's own Ciudadanía y Valores books — except the
**Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people
making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the
books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book list and the
acceptance reckoning. **All 73 lessons stand on student
books; no lesson in this course stands on a teacher guide
or a programme.**

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **Taking Part for the First Time** — The Grade 5 book's citizenship strand: one week on governing the Central American territory, then the participation unit week by week — finding one's personal and social interests, recognising and expressing emotions, managing conflict at school and in the locality, cultural diversity and respect, intercultural coexistence, and citizen participation at local and national level — closing with children's rights and their legal guarantees, road education, sustainability challenges, environmental citizenship, and digital citizenship with its rights and responsibilities. 13 lessons, in 3 title(s): `governing-the-territory/` (1 lessons); `me-and-my-participation/` (6 lessons); `rights-and-sustainability/` (6 lessons).
2. **Taking Part, Managing Myself and Human Rights** — The Grade 6 book's citizenship strand: environmental governance in Central America first, then participation and self-management — finding one's interests, managing emotions, managing conflict in public spaces, protecting heritage, interculturality in Central America and Latin America, and the participation spaces of childhood and adolescence — closing with human rights for a dignified life and for vulnerable people, road education at school and in the community, sustainability challenges in Latin America, citizen initiatives for the environment, and knowledge technologies in daily life. 13 lessons, in 3 title(s): `governing-the-territory/` (1 lessons); `participation-and-self/` (6 lessons); `citizenship-and-human-rights/` (6 lessons).
3. **Interculturality, Rights and Public Spaces** — The Grade 7 book's citizenship strand: interculturality and human rights — socioemotional development, the global village, similarities and differences between continents, the life project and equal opportunities for men and women, the national and international mechanisms and instruments of human rights, and the institutions that guarantee them — then participation in public spaces: participation as a democratic conquest, cybersecurity principles, sustainability and risk management in El Salvador, and the tax system and the citizen's role in it. 10 lessons, in 2 title(s): `interculturality-and-rights/` (6 lessons); `participation-in-public-spaces/` (4 lessons).
4. **The State, Rights in History and the Digital Citizen** — The Grade 8 book's citizenship strand: the administration and political organisation of a territory; two history weeks that teach rights directly — women and politics, and the struggle for women's civil and political rights in the 19th and 20th centuries; intercultural coexistence — emotions in social spaces, coexistence, the life project and youth, human rights as guarantees of a dignified life, and citizen action for vulnerable populations; and digital and environmental citizenship for the 21st century — access to public information and institutional transparency, technologies in the knowledge society, environmental citizenship, citizen organisation and law for the environment, and the tax system and citizen participation. The Environmental Citizenship lesson at this level also carries, as its second sub lesson, the Grade 9 book's participatory-projects week on the same coined lesson name (see ABOUT.md's dedupe note). 13 lessons, in 4 title(s): `territory-and-the-state/` (1 lessons); `rights-in-history/` (2 lessons); `intercultural-coexistence/` (5 lessons); `digital-and-environmental-citizenship/` (5 lessons).
5. **Rights Defended and the Citizen's Wider World** — The Grade 9 book's citizenship strand: water resources and the local, national and regional governments that guard them; the struggle for human rights in the twentieth century; interculturality and rights — emotions in public life, intercultural coexistence, global initiatives for interculturality, the life project, and human rights' defence mechanisms with citizen participation; and the closing unit — technology in daily life, democratic participation and governance without borders, digital citizenship and cybersecurity, and El Salvador's own tax system. The book's environmental-citizenship week stocks into the Environmental Citizenship lesson at Level 4, whose coined name it shares (see ABOUT.md's dedupe note), so this level holds 11 lessons stocked by 12 placements. 11 lessons, in 4 title(s): `land-and-governance/` (1 lessons); `rights-in-history/` (1 lessons); `interculturality-and-rights/` (5 lessons); `digital-and-environmental-citizenship/` (4 lessons).
6. **The Democratic System and Personal Fulfilment** — The bachillerato book's citizenship strand (1.° año de bachillerato, filed as Grade 10): the legal framework for using natural resources and sustainability and environmental governance in El Salvador; then the democratic system whole — political systems and democracy, democracy and the rule of law, El Salvador's own political system and its institutions for fostering democracy, human rights as principles of dignity and the common good, and the mechanisms for protecting and demanding rights; and participation and personal fulfilment — digital rights in the knowledge society, environmental awareness and participation spaces, public spaces for youth participation, initiatives for intercultural coexistence in El Salvador, managing emotions for conflict resolution and harmonious coexistence, and personal fulfilment. 13 lessons, in 3 title(s): `environmental-governance/` (2 lessons); `the-democratic-system/` (5 lessons); `participation-and-fulfilment/` (6 lessons).

**Totals: 73 lessons** (Level 1: 13 · Level 2: 13 · Level 3: 10 · Level 4: 13 · Level 5: 11 · Level 6: 13 — from six read student books: *Ciudadanía y Valores — Libro de texto, 5.° grado*, *6.° grado*, *7.° grado*, *8.° grado*, *9.° grado* and *1.° año de bachillerato* (Ministerio de Educación, Gobierno de El Salvador, "Mi Nueva Escuela" reform, 2026 materials series, Spanish, no ISBN printed on the pages read, all COMPLETE as filed for the civics strand); 74 placements stocking 73 lessons).
