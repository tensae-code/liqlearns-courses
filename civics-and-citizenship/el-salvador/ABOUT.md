# About the Civics and Citizenship — El Salvador course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Ciudadanía y Valores (Citizenship and Values) books. The one
exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us,
the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are
the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for El Salvador inside the Civics and
Citizenship folder (one course per country). Inside it, the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place
is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the
ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced
gate. The school grades the books come from appear only in
each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

El Salvador's own way, preserved whole, as filed: in the
Ministry of Education's new Ciudadanía y Valores series
("Mi Nueva Escuela" reform) the citizenship teaching sits
in the last two units of each book and is built week by
week (semanas): knowing oneself and one's emotions,
handling conflict, cultural diversity and intercultural
coexistence, then participation at local and national
level, children's rights with their legal guarantees, road
education, environmental and digital citizenship — and, as
the books climb, human-rights mechanisms and the
institutions that guarantee them, the tax system as a
citizen's topic, and at the top the democratic system
whole: political systems, the rule of law, El Salvador's
own political system, rights as dignity and the common
good, and demanding rights. Every book repeats the same
arc — territory and history first, citizenship and rights
last — at rising depth, and the ladder follows that order:
the series' own grade order is the ladder.

The course as built has **six stocked levels**, from the
filing for this build (`el-salvador-placements-b.md` —
0 census records; 8 link leads. Six leads name the
Ministry of Education's new Ciudadanía y Valores textbooks
for Grades 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 and 1° año de bachillerato
(Grade 10) — none in the census (the country's staging
folders hold the older Estudios Sociales series, a
different set of books). The ministry host (mined.gob.sv)
answers this machine with a Cloudflare check page, so the
books were fetched from the Wayback Machine's 2026-03-06
captures of the same ministry URLs; the captured files
match the leads' byte sizes exactly (Grade 5: 63,275,688;
Grade 6: 72,905,841; Grade 7: 100,099,183; Grade 8:
82,415,498; Grade 9: 52,448,852). The remaining two leads
are ministry hub pages (a methodological-guides page
holding only physical-education material, and an
English-subject page) — record-only in the group log.
Track: combined — despite the subject name, each book
mixes geography/history units with citizenship-and-values
units; only the citizenship, rights, participation,
values and governance teaching is placed, and the
geography/history units are named in each contents map.
The books carry the stamp "Material en validación"
(material under validation). 74 placements in all,
stocking 73 lessons — the coined lesson names were
deduped before building: one coined name,
`digital-and-environmental-citizenship/environmental-citizenship`,
is used by two books (Grade 8, printed p. 232, and
Grade 9, printed p. 238) and stocks ONE lesson with one
sub lesson per placement; every other coined name was
already distinct, and every placement stocks into exactly
one lesson):

1. **Level 1 — Taking Part for the First Time
   (13 lessons).** Governing the territory in Central
   America; discovering my personal and social interests,
   recognising and expressing emotions, managing conflict
   at school and in the locality, cultural diversity and
   respect, the elements of intercultural coexistence,
   taking part locally and nationally; children's rights
   and their legal guarantees, road education,
   sustainability challenges, environmental citizenship,
   and digital citizenship with its rights and
   responsibilities (Grade 5 book, Unidades 2, 5 and 6).
2. **Level 2 — Taking Part, Managing Myself and Human
   Rights (13 lessons).** Environmental governance in
   Central America; finding my interests, managing
   emotions, managing conflict in public spaces,
   protecting heritage, interculturality in Central
   America and Latin America, and where children and
   teenagers take part; human rights for a dignified life
   and for vulnerable people, road education at school
   and in the community, sustainability challenges in
   Latin America, citizen initiatives for the environment,
   and knowledge technologies in daily life (Grade 6
   book, Unidades 1, 5 and 6).
3. **Level 3 — Interculturality, Rights and Public
   Spaces (10 lessons).** Socioemotional development, the
   global village, similarities and differences between
   continents, the life project and equal opportunities,
   human-rights mechanisms and instruments, and the
   institutions that guarantee rights; participation as a
   democratic conquest, cybersecurity principles,
   sustainability and risk management in El Salvador, and
   the tax system and the citizen (Grade 7 book,
   Unidades 5 and 6).
4. **Level 4 — The State, Rights in History and the
   Digital Citizen (13 lessons).** How a territory is
   organised politically; women and politics and women's
   fight for civil and political rights in history;
   managing emotions in social spaces, interculturality
   and coexistence, the life project and youth, human
   rights as guarantees of a dignified life, and citizen
   action for vulnerable people's rights; access to
   public information and transparency, technologies in
   the knowledge society, environmental citizenship
   (carrying also the Grade 9 book's participatory-projects
   week as its second sub lesson), citizens organised to
   protect the environment, and the tax system and citizen
   participation (Grade 8 book, Unidades 1, 4, 5 and 6,
   plus the one Grade 9 placement deduped into the
   Environmental Citizenship lesson).
5. **Level 5 — Rights Defended and the Citizen's Wider
   World (11 lessons).** Water and the governments that
   guard it; the fight for human rights in the twentieth
   century; emotions in public life, intercultural
   coexistence, global initiatives for interculturality,
   my life project, and defending human rights;
   technology in daily life, democratic participation and
   governance without borders, digital citizenship and
   cybersecurity, and El Salvador's tax system (Grade 9
   book, Unidades 1, 4, 5 and 6 — 12 placements, one of
   them stocked into the Level 4 Environmental
   Citizenship lesson by the dedupe).
6. **Level 6 — The Democratic System and Personal
   Fulfilment (13 lessons).** The legal frame for natural
   resources and sustainability and environmental
   governance in El Salvador; political systems and
   democracy, democracy and the rule of law, El
   Salvador's political system, human rights as dignity
   and the common good, and protecting and demanding
   human rights; the knowledge society and digital
   rights, environmental awareness and taking part, youth
   in public spaces, initiatives for intercultural
   coexistence in El Salvador, emotions, conflict
   resolution and living in harmony, and personal
   fulfilment (1.° año de bachillerato book, Unidades 2,
   5 and 6).

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later;
the books' own shape, walked in order):

- *El Salvador's own order:* "First, how the territory is
  governed — and then you: your interests, your emotions,
  the conflicts you learn to manage, the cultures you learn
  to respect and live among, and your first taking part,
  at home in your locality and in the nation. Then your
  rights: children's rights with their legal guarantees,
  human rights for a dignified life and for the vulnerable,
  road, environmental and digital citizenship. Higher up,
  the machinery of rights — the mechanisms, the instruments
  and the institutions that guarantee them — and
  participation in public spaces, cybersecurity, and the
  tax system you will pay into. Then rights in history,
  including women's long fight for civil and political
  rights, and rights defended in the present. And at the
  top, the democratic system whole — democracy, the rule
  of law, El Salvador's own institutions — and the citizen
  fulfilled: digital rights, youth in public spaces,
  conflict resolved, a life project of your own."

## Honesty notes (course level)

- **What the filing does and does not contain.** The
  filing contains 6 distinct read books (the Ciudadanía
  y Valores student books for Grades 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 and
  1.° año de bachillerato), 74 placements, and the books'
  own contents maps. The filing does not contain any
  other El Salvador book — no book for any other grade,
  no teacher guide, no programme. Nothing of that kind is
  in this course, and no lesson pretends to one. The
  ladder therefore covers exactly the six books' own
  progression, Grade 5 first and bachillerato last, as
  filed. The filing names no staged-pending or
  push-don't-delete file for any book; nothing of that
  kind is claimed here.

  states 0 census records and 8 link leads; six leads

  the 6 filed books are in this course; no other record
