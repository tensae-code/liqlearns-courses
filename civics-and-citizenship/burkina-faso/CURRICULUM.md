# Civics and Citizenship — Burkina Faso — curriculum

One course for Burkina Faso inside the Civics and Citizenship
folder, built from Burkina Faso's own government textbooks:
Éducation Civique et Morale (ECM — Civic and Moral Education),
the MENAPLN subject taught through ministry preparation
fiches and teacher guides — the CP2 fiches (édition 2020),
the CP1 fiches (édition 2020), the CP1 Guide de l'enseignant
(reform-curriculum edition), the CE1 fiches (édition 2020) and
the CM2 fiches (édition 2020), all French, all COMPLETE as
filed, and all on TEACHER footing: no pupil book for this
country is in the census, so every level of this course
stands on teacher-guide/fiches evidence only, acceptance 0,
labelled as such (see ABOUT.md for the full book list, the
reading routes, and every label carried exactly as filed).
The organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's
place is found by what they prove. The path is recommended,
never forced — a learner can jump in higher; the "start here
instead" hints point down this ladder. The grades the books
come from (CP1, CP2, CE1, CM2) appear only in provenance
(ABOUT.md and each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`).

The ladder reconciles the two filings' sketches into one.
The first filing (`burkina-faso-placements.md`, the CP books)
lays out five thematic levels — hygiene, virtues, school
life, rights and duties, eco-citizenship and road safety —
and every placement's own level mark is carried exactly as
filed. The second filing (`burkina-faso-placements-b.md`)
lays out a grade ladder — CP at its base, CE1 next, CE2 and
CM1 fetched with their filings pending, CM2 at the top — and
its placement marks agree with this build at the two rungs
it stocks: every CE1 placement is marked Level 2 and every
CM2 placement Level 5, and they stand at those levels here.
The CE2 and CM1 fiche sets stock nothing: their filings are
pending, exactly as filed, and no lesson pretends to them
(see ABOUT.md).

## Level 1 — Good Habits and Hygiene — The Body and the Shared Place — 15 lessons

The CP course opens with the body and the shared place: cleanliness of body, clothes, teeth, eyes and hands; using and caring for latrines and the faecal peril; protecting food and drinking water; sweeping the classroom and cleaning the schoolyard; and the dangers of bathing in unsafe water. Citizenship begins as habits of the body, respect for people, and care for the shared place. Lessons: Cleaning the Schoolyard · Clean Body, Clothes and Shoes · Hygiene of the Body · Not Playing with Blood-Soiled Objects · Hygiene of the Eyes and Teeth · Protecting Food · The Faecal Peril · The Dangers of Bathing · Protecting Drinking Water · Cleaning Our Classroom · Using Latrines · Caring for Latrines · Hygiene of the Teeth · Picking Up Rubbish and Using Bins · Washing Hands and Wearing Shoes.

## Level 2 — Respect, Virtues and Everyday Citizenship — 19 lessons

The CP books' individual and social virtues — order, politeness, courtesy, obedience, tolerance, forgiveness, mutual help, charity and frankness, love of animals and plants, and respect for parents, teachers, classmates, elders and vulnerable people — widened by the whole CE1 course: road safety, school life, good habits, hygiene and virtues in the ministry's fiche order, then elementary citizenship named outright, the environment, and the rights of the child, including protection from female genital mutilation. Lessons: Mutual Help and Solidarity · Love of Animals and Plants · Respect for Parents, Elders and the Vulnerable · Respect for Teachers and Classmates · Forgiveness and Tolerance · Help, Charity and Frankness · Order · Obedience · Tolerance · Order and Politeness · Courtesy · Road Safety · School Life · Good Habits · Body and Clothing Hygiene · Individual and Social Virtues · Protecting the Environment · Everyday Citizenship · Children's Rights and Protection from Excision.

## Level 3 — School Life and Citizenship — The Child's First Civic Institutions — 7 lessons

The child's first civic institutions, all from the CP books: school and class rules, choosing class leaders — the child's first election — the school cooperative and the spirit of cooperation, behaviour at the raising and lowering of the national flag, respecting common goods, planting trees, and fighting the wandering of animals as a civic duty in the community. Lessons: Respecting School Rules · Choosing Class Leaders · The School Cooperative · The National Flag Ceremony · Fighting Stray Animals · Planting Trees · Respecting Common Goods.

## Level 4 — Rights and Duties of the Child — 11 lessons

Belonging to a family and the birth certificate — the child's first civic document; the rights to a birth certificate, to a name, to a name, food and education, to participate, and to protection and physical integrity; the child's duties toward classmates, teachers, parents and vulnerable people; equality of girls and boys, practised in the same tasks; and the fight against excision — what it is and the strategies for fighting it — all from the CP books. Lessons: Against Excision — What It Is · Against Excision — Strategies for Fighting It · Belonging to a Family and the Birth Certificate · Duties Toward Others · Equality of Girls and Boys · The Right to a Birth Certificate · The Right to a Name · The Right to Protection and Physical Integrity · The Right to a Name, Food and Education · The Right to Participate · Duties Toward the Vulnerable.

## Level 5 — Eco-Citizenship, Road Safety and the Citizen's Wider World — 31 lessons

The CP books' two practical theatres — eco-citizenship in action (first notions of climate change, caring for plants and flowers, fighting plastic waste, abusive wood cutting and bush fires, beautifying the living place, caring for animals) and road safety (the stop and the traffic lights, the pedestrian crossing, moving on foot and overtaking on the road) — widened by the whole CM2 course: Burkina Faso itself and patriotism, the highway code at CM2 depth, school life, child protection, privacy, the rights of the child and civic rights, African organisations, the economy, the environment, solidarity, the family, social relations and social practices, sexually transmitted diseases as civic health conduct, safety and security, and the closing synthesis — man and society. Lessons: The Stop and the Traffic Lights · Moving and Overtaking on the Road · Climate Change — First Notions · Caring for Plants and Flowers · Fighting Plastic Waste · Fighting Abusive Wood Cutting · The Stop · Traffic Lights · The Pedestrian Crossing · Moving on Foot · Fighting Bush Fires · Beautifying Our Living Place · Caring for Animals · Road Safety and the Highway Code · School Life and Its Organisation · Burkina Faso · Protection from the Worst Forms of Labour and Exploitation · The Environment — Waste Water, Care and Protection · Solidarity · Respect for Private Life · The Family · Patriotism · Social Relations · The Rights of the Child · African Organisations · The Economy · Safety and Security · Social Practices · Sexually Transmitted Diseases · Civic Rights · Man and Society.
