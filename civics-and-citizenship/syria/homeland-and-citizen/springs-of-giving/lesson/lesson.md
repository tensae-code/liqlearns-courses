# Springs of Giving

## Do it first

Write a short pledge of your own: one real thing you will give to your homeland — your work, your care, your learning — and when you will give it.

## What the book teaches

The homeland gives to the citizen; the citizen gives back. This lesson names the homeland's givers as springs of giving — sources that do not run dry — and places at their head those who gave most: the martyrs, whose martyrdom the homeland honours.

## The lesson in full

The lesson does not end in looking backward. It ends in a pledge the pupil writes himself: what he will give. Honouring those who gave everything becomes, in the pupil's own hand, a promise about his own giving.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: giving to the homeland is a spring that never runs dry — the homeland honours martyrdom and its martyrs, and the pupil answers with a pledge of his own. Give one real example from your own life in Syria, and name the lesson's key words: giving, homeland, martyrs, pledge. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Syria's own books teach civics by climbing from the pupil's own self — self-esteem, family, friends, community — straight up to the state and the homeland, and closing facing forward: environment, volunteering and science as the duties of the coming generation. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
