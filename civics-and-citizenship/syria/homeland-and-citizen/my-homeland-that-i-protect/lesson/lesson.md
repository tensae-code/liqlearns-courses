# My Homeland That I Protect

## Do it first

Write one thing about your homeland that was handed to you by the generation before you, and one thing you must hand on, protected, to the generation after you.

## What the book teaches

Love of the land is not invented by each generation; it is handed down, generation after generation, and each generation receives the homeland as a trust. The lesson teaches the pupil to stand in that line: receiver today, protector and passer-on tomorrow.

## The lesson in full

Protection is taught plainly: resistance is named as a basic element in recovering rights. In the book's own framing, to protect the homeland and to work for the recovery of its rights are one duty, and the pupil's love of the land is proved by taking his place in it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: love of the land is handed down generation after generation, and protecting the homeland — with resistance named as a basic element in recovering rights — is the citizen's duty. Give one real example from your own life in Syria, and name the lesson's key words: homeland, protection, land, rights. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Syria's own books teach civics by climbing from the pupil's own self — self-esteem, family, friends, community — straight up to the state and the homeland, and closing facing forward: environment, volunteering and science as the duties of the coming generation. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
