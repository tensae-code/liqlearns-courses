# The International Community

## Do it first

Name three matters that no single country can settle alone. Write beside each one which peoples or states must act together to settle it.

## What the book teaches

The circle widens to its full width: beyond the Arab world stands the international community — all the states and peoples of the world, joined in shared bodies and bound by shared laws and dealings.

## The lesson in full

The citizen formed by this course now stands at the widest view the books give: his homeland inside the Arab community, and both inside the world. The lesson teaches what that membership means — cooperation, obligation and a place among nations that a small citizen, grown, helps his country to keep.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: beyond the Arab world stands the international community — the states and peoples of the whole world, joined in shared bodies and shared laws. Give one real example from your own life in Syria, and name the lesson's key words: international community, world, states, cooperation. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Syria's own books teach civics by climbing from the pupil's own self — self-esteem, family, friends, community — straight up to the state and the homeland, and closing facing forward: environment, volunteering and science as the duties of the coming generation. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
