# Human Heritage

## Do it first

Name three pieces of heritage — a place, a craft, a song, a ruin — one from your town, one from your country, one from another people. Write what would be lost if each were destroyed.

## What the book teaches

Heritage is what the past hands to the present. Human heritage — التراث الإنساني — is that inheritance at its widest: the places, works and memories of all peoples together, a shared inheritance no single nation owns alone.

## The lesson in full

The citizen's part is guarding. The lesson teaches that to protect heritage — his own country's first, and mankind's with it — is a duty of belonging: the same loyalty that guards the homeland's land guards what its people, and all people, have made and remembered.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: human heritage (التراث الإنساني) is a shared inheritance from all peoples' past — and guarding it is part of the citizen's duty. Give one real example from your own life in Syria, and name the lesson's key words: heritage, inheritance, culture, guarding. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Syria's own books teach civics by climbing from the pupil's own self — self-esteem, family, friends, community — straight up to the state and the homeland, and closing facing forward: environment, volunteering and science as the duties of the coming generation. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
