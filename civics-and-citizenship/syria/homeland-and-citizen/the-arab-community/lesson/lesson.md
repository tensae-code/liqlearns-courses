# The Arab Community

## Do it first

List the ties that join your homeland to the wider Arab community — language, history, custom, cause. Beside each tie, write one real example you know.

## What the book teaches

The older book's third unit widens the circle in steps, and the first step is the nearest: the Arab community. For the Syrian citizen this is the first wider belonging — a community beyond the homeland's borders, joined by shared language, shared history and shared ties.

## The lesson in full

The lesson teaches the citizen to hold two belongings at once without confusion: Syria is his homeland, and the Arab community is the larger house his homeland stands in. What he owes each, and how each strengthens the other, is the unit's work.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the Arab community is the Syrian citizen's first wider belonging — beyond the homeland, a larger community of shared language, history and ties. Give one real example from your own life in Syria, and name the lesson's key words: Arab community, belonging, language, unity. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Syria's own books teach civics by climbing from the pupil's own self — self-esteem, family, friends, community — straight up to the state and the homeland, and closing facing forward: environment, volunteering and science as the duties of the coming generation. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
