# My Homeland That I Love

## Do it first

Write six acts a pupil might do this week. Sort them into two columns: acts that show strong belonging, and acts that show weak belonging. Keep the sorted list; the lesson works the same sort.

## What the book teaches

The third unit turns from the citizen's frame to the citizen's heart: national belonging, the bond between citizen and homeland. Belonging is taught as something deeper than a feeling — it is a bond that shows itself, or fails to show itself, in what a person does.

## The lesson in full

The book's own exercise is a sorting one: acts that show strong belonging are separated from acts that show weak belonging. The pupil learns to judge acts — his own first — by which side of that line they fall on. Belonging, in Syria's own way, is measured in acts.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: national belonging is the bond between citizen and homeland — and it is measured in acts: some acts strengthen it and others weaken it. Give one real example from your own life in Syria, and name the lesson's key words: belonging, homeland, citizen, acts. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Syria's own books teach civics by climbing from the pupil's own self — self-esteem, family, friends, community — straight up to the state and the homeland, and closing facing forward: environment, volunteering and science as the duties of the coming generation. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
