# Keeping My Word

## Do it first

Write one promise you have made and kept, and one you made and broke. Write what each one did to the trust others place in you.

## What the book teaches

The unit on the homeland closes small and personal: the pledge. Keeping one's word is taught as a citizen's duty — loyalty to what you have promised, at home, at school and before the homeland itself.

## The lesson in full

A homeland is built by people whose word can be trusted. The pupil who keeps a small pledge is practising the same faithfulness the citizen owes in great things; the book ends the unit here so belonging finishes not in words about loyalty, but in the act of being loyal.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: keeping your pledge — loyalty to your word — is a citizen's duty: a homeland is built by people whose word can be trusted. Give one real example from your own life in Syria, and name the lesson's key words: pledge, loyalty, word, trust. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Syria's own books teach civics by climbing from the pupil's own self — self-esteem, family, friends, community — straight up to the state and the homeland, and closing facing forward: environment, volunteering and science as the duties of the coming generation. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
