# I Love My Family

## Do it first

Name the people in your family, and beside each name write one thing that person does for the others. Keep the list; the lesson names what you found.

## What the book teaches

After the self, the book widens one circle: the family. The family is the first circle of belonging — the first people a child belongs to, and the first people who belong to the child. Love of family is taught here not as a feeling only, but as the ground every wider belonging is built on.

## The lesson in full

The lesson teaches through a story: a boy whose family moves to a new place. What travels with him is not the house but the family itself — its care, its duties, its holding together. The pupil is asked to see his own family the same way: the first homeland in miniature.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the family is the first circle of belonging — the place a citizen first learns love, care and duty. Give one real example from your own life in Syria, and name the lesson's key words: family, belonging, care, duty. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Syria's own books teach civics by climbing from the pupil's own self — self-esteem, family, friends, community — straight up to the state and the homeland, and closing facing forward: environment, volunteering and science as the duties of the coming generation. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
