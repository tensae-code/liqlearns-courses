# I Value Myself

## Do it first

Write three true sentences about yourself: one thing you can do well, one thing you are learning, and one way you help at home. Keep them; the lesson names what you just did.

## What the book teaches

Self-esteem is the picture you hold of yourself: what you believe you are worth and what you believe you can do. The book opens the whole course here, inside the pupil, because a citizen who cannot value himself cannot yet value anyone else.

## The lesson in full

That picture is not made alone. Family and community shape it — the words spoken at home, the trust given at school, the place a child is given among others. The lesson asks the pupil to see those shaping hands and to build the picture deliberately: honestly, without shrinking and without boasting.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: self-esteem is the picture you hold of yourself — knowing your own worth is the first civic skill, and family and community help shape that picture. Give one real example from your own life in Syria, and name the lesson's key words: self-esteem, worth, family, community. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Syria's own books teach civics by climbing from the pupil's own self — self-esteem, family, friends, community — straight up to the state and the homeland, and closing facing forward: environment, volunteering and science as the duties of the coming generation. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.

3. Teach this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
