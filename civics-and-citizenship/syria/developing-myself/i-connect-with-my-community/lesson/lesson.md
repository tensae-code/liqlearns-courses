# I Connect with My Community

## Do it first

List three parts of your community's life you could take part in this month — a group, a task, an occasion. Mark the one you will actually join.

## What the book teaches

The first unit closes at the widest circle it reaches: the surrounding community. To communicate with your community is to take part in its life — not to watch it from outside, but to know its people, join its work and share its occasions.

## The lesson in full

The pupil who has valued himself, loved his family and respected his friends now steps outward: the community is where those three lessons are practised on people who are not kin and not chosen friends. This is the bridge from the self to society — the rest of the course walks over it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: connecting with your community means taking part in the life around you — knowing its people, joining its work and sharing its occasions. Give one real example from your own life in Syria, and name the lesson's key words: community, communication, participation, neighbours. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Syria's own books teach civics by climbing from the pupil's own self — self-esteem, family, friends, community — straight up to the state and the homeland, and closing facing forward: environment, volunteering and science as the duties of the coming generation. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
