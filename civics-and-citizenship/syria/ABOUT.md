# About the Civics and Citizenship — Syria course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own التربية الوطنية (National Education) student books of the
Syrian Arab Republic, developed by the National Center for
Curriculum Development (NCCD). The one exception is the
**Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Syria inside the Civics and
Citizenship folder (one course per country). Inside it, the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place
is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the
ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced
gate. The school grades the books come from (Grade 7 and
Grade 10, first year secondary) appear only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

The course has **eight stocked levels**, following Syria's
own books' order — the ladder climbs from the pupil's own
self straight up to the state, then out to the widest
communities, and closes facing forward:

1. **Level 1 — Developing Myself** (4 lessons, in the title
   `developing-myself/`). Self-esteem as the picture you
   hold of yourself; the family as the first circle of
   belonging; respect among friends; taking part in the
   surrounding community (Grade 7, Unit 1).
2. **Level 2 — Rights and Duties** (5 lessons, in the
   titles `rights-and-duties/` and `taking-part/`). How
   society's affairs are organised; respecting and
   protecting human rights; the powers of the state; the
   protectors of the homeland; and the first-term research
   project, gathering documents (Grade 7, Unit 2).
3. **Level 3 — Homeland and Citizen** (4 lessons, in the
   title `homeland-and-citizen/`). National belonging
   measured in acts; protecting the homeland; springs of
   giving and the honouring of martyrs; keeping one's
   pledge (Grade 7, Unit 3).
4. **Level 4 — The Future Generation** (5 lessons, in the
   titles `future-generation/` and `taking-part/`).
   Environmental citizenship; voluntary work; spotlights
   on the problems of the young; scientific and technical
   development; and the second-term research project, a
   wall magazine (Grade 7, Unit 4).
5. **Level 5 — Developing the Self** (4 lessons, in the
   title `developing-the-self/`). Decision-making as the
   first civic skill; social awareness; initiative
   (المبادرة); public opinion met critically (Grade 10,
   Unit 1).
6. **Level 6 — Rights and Duties: the State and Its
   Systems** (4 lessons, in the title
   `rights-and-duties/`). Political thought; the state and
   its pillars — people, territory and authority; systems
   of government; how economic systems developed
   (Grade 10, Unit 2).
7. **Level 7 — Homeland and Citizen: the Wider World**
   (4 lessons, in the title `homeland-and-citizen/`). The
   Arab community; the international community; human
   heritage (التراث الإنساني); the Arab citizen and
   Zionist thought, in Syria's own framing (Grade 10,
   Unit 3).
8. **Level 8 — Sustainable Development** (4 lessons, in
   the title `sustainable-development/`). National policy
   and the environment; the problems of poverty; "my
   project for change" (مشروعي للتغيير); public health
   (السلامة العامة) (Grade 10, Unit 4).

**How the two filings' ladders were reconciled:** the
first placements file (syria-placements.md) files the
Grade 7 book's four units at Levels 1–4; the second file
(syria-placements-b.md) sketches the Grade 7 book as its
Level 1 and the Grade 10 book as its Level 2, in the Grade
10 book's own unit order (developing-the-self/ →
rights-and-duties/ → homeland-and-citizen/ →
sustainable-development/). Reconciled into one ladder, the
Grade 7 book stands at Levels 1–4 exactly as filed, and
the Grade 10 book follows it whole at Levels 5–8 in its
own order — the self first, then the state and its
systems, then the widening communities, then development:
the citizen acts last, on the world. The titles
`rights-and-duties/`, `homeland-and-citizen/` and
`taking-part/` are shared across levels, as the books
share them.

**Syria's own way:** the course climbs from the pupil's
own self-esteem straight up to the state — rights are
taught together with the powers of the state and the
defence of the homeland, and the book closes facing
forward: environment, volunteering and science as the
duties of the coming generation. National belonging is
measured in acts (a sorting exercise separates acts that
show strong belonging from acts that show weak belonging).
The older book opens inside the pupil again — decision-
making, awareness, initiative, public opinion — before it
ever reaches the state; rights and duties are then taught
through political thought and the state's pillars, and
the course closes on sustainable development and a
personal "project for change".

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later;
the books' own shape, walked in order):

- *From yourself to the state, and out to the world:*
  "Start inside yourself — value yourself, love your
  family, respect your friends, join your community.
  Then learn the frame: how society is organised, your
  human rights, the powers of the state, and those who
  protect the homeland — and gather your first documents
  like a researcher. Then the homeland itself: belonging
  proved in acts, the land protected, giving honoured,
  your word kept. Face forward: the environment,
  volunteering, the problems of the young, and science.
  Then climb again, older: decide well, read your
  society, take initiative, question public opinion.
  Meet the ideas behind the state, its pillars, its
  systems, its economies. Widen out — the Arab community,
  the world, mankind's heritage — and close by acting:
  policy and poverty understood, your own project for
  change carried out, and your community's health
  guarded."
