# Research Project: A Wall Magazine

## Do it first

Plan a wall magazine page with classmates: choose its message, sketch its poster, and write the advice it will carry. This plan is the lesson's opening act.

## What the book teaches

The second term, like the first, closes in work: a research project done with classmates. This time the product is a wall magazine — posters and pictures designed together and put where the whole school will see them.

## The lesson in full

Its subject is the danger studied in the unit: the dangers of smoking, with advice to stay away from it. The civic skill is the same as the first project's, turned outward — take what the class has learned, make it clear and visible, and teach the school with it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: a wall magazine made with classmates — posters and pictures — can teach a whole school: here, the dangers of smoking and advice to stay away from it. Give one real example from your own life in Syria, and name the lesson's key words: magazine, posters, smoking, health. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Syria's own books teach civics by climbing from the pupil's own self — self-esteem, family, friends, community — straight up to the state and the homeland, and closing facing forward: environment, volunteering and science as the duties of the coming generation. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
