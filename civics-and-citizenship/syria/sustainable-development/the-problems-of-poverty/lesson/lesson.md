# The Problems of Poverty

## Do it first

List the problems that follow poverty into a family and into a town — keep the two lists separate. The lesson teaches poverty as a problem of the whole community.

## What the book teaches

Poverty is usually seen through one family's door. This lesson widens the view: the problems of poverty are a community problem — they touch schooling, health, work and dignity across the whole society, not only in the poor household.

## The lesson in full

Understanding comes before standing-with. The citizen who sees poverty's problems clearly — what causes them, what they break, what eases them — is being prepared for the lesson after this one: not only to understand a problem, but to design the change.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: poverty is a community problem, not only a private one — the citizen must understand its problems to stand with his society against them. Give one real example from your own life in Syria, and name the lesson's key words: poverty, community, problems, solidarity. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Syria's own books teach civics by climbing from the pupil's own self — self-esteem, family, friends, community — straight up to the state and the homeland, and closing facing forward: environment, volunteering and science as the duties of the coming generation. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
