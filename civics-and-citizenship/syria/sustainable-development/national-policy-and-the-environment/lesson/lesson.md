# National Policy and the Environment

## Do it first

Write two lists: what the state can do to protect the environment that a citizen cannot, and what citizens do daily that no law can do for them. Keep both lists; the lesson joins them.

## What the book teaches

The younger book taught environmental citizenship as the pupil's own duty. The older book returns at the level of the state: national policy toward the environment — how the state protects the environment by law, plan and institution.

## The lesson in full

Policy and duty meet in the middle. The state's protection fails without citizens who keep it; the citizen's care is weak without the state's frame. The final unit opens with this joining because sustainable development is exactly that: a whole country, state and citizens together, keeping what it lives on.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the state sets a national policy toward the environment — protecting it by law and plan — and the citizen owes the environment his share of that protection. Give one real example from your own life in Syria, and name the lesson's key words: policy, environment, protection, citizen. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Syria's own books teach civics by climbing from the pupil's own self — self-esteem, family, friends, community — straight up to the state and the homeland, and closing facing forward: environment, volunteering and science as the duties of the coming generation. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
