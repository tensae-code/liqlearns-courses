# Public Health

## Do it first

List the habits and services that keep your community healthy — yours at home, your school's, your town's. Mark which are carried by citizens and which by the state.

## What the book teaches

The course closes with the body of the community itself: public health — السلامة العامة. Keeping a community healthy is shared civic work: clean water, sound habits, care for the sick and protection from dangers are carried by citizens and state together.

## The lesson in full

It is a fitting last lesson. The course began with a child valuing himself; it ends with that child, grown in the books' climb, guarding the health of everyone around him. The citizen the two books set out to form stands complete: self, state, world — and service.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: public health (السلامة العامة) is a shared civic responsibility — keeping the community healthy is work the citizens and the state do together. Give one real example from your own life in Syria, and name the lesson's key words: public health, safety, community, responsibility. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Syria's own books teach civics by climbing from the pupil's own self — self-esteem, family, friends, community — straight up to the state and the homeland, and closing facing forward: environment, volunteering and science as the duties of the coming generation. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
