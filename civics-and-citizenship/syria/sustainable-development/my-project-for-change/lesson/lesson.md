# My Project for Change

## Do it first

Design your own project for change on one page: the problem, the change you aim at, the steps, the people, and how you will know it worked. This page is the lesson's opening act.

## What the book teaches

Everything the two books have taught converges here. ‘My project for change’ — مشروعي للتغيير — asks the pupil to design a real change project: a problem he has read with social awareness, a decision made on grounds, initiative to begin it, and a plan to carry it through.

## The lesson in full

This is the course's own proof. The self developed, the state understood, the communities widened, development grasped — all of it is tested by whether the pupil can change one real thing. The book gives the course's last word not to a definition but to a project with the pupil's name on it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: ‘my project for change’ (مشروعي للتغيير) puts the whole course into action — the pupil designs a real change project and carries it out. Give one real example from your own life in Syria, and name the lesson's key words: project, change, action, plan. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Syria's own books teach civics by climbing from the pupil's own self — self-esteem, family, friends, community — straight up to the state and the homeland, and closing facing forward: environment, volunteering and science as the duties of the coming generation. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
