# Civics and Citizenship — Syria course — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

Civics belongs to the country: this course is Syria's
own — built from Syria's التربية الوطنية (National
Education) student books for Grade 7 and Grade 10
(first year secondary) (National Center for Curriculum
Development (NCCD), Syrian Arab Republic, Arabic).
Inside the course the organisation is by **levels, not
grades**: the titles below are the units the placements
coin from the books' own units, and the lessons inside
them climb the stocked levels in path order — what the
learner proves, step by step. The grades the books come
from live only in each lesson's sources.

The path's own logic is the books': climb from the
pupil's own self-esteem straight up to the state —
self, family, friends, community; then society's order,
rights, the state's powers and the homeland's
protectors; then belonging deepened into duty; then the
forward look of the future generation. Then climb the
same spine older and wider: the self re-formed
(deciding, awareness, initiative, opinion), the state
understood (its ideas, pillars, systems, economies),
the communities widened to the world, and development
closed by the pupil's own project for change. That
ladder IS the path order (recommended, never forced — a
learner can jump in anywhere; the "start here instead"
hints point down this ladder). Every lesson stands on
student books — taught in 1 country (Syria); see
ABOUT.md for the filings' labels carried for the two
books.

## The titles

- `developing-myself/` — Level 1. The Grade 7 book's
  opening unit: I value myself, I love my family, I
  respect my friends, I connect with my community. It
  sits first because the book itself starts inside the
  pupil — self-esteem is Syria's first civic lesson.
- `rights-and-duties/` — Levels 2 and 6. One title
  shared by two levels, as the two books share the unit
  name: at Level 2, organising society's affairs,
  human rights, the powers of the state and the
  homeland's protectors (Grade 7); at Level 6,
  political thought, the state and its pillars, systems
  of government and how economic systems developed
  (Grade 10). It spans the levels because rights and
  duties are first the young citizen's frame and later
  the older citizen's system.
- `taking-part/` — Levels 2 and 4. The Grade 7 book's
  two term research projects: gathering documents
  (first term) and a wall magazine (second term). Each
  sits at the level of the unit it closes, because in
  this book a term's learning is proved by work done
  with classmates, not by a test.
- `homeland-and-citizen/` — Levels 3 and 7. One title
  shared by two levels, as the books share it: at
  Level 3, the homeland loved, protected, given to and
  pledged to (Grade 7); at Level 7, the Arab community,
  the international community, human heritage, and the
  Arab citizen and Zionist thought (Grade 10). It spans
  the levels because belonging is first the homeland
  itself, then the homelands' widening circles.
- `future-generation/` — Level 4. Environmental
  citizenship, voluntary work, problems of the young,
  and science and technical progress. It closes the
  Grade 7 climb because the book closes facing forward:
  the duties of the generation now in school.
- `developing-the-self/` — Level 5. Decision-making,
  social awareness, initiative and public opinion. It
  opens the Grade 10 climb where the older book opens:
  inside the pupil again, at an older pupil's depth —
  the self re-formed before the state is re-met.
- `sustainable-development/` — Level 8. National
  policy and the environment, the problems of poverty,
  my project for change, and public health. It closes
  the ladder because the course that began with a child
  valuing himself ends with that child acting on the
  world: policy understood, poverty faced, a real
  change project carried out, and the community's
  health guarded.
