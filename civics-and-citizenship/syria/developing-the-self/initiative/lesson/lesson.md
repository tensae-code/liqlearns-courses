# Initiative

## Do it first

Name one useful thing in your school or neighbourhood that everyone waits for someone else to start. Write the first step you could take yourself, this week.

## What the book teaches

Initiative — المبادرة — is starting useful action oneself instead of waiting. The lesson names the habit that separates the citizen who improves his world from the spectator who only describes it.

## The lesson in full

Initiative is not recklessness: the two lessons before it — deciding well and reading society clearly — are its tools. With grounds weighed and society read, the pupil is asked to begin: to be the one who starts the repair, the campaign, the help, and lets others join a moving thing.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: initiative (المبادرة) is starting useful action yourself instead of waiting to be told — the citizen who begins. Give one real example from your own life in Syria, and name the lesson's key words: initiative, action, beginning, responsibility. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Syria's own books teach civics by climbing from the pupil's own self — self-esteem, family, friends, community — straight up to the state and the homeland, and closing facing forward: environment, volunteering and science as the duties of the coming generation. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
