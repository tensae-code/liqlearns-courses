# Decision-Making

## Do it first

Take one real decision facing you this week. Write its grounds, then two options, then which you choose and why. Keep the page; the lesson names the steps you just walked.

## What the book teaches

The older book opens where the younger one opened — inside the pupil — but with a harder skill: how a decision is made. A decision is not a guess or a mood. It is made in steps: gathering the grounds, weighing the options, and only then taking the decision.

## The lesson in full

The book calls this the first civic skill because everything after it depends on it. A citizen decides — about his work, his community, his homeland's affairs — and a citizen who can show his grounds and his weighing can be trusted with bigger decisions than his own.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: a decision is made in steps — gather the grounds, weigh the options, then take the decision — and this is the first civic skill of the older pupil. Give one real example from your own life in Syria, and name the lesson's key words: decision, options, grounds, choice. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Syria's own books teach civics by climbing from the pupil's own self — self-esteem, family, friends, community — straight up to the state and the homeland, and closing facing forward: environment, volunteering and science as the duties of the coming generation. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
