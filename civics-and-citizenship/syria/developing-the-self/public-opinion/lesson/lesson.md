# Public Opinion

## Do it first

Take one thing 'everyone says' in your school or town. Write where you first heard it, who repeats it, and whether you have any ground for it besides the repeating.

## What the book teaches

A society does not only act together; it thinks together. Public opinion is that shared thinking — what the people around you hold to be true, wise or shameful — and it forms through talk, custom and media.

## The lesson in full

The citizen's duty toward it is double: respect it, because it is his society's voice; and meet it critically, because a crowd can repeat an error as easily as a truth. The unit on the self closes here — the pupil who can decide, read, begin and question opinion is ready for the state.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: public opinion is what a society thinks together — it forms through talk and media, and the citizen meets it critically, not blindly. Give one real example from your own life in Syria, and name the lesson's key words: public opinion, media, criticism, society. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Syria's own books teach civics by climbing from the pupil's own self — self-esteem, family, friends, community — straight up to the state and the homeland, and closing facing forward: environment, volunteering and science as the duties of the coming generation. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
