# Social Awareness

## Do it first

Describe your society as a stranger would see it: its work, its gatherings, its troubles, its hopes. Then write where you stand inside that picture.

## What the book teaches

Before the older pupil can act on society, he must be able to read it. Social awareness is that reading: seeing one's society as it is — its groups, its customs, its problems and its strengths — and seeing one's own place inside it.

## The lesson in full

Awareness is the opposite of drifting. The pupil who reads his society knows what his acts will touch; the pupil who does not, acts blindly even with good intentions. The book places this lesson second, right after decision-making, because decisions need eyes.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: social awareness is reading your own society clearly — seeing its life and your place in it — before you act in it. Give one real example from your own life in Syria, and name the lesson's key words: awareness, society, place, understanding. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Syria's own books teach civics by climbing from the pupil's own self — self-esteem, family, friends, community — straight up to the state and the homeland, and closing facing forward: environment, volunteering and science as the duties of the coming generation. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
