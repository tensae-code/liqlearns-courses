# How Economic Systems Developed

## Do it first

Trace one thing your family uses every day — bread, cloth, fuel — back through the hands that made and traded it. Write the chain; the lesson traces the whole system's growth.

## What the book teaches

Rights and duties stand on material ground: how people produce, exchange and live. This lesson traces how economic systems developed — the stages by which societies organised their work and wealth, one system growing out of another.

## The lesson in full

The unit closes here on purpose. The pupil who has met the ideas (political thought), the frame (the state's pillars), the rule (systems of government) and now the ground (economic systems) holds the whole citizen's frame the younger book only sketched — and is ready to walk out into the wider communities.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: economic systems developed step by step through history, and they form the material ground on which rights and duties stand. Give one real example from your own life in Syria, and name the lesson's key words: economy, systems, development, history. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Syria's own books teach civics by climbing from the pupil's own self — self-esteem, family, friends, community — straight up to the state and the homeland, and closing facing forward: environment, volunteering and science as the duties of the coming generation. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
