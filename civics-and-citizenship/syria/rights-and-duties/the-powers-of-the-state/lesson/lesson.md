# The Powers of the State

## Do it first

Name three big jobs a state must do, and write beside each one which part of the state you think does it. Keep the list; the lesson names the powers.

## What the book teaches

The state does its work through distinct powers, and each power has its own job. The lesson names the powers of the state one by one and shows what each one does, so the citizen knows the machine he lives under — and whom to ask for what.

## The lesson in full

The powers are not taught as force alone. Through them the state guarantees rights: the book names the state's guarantee of the right to education for all its children. Power and guarantee stand in one lesson because, in the book's teaching, the state's powers exist to carry its duties.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the state works through distinct powers, each with its own job — and through them the state guarantees rights such as education for all its children. Give one real example from your own life in Syria, and name the lesson's key words: state, powers, education, guarantee. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Syria's own books teach civics by climbing from the pupil's own self — self-esteem, family, friends, community — straight up to the state and the homeland, and closing facing forward: environment, volunteering and science as the duties of the coming generation. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
