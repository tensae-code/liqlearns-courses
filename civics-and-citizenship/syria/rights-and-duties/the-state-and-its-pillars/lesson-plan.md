1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: a state stands on pillars — people, territory and authority — and thinkers have explained in different ways how states arose.

Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the learner's own life in Syria and the book's own teaching, as Syria's National Education climbs from the pupil's own self-esteem straight up to the state — rights taught together with the powers of the state and the defence of the homeland — and closes facing forward: environment, volunteering and science as the duties of the coming generation.
