# Political Thought

## Do it first

Write what you think power is for: what should the one who holds power do with it, and what should he never do? Keep your answer; the lesson sets the great answers beside it.

## What the book teaches

Rights and duties do not float in the air; they rest on ideas. Political thought is the store of those ideas — the main answers thinkers and peoples have given about power and the state: where authority comes from, what it is for, and what limits it.

## The lesson in full

The older book teaches the frame before the machinery. A pupil who knows the ideas behind rights and duties can judge the systems he meets next — the state, its pillars, its forms of government — instead of only memorising their names.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: political thought is the store of ideas about power and the state that stands behind rights and duties — knowing the ideas explains the frame. Give one real example from your own life in Syria, and name the lesson's key words: political thought, power, state, ideas. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Syria's own books teach civics by climbing from the pupil's own self — self-esteem, family, friends, community — straight up to the state and the homeland, and closing facing forward: environment, volunteering and science as the duties of the coming generation. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
