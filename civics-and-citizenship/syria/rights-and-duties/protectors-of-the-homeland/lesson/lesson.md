# Protectors of the Homeland

## Do it first

Name the people whose work guards your homeland and your town, and write one ordinary thing — a school day, a market, a journey — that their guarding makes possible.

## What the book teaches

The unit on rights and duties closes with those who guard the homeland. The protectors of the homeland stand between the citizen and danger, and their protection is the quiet condition under which every other lesson in the book can happen at all.

## The lesson in full

The lesson asks the pupil to see protection as a duty carried by real people, and to understand what it makes possible: children in school, families at home, work and travel in safety. Gratitude here is not decoration — it is the citizen recognising a duty he may one day share.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the homeland is guarded by protectors whose work makes ordinary life — home, school and work — possible. Give one real example from your own life in Syria, and name the lesson's key words: homeland, protection, guard, safety. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Syria's own books teach civics by climbing from the pupil's own self — self-esteem, family, friends, community — straight up to the state and the homeland, and closing facing forward: environment, volunteering and science as the duties of the coming generation. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
