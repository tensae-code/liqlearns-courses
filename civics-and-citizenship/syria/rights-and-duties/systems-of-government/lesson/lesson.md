# Systems of Government

## Do it first

Take two states you know of. Write how rule is organised in each — who rules, and how that ruler comes to rule. Keep the comparison; the lesson widens it.

## What the book teaches

The pillars say what a state is; this lesson asks how it is ruled. Systems of government are the different ways rule is organised in different states — who holds authority, how they come to hold it, and how the citizen stands toward it.

## The lesson in full

The lesson is comparative: the pupil meets more than his own country's arrangement, so his own system becomes visible as one choice among systems, with its own shape and reasons. Knowing the alternatives is what makes a citizen's judgement of his own government a judgement, not a habit.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: states organise rule in different systems of government — how rule is arranged decides how citizens are governed and how they take part. Give one real example from your own life in Syria, and name the lesson's key words: government, systems, rule, state. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Syria's own books teach civics by climbing from the pupil's own self — self-esteem, family, friends, community — straight up to the state and the homeland, and closing facing forward: environment, volunteering and science as the duties of the coming generation. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
