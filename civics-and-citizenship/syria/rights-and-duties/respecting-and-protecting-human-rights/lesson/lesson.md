# Respecting and Protecting Human Rights

## Do it first

Write two lists: rights a person holds from birth, just as a human being, and rights a person gains as a member of society. Keep both lists; the lesson names what belongs on each.

## What the book teaches

Some rights are held from birth, as a human being: all people are born free and equal in dignity and rights. Other rights are gained as a member of society. The lesson separates the two kinds so the pupil can name each right and say where it comes from.

## The lesson in full

Rights are not only held; they are to be respected and protected — by the state, by society and by each citizen toward every other. The book is plain that there is no discrimination among children: the rights of the child beside you are the same rights as your own.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: every human being holds rights from birth — all people are born free and equal in dignity and rights — and further rights as a member of society, with no discrimination among children. Give one real example from your own life in Syria, and name the lesson's key words: human rights, dignity, equality, children. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Syria's own books teach civics by climbing from the pupil's own self — self-esteem, family, friends, community — straight up to the state and the homeland, and closing facing forward: environment, volunteering and science as the duties of the coming generation. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
