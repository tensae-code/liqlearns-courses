# Voluntary Work

## Do it first

Picture your neighbourhood or your school after one planned voluntary campaign: write what would be different, and what work would make it so.

## What the book teaches

Volunteering is the future generation's giving practised now: time and work offered freely, in the pupil's own neighbourhood and school. The lesson teaches it as planned work, not a passing impulse — a campaign is pictured, organised and carried out.

## The lesson in full

The book's own move is to picture both places after the campaign: the neighbourhood and the school as they would look if the pupils acted. Seeing the 'after' first is what turns a wish into a plan — and a plan into the citizen's habit of serving without being ordered.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: voluntary work is the citizen giving time freely — a planned campaign can change a neighbourhood and a school, and you can picture both before and after. Give one real example from your own life in Syria, and name the lesson's key words: volunteering, neighbourhood, school, campaign. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Syria's own books teach civics by climbing from the pupil's own self — self-esteem, family, friends, community — straight up to the state and the homeland, and closing facing forward: environment, volunteering and science as the duties of the coming generation. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
