# Science and Technical Progress

## Do it first

Name three ways science or technology has changed daily life in your home or town in your lifetime. Beside each, write whether the change helped, harmed, or both.

## What the book teaches

The Grade 7 course closes with change itself: how scientific and technical development affects society. Machines, medicine, communication — the lesson traces what science does to the way people live, work and learn.

## The lesson in full

The question is handed to the pupil's own generation: this change will be led by the young now in school. The book's closing teaching is that the future generation's duty is not to watch the change but to master it — to learn science deeply and direct its power to the homeland's good.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: science and technical development change society, and the young generation's part is to learn, master and direct that change for the homeland. Give one real example from your own life in Syria, and name the lesson's key words: science, technology, society, progress. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Syria's own books teach civics by climbing from the pupil's own self — self-esteem, family, friends, community — straight up to the state and the homeland, and closing facing forward: environment, volunteering and science as the duties of the coming generation. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
