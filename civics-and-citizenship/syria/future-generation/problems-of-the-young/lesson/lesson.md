# Problems of the Young

## Do it first

List problems a young person might face. Beside each one, write what a good classmate does — and what a good classmate never does.

## What the book teaches

The forward-looking unit does not pretend the young are safe. It puts spotlights on the problems of the young: the real troubles that reach pupils of this age, named plainly so they can be faced plainly.

## The lesson in full

The lesson's centre is duty toward the other: what a pupil owes a classmate who suffers one of these problems. The term project takes up one of them — smoking, and how to warn against it — so the class practises the duty, not only reads it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: young people face real problems, and a pupil's duty includes standing by a classmate who suffers one — the term's project takes up smoking and how to warn against it. Give one real example from your own life in Syria, and name the lesson's key words: youth, problems, smoking, duty. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

> **Tip:** Syria's own books teach civics by climbing from the pupil's own self — self-esteem, family, friends, community — straight up to the state and the homeland, and closing facing forward: environment, volunteering and science as the duties of the coming generation. When a lesson asks you to look at your own community, really look: your community is this course's first text.
