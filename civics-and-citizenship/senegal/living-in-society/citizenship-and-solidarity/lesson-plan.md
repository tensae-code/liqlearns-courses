# Lesson plan — Citizenship and Solidarity

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Senegal · **Title:** Living in Society · **Lesson:** Citizenship and Solidarity
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — Living in Society, Administering Senegal, Peace and Solidarity (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** citizenship shows in solidarity — taking part in the life of the city, protecting the environment, and guarding individual and collective security. This lesson stands on teacher guides alone — evidence only, labelled as such.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Taking part in the life of the city** — A citizen takes part in the life of the city — its shared affairs are also theirs.
2. **Protecting the environment and our security** — Solidarity shows in protecting the environment and in individual and collective security — guarding the life everyone shares.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: citizenship shows in solidarity — taking part in the life of the city, protecting the environment, and guarding individual and collective security.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, school or town — or, for this book's lessons, from the public life of Senegal they live in.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the learner's own life in Senegal and the book's own teaching, as the book itself works.
