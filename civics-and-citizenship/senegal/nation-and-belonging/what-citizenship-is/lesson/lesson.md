# What Citizenship Is

## Do it first

Say, in one sentence, what makes a person a citizen and not only an inhabitant. This lesson stops and defines the course's central word.

The notion of citizenship itself is taught: what it means to be a citizen, as the idea the rest of the course builds on.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: citizenship is the standing of a member of the nation — the idea on which the whole course is built. Give one real example from your own life or from the Senegal you live in, and name the lesson's key words: citizenship, citizen, notion, belonging. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: citizenship is the standing of a member of the nation — the idea on which the whole course is built.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
