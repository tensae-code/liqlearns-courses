# About the Civics and Citizenship — Senegal course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Éducation Civique guides. The one exception is the
**Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Senegal inside the Civics and
Citizenship folder (one course per country). Inside it, the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place
is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the
ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced
gate. The school grades the books come from appear only in
each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

The course as built has **four stocked levels**, from the
single filing for this build (`senegal-placements-b.md` —
0 census records / 7 link leads triaged by links-africa,
as filed: 4 books read, all teacher-side programme /
pedagogical guides in French; 3 leads record-only):

1. **Level 1 — My Close World, First Local Government and
   Belonging (11 lessons).** The 6e opening of the programme
   guide, in its own order: the child's close world (the
   family, the quarter, the village, the school), then the
   first decentralised structures (the rural community, the
   district commune, the commune), then nation and
   belonging (the nation and its symbols, homeland and
   patriotism, what citizenship is) and the citizen's
   rights and duties.
2. **Level 2 — Living in Society, Administering Senegal,
   Peace and Solidarity (9 lessons).** The 5e year: living
   in society (rules, responsibility, work well done) and
   citizenship and solidarity; then how Senegal is
   administered — the evolution since 1960, the
   arrondissement, the department, the region; then peace
   as progress, and national and international solidarity.
   Two of its lessons are stocked also by the 5e pedagogical
   guide (Book 2), and living-in-society carries both
   books' provenance rows.
3. **Level 3 — The Nation, the State, Democracy and Rights
   (10 lessons).** The 4e year: building the Senegalese
   nation and its unity in diversity; the state and its
   three powers (executive, legislative, judicial); then
   democracy and rights — rule of law and good governance,
   human rights, women's rights and parity, and the rights
   of vulnerable people. Three of its lessons are stocked
   also by the 4e pedagogical guide (Book 3).
4. **Level 4 — Heritage, Peace at World Scale and National
   Challenges (8 lessons).** The 3e year, as the programme
   itself ends: natural heritage and historical and
   cultural heritage; the culture of peace, African
   solidarity and integration (the African Union, ECOWAS)
   and the United Nations system; and the national
   challenges — fighting poverty, fighting malaria and
   HIV, fighting drugs. Two of its lessons are stocked also
   by the 3e pedagogical guide (Book 4).

Senegal's own way, preserved whole, as filed: the course
widens circle by circle — family, quarter, village, school,
then the decentralised local state, then nation and
citizenship, then the Republic's institutions and human
rights, ending with heritage, peace culture and national
challenges (poverty, malaria/HIV, drugs). Its peace teaching
is Senegal's own: a culture of peace and mediation built on
Senegalese situations, tolerance and dialogue of cultures
and religions, including Senegal's Islamo-Christian
dialogue, taught from religious peace texts, proverbs and
legends.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
books' own shape, walked in order):

- *Outward, circle by circle:* "First, your close world:
  your family, your quarter or village, your school — then
  the local state that serves them, the rural community and
  the communes. Then the nation: what it is, its symbols,
  your homeland, what citizenship means, and your rights
  and duties. Then living in society, how Senegal is
  administered from the arrondissement to the region, and
  peace and solidarity. Then the nation built in history,
  the state and its three powers, the rule of law and
  human rights — women's rights and parity, and the rights
  of the vulnerable. And last, what you inherit and what
  you face: Senegal's natural and cultural heritage, a
  culture of peace reaching to Africa and the United
  Nations, and the national challenges — poverty, malaria
  and HIV, drugs — that your generation of citizens is
  asked to help answer."
