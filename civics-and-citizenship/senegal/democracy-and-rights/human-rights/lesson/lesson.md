# Human Rights

## Do it first

Name one human right and the text where you could read it proclaimed. This lesson gives the rights the next two lessons protect for named people.

## Sub lesson 1 — What human rights are, and their history

Human rights are defined, and their history told — the rights every person holds simply as a person.

## Sub lesson 2 — The founding texts

Human rights stand in fundamental texts — declarations, pacts, charters and treaties the citizen can name and read.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: human rights belong to every person — they are defined, they have a history, and they stand in founding texts a citizen can name. Give one real example from your own life or from the Senegal you live in, and name the lesson's key words: human rights, droits de l'homme, declarations, pacts, charters. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: human rights belong to every person — they are defined, they have a history, and they stand in founding texts a citizen can name.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
