# Hands-on — Women's Rights and Parity

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Name one place in public life where women's presence has changed in your lifetime, and say what helped it change. This lesson takes human rights to the half of the nation whose rights were longest denied.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: women's rights are protected and promoted — and parity, women's equal place in decision-making, is the question Senegal's course puts openly. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
