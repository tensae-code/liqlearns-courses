# Lesson plan — The Culture of Peace

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Senegal · **Title:** Peace and Solidarity · **Lesson:** The Culture of Peace
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — Heritage, Peace at World Scale and National Challenges (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a culture of peace is built — through tolerance, dialogue of cultures and religions, and pro-peace attitudes learned and practised. This lesson stands on teacher guides alone — evidence only, labelled as such.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 4 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Tolerance and dialogue of cultures and religions** — A culture of peace grows through tolerance and through dialogue between cultures and between religions.
2. **Senegal's dialogue, and learning pro-peace attitudes** — Senegal's Islamo-Christian dialogue is the home example, and pro-peace attitudes are learned from religious peace texts, proverbs and legends.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Stands in path order after **Historical and Cultural Heritage** — do that lesson first if this one feels hard (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: a culture of peace is built — through tolerance, dialogue of cultures and religions, and pro-peace attitudes learned and practised.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, school or town — or, for this book's lessons, from the public life of Senegal they live in.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the learner's own life in Senegal and the book's own teaching, as the book itself works.
