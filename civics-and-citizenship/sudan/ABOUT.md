# About the Civics and Citizenship — Sudan course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this
course comes from government textbooks — this country's own
History and National Education book for Grade 3 of Basic
Education. The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box
is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the
lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Sudan inside the Civics and Citizenship
folder (one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what they
prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a
recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school grade the
book comes from appears only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

The course as built has **one level**:

1. **Level 1 — the homeland and the self as Sudanese.** Sudan's
   earliest book in hand teaches identity before institutions:
   first what "Sudan" means and how it was formed, with its
   civilisation as a source of pride; then, at the book's close,
   "I am Sudanese" — nationality learned through a dialogue with a
   boy and his identity card, and belonging taught through faces,
   dress, food and a closing homeland poem. All six lessons stand
   on a **student book** and count in the acceptance reckoning
   below.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
book's own shape, walked in order):

- *Our homeland, then ourselves:* "First the homeland: what Sudan
  means, how it was formed, and the civilisation it built. Then
  yourself in it: I am Sudanese — my nationality, my people's
  traits and morals, our customs and traditions."
