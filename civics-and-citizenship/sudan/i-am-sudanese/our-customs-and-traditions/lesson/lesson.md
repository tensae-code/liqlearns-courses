# Our Customs and Traditions

## Do it first

Think of what people wear and eat in your home on an ordinary day, and on a special day. Those dresses, foods and ways are customs. Ask: which customs and traditions are Sudanese? That is this lesson — the book's closing lesson.

## Dress, food and ways — and the closing poem

Our customs and traditions — عاداتنا وتقاليدنا — are the ways Sudanese people live: the dress they wear, the food they eat, and the ways they do things together.

Customs are how belonging shows in daily life: you can see a people's traditions at the table, in clothing, and in how people greet and gather.

The book closes this unit — and the placed course — with the “Our Sudan” poem: belonging sung, after it has been named, formed, and lived.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: our customs and traditions — Sudanese dress, food and ways — are ours to know and keep, and the book closes with the “Our Sudan” poem. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: customs, traditions, dress, food, Our Sudan poem. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
