# Hands-on — Our Customs and Traditions

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Think of what people wear and eat in your home on an ordinary day, and on a special day. Those dresses, foods and ways are customs. Ask: which customs and traditions are Sudanese? That is this lesson — the book's closing lesson.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: our customs and traditions — Sudanese dress, food and ways — are ours to know and keep, and the book closes with the “Our Sudan” poem. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
