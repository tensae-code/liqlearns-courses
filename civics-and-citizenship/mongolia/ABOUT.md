# About the Civics and Citizenship — Mongolia course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Иргэний боловсрол (Civic Education) and Иргэний ёс зүйн
боловсрол (Civic Ethics Education) books.
The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is
from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the
lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Mongolia inside the Civics and
Citizenship folder (one course per country). Inside it, the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place
is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the
ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced
gate. The school grades the books come from appear only in
each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

Mongolia's own way, preserved whole, as filed: the youngest
book in the series "Би — Монгол улсын иргэн" (I am a citizen
of Mongolia) starts with greetings, parents, the teacher and
the ger — citizenship as manners and belonging at home
before any state content. Grade 2 names the customs
themselves — a custom of respect, a custom of friendship —
and reaches the state flag, the anthem and Naadam a year
before the state-and-government chapter of Grade 3, whose
state chapter already teaches elections, taxes and the
budget. Grade 4 turns the customs into knowledge "from the
customs of…" (ёсноос) and closes with the series' most civic
primary block: how the state serves citizens, pupil
self-government, why rules bind, and fairness. Grade 5
completes the primary arc — customs become systems (dress
symbolism, traditional measures, heritage protection) — and
closes by naming rules, duties and responsibilities as one
set, with the local state organisations in view. At Grade 6
the subject changes name to Civic Ethics and turns inward —
body, speech and mind watched and corrected, faults found in
oneself before others, and lineage kept as the beginning of
protecting the homeland; Grade 7 completes the arc from the
senses to the fruits of actions — deeds ripen into
consequences, meritorious deeds are multiplied and sinful
ones restrained — and closes with national pride embodied in
Chinggis Khan.
