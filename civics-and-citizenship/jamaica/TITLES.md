# Civics and Citizenship — Jamaica course — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

Civics belongs to the country: this course is Jamaica's own —
built from Jamaica's Ministry of Education and Youth National
Standards Curriculum Civics Education guides (Grades 4-6 and
Grades 7-9, English, 2022, both COMPLETE as filed). Inside
the course the organisation is by **levels, not grades**: the
titles below ARE the levels' titles, in path order — what the
learner proves, step by step. The grades the books come from
live only in each lesson's sources.

The path's own logic is Jamaica's, from the filing's ladder
sketch: three recurring themes — identity, culture and
heritage; symbols, rituals and celebrations; rights,
responsibilities and stewardship — spiralling from the
child's self in Grade 4 to governance and leadership in
Grade 9. Level 1 is the Grade 4 opening (self, family and
community identity, the symbols around the child, a child's
own rights and care for the environment); Level 2 widens to
the school, community builders, the Caribbean citizen,
national symbols and awards, and government as the people's
expression; Level 3 takes identity global through sport and
festivals, with stewardship as the senior primary theme;
Level 4 reworks identity for adolescence (Free Villages,
respect), symbols through school reputation, and rights
through Emancipation and the heroes who defended them;
Level 5 is independence, diversity and Vision 2030, protocol
and ceremony, neighbours' rights, the regional environment
and social justice; Level 6 ends at destiny and national
identity documents, nation builders and nation-building,
good governance, and leadership and legacy. That ladder IS
the path order (recommended, never forced — a learner can
jump in higher; the "start here instead" hints point down
this ladder). Every lesson in this course stands on
curriculum guides — evidence only, acceptance 0, labelled in
every lesson's sources.

## The titles

**Level 1 — self, community and first symbols** (the Grade 4
books' order):

- `identity-culture-and-heritage/` — Level 1. Personal
  Identity (defining and valuing your own identity, family
  roots and group identity) · Community and Group Culture
  (what a community is, living and working together, the
  events that hold it together) · Being Jamaican
  (Jamaicaness and "smaddyness" as a template for personal
  identity). These sit first because Jamaica's own course
  starts with who the child is.
- `symbols-rituals-and-celebrations/` — Level 1. Symbols,
  Rituals and Celebrations Around Us (family, community,
  parish and national symbols; heroes, heroine and cultural
  icons) · Civic and Cultural Celebrations (their kinds,
  meanings and belonging) · Honouring Contributors (people
  who contributed, and behaviour at functions honouring
  them).
- `rights-responsibilities-and-stewardship/` — Level 1.
  Environmental Stewardship and Disasters (care for the
  environment, hurricane preparation, protecting natural
  resources) · Rights and Responsibilities of the Child
  (children's rights and their protection, rules and laws,
  informed decisions).

**Level 2 — school, community builders and the Caribbean
citizen** (the Grade 5 books' order):

- `identity-culture-and-heritage/` — Level 2. My School and
  My Identity (the school's history and image, its
  community, your talents in a group) · Community Builders
  (the community's role in forming citizens; builders who
  pass on skills and values) · Caribbean Citizens (yourself
  as a Caribbean citizen, shaped by shared history and
  culture).
- `symbols-rituals-and-celebrations/` — Level 2.
  Celebrations at School (the school's symbols and rituals,
  and respect for them) · Celebrations at Home and in the
  Community (how they evolved, and why they must continue)
  · National Symbols and National Awards (the nation's
  symbols, and the awards honouring achievement and
  service).
- `rights-responsibilities-and-stewardship/` — Level 2.
  Rights, Leadership and the Environment (rights with
  responsible behaviour, answering violations, leadership
  for the greater good, sustainable practice) · Government
  and the People (the interdependence of government and
  people — "before you have a government you must have the
  people").

**Level 3 — Jamaica in the world: sport, festivals and
stewardship** (the Grade 6 books' order):

- `identity-culture-and-heritage/` — Level 3. Jamaica in
  World Sport (Jamaica's contribution through football,
  cricket and track and field) · Sport as National Identity
  (sporting performance as a symbol of cultural identity)
  · Global Citizenship Through Sport (tolerance, respect
  and unity through international sport).
- `symbols-rituals-and-celebrations/` — Level 3. Caribbean
  Festivals Overseas (their origins, purposes and why they
  continue) · Caribbean Festival Characters (Jonkonnu and
  the characters from the colonial and ancestral past) ·
  Cultural Retentions (what Jamaica and the Caribbean keep,
  and how to help keep it).
- `rights-responsibilities-and-stewardship/` — Level 3.
  Caring for the Environment (good stewardship and the
  international organisations fighting pollution and waste)
  · Stewardship and National Development (how one person's
  stewardship and civic pride build the nation).

**Level 4 — keeping self, respect and rights in action**
(the Grade 7 books' order):

- `identity-culture-and-heritage/` — Level 4. Keeping Self,
  Changing Context (holding your integrity in a larger
  group, through the Free Villages) · Valuing Heritage —
  Yours and Mine (a Free Village's self-reliance,
  cooperation and cohesion) · Respect Due (respect for
  self, peers, property, time and authority).
- `symbols-rituals-and-celebrations/` — Level 4. Celebrate
  Me, Celebrate My School (how rituals and symbols build a
  school's reputation, through the ISSA Championships) ·
  Honouring the Name That I Bear (school pride, loyalty and
  reputation, the Championships case continued).
- `rights-responsibilities-and-stewardship/` — Level 4. Get
  Up, Stand Up (rights restricted and defended, through
  Emancipation) · Wake Up and Live (rights and responsible
  participation through Paul Bogle and George William
  Gordon) · Valuing Our Cultural Heritage (preserving
  heritage by taking part, volunteering and stewarding).

**Level 5 — independence, unity and social justice** (the
Grade 8 books' order):

- `identity-culture-and-heritage/` — Level 5. Growing Up
  Jamaican (personal independence through National
  Independence) · Unity and Prosperity (diversity and
  cohesion, patriotism and Vision 2030) · Forests, Waters
  and Shining Sun (each island's uniqueness inside regional
  identity).
- `symbols-rituals-and-celebrations/` — Level 5. Life Is One
  Big Road (community signs, names and symbols as life's
  guides) · Protocol at Rituals and Ceremonies (the right
  procedures and behaviour) · Standing on Ceremony (values
  in national emblems, awards, honours and uniformed
  groups).
- `rights-responsibilities-and-stewardship/` — Level 5. One
  Han' Caan Clap (a neighbour's rights; collaboration,
  fairness and regional integration) · United We Stand
  (character and integrity through the region's shared
  environmental problems) · Unity in the Community — Social
  Justice (the principles of social justice, and your gifts
  used in a crisis).

**Level 6 — destiny, nation-building, governance and
leadership** (the Grade 9 books' order, as the guides
themselves end):

- `identity-culture-and-heritage/` — Level 6. Every Man Has
  a Right (deciding your own destiny; success and its
  values) · I Am a Vision — National Identity Documents
  (the documents that symbolise national identity, and how
  they are acquired) · From the Local I to the Global Me
  (Jamaica's and its citizens' impact on the world).
- `symbols-rituals-and-celebrations/` — Level 6. Sharing Our
  Success with Others (Jamaica in global cultural affairs;
  devising your own contribution) · Share the Vision, Reap
  the Rewards (the nation builders' contributions, and
  adding yours).
- `rights-responsibilities-and-stewardship/` — Level 6. The
  Community and I — Governance (good governance, collective
  responsibility, social justice and peace) · Help Me Big
  Up Jamaica (Bustamante and Manley, their legacies, and
  promoting development) · To the World — Leadership and
  Legacy (Garvey and the responsibilities and challenges of
  wholesome leadership). The course ends where Jamaica's
  own guides end: with the citizen ready to contribute and
  lead.
