# Government and the People

## Do it first

Say in one sentence what a government is for. This lesson starts from Jamaica's own order: before you have a government, you must have the people.

The interdependent relationship between government and the people ("before you have a government you must have the people"), civic pride, care for property and coexisting.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The interdependent relationship between government and the people ("before you have a government you must have the people"), civic pride, care for property and coexisting. Give one real example from your own life or from the Jamaica you live in, and name the lesson's key words: government, people. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: The interdependent relationship between government and the people ("before you have a government you must have the people"), civic pride, care for property and coexisting.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
