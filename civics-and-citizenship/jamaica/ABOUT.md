# About the Civics and Citizenship — Jamaica course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Civics Education curriculum guides. The one exception
is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people
making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Jamaica inside the Civics and
Citizenship folder (one course per country). Inside it, the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place
is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the
ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced
gate. The school grades the books come from appear only in
each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

The course as built has **six stocked levels**, from the
single filing for this build (`jamaica-placements.md` —
2 records / 2 distinct books, both read in place at
phase3-staging, as filed: both are National Standards
Curriculum guides, teacher-facing curriculum documents in
English, both marked TEACHER (curriculum), both COMPLETE
as filed; 49 placements in all, stocking 49 lessons — every
placement stocks into exactly one lesson):

1. **Level 1 — Self, Community and First Symbols
   (8 lessons).** The Grade 4 opening: personal identity,
   community and group culture, and being Jamaican; the
   symbols, rituals and celebrations around the child, and
   honouring contributors; then a child's environmental
   stewardship and disaster preparation, and the rights and
   responsibilities of the child.
2. **Level 2 — School, Community Builders and the
   Caribbean Citizen (8 lessons).** The Grade 5 widening:
   the school and the learner's identity inside it,
   community builders, and the self as a Caribbean citizen;
   celebrations at school, at home and in the community,
   and national symbols and National Awards; then rights
   with leadership and the environment, and government as
   the people's expression.
3. **Level 3 — Jamaica in the World — Sport, Festivals and
   Stewardship (8 lessons).** The Grade 6 year: Jamaica's
   identity carried worldwide through sport, and global
   citizenship learned through it; Caribbean festivals
   overseas, their characters, and cultural retentions;
   and stewardship grown senior — care for the environment
   and stewardship as national development.
4. **Level 4 — Keeping Self, Respect and Rights in Action
   (8 lessons).** The Grade 7 reworking for adolescence:
   keeping self in a changing context and valuing heritage,
   taught through the post-emancipation Free Villages, and
   respect due; school symbols and reputation through the
   ISSA Championships; and rights in action — Emancipation,
   and Paul Bogle and George William Gordon — closing with
   valuing cultural heritage.
5. **Level 5 — Independence, Unity and Social Justice
   (9 lessons).** The Grade 8 year: growing up Jamaican
   taught through National Independence, unity and
   prosperity in diversity under Vision 2030, and each
   Caribbean island's uniqueness; protocol and ceremony;
   and neighbours' rights, the region's shared environment,
   and social justice.
6. **Level 6 — Destiny, Nation-Building, Governance and
   Leadership (8 lessons).** The Grade 9 ending: every
   person's right to decide their own destiny, national
   identity and its documents, and the local self become
   global; sharing success and the vision of the nation
   builders; good governance in the community; and
   leadership and legacy — Bustamante, Manley and Marcus
   Garvey — the citizen ready to contribute and lead.

Jamaica's own way, preserved whole, as filed: civics is
taught through identity first — self, family and community,
Garvey quotations, the motto "Out of Many, One People",
national symbols carried through every unit, and
Jamaican/Caribbean cultural forms (festivals, Jonkonnu,
sporting heroes) as the substance of citizenship before
rights and stewardship. The guides run on three recurring
themes — identity, culture and heritage; symbols, rituals
and celebrations; rights, responsibilities and stewardship
— spiralling from the child's self in Grade 4 to governance
and leadership in Grade 9, the secondary years teaching
through Jamaican case studies: Free Villages and
emancipation, Paul Bogle and George William Gordon, the
ISSA championships, Bustamante and Manley, Vision 2030 and
"Brand Jamaica".

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
books' own shape, walked in order):

- *Identity first, widening to the world:* "First, who you
  are: your own identity, your family and community, and
  what being Jamaican means — the symbols and celebrations
  around you, the people who contributed, your rights as a
  child, and your care for the environment. Then your
  school, the builders of your community, and yourself as a
  Caribbean citizen — national symbols and awards, rights
  with leadership, and government as the people's own
  expression. Then Jamaica in the world: sport, festivals
  and the culture Jamaica keeps. Then, as you grow: keeping
  yourself in new places, respect, and rights defended —
  Emancipation, Bogle and Gordon. Then independence, unity
  in diversity, ceremony done properly, your neighbour's
  rights and social justice. And last, your destiny in your
  own hands: identity documents, nation builders, good
  governance, and leadership that leaves a legacy."
