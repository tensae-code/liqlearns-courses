# Civics and Citizenship — India course — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

Civics belongs to the country: this course is India's own —
built from India's Democratic Politics I and II student books
(NCERT, English, 2020-21 reprints, COMPLETE), the Political
Science component of Social Science, filed combined-track
(the whole of each book is the civics component). Inside the
course the organisation is by **levels, not grades**: the
titles below ARE the levels' titles, in path order — what the
learner proves, step by step. The grades the books come from
live only in each lesson's sources.

The path's own logic is India's: a single two-year "tour of
democracy" (the books' own name for their design). The first
year founds: what democracy is and why it is worth having;
how a constitution is designed (South Africa's story, then
India's making and values); how elections work and what makes
them fair; how the institutions actually work; and the
democratic rights that complete the frame. The second year
tests and deepens: power-sharing and federalism; democracy
among diversity; the people's own instruments — struggles,
movements and parties; and finally judgement — outcomes,
challenges, and the tour read whole. That ladder IS the path
order (recommended, never forced — a learner can jump in
higher; the "start here instead" hints point down this
ladder).

## The titles

**Level 1 — democracy and the constitution founded**
(Democratic Politics I, the Class 9 book):

- `democracy/` — Level 1, first title (Ch. 1: What is
  Democracy? Why Democracy?). What Democracy Is (the
  definition built step by step into its features) · Why
  Democracy (the arguments for and against, argued against
  each other).
- `constitution/` — Level 1, second title (Ch. 2:
  Constitutional Design). South Africa's Constitution Story
  (constitution-making learned from apartheid's end and
  Mandela's stand against domination of any kind) · Making
  India's Constitution (how the Constitution was made, its
  foundational values, and its framework for democratic
  politics).
- `elections/` — Level 1, third title (Ch. 3: Electoral
  Politics). Electoral Politics (why elections are needed,
  what makes them democratic, political competition) · Free
  and Fair Elections (the independent Election Commission,
  nomination, participation, and the named challenges —
  money, muscle, the level field).
- `institutions/` — Level 1, fourth title (Ch. 4: Working of
  Institutions). The Two Houses of Parliament (the Council of
  States and the House of the People, and a day in the life
  of the Lok Sabha) · The Prime Minister and the Council of
  Ministers (the political executive as a team, assisted by
  the secretaries and the civil services) · The President
  (the formal head, and how presidential powers are
  exercised within the institutions).
- `rights/` — Level 1, fifth title (Ch. 5: Democratic
  Rights). Democratic Rights (the rights the Constitution
  gives citizens) · Rights and the Reservations Debate
  (rights argued in real cases — reservations for Scheduled
  Castes and Scheduled Tribes tested against equality, and
  the UN Secretary-General's words on rights).

**Level 2 — democracy at work** (Democratic Politics II, the
Class 10 book):

- `power-sharing/` — Level 2, first title (Unit I). Power-
  Sharing (power shared among legislature, executive and
  judiciary, and among communities — Belgium's accommodation
  against Sri Lanka's majoritarianism) · Federalism (the
  vertical division of power, in general terms and then in
  India's own federal working).
- `diversity/` — Level 2, second title (Unit II). Democracy
  and Diversity (accommodating linguistic and regional
  diversities; why social diversity does not threaten
  democracy) · Gender, Religion and Caste (three social
  differences, how each becomes division and inequality, and
  how each is expressed in politics).
- `movements/` — Level 2, third title (Unit III, first
  half). Popular Struggles and Movements (how people press
  demands outside elections — struggles and movements as
  democracy's own instruments).
- `parties/` — Level 2, fourth title (Unit III, second
  half). Political Parties (parties studied for themselves —
  their role in the rise of democracies, constitutional
  design, electoral politics and the making and working of
  governments).
- `judging-democracy/` — Level 2, fifth title (Unit IV, plus
  the books' own design read whole). Outcomes of Democracy
  (what democracy does; what outcomes can be expected from
  it) · Challenges to Democracy (the challenges democracy
  faces in India and elsewhere, and reforming democratic
  politics) · The Tour of Democracy (Books 1–2 as one course
  — the "tour of democracy" the books name, each chapter's
  overview carrying the argument forward).
