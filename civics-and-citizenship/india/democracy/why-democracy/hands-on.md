# Hands-on — Why Democracy

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
List one decision a government made that you think was good and one you think was bad. For the bad one, write: what could ordinary people do about it? This lesson argues the case for democracy against the case against it.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: democracy is worth having because it gives an accountable, responsive and legitimate government, handles social conflicts, and can correct its own mistakes. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
