# Why Democracy

## Do it first

List one decision a government made that you think was good and one you think was bad. For the bad one, write: what could ordinary people do about it? This lesson argues the case for democracy against the case against it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The arguments against, faced honestly

The case against democracy is argued first: it can be slow, leaders keep changing, and ordinary people may not know what is best for them.

The lesson does not hide these charges — it answers them one by one.

## Sub lesson 2 — Accountable, responsive, legitimate

Democracy answers with a government that is accountable to the people, responsive to their needs, and legitimate because the people themselves chose it.

A government that must face the people regularly cannot ignore them for long.

## Sub lesson 3 — Handling conflicts and correcting mistakes

Democracy gives a peaceful way to handle social conflicts — by discussion and majority decision instead of force.

And when a democratic government makes a mistake, the mistake can be exposed and corrected; no ruler is beyond being changed.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: democracy is worth having because it gives an accountable, responsive and legitimate government, handles social conflicts, and can correct its own mistakes. Give one real example from your own life or from the India you live in, and name the lesson's key words: accountable government, legitimacy, self-correction. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
