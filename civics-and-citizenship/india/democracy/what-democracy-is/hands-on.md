# Hands-on — What Democracy Is

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Think of any group you belong to — a class, a team, a family. Write down: who makes the decisions, how did that person get the power to decide, and can the others change the decider? This lesson works out, step by step, what makes a way of ruling a democracy.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: democracy means rulers elected by the people, free and fair elections, one person one vote one value, and rule of law with respect for rights. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
