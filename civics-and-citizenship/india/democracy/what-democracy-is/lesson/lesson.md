# What Democracy Is

## Do it first

Think of any group you belong to — a class, a team, a family. Write down: who makes the decisions, how did that person get the power to decide, and can the others change the decider? This lesson works out, step by step, what makes a way of ruling a democracy.

## Sub lesson 1 — Rulers elected by the people

In a democracy the rulers are elected by the people: the people choose who will govern them, and the government rules in their name.

That choice is what makes the government the people's government, not a ruler's private possession.

## Sub lesson 2 — Free and fair elections, one vote one value

Elections in a democracy must be free and fair, and every adult citizen's vote counts the same: one person, one vote, one value.

No vote weighs more than another because its owner is richer, stronger or better known.

## Sub lesson 3 — Rule of law and respect for rights

Democracy also means the rule of law: everyone, rulers included, is bound by the law, and the rights of citizens are respected.

A government that wins an election and then ignores the law and people's rights has stepped outside democracy.

Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
