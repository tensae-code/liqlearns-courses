# Hands-on — The Tour of Democracy

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Draw the course you have just climbed as a road on paper: mark where it started, the towns it passed through, and where it ends. This lesson walks that road as the books themselves name it — their own design, stated whole.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: the two books are one course — a tour of democracy from what democracy is to what challenges it — and each chapter's overview carries the argument one step forward. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
