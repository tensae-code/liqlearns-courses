# The Tour of Democracy

## Do it first

Draw the course you have just climbed as a road on paper: mark where it started, the towns it passed through, and where it ends. This lesson walks that road as the books themselves name it — their own design, stated whole.

## Sub lesson 1 — One course in two books

The books name their own design: Classes 9 and 10 together form a single tour of democracy.

The first book founds the frame; the second tests and deepens it — neither half is the whole course alone.

## Sub lesson 2 — From what democracy is to what challenges it

The tour runs in one direction: from what democracy is and why it is worth having, through constitution, elections, institutions and rights, to power-sharing, diversity, parties, outcomes and challenges.

Each stop answers the question the stop before it raised.

## Sub lesson 3 — Overviews carry the argument

Each chapter opens with an overview that carries the argument forward from the chapters before it.

Reading the overviews in a row is reading the tour's map — the design this whole course has followed.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the two books are one course — a tour of democracy from what democracy is to what challenges it — and each chapter's overview carries the argument one step forward. Give one real example from your own life or from the India you live in, and name the lesson's key words: tour of democracy, overview, one course. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
