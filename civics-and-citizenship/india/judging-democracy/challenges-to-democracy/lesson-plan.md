# Lesson plan — Challenges to Democracy

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — India · **Title:** Judging Democracy · **Lesson:** Challenges to Democracy
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — Democracy at Work (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** democracy faces challenges in India and elsewhere — and the course ends by asking what can be done to reform democratic politics.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Gathering both years** — The concluding chapter gathers both years of the tour: everything studied — constitution, elections, institutions, rights, parties, outcomes — is now brought to one question.
2. **Challenges in India and elsewhere** — The challenges are named for India and for democracies elsewhere: the founding challenge of making the transition, the challenge of expansion, and the challenge of deepening democracy.
3. **Reforming democratic politics** — The chapter does not end in complaint: it asks what can be done to reform democratic politics.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: democracy faces challenges in India and elsewhere — and the course ends by asking what can be done to reform democratic politics.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or town — or, for this book's lessons, from the public life of India they live in.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the learner's own life in India and the book's own teaching, as the book itself works.

- Thinks naming challenges means democracy is hopeless → the chapter ends in reform: challenges are stages to be worked through, and the formed citizen is how they are met.
- Go back to the one thing: democracy faces challenges in India and elsewhere — and the course ends by asking what can be done to reform democratic politics. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
