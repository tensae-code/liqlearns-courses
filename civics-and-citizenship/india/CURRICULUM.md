# Civics and Citizenship — India course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for India inside the Civics and Citizenship folder,
organised by **levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they
have proved the level, not when a school calendar says so. The
school grades the books come from appear only in each lesson's
sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation. The
path follows India's own order as its two books lay it out: one
two-year "tour of democracy" — the first year founds democracy
and the constitution, the second year tests democracy at work:
sharing power, diversity, parties, outcomes.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this
course comes from government textbooks — this country's own
Democratic Politics books (the Political Science component of
NCERT Social Science) — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a
box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the
lessons are the books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book list,
the library-debt recovery of both books, and the acceptance
reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **Democracy and the Constitution Founded** — what democracy
   is, built step by step into its features (rulers elected by
   the people, free and fair elections, one person one vote one
   value, rule of law and respect for rights), and why it is
   worth having (accountable, responsive, legitimate;
   conflict-handling; self-correction); how a constitution is
   designed, learned from South Africa's story and then India's
   own making, values and framework; how elections work and
   what makes them free and fair (the independent Election
   Commission; money, muscle and the level field named as
   challenges); how the institutions work — Parliament's two
   Houses, the Prime Minister and Council of Ministers, the
   President; and the democratic rights that complete the
   frame, argued through the reservations debate. 11 lessons,
   in five titles: `democracy/` (2 lessons), `constitution/`
   (2), `elections/` (2), `institutions/` (3), `rights/` (2).
2. **Democracy at Work** — power-sharing (Belgium's
   accommodation against Sri Lanka's majoritarianism) and
   federalism, India's vertical division of power; democracy
   among diversity — accommodating linguistic and regional
   diversities, and gender, religion and caste as differences
   that can harden into division; the people's own instruments
   between elections — popular struggles and movements, and
   political parties studied for themselves; and finally
   judgement — democracy's outcomes weighed honestly, its
   challenges named, and the tour itself read whole as the
   books name it. 9 lessons, in five titles: `power-sharing/`
   (2 lessons), `diversity/` (2), `movements/` (1),
   `parties/` (1), `judging-democracy/` (3).

**Totals: 20 lessons** (Level 1: 11 · Level 2: 9 — all from
two student books).
