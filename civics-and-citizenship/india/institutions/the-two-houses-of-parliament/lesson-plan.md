# Lesson plan — The Two Houses of Parliament

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — India · **Title:** Working of Institutions · **Lesson:** The Two Houses of Parliament
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — Democracy and the Constitution Founded (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Parliament has two Houses — the Council of States and the House of the People — and law-making and questioning happen in their daily working, as a day in the Lok Sabha shows.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Democracy is not just electing rulers** — Electing rulers is only the start: democracy lives in how the institutions work every day between elections.
2. **The two Houses** — Parliament has two Houses: the Council of States (Rajya Sabha) and the House of the People (Lok Sabha).
3. **A day in the life of the Lok Sabha** — The lesson walks through a day in the Lok Sabha: questions to ministers, debates, and the making of laws.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
