# Democratic Rights

## Do it first

Write down one thing you believe no one — not a teacher, not an official, not even a majority — should be allowed to do to you. Keep it in front of you: this lesson is about rights of exactly that kind, written into the Constitution.

## Sub lesson 1 — Rights in the Constitution

The Constitution gives citizens rights — claims that the state itself must respect.

Because they stand in the Constitution, no ordinary law or official order can simply take them away.

## Sub lesson 2 — Rights complete the frame

This chapter stands last in the first book on purpose: elections choose the rulers, institutions do the governing, and rights protect the citizen within both.

A democracy without protected rights would leave the citizen defenceless between elections.

## Sub lesson 3 — Rights in a citizen's hands

Rights are not decorations: they are what a citizen can stand on when power is misused.

Knowing your rights is therefore part of being a citizen, not an extra.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the Constitution gives citizens democratic rights, and those rights complete the frame built by the elections and institutions of the earlier chapters. Give one real example from your own life or from the India you live in, and name the lesson's key words: democratic rights, Constitution, citizen. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
