# Hands-on — Democratic Rights

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Write down one thing you believe no one — not a teacher, not an official, not even a majority — should be allowed to do to you. Keep it in front of you: this lesson is about rights of exactly that kind, written into the Constitution.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: the Constitution gives citizens democratic rights, and those rights complete the frame built by the elections and institutions of the earlier chapters. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
