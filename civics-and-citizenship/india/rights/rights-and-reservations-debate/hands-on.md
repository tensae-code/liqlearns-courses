# Hands-on — Rights and the Reservations Debate

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Two classmates both claim fairness: one says fairness means exactly the same treatment for everyone; the other says fairness sometimes means extra help for those who start far behind. Write which claim you find stronger, and why. This lesson argues a real Indian case of exactly this kind.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: rights are argued through real cases — reservations for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes are tested against equality, and the world's rights language is heard in the UN Secretary-General's words. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
