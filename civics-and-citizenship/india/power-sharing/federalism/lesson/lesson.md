# Federalism

## Do it first

Make two lists for your own locality: things a very local body should decide (a street, a school) and things only a whole-country government can decide (defence, currency). The line between your lists is this lesson's subject.

## Sub lesson 1 — The vertical division of power

Federalism is the vertical division of power: power is divided among levels of government, not held at one level alone.

Each level governs in its own sphere, and the constitution protects the division.

## Sub lesson 2 — Federalism in general terms

The lesson first describes federalism in general — what makes a system federal anywhere: more than one level, each with its own jurisdiction, guaranteed by the constitution.

Only then does it turn to any one country, so the idea is clear before the example.

## Sub lesson 3 — India's federal working

India's own working is then studied: a union government and state governments dividing the country's governing between them.

The Indian case shows federalism alive — two levels of elected government answering to the same citizens.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: federalism divides power vertically among levels of government — and India works as a federation of a centre and states, each governing in its own sphere. Give one real example from your own life or from the India you live in, and name the lesson's key words: federalism, levels of government, jurisdiction. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
