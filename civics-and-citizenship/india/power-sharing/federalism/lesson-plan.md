# Lesson plan — Federalism

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — India · **Title:** Power-Sharing and Federalism · **Lesson:** Federalism
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — Democracy at Work (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** federalism divides power vertically among levels of government — and India works as a federation of a centre and states, each governing in its own sphere.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The vertical division of power** — Federalism is the vertical division of power: power is divided among levels of government, not held at one level alone.
2. **Federalism in general terms** — The lesson first describes federalism in general — what makes a system federal anywhere: more than one level, each with its own jurisdiction, guaranteed by the constitution.
3. **India's federal working** — India's own working is then studied: a union government and state governments dividing the country's governing between them.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
