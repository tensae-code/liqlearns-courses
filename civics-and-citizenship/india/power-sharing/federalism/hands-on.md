# Hands-on — Federalism

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Make two lists for your own locality: things a very local body should decide (a street, a school) and things only a whole-country government can decide (defence, currency). The line between your lists is this lesson's subject.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: federalism divides power vertically among levels of government — and India works as a federation of a centre and states, each governing in its own sphere. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
