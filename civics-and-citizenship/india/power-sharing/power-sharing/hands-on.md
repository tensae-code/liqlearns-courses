# Hands-on — Power-Sharing

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Think of a household or team where one person holds every power — money, rules and judging. Write what goes wrong over time. Then write how the powers could be divided. This lesson learns the same division from two neighbouring countries' stories.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: power in a democracy is shared — among legislature, executive and judiciary, and among communities — as Belgium's accommodation and Sri Lanka's majoritarianism teach from opposite sides. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
