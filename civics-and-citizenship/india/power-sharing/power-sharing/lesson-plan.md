# Lesson plan — Power-Sharing

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — India · **Title:** Power-Sharing and Federalism · **Lesson:** Power-Sharing
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — Democracy at Work (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** power in a democracy is shared — among legislature, executive and judiciary, and among communities — as Belgium's accommodation and Sri Lanka's majoritarianism teach from opposite sides.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Two stories — Belgium and Sri Lanka** — The chapter opens with two neighbours: Belgium, which accommodated its communities by sharing power, and Sri Lanka, where the majority community imposed its will.
2. **Sharing among the organs of government** — Power is shared horizontally among the organs of government — legislature, executive and judiciary — so no organ can exercise unlimited power.
3. **Sharing among communities** — Power is also shared among communities and groups, so that all feel they have a stake in the country.
