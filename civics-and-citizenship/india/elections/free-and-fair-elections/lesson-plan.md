# Lesson plan — Free and Fair Elections

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — India · **Title:** Elections · **Lesson:** Free and Fair Elections
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — Democracy and the Constitution Founded (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** India's elections are run by an independent Election Commission, and their fairness is tested against named challenges — money, muscle and an unlevel field.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The independent Election Commission** — India's elections are conducted by the Election Commission, an independent body that stands apart from the government of the day.
2. **Taking part — nomination and participation** — Candidates are nominated, campaigns are held, and the people take part in large numbers.
3. **The challenges — money, muscle, a level field** — The lesson names the challenges plainly: the power of money, the use of muscle, and whether the field is level for all contestants.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
