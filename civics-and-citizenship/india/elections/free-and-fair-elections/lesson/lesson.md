# Free and Fair Elections

## Do it first

Imagine a school election where one candidate's family pays for everyone's lunch and another candidate's friends threaten voters. Write down what is unfair, and who could stop it. This lesson meets the body India built for exactly that job.

## Sub lesson 1 — The independent Election Commission

India's elections are conducted by the Election Commission, an independent body that stands apart from the government of the day.

Its independence is the point: the referee of the contest must not play for one of the teams.

## Sub lesson 2 — Taking part — nomination and participation

Candidates are nominated, campaigns are held, and the people take part in large numbers.

Popular participation is itself a sign of health: people vote because they believe the vote counts.

## Sub lesson 3 — The challenges — money, muscle, a level field

The lesson names the challenges plainly: the power of money, the use of muscle, and whether the field is level for all contestants.

Naming the challenges is part of the teaching: a fair election is judged by how it handles them.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: India's elections are run by an independent Election Commission, and their fairness is tested against named challenges — money, muscle and an unlevel field. Give one real example from your own life or from the India you live in, and name the lesson's key words: Election Commission, nomination, level playing field. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
