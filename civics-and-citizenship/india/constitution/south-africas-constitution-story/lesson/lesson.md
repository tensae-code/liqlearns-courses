# South Africa's Constitution Story

## Do it first

Imagine two groups in one country who have been enemies for years must now write the rules for living together. Write down: what must each side give up, and what must each side be promised? This lesson tells how South Africa actually did it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Apartheid and its end

South Africa was ruled under apartheid, a system that divided people by race and denied black South Africans equal rights.

The struggle against apartheid, led by Nelson Mandela and his movement, ended that system and opened the way to a new constitution.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mandela's words — no domination

Mandela's stand, in the book's own telling, was against domination of any kind: he had fought white domination, and he fought black domination too.

The new country was not to swap one domination for another; it was to end domination itself.

## Sub lesson 3 — Designing a constitution together

South Africans of all races then designed their constitution together, as citizens of one country.

The story teaches what a constitution is for: it is the agreed frame within which former enemies agree to live and rule together.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: South Africa shows how a divided country designs a constitution together — apartheid ended, and black and white South Africans wrote their new rules as equals. Give one real example from your own life or from the India you live in, and name the lesson's key words: apartheid, Nelson Mandela, constitution-making. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
