# About the Civics and Citizenship — India course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this
course comes from government textbooks — this country's own
Democratic Politics books (Democratic Politics I and II, the
Political Science component of NCERT Social Science). The one
exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the
people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the
books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for India inside the Civics and Citizenship
folder (one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is
by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what
they prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a
recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school grades
the books come from appear only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

The course as built has **two stocked levels**, one from each
student book in hand:

1. **Level 1 — Democracy and the constitution founded (11
   lessons).** Democratic Politics I: what democracy is and
   why it is worth having; constitutional design (South
   Africa's story, then India's making, values and
   framework); electoral politics and free and fair elections;
   the working of institutions (Parliament's two Houses, the
   Prime Minister and Council of Ministers, the President);
   and democratic rights, argued through the reservations
   debate.
2. **Level 2 — Democracy at work (9 lessons).** Democratic
   Politics II: power-sharing and federalism; democracy and
   diversity, and gender, religion and caste; popular
   struggles and movements; political parties; outcomes of
   democracy; challenges to democracy; and the two books read
   whole as the "tour of democracy" they name themselves.

India's own way, preserved whole: civics is taught as a
two-year "tour of democracy" — concepts are built from stories
and cartoons (R. K. Laxman's among them), every chapter opening
with an overview and closing with exercises that ask for
reasoning, not recall. The second year tests democracy against
India's own diversity — and opens its power-sharing chapter
with two neighbour stories, Belgium and Sri Lanka, the second
told as the warning of what majoritarianism does.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
books' own shape, walked in order):

- *The tour of democracy:* "First, the founding: what
  democracy is and why it is worth having, how a constitution
  is designed — South Africa's story, then India's own — how
  elections are made free and fair, how Parliament, the Prime
  Minister and the President actually work, and the rights
  that complete the frame. Then the testing: power shared and
  divided, democracy living among diversity, the people's
  struggles, movements and parties — and at the end, judgement:
  what democracy delivers, what challenges it, and the whole
  tour seen as one road."
