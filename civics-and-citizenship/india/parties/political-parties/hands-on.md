# Hands-on — Political Parties

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Name any political party you have heard of. Write: what does it actually do all year — not just at elections? This lesson answers that question fully, meeting parties again, now as the subject itself.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: political parties are democracy's working machinery — they contest elections, form governments and hold them to account, and this chapter studies them for themselves. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
