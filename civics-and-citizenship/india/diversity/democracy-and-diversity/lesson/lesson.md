# Democracy and Diversity

## Do it first

List the languages spoken and the home regions of people in your own class or street. Then write: does that variety make shared decisions harder, impossible, or richer? This lesson gives democracy's own answer.

## Sub lesson 1 — Diversity is a fact of social life

Societies differ within themselves — by language, region, origin and more — and those differences are a fact, not a fault.

The question is never whether diversity exists, but how a political system handles it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Power distributed to accommodate diversity

Power can be distributed so that linguistic and regional diversities are accommodated — given room and a share in governing.

Accommodation turns difference from a threat into a part of the country's shared life.

## Sub lesson 3 — Why diversity does not threaten democracy

Social diversity does not threaten democracy, because democracy gives every group a voice and a peaceful way to press its claims.

Democracy is threatened not by diversity but by refusing to accommodate it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: social diversity does not threaten democracy — democracy is the system that can accommodate linguistic and regional diversities by distributing power. Give one real example from your own life or from the India you live in, and name the lesson's key words: diversity, accommodation, linguistic diversity. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
