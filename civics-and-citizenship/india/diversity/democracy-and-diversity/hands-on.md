# Hands-on — Democracy and Diversity

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
List the languages spoken and the home regions of people in your own class or street. Then write: does that variety make shared decisions harder, impossible, or richer? This lesson gives democracy's own answer.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: social diversity does not threaten democracy — democracy is the system that can accommodate linguistic and regional diversities by distributing power. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
