# Gender, Religion and Caste

## Do it first

Write down three ways people around you are grouped — by the work men and women are expected to do, by religion, by caste. For each, note one way the grouping can turn unfair. This lesson studies exactly those three differences and what politics does with them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Three kinds of social difference

The chapter takes three differences one by one: gender, religion and caste.

Each begins as a social difference — a real variety in how people live — before it is anything else.

## Sub lesson 2 — When difference becomes division and inequality

A difference becomes a social division when it hardens into unequal treatment — when one group is placed above another.

Gender, religion and caste have each, in India, become sources of inequality of this kind, and the chapter shows how for each.

## Sub lesson 3 — How each is expressed in politics

Each difference is also expressed in politics: parties appeal to it, voters are counted by it, and demands are raised in its name.

Seeing the expression clearly is the lesson's aim — a citizen who can name it can judge it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: gender, religion and caste are three kinds of social difference that can become social division and inequality in India — and each is expressed in politics. Give one real example from your own life or from the India you live in, and name the lesson's key words: gender, caste, social division. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
