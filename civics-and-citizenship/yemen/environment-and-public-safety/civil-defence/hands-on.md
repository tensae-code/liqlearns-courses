# Hands-on — Civil Defence

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: Civil Defence. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Yemen's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The civil defence as the body charged with protecting people and property from fires, disasters and wars — relieving the stricken, training volunteers, fighting fires, evacuation, searching for the trapped, checking safety conditions and guiding citizens; why fires start; keeping the home safe from fire; using an extinguisher; and first aid for a burn — cut off the air, cool with water, do not strip the burned clothes, no creams or powders, and get the casualty to hospital. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
