# About the Civics and Citizenship — Yemen course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own National Education books. The one exception is the
**Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Yemen inside the Civics and
Citizenship folder (one course per country). Inside it, the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place
is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the
ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced
gate. The school grades the books come from appear only in
each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

Yemen's own way, preserved whole, as filed: national
education opens with resistance — Yemen as the graveyard of
invaders and its revolutions — before the homeland's
symbols, its administration and its three powers, closing
the Grade 5 book on four Yemeni lives held up as models.
The seventh-grade book turns from the state to the person —
how a child is raised, the values he is raised by, the
family that produces instead of importing, and the place of
people with disabilities inside society. The eighth-grade
book names the citizen — first who he is (identity,
independence, defence, betrayal), then what is his (rights,
one family at a time) and what he owes (responsibilities),
then the safety of the world around him, closing on his
time, his youth and the media that reach him. And one guide
carries three grades at once, its last chapter a small
encyclopaedia for the teacher — the state trusts the teacher
with the substance (the family, children's rights in Yemen,
the sanctity of public money, civil-society institutions)
and lets the pupil's book stay thin. Unit assessments
(تقويم الوحدة) are named in the book entries below and
excluded, as filed; constitutional content is named by
topic and summarised, never transcribed.

The course as built has **thirteen stocked levels**, from
the filings for this build (`yemen-placements.md` — 5
records / 5 distinct books; 50 placements from three read
student books — and `yemen-placements-b.md` — the combined
Grades 7–9 teacher guide, not in the census, 8 placements,
TEACHER evidence; 58 placements in all, stocking 58 lessons
— the coined lesson names were deduped before building, no
two placements shared a lesson, and every placement stocks
into exactly one lesson):

1. **Level 1 — National Symbols and Revolutions
   (5 lessons).** Yemen, graveyard of invaders; the Yemeni
   revolutions; Yemeni unity; Yemeni steadfastness; the
   symbols of the homeland.
2. **Level 2 — How My Homeland Is Administered
   (3 lessons).** The administrative division, the
   governorate and the directorate, and facilities and
   services.
3. **Level 3 — Powers of the State (4 lessons).** The
   legislative, executive and judicial powers, and shura
   beside democracy.
4. **Level 4 — Yemeni Figures (4 lessons).** Imam al-Qasim
   ibn Muhammad, Rajih Ghalib Labouza, President Ibrahim
   al-Hamdi, and the poet Abdullah al-Baradouni.
5. **Level 5 — Family and Social Upbringing (4 lessons).**
   Social upbringing, its methods, Islam and social
   upbringing, and the family and social morals.
6. **Level 6 — Values (6 lessons).** What values are,
   honesty and trustworthiness, patience and self-control,
   sincerity and mastery in work, cooperation and social
   solidarity, and order and responsibility.
7. **Level 7 — Productive Families and Self-Sufficiency
   (4 lessons).** Productive families, in the countryside
   and in the city, and self-sufficiency.
8. **Level 8 — People with Special Needs (4 lessons).**
   Who they are, what they need, the institutions for them,
   and inclusion in society.
9. **Level 9 — National Identity (4 lessons).** National
   identity, independence and sovereignty, defending the
   homeland, and betraying the homeland.
10. **Level 10 — Rights and Responsibilities (5 lessons).**
    Rights and responsibilities, personal rights, political
    and cultural rights, economic and social rights, and
    the citizen's responsibilities.
11. **Level 11 — Environment and Public Safety
    (4 lessons).** Environmental health, security and
    public safety, civil defence, and Islam and the
    environment.
12. **Level 12 — Social Issues (3 lessons).** Organising
    and investing time, summer centres and youth, and the
    media.
13. **Level 13 — Teacher-Guide Background — Evidence Only
    (8 lessons).** The combined Grades 7–9 teacher guide's
    background essays: the family; childhood and children's
    rights in Yemen; values, honesty, patience and
    solidarity; Yemeni emigration; the sanctity of public
    money; global media; civil-society institutions; and
    cultural rights and authority. Evidence only —
    acceptance 0, labelled in every lesson's sources.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later;
the books' own shape, walked in order):

- *Yemen's own order:* "First, the homeland's story: Yemen,
  graveyard of invaders, its revolutions, its unity and its
  steadfastness — and its symbols, the flag, the anthem and
  the eagle. Then the map: governorates and directorates,
  and the services you meet. Then the republic's three
  powers, with shura beside democracy — and four Yemeni
  lives to imitate. Then the turn inward: how a person is
  raised, and the values he is raised by — honesty,
  patience, sincerity in work, cooperation, order. Then the
  family at work, in village and city, needing no imports —
  and the full place of people with special needs inside
  society. Then the citizen named: identity, independence,
  defence — and betrayal as its opposite; his rights family
  by family, answered by his responsibilities. Then the
  world he guards: a healthy environment, everyday safety,
  civil defence. And last, his own life: his time, his
  summer, and the media that reach him. The teacher guide's
  background essays stand at the end, labelled as the
  teacher's evidence they are."

## Honesty notes (course level)

- **What the filings do and do not contain.** The filings
  contain 4 distinct read books (three student books — the
  Grade 5, Grade 7 and Grade 8 National Education books, in
  Arabic — and one combined Grades 7–9 teacher guide, in
  Arabic), 58 placements, the books' own contents maps,
  and two UNREAD-pending records (Books 2 and 5). The
  filings do not contain any Yemen book for Grades 1–4, any
  secondary-stage book, any programme, or any book in any
  language other than Arabic. Nothing of that kind is in
  this course, and no lesson pretends to one. The ladder
  therefore begins with the Grade 5 book's opening; there
  is no earlier rung in hand to build from.

  of the five student-book records was marked pushed but
