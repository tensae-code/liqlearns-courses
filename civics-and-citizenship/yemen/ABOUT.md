# About the Civics and Citizenship — Yemen course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own National Education books. The one exception is the
**Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Yemen inside the Civics and
Citizenship folder (one course per country). Inside it, the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place
is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the
ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced
gate. The school grades the books come from appear only in
each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

Yemen's own way, preserved whole, as filed: national
education opens with resistance — Yemen as the graveyard of
invaders and its revolutions — before the homeland's
symbols, its administration and its three powers, closing
the Grade 5 book on four Yemeni lives held up as models.
The sixth-grade book builds the citizen from his two first
belongings — the family that forms him (by marriage, with
its documents, its functions and its rights and duties) and
the tribe that stands behind him (its urf, its flags and its
peacemaking) — then hands him his constitution, and closes
on Yemeni lives again: an imam who made peace, a president
who built, a fighter for dialogue, and a rebel poet. The
seventh-grade book turns from the state to the person —
how a child is raised, the values he is raised by, the
family that produces instead of importing, and the place of
people with disabilities inside society. The eighth-grade
book names the citizen — first who he is (identity,
independence, defence, betrayal), then what is his (rights,
one family at a time) and what he owes (responsibilities),
then the safety of the world around him, closing on his
time, his youth and the media that reach him. The
ninth-grade book faces the young citizen himself — his
youth and the habits that can ruin his mind and body, the
social phenomena pressing on his family (children at work,
the father abroad, dowries swollen past bearing) — then
lifts his eyes to the whole Arab-Islamic homeland and its
integration, and closes where the citizen acts: his
participation, his manners in public, his guard over
property, and his refusal of bribery, cheating and monopoly.
And one guide carries three grades at once, its last chapter
a small encyclopaedia for the teacher — the state trusts the
teacher with the substance (the family, children's rights in
Yemen, the sanctity of public money, civil-society
institutions) and lets the pupil's book stay thin. Unit
assessments (تقويم الوحدة) are named in the book entries
below and excluded, as filed; constitutional content is
named by topic and summarised, never transcribed.

The course as built has **twenty-one stocked levels**, from
the filings for this build (`yemen-placements.md` — 5
records / 5 distinct books; 78 placements from five read
student books — and `yemen-placements-b.md` — the combined
Grades 7–9 teacher guide, not in the census, 8 placements,
TEACHER evidence; 86 placements in all, stocking 86 lessons
— the coined lesson names were deduped before building, no
two placements shared a lesson, and every placement stocks
into exactly one lesson):

1. **Level 1 — National Symbols and Revolutions
   (5 lessons).** Yemen, graveyard of invaders; the Yemeni
   revolutions; Yemeni unity; Yemeni steadfastness; the
   symbols of the homeland.
2. **Level 2 — How My Homeland Is Administered
   (3 lessons).** The administrative division, the
   governorate and the directorate, and facilities and
   services.
3. **Level 3 — Powers of the State (4 lessons).** The
   legislative, executive and judicial powers, and shura
   beside democracy.
4. **Level 4 — Yemeni Figures (4 lessons).** Imam al-Qasim
   ibn Muhammad, Rajih Ghalib Labouza, President Ibrahim
   al-Hamdi, and the poet Abdullah al-Baradouni.
5. **Level 5 — The Yemeni Family (4 lessons).** The family
   and its formation, functions of the family, the family's
   roles in upbringing, and family rights and duties.
6. **Level 6 — The Yemeni Tribe (3 lessons).** The tribe as
   a social component, the tribe's customs, habits and
   traditions, and the tribe and social peace.
7. **Level 7 — The Yemeni Constitution (3 lessons).**
   Foundations of the Yemeni state in the constitution, the
   citizen's rights and duties in the constitution, and
   qualities of the good citizen.
8. **Level 8 — Yemeni Personalities and Figures
   (4 lessons).** Imam al-Hadi ila al-Haqq, the martyr
   President Salih Ali al-Sammad, the martyr fighter Jar
   Allah Omar, and the poet al-Hasan ibn Ali al-Habal.
9. **Level 9 — Family and Social Upbringing (4 lessons).**
   Social upbringing, its methods, Islam and social
   upbringing, and the family and social morals.
10. **Level 10 — Values (6 lessons).** What values are,
    honesty and trustworthiness, patience and self-control,
    sincerity and mastery in work, cooperation and social
    solidarity, and order and responsibility.
11. **Level 11 — Productive Families and Self-Sufficiency
    (4 lessons).** Productive families, in the countryside
    and in the city, and self-sufficiency.
12. **Level 12 — People with Special Needs (4 lessons).**
    Who they are, what they need, the institutions for them,
    and inclusion in society.
13. **Level 13 — National Identity (4 lessons).** National
    identity, independence and sovereignty, defending the
    homeland, and betraying the homeland.
14. **Level 14 — Rights and Responsibilities (5 lessons).**
    Rights and responsibilities, personal rights, political
    and cultural rights, economic and social rights, and
    the citizen's responsibilities.
15. **Level 15 — Environment and Public Safety
    (4 lessons).** Environmental health, security and
    public safety, civil defence, and Islam and the
    environment.
16. **Level 16 — Social Issues (3 lessons).** Organising
    and investing time, summer centres and youth, and the
    media.
17. **Level 17 — Youth and Unhealthy Habits (3 lessons).**
    The youth stage, the human mind and body, and unhealthy
    habits and how to treat them.
18. **Level 18 — Social Phenomena and the Yemeni Family
    (3 lessons).** Child labour, the family head's working
    abroad, and exaggeration in dowries.
19. **Level 19 — Arab-Islamic Integration (4 lessons).**
    Arab-Islamic integration, its economic, cultural and
    educational, and political and military fields.
20. **Level 20 — Community Participation (4 lessons).**
    The individual's participation in community life,
    public manners, preserving public and private property,
    and fighting bribery, cheating and monopoly.
21. **Level 21 — Teacher-Guide Background — Evidence Only
    (8 lessons).** The combined Grades 7–9 teacher guide's
    background essays: the family; childhood and children's
    rights in Yemen; values, honesty, patience and
    solidarity; Yemeni emigration; the sanctity of public
    money; global media; civil-society institutions; and
    cultural rights and authority. Evidence only —
    acceptance 0, labelled in every lesson's sources.
