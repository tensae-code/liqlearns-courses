# The Human Mind and Body

## Do it first

Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: The Human Mind and Body. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Yemen's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Sub lesson 1 — The mind, God's gift

The blessing that singled man out and made him answerable, telling right from wrong and useful from harmful — by it he knows his life's purpose, builds the earth, and makes all his progress; Islam guarding mind and body among its five necessities.

## Sub lesson 2 — The dangers to mind and body

Alcohol, which takes the mind and withers the body; drugs, which sicken and can kill; smoking, which harms the breathing system; and poison gases and volatile solvents, among the further harms named.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The mind — God's blessing that singled man out from the other creatures and made him answerable, for the address of obligation came to the sane, who tell right from wrong and useful from harmful through the mind's work of thinking — and its importance: by it man knows his life and his existence's purpose, builds and improves the earth, tells truth from falsehood, and is the cause of all the progress man has made; and Islam's guarding of mind and body among the five necessities the Sharia came to preserve — religion, life, mind, progeny and property — with the prohibition of casting oneself into destruction; and the chief dangers to man's mind and body — alcohol, forbidden by a clear text, which takes the mind, withers the body and leads to deaths and crimes; drugs, forbidden, harmful and poisonous, which sicken the body, destroy some of the body's and mind's functions and can kill; smoking, which harms the breathing system and clouds the mind, harming the smoker and those around him; and poison gases and volatile solvents, which destroy the breathing passages, among the further harms the lesson names, spoiled food among them. Give one real example from your own life or from the Yemen you live in, and name the lesson's key words: the, human, mind. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
