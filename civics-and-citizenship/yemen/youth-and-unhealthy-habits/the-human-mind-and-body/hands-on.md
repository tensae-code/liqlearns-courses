# Hands-on — The Human Mind and Body

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: The Human Mind and Body. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Yemen's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The mind — God's blessing that singled man out from the other creatures and made him answerable, for the address of obligation came to the sane, who tell right from wrong and useful from harmful through the mind's work of thinking — and its importance: by it man knows his life and his existence's purpose, builds and improves the earth, tells truth from falsehood, and is the cause of all the progress man has made; and Islam's guarding of mind and body among the five necessities the Sharia came to preserve — religion, life, mind, progeny and property — with the prohibition of casting oneself into destruction; and the chief dangers to man's mind and body — alcohol, forbidden by a clear text, which takes the mind, withers the body and leads to deaths and crimes; drugs, forbidden, harmful and poisonous, which sicken the body, destroy some of the body's and mind's functions and can kill; smoking, which harms the breathing system and clouds the mind, harming the smoker and those around him; and poison gases and volatile solvents, which destroy the breathing passages, among the further harms the lesson names, spoiled food among them. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
