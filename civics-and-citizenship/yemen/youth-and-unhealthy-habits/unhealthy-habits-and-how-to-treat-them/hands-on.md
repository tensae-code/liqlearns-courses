# Hands-on — Unhealthy Habits and How to Treat Them

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: Unhealthy Habits and How to Treat Them. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Yemen's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The unhealthy habits and their harms — smoking's severe harms and waste of money (heart disease, blocked arteries, clots and heart attacks; gum disease, mouth disease, foul breath and yellowed, ruined teeth; the shared shisha and mada'a passing infectious disease from mouth to mouth; deformities of the unborn when the smoker is pregnant; second-hand smoke harming whoever sits beside the smoker; early wrinkles; nicotine addiction and the difficulty of leaving it; raised chances of the cancers of the breathing system, lungs and throat; and breathing problems), the harms of shamma which the lesson counts, and qat chewing's harms (the family squeezed by the part of its income spent on qat, hours wasted, family break-up and rising divorce from the chewer's constant absence from family and children, children lost and neglected, and bodily harms — mouth cancer, lost appetite, indigestion, sleeplessness, raised blood pressure and inflammation of mouth and stomach — with groundwater drained and pesticides sprayed at random, and poverty spread); and the ways of treating the unhealthy habits — holding to God's commands and avoiding His prohibitions, which guard man's health, health education and awareness, and the media's role in making the harms known. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
