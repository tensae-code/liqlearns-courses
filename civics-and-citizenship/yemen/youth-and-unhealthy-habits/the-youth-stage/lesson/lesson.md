# The Youth Stage

## Do it first

Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: The Youth Stage. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Yemen's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Sub lesson 1 — What youth is, and why it matters

The stage where the person gains the growth and strength everything after rests on; and youth as every nation's pillar of renaissance, builders of its future, the element of every transformation and the core of development.

## Sub lesson 2 — The marks of youth

Giving that does not run dry; ambition with goals set clearly by a mature mind; and bearing the responsibilities childhood could not carry.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: What the youth stage is — the stage of man's life in which he gains the growth, strength and readiness everything after rests on, set against old age's weakness and interruption; why youth matter — the youth of every nation are the foundation of its renaissance, its arteries and the builders of its awaited future, the workers for their religion, nation and country, the basic element in every political, economic and social transformation, the tool of liberation from occupation and colonisation — the book's Yemeni example is youth in the front of the defence of Yemen — and the core of development, when they hold to their religion, their Islamic morals and their faith identity drawn from God's book and His Prophet's sunna; and the marks of youth — giving, a spring that does not run dry because its energy is stored and renewing; ambition and setting goals, the mature youth able to set his aims clearly and tell the reachable from the unreachable, unlike the child's dreams; and bearing responsibility, the burdens of life and its duties taken up with the strength of the age. Give one real example from your own life or from the Yemen you live in, and name the lesson's key words: the, youth, stage. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
