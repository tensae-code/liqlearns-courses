# Hands-on — The Media

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: The Media. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Yemen's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The media — satellite channels, the internet, radio and press — and their power to inform and educate; their good side (news, learning, awareness, skills) and their dangers as the book presents them (habits and values against the nation's identity, dulled creativity, isolation, violence, wasted time, a consumer culture carried to the developing world); and protection — holding to one's faith and values, and filling time with reading, languages, sport and family. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
