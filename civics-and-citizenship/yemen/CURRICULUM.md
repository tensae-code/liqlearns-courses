# Civics and Citizenship — Yemen course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Yemen inside the Civics and Citizenship
folder, organised by **levels, not grades**: a learner climbs
when they have proved the level, not when a school calendar
says so. The school grades the books come from appear only in
each lesson's sources (provenance), never as the course's
organisation. The path follows Yemen's own order as its books
lay it out — the Grade 5 book first: the homeland's story of
resistance, its revolutions, unity and symbols, then its
administration and its three powers, closing on four Yemeni
lives; then the Grade 7 book, which turns from the state to
the person — upbringing, values, the productive family, and
people with special needs; then the Grade 8 book, which names
the citizen — identity, rights family by family answered by
responsibilities, the safety of the world around him, and his
own time, youth and media. The combined Grades 7–9 teacher
guide's background essays stand whole at the end as a
labelled evidence-only level, in that filing's own order. The
filings for this build (`yemen-placements.md` — 5 records /
5 distinct books, 50 placements from three read student
books, with Grades 6 and 9 UNREAD-pending — and
`yemen-placements-b.md` — the combined Grades 7–9 teacher
guide, 8 placements, TEACHER evidence) stock 58 lessons in
all: every placement names its own coined lesson, the coined
names were deduped before building (no two placements shared
a lesson), and every placement stocks into exactly one
lesson.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own National Education books — except the **Tip boxes**: a
tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips
are ours; the lessons are the books'. See ABOUT.md for the
full book list and the acceptance reckoning. **Fifty lessons
stand on student books; eight stand on teacher-guide
evidence only and count 0 in the acceptance reckoning, as
ABOUT.md states.** The Grade 6 and Grade 9 student books are
UNREAD-pending under the 2026-11-17 disk hold — not failed —
so the ladder honestly carries the Grade 5, 7 and 8 books in
hand, and their units will be slotted in when they are read,
which may renumber the middle levels, exactly as the first
filing's ladder sketch warns.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **National Symbols and Revolutions** — the course opens where the Grade 5 book opens: the homeland's story of resistance, its revolutions, its unity and its symbols (flag, anthem, emblem). 5 lessons, in one title: `national-symbols-and-revolutions/` (5 lessons).
2. **How My Homeland Is Administered** — from the story to the map: governorates and directorates, and the services the citizen meets. 3 lessons, in one title: `how-my-homeland-is-administered/` (3 lessons).
3. **Powers of the State** — the three powers of the republic and the citizen's first electoral rights and duties, with shura beside democracy. 4 lessons, in one title: `powers-of-the-state/` (4 lessons).
4. **Yemeni Figures** — the Grade 5 book closes on four lives to imitate, from the imam who rose against the Ottomans to the blind national poet. 4 lessons, in one title: `yemeni-figures/` (4 lessons).
5. **Family and Social Upbringing** — Grade 7 turns inward: how a person is raised, by family, school and companions, and the family's moral work. 4 lessons, in one title: `family-and-social-upbringing/` (4 lessons).
6. **Values** — the virtues that upbringing plants: honesty, patience, sincerity in work, cooperation, order and responsibility. 6 lessons, in one title: `values/` (6 lessons).
7. **Productive Families and Self-Sufficiency** — the family at work, in village and city, and the homeland that needs no imports. 4 lessons, in one title: `productive-families-and-self-sufficiency/` (4 lessons).
8. **People with Special Needs** — who people with special needs are, what they need, the institutions that serve them, and their full place in society. 4 lessons, in one title: `people-with-special-needs/` (4 lessons).
9. **National Identity** — Grade 8 names the bond itself: identity, independence and sovereignty, defence, and betrayal as its opposite. 4 lessons, in one title: `national-identity/` (4 lessons).
10. **Rights and Responsibilities** — the citizen's rights family by family, answered by his responsibilities in the same four fields. 5 lessons, in one title: `rights-and-responsibilities/` (5 lessons).
11. **Environment and Public Safety** — the citizen as guardian of his surroundings: environmental health, everyday safety, civil defence, and Islam's cleanliness rules. 4 lessons, in one title: `environment-and-public-safety/` (4 lessons).
12. **Social Issues** — the course's student-book ladder closes on the young citizen's own life: his time, his summer, and the media that reach him. 3 lessons, in one title: `social-issues/` (3 lessons).
13. **Teacher-Guide Background — Evidence Only** — the combined Grades 7–9 teacher guide's background essays, kept whole and labelled as evidence only — home and values first, then rights and public trusts, then the institutions a citizen acts through, in the guide filing's own order. 8 lessons, in 6 titles: `family-and-society/` (1 lesson), `rights-and-duties/` (2 lessons), `character-and-values/` (1 lesson), `homeland-and-world/` (2 lessons), `law-and-public-life/` (1 lesson), `taking-part/` (1 lesson).
