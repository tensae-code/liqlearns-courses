# Hands-on — Fighting Bribery, Cheating and Monopoly

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: Fighting Bribery, Cheating and Monopoly. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Yemen's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Bribery — a person's giving a sum of money or gifts of any kind to an employee anywhere, a judge or a person responsible for people's interests, in order to obtain something he does not deserve or to harm others — a betrayal of trust, forbidden by Islam and punished by Yemeni law, fought by abstaining from paying it and reporting whoever requests it, for the Prophet cursed the briber and the bribed; cheating — dealings between people stand on honesty and trustworthiness, not lying, cheating and betrayal, and the Yemeni penalties law the book cites punishes whoever cheats or attempts it with imprisonment up to a year or a fine — and cheating in examinations as the most dangerous kind of cheating and the most destructive to generations, the cause producing the other kinds: it lessens care for learning, wastes the future of the persons who rely on it, squanders society's competences, creates false success, destroys the values of diligence and perseverance, and produces superficial generations easily filled with destructive ideas; and monopoly, named with them throughout; and the means of fighting bribery, cheating and monopoly — abstaining from dealing with those who practise them, reporting to the competent bodies the individuals proven to cheat, monopolise or take bribes, awakening our colleagues, friends and families not to fall prey to their practitioners, and awakening the practitioners themselves to their deeds' harms to themselves, their families and their society; and the fight's benefits — wealth not eaten wrongfully, care for the generations' future against loss, goods and services provided at fair prices, individuals performing their duties and others' rights not lost, and the life and health of society's members preserved. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
