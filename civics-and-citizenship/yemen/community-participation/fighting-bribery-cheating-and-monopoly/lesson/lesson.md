# Fighting Bribery, Cheating and Monopoly

## Do it first

Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: Fighting Bribery, Cheating and Monopoly. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Yemen's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Sub lesson 1 — Bribery, cheating and monopoly — what they are

Bribery: money or gifts to an official for what one does not deserve, a betrayal of trust forbidden by Islam and punished by law; cheating, and cheating in examinations as its most dangerous kind; and monopoly.

## Sub lesson 2 — How they are fought, and what the fight wins

Refusing to pay or deal with their practitioners, reporting them to the competent bodies, and awakening colleagues, families and the practitioners themselves — winning wealth not eaten wrongfully, the generations' future kept, fair prices, and rights not lost.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Bribery — a person's giving a sum of money or gifts of any kind to an employee anywhere, a judge or a person responsible for people's interests, in order to obtain something he does not deserve or to harm others — a betrayal of trust, forbidden by Islam and punished by Yemeni law, fought by abstaining from paying it and reporting whoever requests it, for the Prophet cursed the briber and the bribed; cheating — dealings between people stand on honesty and trustworthiness, not lying, cheating and betrayal, and the Yemeni penalties law the book cites punishes whoever cheats or attempts it with imprisonment up to a year or a fine — and cheating in examinations as the most dangerous kind of cheating and the most destructive to generations, the cause producing the other kinds: it lessens care for learning, wastes the future of the persons who rely on it, squanders society's competences, creates false success, destroys the values of diligence and perseverance, and produces superficial generations easily filled with destructive ideas; and monopoly, named with them throughout; and the means of fighting bribery, cheating and monopoly — abstaining from dealing with those who practise them, reporting to the competent bodies the individuals proven to cheat, monopolise or take bribes, awakening our colleagues, friends and families not to fall prey to their practitioners, and awakening the practitioners themselves to their deeds' harms to themselves, their families and their society; and the fight's benefits — wealth not eaten wrongfully, care for the generations' future against loss, goods and services provided at fair prices, individuals performing their duties and others' rights not lost, and the life and health of society's members preserved. Give one real example from your own life or from the Yemen you live in, and name the lesson's key words: fighting, bribery,, cheating. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
