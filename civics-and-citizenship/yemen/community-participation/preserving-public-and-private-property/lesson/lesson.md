# Preserving Public and Private Property

## Do it first

Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: Preserving Public and Private Property. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Yemen's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Sub lesson 1 — Public and private property — what each is

Public property: the buildings, institutions, companies and factories the state builds for all — schools, mosques, hospitals, parks, roads, ministries and antiquities; private property: what a person or group owns, guaranteed by the Sharia and the laws.

## Sub lesson 2 — The duties toward public property, and why guarding both matters

Keeping it clean, not tampering with its contents, reporting attackers, no random speed bumps, and guarding the antiquities — so it serves longer, spares the state rebuilding costs, and grows public taste and the national sense; private property guarded too, for it grows the economy and employs hands.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Public property — the buildings, installations, institutions, companies and factories the state builds to serve society's interests, owned by all society's members, who have the right to benefit from them according to law — and private property, the buildings, shops, institutions, companies, factories and other things an individual or group of society's sons owns; the kinds of public property — schools, mosques, hospitals, parks, stadiums, government factories, police departments, roads, ministries and government offices and antiquities, and all that follows them of cars, machines and equipment — the government school one studies in being one's own and every society member's; the duties of dealing with public property — keeping its cleanliness since it is owned by all, keeping its contents and not tampering with or wrecking them or trifling with its tools (windows, doors and blackboards among them), reporting whoever attacks it so he is punished, never wrecking the asphalt roads with random speed bumps without the traffic authority's directions, and caring for the Yemeni antiquities and protecting the archaeological areas from theft, smuggling and tampering; the importance of preserving public property — benefiting from it the longest possible time, lightening the state's building and restoration expenses, reflecting society's awareness and sense of responsibility, and growing public taste and the national sense in persons; and preserving private property — a right the Islamic Sharia and the laws guarantee to every individual, markets, restaurants, clubs, companies and factories among it serving society, growing the national economy and employing working hands. Give one real example from your own life or from the Yemen you live in, and name the lesson's key words: preserving, public, and. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
