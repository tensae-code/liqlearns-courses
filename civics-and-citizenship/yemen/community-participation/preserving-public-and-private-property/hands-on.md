# Hands-on — Preserving Public and Private Property

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: Preserving Public and Private Property. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Yemen's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Public property — the buildings, installations, institutions, companies and factories the state builds to serve society's interests, owned by all society's members, who have the right to benefit from them according to law — and private property, the buildings, shops, institutions, companies, factories and other things an individual or group of society's sons owns; the kinds of public property — schools, mosques, hospitals, parks, stadiums, government factories, police departments, roads, ministries and government offices and antiquities, and all that follows them of cars, machines and equipment — the government school one studies in being one's own and every society member's; the duties of dealing with public property — keeping its cleanliness since it is owned by all, keeping its contents and not tampering with or wrecking them or trifling with its tools (windows, doors and blackboards among them), reporting whoever attacks it so he is punished, never wrecking the asphalt roads with random speed bumps without the traffic authority's directions, and caring for the Yemeni antiquities and protecting the archaeological areas from theft, smuggling and tampering; the importance of preserving public property — benefiting from it the longest possible time, lightening the state's building and restoration expenses, reflecting society's awareness and sense of responsibility, and growing public taste and the national sense in persons; and preserving private property — a right the Islamic Sharia and the laws guarantee to every individual, markets, restaurants, clubs, companies and factories among it serving society, growing the national economy and employing working hands. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
