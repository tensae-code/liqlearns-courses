# Lesson plan — Public Manners

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Yemen · **Title:** Community Participation · **Lesson:** Public Manners
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 20 — Community Participation (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Public manners — the behaviours we practise in our dealing with others and in public places, which the Qur'an urges in all our behaviour, directing deliberateness in walking on foot or driving a car, as a mark of fine character, and lowering the voice when speaking with others; their importance; the public manners in dealing with others — respecting others, not forcing our opinions on them or offending their convictions, speaking well with them and showing them the truth in what we differ on; respecting the elderly, speaking and dealing with them politely and never mocking them; respecting constructive dialogue, which rests on persuasion by proof and logic; respecting the social order — standing where the queue ends and never breaking past others; respecting people with special needs, helping the disabled and those with impairments and considering their feelings; not intervening in others' privacies; and not setting wedding camps in the streets, closing the road, running loudspeakers in wedding camps or using live rounds that disturb others and may injure them bodily; and the manners of dealing with the laws — respecting the state's constitution in harmony with the Qur'an, obeying the laws and directions of the security bodies concerning society's security and safety, obeying the health bodies' laws and keeping away from wrong practices, and likewise the other bodies' directions in organising life and the relations between society's members. This lesson stands on a student book.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 20 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What public manners are** — The behaviours we practise with others and in public places, urged by the Qur'an — deliberateness in walking and driving, and lowering the voice in speech among them.
2. **Manners toward others, and toward the laws** — Respecting others, the elderly, constructive dialogue, the queue and people with special needs; no prying, no blocked streets or celebratory gunfire; and obeying the security, health and other bodies' laws that organise life.

Gate pool: at most 5 questions for the prove-it check, mixed with earlier parts, picked as the hardest from the rank right under the wanted sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
