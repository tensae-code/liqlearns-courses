# Civics and Citizenship — Yemen course — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

Civics belongs to the country: this course is Yemen's own —
built from Yemen's National Education books for Grades 5, 6,
7, 8 and 9 of Basic Education and the combined Grades 7–9
teacher guide (Ministry of Education, Republic of Yemen, in
Arabic). Inside the course the organisation is by **levels,
not grades**: the titles below ARE the levels' titles, in
path order — what the learner proves, step by step. The
grades the books come from live only in each lesson's
sources.

The path's own logic is Yemen's, from the filings' ladder
sketches reconciled into one ladder. The first filing's
sketch gives the student-book levels in the books' own grade
order (Grade 5, then 6, then 7, then 8, then 9): national
education opens with resistance — Yemen as the graveyard of
invaders and its revolutions — before the homeland's symbols,
its administration and its three powers, closing the Grade 5
book on four Yemeni lives held up as models; the Grade 6 book
builds the citizen from his two first belongings — the family
that forms him and the tribe that stands behind him — then
hands him his constitution, and closes on four more Yemeni
lives; the Grade 7 book turns from the state to the person —
how a child is raised, the values he is raised by, the family
that produces instead of importing, and the place of people
with disabilities inside society; the Grade 8 book names the
citizen — first who he is, then what is his and what he owes,
then the safety of the world around him, closing on his time,
his youth and the media that reach him; the Grade 9 book
faces the young citizen himself — his youth and the habits
that can ruin it, the social phenomena pressing on his
family, the whole Arab-Islamic homeland's integration, and
his own part in community life. The second filing sketched
its guide at its own "L2" — the Grades 7–9 band — with its
background essays widening in the books' order: home and
values first, then rights and public trusts, then the
institutions a citizen acts through. The build reconciles
the two sketches by keeping the guide whole as the closing
Level 21, labelled teacher-guide evidence only, so no
student-book level is displaced and no guide lesson is mixed
into the student-book reckoning. That ladder IS the path
order (recommended, never forced — a learner can jump in
higher; the "start here instead" hints point down this
ladder). The Grade 6 and Grade 9 books were UNREAD-pending
in the first build; in the 2026-11-22 unread-queue phase
they were read and their units slotted in in grade order,
renumbering the middle levels exactly as the first filing's
sketch warned (Grade 7's units moved from Levels 5–8 to
9–12, Grade 8's from 9–12 to 13–16, and the guide from
Level 13 to Level 21).

## The titles

**Level 1 — national symbols and revolutions** (5 lessons):

- `national-symbols-and-revolutions/` — Level 1. Yemen, Graveyard of Invaders · The Yemeni Revolutions · Yemeni Unity · Yemeni Steadfastness · Symbols of My Homeland. The course opens where the grade 5 book opens: the homeland's story of resistance, its revolutions, its unity and its symbols (flag, anthem, emblem) — this title sits at Level 1 because that is where Yemen's own books teach it.

**Level 2 — how my homeland is administered** (3 lessons):

- `how-my-homeland-is-administered/` — Level 2. The Administrative Division · The Governorate and the Directorate · Facilities and Services. From the story to the map: governorates and directorates, and the services the citizen meets — this title sits at Level 2 because that is where Yemen's own books teach it.

**Level 3 — powers of the state** (4 lessons):

- `powers-of-the-state/` — Level 3. The Legislative Power · The Executive Power · The Judicial Power · Shura and Democracy. The three powers of the republic and the citizen's first electoral rights and duties, with shura beside democracy — this title sits at Level 3 because that is where Yemen's own books teach it.

**Level 4 — yemeni figures** (4 lessons):

- `yemeni-figures/` — Level 4. Imam al-Qasim ibn Muhammad · Rajih Ghalib Labouza · President Ibrahim al-Hamdi · The Poet Abdullah al-Baradouni. The grade 5 book closes on four lives to imitate, from the imam who rose against the ottomans to the blind national poet — this title sits at Level 4 because that is where Yemen's own books teach it.

**Level 5 — the yemeni family** (4 lessons):

- `the-yemeni-family/` — Level 5. The Family and Its Formation · Functions of the Family · The Family's Roles in Upbringing · Family Rights and Duties. Grade 6 begins with the family: how it is formed by marriage, its functions, its roles in raising children, and the rights and duties inside it — this title sits at Level 5 because that is where Yemen's own books teach it.

**Level 6 — the yemeni tribe** (3 lessons):

- `the-yemeni-tribe/` — Level 6. The Tribe as a Social Component · The Tribe's Customs, Its Habits and Traditions · The Tribe and Social Peace. The second belonging: the tribe as a social component, its urf, habits and traditions, and its peacemaking — this title sits at Level 6 because that is where Yemen's own books teach it.

**Level 7 — the yemeni constitution** (3 lessons):

- `the-yemeni-constitution/` — Level 7. Foundations of the Yemeni State in the Yemeni Constitution · The Citizen's Rights and Duties in the Constitution · Qualities of the Good Citizen. The citizen handed his constitution: what it is, the state's foundations in it, his rights and duties in it, and the good citizen's qualities — this title sits at Level 7 because that is where Yemen's own books teach it.

**Level 8 — yemeni personalities and figures** (4 lessons):

- `yemeni-personalities-and-figures/` — Level 8. Imam al-Hadi ila al-Haqq (Yahya ibn al-Husayn) · The Martyr President Salih Ali al-Sammad · The Martyr Fighter Jar Allah Omar · The Poet (al-Hasan ibn Ali ibn Jaber al-Habal). The grade 6 book closes on four more lives: the imam who made peace among the tribes, the president who built, the fighter for dialogue, and the rebel poet — this title sits at Level 8 because that is where Yemen's own books teach it.

**Level 9 — family and social upbringing** (4 lessons):

- `family-and-social-upbringing/` — Level 9. Social Upbringing · Methods of Social Upbringing · Islam and Social Upbringing · The Family and Social Morals. Grade 7 turns inward: how a person is raised, by family, school and companions, and the family's moral work — this title sits at Level 9 because that is where Yemen's own books teach it.

**Level 10 — values** (6 lessons):

- `values/` — Level 10. What Values Are · Honesty and Trustworthiness · Patience and Self-Control · Sincerity and Mastery in Work · Cooperation and Social Solidarity · Order and Responsibility. The virtues that upbringing plants: honesty, patience, sincerity in work, cooperation, order and responsibility — this title sits at Level 10 because that is where Yemen's own books teach it.

**Level 11 — productive families and self-sufficiency** (4 lessons):

- `productive-families-and-self-sufficiency/` — Level 11. Productive Families · The Productive Family in the Countryside · The Productive Family in the City · Self-Sufficiency. The family at work, in village and city, and the homeland that needs no imports — this title sits at Level 11 because that is where Yemen's own books teach it.

**Level 12 — people with special needs** (4 lessons):

- `people-with-special-needs/` — Level 12. People with Special Needs · What People with Disabilities Need · Institutions for People with Disabilities · Inclusion in Society. Who people with special needs are, what they need, the institutions that serve them, and their full place in society — this title sits at Level 12 because that is where Yemen's own books teach it.

**Level 13 — national identity** (4 lessons):

- `national-identity/` — Level 13. National Identity · Independence and Sovereignty · Defending the Homeland · Betraying the Homeland. Grade 8 names the bond itself: identity, independence and sovereignty, defence, and betrayal as its opposite — this title sits at Level 13 because that is where Yemen's own books teach it.

**Level 14 — rights and responsibilities** (5 lessons):

- `rights-and-responsibilities/` — Level 14. Rights and Responsibilities · Personal Rights · Political and Cultural Rights · Economic and Social Rights · The Citizen's Responsibilities. The citizen's rights family by family, answered by his responsibilities in the same four fields — this title sits at Level 14 because that is where Yemen's own books teach it.

**Level 15 — environment and public safety** (4 lessons):

- `environment-and-public-safety/` — Level 15. Environmental Health · Security and Public Safety · Civil Defence · Islam and the Environment. The citizen as guardian of his surroundings: environmental health, everyday safety, civil defence, and islam's cleanliness rules — this title sits at Level 15 because that is where Yemen's own books teach it.

**Level 16 — social issues** (3 lessons):

**Level 17 — youth and unhealthy habits** (3 lessons):

- `youth-and-unhealthy-habits/` — Level 17. The Youth Stage · The Human Mind and Body · Unhealthy Habits and How to Treat Them. **Level 19 — arab-islamic integration** (4 lessons):

- `arab-islamic-integration/` — Level 19. Arab-Islamic Integration · Arab-Islamic Economic Integration · Arab-Islamic Cultural and Educational Integration · Arab-Islamic Political and Military Integration. The eyes lifted to the whole Arab-Islamic homeland: what integration is and its factors, then its economic, cultural-educational and political-military fields — this title sits at Level 19 because that is where Yemen's own books teach it.

**Level 20 — community participation** (4 lessons):

The course's student-book ladder closes where the citizen acts: taking part in community life, public manners, preserving public and private property, and fighting bribery, cheating and monopoly — this title sits at Level 20 because that is where Yemen's own books teach it.
