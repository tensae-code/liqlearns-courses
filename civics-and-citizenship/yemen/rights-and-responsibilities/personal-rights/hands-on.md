# Hands-on — Personal Rights

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: Personal Rights. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Yemen's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The personal rights and why they give the citizen a secure life: the right to security over oneself and one's property, the inviolability of the home — no entry or search except by a court's order — the right to freedom, no arrest without lawful cause, and the right to litigation, with its courtroom guarantees: equality of the parties, the accused innocent until proven guilty, and punishment personal to the offender, in summary. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
