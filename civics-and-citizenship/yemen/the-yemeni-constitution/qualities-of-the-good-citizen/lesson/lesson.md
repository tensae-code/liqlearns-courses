# Qualities of the Good Citizen

## Do it first

Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: Qualities of the Good Citizen. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Yemen's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Sub lesson 1 — Love of the homeland, and who the good citizen is

Belonging made deed, not slogan — every worker, from teacher to pupil, doing his work sincerely; and the good citizen defined: enjoying the constitution's rights, performing his duties, keeping the laws and ready to defend his homeland anywhere.

## Sub lesson 2 — Guarding public property, and constructive criticism

The installations and funds owned by all — schools, hospitals, parks, roads, mosques — used in a civilised way and defended from tampering; and criticism that alerts the responsible bodies to violations, by press, letters, complaints and social media, within the bounds of good manners.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Love of the homeland as belonging and pride in its history and civilisation made deed, not slogans — the teacher teaching sincerely, the soldier guarding, the official serving, the doctor healing, the minister and the president serving the people's interests, the cleaner proud of his work, the mother raising her children and working, the trader and factory owner without cheating, exploitation or monopoly, and the pupil studying sincerely; who the good citizen is — every Yemeni living on the republic's land or outside it, holding its nationality, enjoying the rights the constitution guarantees, performing his duties to the homeland, keeping its laws and ready to defend it in any place; and two of his marks — guarding public property (the installations and funds owned by all the homeland's sons — schools, universities, hospitals, health centres, public parks, roads, mosques, ministries, sports stadiums and police stations — using them in a civilised way, protecting them from tampering and never staying silent at an attack on them) and constructive criticism, the civilised behaviour of alerting the responsible bodies, institutions and persons to violations touching the homeland's and the citizen's interest, by press articles, interviews, letters, complaints and social media, without leaving the bounds of the homeland's and the citizen's interest. Give one real example from your own life or from the Yemen you live in, and name the lesson's key words: qualities, of, the. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
