# Hands-on — The Citizen's Rights and Duties in the Constitution

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: The Citizen's Rights and Duties in the Constitution. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Yemen's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The rights the constitution guarantees the citizen — personal rights and freedoms (personal freedom and dignity guarded, no arrest, search, detention, surveillance or restriction of freedom except under law and a competent court's ruling; movement and residence anywhere in the republic; the home's inviolability, unwatched and unsearched except as the law states; and the secrecy of postal, electronic and telephone communications, never watched, delayed or seized except in the cases touching the homeland's security that the law defines), cultural, economic and social rights (education a right for every citizen, compulsory and free to Grade 9 at the state's expense; ownership of property, shops, companies and factories, never confiscated or attacked; health care in the state's hospitals and centres and social care — social security for the poor, the unable, martyrs' families and people with special needs; and the rights of women and children, the state fostering motherhood, childhood, youth and women, women being men's partners in rights and duties as the Sharia guarantees and the law states), and political rights (freedom of opinion, founding and joining parties, associations, unions and civil-society organisations without compulsion, standing and voting for the candidate one is convinced by, and assembly for the public benefit with notice to the competent bodies) — and the citizen's duties the constitution fixes (defence of religion and homeland a sacred duty and military service an honour; paying the general taxes according to law; keeping national unity, guarding the state's secrets, respecting the laws and abiding by their rulings) — the articles named by topic and summarised, never transcribed. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
