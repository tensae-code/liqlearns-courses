# Lesson plan — Functions of the Family

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Yemen · **Title:** The Yemeni Family · **Lesson:** Functions of the Family
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 5 — The Yemeni Family (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The family's general functions — social (teaching the children morals, the society's values like honesty, trustworthiness and helping the needy, and its customs), educational (the Arabic language, knowledge, skills and sound attitudes), psychological (love, tenderness and kinship planted in the children, and security), economic (providing food, drink, housing and teaching within the family's means without waste, with members sharing income work like sewing, farming, fishing and trade) and religious (teaching the children their Islam — prayer, fasting, the Qur'an, commanding right and forbidding wrong) — and each member's own function: the father, head of the family, providing and caring with his wife; the mother raising the children, running the house and sharing its decisions; the children honouring their parents and doing the work asked of them — and how cooperation knits the family in affection, love and responsibility. This lesson stands on a student book.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 5 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. ## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The family's general functions** — Social, educational, psychological, economic and religious: teaching morals and customs, the Arabic language and knowledge, love and security, provision within the family's means, and the children's Islam.
2. **Each member's function, and cooperation** — The father providing and caring with his wife; the mother raising the children and running the house; the children honouring their parents and doing the work asked of them — and cooperation knitting the family in affection, love and responsibility.

Gate pool: at most 5 questions for the prove-it check, mixed with earlier parts, picked as the hardest from the rank right under the wanted sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
