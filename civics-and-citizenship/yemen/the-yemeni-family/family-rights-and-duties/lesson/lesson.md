# Family Rights and Duties

## Do it first

Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: Family Rights and Duties. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Yemen's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Sub lesson 1 — The spouses' rights over each other

The wife's maintenance, good treatment, full disposal of her own money and share in the family's work; the husband's obedience short of disobedience to God, and his wife's guarding of herself and his money and his house's door.

## Sub lesson 2 — The children's rights, and the children's duties

Nursing, care, mercy and a righteous upbringing owed to the children; and the children's devotion, prayer, kindness, help, gentle speech and honouring of elders owed to the parents.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The rights and duties inside the family — the wife's rights over her husband (maintenance in food, clothing, housing and medicine within his ability, good treatment, full disposal of her own money, and sharing the family's work within the Sharia), the husband's rights over his wife (obedience short of disobedience to God, guarding herself and his money in his absence, and admitting no one to his house without his leave), the children's rights over their parents (nursing, care and mercy, and a righteous upbringing), and the children's duties toward their parents (devotion and prayer for them, kindness and love, help in spending, service and care, gentle speech, helping the mother with the little ones, and honouring the family's elders) — and that keeping these rights and duties is what steadies family life. Give one real example from your own life or from the Yemen you live in, and name the lesson's key words: family, rights, and. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
