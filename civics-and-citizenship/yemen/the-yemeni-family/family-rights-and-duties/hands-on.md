# Hands-on — Family Rights and Duties

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: Family Rights and Duties. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Yemen's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The rights and duties inside the family — the wife's rights over her husband (maintenance in food, clothing, housing and medicine within his ability, good treatment, full disposal of her own money, and sharing the family's work within the Sharia), the husband's rights over his wife (obedience short of disobedience to God, guarding herself and his money in his absence, and admitting no one to his house without his leave), the children's rights over their parents (nursing, care and mercy, and a righteous upbringing), and the children's duties toward their parents (devotion and prayer for them, kindness and love, help in spending, service and care, gentle speech, helping the mother with the little ones, and honouring the family's elders) — and that keeping these rights and duties is what steadies family life. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
