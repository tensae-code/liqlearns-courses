# Lesson plan — Family Rights and Duties

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Yemen · **Title:** The Yemeni Family · **Lesson:** Family Rights and Duties
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 5 — The Yemeni Family (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The rights and duties inside the family — the wife's rights over her husband (maintenance in food, clothing, housing and medicine within his ability, good treatment, full disposal of her own money, and sharing the family's work within the Sharia), the husband's rights over his wife (obedience short of disobedience to God, guarding herself and his money in his absence, and admitting no one to his house without his leave), the children's rights over their parents (nursing, care and mercy, and a righteous upbringing), and the children's duties toward their parents (devotion and prayer for them, kindness and love, help in spending, service and care, gentle speech, helping the mother with the little ones, and honouring the family's elders) — and that keeping these rights and duties is what steadies family life. This lesson stands on a student book.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 5 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The spouses' rights over each other** — The wife's maintenance, good treatment, full disposal of her own money and share in the family's work; the husband's obedience short of disobedience to God, and his wife's guarding of herself and his money and his house's door.
2. **The children's rights, and the children's duties** — Nursing, care, mercy and a righteous upbringing owed to the children; and the children's devotion, prayer, kindness, help, gentle speech and honouring of elders owed to the parents.

Gate pool: at most 5 questions for the prove-it check, mixed with earlier parts, picked as the hardest from the rank right under the wanted sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
