# Hands-on — The Family and Its Formation

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: The Family and Its Formation. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Yemen's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: What a family is — a social bond of husband, wife and children reaching to grandparents, grandchildren and kin, in its small and its extended Yemeni forms — the steps that form it in Islam (the proposal to her guardian, the marriage contract with offer and acceptance, two witnesses and the mahr, then the household), why marriage matters as the road to lawful children and their upbringing, the family's documents (the personal and family cards, the birth and death certificates, from the Civil Status and Civil Register authority, and the marriage contract from the Ministry of Justice, registered so the family card can issue), and what divorce does to the family. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
