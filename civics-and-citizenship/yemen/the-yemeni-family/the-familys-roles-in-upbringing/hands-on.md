# Hands-on — The Family's Roles in Upbringing

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: The Family's Roles in Upbringing. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Yemen's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: How the family raises its children — the good example, for the children's eyes are on their parents and they imitate deeds more than words; advice by story — the Qur'an's stories, the Prophet's life and household, the companions, the scholars and the righteous; justice among the children in mercy, shown love, treatment and gifts, which wins their devotion and love of one another, where favouring one breeds hatred and severed kinship; the parents' prayer for their children, as Abraham prayed for righteous offspring; and the protective role — guarding the children in the age of the information revolution from intellectual invasion and bad companions (crime, drugs and deviant thought) by awareness, useful alternatives like sport and purposeful programmes, and filling their time with training and learning. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
