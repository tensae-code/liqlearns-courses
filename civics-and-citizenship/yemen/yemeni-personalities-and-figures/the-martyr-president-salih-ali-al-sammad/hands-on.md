# Hands-on — The Martyr President Salih Ali al-Sammad

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: The Martyr President Salih Ali al-Sammad. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Yemen's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The life of President Salih Ali al-Sammad as the book tells it — born 6 July 1979 at Bani Mu'adh, Sahar directorate, Sa'dah governorate, and raised in a modest farming family of the Sahar tribe; his first schooling at Sa'dah, then Sana'a University, where he took his bachelor's in the Qur'an and its sciences, then a teacher at the Abdullah ibn Mas'ud school in Sa'dah who kept seeking sacred learning from Yemen's scholars; his personal qualities — God-consciousness, honesty, piety, forbearance, manliness, asceticism, generosity, chivalry, courage, daring, dignity, tolerance, mercy and humility among them; the high posts he held — adviser to the president from 24 September 2014 after the September 2014 revolution, then chosen president of the Supreme Political Council on 6 August 2016, taking up the presidency after the constitutional oath in the Council of Representatives on 14 August 2016, and forming the National Salvation Government in which all Yemen's sons were represented; his project for building the Yemeni state — 'a hand protects and a hand builds' — joining the battle of protection to the battle of building; and his martyrdom on Thursday 19 April 2018 in an air raid, as the book presents it. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
