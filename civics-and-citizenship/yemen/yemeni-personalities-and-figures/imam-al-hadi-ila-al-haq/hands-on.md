# Hands-on — Imam al-Hadi ila al-Haqq (Yahya ibn al-Husayn)

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: Imam al-Hadi ila al-Haqq (Yahya ibn al-Husayn). Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Yemen's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The life of Imam al-Hadi Yahya ibn al-Husayn ibn al-Qasim — born in 245 AH at al-Rass near Medina and raised in a house of learning, worship and asceticism; his coming to Yemen in 284 AH after delegations of its sheikhs and tribes called him because of the problems and strife between the tribes that they could not settle — he ended their problems, made a peace between them at Khawlan, and received their allegiance to hear, obey and strive under his banner; how he ran his rule — social reform, making peace among the Yemeni tribes and settling security in a short time, a rule of transparency that made the people partners in the responsibility of commanding right and forbidding wrong and closed the gap between ruler and ruled, and economic care for livestock and farming wealth and the zakat that comes from them; his standing in learning and religion, his piety and asceticism — he did not take his personal expenses from the Yemenis' money but from his own money at al-Rass, down to his mount's fodder; his works — the book of rulings in jurisprudence, the book of arts in jurisprudence, and the collection of his books and letters on the principles of religion; and his death on Sunday 20 Dhu al-Hijja 298 AH, buried at Sa'dah beside his mosque. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
