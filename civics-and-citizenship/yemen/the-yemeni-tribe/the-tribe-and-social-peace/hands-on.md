# Hands-on — The Tribe and Social Peace

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: The Tribe and Social Peace. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Yemen's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The tribe as peacemaker — Yemeni society as one of the Arab and Islamic world's great conservative models, where tribal urf still does the largest work of settling the problems between individuals and groups: blood-feuds inside the tribe and outside it, disputes over agricultural and housing land, family problems and social problems; and the Yemeni tribe's marks — it takes the initiative in settling problems, guards the foundations of social peace with great care, answers to protect the homeland's higher interests, lightens the suffering of some families, and closes ranks and stands together against fate's blows and dangers. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
