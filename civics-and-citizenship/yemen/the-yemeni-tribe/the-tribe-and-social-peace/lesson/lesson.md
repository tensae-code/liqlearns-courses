# The Tribe and Social Peace

## Do it first

Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: The Tribe and Social Peace. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Yemen's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Sub lesson 1 — The questions the tribe settles

Blood-feuds inside the tribe and outside it, disputes over agricultural and housing land, family problems and social problems — settled by the tribe's urf.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Yemeni tribe's marks

It moves first to settle problems, guards social peace, answers to protect the homeland's higher interests, lightens some families' suffering, and closes ranks against fate's blows.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The tribe as peacemaker — Yemeni society as one of the Arab and Islamic world's great conservative models, where tribal urf still does the largest work of settling the problems between individuals and groups: blood-feuds inside the tribe and outside it, disputes over agricultural and housing land, family problems and social problems; and the Yemeni tribe's marks — it takes the initiative in settling problems, guards the foundations of social peace with great care, answers to protect the homeland's higher interests, lightens the suffering of some families, and closes ranks and stands together against fate's blows and dangers. Give one real example from your own life or from the Yemen you live in, and name the lesson's key words: the, tribe, and. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
