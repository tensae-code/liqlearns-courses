# Hands-on — The Tribe as a Social Component

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: The Tribe as a Social Component. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Yemen's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: What a tribe is — a group of people bound by ties of blood and descent, mostly living in one stretch of land, with a social body that helps solve their problems and binds them in mutual support, solidarity and cooperation — the second component of society after the family, the basic building block of Yemeni society, whose members hold to its habits, traditions and urf; why tribes arose — God's will that mankind be made peoples and tribes to know one another, and man's nature as a social being who prefers life in a group, for security and for meeting his needs; and the Yemeni tribesman's praised traits — chivalry, manliness, daring, generosity, courage, loyalty, honesty, and pardoning when able. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
