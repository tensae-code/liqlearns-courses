# Lesson plan — Child Labour

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Yemen · **Title:** Social Phenomena and the Yemeni Family · **Lesson:** Child Labour
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 18 — Social Phenomena and the Yemeni Family (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Child labour — a child's practising work beyond his capacity, before the lawful age for work, in a way that threatens his safety and health, children leaving school for it; why children work beyond their capacity — the family's weak cultural level, employers and tradesmen exploiting children and giving them low wages, family break-up driving some children to flee school into hard work, the parents' inability to spend on their children through poverty, occupation, wars and crises laying an economic burden on the country and then the family, the wish to help the family in work like farming and herding, and weak knowledge of the child-labour laws; its negative effects — the various diseases, illiteracy spread, psychological harms, exploitation, behavioural and sexual delinquency, and social crimes such as begging and theft; the means of prevention — awakening children's guardians to the importance of teaching their children and not letting them drop out, never forcing children to work beyond their capacity, closing the establishments that exploit children, and applying the Child Rights Law and its penalties; and the state's efforts — deterrent laws punishing employers who exploit children and violate their rights, the Child Rights Law and its executive regulation, widening the social security that cares for poor families and their children, and encouraging youth to join the technical institutes to learn crafts and trades they can earn from. This lesson stands on a student book.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 18 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What child labour is, and why children work** — Work beyond a child's capacity before the lawful age, threatening his safety and health; and its causes — poverty, exploitation for low wages, family break-up, wars and crises, helping in farm and herding work, and ignorance of the laws.
2. **Its harms, its prevention, and the state's efforts** — Disease, illiteracy, psychological harm, exploitation, delinquency and social crimes; prevention by awakening parents, closing exploiting establishments and applying the Child Rights Law; and the state's deterrent laws, social security and technical institutes.

Gate pool: at most 5 questions for the prove-it check, mixed with earlier parts, picked as the hardest from the rank right under the wanted sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
