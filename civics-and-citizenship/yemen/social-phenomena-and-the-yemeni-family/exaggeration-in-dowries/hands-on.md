# Hands-on — Exaggeration in Dowries

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: Exaggeration in Dowries. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Yemen's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: The mahr — a sum of money or goods a man presents to a woman, which God imposed for the woman by reason of the marriage contract — and the exaggeration in dowries as a phenomenon foreign to a society that inclines to tolerance and simplicity; why dowries swell — weak religious restraint in the fathers, not following the Messenger's example and guidance, ignorance of the religion's rulings on marriage, showing off and boasting, greed and covetousness, and imitating others and running after appearances; its negative effects — ightirab, young men unable to pay the great dowries guardians demand being driven abroad to provide marriage costs; family break-up, some young men turning to delinquency where media abound, young men forced to shun marriage, and the high dowry becoming a justification for divorce amid marital disputes, leaving the children exposed to loss; boasting in wedding appearances — the great feasts, lavish parties and wasted money; and increased rivalry; and Islam's treatment — Islam urged against exaggeration and called to ease in the mahr as a guarantee of the stable, reassured family, the woman's blessedness lying in the easing of her betrothal and her dowry, and against extravagance in the great costs and feasts; and the particular treatments — taking the Prophet's example in easing marriage, spreading awareness among society of the phenomenon's effects, preparing a document in the frame of each governorate, village or directorate in which families pledge not to exaggerate in dowries or boast in wedding appearances, and activating the role of the mosque, civil-society organisations and media institutions in fighting the phenomenon. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
