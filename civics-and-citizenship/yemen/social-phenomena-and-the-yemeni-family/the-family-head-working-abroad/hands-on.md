# Hands-on — The Family Head's Working Abroad

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: The Family Head's Working Abroad. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Yemen's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: What ightirab is — the family head's travelling from his homeland to another country in order to improve the family's income; its causes — wars and conflicts and the family's low living standard leading to poverty, illiteracy and sickness and few work chances at home, political and tribal or population problems and strife, the exaggeration in dowries and family disputes among them — political causes like wars, economic causes like low living conditions, social causes like family problems and swollen dowries, and others like continuing education; its positive effects — the family's living standard improved, the emigrants' cash remittances supporting economic development by setting up projects and real-estate installations, and some emigrants gaining knowledge, experience and skill and transferring scientific and industrial expertise home; its negative effects — on the family: the wife carrying doubled burdens psychologically, socially and economically, the father's absent role in raising the children raising problems and delinquency among some of them, the weakening of family and social bonds, and children acquiring negative values from emigrants on their return, with the difficulty of re-adapting after return; on society: falling care for agricultural land and the loss of some traditional crafts, rising farm wages and falling production of some crops and the turn to importing instead of producing, and inflation from the emigrants' cash flow, with ill behaviour — extravagance, luxury goods and waste — driving land prices up; and the proposed solutions the lesson names. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
