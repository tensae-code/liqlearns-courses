# Arab-Islamic Political and Military Integration

## Do it first

Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: Arab-Islamic Political and Military Integration. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Yemen's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Sub lesson 1 — Political integration

The Islamic states coordinating unified political stands for the nation's fateful causes, Palestine at their head — their strength in unity, their weakness in fragmentation.

## Sub lesson 2 — Military integration and its means

Joint Islamic defence forces against outside dangers, urgent for a homeland commanding Bab al-Mandab, Suez, Hormuz and Gibraltar — reached by a shared military creed and policy, joint training, joint units and a shared military industry.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Political integration — the Islamic states' cooperation and coordination in taking the unified political stands that serve the Islamic nation's interests and its fateful causes, Palestine among them, the Muslims' strength lying in their unity and integration and their weakness in their fragmentation and abandoning their faith identity; and military integration — the Islamic states' cooperation in facing the outside dangers that threaten them through forming joint Islamic defence forces, of the utmost importance because the great Arab and Islamic homeland commands a distinguished geographic place among the world's continents, overlooking the most important water straits — Bab al-Mandab, the Suez Canal, the Strait of Hormuz and the Strait of Gibraltar, and the Red and Mediterranean seas and the Arab Gulf — which made it the object of outside states' coveting for control of those straits; and the means of achieving military integration — beginning by planting a military jihad creed embodying the faith identity and strengthening belonging to the principles of right, justice, freedom and dignity, setting one military policy between the Islamic states, caring for the joint qualification and training of the armed forces' members, forming joint military units charged with defending the nation from the dangers, and founding a shared, developed military industry. Give one real example from your own life or from the Yemen you live in, and name the lesson's key words: arab-islamic, political, and. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
