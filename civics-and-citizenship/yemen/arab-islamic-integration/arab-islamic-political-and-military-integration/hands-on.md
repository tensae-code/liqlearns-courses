# Hands-on — Arab-Islamic Political and Military Integration

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: Arab-Islamic Political and Military Integration. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Yemen's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Political integration — the Islamic states' cooperation and coordination in taking the unified political stands that serve the Islamic nation's interests and its fateful causes, Palestine among them, the Muslims' strength lying in their unity and integration and their weakness in their fragmentation and abandoning their faith identity; and military integration — the Islamic states' cooperation in facing the outside dangers that threaten them through forming joint Islamic defence forces, of the utmost importance because the great Arab and Islamic homeland commands a distinguished geographic place among the world's continents, overlooking the most important water straits — Bab al-Mandab, the Suez Canal, the Strait of Hormuz and the Strait of Gibraltar, and the Red and Mediterranean seas and the Arab Gulf — which made it the object of outside states' coveting for control of those straits; and the means of achieving military integration — beginning by planting a military jihad creed embodying the faith identity and strengthening belonging to the principles of right, justice, freedom and dignity, setting one military policy between the Islamic states, caring for the joint qualification and training of the armed forces' members, forming joint military units charged with defending the nation from the dangers, and founding a shared, developed military industry. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
