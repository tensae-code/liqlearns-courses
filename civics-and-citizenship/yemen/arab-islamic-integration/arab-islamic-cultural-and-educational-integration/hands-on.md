# Hands-on — Arab-Islamic Cultural and Educational Integration

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: Arab-Islamic Cultural and Educational Integration. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Yemen's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: What culture is, in its Arab sense — the practical and intellectual yield arising from studying the Sharia texts and the value and moral inheritance the nation gained by experience — and what upbringing is, the individual and social activity aiming to raise the human being in every side of his personality; what Arab-Islamic cultural and educational integration is — the cooperation and coordination between the means of upbringing and culture at the level of the Arab homeland and the Islamic world, leading to raising the citizen's scientific and cultural level, and so fixing the nation's identity and course; its importance — reviving the spirit of faith brotherhood and solidarity among the Arab and Islamic nation's sons, foiling the attempts of the intellectual invasion that seeks to replace Islamic culture with Western culture carrying much that demolishes Islamic values and principles, and reviving the spirit of pride in belonging to the nation; and the ways of achieving it — adopting one comprehensive Islamic educational philosophy built on the Qur'an and its view of man, the universe and life, freed from dependence on Western education, strengthening the faith and national identity in the nation's sons, and bringing forward the leading models in the nation's history. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
