# Lesson plan — Arab-Islamic Integration

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Yemen · **Title:** Arab-Islamic Integration · **Lesson:** Arab-Islamic Integration
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 19 — Arab-Islamic Integration (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** What Arab-Islamic integration means — the cooperation of the Arab and Islamic homelands' states among themselves, each state contributing what it has of raw materials, money or manpower, across the cultural, economic, educational, health and military fields, a thought born of the Arab homeland's and Islamic world's variety of climate, natural and human wealth and religious, cultural and value heritage; and its factors — cultural: the Qur'an, the source of knowledge and culture and the methodology the nation must move by, whose guidance can carry it past division and fragmentation, and the Arabic language, the Qur'an's language, the effective means of carrying thought and feeling between the Arab-Islamic nation's sons, whose keeping and developing is a religious duty; historical: a past carrying models of the nation's great sons who spread right, justice, asceticism and virtue and left a scientific and moral heritage that is all mankind's pride, able to revive the spirit of integration and unity; social: the Arabs' likeness in their social make-up and most customs and traditions — cooperation, honouring the neighbour and keeping the family's cohesion — a strong spur to strengthen the social bonds; and natural: one geographic homeland whose varied environments and climates complete one another, which the invaders were keen to cut into statelets so as to seize and plunder its goods. This lesson stands on a student book.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 19 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. ## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What Arab-Islamic integration means** — The Arab and Islamic states cooperating, each contributing what it has of raw materials, money or manpower, across the cultural, economic, educational, health and military fields.
2. **Its factors** — Cultural — the Qur'an and the Arabic language; historical — a shared heritage of models of right and justice; social — peoples alike in cooperation, neighbourliness and family cohesion; and natural — one varied geographic homeland the invaders cut into statelets.

Gate pool: at most 5 questions for the prove-it check, mixed with earlier parts, picked as the hardest from the rank right under the wanted sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
