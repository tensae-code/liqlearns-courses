# Hands-on — Arab-Islamic Economic Integration

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: Arab-Islamic Economic Integration. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Yemen's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: What Arab-Islamic economic integration means — the states exchanging their varied resources among themselves, the states where human resources are available and capital is lacking — Egypt and Sudan are the book's examples — supplying the other states with workers and receiving money in return, and likewise in the other fields: agriculture, livestock and fish wealth, mineral wealth and energy, so that all can be benefited from; its importance; and the steps to achieving it — a sincere political will in the Arab and Muslim rulers and a sense of responsibility toward their peoples' interests, removing the customs barriers between the states, cooperating in carrying out economic development projects and protecting them as this integration's infrastructure, exchanging Arab and Islamic manufactures and finding them a place before foreign manufactures, and preparing the climate for the Arab and Islamic economic cadres from across the world and gathering them to benefit from their accumulated experience in running the integration's projects. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
