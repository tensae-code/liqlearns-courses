# Lesson plan — Arab-Islamic Economic Integration

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Yemen · **Title:** Arab-Islamic Integration · **Lesson:** Arab-Islamic Economic Integration
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 19 — Arab-Islamic Integration (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** What Arab-Islamic economic integration means — the states exchanging their varied resources among themselves, the states where human resources are available and capital is lacking — Egypt and Sudan are the book's examples — supplying the other states with workers and receiving money in return, and likewise in the other fields: agriculture, livestock and fish wealth, mineral wealth and energy, so that all can be benefited from; its importance; and the steps to achieving it — a sincere political will in the Arab and Muslim rulers and a sense of responsibility toward their peoples' interests, removing the customs barriers between the states, cooperating in carrying out economic development projects and protecting them as this integration's infrastructure, exchanging Arab and Islamic manufactures and finding them a place before foreign manufactures, and preparing the climate for the Arab and Islamic economic cadres from across the world and gathering them to benefit from their accumulated experience in running the integration's projects. This lesson stands on a student book.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 19 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. ## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What economic integration is** — The states exchanging their varied resources — manpower for capital, Egypt and Sudan the book's examples — across farming, livestock and fisheries, minerals and energy.
2. **The steps to achieve it** — Sincere political will; removing customs barriers; joint development projects as its infrastructure; a place for Arab and Islamic manufactures against foreign ones; and gathering the economic cadres to run its projects.

Gate pool: at most 5 questions for the prove-it check, mixed with earlier parts, picked as the hardest from the rank right under the wanted sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
