# Arab-Islamic Economic Integration

## Do it first

Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: Arab-Islamic Economic Integration. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Yemen's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Sub lesson 1 — What economic integration is

The states exchanging their varied resources — manpower for capital, Egypt and Sudan the book's examples — across farming, livestock and fisheries, minerals and energy.

## Sub lesson 2 — The steps to achieve it

Sincere political will; removing customs barriers; joint development projects as its infrastructure; a place for Arab and Islamic manufactures against foreign ones; and gathering the economic cadres to run its projects.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: What Arab-Islamic economic integration means — the states exchanging their varied resources among themselves, the states where human resources are available and capital is lacking — Egypt and Sudan are the book's examples — supplying the other states with workers and receiving money in return, and likewise in the other fields: agriculture, livestock and fish wealth, mineral wealth and energy, so that all can be benefited from; its importance; and the steps to achieving it — a sincere political will in the Arab and Muslim rulers and a sense of responsibility toward their peoples' interests, removing the customs barriers between the states, cooperating in carrying out economic development projects and protecting them as this integration's infrastructure, exchanging Arab and Islamic manufactures and finding them a place before foreign manufactures, and preparing the climate for the Arab and Islamic economic cadres from across the world and gathering them to benefit from their accumulated experience in running the integration's projects. Give one real example from your own life or from the Yemen you live in, and name the lesson's key words: arab-islamic, economic, integration. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
