# Civics and Citizenship — Nigeria course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Nigeria inside the Civics and Citizenship
folder, organised by **levels, not grades**: a learner climbs
when they have proved the level, not when a school calendar
says so. The school grades the books come from appear only in
each lesson's sources (provenance), never as the course's
organisation. The path follows Nigeria's own order as its
books lay it out — government built from first concepts
(power, authority, sovereignty) before any machinery, then
the menu of forms, the constitution and the arms, the
principles that judge the machinery, the citizen's own place
in the state, and the channels between citizen and state;
then the senior book applies all of it to Nigeria itself —
its federal bargain, its party history republic by republic,
its crises and coups taught as cautionary history, peace —
and last Nigeria in Africa and the world. The filing for
this build (`nigeria-placements.md` — 21 records / 15
distinct books / 2 read in full in place, 52 placements)
stocks 52 lessons: every placement names its own coined
lesson, the coined names were deduped before building (no
two placements shared a lesson), and every placement stocks
into exactly one lesson.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own government books — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a
box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours;
the lessons are the books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book
list and the acceptance reckoning. **No lesson in this
course rests on a teacher guide: both books read are student
books.** The primary and junior/senior Civic Education
ladder that should sit under Level 1 exists only as
catalogue titles (record-only, no file held), and Government
SS2 is UNREAD — see ABOUT.md.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **Foundations of Government** — what government is and
   why it is studied; the state and its features; power,
   authority and legitimacy; sovereignty; political culture
   and socialisation; and the big organising ideas —
   communalism, socialism, communism, capitalism, oligarchy,
   feudalism, fascism and totalitarianism. 8 lessons, in one
   title: `foundations-of-government/` (8 lessons).
2. **Forms of Government** — the menu of forms that follows
   the concepts: unitary, federal, confederal, presidential,
   parliamentary, monarchy and republicanism, and military
   government. 7 lessons, in one title:
   `forms-of-government/` (7 lessons).
3. **The Constitution and Arms of Government** — what a
   constitution is and constitutionalism, then the machinery
   the forms arrange: the executive, the legislature and
   bills, and the judiciary. 5 lessons, in two titles:
   `the-constitution/` (2 lessons), `arms-of-government/`
   (3 lessons).
4. **Principles of Government** — the principles that judge
   the machinery: the rule of law, fundamental human rights,
   separation of powers with checks and balances,
   representative government and participation, and
   centralisation and decentralisation. 5 lessons, in one
   title: `principles-of-government/` (5 lessons).
5. **Citizenship** — the pupil's own place in the state,
   placed after the state is understood: how citizenship is
   acquired and its types, and the rights, duties and
   obligations of citizens (and how a citizen differs from
   an alien). 2 lessons, in one title: `citizenship/`
   (2 lessons).
6. **Parties and Pressure Groups** — the channels between
   citizen and state: political parties, party systems and
   the manifesto, and pressure groups. 3 lessons, in one
   title: `parties-and-pressure-groups/` (3 lessons).
7. **Nigerian Federalism and Parties** — the senior book
   applies the ladder to Nigeria's own record: how
   federalism emerged, its structure and features, and its
   standing problems; then party history republic by
   republic, 1923–1966, the Second Republic, and the Third
   and Fourth Republics. 6 lessons, in two titles:
   `nigerian-federalism/` (3 lessons), `nigerian-parties/`
   (3 lessons).
8. **Crises, Military Rule and Peace** — what happens when
   the machinery fails, and how peace is rebuilt: the major
   political crises, the electoral crises, military
   intervention and military rule assessed, then conflict
   resolution and peace education. 6 lessons, in two titles:
   `crises-and-military-rule/` (4 lessons),
   `peace-and-conflict/` (2 lessons).
9. **Nigeria and the World** — the widest circle, last in
   the books' own order: interdependence and globalisation,
   Nigeria's foreign policy, non-alignment, the OAU and the
   African Union, the Commonwealth and ECOWAS, OPEC and the
   United Nations, the Millennium Development Goals, NEPAD,
   e-government, and leadership and followership. 10
   lessons, in one title: `nigeria-and-the-world/`
   (10 lessons).

**Totals: 52 lessons** (Level 1: 8 · Level 2: 7 · Level 3:
5 · Level 4: 5 · Level 5: 2 · Level 6: 3 · Level 7: 6 ·
Level 8: 6 · Level 9: 10 — from two read books: *New
Government for Senior Secondary Schools 1* (Learn Africa
Plc, 2013, reprinted 2016, COMPLETE) and *New Government
for Senior Secondary Schools 3* (Learn Africa Plc, 2014,
COMPLETE); 52 placements stocking 52 lessons).
