# Civics and Citizenship — Nigeria course — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

Civics belongs to the country: this course is Nigeria's own —
built from Nigeria's government books (Learn Africa's *New
Government for Senior Secondary Schools 1* and *3*, the two
books read in full in place). Inside the course the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: the titles below
ARE the levels' titles, in path order — what the learner
proves, step by step. The grades the books come from live
only in each lesson's sources.

The path's own logic is Nigeria's, taken from the filing's
ladder sketch, which is the books' own order: SS1 opens with
concepts before any institution (what government and the
state are; power, authority, legitimacy, sovereignty; the
big -isms), then the menu of forms, then the machinery the
forms arrange (constitution, constitutionalism, the three
arms), then the principles that judge the machinery (rule of
law, rights, separation of powers, participation,
decentralisation), then the pupil's own place in the state
(citizenship, rights and duties), then the channels between
citizen and state (parties, party systems, manifestos,
pressure groups). SS3 then applies all of it to Nigeria
itself: the federation's making and problems, party history
republic by republic, crises and coups assessed as
cautionary history, conflict resolution and peace education,
and last the widest circle — Nigeria in Africa and the
world. That ladder IS the path order (recommended, never
forced — a learner can jump in higher; the "start here
instead" hints point down this ladder). All 52 lessons stand
on student books; none stands on a teacher guide.

## The titles

**Level 1 — foundations of government** (SS1, Theme 1
opening chapters):

- `foundations-of-government/` — Level 1. What Government
  Is · The State · Power, Authority and Legitimacy ·
  Sovereignty · Political Culture and Political
  Socialisation · Communalism and Socialism · Communism and
  Capitalism · Oligarchy, Feudalism, Fascism and
  Totalitarianism. The course opens with concepts, because
  SS1 opens there — government is understood before any
  Nigerian machinery is named.

**Level 2 — forms of government** (SS1, the forms chapters):

- `forms-of-government/` — Level 2. Unitary Government ·
  Federal Government · Confederal Government · The
  Presidential System · The Parliamentary System · Monarchy
  and Republicanism · Military Government. The menu of forms
  follows the concepts: each form is a different answer to
  where power sits and who holds it.

**Level 3 — the constitution and arms of government**
(SS1, the machinery chapters):

- `the-constitution/` — Level 3. The Constitution ·
  Constitutionalism. The rule-book first: what a
  constitution is, and government itself bound by it.
- `arms-of-government/` — Level 3. The Executive · The
  Legislature and Bills · The Judiciary. The machinery that
  the forms arrange, arm by arm.

**Level 4 — principles of government** (SS1, the principles
chapters):

- `principles-of-government/` — Level 4. The Rule of Law ·
  Fundamental Human Rights · Separation of Powers ·
  Representative Government · Centralisation and
  Decentralisation. Principles judge the machinery: this
  level names the standards the arms must answer to.

**Level 5 — citizenship** (SS1, the citizenship chapter):

- `citizenship/` — Level 5. Becoming a Citizen · Rights and
  Duties of Citizens. The pupil's own place in the state,
  placed after the state is understood — how one becomes a
  citizen, and what the citizen holds and owes.

**Level 6 — parties and pressure groups** (SS1, Theme 3):

- `parties-and-pressure-groups/` — Level 6. Political
  Parties · Party Systems and the Manifesto · Pressure
  Groups. The channels between citizen and state: parties
  that seek power, and groups that seek influence.

**Level 7 — Nigerian federalism and parties** (SS3,
Theme 1):

- `nigerian-federalism/` — Level 7. The Emergence of
  Federalism in Nigeria · The Structure of Nigerian
  Federalism · Problems of Nigerian Federalism. SS3 applies
  the ladder to Nigeria's own record, starting with its
  federal bargain — how it was forced and built, how it
  stands, and its standing problems.
- `nigerian-parties/` — Level 7. Political Parties in
  Nigeria, 1923–1966 · Political Parties in the Second
  Republic · Parties in the Third and Fourth Republics.
  Party history republic by republic, in the book's own
  order.

**Level 8 — crises, military rule and peace** (SS3,
Theme 2):

- `crises-and-military-rule/` — Level 8. Major Political
  Crises in Nigeria · Electoral Crises in Nigeria · Military
  Intervention in Politics · Military Rule Assessed. What
  happens when the machinery fails, taught as cautionary
  history.
- `peace-and-conflict/` — Level 8. Conflict Resolution and
  Management · Peace Education. How peace is rebuilt:
  conflict understood, resolved, and peace itself taught.

**Level 9 — Nigeria and the world** (SS3, Theme 3):

- `nigeria-and-the-world/` — Level 9. Interdependence of
  Nations and Globalisation · Nigeria's Foreign Policy ·
  Non-Alignment · The OAU and the African Union · The
  Commonwealth and ECOWAS · OPEC and the United Nations ·
  The Millennium Development Goals · NEPAD · E-Government ·
  Leadership and Followership. The widest circle, last in
  the books' own order: Nigeria among nations, its
  continental and world bodies, and the citizen's closing
  pair — leadership and followership.
