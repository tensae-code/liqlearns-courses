# Lesson plan — Conflict Resolution and Management

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Nigeria · **Title:** Peace and Conflict · **Lesson:** Conflict Resolution and Management
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 8 — Crises, Military Rule and Peace (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Conflict has a meaning, types, causes and consequences, and conflicts can be resolved and managed by known methods. This lesson stands on a student book.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 8 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.
## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What conflict is** — Conflict has a meaning, types, causes and consequences — this part names them.
2. **Resolving and managing conflict** — Conflicts are resolved and managed by known methods, and this part works through them.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Stands in path order after **Military Rule Assessed** — do that lesson first if this one feels hard (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Conflict has a meaning, types, causes and consequences, and conflicts can be resolved and managed by known methods.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, school or town — or, for this book's lessons, from the public life of Nigeria they live in.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the learner's own life in Nigeria and the book's own teaching, as the book itself works.
