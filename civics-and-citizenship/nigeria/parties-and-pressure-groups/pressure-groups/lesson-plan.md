# Lesson plan — Pressure Groups

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Nigeria · **Title:** Parties and Pressure Groups · **Lesson:** Pressure Groups
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 6 — Parties and Pressure Groups (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Pressure groups seek to influence government without seeking to govern themselves; they have their own types, functions and modes of operation, and they differ from political parties. This lesson stands on a student book.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 6 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.
## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What pressure groups are, and their types** — A pressure group seeks to influence government without seeking to govern itself, and pressure groups come in different types.
2. **How pressure groups work — and how they differ from parties** — Pressure groups have their own functions and modes of operation, and they differ from political parties, which seek power itself.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
