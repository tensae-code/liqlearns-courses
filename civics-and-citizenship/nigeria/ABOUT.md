# About the Civics and Citizenship — Nigeria course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own government books. The one exception is the **Tip
boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Nigeria inside the Civics and
Citizenship folder (one course per country). Inside it, the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place
is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the
ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced
gate. The school grades the books come from appear only in
each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

Nigeria's own way, preserved whole, as filed: the SS1 book
builds government from first concepts (power, authority,
sovereignty) before any Nigerian machinery, then lands
citizenship and parties as the pupil's own entry points into
the state. The senior book (SS3) is Nigeria itself — its
federal bargain, its party history republic by republic, its
crises and coups taught as cautionary history, then its
place in Africa and the world.

The course as built has **nine stocked levels**, from the
filing for this build (`nigeria-placements.md` — 21 records
/ 15 distinct books / 2 read in full in place (Government
SS1, SS3), 1 UNREAD (Government SS2, phantom push), 12
RECORD-ONLY (Learn Africa catalogue-only titles, no file
held); 52 placements in all, stocking 52 lessons — the
coined lesson names were deduped before building, no two
placements shared a lesson, and every placement stocks into
exactly one lesson):

1. **Level 1 — Foundations of Government (8 lessons).**
   What government is; the state; power, authority and
   legitimacy; sovereignty; political culture and
   socialisation; and the big organising ideas.
2. **Level 2 — Forms of Government (7 lessons).** Unitary,
   federal, confederal, presidential, parliamentary,
   monarchy and republicanism, and military government.
3. **Level 3 — The Constitution and Arms of Government
   (5 lessons).** The constitution and constitutionalism;
   the executive, the legislature and bills, and the
   judiciary.
4. **Level 4 — Principles of Government (5 lessons).** The
   rule of law, fundamental human rights, separation of
   powers, representative government, and centralisation
   and decentralisation.
5. **Level 5 — Citizenship (2 lessons).** Becoming a
   citizen; rights, duties and obligations of citizens.
6. **Level 6 — Parties and Pressure Groups (3 lessons).**
   Political parties, party systems and the manifesto, and
   pressure groups.
7. **Level 7 — Nigerian Federalism and Parties
   (6 lessons).** The federation's emergence, structure and
   problems; party history 1923–1966, the Second Republic,
   and the Third and Fourth Republics.
8. **Level 8 — Crises, Military Rule and Peace
   (6 lessons).** Political and electoral crises, military
   intervention and military rule assessed, conflict
   resolution and peace education.
9. **Level 9 — Nigeria and the World (10 lessons).**
   Interdependence and globalisation, foreign policy,
   non-alignment, the AU, the Commonwealth and ECOWAS, OPEC
   and the UN, the MDGs, NEPAD, e-government, and leadership
   and followership.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later;
the books' own shape, walked in order):

- *Government, Nigeria's own way:* "First, the concepts:
  what government is, what the state is, and the ideas that
  make rule accepted — power, authority, legitimacy,
  sovereignty. Then the menu of forms a state can take, and
  the machinery: a constitution that binds government
  itself, and three arms that carry it out, make its laws
  and judge under them. Then the principles that judge the
  machinery — the rule of law, your fundamental rights,
  powers kept apart. Then your own place: how you become a
  citizen, and the rights and duties that come together.
  Then the channels — parties and pressure groups. And then
  Nigeria itself: how its federation was made and what
  troubles it, its parties republic by republic, its crises
  and its years of military rule, taught plainly — and how
  peace is rebuilt. Last, Nigeria in the world: Africa at
  the centre of its foreign policy, the African Union,
  ECOWAS, the United Nations — and the closing pair every
  citizen lives: leadership, and followership."
