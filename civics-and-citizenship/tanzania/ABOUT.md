# About the Civics and Citizenship — Tanzania course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Civic and Moral Education / Uraia na Maadili books. The
one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from
us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons
are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Tanzania inside the Civics and
Citizenship folder (one course per country). Inside it, the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place
is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the
ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced
gate. The school grades the books come from appear only in
each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

Tanzania's own way, preserved whole, as filed: civics begins
with love — of self, family, school and country — and only
then meets government; every virtue chapter is taught
through school and home duties. The youngest books
(Standards 3 and 4) teach belonging and good habits before
any institution is named, with democracy and law appearing
inside that frame at Standard 4; the Standards 5–7 books run
their own six-step ladder (love and care; pride in school
and country; government and democracy; duties and
resources; law and character; rights, peace and the world),
and the leaving-primary book turns the virtues outward —
community education, guarding national resources against
corruption and fraud, protecting human rights, and
friendship with other countries, including coping with
globalisation. Parallel language editions (English and
Kiswahili) are placed once, with both editions named, as
filed.

The course as built has **eight stocked levels**, from the
filings for this build (`tanzania-placements.md` — 4 records
/ 3 distinct books / 3 read in full; and
`tanzania-placements-b.md` — 4 census records / 87 link leads
triaged, the leads collapsing to the TIE pupil's books for
Standards 3–7, of which Standards 3 and 4 in both languages
were NOT in the census and are read and placed there, and
the English Standard 5 is record-only there; 62 placements
in all, stocking 60 lessons — the coined lesson names were
deduped before building: two lessons are stocked by two
placements each, the Standard 3 and Standard 4 passes of
the same teaching — loving our school
(`belonging/loving-our-school`) and loving our country
(`belonging/loving-our-country`) — and every placement
stocks into exactly one lesson):

1. **Level 1 — Belonging and Good Habits (8 lessons).**
   Loving our society, our school and our country; valuing
   our society; being responsible, handling difficulties,
   being honest; maintaining peace.
2. **Level 2 — Widening the Circle — Democracy and Law
   Appear (9 lessons).** Loving yourself and others; living
   with people of different origins; democracy; caring,
   responsibility and resources, tolerance, goals, critical
   learning; obeying laws and rules.
3. **Level 3 — Love and Care (6 lessons).** Loving yourself
   and others, loving family members, loving others in the
   community; caring for yourself and others, caring for
   others, caring for ourselves and others (including
   reproductive health education).
4. **Level 4 — Pride in School and Country (5 lessons).**
   Proud of your school and loving your country (through
   Tanzania's money and national symbols), school pride and
   national symbols and public holidays, proud of your
   school, proud of your country, valuing the community.
5. **Level 5 — Government and Democracy (4 lessons).** The
   structure of government leadership from ward (kata)
   level; the structure of the Government of the United
   Republic of Tanzania, with its history; democracy, and
   democracy in Tanzania.
6. **Level 6 — Duties and Resources (9 lessons).**
   Protecting national and public resources, fulfilling
   responsibilities (at school and home, and in society
   without corruption or fraud), environmental
   conservation, building good relationships.
7. **Level 7 — Law and Character (13 lessons).** Patience,
   goals, trustworthiness; obeying laws and regulations,
   self-discipline, tolerance, learning inquisitively and
   by critical analysis, being trustworthy in the
   community, being patient.
8. **Level 8 — Rights, Peace and the World (6 lessons).**
   Keeping peace; observing human rights, then protecting
   them; cooperating with other nations, Tanzania's
   relations with other countries, building friendships
   with other countries.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later;
the books' own shape, walked in order):

- *Tanzania's own order:* "First, love: loving our society,
  our school and our country, and valuing the people who
  serve — then the habits of a good member of the
  community: responsibility, handling difficulties,
  honesty, and keeping peace. The circle widens: loving
  yourself and others, living with people of different
  origins — and here democracy and the law appear for the
  first time. Then, as every older book opens: love and
  care — for yourself, your family, your community —
  pride in your school and your country, through its money
  and its symbols and its holidays. Then the state, taught
  plainly: leadership from the ward upward, the structure
  of the Government of the United Republic, and democracy
  as Tanzania practises it. Then your duties: guarding the
  nation's resources, fulfilling your responsibilities,
  caring for the environment and for each other. Then the
  character that keeps the law willingly — patience,
  goals, self-discipline, tolerance, trustworthiness,
  learning that asks questions. And last, the widest
  circle: peace, human rights observed and then protected,
  and Tanzania in friendship with the other countries of
  the world."

## Honesty notes (course level)

- **What the filings do and do not contain.** The filings
  contain 5 distinct read books (the TIE Civic and Moral
  Education / Uraia na Maadili pupil's books for Standards
  3–7, in English and Kiswahili), 1 record-only parallel
  edition (English Standard 5), 62 placements, and the
  books' own contents maps. The filings do not contain any
  other Tanzania book — no book for any other standard, no
  teacher guide, no programme. Nothing of that kind is in
  this course, and no lesson pretends to one. The ladder
  therefore covers exactly the five books' own progression,
  Standards 3–4 first and the Standards 5–7 ladder after
  them, as filed.

  the filings carry none.** All five read books are filed
  COMPLETE. No placement in the filings carries a PARTIAL,

  census books' absence from the GitHub tree (phantom
