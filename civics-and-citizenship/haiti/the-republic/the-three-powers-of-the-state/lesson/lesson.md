# The Three Powers of the State

## Do it first

Name three different jobs the state does — judging, making laws, running the country. This lesson shows why those jobs are kept in separate hands.

## Sub lesson 1 — The three powers and their missions

The programme teaches the three constituted powers of the state: their principles, their missions, and how each one functions.

## Sub lesson 2 — Where each power stops

Each power is delimited — it has a boundary. Knowing where one power ends and another begins is how the citizen sees that the division is real.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: the state's power is divided into three constituted powers, each with its own principles, mission and limits — none may swallow the others. Give one real example from your own life or from the Haiti you live in, and name the lesson's key words: three powers, state, delimitation. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
