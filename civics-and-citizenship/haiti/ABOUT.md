# About the Civics and Citizenship — Haiti course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Éducation à la citoyenneté programmes. The one exception
is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people
making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Haiti inside the Civics and
Citizenship folder (one course per country). Inside it, the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place
is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the
ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced
gate. The school grades the books come from appear only in
each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

The course as built has **four stocked levels**, from two
filings combined into one course (`haiti-placements.md`, the
main filing — five MENFP programmes, 46 placements — and
`haiti-placements-b.md`, a record-only programme filing with
no placement lines, carried below exactly as filed):

1. **Level 1 — The Republic and the citizen (22 lessons).**
   The NS1 year, taught by two programmes at once: the 2019
   minimal programme (what citizenship education is, citizen
   concepts, rights and duties, the Republic — history,
   symbols, three powers, guaranteeing institutions, the
   citizenship crisis — and who forms citizens) and the
   fuller 2015 operational programme (identity and memory,
   government and school government, elections, peace,
   participation, environmental culture and climate).
2. **Level 2 — Constitution and governance (5 lessons).**
   NS2's own new teaching: the Haitian constitution and its
   history 1805–1987, peace and non-violence, governance as
   power–government–ethics, local governance, and electoral
   practice. (NS2 also re-teaches identity, memory, forms of
   government, governance-and-ethics and school government;
   those five lessons are stocked once, at Level 1, with
   both programmes' provenance rows.)
3. **Level 3 — The world, the environment and taking part
   (7 lessons).** NS3 turns outward: world citizenship,
   globalisation and mondialité, rights and social justice —
   bound back to the local through eco-citizenship, climate
   and pollution, and participation in local development.
4. **Level 4 — Ethics, rights and the city (7 lessons).**
   NS4, the final year, is ethics: bioethics, the ethics of
   responsibility, principles of justice, the three
   generations of human rights judged against Haiti, and the
   citizen's share in organising the city and building "a
   new Haiti".

Haiti's own way, preserved whole: citizenship education is
framed as rebuilding. The citizen's rights and duties, the
Republic's principles and the institutions that guarantee
citizenship are taught against an openly named "crise de la
citoyenneté" — a crisis of citizenship: collapsed values,
crises of responsibility, leadership and institutions — that
pupils are asked to understand and help resolve. The climb
then runs through the constitution's long history and
governance judged by public ethics, out to the world citizen
under globalisation and back to eco-citizenship, and ends in
ethics and the city: bioethics, responsibility, justice, and
the common good.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
books' own shape, walked in order):

- *Republic first, then the world, then conscience:*
  "First, what a citizen is: the concepts, the rights and
  duties, the Republic and its symbols and powers — and the
  crisis of citizenship Haiti's own programme names, and
  asks you to help resolve. Then the constitution, from 1805
  to the one in force, and governance with public ethics,
  down to your own school government and your local
  elections. Then the world: the Haitian as world citizen,
  human rights and social justice, and the eco-citizen's
  duties to the place they live. And last, conscience
  itself: bioethics, responsibility, justice — and the city,
  organised together for the common good of a new Haiti."

## Honesty notes (course level)

- **What the filings do and do not contain.** The main
  filing contains five MENFP teacher programmes (the four
  2019 minimal programmes NS1–NS4 and the 2015 operational
  programme for NS1), 46 placements, all marked TEACHER.
  The second filing contains no placement lines at all: one
  record-only programme (the 2024 fundamental-cycle
  programme, Grades 7–9) and four record-only leads. The
  filings do not contain any Haitian pupil civics textbook,
  any NS2–NS4 operational programme, or any
  fundamental-cycle pupil material. Those books are
  therefore **not in this course**, and no lesson pretends

  teacher programmes alone; every lesson's sources say so
