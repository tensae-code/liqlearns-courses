# Hands-on — Elections and Types of Vote

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say how you think an election gets organised — who prints, who counts, who decides disputes. This lesson names the machinery.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: elections are set up by named bodies under electoral law, and a vote can be direct and universal or indirect — the citizen knows the machinery that counts their voice. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
