# Elections and Types of Vote

## Do it first

Say how you think an election gets organised — who prints, who counts, who decides disputes. This lesson names the machinery.

## Sub lesson 1 — Who organises elections

Elections are set up by organising bodies — a permanent or provisional electoral council — working under the electoral law and decree.

## Sub lesson 2 — Types of vote

The programme teaches the typology of votes: the direct, universal vote, and the indirect vote — two different roads from the citizen to the office.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: elections are set up by named bodies under electoral law, and a vote can be direct and universal or indirect — the citizen knows the machinery that counts their voice. Give one real example from your own life or from the Haiti you live in, and name the lesson's key words: elections, electoral council, vote, direct, indirect. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
