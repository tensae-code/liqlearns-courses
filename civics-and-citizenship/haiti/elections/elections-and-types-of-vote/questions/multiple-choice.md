# Elections and Types of Vote — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1.** Which statement best gives this lesson's teaching — Who organises elections?
a) Elections are set up by organising bodies — a permanent or provisional electoral council — working under the electoral law and decree.  b) it has nothing to do with how Haiti governs itself  c) it was invented by foreign governments  d) it matters only in examinations
**Answer: a) Elections are set up by organising bodies — a permanent or provisional electoral council — working under the electoral law and decree.** — That is what this lesson teaches.

**2.** Which statement best gives this lesson's teaching — Types of vote?
a) The programme teaches the typology of votes: the direct, universal vote, and the indirect vote — two different roads from the citizen to the office.  b) it has nothing to do with how Haiti governs itself  c) it was invented by foreign governments  d) it matters only in examinations
**Answer: a) The programme teaches the typology of votes: the direct, universal vote, and the indirect vote — two different roads from the citizen to the office.** — That is what this lesson teaches.

**3.** What is this lesson's one thing?
a) elections are set up by named bodies under electoral law, and a vote can be direct and universal or indirect — the citizen knows the machinery that counts their voice.  b) citizens should never ask where a rule comes from  c) only officials need to know this  d) it applies in other countries but not in Haiti
**Answer: a) elections are set up by named bodies under electoral law, and a vote can be direct and universal or indirect — the citizen knows the machinery that counts their voice.** — That is what this lesson teaches.
