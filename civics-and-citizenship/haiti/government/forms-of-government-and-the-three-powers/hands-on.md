# Hands-on — Forms of Government and the Three Powers

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Name one other country and say how you think it is ruled. This lesson compares forms of government — and Haiti's three powers against the world's.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: countries are governed in different forms, and Haiti's three powers can be compared with how other states arrange theirs — across the Caribbean, the Americas, Europe, Africa and Asia. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
