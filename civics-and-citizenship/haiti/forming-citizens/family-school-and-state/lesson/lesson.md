# Family, School and State

## Do it first

Name one thing your family taught you about living with others, and one thing school taught you. This lesson maps who forms a citizen.

The programme's progression grid gives a whole theme to the contribution of family, school and state — with religion named alongside them — in forming citizens.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: citizens are formed, not born — the family, the school and the state (and religion) each contribute their part to forming them. Give one real example from your own life or from the Haiti you live in, and name the lesson's key words: family, school, state, formation. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: citizens are formed, not born — the family, the school and the state (and religion) each contribute their part to forming them.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
