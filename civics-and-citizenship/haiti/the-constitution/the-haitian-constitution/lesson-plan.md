# Lesson plan — The Haitian Constitution

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Haiti · **Title:** The Constitution · **Lesson:** The Haitian Constitution
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — Constitution and Governance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a constitution is the country's founding law — Haiti's runs from 1805 to 1987, and the citizen knows the constitution in force and why it matters to them. This lesson stands on a teacher programme alone — evidence only, labelled as such.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What a constitution is** — The programme presents and defines the constitution: what kind of law it is, and what it does for a country.
2. **Haiti's constitutions, 1805–1987** — Haiti's constitutional history runs from 1805 to 1987; the pupil studies that history, the constitution in force, and why the constitution matters to a citizen.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: a constitution is the country's founding law — Haiti's runs from 1805 to 1987, and the citizen knows the constitution in force and why it matters to them.

3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.
