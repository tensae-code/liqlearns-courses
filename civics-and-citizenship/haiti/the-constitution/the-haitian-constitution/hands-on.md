# Hands-on — The Haitian Constitution

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Say what you think a constitution is, in one sentence. This lesson presents Haiti's constitution — and the long line of constitutions behind it.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: a constitution is the country's founding law — Haiti's runs from 1805 to 1987, and the citizen knows the constitution in force and why it matters to them. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
