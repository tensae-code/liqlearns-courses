# Lesson plan — Managing the City and the Common Good

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Haiti · **Title:** The City · **Lesson:** Managing the City and the Common Good
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — Ethics, Rights and the City (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** public things must be managed for the common good — and the programme names the cost when they are not: bad management of the Haitian state's goods feeds poverty. This lesson stands on a teacher programme alone — evidence only, labelled as such.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 4 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Managing public things** — Managing the city is managing public things for the common good — the standard every manager of the shared world is judged by.
2. **Bad management feeds poverty** — The programme states the consequence plainly: bad management of the Haitian state's goods feeds poverty.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Stands in path order after **Living Together: Tolerance and Respect** — do that lesson first if this one feels hard (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: public things must be managed for the common good — and the programme names the cost when they are not: bad management of the Haitian state's goods feeds poverty.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, school or town — or, for this book's lessons, from the public life of Haiti they live in.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the learner's own life in Haiti and the book's own teaching, as the book itself works.
