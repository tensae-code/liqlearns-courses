# The Three Generations of Human Rights

## Do it first

List the rights you can name, then sort them: freedoms, life's necessities, and rights we hold together. This lesson teaches that sorting — and asks why the rights on paper are not the rights in life, in Haiti.

## Sub lesson 1 — Three generations of rights

The first generation is freedoms and political rights; the second, social, economic and cultural rights; the third, solidarity rights — peace, development, a healthy environment, and the common heritage of humanity.

## Sub lesson 2 — Rights on paper, rights in Haiti

The programme names the ineffectiveness of human rights in Haiti — the gap between the generations of rights as declared and as lived.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: human rights came in three generations — freedoms and political rights; social, economic and cultural rights; and solidarity rights — and the programme judges their ineffectiveness in Haiti honestly. Give one real example from your own life or from the Haiti you live in, and name the lesson's key words: human rights, generations, solidarity rights. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
