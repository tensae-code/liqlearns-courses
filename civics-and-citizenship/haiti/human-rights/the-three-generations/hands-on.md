# Hands-on — The Three Generations of Human Rights

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
List the rights you can name, then sort them: freedoms, life's necessities, and rights we hold together. This lesson teaches that sorting — and asks why the rights on paper are not the rights in life, in Haiti.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: human rights came in three generations — freedoms and political rights; social, economic and cultural rights; and solidarity rights — and the programme judges their ineffectiveness in Haiti honestly. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
