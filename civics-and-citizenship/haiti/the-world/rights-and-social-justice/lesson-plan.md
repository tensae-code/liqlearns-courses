# Lesson plan — Rights and Social Justice

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Haiti · **Title:** The World · **Lesson:** Rights and Social Justice
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — The World, the Environment and Taking Part (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the Universal Declaration of Human Rights is read and commented in class — and citizenship is tied to social justice, in values and in behaviour. This lesson stands on a teacher programme alone — evidence only, labelled as such.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The Universal Declaration, read** — The Universal Declaration of Human Rights is read and commented — the pupil meets the text itself, not only a summary of it.
2. **Citizenship and social justice** — The programme links citizenship to social justice, through the values and behaviours of citizenship education: a citizen's rights imply a fairer society.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Stands in path order after **Globalisation and Mondialité** — do that lesson first if this one feels hard (recommended, never forced).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: the Universal Declaration of Human Rights is read and commented in class — and citizenship is tied to social justice, in values and in behaviour.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, school or town — or, for this book's lessons, from the public life of Haiti they live in.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson is worked from the learner's own life in Haiti and the book's own teaching, as the book itself works.
