# Civics and Citizenship — Uganda course — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

Civics belongs to the country: this course is Uganda's own —
built from Uganda's History and Political Education Textbook,
Senior One (Lower Secondary Curriculum, prototype, student book),
with its Teacher's Guide standing inside two lessons as labelled
evidence. Inside the course the organisation is by **levels, not
grades**: the titles below ARE the levels, in path order — what
the learner proves, step by step. The grade the books come from
(Senior One) lives only in each lesson's sources.

The path's own logic is Uganda's: the combined book opens
political education inside identity and the family, before any
state — belonging widens from family to the cultures of the
region; peacemaking is taught from East Africa's own traditions,
at the centre of the book; and governance is introduced through
East Africa's own early states. That ladder IS the path order
(recommended, never forced — a learner can jump in higher; the
"start here instead" hints point down this ladder).

## The titles

- `identity-and-belonging/` — Level 1. What political education
  is and how it relates to history; identity through name, clan,
  tribe and ancestral roots; cultural values in families — the
  family as the source of moral being and discipline, respect
  for traditions and marriage, children's rights inside family
  decisions; and the roles of men, women and children, and how
  they are changing.
- `community-and-culture/` — Level 2. Cultural institutions in
  East Africa (Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda), cultural sites, and
  culture as a source of income and entrepreneurship.
- `peace-and-conflict/` — Level 3. East African traditions in
  conflict resolution — mediation, adjudication, reconciliation,
  arbitration and negotiation — and transitional justice after
  mass violations; and the Acholi approach: Mato Oput,
  restorative justice sealed by ceremony.
- `how-societies-govern-themselves/` — Level 4. State formation
  in East Africa: centralised states with one leader (Buganda
  named) against non-centralised clan-led societies, and the
  Bachwezi as an early model of governance.
