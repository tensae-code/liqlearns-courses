# Roles in the Family

## Do it first

Name one job you do in your family. Now name one job your mother or father does, and one a grandparent or elder does. Hold the three side by side — this lesson is about how a family's work is shared.

## Roles in the Family

In East African families, different people carry different roles. Men, women and children each have work that is theirs: providing, caring, cooking, farming, fetching, learning, guiding — the family's life is held up by all of them together.

No role stands alone. When one person's part is missing, the others feel it at once — that is why each role is respected, and why children learn their part early.

These roles are also changing. Work that once belonged to one person alone is now shared in new ways: girls and boys both go to school, women work outside the home, and men take up more of the care of children. The lesson asks learners to notice both what stays and what changes — and to value the people in every role.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: everyone in a family has a role — men, women and children each carry part of the family's life — and those roles are changing over time. Give one real example from your own home, school or neighbourhood, and name the lesson's key words: family roles, men, women, children, changing roles. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the opening act of this lesson again today, on purpose: repeat the Do it first act and notice what changes.
2. Write or draw the lesson's main teaching in your own words: everyone in a family has a role — men, women and children each carry part of the family's life — and those roles are changing over time.
3. Teach one part of this lesson to someone at home, and watch for one place this week where the lesson happens in real life. Note it.
