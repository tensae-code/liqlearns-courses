# Lesson plan — Cultural Values in Families

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Uganda · **Title:** Identity and Belonging · **Lesson:** Cultural Values in Families
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — Identity and Belonging (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the family is where moral being and discipline are first learned — through respect for traditions and marriage, and through children's rights being heard in family decisions.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The family as the source of moral being and discipline** — The family is the first source of moral being and discipline
2. **Respect for traditions and the marriage institution** — Families keep traditions with respect, and marriage is honoured as an institution that holds families together
3. **Children's rights inside family decisions** — Children have rights inside family decisions — their views are heard and their good is considered

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: What Political Education Is (the previous lesson in this title). Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: the family is where moral being and discipline are first learned — through respect for traditions and marriage, and through children's rights being heard in family decisions.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson's scenes are the learner's own home, school and neighbourhood.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner does the opening act of lesson/lesson.md before any explanation.
2. **The explanation** — the lesson's parts in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of a family at home together — elders, parents and children talking around one table, a family decision being made with the children listening and speaking — the student names who is in the picture and what each person is doing.
3. **Say it to your companion** — the learner explains the lesson's one thing and gives a real example (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-or-false, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the closing doing block (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks children's rights mean children decide everything → go back to the lesson's own words: the child's good matters and the child's view is heard, while parents keep their duty to guide and decide.
- Go back to the one thing: the family is where moral being and discipline are first learned — through respect for traditions and marriage, and through children's rights being heard in family decisions. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
