# Lesson plan — What Political Education Is

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Uganda · **Title:** Identity and Belonging · **Lesson:** What Political Education Is
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — Identity and Belonging (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** political education is learning how to live well in a civic society — and it starts with who you are: your name, your clan, your tribe and your roots.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What history studies** — History studies the past — how people lived, moved, traded and ruled long ago
2. **What political education studies** — Political education studies how to live well in a civic society, with good leadership as its model
3. **Who you are: name, clan, tribe and roots** — Identity begins with name, clan, tribe and ancestral roots

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- This is the first lesson of the course. Nothing is recommended before it.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: political education is learning how to live well in a civic society — and it starts with who you are: your name, your clan, your tribe and your roots.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson's scenes are the learner's own home, school and neighbourhood.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner does the opening act of lesson/lesson.md before any explanation.
2. **The explanation** — the lesson's parts in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of a young learner standing with family elders, a family tree drawn behind them joining a name, a clan and a homeland — the student names each part of the picture in the lesson's own words.
3. **Say it to your companion** — the learner explains the lesson's one thing and gives a real example (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-or-false, short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, matching — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the closing doing block (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Gives the words but no example → ask for the lesson happening once in their own home, school or street today; the example is the proof.
- Go back to the one thing: political education is learning how to live well in a civic society — and it starts with who you are: your name, your clan, your tribe and your roots. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
