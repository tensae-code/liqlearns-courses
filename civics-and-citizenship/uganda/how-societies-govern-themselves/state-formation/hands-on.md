# Hands-on — State Formation in East Africa

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Ask yourself: who settles a dispute in your home area, and who did it a hundred years ago? The answer has a history. This lesson goes back to how East Africa's peoples first organised themselves to be governed.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: before colonial rule, East Africans governed themselves in two great ways — centralised states under one leader, like Buganda, and non-centralised societies led by clans — with the Bachwezi remembered as an early model of governance. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
