# Lesson plan — State Formation in East Africa

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Uganda · **Title:** How Societies Govern Themselves · **Lesson:** State Formation in East Africa
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4 — How Societies Govern Themselves (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** before colonial rule, East Africans governed themselves in two great ways — centralised states under one leader, like Buganda, and non-centralised societies led by clans — with the Bachwezi remembered as an early model of governance.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 4 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Centralised states: one leader** — A centralised state gathers power in one leader and one government, as Buganda did
2. **Non-centralised societies: led by clans** — A non-centralised society has no single ruler; clans and elders lead together
3. **The Bachwezi: an early model of governance** — The Bachwezi are remembered as early rulers whose realm shows governance taking shape in the region

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## If the learner is stuck
- Calls non-centralised societies ungoverned → ask who settled disputes and made decisions there, until clans, elders and councils are named; spread-out government is still government.
- Go back to the one thing: before colonial rule, East Africans governed themselves in two great ways — centralised states under one leader, like Buganda, and non-centralised societies led by clans — with the Bachwezi remembered as an early model of governance. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
