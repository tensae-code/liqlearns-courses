# Civics and Citizenship — Uganda course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Uganda inside the Civics and Citizenship folder,
organised by **levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they
have proved the level, not when a school calendar says so. The
school grade the books come from (Senior One) appears only in
each lesson's sources (provenance), never as the course's
organisation. The path follows Uganda's own order: political
education opens inside identity and the family, widens to the
cultures of the region, centres on East Africa's own ways of
making peace, and only then asks how societies govern themselves
— through East Africa's own early states. The modern constitution
and state sit in later Senior years, whose books are not in hand;
the ladder honestly rests on the Senior One prototype pair alone.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this
course comes from government textbooks — this country's own
History and Political Education books — except the **Tip boxes**:
a tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are
ours; the lessons are the books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book
list, the PARTIAL combined-book scope, the UNREAD Senior Four
guide, and the acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **Identity and Belonging** — what political education is;
   identity through name, clan, tribe and ancestral roots; the
   family as the source of moral being and discipline, respect
   for traditions and marriage, children's rights inside family
   decisions; the roles of men, women and children, and how they
   are changing. 3 lessons, in one title:
   `identity-and-belonging/`.
2. **Community and Culture** — cultural institutions across
   Kenya, Tanzania and Uganda, cultural sites, and culture as a
   source of income and entrepreneurship. 1 lesson, in one
   title: `community-and-culture/`.
3. **Peace and Conflict Resolution** — mediation, adjudication,
   reconciliation, arbitration and negotiation as traditional
   mechanisms; transitional justice after mass violations
   (northern Uganda named); and the Acholi Mato Oput —
   restorative justice through respect, sincerity, forgiveness,
   reconciliation and discussion, sealed by its ceremony.
   2 lessons, in one title: `peace-and-conflict/`.
4. **How Societies Govern Themselves** — how pre-colonial
   societies organised themselves for governance: centralised
   states with one leader (Buganda named) against non-centralised
   clan-led societies, and the Bachwezi as an early model of
   governance. 1 lesson, in one title:
   `how-societies-govern-themselves/`.

**Totals: 7 lessons** (Level 1: 3 · Level 2: 1 · Level 3: 2 ·
Level 4: 1 — all from the Senior One student book, with
teacher-guide evidence inside two lessons).
