# About the Civics and Citizenship — Uganda course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this
course comes from government textbooks — this country's own
History and Political Education books for Senior One (Lower
Secondary Curriculum, prototype). The one exception is the
**Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Uganda inside the Civics and Citizenship
folder (one course per country). Inside it, the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found by what they
prove, and each level stands on the ones before it as a
recommended-first path — never a forced gate. The school grade the
books come from (Senior One) appears only in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

The course as built has **four levels**, following Uganda's own
order — political education opens inside identity and the family,
before any state:

1. **Level 1 — Identity and Belonging.** What political education
   is; identity through name, clan, tribe and ancestral roots;
   family values and family roles. All three lessons stand on the
   Senior One **student book**; one of them (Cultural Values in
   Families) also carries labelled teacher-guide evidence.
2. **Level 2 — Community and Culture.** Cultural institutions of
   East Africa, cultural sites, and culture as a livelihood.
   Student book.
3. **Level 3 — Peace and Conflict Resolution.** Traditional
   mechanisms — mediation, adjudication, reconciliation,
   arbitration, negotiation — transitional justice after mass
   violations (northern Uganda named), and the Acholi Mato Oput.
   Student book, with labelled teacher-guide evidence inside
   Traditional Conflict Resolution.
4. **Level 4 — How Societies Govern Themselves.** Pre-colonial
   state formation: centralised states (Buganda named) against
   non-centralised clan-led societies, and the Bachwezi as an
   early model of governance. Student book. The modern
   constitution and state sit in later Senior years, whose books
   are not in this wave's targets — the ladder honestly stops
   here (see Honesty notes).

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later; the
book's own shape, walked in order):

- *From who you are to how you are governed:* "Start with who you
  are — your name, your clan, your family and its values. Then
  widen: the cultures of East Africa and the institutions that
  keep them. Then learn how your peoples make peace — five old
  ways, and Mato Oput. Last, see how East Africans governed
  themselves long ago: kingdoms and clan societies, side by side."

## The ways of the books (their own shapes, preserved)

**Uganda's own way (as filed):** political education is taught as
living well in a civic society with models of good leadership
(the book's own framing), grounded in family and clan identity,
cultural institutions, and East Africa's own traditional ways of
making peace — above all the Acholi Mato Oput. The combined
subject's two strands were split for home-study printing; the
home-study volumes are the history half only.

**The teacher's guide's way** *(evidence)*: the guide teaches the
teacher to run political education through activity and
discussion — pairs and groups defining the subject themselves,
debating changing family roles, and working case studies of
traditional justice.
