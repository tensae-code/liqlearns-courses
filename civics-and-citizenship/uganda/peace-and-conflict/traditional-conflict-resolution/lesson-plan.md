# Lesson plan — Traditional Conflict Resolution

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Uganda · **Title:** Peace and Conflict · **Lesson:** Traditional Conflict Resolution
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — Peace and Conflict Resolution (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** East Africa's traditions settle conflict without courts first — by mediation, adjudication, reconciliation, arbitration and negotiation — and after mass violations, transitional justice rebuilds a broken society.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Five traditional ways of settling conflict** — Mediation, adjudication, reconciliation, arbitration and negotiation each settle conflict in their own way
2. **Transitional justice after mass violations** — After mass violations, transitional justice tells the truth, holds wrongdoers to account and rebuilds relations

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: East Africa's traditions settle conflict without courts first — by mediation, adjudication, reconciliation, arbitration and negotiation — and after mass violations, transitional justice rebuilds a broken society.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson's scenes are the learner's own home, school and neighbourhood.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes up mediation and arbitration → ask one question: who decides? A helper guiding the sides is mediation; a chosen judge whose decision both sides agreed to accept is arbitration.
- Go back to the one thing: East Africa's traditions settle conflict without courts first — by mediation, adjudication, reconciliation, arbitration and negotiation — and after mass violations, transitional justice rebuilds a broken society. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
