# Mato Oput

## Do it first

Imagine two families who cannot greet each other because of a grave wrong between them. What would have to happen before they could eat together again? Hold your answer — this lesson shows how the Acholi answer that question.

## Sub lesson 1 — The guiding principles

Mato Oput is the Acholi people's practice of restorative justice: justice that restores, rather than only punishing. It is used above all after a killing, when two clans stand divided by the gravest of wrongs.

Five principles guide it: **respect** for every person in the process; **sincerity** — the truth is spoken plainly; **forgiveness** — the wronged side is asked, in time, to let go of revenge; **reconciliation** — the goal is relations made right again; and **solving problems through discussion** — the matter is talked through, together, until it is settled.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Mato Oput ceremony

The process ends in a ceremony — Mato Oput means, in Acholi, drinking the bitter root. Elders lead both clans through the telling of what happened and the accepting of responsibility; compensation is agreed; then the two sides drink together from the same vessel, tasting the bitterness of the wrong and leaving it behind them.

After the ceremony, the clans are no longer enemies. They can greet, visit, trade and intermarry again. That is the justice Mato Oput seeks: not a winner and a loser, but a community made whole.
