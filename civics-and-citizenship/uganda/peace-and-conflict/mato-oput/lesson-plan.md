# Lesson plan — Mato Oput

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Uganda · **Title:** Peace and Conflict · **Lesson:** Mato Oput
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3 — Peace and Conflict Resolution (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Mato Oput is the Acholi way of restorative justice — after a killing, truth, responsibility and a shared ceremony restore relations between clans.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 3 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The guiding principles** — Respect, sincerity, forgiveness, reconciliation and solving problems through discussion guide Mato Oput
2. **The Mato Oput ceremony** — The ceremony joins the two clans in truth-telling, responsibility and a shared drink that seals restored relations

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: Mato Oput is the Acholi way of restorative justice — after a killing, truth, responsibility and a shared ceremony restore relations between clans.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson's scenes are the learner's own home, school and neighbourhood.
