# Hands-on — Mato Oput

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Imagine two families who cannot greet each other because of a grave wrong between them. What would have to happen before they could eat together again? Hold your answer — this lesson shows how the Acholi answer that question.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: Mato Oput is the Acholi way of restorative justice — after a killing, truth, responsibility and a shared ceremony restore relations between clans. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
