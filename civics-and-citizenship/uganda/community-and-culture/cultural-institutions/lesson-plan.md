# Lesson plan — Cultural Institutions

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Uganda · **Title:** Community and Culture · **Lesson:** Cultural Institutions
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2 — Community and Culture (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** cultural institutions keep a people's culture alive across East Africa — and their sites and crafts can earn a living, turning culture into work.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 2 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the lesson's one thing in their own words: cultural institutions keep a people's culture alive across East Africa — and their sites and crafts can earn a living, turning culture into work.
2. Give one real example of it from their own home, street or school.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson, without looking back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the lesson's scenes are the learner's own home, school and neighbourhood.

## If the learner is stuck
- Names a site but no institution → ask who keeps that place and leads its ceremonies; every living site has keepers, and the keepers are the lesson.
- Go back to the one thing: cultural institutions keep a people's culture alive across East Africa — and their sites and crafts can earn a living, turning culture into work. Work the Do it first act again, slowly, and name each step aloud.
