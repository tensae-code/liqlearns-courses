# Hands-on — World Religions by Numbers

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the opening act again
Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: World Religions by Numbers. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Pakistan's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Show it with real things
Take paper and a pencil — or real objects from your home — and show the lesson's main teaching: the world's religions compared — Zoroastrianism (small in number, strong in moral teaching: truthfulness, night-vigil?), Buddhism (about 500 million followers; its founder's search for the end of suffering), Christianity (the largest, about 2.6 billion), Islam (the second largest, about 1.8 billion), Sikhism (founded. Arrange the objects, draw the scene, or act it with someone at home. Say each part aloud as you place it.

## Watch for it for one day
For one whole day, watch for this lesson happening in real life — at home, at school, in the street, or in the public life of your town. Each time you see it (or see its opposite), note it: what happened, who was there, what the lesson would say about it. Bring your notes back to the lesson.
