# Civics and Citizenship — Pakistan — curriculum

One course for Pakistan inside the Civics and Citizenship
folder, built from Pakistan's own government textbooks:
the Punjab Curriculum and Textbook Board's Ethics
(Akhlaqiat) books for Classes 1–10 — the state's ethics
course taken by pupils of minority religions in place of
Islamiat — and its Civics (Shehriyat) books for Classes
11–12, together with the Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Textbook
Board's Civics books for Classes 11–12, all Urdu medium,
as filed in the two placements filings for this build
(`pakistan-placements.md` and `pakistan-placements-b.md` —
see ABOUT.md for the full book list, the verdicts, and
every label carried exactly as filed). The organisation
is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place is found
by what they prove. The path is recommended, never
forced — a learner can jump in higher; the "start here
instead" hints point down this ladder. The grades the
books come from appear only in provenance (ABOUT.md and
each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`).

The ladder follows Pakistan's own books' progression, as
the two filings' ladder sketches lay it out, reconciled
into one ladder: Pakistan's own course is two ladders
joined at Class 11. The Ethics ladder (Classes 1–10)
climbs by widening circles — the religions around us,
respect and truth (Level 1, Classes 1–3); values in
family, school and neighbourhood (Level 2, Classes 4–6);
society, rights and the good citizen (Level 3, Classes
7–10) — and the Civics ladder (Level 4, Classes 11–12)
then teaches the state itself: constitution, government
and citizenship.

## Level 1 — The Religions Around Us, Respect and Truth — 23 lessons

Level 1 rests on the Ethics (Akhlaqiat) books of Classes 1–3: the youngest pupils start from one Creator of the whole universe, a first list of moral values, and their rights and duties; they meet the religions of the classroom and neighbourhood — Hinduism, Sikhism, Judaism and Christianity taught from inside each faith — learn respect for parents, teachers and classmates and truthfulness through its story-exemplars, study the lives of righteous exemplars, and meet the national anthem as the first national text. Lessons: What Religion is · Moral Values · Respect for Human Life · Rights and Duties · Education for Everyone · Jesus · Moses · Qaumi Tarana · Hinduism · Sikhism · Judaism · Christianity · Respect for Parents Family and Siblings · Respect for Teachers and Classmates · Speaking the Truth · Shri Krishna Bhagwan · Sri Guru Nanak · How Religion Began · Nature Worship · Revealed Religions · Gods of Old Nations · Ibrahim the Journey · Ibrahim Righteousness and Sacrifice.

## Level 2 — Values in Family, School and Neighbourhood — 32 lessons

Level 2 rests on the Ethics books of Classes 4–6: values practised in family, school and neighbourhood — the world religions surveyed in turn (seven religions in one Class 4 chapter; Hinduism and Buddhism each as a full chapter; Sikhism given a whole chapter at Class 6), religion defined by its three parts, Pakistan’s shared festival calendar, respect for neighbours, elders, religions and humanity itself, cleanliness, helping others, punctuality, and the ethics course’s first talk of rules and law. Lessons: Why Religious Ethics Matter · Religion and Morals · Respect for All Life · Places of Worship Education Health · Seven Religions Survey · Hinduism in Depth · Buddhism in Depth · Honesty · Shri Krishna · Mahatma Buddha · What Religion Means · How Religions Began · The Great Prophets · Judaism at Class 5 · Christianity at Class 5 · Islam at Class 5 · Charter of Madina · Respect for Neighbours · Respect for Elders and Religions · Clean Environment and Helping Others · Punctuality · Dawud and Paul · Building Humanity · Moral Stories · Pearls of Knowledge · Sikhism Whole Chapter · Religious Festivals in Pakistan · Children and Family · Honesty at Class 6 · Respect for Humanity · Rules and Law · Maryam Pope Aquinas.

## Level 3 — Society, Rights and the Good Citizen — 33 lessons

Level 3 rests on the Ethics books of Classes 7–10: religion studied as a subject — definitions by named thinkers, Zoroastrianism, Jainism and Mahavira, Confucianism and Taoism studied whole, worship and its effects on behaviour, manners of worship places and public places, respect for law, and studied lives from Mirabai and Augustine to Socrates, Kant and Aurobindo — all of it framed, in the books’ own prefaces, by the 1973 Constitution’s guarantee of religious freedom and the Quaid’s 11 August promise. Lessons: Religion Defined · Religions and Human Progress · World Religions by Numbers · Zoroastrianism · Festivals of Pakistan · Punctuality is Honour · Honesty Everywhere · Manners of Conversation · Four Lives · Religions and Progress · Hinduism at Class 8 · Buddhism Christianity Islam Sikhism · Social Welfare and Character · Confucianism · Taoism · Festivals Janmashtami and Vesak · Respect for Law · Mirabai Augustine Lincoln Miskawayh · Religion and the Mind · Religion Society and Ethics · Jainism · Worship and Human Behaviour · Manners of Worship Places · Manners of Public Places · Ghazali and Others · Constitution and Minority Rights · How Thinkers Define Religion · Purposes of Religion · Religion Guides Problems · Sin and Dogma · Mahavira · Worships Effects and World Values · Socrates Kant Aurobindo.

## Level 4 — The State — Constitution, Government and Citizenship — 57 lessons

Level 4 rests on the Civics (Shehriyat) books of Classes 11–12 of both boards in hand — Punjab and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa. The Punjab Class 11 book founds the concepts (civics, the state, sovereignty, government, law, citizen and citizenship, constitution, political dynamics) and its Class 12 book turns them on Pakistan (the Movement and its ideology, constitution-making 1947–73, social services, social order and the police, national unity, and Pakistan in the world). The Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Class 11 book builds the theory (the political system, forms of government, rights, political economy, conflict and its resolution, academic skills, mass media and civil society, human values) and its Class 12 book applies it to Pakistan’s own system (its three organs, President and Prime Minister, police and bureaucracy), to children and women, to the world’s conflicts, and ends on the active, responsible citizen. Lessons: What Civics is · Nature and Use of Civics · Civics and Other Sciences · Basic Concepts · The State · Sovereignty · Government · Law · Citizen and Citizenship · Constitution · Political Dynamics · Ideology of Pakistan · Significance of Ideology · The Movement Milestones · Constitution Making 1947 73 · Systems and Citizenship · Education and Health · Order Discipline and Police · Unity and Integrity · Foreign Policy and World Bodies · State Community and Government · The Islamic State and the Constitution · Pakistan's 1973 Constitution · Monarchy Democracy Dictatorship · Islamic Government · Human Rights History and Types · Human Rights in Islam · Declarations and Conventions · Trade and Pakistan · ECO and Free Trade · Anatomy of Conflict · Resolving Conflict · Pakistan's National Conflicts · Inquiry and Issues · Critical Thinking and Propaganda · Media and Democracy · Civil Society and NGOs · Volunteering · Equality Tolerance and Inequality · Promoting Justice · Structure and Institutions · The Three Organs · President Premier Police and Bureaucracy · Children's Rights · Women's Rights · Global Finance and Trade · Economic Justice and Debt · Causes of Global Conflict · Resolving International Conflicts · Finding and Judging Information · Political Parties · Social Movements · Diversity in Pakistani Society · Accepting Diversity · Public Service · Community Service and Stewardship · Glossary of Terms.
