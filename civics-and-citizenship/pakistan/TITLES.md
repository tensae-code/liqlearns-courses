# Civics and Citizenship — Pakistan course — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

Civics belongs to the country: this course is Pakistan's
own — built from Pakistan's Ethics (Akhlaqiat) and Civics
(Shehriyat) books (Punjab Curriculum and Textbook Board,
Lahore, and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Textbook Board, Peshawar,
Urdu, exactly as filed). Inside the course the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: the titles
below ARE the levels' titles, in path order — what the
learner proves, step by step. The grades the books come
from live only in each lesson's sources.

The path's own logic is Pakistan's, from the two filings'
ladder sketches, reconciled into one ladder: two ladders
joined at Class 11 — the Ethics ladder climbing by
widening circles (the religions of the classroom and
neighbourhood, respect for all, truthfulness and its
story-exemplars; then values practised in family, school
and neighbourhood, the world religions surveyed in turn,
Pakistan's shared festival calendar, and the first talk
of rules and law; then religion studied as a subject,
framed by the 1973 Constitution's guarantee of religious
freedom and the Quaid's 11 August promise), and the
Civics ladder teaching the state itself (Class 11 founding
the concepts, Class 12 turning them on Pakistan; the
Khyber Pakhtunkhwa books building the theory in Class 11
and applying it to Pakistan's own system in Class 12,
ending on the active, responsible citizen). That ladder
IS the path order (recommended, never forced — a learner
can jump in higher; the "start here instead" hints point
down this ladder).

The coined lesson names in the filings were deduped
before building (see CURRICULUM.md, "Where the build
stands"): 151 placements stock 145
lessons. Every placement stocks into exactly one
lesson, and each placement's provenance row (book +
printed page, exactly as filed) is in that lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`. Unit folders are the filings' own
coined titles; a unit that a book returns to at a higher
level appears at each level it is stocked at, as
Pakistan's own books return to it.

The Punjab Class 11 book founds the concepts (civics, the state, sovereignty, government, law, citizen and citizenship, constitution, political dynamics) and its Class 12 book turns them on Pakistan (the Movement and its ideology, constitution-making 1947–73, social services, social order and the police, national unity, and Pakistan in the world). The Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Class 11 book builds the theory (the political system, forms of government, rights, political economy, conflict and its resolution, academic skills, mass media and civil society, human values) and its Class 12 book applies it to Pakistan’s own system (its three organs, President and Prime Minister, police and bureaucracy), to children and women, to the world’s conflicts, and ends on the active, responsible citizen.
