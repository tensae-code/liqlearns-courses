# About the Civics and Citizenship — Ecuador course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Educación para la Ciudadanía books. The one exception is
the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people
making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Ecuador inside the Civics and
Citizenship folder (one course per country). Inside it, the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place
is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the
ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced
gate. The school grades the books come from appear only in
each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

Ecuador's own way, preserved whole, as filed: a bachillerato
course that treats citizenship academically — citizenship's
history from Athens, equality as a natural and legal
condition, the three modern democracies, deliberation as the
mechanism that legitimises power (Bourdieu and Habermas are
cited by name in the first year's book), and Ecuador's own
democratic history: 20 constitutions, the constituent
assemblies, and the intercultural State of 2008. The second
year is held twice over — its student book climbs the same
arc deeper, while its teacher guide builds citizenship from
living together outward (coexistence and conflict, values,
norms, power, authority) and crowns the year with democracy
as a form of life and as a form of government. The third
year, held in its teacher guide, is rights-first and closes
on the Buen Vivir regime, Ecuador's own constitutional frame
of "living well".

The course as built has **three stocked levels**, from the
two filings for this build (`ecuador-placements.md` — 1
record / 1 distinct book, 28 placements; and
`ecuador-placements-b.md` — 1 census record and 23 link
leads triaged, 3 distinct books read, 22 placements; 50
placements in all, stocking 50 lessons — every placement
stocks into exactly one lesson):

1. **Level 1 — Citizenship, Equality and Democracy —
   Ecuador's Own Story (28 lessons).** The 1ro BGU student
   book's six sections in its own order: citizenship and
   human rights through history; equality as a natural and
   legal condition, with parity and the optional vote from
   Ecuador's Constitution; the modern democracies and the
   citizen's role in each; deliberation as the legitimation
   of power; consensus and dissent in everyday spaces; and
   Ecuador's own democracy — exclusion, first elections,
   interculturality, the national State, the constituent
   assemblies, 1998 against 2008, and republicanism.
2. **Level 2 — Citizenship Deepened — Deliberation, the
   State, and Living Together (16 lessons).** The 2do BGU
   year twice over: 10 lessons from the 2024 student book
   (citizenship through history, equality, the citizen in
   modern democracies, deliberation and consensus/dissent,
   Ecuador's democracy and the intercultural society, the
   national State, the People and constitutions, and
   republicanism) and 6 lessons from the Segundo curso
   teacher guide, marked TEACHER (coexistence and conflict,
   values, norms, power, authority, and democracy as life
   and government).
3. **Level 3 — Rights, Participation and Buen Vivir
   (6 lessons).** The Tercer curso year, on teacher's-guide
   evidence: rights and responsibilities, equality and
   diversity, constitutional rights and guarantees, politics
   and participation, the construction of citizenship, and
   the Buen Vivir regime.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later;
the books' own shape, walked in order):

- *From Athens to Buen Vivir:* "First, where citizenship
  came from: Athens, where it began as a privilege; the
  milestones — Magna Carta, 1776, 1789; the women's
  declaration that answered an exclusion; the generations
  of rights and the Universal Declaration. Then equality:
  natural and legal, formal and material, in the vote, in
  parity, in every way a citizen takes part. Then the
  democracies — direct, representative, deliberative — and
  deliberation itself, the reasoning that legitimises
  power, practised as consensus and dissent in everyday
  spaces. Then Ecuador: its excluded and its first
  elections, its intercultural society, its State, its
  twenty constitutions and its republic. Then the same
  ground one year deeper — and beside it, living together:
  conflict, values, norms, power, authority, democracy as a
  way of life. And last, rights and their guarantees,
  participation, citizenship built by practice — and Buen
  Vivir, living well, Ecuador's own word for what the whole
  course is for."

## Honesty notes (course level)

- **What the filings do and do not contain.** The filings
  contain 4 distinct read books (two student books read in
  full; two teacher guides read in full as TEACHER
  evidence), 50 placements, and the books' own contents
  maps. The filings do not contain any Ecuadorian civics
  book for the primary or lower-secondary years, any Tercer
  curso student book, or any book beyond the bachillerato
  Educación para la Ciudadanía course. Nothing of that kind
  is in this course, and no lesson pretends to one.
- **Guides-only footing, labelled, never hidden.** Level 3
  rests on a teacher guide alone, and 6 of Level 2's 16
  lessons rest on a teacher guide; every such lesson's
  sources say so and count 0 in the acceptance reckoning.
- **The guides' shifted file naming, carried exactly as
  filed.** As the second filing states, the ministry's own
  file naming is shifted: the file named
  "…Ciudadania-1…" is printed as the SEGUNDO curso guide
  (Book 2 here) and the file named "…Ciudadania-2…" as the
  TERCER curso guide (Book 3 here); both are filed and built
  here by their printed titles.
- **No wrong-file label is carried, because these filings
  carry none for these books.** Neither filing states a
  wrong-file or wrong-grade-body label for any of the four
  books above; nothing of that kind has been added here.
- **PARTIAL labels: none carried, because the filings carry
  none.** All four books are filed COMPLETE (Books 2–3
  COMPLETE as TEACHER evidence). Book 4's closing readings
  and annexes are filed inventory only — named above, not
  placed. No other PARTIAL label is carried, because the
  filings carry no other.
- **UNREAD books: none is filed as a book record.** No book
  in either filing is filed UNREAD. The Tercer curso student
  book is simply not among the filed books — Level 3
  therefore rests on its guide alone, as labelled above;
  if that student book is read later, Level 3 can be
  re-based on it. The duplicate leads for Book 1 in its
  several editions and mirrors (2016 Maya file, 2018
  currículo file, 2018/08 file, fascicle F2, Scribd "libro
  unificado", the 2023 combined Bachillerato file) are filed
  record-only in the group log and are not separate books.
- **Record-only leads, as filed.** The Sociales 8vo
  teacher-guide leads name a social-studies guide, not this
  subject — record-only, not placed. The LOTAIP transparency
  guide and the Proyectos Escolares instructivo leads are
  not textbooks — record-only, not placed.
- **The same ISBN on two books, carried exactly as filed.**
  Books 1 and 4 are both filed with ISBN 978-9942-662-37-8.
  That duplication is in the filings themselves and is
  carried as filed, not corrected here.
