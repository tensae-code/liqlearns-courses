# Civics and Citizenship — Ecuador course — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

Civics belongs to the country: this course is Ecuador's own —
built from Ecuador's Educación para la Ciudadanía books
(Ministerio de Educación: the 1ro and 2do BGU student books of
2024, and the Segundo and Tercer curso teacher guides,
Spanish). Inside the course the organisation is by
**levels, not grades**: the titles below ARE the levels'
titles, in path order — what the learner proves, step by
step. The grades the books come from live only in each
lesson's sources.

The path's own logic is Ecuador's, reconciled from the two
filings' ladder sketches: the first filing sketches a single
rung (the 1ro BGU student book's six sections, one complete
arc from the concept of citizenship to Ecuador's own
constitutional story) and the second filing places the 2do
BGU year above it — held twice over, in its 2024 student book
and in its teacher guide — with the Tercer curso guide as the
rung above that. So: Level 1 is the first year's arc
(citizenship and rights through history → equality →
the modern democracies → deliberation → consensus and
dissent → Ecuador's own democracy). Level 2 climbs the same
ground one year deeper in the student book (history →
equality → the citizen in each democracy → deliberation,
consensus and dissent → Ecuador's democracy and the
intercultural society → the national State, the People and
constitutions → republicanism) and, beside it, the guide's
own order from living together outward (coexistence and
conflict → values → norms → power → authority →
democracy as life and government). Level 3 is the Tercer
curso guide's rights-first order (rights and responsibilities
→ equality and diversity → constitutional guarantees →
politics and participation → building citizenship →
Buen Vivir). That ladder IS the path order (recommended,
never forced — a learner can jump in higher; the "start here
instead" hints point down this ladder). Levels 1 and the
student-book part of Level 2 stand on student books; the
guide part of Level 2 and all of Level 3 stand on teacher
guides — evidence only, acceptance 0, labelled in every
lesson's sources.

## The titles

**Level 1 — citizenship, equality and democracy — Ecuador's
own story** (the 1ro BGU student book's order):

- `citizenship-and-rights/` — Level 1. Citizenship in Athens
  (citizenship as a privilege tied to wealth and arms) ·
  Milestones Toward Human Rights (Magna Carta 1215, the
  Declaration of Rights 1689, 1776, 1789) · The Women's
  Rights Declaration · Generations of Rights · The Universal
  Declaration of Human Rights. The course opens with
  citizenship itself, through its history, because Ecuador's
  book opens there.
- `equality/` — Level 1. Citizen Equality, Natural and Legal
  · Equality and Equity (formal against material) · Universal
  Suffrage · Parity and the Optional Vote (Constitution
  Articles 65 and 62) · Forms of Participation. Equality
  follows rights as the book's second section does.
- `modern-democracies/` — Level 1. Forms of Government
  Compared · Democracy in Crisis · Government and Media
  Power · The Citizen's Role in Each Democracy.
- `deliberation/` — Level 1. Civil Rights and Kinds of Power
  · Deliberative and Social Democracy · Participation in
  School Life · Legitimating Political Power (Bourdieu and
  Habermas, as the book cites them).
- `consensus-and-dissent/` — Level 1. Equality of Thought ·
  The Main Rule of Deliberation · Building Consensus.
  Deliberation brought down to everyday spaces.
- `democracy-in-ecuador/` — Level 1. History and Exclusion ·
  First Elections and Accountability · The Intercultural
  Society · The National State · The People as Legitimator
  of Power · The 1998 and 2008 Constitutions · Republicanism
  in Ecuador. The level closes on Ecuador itself, as the
  book closes.

**Level 2 — citizenship deepened** (the 2do BGU year: the
2024 student book's order, then its teacher guide's order;
the guide's lessons are marked TEACHER in their sources):

- `citizenship-and-rights/` — Level 2. Citizenship Through
  History · Equality of Citizens. The first year's opening,
  climbed again at the second year's depth.
- `democracy/` — Level 2. The Citizen in Modern Democracies
  · Citizen Deliberation · Consensus and Dissent ·
  Democracy as Life and as Government (TEACHER — the
  guide's closing bloque, democracy taught as a form of life
  and as a form of government).
- `democracy-in-ecuador/` — Level 2. Ecuador's Democracy
  (including the indigenous movement) · The Intercultural
  Society — Building a Real Democracy.
- `the-state/` — Level 2. The National State and Social
  Control · The People and Constitutions (Montecristi
  included) · Republicanism and Other Forms. New at this
  level: the State as its own title.
- `living-together/` — Level 2 (TEACHER). Coexistence and
  Conflict · The Question of Values. The guide begins where
  life is lived: living with others.
- `norms-and-power/` — Level 2 (TEACHER). Norms · Power ·
  Authority. The guide's middle: the rules that bind, the
  power that acts, and authority against mere power.

**Level 3 — rights, participation and Buen Vivir** (the
Tercer curso teacher guide's order; teacher's-guide evidence):

- `rights/` — Level 3 (TEACHER). Rights and
  Responsibilities · Equality and Diversity · Constitutional
  Rights and Guarantees. Rights first, as the guide orders
  them.
- `participation/` — Level 3 (TEACHER). Politics and
  Participation · Building Citizenship.
- `buen-vivir/` — Level 3 (TEACHER). The Buen Vivir Regime
  (Sumak Kawsay). The course closes on Ecuador's own
  constitutional frame of living well — a topic only
  Ecuador's books teach, in its own words.
