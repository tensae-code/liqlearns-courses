# The Citizen's Role in Each Democracy

## Do it first

Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: the citizen's role in each democracy. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Ecuador's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Sub lesson 1 — Direct democracy

In direct democracy citizens decide matters themselves; the citizen's role is to take part in the decision directly.

## Sub lesson 2 — Representative democracy

In representative democracy citizens choose representatives to decide for them; the citizen's role is to choose well and to hold representatives to account.

## Sub lesson 3 — Deliberative democracy

In deliberative democracy citizens reason together about public matters before decisions are taken; the citizen's role is to argue, listen and be persuadable.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: The citizen's role differs in each form of modern democracy — direct, representative and deliberative — and each form has its own participation mechanisms, advantages, disadvantages and challenges. Give one real example from your own life or from the Ecuador you live in, and name the lesson's key words: citizen, role. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
