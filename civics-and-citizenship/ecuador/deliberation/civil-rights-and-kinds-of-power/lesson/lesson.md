# Civil Rights and Kinds of Power

## Do it first

Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: civil rights and kinds of power. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Ecuador's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Sub lesson 1 — What civil rights protect

Civil rights protect the person's own freedoms — the space in which no power may simply command the person.

## Sub lesson 2 — Kinds of power

Power is not one thing: there is absolute power, answerable to no one; everyday power, exercised in daily relations; and citizen power, the power citizens hold together.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Civil rights protect the person's freedoms, and power comes in kinds the book names: absolute power, everyday power, and citizen power. Give one real example from your own life or from the Ecuador you live in, and name the lesson's key words: civil, rights. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
