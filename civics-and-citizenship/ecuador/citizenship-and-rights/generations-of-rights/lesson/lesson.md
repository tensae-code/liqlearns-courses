# Generations of Rights

## Do it first

Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: generations of rights. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Ecuador's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Sub lesson 1 — The first generations

The first generation is civil and political rights; the second adds social rights — the conditions a person needs to live with dignity.

## Sub lesson 2 — Solidarity and the newest

The third generation is the rights of solidarity, held together with others, and a newest generation follows, answering new conditions of life.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Rights came in generations: civil and political rights first, then social rights, then the rights of solidarity, and a newest generation after them. Give one real example from your own life or from the Ecuador you live in, and name the lesson's key words: generations, rights. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
