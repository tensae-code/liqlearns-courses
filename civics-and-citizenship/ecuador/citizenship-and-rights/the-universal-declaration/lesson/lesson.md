# The Universal Declaration of Human Rights

## Do it first

Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: the universal declaration of human rights. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Ecuador's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Sub lesson 1 — What the Declaration proclaims

The Universal Declaration of Human Rights, of 10 December 1948, proclaims the rights of every person, everywhere, without distinction.

## Sub lesson 2 — Why violations persist

Violations persist despite the Declaration: naming a right is not the same as protecting it, and the book's challenge is to analyse a real case and say where the protection failed.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Human rights are the rights every person holds, proclaimed for all in the Universal Declaration of Human Rights of 10 December 1948 — yet violations persist despite it, and the book sets the learner to analyse a real case of a rights violation. Give one real example from your own life or from the Ecuador you live in, and name the lesson's key words: universal, declaration. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
