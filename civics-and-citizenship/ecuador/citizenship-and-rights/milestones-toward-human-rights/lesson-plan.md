# Lesson plan — Milestones Toward Human Rights

**Course:** Civics and Citizenship — Ecuador · **Title:** Citizenship and Rights · **Lesson:** Milestones Toward Human Rights
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1 — Citizenship, Equality and Democracy — Ecuador's Own Story (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The events that led to the declaration of human rights form a chain: the Carta Magna of 1215, the Declaration of Rights of 1689, United States independence in 1776, and the French Revolution of 1789. This lesson stands on a student book.
**This lesson's own level:** Level 1 — it stands on the lessons before it in path order (recommended, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The early limits on power** — The Carta Magna of 1215 and the Declaration of Rights of 1689 each limited a ruler's power and wrote down rights that could be claimed.
2. **Independence and revolution** — United States independence in 1776 and the French Revolution of 1789 carried the claim further: rights belong to people, and peoples may govern themselves.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
