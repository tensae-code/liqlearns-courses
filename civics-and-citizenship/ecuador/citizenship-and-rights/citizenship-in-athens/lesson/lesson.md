# Citizenship in Athens

## Do it first

Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: citizenship in athens. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Ecuador's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Sub lesson 1 — Who counted in Athens

At first a citizen of Athens was a man who could buy his own arms: citizenship followed wealth and the ability to fight, not simply being born in the city.

## Sub lesson 2 — The requirement falls

Over time the economic requirement fell, and citizenship widened beyond the men who could pay for arms.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Citizenship began as a privilege tied to wealth and arms — in Athens only men who could buy arms counted as citizens at first, and the economic requirement later fell. Give one real example from your own life or from the Ecuador you live in, and name the lesson's key words: citizenship, athens. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
