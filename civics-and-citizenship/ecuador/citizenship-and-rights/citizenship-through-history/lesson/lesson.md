# Citizenship Through History

## Do it first

Before anyone explains anything, say in your own words what you already think this is about: citizenship through history. Write it or say it aloud — this lesson starts from what Ecuador's own book teaches about it, and you will compare your first thought with the book's teaching as you go.

## Sub lesson 1 — From Athens to the revolutions

Citizenship begins with the armed citizen of Athens and is remade by Magna Carta, by the independence of 1776 and by the revolution of 1789.

## Sub lesson 2 — The generations of rights

Rights then arrive in generations — civil and political, social, and the rights of solidarity — each generation answering its time.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what this lesson teaches: Citizenship and human rights have a historical, social and political evolution: from the armed citizen of Athens through Magna Carta, 1776 and 1789, to the generations of rights. Give one real example from your own life or from the Ecuador you live in, and name the lesson's key words: citizenship, through. Your companion asks one question back — answer it from the lesson.
