# Civics and Citizenship — Ecuador course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Ecuador inside the Civics and Citizenship
folder, organised by **levels, not grades**: a learner climbs
when they have proved the level, not when a school calendar
says so. The school grades the books come from appear only in
each lesson's sources (provenance), never as the course's
organisation. The path follows Ecuador's own order as its
Educación para la Ciudadanía books lay it out — citizenship
and human rights through their history, equality as the human
being's natural and legal condition, the three modern
democracies and deliberation, consensus and dissent in
everyday spaces, and Ecuador's own democracy and constitution,
then the same ground climbed one year deeper, with living
together, norms, power and authority beside it, and at the top
rights and responsibilities, constitutional guarantees,
participation, the construction of citizenship and the Buen
Vivir regime. The filings for this build
(`ecuador-placements.md` and `ecuador-placements-b.md`) carry
50 placements from four distinct books read (two student
books and two teacher guides), and every placement stocks
into exactly one lesson, so the course holds 50 lessons.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Educación para la Ciudadanía books — except the **Tip
boxes**: a tip in a box is from us, the people making
LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'. See
ABOUT.md for the full book list and the acceptance
reckoning. **Level 3 of this course, and six lessons of
Level 2, rest on teacher guides alone and are labelled
guides-only throughout.**

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **Citizenship, Equality and Democracy — Ecuador's Own
   Story** — citizenship in Athens and the milestones toward
   human rights, the women's rights declaration, the
   generations of rights and the Universal Declaration;
   citizen equality as a natural and legal condition,
   equality against equity, universal suffrage, parity and
   the optional vote, and the forms of participation; the
   forms of government compared, democracy in crisis,
   government and media power, and the citizen's role in
   direct, representative and deliberative democracy;
   deliberation — civil rights and kinds of power,
   deliberative and social democracy, participation in
   school life, and how political power is legitimised;
   consensus and dissent — equality of thought, the main
   rule of deliberation, and building consensus; and
   Ecuador's own democracy — its history and exclusion, the
   first elections and accountability, the intercultural
   society, the national State, the people as legitimator
   of power, the 1998 and 2008 constitutions compared, and
   republicanism in Ecuador. 28 lessons, in six titles:
   `citizenship-and-rights/` (5 lessons), `equality/`
   (5 lessons), `modern-democracies/` (4 lessons),
   `deliberation/` (4 lessons), `consensus-and-dissent/`
   (3 lessons), `democracy-in-ecuador/` (7 lessons).
2. **Citizenship Deepened — Deliberation, the State, and
   Living Together** — the second bachillerato year, held
   twice over, as filed. From the 2024 student book:
   citizenship through history and equality of citizens;
   the citizen in modern democracies, citizen deliberation,
   and consensus and dissent; Ecuador's democracy and the
   intercultural society building a real democracy; and the
   national State and social control, the People and
   constitutions, and republicanism against other forms.
   From its teacher guide (marked TEACHER): coexistence and
   conflict, the question of values, norms, power,
   authority, and democracy as a form of life and as a form
   of government. 16 lessons, in six titles:
   `citizenship-and-rights/` (2 lessons), `democracy/`
   (4 lessons), `democracy-in-ecuador/` (2 lessons),
   `the-state/` (3 lessons), `living-together/` (2 lessons),
   `norms-and-power/` (3 lessons). **Six of this level's
   lessons rest on a teacher's guide alone — evidence only,
   acceptance 0.**
3. **Rights, Participation and Buen Vivir** — the third
   course's year, on teacher's-guide evidence: rights and
   responsibilities (worked through women's rights, gender
   stereotypes and equality, with Ecuador's Constitution as
   the reference), equality and diversity, and
   constitutional rights and guarantees; then politics and
   participation and the construction of citizenship; and
   last the Buen Vivir regime — Sumak Kawsay, Ecuador's own
   constitutional order of living well. 6 lessons, in three
   titles: `rights/` (3 lessons), `participation/`
   (2 lessons), `buen-vivir/` (1 lesson). **This level rests
   on a teacher's guide alone — evidence only, acceptance 0.**

**Totals: 50 lessons** (Level 1: 28 · Level 2: 16 ·
Level 3: 6 — from four read books: the Educación para la
Ciudadanía 1 student book (Ministerio de Educación, 2024,
COMPLETE), the Educación para la Ciudadanía 2 student book
(Ministerio de Educación, 2024, COMPLETE), and the Segundo
curso and Tercer curso teacher guides (Ministerio de
Educación, COMPLETE as TEACHER evidence); 50 placements
stocking 50 lessons).
