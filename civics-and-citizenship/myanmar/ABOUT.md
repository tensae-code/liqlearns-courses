# About the Civics and Citizenship — Myanmar course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own Morality and Civics
books.
The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is
from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the
lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Myanmar inside the Civics and
Citizenship folder (one course per country). Inside it, the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place
is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the
ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced
gate. The school grades the books come from appear only in
each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

Myanmar's civics-type course is Morality and Civics
(စာရိတ္တနှင့်ပြည်သူ့နီတိ), the Ministry of Education subject
whose series frame is constant, as filed: four learning
values — Social Ethics, Rights and Responsibilities,
Discipline (and Law), Peaceful Coexistence — carried across
four learning areas: oneself, others/family, community
(village/neighbourhood) and the natural environment
(world). Every book in hand runs that same four-chapter
frame, deepened each year.

Myanmar's own way, preserved whole, as filed: the first
book teaches civics almost entirely through named kinds of
person and small stories — the trustworthy person, the
empathetic person, the one who fulfils their duty — so the
citizen is a character before they are a rule-follower.
The guides' three-period rhythm — know the value,
understand the value, apply the value — is the series'
signature pedagogy. Grade 3 is where the series first
names modern civic content outright — using ICT properly,
caring for public goods, one's role and responsibility —
inside the same story-and-character method. Grade 4 opens
with fairness itself and is the first book in hand to name
"our rights" as a lesson. Grade 6 states the four-chapter
frame in its mature primary form, with new civic machinery
inside it: the school council, the importance of a
country's rules and laws, and peace studied as a subject
in itself, taught with Myanmar's own materials — the
bundle-of-firewood example for unity, the "Forever
Myanmar" song, a poem on grandparents' heritage. At
Grade 10 the same frame turns fully civic — human rights,
the Child Rights Law, elections and taxes, and peace for
the Union.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later;
the books' own shape, walked in order):

- *Myanmar's own order:* "First, character at home and at
  school: my family, helping parents and elders, politeness,
  not lying, honesty, trust, empathy, fairness and the
  pupil's duty — then the same lessons carried into school
  life, with discipline, the school garden and the purse
  returned to the headmaster. Then the four values made
  civic: caring for younger ones, using ICT properly,
  public goods, our rights, duty wherever you are placed —
  and the frame completed with the school council, the
  importance of rules and law, and peace and unity studied
  as subjects. Then the frame at secondary school: manners
  and empathy, children's right to education, the
  rule-respecting citizen, community affairs, and living
  together in peace. Last, the frame completed: social
  ethics and social rules, human rights, the Child Rights
  Law, elections, taxes, and peace for the Union. The
  grades not yet in hand — Grade 5, and Grades 8, 9, 11
  and 12 — are named in this course's About page, not
  filled in."
