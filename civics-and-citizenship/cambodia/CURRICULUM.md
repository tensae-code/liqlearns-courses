# Civics and Citizenship — Cambodia course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Cambodia inside the Civics and
Citizenship folder, organised by **levels, not grades**: a
learner climbs when they have proved the level, not when a
school calendar says so. The school grades the books come
from appear only in each lesson's sources (provenance),
never as the course's organisation. The path follows
Cambodia's own order as its books lay it out, from the
filing's ladder sketch (`cambodia-placements.md`):
Cambodia teaches one subject, សីលធម៌-ពលរដ្ឋវិជ្ជា
(Morality–Civics), in a repeating chapter frame — Human
Value, Clean Living, and Taking Part in Community/Social
Development — the same frame at Grades 11 and 12, going
deeper each year, with the Grade 10 detailed study
programme (the ministry framework, Book 3) laying out the
frame where it starts. Values and character first, then
belief, health and rights in daily life, then democracy,
law codes and the state. The filing stocks 104 placements
into 103 lessons: the coined lesson names were deduped
before building, by lesson name — every placement stocks
into exactly one lesson (one coined name, Creation, is
used by both the Grade 10 framework and the Grade 12 book
and stocks into one lesson with two sub lessons).

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this
country's own សីលធម៌-ពលរដ្ឋវិជ្ជា (Morality–Civics)
books — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is from
us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the
lessons are the books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book
list and the acceptance reckoning. **71 of the 103 lessons
stand on student books; 32 lessons stand on the Grades
10–12 curriculum framework alone (Book 3, programme
evidence — acceptance 0, labelled in every such lesson's
sources).**

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **The Person and Their Worth** — The Grade 10 framework opens the frame — concepts of philosophy, moral nature, creation, decision-making, change, promoting and protecting well-being, the good clean citizen, needs and rights, citizens' rights and duties, goals and means. The Grade 11 book then teaches what human value is and its kinds — bodily, economic, intellectual, moral and social value — before any institution is named: work traditions, moral teaching, good conduct, freedom, national unity, judgement, tolerance and self-knowledge, honesty, logic, solidarity. The Grade 12 book deepens the same ground philosophically: problems, creating new things, the pain of thought, human natural tendencies, resources, values and ethics, psychology and ethics, responsibility and discipline, virtue, rights and law, justice. Character before institutions — that is Cambodia's own order, both years. 31 lessons, in 1 title: `human-value/` (31 lessons).
2. **Living Clean Among Others** — The Grade 10 framework teaches civilisation, Khmerisation, Khmer beliefs and customs, national traditional festivals, gratitude to parents, mental health, and the harms of drinking alcohol and smoking. The Grade 11 book teaches religions as Cambodia teaches them — the origin of religion, Buddhist institutions and the state, pagodas and Buddhism in Cambodia, being a person in Theravada Buddhism, Mahayana Buddhism, Christianity, Islam and Taoism, practising religion in today's society — then clean living's dangers: the harms of drugs, of alcohol and cigarettes, and contagion and disease. The Grade 12 book turns to rights in daily life: the 38 blessings, harmony in communities, universal morality, the origin of human morality, international human-rights law, economic and social relations, the UN Declaration on the Right to Development, human rights from a Buddhist perspective, national rights in Cambodia, women's rights, children's rights, dissemination through media networks, and psycho-social problems. 32 lessons, in 1 title: `clean-living-and-belief/` (32 lessons).
3. **The Citizen in the State** — The Grade 10 framework teaches identity, self-respect and respect for others, family happiness, the peaceful community, roles duties and taking part, gender and finance, capital–provincial administration, youth and development, parents and heritage, conscience, Cambodia's judicial organisation, equality and inequality, peace and conflict, research, the census in Cambodia, and road use and traffic law. The Grade 11 book teaches democracy, citizens' rights, rights and responsibility, accountability in the community, and the law codes a citizen meets — labour law, marriage and family law, the Code of Civil Procedure, the Code of Criminal Procedure, land law — then public administration, political parties and regions, and invasion and strengthening relations. The Grade 12 book teaches the Kingdom's own machinery — the Royal Government, the legislative institutions, local administration, the judicial institutions — community problems and solutions, the importance of planning, how the Khmer govern the country, immigration law, nationality law, and the widening world: world economy, world environment, world citizens. 40 lessons, in 1 title: `taking-part-in-community-development/` (40 lessons).

**Totals: 103 lessons** (Level 1: 31 · Level 2: 32 · Level 3: 40 — from three read books: the *Morality–Civics* student books for Grade 11 and Grade 12 and the *Morality–Civics for Grades 10–12* curriculum framework (Ministry of Education, Youth and Sport (MoEYS), Khmer, all PARTIAL as filed); 104 placements stocking 103 lessons).

## Where the build stands

- **Course frame — BUILT** (this CURRICULUM, TITLES.md,
  ABOUT.md, and the folder README entry).
- **Levels 1–3 — BUILT**: 103 lessons, stocked from 104
  placements in the three read books (Grade 11 and
  Grade 12 student books; the Grade 10 lesson map from
  the Grades 10–12 curriculum framework, Book 3).
- **Not built, named plainly**: the census holds only
  Grades 10–12 for Cambodia — the ladder below Level 1
  (primary morality) is not in hand in this wave, as
  filed. Grade 10 is held only as the MoEYS detailed
  study programme (Book 3, framework/teacher evidence) —
  no Grade 10 pupil textbook is in hand. The framework's
  Grade 11 and Grade 12 lesson lists (34 and 35 lessons)
  differ from the in-hand textbooks and are named in
  Book 3's contents map but not separately placed — the
  textbooks govern those grades. One Grade 10 lesson
  (Ch. 3 L5) has a title that does not extract from the
  legacy-font text layer and is not placed. Nothing else
  is in the course, and no lesson pretends to one (see
  ABOUT.md).
