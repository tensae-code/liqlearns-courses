# About the Civics and Citizenship — Cambodia course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in
this course comes from government textbooks — this country's
own សីលធម៌-ពលរដ្ឋវិជ្ជា (Morality–Civics) books.
The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a box is
from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the
lessons are the books'.

## How this course is organised

This is ONE course for Cambodia inside the Civics and
Citizenship folder (one course per country). Inside it, the
organisation is by **levels, not grades**: a learner's place
is found by what they prove, and each level stands on the
ones before it as a recommended-first path — never a forced
gate. The school grades the books come from appear only in
each lesson's `lesson/sources.md`, as provenance.

Cambodia's own way, preserved whole, as filed: one subject,
សីលធម៌-ពលរដ្ឋវិជ្ជា (Morality–Civics), in three repeating
chapters — Human Value, Clean Living, and Taking Part in
Community/Social Development — the same three-chapter frame
in Grades 11 and 12, going deeper each year: values and
character first, then belief, health and rights in daily
life, then democracy, law codes and the state. Every lesson
opens with an objectives box ("at the end of this lesson,
students can…") and teaches by definition, typology lists,
and Cambodian examples. The Grade 10 detailed study
programme (Book 3) states the frame in a four-chapter form —
human value, good relations in the community, culture and
religion, our society — and Grade 10 is where the frame
starts: philosophy concepts, decision-making and change
first, then identity, family happiness and gender-and-finance,
then Khmer civilisation and belief, then conscience, the
courts, equality, peace and the census.

**Course-intro video script** (written here, made later;
the books' own shape, walked in order):

- *Cambodia's own order:* "First, the person and their
  worth — what a human life is worth, its kinds, honesty,
  freedom, unity, judgement, logic and solidarity, deepened
  into problems, creation, virtue and justice. Then living
  clean among others — the faiths as Cambodia teaches them,
  Theravada first, the harms of drugs, alcohol and disease,
  and rights in daily life: women's rights, children's
  rights, human rights in law and in Buddhist teaching.
  Then the citizen in the state — democracy, citizens'
  rights, the law codes a citizen meets, the Royal
  Government, the legislature and the courts of the
  Kingdom, nationality and immigration law, and at the end
  the world citizen."
