# Civics and Citizenship — Laos course — CURRICULUM (the path)

One course for Laos inside the Civics and Citizenship folder,
organised by **levels, not grades**: a learner climbs when they
have proved the level, not when a school calendar says so. The
school grades the books come from appear only in each lesson's
sources (provenance), never as the course's organisation. The
path follows Laos's own books' progression, as sketched at the
end of the filing: primary school teaches ຄຸນສົມບັດສຶກສາ
(Moral Education) in widening circles — myself, family, school,
community, nation, world — one small duty or virtue per lesson;
secondary school teaches ສຶກສາພົນລະເມືອງ (Civic Education)
as the citizen's formation: character and self-mastery,
democracy and peace, then rights and duties named sphere by
sphere (marriage, labour, the law), and the youth's mission
toward the nation and the world.

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this
course comes from government textbooks — this country's own
ຄຸນສົມບັດສຶກສາ (Moral Education) and ສຶກສາພົນລະເມືອງ
(Civic Education) books — except the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a
box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are ours; the
lessons are the books'. See ABOUT.md for the full book list,
every gap and label carried exactly as filed, and the
acceptance reckoning.

## The levels (the path, in order)

1. **Myself, Family and School** — the child's own conduct first, following the filing's ladder sketch (Grades 1–2, reaching into 3–5) — being a pupil and keeping the school's rules, diligence, honesty, thrift, courage and keeping time, then family, friends and manners. The circle starts at the child's own desk and door. 101 lessons (182 placements filed at the level).

2. **Community, Nation and World** — the circle widened (Grades 3–5, prepared in 1–2) — neighbours and the community and its public property, the nation and its benefactors, culture and traditions, the world (peace, the United Nations, solidarity), the environment and resources, and the first rights named (children's rights, the right to protection, respecting others' rights). Primary school ends with the widest circle already drawn. 34 lessons (66 placements filed at the level).

3. **Character and Relations** — secondary civics re-founds character before it teaches the state (Grades 6–7, with Grade 9's opening character lessons filed at this level) — justice and self-mastery, diligence and endurance, honesty, solidarity and human relations, democracy and peace, the cultured family, cultural heritage and living with nature. 44 lessons (57 placements filed at the level).

4. **Rights and Duties Named** — rights arrive as law, not as slogans (Grades 6–9) — the child-rights convention, legal protection, the inviolable home, confidentiality, traffic law, freedom of belief, rights in the Lao PDR, and the citizen's rights and duties named sphere by sphere: marriage, labour, and responsibility toward the law. 20 lessons (29 placements filed at the level).

5. **Worldview, Economy, the State and Law** — the upper-secondary band (Grades 9–12) — Grade 9's nation-and-world lessons and the youth's mission, Grade 10's materialist worldview and morality, Grade 11's economy and the state and its policies field by field, closing with Grade 12's law and living: what law is, observing it, and law's part in citizens, the nation, peace and human progress. 41 lessons (78 placements filed at the level).

6. **The Citizen's Basic Rights and the Regime** — the summit (Grade 12, second half) — equality in social life, equality before the law, equality among ethnic groups and religions, and citizens' basic freedoms. The ladder ends where it aimed from Grade 1: the citizen standing equal under law. 4 lessons (10 placements filed at the level).
