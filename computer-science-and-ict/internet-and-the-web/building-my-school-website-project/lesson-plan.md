# Lesson plan — Building My School Website — the Digital Project

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Internet and the Web · **Lesson:** Building My School Website — the Digital Project
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Egypt (2026-11-14) — in the title Internet and the Web on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Egypt's student books (Egypt Ministry of Education and Technical Education / Ministry of Education), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Pupils, parents, teachers and visitors.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Planning the school's site — audience and pages** — A school website serves named **audiences** — pupils, parents, teachers, visitors — and planning starts from their questions: What are the term dates? Where is the school? What did the teams win? The plan lists the **pages** those answers need, the order a visitor meets them, and what each page must hold, applying the unit's design principles — clear navigation, consistency between pages, simplicity, and accessibility for every reader. Egypt makes the project the school's own because a real audience disciplines every choice: a page nobody can find, for a school everybody knows, is a fault its maker can see.
2. **Building and judging the project** — Building follows the plan page by page in the **format** the unit taught — the HTML structure under every page, headings, text, pictures and links in their places — and the project is **judged as a whole**: does the visitor reach every answer in a few clicks; do the pages look like one site; does it open correctly and read clearly on the devices its audience actually has? The unit's final lesson then re-does this project with AI help, so this first build is deliberately by hand: a pupil who has built a site unaided can judge what the machine later offers, and a pupil who has not, cannot. That is Egypt's order, and this lesson keeps it.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Pupils, parents, teachers and visitors.
2. The audiences' questions — dates, place, news — that the site must answer.
3. The HTML structure the unit taught — headings, text, pictures and links in their places.
4. As a whole — reachability in a few clicks, one-site consistency, correct opening on real devices.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil, and a computer with the program this lesson uses, if available.

1. **Do it first** — Egypt's Grade 8 website unit ends by spending everything it taught: after design principles and page format comes Lesson 3, My Digital Project for My School — a real website for a real school, planned, built and judged as a project, not an exercise. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of planning the school's site — audience and pages as the lesson describes it — the student studies the picture and names what each part shows.

   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the lesson's key names and definitions aloud, in the order the lesson gives them — the student listens once through, then reads the same lines aloud along with the voice. (Audio is a described slot in this build; no audio file is made.)

4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-false, matching, short-answer — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
