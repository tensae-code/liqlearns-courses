# Building My School Website — the Digital Project

## Do it first

Egypt's Grade 8 website unit ends by spending everything it taught: after design principles and page format comes Lesson 3, My Digital Project for My School — a real website for a real school, planned, built and judged as a project, not an exercise.

## Sub lesson 1 — Planning the school's site — audience and pages

A school website serves named **audiences** — pupils, parents, teachers, visitors — and planning starts from their questions: What are the term dates? Where is the school? What did the teams win? The plan lists the **pages** those answers need, the order a visitor meets them, and what each page must hold, applying the unit's design principles — clear navigation, consistency between pages, simplicity, and accessibility for every reader. Egypt makes the project the school's own because a real audience disciplines every choice: a page nobody can find, for a school everybody knows, is a fault its maker can see.

## Sub lesson 2 — Building and judging the project

Building follows the plan page by page in the **format** the unit taught — the HTML structure under every page, headings, text, pictures and links in their places — and the project is **judged as a whole**: does the visitor reach every answer in a few clicks; do the pages look like one site; does it open correctly and read clearly on the devices its audience actually has? The unit's final lesson then re-does this project with AI help, so this first build is deliberately by hand: a pupil who has built a site unaided can judge what the machine later offers, and a pupil who has not, cannot. That is Egypt's order, and this lesson keeps it.
