## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Examples of websites — the kinds** — Websites sort by their **work**: **informational** (news, government, school sites — to tell), **commercial** (shops and company sites — to sell and serve), **educational** (courses and encyclopedias — to teach), **communication and social** (mail, forums, platforms — to connect), **entertainment** (video, games, music — to delight), and **service** sites (banking, booking — to do errands). Most real sites mix kinds; the sort is by the main work, because the work decides the design — the unit's later lessons all begin from a site's kind.
2. **The languages used to build them** — A website is built in layers of language: **HTML** — the structure and content (this unit's own work), **CSS** — the presentation (colour, layout, type), and **JavaScript** — the behaviour (response, movement, checking), with **server-side languages** behind dynamic sites (building pages on request, holding the data). The pupil is not asked to master them here; the lesson's gift is the map: when a page is plain, styled or alive, the pupil can now say **which language is speaking** — and knows what the rest of their web learning is for.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
