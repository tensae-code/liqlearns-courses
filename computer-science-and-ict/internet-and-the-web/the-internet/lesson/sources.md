**Acceptance count: taught in 24 countries (Ethiopia, Turkmenistan, Cambodia, Mozambique, Nepal, China, Afghanistan, Yemen, North Macedonia, Iraq, Sudan, Uzbekistan, Cuba, Egypt, Fiji, Bhutan, Libya, Mongolia, Sri Lanka, Rwanda, Laos, Greece, Moldova, Jordan).**

Yemen's own way (Grade 11): the book pivots from operating the machine to understanding information itself — data kinds, number systems and coding get full units before the applications (presentations, Excel) and a networks/Internet finale; every unit closes with a formal تقويم (assessment).

Afghanistan's own way (Grade 10): a Windows XP-era office apprenticeship — one full chapter per application, taught menu by menu (File, Edit, Insert, Format), with the Dari keyboard language added as its own lesson; networks arrive only in the final chapter.

Afghanistan's own way (Grade 12): the final year turns outward — computers in society (e-commerce, communications, even military systems), system software and virus protection, then Excel for the first time and a closing block on the Web, e-mail and browsers (taught through Internet Explorer).

Growth note (Wave 3, band B2): this lesson was GROWN, not replaced — Sub lesson 1 is the original Grade 7 lesson, unchanged in content; Sub lesson 2 is the Grade 8 depth added from the Grade 8 book. The Grade 8 student book's glossary defines a modem as connecting a computer "to the intranet" — a glossary fault. This lesson teaches the book's unit-text meaning, that a modem joins a computer to the internet, and flags the glossary wording here, as the placements direct. The teacher guide's §4.1 also carries a wrong cross-reference (it sends the teacher to "page 7" of the guide for a topic the guide itself treats on printed pp. 35–36); the method row above cites the guide's real pages. The guide's Unit 4 outcomes also print "escribe" for "describe" — a typo, taught as nothing.

North Macedonia's own way (Year 1): five themes move from modern digital technology and online living into a full structured-programming course taught in C++ (with Scratch/C++/Java/Python/PHP/Lisp compared), then advanced Word work (styles, contents, templates) and spreadsheets up to pivot tables.

Iraq's own way (Grade 7): a four-unit spiral repeated at every grade — computer fundamentals, computer software (here: the operating system plus an unusually deep treatment of Windows right-click shortcut menus), algorithms and programming principles (ending in Scratch), and information technology and the internet — every lesson built on the Iraqi five-step learning cycle with opening activity, main idea, learning outcomes, vocabulary and lesson review.

Sudan's own way (Grade 7): the middle-stage book spends half its length on one database built step by step in Microsoft Access (tables, relationships, forms, queries, reports on a pupils-and-subjects database), then teaches the internet through Sudanese examples — the DNS lesson resolves the Khartoum Ministry of Education site (moekh.gov.sd).


Uzbekistan's own way (Grade 9): grade 9 is the capstone — computer graphics, Access, Excel, then a nine-lesson algorithms block and a 28-lesson Turbo Pascal 7.0 course that runs from the alphabet of the language to files, procedures and graphics mode, closing with the Internet. Every chapter ends with a dedicated repetition lesson, placed here to that chapter's main lesson.

Cuba's own way (Grade 7): Secondary Informática opens by contrasting proprietary Windows with Cuba's free operating system Nova; programming continues the primary Scratch line, here centred on the Scratch pen tool for drawing geometric figures and the linear function; word-processor work ends by exporting documents to HTML for the web.

Cuba's own way (Grade 12): The final pre-university year is networks in depth (types, topologies, protocols, Internet services, Web 2.0/Web 3.0 and social networks), then teleformación (distance learning) through the Moodle platform, and bibliographic managers for citations in scientific research — a university-preparation finish.

Egypt's own way (Grade 10, Term 1 Programming and Artificial Intelligence student book): A full Japanese-style "Information Study" course adopted for Egypt's new secondary track: 13 chapters running from information theory, ethics and security through number systems, data analysis (frequency, variance, correlation, regression in spreadsheets), simulation and queues, Python, and a final AI-programming project chapter building web pages, a quiz app and a block-breaker game with generative AI.

Egypt's own way (Grade 5, Term 2 student book (Arabic)): Primary ICT is organised as themed units — "The Digital Citizen in the Internet World" then "My Digital Project" — with every lesson phrased as a journey or a how-to question.

Fiji's own way (Year 12 Computer Studies student book): Written locally by the same TEST/USP panel for the trialled Year 12 syllabus; the Internet chapter is grounded in Fiji (fiji.gov.fj URL anatomy with the .fj country code, and Fiji ISPs Connect Fiji, Unwired Fiji, Vodafone, Digicel and Kidanet by Fintel), programming is a full Visual Basic course with hands-on examples, and data management is taught as a move to cloud storage (Google Drive, Dropbox, SpiderOak).

Bhutan's own way (Class IV, Literacy with ICT student book): Competency-based "Literacy with ICT" (not procedure drill): every chapter opens with learning objectives, mixes off-machine "Try This" with "Try This On Machine", and closes with Now You Know / Check Your Progress / Explore Further; content is localised (Bhutanese names, kuenselonline, education.gov.bt, Bhutan's 1999 Internet start) and one class per week across the year.

Bhutan's own way (Class VI, Literacy with ICT student book): School computing runs on Google's G Suite for Education — email is Gmail on the school account, collaboration is Google Classroom, and the end-of-year food-chain project is agreed in Classroom, built in Word, password-protected, cited, filed in folders and reported to the teacher by email.

Libya's own way (Grade 9, Computer student book): the whole year is networks and the internet — a full networks unit (types, cables, topology, components), then Internet Explorer and Google search taught as named skills, then email and a closing tour of social sites (Facebook, YouTube, Twitter, Instagram, Yahoo) with practical activities.

Libya's own way (Grade 11 Term 2, Information Technology student book): Term 2 is web authoring from first principles — internet architecture and protocols, then hand-written HTML (structure, text formatting, links, lists, tables), then publishing a site properly (domain, hosting, FTP) and three full website projects. The book is printed for BOTH the Science and Humanities sections, although the records label it Humanities only — cited as printed.

Libya's own way (Grade 11 Term 2, Information Technology teacher guide): each web chapter is taught as target outcomes + a chapter summary + scripted lesson steps (an introduction and set activities — e.g. pupils role-play the devices of a network) + answers to the chapter questions. The guide is printed for both the Science and Humanities sections. (teacher guide — evidence)

Mongolia's own way (Grade 6): ICT is built on Mongolian examples throughout — information and objects taught through the Mazaalai (Gobi bear), the ger (with a paper origami model), Mongolian pattern (khee) motifs and the national flag; touch typing is taught on the Mongolian Cyrillic keyboard layout with local tutor programs (MonTT, Fast type); web work uses Mongolian sites (surguuli.edu.mn, kids.gogo.mn, tsag-agaar.mn).

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 6 Reading Book): a story/dialogue-led "Reading Book" with a talking computer character; every chapter cross-refers to numbered Workbook activities; local sectors (school office, ATM, ICU, factories, agriculture) and the 3R e-waste method are taught from Grade 6.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 7 Reading Book): the CPU is taught through a hotel-kitchen/chef analogy; programme development (decomposition, flowcharts, variables, bugs) precedes presentation software; HTML page-building is already in Grade 7.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 7 Workbook): a companion workbook mirroring the Reading Book chapter-for-chapter (CPU, OS, security, word processing, program development, presentations, internet).

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 11, Tamil): a full Visual Basic 6.0 programming course inside the O/L book (IDE, controls, data types, Select Case, arrays), an SDLC chapter, Photoshop-based multimedia, hand-coded HTML with Sri Lankan examples (the Sinharaja rainforest page, ucsc.cmb.ac.lk addresses, sltnet.lk domains), and sector chapters (agriculture, industry, government, commerce) before malware and health.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 12 Science for Technology, Tamil): the A/L technology-stream ICT volume teaches every office skill twice — once in Microsoft Office 2010 and once in LibreOffice (Writer/Calc/Impress 4.1) — and closes with creating a real Gmail account, mailbox folders, attachments and password safety; hardware goes down to cache levels L1/L2/L3, ROM types and file systems (FAT/NTFS/ext4).

Rwanda's own way (S1 ICT Student Book, KLB): competency-based units each opening with a Key Unit Competency and keywords; a full GIS/ArcGIS unit and a Scratch music-making unit sit inside the first secondary year, and ICT use is grounded in Rwandan daily life.

Laos's own way (Grade 8 ICT student book): a full PC-technician year in full colour — learners take a computer apart and rebuild it (CPU, RAM, board, cards, drives), install Windows XP and drivers, fix install faults by BIOS beep codes, then finish with networks and the internet.

Greece's own way (Grade 7, Pliroforiki A' Gymnasiou, 2024): the new Gymnasium informatics course is built around algorithms from the first page (a Python binary example in Unit 1), then hardware/software, the internet with a full copyright/Creative Commons/AI-copyright sequence, cybersecurity with cryptography, word processing, algorithmics, Scratch storytelling/chatbot projects, and a long society unit (robots, drones, autonomous cars, e-government, AI in education/medicine).

Greece's own way (Grade 10, Efarmoges Pliroforikis A' Lykeiou): the older Lykeio applications book in four thematic units — hardware/software and applications, programming environments and creating applications (life cycle, modern IDEs, mobile-app and 3D object-oriented programming), communication and the internet (HTML5/CSS, learning online), and collaboration and safety online (cloud applications, telework, social networks, privacy).

Moldova's own way (Grade 11, Informatică): Grade 11 is the theory year — how numbers, logic and circuits actually work inside the machine (Boolean algebra, adders, flip-flops, instruction formats, assembly language) — with structured programming (arrays, records, sets, files) carried over from Grade 10's languages; some sections are profile-specific (Real/Umanist).

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 7 G7T1 Student): Grade 7 starts the whole subject from zero — what a computer is and how it works, hardware inside and out, software, operating systems and human-computer interaction — then networks from definition through topologies to secure communication and network performance.
