**Acceptance count: taught in 12 countries (Ethiopia, Turkmenistan, Cambodia, Mozambique, Nepal, China, Afghanistan, Yemen, North Macedonia, Iraq, Sudan, Uzbekistan).**

Yemen's own way (Grade 11): the book pivots from operating the machine to understanding information itself — data kinds, number systems and coding get full units before the applications (presentations, Excel) and a networks/Internet finale; every unit closes with a formal تقويم (assessment).

Afghanistan's own way (Grade 10): a Windows XP-era office apprenticeship — one full chapter per application, taught menu by menu (File, Edit, Insert, Format), with the Dari keyboard language added as its own lesson; networks arrive only in the final chapter.

Afghanistan's own way (Grade 12): the final year turns outward — computers in society (e-commerce, communications, even military systems), system software and virus protection, then Excel for the first time and a closing block on the Web, e-mail and browsers (taught through Internet Explorer).

Growth note (Wave 3, band B2): this lesson was GROWN, not replaced — Sub lesson 1 is the original Grade 7 lesson, unchanged in content; Sub lesson 2 is the Grade 8 depth added from the Grade 8 book. The Grade 8 student book's glossary defines a modem as connecting a computer "to the intranet" — a glossary fault. This lesson teaches the book's unit-text meaning, that a modem joins a computer to the internet, and flags the glossary wording here, as the placements direct. The teacher guide's §4.1 also carries a wrong cross-reference (it sends the teacher to "page 7" of the guide for a topic the guide itself treats on printed pp. 35–36); the method row above cites the guide's real pages. The guide's Unit 4 outcomes also print "escribe" for "describe" — a typo, taught as nothing.

North Macedonia's own way (Year 1): five themes move from modern digital technology and online living into a full structured-programming course taught in C++ (with Scratch/C++/Java/Python/PHP/Lisp compared), then advanced Word work (styles, contents, templates) and spreadsheets up to pivot tables.

Iraq's own way (Grade 7): a four-unit spiral repeated at every grade — computer fundamentals, computer software (here: the operating system plus an unusually deep treatment of Windows right-click shortcut menus), algorithms and programming principles (ending in Scratch), and information technology and the internet — every lesson built on the Iraqi five-step learning cycle with opening activity, main idea, learning outcomes, vocabulary and lesson review.

Sudan's own way (Grade 7): the middle-stage book spends half its length on one database built step by step in Microsoft Access (tables, relationships, forms, queries, reports on a pupils-and-subjects database), then teaches the internet through Sudanese examples — the DNS lesson resolves the Khartoum Ministry of Education site (moekh.gov.sd).


Uzbekistan's own way (Grade 9): grade 9 is the capstone — computer graphics, Access, Excel, then a nine-lesson algorithms block and a 28-lesson Turbo Pascal 7.0 course that runs from the alphabet of the language to files, procedures and graphics mode, closing with the Internet. Every chapter ends with a dedicated repetition lesson, placed here to that chapter's main lesson.
