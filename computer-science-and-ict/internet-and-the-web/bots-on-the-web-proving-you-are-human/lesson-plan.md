## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The web's other visitors** — A large share of web traffic is not people but **bots (les “robots”)** — programs that visit pages automatically. Good bots do useful work: search engines' crawlers read the web to build their indexes. Bad bots scrape addresses, test stolen passwords, flood forms and hunt weaknesses. The activity's sentence is the one to keep: **there are not only humans on the web** — every site must decide, visit by visit, who is knocking.
2. **Proving you are human** — Sites defend themselves with **humanity tests**: distorted letters to read, pictures to pick, boxes to tick — tasks easy for a person and (for now) hard for a program. When you meet one while connecting to a protected site, you are watching the defence work: the site is checking that a person, not a password-testing bot, is asking to enter. The tests annoy — and their annoyance is the receipt that the border is guarded.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — A website just asked you to click every picture with a bus in it. Why would a website doubt you are a person? Before reading on, name one thing a program might want from a website that a person would not. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of the web's other visitors as the lesson describes it — the student studies the picture and names what each part shows.
