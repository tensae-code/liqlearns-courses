# Video script — Bots on the Web — Proving You Are Human

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

A large share of web traffic is not people but **bots (les “robots”)** — programs that visit pages automatically. Good bots do useful work: search engines' crawlers read the web to build their indexes. Bad bots scrape addresses, test stolen passwords, flood forms and hunt weaknesses. The activity's sentence is the one to keep: **there are not only humans on the web** — every site must decide, visit by visit, who is knocking.
Sites defend themselves with **humanity tests**: distorted letters to read, pictures to pick, boxes to tick — tasks easy for a person and (for now) hard for a program. When you meet one while connecting to a protected site, you are watching the defence work: the site is checking that a person, not a password-testing bot, is asking to enter. The tests annoy — and their annoyance is the receipt that the border is guarded.
