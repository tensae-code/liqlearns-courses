# Lesson plan — Planning and Designing a Website

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Internet and the Web · **Lesson:** Planning and Designing a Website
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Mongolia (2026-11-14) — in the title Internet and the Web on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Mongolia's student books (Ministry of Education, Culture and Science(s) of Mongolia, Ulaanbaatar), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree and live lesson content were checked for the slug and for near matches before building.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Website planning and design — fixing audience and purpose, choosing the site's structure (linear, hierarchical, cycle or web), laying out pages, and choosing how the site will be built (CMS, hand-coded HTML or design programs), then hosting and testing it
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Audience, purpose and structure** — A website is planned from its **audience and purpose**: who must it serve, and what must those visitors be able to do? The pages are then hung on a **structure**: **linear** (pages in one line, like a story), **hierarchical** (a tree from the home page down), **cycle** (a ring returning on itself) or **web** (pages linked freely, many to many). Structure is a promise to the visitor: at any page, the way onward and the way home must be guessable from the shape alone.
2. **Layout and the way of building** — Each page gets a **layout** — where the title, menu, text and pictures stand — kept the same across the site so visitors learn it once. Then the builder chooses the **way of building**: a **CMS** (content management system) that assembles pages from parts, **hand-coded HTML** written directly, or **design programs** that draw pages and write the code behind them. The plan ends with **hosting** — putting the site on a server — and **testing** every page and link before the world is invited. Mongolia's order is the professional one: plan, structure, build, host, test — in that sequence, never code first.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
