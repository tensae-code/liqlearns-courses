# Forms of Online Communication

## Do it first

People talked across distance long before the internet — the internet gave that talk new forms, each with its own speed, shape and manners. Fiji's Years 7–8 book names the forms of online communication as their own section. This lesson sorts them.

## Sub lesson 1 — The forms — mail, messages, calls, shared spaces

Online communication takes named forms. **Email** — letters that wait in an inbox until read. **Instant messaging and chat** — written talk that happens now, line by line. **Voice and video calls** — speech and faces carried by the network. **Social networking and forums** — talk in shared spaces, where a message is read by a group, not one person. Fiji's sorting question for each form is the practical one: is it **now or later**, **one person or many**, **writing, voice or face**?

## Sub lesson 2 — Choosing the form for the job

Each form fits a job. A message that must be **kept and reread** — an assignment, an official note — belongs in email. A quick question between friends belongs in **messaging**. A family talk across the sea belongs in a **video call**. News for a whole class belongs in the **shared space**. Choosing wrong has costs: urgent words sent by email wait unread; official words sent by chat vanish in the flow. The form is part of the message — that is Fiji's point in teaching the forms together.
