**Acceptance count: taught in 10 countries (Ethiopia, Iraq, Egypt, Bhutan, Libya, Laos, Jordan, Armenia, Vietnam, South Sudan).**

The teacher guide's ISP answers contradict each other within two pages (Ethio-Telecom as the only ISP, then Ethio-Telecom and Safaricom-Ethiopia together) — flagged in the placements. The lesson teaches the student book's own line (Ethio-telecom as Ethiopia's example ISP) and records the guide's contradiction here rather than teaching either keyed answer as settled.

Growth note (Wave 3, band B2): Sub lessons 1–2 are the original Grade 7 lesson, unchanged in content; Sub lesson 3 is the Grade 8 depth added from the Grade 8 book (printed pp. 66–67). Two faults touch this growth and are taught as nothing: the student book's printed p. 66 carries a stray character run (a run of the letter h) typeset into the text, and the teacher guide's §4.2 cites the textbook "beginning on page 33" for this topic (the student §4.2 is on printed p. 66) and leaves its own guide page blank — the rows above cite the real pages.

Iraq's own way (Grade 7): a four-unit spiral repeated at every grade — computer fundamentals, computer software (here: the operating system plus an unusually deep treatment of Windows right-click shortcut menus), algorithms and programming principles (ending in Scratch), and information technology and the internet — every lesson built on the Iraqi five-step learning cycle with opening activity, main idea, learning outcomes, vocabulary and lesson review.

Morocco's own way (Grade 2): Grade 2 goes online before it goes deep on the machine — browser and search first, then saving what the web gives (page, image, video), then creating in Paint and WordPad, with removable-disk file handling taught as a whole unit of its own. (teacher guide — evidence)

Egypt's own way (Grade 5, Term 2 student book (Arabic)): Primary ICT is organised as themed units — "The Digital Citizen in the Internet World" then "My Digital Project" — with every lesson phrased as a journey or a how-to question.

Bhutan's own way (Class IV, Literacy with ICT student book): Competency-based "Literacy with ICT" (not procedure drill): every chapter opens with learning objectives, mixes off-machine "Try This" with "Try This On Machine", and closes with Now You Know / Check Your Progress / Explore Further; content is localised (Bhutanese names, kuenselonline, education.gov.bt, Bhutan's 1999 Internet start) and one class per week across the year.

Libya's own way (Grade 9, Computer student book): the whole year is networks and the internet — a full networks unit (types, cables, topology, components), then Internet Explorer and Google search taught as named skills, then email and a closing tour of social sites (Facebook, YouTube, Twitter, Instagram, Yahoo) with practical activities.

Libya's own way (Grade 11 Term 2, Information Technology student book): Term 2 is web authoring from first principles — internet architecture and protocols, then hand-written HTML (structure, text formatting, links, lists, tables), then publishing a site properly (domain, hosting, FTP) and three full website projects. The book is printed for BOTH the Science and Humanities sections, although the records label it Humanities only — cited as printed.

Laos's own way (Grade 12 ICT student book): the final year is a modern, applied course — problem-solving method, a long English-titled internet-safety sequence (Internet Safety, Email Tips for Scams and Spam, Smart Social Networking, Cyber harassment/Stalking/Addiction), data summaries and PivotTables for decision-making, copyright and Creative Commons licensing, a full PowerPoint course, and choosing technology for your future occupation.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 10 G10T1 Student): Grade 10 Term 1 is a data year: data and binary representation first, then big data and visualisation, then a long practical Excel lesson (text functions, AVERAGEIF/SUMIF, VLOOKUP, Pivot Tables, FORECAST and the Analysis ToolPak); the second unit builds web pages in HTML topic by topic.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 7 G7 student): A new author team (Topchyan & Melkonyan, 2025) rebuilds the course around the "virtual world": data and encoding, networks, e-security and e-communication, then a full Python chapter — decisions, lists, loops, functions, objects and classes — taught as making the real world virtual.

Vietnam's own way (Grade 10 Tin học 10): the Grade 10 book is one shared book for both orientations (ICT-coloured and CS-coloured lesson title bands tell each track its own lessons, per its preface); its data strand is unusually deep for school level — binary integers, logic data, sound and image data — and its programming strand is a complete first Python course from variables to functions, scope, error recognition and testing.

South Sudan's own way (Grade 9 S1 Student): Opens ICT at secondary level entirely through doing — the whole first year is Microsoft Office application skills (Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Publisher, Paint) taught as step-by-step keyboard/mouse procedures with local data (South Sudan state populations, local disease statistics for charts), then internet use, hands-on troubleshooting, and Windows access control/security settings.
