# Searching and Downloading Information — Saving a Webpage

## Do it first

Finding is only half the job — the other half is keeping what you found. Fiji's Years 7–8 book teaches searching, extracting and downloading as one practical: search, take the information out, save the page and its pictures, print what you need, and name where it came from. This lesson is that whole practical.

## Sub lesson 1 — Searching and extracting — take out what you came for

Fiji's searching is practised on real Fijian ground — the **Map of Fiji** — with search engines made for children. The searcher's order is: turn the need into **search words**, run the search, open the promising results, and **extract** — pick out the facts, words or pictures the task actually needs, leaving the rest behind. Extracting is a reading skill: the page is not copied whole; the searcher takes the part that answers the question and can say why that part.

In your mind's eye: searching and extracting — take out what you came for.

## Sub lesson 2 — Downloading and saving — page, picture, print

**Downloading** brings a copy from the web to your own computer. A **webpage is saved** from the browser so it can be opened again without the internet; a **picture is saved** by its own save command into your folder; and a page can be **printed** when paper is what the task needs. Fiji ties the practical with its rule of honesty: work built from downloaded material **acknowledges its sources** — the searcher names where each fact and picture came from, because downloaded does not mean yours.

In your mind's eye: downloading and saving — page, picture, print.
