# Lesson plan — Kinds of web browser

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Internet and the Web · **Lesson:** Kinds of web browser
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 8 — Internet and the Web in the Computer Science and ICT ladder (TITLES.md); stocked from Ethiopia's Grade 7 book, Unit 4 — Internet. The ladder is the recommended order, never a forced one.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the book pictures five common browsers: Internet Explorer, Mozilla Firefox, Google Chrome, Safari and Opera.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the browser lesson — the tool is known; this lesson names the kinds of it the book pictures.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or in steps — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Browsers and search engines.  Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the five browsers the book pictures.
2. Say that all five do the same job: exploring the internet.
3. Recognise each name when you meet it on a computer.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil; any computer or phone to hunt icons on.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner names every browser they have ever seen an icon for, before the book's five are shown (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the parts below, in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 10514]: a picture of the five browser names in a row — the student reads the five names and ticks each one.
3. **Say it to your companion** — explain the lesson back in your own words (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the lesson retold in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-or-false, fill-in-the-blank — served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the doing block at the end of lesson/lesson.md.
