# Lesson plan — Cookies — Small Files That Remember You

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Internet and the Web · **Lesson:** Cookies — Small Files That Remember You
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Germany (2026-11-14) — in the title Internet and the Web on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Germany's *Informatik 5 (Bayern NTG)*, because no existing lesson covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a cookie is a small text file a website asks your browser to store and hand back on later visits — it lets the site remember you (your basket, your sign-in, your settings), and, in its third-party form, lets advertisers follow you across sites; knowing what cookies are is what makes the consent box a real choice.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on everyday browsing — baskets that remember, sites that greet you — and on this course's web-address and browser lessons. Germany reaches it inside its networks chapter, from the solutions-verified part of the book.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. ## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the Internet and the Web lessons on browsers and on the parts of a web address in this course. Recommended, never forced — a learner may land on this lesson directly, and it stands alone.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a cookie is, where it lives, and what travels in it.
2. Tell a first-party cookie from a third-party cookie, and name what each is for.
3. Clear or refuse cookies in a browser, and say what changes when you do.

## What you need
A browser on any device, and one shop or news site you visit often — the lesson's examples are read off your own screen.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Put something in a basket on a shopping site, close the tab, come back tomorrow — and the basket is still full. The site has no memory of its own between visits. Something on your side remembered. This lesson names that something and shows its two faces, helpful and watchful. (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
Deletes every cookie and is surprised the basket empties → that forgetting is the cookie made visible; sign in again and watch the memory return. Confuses a cookie with a virus → a cookie is plain text: it cannot run, install anything or read files.
