# Lesson plan — Electronic Communication and Its Technologies

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Internet and the Web · **Lesson:** Electronic Communication and Its Technologies
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Mongolia (2026-11-14) — in the title Internet and the Web on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Mongolia's student books (Ministry of Education, Culture and Science(s) of Mongolia, Ulaanbaatar), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree and live lesson content were checked for the slug and for near matches before building.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Electronic communication and its technologies — communicating through networks set against face-to-face talk, and the technology family that carries it: the WWW, social networks, e-mail, chat and video conferences
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **E-communication against face-to-face** — **Electronic communication** is communication carried over networks between people who are not together: written, spoken or seen, it travels as data and arrives across any distance. Set against **face-to-face** talk, its trades are plain: it gains distance, speed and a kept record; it loses the face, the tone and the touch — and gains dangers the room never had, which the unit's ethics lesson will name. Naming the trade first is Mongolia's way: the technologies that follow are judged against the talk they replace.
2. **The technology family — WWW, social networks, e-mail, chat, video conferences** — The carriers form one family, each with its own tempo. The **WWW** publishes pages for anyone to read. **Social networks** link people into ongoing shared spaces. **E-mail** sends letters that wait to be read. **Chat** talks live in short written turns. **Video conferences** restore face and voice across the distance. One family, five tempos — from the page that waits for the world to the call that happens now — and the unit walks them in that order: read, link, write, chat, meet.

2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
