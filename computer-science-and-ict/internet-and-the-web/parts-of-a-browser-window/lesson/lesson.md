# Parts of a browser window

## Do it first

Open a browser — in the school lab if you can. Look for bars: one with menus, one where an address could be typed, one at the very bottom. You have found this lesson's three parts before being told their names. Now learn them properly.

## Sub lesson 1 — The book's three elements

The **menu bar** is the horizontal strip of available menus. The **address bar** — also called the **URL bar** — is where you **type a web address**; then you click **GO** or press **Enter**, and the browser travels to that address. Watch the GIF: the address is typed letter by letter, Enter is pressed, the page loads. The **status bar** is the bottom bar, showing messages such as **download progress**. The book's practical with this lesson: open a browser in the school lab, list and describe its elements, and write them up in Microsoft Word.

## Sub lesson 2 — Two more from the teacher guide (teacher guide — evidence)

The teacher guide's answer adds two more common elements, and a warning: browser screens vary with the software installed, so your browser may not match the picture exactly. Its full list runs to five: menu bar, address bar, **standard toolbar**, status bar and **workspace** — the workspace being the area where the page itself is shown. Learn the book's three cold; know the guide's two when you meet them.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the three book elements and the two guide additions, with one job each.

## Do It Yourself

1. Label a drawn browser window from memory.
2. Type an address and load the page, saying each step aloud.
3. Write the elements up in Word, as the book's practical asks.
