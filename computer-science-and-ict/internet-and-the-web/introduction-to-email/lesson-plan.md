## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What email is — the internet's letter** — **Email** — electronic mail — is a **message sent from one computer to another over the internet**, to an address that names one person's mailbox. It carries words, and can carry files attached. Unlike a phone call, email **waits**: the message sits in the receiver's **inbox** until they open it, whether that is in a minute or in a week — the writer and the reader never need to be present together.
2. **Why email matters — fast, written and kept** — Email is **fast** where a posted letter is slow, **written** where a call leaves no record, and **kept** — a message can be reread, searched, forwarded and filed. Those three are why schools and offices run on it: Bhutan's Class VI pupils use Gmail on their **G Suite for Education** school account, the account this chapter's next lessons sign into and work from.
