## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Div — the grouping box** — The **<div>** element groups **block-level content** — paragraphs, headings, images — into one unit: a section, a card, a column. A div means nothing and displays nothing by itself; its power arrives with an attribute — a class or id — that CSS and scripts use to take hold of the whole group at once: style this section, move that card. Modern pages are built as trees of divs, each a named region of the page — the nesting lesson's boxes, given their working form.
2. **Span — picking out words in the flow** — The **<span>** element is div's inline twin: it picks out a **run of text inside a block** — one word, a price, a name — without breaking the flow or starting a new line. With a class, that run can be coloured, enlarged or scripted while its sentence flows on around it. The pair divide the world cleanly: **div for blocks, span for words inside blocks** — and choosing wrongly (a div where a span belongs) breaks the line the reader was following, which is how you know the division matters.
