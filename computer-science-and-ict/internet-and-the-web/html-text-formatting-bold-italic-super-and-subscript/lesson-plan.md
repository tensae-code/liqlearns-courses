## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Standing out — bold, italic, underline, big and small** — The emphasis family: **<b>** makes text **bold** — weight that catches the eye; **<i>** makes it *italic* — the lean used for titles and foreign words; **<u>** underlines (sparingly — readers now read underlines as links); **<big>** and **<small>** step the size up or down relative to their neighbours — small is where the fine print and the quiet asides live. Each element is a container: opening tag, text, closing tag, nested by the mirror rule of the companion lesson.
2. **Raised and lowered — superscript and subscript** — Two elements move text off the line instead of restyling it: **<sup>** raises its text as a **superscript** — the 2 in x<sup>2</sup>, the th in 10<sup>th</sup>; **<sub>** lowers it as a **subscript** — the 2 in H<sub>2</sub>O. Without them, science writing on the web would be crippled: formulas, chemical names, units and footnotes all live above and below the line. The formatting family's rule of use closes the lesson: formatting serves meaning — emphasis where the meaning leans, never decoration sprayed everywhere, which emphasises nothing.
