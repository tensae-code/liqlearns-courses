## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Live written talk — presence and the flowing line** — **Instant messaging** is sending written messages that arrive **instantly**, between people who are **online at the same time**; **online chat** is the same live talk in a shared room, often among several people. Both rest on **presence**: the program shows who is available, the talk opens, and lines appear as they are typed — question, answer, joke, in seconds. Fiji places them in the communication chapter because they changed what 'writing to someone' means: writing became a form of being together.
2. **The behaviour of live talk — fast, informal, unretractable** — Live talk has a nature its users must know. It is **fast** — answers are expected in moments, and silence itself sends a message. It is **informal** — short lines, abbreviations, unfinished sentences, closer to speech than to letters. And it is **kept** — for all its speech-like flow, the words are written and can be saved, copied and shown later. Fiji's user rule follows from the three together: enjoy the speed, but write every line as something that may outlive the moment.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Email waits; chat does not. Fiji's Year 12 communication chapter sets instant messaging and online chat beside email as the internet's live written talk — messages that arrive the moment they are sent, and conversation that flows like speech. This lesson is that live talk: how it works and how it behaves. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
