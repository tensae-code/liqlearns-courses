# Instant Messaging and Online Chat

## Do it first

Email waits; chat does not. Fiji's Year 12 communication chapter sets instant messaging and online chat beside email as the internet's live written talk — messages that arrive the moment they are sent, and conversation that flows like speech. This lesson is that live talk: how it works and how it behaves.

## Sub lesson 1 — Live written talk — presence and the flowing line

**Instant messaging** is sending written messages that arrive **instantly**, between people who are **online at the same time**; **online chat** is the same live talk in a shared room, often among several people. Both rest on **presence**: the program shows who is available, the talk opens, and lines appear as they are typed — question, answer, joke, in seconds. Fiji places them in the communication chapter because they changed what 'writing to someone' means: writing became a form of being together.

In your mind's eye: live written talk — presence and the flowing line.

## Sub lesson 2 — The behaviour of live talk — fast, informal, unretractable

Live talk has a nature its users must know. It is **fast** — answers are expected in moments, and silence itself sends a message. It is **informal** — short lines, abbreviations, unfinished sentences, closer to speech than to letters. And it is **kept** — for all its speech-like flow, the words are written and can be saved, copied and shown later. Fiji's user rule follows from the three together: enjoy the speed, but write every line as something that may outlive the moment.

In your mind's eye: the behaviour of live talk — fast, informal, unretractable.
