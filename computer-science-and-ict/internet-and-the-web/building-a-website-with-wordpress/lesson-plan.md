# Lesson plan — Building a Website With WordPress

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Internet and the Web · **Lesson:** Building a Website With WordPress
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Bangladesh (2026-11-14) — in the title Internet and the Web on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Bangladesh's NCTB student books (Bangladesh Grade 9 Digital Technology), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree and live lesson content were checked for the slug and for near matches before building.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** building a website with WordPress: making a real site for a citizen service on an open-source platform — pages, posts and menus through an admin panel, with Google services added (Bangladesh Grade 9 Digital Technology, Learning Experience 3, Sessions 6–8).
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **WordPress — the open-source building platform** — **WordPress** is **open-source** website software: free to use and modify, installed on a host, and worked through an **admin panel** in the browser — no code needed for the ordinary work of pages and posts. The builder chooses a **theme** (the site's look), writes **pages** for standing content (the service, its fees, its steps — the transparency lessons made visible) and **posts** for news, and arranges **menus** so a citizen finds any page in two clicks. Plugins extend the platform; the book keeps to the core craft.
2. **Adding Google services — maps, forms and the launch** — The book's sessions add **Google services** to the site: a **map** showing the office, a **form** citizens can submit, and the sharing and search tools that let the service be found. Then the launch discipline: check every page as a citizen would — on a phone, on a slow connection, hunting the fee and the phone number — fix what fails, and only then announce it. A website for a public service is a promise; WordPress is how Grade 9 keeps it with their own hands.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — The design lesson planned a citizen-service website. This lesson builds it — not by hand-coding every page, but on WordPress, the open-source platform behind a huge share of the world's sites, adding Google services as the book's sessions do. A real site, live, made by Grade 9 hands. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.

   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the lesson's key names and definitions aloud, in the order the lesson gives them — the student listens once through, then reads the same lines aloud along with the voice. (Audio is a described slot in this build; no audio file is made.)
