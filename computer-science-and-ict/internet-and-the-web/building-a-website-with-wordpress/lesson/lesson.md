# Building a Website With WordPress

## Do it first

The design lesson planned a citizen-service website. This lesson builds it — not by hand-coding every page, but on WordPress, the open-source platform behind a huge share of the world's sites, adding Google services as the book's sessions do. A real site, live, made by Grade 9 hands.

## Sub lesson 1 — WordPress — the open-source building platform

**WordPress** is **open-source** website software: free to use and modify, installed on a host, and worked through an **admin panel** in the browser — no code needed for the ordinary work of pages and posts. The builder chooses a **theme** (the site's look), writes **pages** for standing content (the service, its fees, its steps — the transparency lessons made visible) and **posts** for news, and arranges **menus** so a citizen finds any page in two clicks. Plugins extend the platform; the book keeps to the core craft.

In your mind's eye: wordPress — the open-source building platform.

## Sub lesson 2 — Adding Google services — maps, forms and the launch

The book's sessions add **Google services** to the site: a **map** showing the office, a **form** citizens can submit, and the sharing and search tools that let the service be found. Then the launch discipline: check every page as a citizen would — on a phone, on a slow connection, hunting the fee and the phone number — fix what fails, and only then announce it. A website for a public service is a promise; WordPress is how Grade 9 keeps it with their own hands.

In your mind's eye: adding Google services — maps, forms and the launch.
