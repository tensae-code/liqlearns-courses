# Lesson plan — The structure of an HTML document

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Internet and the Web · **Lesson:** The structure of an HTML document
**Place in the curriculum:** Title — Internet and the Web in the Computer Science and ICT ladder (TITLES.md); stocked from Ethiopia's Grade 11 book, Unit 4 — Web Development. The ladder is the recommended order, never a forced one.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** every HTML page shares one skeleton — a document-type declaration, the root element, a head holding information about the page, and a body holding everything displayed.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the tags-and-elements and void-elements lessons — the learner reads a whole page's skeleton once its parts are known.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or in steps — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The four parts of the skeleton** — the declaration, the root, the head and the body
2. **Reading a real page's skeleton** — hunting the skeleton in a real page's source

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Void elements — br, img, hr. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the four parts of every HTML document.
2. Say what the head holds and what the body holds.
3. Find the skeleton in a real page's source.

## What you need
A computer with a browser; a text editor.

## If the learner is stuck
- Puts visible content in the head → the head holds information about the page; everything displayed belongs in the body.
- Thinks each page invents its own skeleton → the skeleton is shared; only head and body contents change.
