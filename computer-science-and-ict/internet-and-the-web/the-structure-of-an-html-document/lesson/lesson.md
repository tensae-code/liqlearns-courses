# The structure of an HTML document

## Do it first

Open a real webpage and look at its source — the code behind it. Somewhere near the top there is a declaration, one big element wrapping everything, a section about the page, and a section the browser actually displays. Find all four before this lesson names them — that hunt is the book's own practice for this part.

## Sub lesson 1 — The four parts of the skeleton

Every HTML page shares one skeleton:

1. A **document-type declaration** — announcing the document type.
2. The **root element** — wrapping the whole page.
3. A **head section** — holding **information about the page**, such as its title.
4. A **body section** — holding **everything the browser actually displays**.

Only what sits inside head and body changes from page to page; the skeleton itself stays the same.

## Sub lesson 2 — Reading a real page's skeleton

The book's practice: open a real page's source and hunt for the familiar tags — the declaration at the top, the root around everything, the title inside the head, the visible content inside the body. If you can find the four parts in a stranger's page, you can write the skeleton of your own from memory.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the four skeleton parts in order, and what goes in the head and what goes in the body.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write a full skeleton with a title in the head and a heading and paragraph in the body.
2. Open a real page's source and label its four parts.
3. Say which two parts' contents change from page to page.
