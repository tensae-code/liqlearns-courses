**Acceptance count: taught in 7 countries (Ethiopia, Afghanistan, North Macedonia, Iraq, Sudan, Cuba, Egypt).**

Afghanistan's own way (Grade 12): the final year turns outward — computers in society (e-commerce, communications, even military systems), system software and virus protection, then Excel for the first time and a closing block on the Web, e-mail and browsers (taught through Internet Explorer).

Growth note (Wave 3, band B2): Sub lessons 1–2 are the original Grade 7 lesson, unchanged in content; Sub lesson 3 is the Grade 8 depth added from the Grade 8 book (printed p. 64) — the same Web, with its server, hyperlink and two-part URL made explicit. Nothing in the growth claims a page the placements do not give.

North Macedonia's own way (Year 2): a strikingly current first theme (cybersecurity, cryptography, blockchain, robotics, AI/ML and large language models) precedes deep C++ (data representation, functions, one-dimensional arrays), a full databases theme ending in SQL, multimedia with generative AI for graphics, and HTML web authoring.

Mauritania's own way (Grade 7): Informatics is taught for the first time in 1AS as thirty 30-minute teacher fiches moving hardware → information systems (illustrated with Mauritanian systems Bankily, Masrvi, Houwiyeti and the Ministry's Sirage platform) → a long Microsoft Word sequence → a full unit on internet ethics before browsers and search are even introduced.

Iraq's own way (Grade 8): the same four-unit spiral one year on — the hardware unit becomes “the computer platform” and opens with choosing a computer's specifications before buying; Word is taught tab by tab (Home, Insert, Design, Layout); the algorithms unit advances to selection statements and logic truth tables, still landing in Scratch; the internet unit ends with writing a report from web research.

Sudan's own way (Grade 7): the middle-stage book spends half its length on one database built step by step in Microsoft Access (tables, relationships, forms, queries, reports on a pupils-and-subjects database), then teaches the internet through Sudanese examples — the DNS lesson resolves the Khartoum Ministry of Education site (moekh.gov.sd).

Cuba's own way (Grade 8): Taught entirely in free software — LibreOffice Impress for presentations and LibreOffice Calc for spreadsheets; web work means designing and editing HTML pages with a plain text editor, and Scratch continues with arithmetic and logic operator blocks.

Egypt's own way (Grade 5, Term 2 student book (Arabic)): Primary ICT is organised as themed units — "The Digital Citizen in the Internet World" then "My Digital Project" — with every lesson phrased as a journey or a how-to question.
