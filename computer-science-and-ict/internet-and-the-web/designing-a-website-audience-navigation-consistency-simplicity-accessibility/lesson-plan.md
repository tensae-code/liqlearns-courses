# Lesson plan — Designing a website — audience, navigation, consistency, simplicity, accessibility

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Internet and the Web · **Lesson:** Designing a website — audience, navigation, consistency, simplicity, accessibility
**Place in the curriculum:** Title — Internet and the Web in the Computer Science and ICT ladder (TITLES.md); stocked from Ethiopia's Grade 11 book, Unit 4 — Web Development. The ladder is the recommended order, never a forced one.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a good website is designed for its audience, with logical navigation, a consistent look, a simple page and access for everybody.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the World Wide Web lesson — the learner knows what a website is before designing one.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or in steps — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The World Wide Web — and its four foundations (the grown lesson in this title). Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what website design covers.
2. Name the five design guidelines.
3. Judge a website against the five guidelines.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil; a browser to look at a real website, if one is at hand.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner judges two imaginary home pages — one crowded, one tidy — and says which is easier to use, before the five guidelines (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the parts below, in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 17301]: a picture of five guideline cards around one tidy web page — the student names each guideline from its card.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the lesson's key words and code aloud — the student listens and repeats each tag name.
3. **Say it to your companion** — explain the lesson back in your own words (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the lesson retold in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-or-false, fill-in-the-blank — served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the doing block at the end of lesson/lesson.md.

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the plan is the build sheet, so nothing the lesson needs gets forgotten.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes design with building → design is the plan for how it looks and works; building comes next lesson.
- Forgets accessibility → a site some people cannot use has failed one of the five rules, however pretty it is.
