# Designing a website — audience, navigation, consistency, simplicity, accessibility

## Do it first

Imagine two school websites. The first is crowded with pictures, colours and links everywhere. The second is tidy, with a clear menu. Which one would you find your timetable on faster? Keep your answer — this lesson gives the five rules that make the second site better.

## Sub lesson 1 — What website design is

**Website design** covers a site's **layout, navigation, appearance and graphics**. Its aim is a site that **appeals to its audience** and makes information **easy to find**. Design is planned before the building starts — the next lesson separates designing from building.

## Sub lesson 2 — The five guidelines

Design by these five rules:

1. **Design for the target audience** — a children's education site must look different from an adults' business site.
2. **Make navigation logical** — visitors find things fast instead of being confused away.
3. **Keep the look and feel consistent** — the same backgrounds, colours and typefaces across pages.
4. **Keep it simple** — do not overcrowd pages with graphics; use about **five colours, plus or minus two**.
5. **Design for accessibility** — nobody, including people with disabilities, is excluded.

The WHO site is the book's example of good navigation. The book's practice is to evaluate the UN Women site against these same five guidelines.
