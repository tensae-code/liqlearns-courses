**Acceptance count: taught in 11 countries (Ethiopia, Belarus, Egypt, Fiji, Malaysia, Moldova, Bangladesh, Armenia, Vietnam, South Sudan, Kazakhstan).**

Belarus's own way (Grade 11): grade 11 is a web-and-data year — hand-written HTML in Notepad, then the same site rebuilt in FrontPage, a long PascalABC graphics practicum borrowed from other school subjects, and a full Access database course (tables, links, forms, queries, sorting, reports).

Unit 4 figure references and captions repeatedly disagree in the student book (the elements text cites Figure 4.3 where the caption is 4.2; the document-structure text cites Figure 4.2 where the caption is 4.4; the align-attribute text cites Figure 4.4 where the caption is 4.5; the table example text cites Figure 4.5 where the caption is 4.11) and table numbers are reused (two different tables are each numbered 4.4/4.5). This lesson teaches from the pictured content and cites no figure or table number. "statment" is misspelled in review headings throughout the book; the correct spelling is taught.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Grade 11): the senior guide turns learners into junior system administrators and builders — computer administration, datasheets ("data books") and timing technology, documenting server and printer configurations, data science, pseudocode, Green IT, and building static web pages with tools like Canva and Figma; typing rises to 40 wpm at 85–100% accuracy. (teacher guide — evidence)

Egypt's own way (Grade 8, Term 1 student book): Two units — "Artificial Intelligence and Protecting Our Digital Data" and "Website Design and Creation" — ending with pupils creating their own school website with AI help.

Egypt's own way (Grade 5, Term 2 student book (Arabic)): Primary ICT is organised as themed units — "The Digital Citizen in the Internet World" then "My Digital Project" — with every lesson phrased as a journey or a how-to question.

Egypt's own way (Grade 11, Term 1 Website Design Project student book): The whole term is one production project — an "Illustrated Dictionary of Computer Terms" website — built with Expression Web, HTML and PHP pages over a MySQL database, including a working Add_Term.php page.

Fiji's own way (Year 13 Computer Education student book): The senior book is built around a Fijian case organisation (Viti Group of Companies, a Fiji furniture manufacturer) for information systems; it teaches the full systems life cycle phase by phase, web design with real HTML and CSS, and text programming in C++ with worked programs (pay calculation, grade from mark, triangle and circle areas) using Fijian student names.

Malaysia's own way (Grade 11, SK Tingkatan 5): the final-year elective is exam-level computer science — computer architecture (fetch-decode-execute, Von Neumann, pipelining) and logic gates with Boolean expressions, a 57-page advanced SQL chapter that starts from ERD and normalisation, and a full web-programming chapter pair: client-side JavaScript (including sorting/searching, standard library, arrays) and server-side PHP working with text files, validation, queries, sign-up/login, ending in an interactive website build.

Moldova's own way (Grade 10, Informatică): the manual teaches Pascal and C++ side by side — each school chooses one or both — and upper-secondary programming is language-formal: BNF metalanguage and syntax diagrams, ordinal types, type conversions, every concept given in Pascal for teaching and in C++ for a technology career.

Bangladesh's own way (Grade 9, Digital Technology): Grade 9 turns practical — a full Python course (Thonny IDE) inside the book, website building with WordPress and Google services for a citizen-service site, and hands-on networking between two school computers. The NCTB Digital Technology line runs in parallel English and Bangla editions; placements are cited from the English Version with the Bangla Version parallel spot-checked.

Bangladesh's own way (HSC ICT, Classes XI–XII and Alim): one compulsory ICT book for every HSC and Alim student in the country, saturated with Bangladesh itself: Bangabandhu Satellite-1 and its ground stations, bdjobs and chakri.com job portals, Daraz and Bikroy e-commerce, the Walton factory's automated production line, the National ID card used to teach primary keys and its database used to teach RDBMS at work, a full Bangla Unicode table and a worked example of putting Bangla text on a web page with meta charset, the national letter-grade scale (A+ from 80) as the else-if programme, "I Love Bangladesh" as the loop example text, an algorithm for reaching college by bicycle or rickshaw, and the Digital Bangladesh programme — National Data Centre, hi-tech parks, Startup Bangladesh and Bangladesh Bank's QR-based Cashless Bangladesh. C is the HSC programming language, taught with scanf/printf in Code::Blocks.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 9 G9 student): Grade 9 turns to making for the web: after digital dangers and data protection, a full HTML/CSS block (document structure, lists and forms, text and tables, pictures and links, site design and styling, colour systems, font units), then operating systems, then Python conditionals, loops, functions and arrays.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 9 G9 teacher): Matches Book 16: 10 standard themes (9-1 to 9-10) with outcomes and competencies, a thematic plan, worked analyses and solutions for the student book's tasks (§1–§17, including the HTML/CSS and Python blocks), and assessment criteria for creative thinking, problem solving, independent and project work.

Vietnam's own way (Grade 12 Tin học 12 Tin học ứng dụng): the final-year common core (Bài 1–21, shared with the CS book) opens with artificial intelligence and network protocols, then teaches real web design — HTML structure, lists, tables, links, forms, then CSS through selector priority — and closes with ICT careers (repair/maintenance services, IT administration). The Applied-only lessons build a whole website in Google Sites.

South Sudan's own way (Grade 12 S4 Student): The final year is specialisation: a full databases unit in Microsoft Access with real SQL, forms and reports, normalisation and joins, ending in integrated library management systems; an advanced-formatting unit that goes from Word tables of contents, styles, track changes, templates and mail merge through PowerPoint multimedia to VBA macros in Excel; and a website-design unit built around HTML, CSS and Adobe Dreamweaver with FTP site management.

South Sudan's own way (Grade 12 S4 Teacher): The guide mirrors the student book's three units with teaching sequences, observation/conversation assessment points and worked projects — designing a database for a local medical practice, asking how books are managed in a school library, and a capstone project to design the school's own website, judged on navigation and usability. (teacher guide — evidence)


Kazakhstan's own way (Salgaraeva G10 Informatika, humanities track): the humanities-track Grade 10 book replaces programming and databases with media production: a short information-security part, then shooting and editing video as a project, design theory ("good design", graphics for the web, developing a site's design), and building, publishing and promoting a real website. Each part closes with sample summative-assessment tasks.

Kazakhstan's own way (Zhunusov G10 Computer Science, English-medium): the English-medium Grade 10 book is the most technical of the wave: the CIA triad and three separate authentication lessons (passwords twice, biometrics), encoding standards, Python dictionaries, DFS/BFS graph traversal, and a full SQL chapter in MySQL (CREATE, INSERT, SELECT/WHERE, MIN/MAX, JOIN, GROUP BY, SELECT INTO) before a JavaScript chapter. (Its preface is the publisher's generic science template, left over from another book.)

Kazakhstan's own way (Kolyeva G10 Informatika, humanities track): Mektep's humanities-track Grade 10 book mirrors the Arman-PV one (information security, video content, design theory, web design) and adds a fifth part no other humanities book has: artificial intelligence and blockchain — machine-learning principles, how neural networks are organised and work, the fields where AI is used, and blockchain technology.
