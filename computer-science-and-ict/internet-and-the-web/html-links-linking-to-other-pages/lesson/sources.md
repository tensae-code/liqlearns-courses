**Acceptance count: taught in 3 countries (Ethiopia, Belarus, Libya).**

Belarus's own way (Grade 11): grade 11 is a web-and-data year — hand-written HTML in Notepad, then the same site rebuilt in FrontPage, a long PascalABC graphics practicum borrowed from other school subjects, and a full Access database course (tables, links, forms, queries, sorting, reports).

Unit 4 figure references and captions repeatedly disagree in the student book (the elements text cites Figure 4.3 where the caption is 4.2; the document-structure text cites Figure 4.2 where the caption is 4.4; the align-attribute text cites Figure 4.4 where the caption is 4.5; the table example text cites Figure 4.5 where the caption is 4.11) and table numbers are reused (two different tables are each numbered 4.4/4.5). This lesson teaches from the pictured content and cites no figure or table number. "statment" is misspelled in review headings throughout the book; the correct spelling is taught. Guide-side faults: the guide prints "HMTL" for HTML in its §4.6 heading and Unit 4 topic lists; the correct HTML is taught. Guide-side: the guide prints "Got to Section…" for "Go to Section…" in a worked link answer; the correct wording is taught.

Libya's own way (Grade 9, Computer student book): the whole year is networks and the internet — a full networks unit (types, cables, topology, components), then Internet Explorer and Google search taught as named skills, then email and a closing tour of social sites (Facebook, YouTube, Twitter, Instagram, Yahoo) with practical activities.

Libya's own way (Grade 11 Term 2, Information Technology student book): Term 2 is web authoring from first principles — internet architecture and protocols, then hand-written HTML (structure, text formatting, links, lists, tables), then publishing a site properly (domain, hosting, FTP) and three full website projects. The book is printed for BOTH the Science and Humanities sections, although the records label it Humanities only — cited as printed.

Libya's own way (Grade 11 Term 2, Information Technology teacher guide): each web chapter is taught as target outcomes + a chapter summary + scripted lesson steps (an introduction and set activities — e.g. pupils role-play the devices of a network) + answers to the chapter questions. The guide is printed for both the Science and Humanities sections. (teacher guide — evidence)
