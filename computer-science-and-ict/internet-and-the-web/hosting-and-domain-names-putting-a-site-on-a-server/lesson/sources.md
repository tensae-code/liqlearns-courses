**Acceptance count: taught in 7 countries (Ethiopia, Fiji, Libya, Kuwait, Bangladesh, South Sudan, Kazakhstan).**

No book error is flagged for this part in the placements. The domain name in the book's ministry address example is printed as "moe.gov" (the part of the address after "www") — the lesson teaches the domain name as the part after www, as printed, without extending the example beyond the placements. This lesson makes no statement about the Web's invention date.

Fiji's own way (Year 13 Computer Education student book): The senior book is built around a Fijian case organisation (Viti Group of Companies, a Fiji furniture manufacturer) for information systems; it teaches the full systems life cycle phase by phase, web design with real HTML and CSS, and text programming in C++ with worked programs (pay calculation, grade from mark, triangle and circle areas) using Fijian student names.

Libya's own way (Grade 11 Term 2, Information Technology student book): Term 2 is web authoring from first principles — internet architecture and protocols, then hand-written HTML (structure, text formatting, links, lists, tables), then publishing a site properly (domain, hosting, FTP) and three full website projects. The book is printed for BOTH the Science and Humanities sections, although the records label it Humanities only — cited as printed.

Libya's own way (Grade 11 Term 2, Information Technology teacher guide): each web chapter is taught as target outcomes + a chapter summary + scripted lesson steps (an introduction and set activities — e.g. pupils role-play the devices of a network) + answers to the chapter questions. The guide is printed for both the Science and Humanities sections. (teacher guide — evidence)

Kuwait's own way (Grade 9 Digital Intelligence Term 1): Grade 9 builds a real website by hand — HTML then CSS in VS Code, developed page by page as a 'Kuwait Future' site and finished as a seven-file project site about AI applications that is actually uploaded (hosting and FTP with FileZilla); the robotics unit reaches PID control theory, a sumo-wrestling robot, and a guard robot programmed with lists and timers.

Bangladesh's own way (HSC ICT, Classes XI–XII and Alim): one compulsory ICT book for every HSC and Alim student in the country, saturated with Bangladesh itself: Bangabandhu Satellite-1 and its ground stations, bdjobs and chakri.com job portals, Daraz and Bikroy e-commerce, the Walton factory's automated production line, the National ID card used to teach primary keys and its database used to teach RDBMS at work, a full Bangla Unicode table and a worked example of putting Bangla text on a web page with meta charset, the national letter-grade scale (A+ from 80) as the else-if programme, "I Love Bangladesh" as the loop example text, an algorithm for reaching college by bicycle or rickshaw, and the Digital Bangladesh programme — National Data Centre, hi-tech parks, Startup Bangladesh and Bangladesh Bank's QR-based Cashless Bangladesh. C is the HSC programming language, taught with scanf/printf in Code::Blocks.

South Sudan's own way (Grade 11 S3 Student): Year 3 is creation and programming: learners build and run a real personal website/blog end-to-end in WordPress (hosting, domain, themes, posts, legal issues and comment moderation), study social networks platform by platform (LinkedIn, Google+ and the Students Circle Network, with South Sudanese business use), then learn programming through data types, arrays and structures in C/C++ and writing programs in Notepad, closing on ethical conduct and intellectual property.


Kazakhstan's own way (Salgaraeva G10 Informatika, natural-science track): in Grade 10 the subject splits by track; this natural-science book is the deepest in the set — networks with IP, domains and VPN, number systems and logic with truth tables and the computer's logical elements, procedures/functions, strings, files, sorting methods and graph algorithms, a full HTML/CSS/scripts web part, and information systems from Big Data to linking web pages with a database. Every topic is doubled with a practicum paragraph.

Kazakhstan's own way (Salgaraeva G10 Informatika, humanities track): the humanities-track Grade 10 book replaces programming and databases with media production: a short information-security part, then shooting and editing video as a project, design theory ("good design", graphics for the web, developing a site's design), and building, publishing and promoting a real website. Each part closes with sample summative-assessment tasks.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kolyeva G10 Informatika, humanities track): Mektep's humanities-track Grade 10 book mirrors the Arman-PV one (information security, video content, design theory, web design) and adds a fifth part no other humanities book has: artificial intelligence and blockchain — machine-learning principles, how neural networks are organised and work, the fields where AI is used, and blockchain technology.
