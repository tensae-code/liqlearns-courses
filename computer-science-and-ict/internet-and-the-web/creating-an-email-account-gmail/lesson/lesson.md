# Creating an email account — Gmail sign-up

## Do it first

Before you touch a keyboard: list everything a new account will ask you for. Name, a username, a password — what else? Keep your list. The twelve steps below will check it, one by one, in the order the form asks.

## First — what email is, and who offers accounts

**Email** sends and receives messages using the internet. It is an **efficient, inexpensive, real-time** way of distributing information. Many services offer **free accounts** — **Yahoo, Outlook and Gmail** are the services pictured. This lesson creates a **Gmail** account, in the twelve steps, in their exact order.

[IMAGE 11728 — the twelve Gmail steps]

## Sub lesson 1 — Steps 1–4 — open the form and name yourself

Do the steps in order — do not skip, do not jump ahead:

1. **Visit the Google account creation page.**
2. **Click Create account.**
3. **On the sign-up form, enter your first and last name.**
4. **Choose a username for the account.**

## Sub lesson 2 — Steps 5–9 — password and verification

5. **Enter a password and type it again to confirm it.** (The instruction given: use **8 or more characters, mixing letters, numbers and symbols**.)
6. **Click Next.**
7. **Enter a phone number to verify the account.**
8. **Enter the verification code sent by text message and tap Verify.**
9. **Enter your date of birth.**

## Sub lesson 3 — Steps 10–12 — finish and agree

10. **Choose gender.**
11. **Click Next.**
12. **Read the terms of service and privacy policy, and click "I agree".**

The account is created. The basic information the whole sign-up needed was: **name, birth date, gender, location, and a chosen name for the new Gmail address**.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the twelve steps in order, without looking. Your companion follows on the list and stops you at any step you miss or swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the twelve steps in order from memory, then check them against the lesson.
2. List the five pieces of basic information a Gmail sign-up needs.
3. Write a password that follows the rule — 8 or more characters mixing letters, numbers and symbols — for practice only, not a real one you use.
