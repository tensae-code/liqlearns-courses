# Lesson plan — Internet Services — and What You Need to Connect

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Internet and the Web · **Lesson:** Internet Services — and What You Need to Connect
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Sudan (2026-11-14) — in the title Internet and the Web on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Sudan's Grade 7 ICT student book, because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The internet earns its place through its services — the things it does for its users: the World Wide Web of pages, electronic mail, searching for information, talking and sending files across the world, and the e-services of governments and banks. Joining the internet takes a kit of things together: a device — a computer or phone; a connection to carry the signals — a line or wireless link with its modem or adaptor; an account with a service provider that lets your device onto the network; and a browser to use the web once you arrive.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Internet services and advantages** — The **internet** earns its place through its **services** — the things it does for its users: the **World Wide Web** of pages, **electronic mail**, searching for information, talking and sending files across the world, and the e-services of governments and banks. Its advantages follow from the joining itself: information from everywhere, communication that is fast and cheap, and services reached from home. Sudan's Grade 7 book opens its internet unit with this lesson — what the internet gives, before how it works.
2. **What you need to connect** — Joining the internet takes a kit of things together: a **device** — a computer or phone; a **connection** to carry the signals — a line or wireless link with its modem or adaptor; an **account with a service provider** that lets your device onto the network; and a **browser** to use the web once you arrive. Miss one and the rest cannot help you: the checklist is the lesson, and it is also the first thing to check, in order, when a connection fails.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

1. **Do it first** — Before a journey you check what you need: vehicle, road, fuel, driver. Joining the internet has the same kind of checklist. In this lesson you learn what the internet gives — and the four things you must have before you can take any of it. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
