# Saving a Presentation as a Web Page — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What job does this section do?**
**Answer: Publication — turning a presentation into pages anyone can view**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What does a presentation saved as a web page become?**
**Answer: A small site — the slides as pages, in the talk's order**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What does the viewer need?**
**Answer: Only a browser — no presentation program and no presenter**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What carries over in the saving?**
**Answer: The slides' content and look — text, pictures and order**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) What does not fully carry over?**
**Answer: The live performance — the speaker's words, timing, and program-run effects**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) How must a web-bound slide differ?**
**Answer: It must speak for itself, with the explanation written on or under it**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What is the readiness check?**
**Answer: Walking the saved pages as a stranger would**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) Where does the viewer start?**
**Answer: At the first page, moving through with the links the saving provides**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) Why publish this way at all?**
**Answer: So anyone with the link can view the talk, without the program or the room**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) Which rung of the book's ladder is this?**
**Answer: The second easy-tool rung — after Word pages, before hand-written HTML**
