# Lesson plan — The parts of an email address

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Internet and the Web · **Lesson:** The parts of an email address
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 8 — Internet and the Web of the Computer Science and ICT ladder (TITLES.md); Ethiopia Grade 8, Unit 4 — Internet. The ladder is the recommended order, never a forced one.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** every email address has the standard format username@domainname — the username the user chooses, the @ (at) symbol, and the provider's domain — written exactly, with no spaces, and capitals making no difference.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands directly on the sign-up lesson — the learner has just chosen a username; this lesson takes that username and builds the full address around it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds, looping.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Creating an email account — Gmail sign-up. Recommended, never forced. A learner can land here directly; the lesson stands alone.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Write the standard format: username@domainname.
2. Split a given address into username, @ and domain.
3. State the rules: enter exactly, no spaces, not case sensitive — and what happens when an address is wrong.

## If the learner is stuck
- Puts the provider before the @ → the username always comes first; the domain — who hosts the account — comes after the @.
- Leaves a space 'to make it clear' → spaces are never allowed; an address is one unbroken string.
