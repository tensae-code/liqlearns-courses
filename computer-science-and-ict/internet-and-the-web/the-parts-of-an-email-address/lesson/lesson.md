# The parts of an email address

## Do it first

Here is an address: abdisa.aga@gmail.com. It is built from parts, like a house from bricks. Split it into its parts as your best guess — where does one part end and the next begin? Then check your split against the standard format.

## The standard format — username@domainname

Each email user has a **unique name** for their account: the **email address**. Its standard format is **username@domainname** — three parts in a row: the **username**, which the user chooses; the **@ symbol**, read as **'at'**; and the **domain name**, the email provider's domain — the website hosting the account. In the worked example, **abdisa.aga@gmail.com** splits as username **abdisa.aga** and domain **gmail.com**.

## The writing rules — exact, no spaces, any case

An address must be **entered exactly**, or the mail **will not be delivered — or may go to the wrong person**. Two more rules: addresses are **not case sensitive** — capitals or small letters reach the same account — and **spaces are not allowed** anywhere in an address.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the three parts of the format, split abdisa.aga@gmail.com aloud, and say the three writing rules.

## Do It Yourself

1. Split two addresses you invent into username and domain.
2. Write one address wrongly three ways — a space in it, a missing @, a wrong domain — and say what goes wrong each time.
3. Write your own practice address in small letters, then in capitals, and say why both reach the same account.
