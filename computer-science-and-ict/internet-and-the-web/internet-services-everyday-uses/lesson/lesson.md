# Internet Services — Everyday Uses

## Do it first

Why connect at all? Mongolia's Grade 6 book ends its internet unit with the answer a pupil can use: the services. Before addresses and browsers grow technical in later grades, the youngest learners meet the internet as a set of useful services, each named by the job it does.

## Sub lesson 1 — Services for money, goods and learning

An **internet service** is a job done for you over the network. **E-banking** moves and checks money through the bank's site instead of its counter. **E-shopping** buys goods from online shops, chosen and paid for from home. **E-learning** teaches over the network — lessons, exercises and courses, including the Mongolian school sites the book's pupils visit. Each service replaces a journey with a connection: the counter, the shop and the classroom come to the computer.

In your mind's eye: services for money, goods and learning.

## Sub lesson 2 — Weather and search — the information services

The **weather service** answers what the sky will do — the book's own example is Mongolia's tsag-agaar.mn, checked by herders and travellers alike. **Search services** find information: name what you need and the service returns the pages that hold it — the door to every other service, since a service you cannot find is a service you cannot use. Mongolia's closing point for Grade 6: the internet is not one thing but a market of services, and the skilled user is the one who can name the right service for the job in hand.

In your mind's eye: weather and search — the information services.
