**Acceptance count: taught in 4 countries (Afghanistan, Bhutan, Pakistan, Kazakhstan).**

Placement choice: this lesson was built new for the ICT expansion (Afghanistan, 2026-11-14) because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree was checked for the slug and for near matches before building. It sits in the Internet and the Web title (internet-and-the-web/), following the book's own order.

Afghanistan's own way (Grade 12): the final year turns outward — computers in society (e-commerce, communications, even military systems), system software and virus protection, then Excel for the first time and a closing block on the Web, e-mail and browsers (taught through Internet Explorer).

Mauritania's own way (Grade 9): The final secondary year is five practical blocks in 30-minute fiches — a full PowerPoint sequence, internet ethics, a first formal algorithms unit (variables, conditions, loops, writing and testing algorithms, no named programming language), simple networks, and email with attachments — each block closed by evaluation fiches.

Bhutan's own way (Class VII, Literacy with ICT student book): Social media is taught hands-on — learners create a Facebook account, post with audience settings, and follow netiquette and an Acceptable Use Policy; backup is taught to Google Drive, and the end-of-year experience is shared on Facebook.


Pakistan's own way (Grade 7 Computer Science 7): Grade 7 opens with emerging technologies and a deep ports-and-peripherals hardware treatment, then teaches email all the way to DKIM/SPF/DMARC authentication and POP3/IMAP/SMTP protocols; binary data representation (ASCII/Unicode, RGB colour, sound sampling) is taught inside the Programming unit, and entrepreneurship becomes business-plan craft — promotion, value proposition, quality assurance, SEO and platform business models, with Twitter and Snapchat origin stories.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kobdikova G4 ICT): Grade 4 ICT deepens every primary strand at once: Scratch grows variables, logical and comparison operators on the way to "My game"; EV3 robotics is taught through its sensors (colour, ultrasonic) towards labyrinth and kegeling; video is a real subject in the NCH VideoPad editor; and the final part pairs e-mail file exchange with password reliability and "computers of the future". Augmented-reality markers (the AR+ app from arman-pv.kz) animate pictures through a phone.
