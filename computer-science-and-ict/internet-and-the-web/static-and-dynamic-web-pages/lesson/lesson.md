# Static and dynamic web pages

## Do it first

Open the same page in your mind twice: once today, once tomorrow. On one kind of page, tomorrow shows exactly what today showed. On the other kind, tomorrow brings something new. Guess the names of the two kinds before you read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — Static pages — read, but do not change

A **static web page** contains only static information. You can **read it, but you cannot modify it or interact with it**. It **stays the same until it is updated** by the person who made it. A static page is the right kind **when information no longer needs to change** — a finished notice that will read the same next month as it reads today.

## Sub lesson 2 — Dynamic pages — new content by itself

A **dynamic web page** contains information that **changes depending on who is viewing it**: the viewer, the **time of day**, the **time zone**, and the viewer's **native language** can all change what the page shows. It **changes automatically, daily**, to give visitors **new content**. Watch the GIF: the static page sits still while the dynamic page refreshes into new content.

Tell your companion the two kinds of page, what each one does, and one page of each kind you have met.

## Do It Yourself

1. Sort four pages you know into static or dynamic, and say why each belongs where you put it.
2. Write one piece of information that should sit on a static page, and say why it no longer needs to change.
3. Write one piece of information that belongs on a dynamic page, and name what it changes with — viewer, time of day, time zone or language.
