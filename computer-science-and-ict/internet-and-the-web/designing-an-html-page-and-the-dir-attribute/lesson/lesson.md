# Designing an HTML Page — and the dir Attribute

## Do it first

Jordan's pupils write pages in Arabic — a language that reads right to left — in a markup born left to right. This lesson learns page design with direction at its heart: the dir attribute.

## Sub lesson 1 — Designing the page — structure before decoration

Designing an HTML page begins with its **structure**: the document's skeleton (head, body), its **sections** in a sensible order, headings marking the levels, and content placed where a reader's eye expects it. Only on a sound structure do the design choices sit: direction, alignment, spacing and the rest. Jordan's Grade 10 order — structure in the last lesson, design in this one — is the craft's own: a beautiful page with broken structure is a decorated ruin, and every later lesson (formatting, images, tables) builds on this frame.

In your mind's eye: designing the page — structure before decoration.

## Sub lesson 2 — The dir attribute — the page's direction

The **`dir` attribute** sets direction: `dir="rtl"` makes the page read **right to left** (Arabic, Hebrew), `dir="ltr"` left to right (English and most languages), set on the `<html>` element for the whole page or on an element for its part. Direction is not a mirror-image decoration: it moves the text's start, the alignment's home, the order of inline elements and the reader's whole path. A mixed page — an Arabic page quoting English terms — is designed by setting direction **per part**, so each language reads in its own direction inside one honest page: Jordan's pupils live in that mixture, and the book teaches it as a first-class design act.

In your mind's eye: the dir attribute — the page's direction.
