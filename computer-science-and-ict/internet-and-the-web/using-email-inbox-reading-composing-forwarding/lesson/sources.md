**Acceptance count: taught in 17 countries (Ethiopia, Cambodia, Mozambique, Afghanistan, Iraq, Egypt, Fiji, Bhutan, Libya, Mongolia, Sri Lanka, Rwanda, Laos, Greece, Bangladesh, Jordan, Armenia).**

Afghanistan's own way (Grade 11): grade 11 is the health-and-PowerPoint year — a full chapter on computer health and strain-relief exercises, then Word finished off (Format, Insert, Edit) before a five-lesson PowerPoint block and a first step onto the Web.

Nothing in this lesson claims a page the placements do not give; the lesson's content is the placements' content, re-expressed for the learner, and its closing activity is the book's own activity, kept as set.

Mauritania's own way (Grade 8): The second year is organised as five applied units of 35 thirty-minute fiches — image processing first (Paint/GIMP/Photoshop, bitmap vs vector), then internet ethics, a full Excel data-processing unit ending in a practical project, web source-checking and responsible downloading, and finally email — each unit closed by evaluation and support fiches with full answer keys at the end.

Mauritania's own way (Grade 9): The final secondary year is five practical blocks in 30-minute fiches — a full PowerPoint sequence, internet ethics, a first formal algorithms unit (variables, conditions, loops, writing and testing algorithms, no named programming language), simple networks, and email with attachments — each block closed by evaluation fiches.

Iraq's own way (Grade 11): the final preparatory year is the most forward-looking of the series — smart devices and the Internet of Things (with a lesson on choosing a computer or smartphone by its specifications), a full Excel unit ending in AutoSum, one- and two-dimensional arrays with nested loops (the series' first data structures), and electronic services: e-government, email and cloud computing with its applications.

Egypt's own way (Grade 7, Term 1 student book): One chapter ("Digital Programs and Applications") of 10 self-assessed lessons, each opening with "I can…" objectives and closing with Learn-by-doing; national platforms are the examples throughout — Digital Egypt platform, InstaPay, IDSC — and a separate Arabic activity booklet mirrors every lesson.

Egypt's own way (Grade 7, Term 1 workbook): A printed activity booklet is issued alongside the student book; the student book directs pupils to it by activity number after each sub-topic. The workbook is counted as its own book and mirrors the student book lesson by lesson (Lessons 1–9; no activity set for the Lesson 10 project).

Fiji's own way (Year 12 Computer Studies student book): Written locally by the same TEST/USP panel for the trialled Year 12 syllabus; the Internet chapter is grounded in Fiji (fiji.gov.fj URL anatomy with the .fj country code, and Fiji ISPs Connect Fiji, Unwired Fiji, Vodafone, Digicel and Kidanet by Fintel), programming is a full Visual Basic course with hands-on examples, and data management is taught as a move to cloud storage (Google Drive, Dropbox, SpiderOak).

Bhutan's own way (Class VI, Literacy with ICT student book): School computing runs on Google's G Suite for Education — email is Gmail on the school account, collaboration is Google Classroom, and the end-of-year food-chain project is agreed in Classroom, built in Word, password-protected, cited, filed in folders and reported to the teacher by email.

Libya's own way (Grade 9, Computer student book): the whole year is networks and the internet — a full networks unit (types, cables, topology, components), then Internet Explorer and Google search taught as named skills, then email and a closing tour of social sites (Facebook, YouTube, Twitter, Instagram, Yahoo) with practical activities.

Mongolia's own way (Grade 7): the whole applications half is one long project — research and produce a printed booklet "Усаа хайрлая" (Cherish the Water) in Word, a Paint.NET layered picture, and a class PowerPoint; image work uses the free Paint.NET (downloaded from getpaint.net) rather than MS Paint; the e-communication chapter teaches Yahoo mail and Yahoo Messenger, Mongolian history of the örtöö relay post and the @ sign, and makes correct Latin transliteration of Mongolian Cyrillic (standard MNS 5217:2003) an ethics topic in its own right.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 11, Tamil): a full Visual Basic 6.0 programming course inside the O/L book (IDE, controls, data types, Select Case, arrays), an SDLC chapter, Photoshop-based multimedia, hand-coded HTML with Sri Lankan examples (the Sinharaja rainforest page, ucsc.cmb.ac.lk addresses, sltnet.lk domains), and sector chapters (agriculture, industry, government, commerce) before malware and health.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 12 Science for Technology, Tamil): the A/L technology-stream ICT volume teaches every office skill twice — once in Microsoft Office 2010 and once in LibreOffice (Writer/Calc/Impress 4.1) — and closes with creating a real Gmail account, mailbox folders, attachments and password safety; hardware goes down to cache levels L1/L2/L3, ROM types and file systems (FAT/NTFS/ext4).

Rwanda's own way (S1 ICT Student Book, KLB): competency-based units each opening with a Key Unit Competency and keywords; a full GIS/ArcGIS unit and a Scratch music-making unit sit inside the first secondary year, and ICT use is grounded in Rwandan daily life.

Laos's own way (Grade 8 ICT student book): a full PC-technician year in full colour — learners take a computer apart and rebuild it (CPU, RAM, board, cards, drives), install Windows XP and drivers, fix install faults by BIOS beep codes, then finish with networks and the internet.

Greece's own way (Grade 7, Pliroforiki A' Gymnasiou, 2024): the new Gymnasium informatics course is built around algorithms from the first page (a Python binary example in Unit 1), then hardware/software, the internet with a full copyright/Creative Commons/AI-copyright sequence, cybersecurity with cryptography, word processing, algorithmics, Scratch storytelling/chatbot projects, and a long society unit (robots, drones, autonomous cars, e-government, AI in education/medicine).

Bangladesh's own way (Grade 7, Digital Technology): the Grade 7 book continues the Learning-Experience project format — intellectual property, virtual identity, cybercrime, robotics-through-pseudocode and e-services are each learned by doing a class project with the recurring robot character Nano. The NCTB Digital Technology line runs in parallel English and Bangla editions; placements are cited from the English Version with the Bangla Version parallel spot-checked.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 7 G7T1 Student): Grade 7 starts the whole subject from zero — what a computer is and how it works, hardware inside and out, software, operating systems and human-computer interaction — then networks from definition through topologies to secure communication and network performance.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 2 G2 student): A story-led primary book built around two Grade 2 characters (Hayk and Arpi); every lesson uses fixed activity "rooms" (key knowledge, key words, skills/tasks) and QR codes link to audio/video material; safety and health come before any machine skills.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 3 G3 student): Grade 3 (Stepanyan, Amirkhanyan, Matinyan) keeps the Hayk-and-Arpi story frame and QR-linked media: device safety, technology around us, picture and text editors, e-mail with attachments, input/output devices, keyboard keys, data collection with programs, then a Scratch strand — environment, first program, pictures, repeated actions, and game scores as a first step into variables.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 7 G7 student): A new author team (Topchyan & Melkonyan, 2025) rebuilds the course around the "virtual world": data and encoding, networks, e-security and e-communication, then a full Python chapter — decisions, lists, loops, functions, objects and classes — taught as making the real world virtual.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 7 G7 teacher): SERIES MISMATCH NOTED — this guide (Velijanyan & Aslanyan) is the teacher guide for the OTHER Grade 7 series, whose student book (§1 Electronic security … §23 Computer hardware maintenance, Python-based) is NOT among the records; it does not match the Topchyan & Melkonyan Grade 7 student book filed as Book 12.
