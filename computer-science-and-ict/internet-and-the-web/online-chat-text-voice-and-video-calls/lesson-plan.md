# Lesson plan — Online Chat — Text, Voice and Video Calls

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Internet and the Web · **Lesson:** Online Chat — Text, Voice and Video Calls
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Mongolia (2026-11-14) — in the title Internet and the Web on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Mongolia's student books (Ministry of Education, Culture and Science(s) of Mongolia, Ulaanbaatar), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree and live lesson content were checked for the slug and for near matches before building.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Online conversation in its three modes — written chat, voice conversation and video conversation — as Mongolia teaches it through Yahoo Messenger: live talk across a network, in text, in voice, or face to face on screen
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Text chat — live talk in writing** — **Text chat** is live written conversation: short turns, sent the moment they are typed, answered in the same minute. Its writing is its own dialect — brief, quick, forgiving of form — but it is **kept**: unlike spoken words, chat lines remain on the screen and in the record. Mongolia teaches the mode with its tool of the era, **Yahoo Messenger**: add your people, see who is present, and talk. The first rule rides with it: write nothing in the live line that you would not want read back later.
2. **Voice and video — the conversation grows senses** — The same conversation can rise in modes. **Voice conversation** sends the talk itself — tone, pace and feeling return, the written record goes. **Video conversation** adds the face: the far speaker is seen as well as heard, the closest the network comes to the room. Each rise costs more connection and more exposure, and Mongolia's pupils learn the choosing as well as the using: text to leave a quick word, voice to truly talk, video to truly meet — the mode matched to the moment, never merely the newest.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
