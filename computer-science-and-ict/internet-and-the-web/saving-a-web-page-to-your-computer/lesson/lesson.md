# Saving a Web Page to Your Computer

## Do it first

A web page lives on the internet — until you bring a copy home. Grade 2's séance teaches saving the whole page onto your own computer, so it is there when you need it, even with no connection.

## Sub lesson 1 — What saving a page keeps

To **save a web page** is to keep **a copy of the page — its words and pictures — as a file on your own computer**. Before saving, the page is borrowed: it is shown to you from far away, and disappears when the browser closes or the connection stops. After saving, the copy is **yours to open again** from your files. The saved copy does not change when the real page changes — it is the page as it was on the day it was saved.

## Sub lesson 2 — Saving the page — and finding it again

The saving is done **from the browser**, while the wanted page is open: the browser's **save** command keeps the page, and the learner gives the copy **a name that says what the page is** and puts it **in the right folder** — the file habits of the course apply to pages as to every file. Afterwards the learner **opens the saved copy from the folder**, without the internet, and checks the words and pictures arrived whole. A saved page that cannot be found again was not saved properly.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: A copy of the page as a file. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: Save from the browser. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: Name it for what it is. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: Open it again to prove it. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
