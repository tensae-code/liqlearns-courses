# Designing an Application Framework (Wireframe)

## Do it first

Builders of houses draw the frame before mixing concrete. Malaysia's web chapter designs the application's frame the same way — as wireframe drawings. In this lesson you design a site's skeleton: every page, its regions, and how the pages join.

## Sub lesson 1 — The wireframe — pages as skeletons

A **wireframe** (kerangka aplikasi — the application framework, drawn) is a **skeleton drawing of one page**: boxes for its regions (banner, menu, content, footer), labels for what each region holds, and **no decoration** — no colours, fonts or images beyond placeholders. One wireframe is drawn **per page**, in the same skeleton style, so the site's pages can be compared and their shared furniture (the standing banner and menu) checked for sameness. The wireframe is where layout arguments happen — while moving a box still costs an eraser.

In your mind's eye: the wireframe — pages as skeletons.

## Sub lesson 2 — Joining the pages — the framework whole

The framework is completed by the **joins**: each page's links drawn to their target pages (menu to every main page, each list item to its detail page), until the whole site stands as a **map** a builder can follow and a client can judge. Malaysia's design step ends with that map reviewed against the brief: every promised page present, every link landing somewhere real, no page reachable by no route (an orphan). Only then does building begin — pages coded from their wireframes, styled after their skeletons stand — because a framework fault found in code costs the rebuild this drawing exists to prevent.

In your mind's eye: joining the pages — the framework whole.
