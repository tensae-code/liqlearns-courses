# Links within a page — jumping to a section

## Do it first

Make a page long — three sections, one after another, long enough to scroll. Now add a link at the top that jumps you straight to section three, without scrolling. That jump is this lesson: a link whose destination lives on the same page. The guide's note (teacher guide — evidence): the page must be made long enough to scroll, or you cannot feel what in-page links are for.

## Sub lesson 1 — Naming a location, then linking to it

Links to sections of the same page work in two moves:

1. The **anchor element names a location** with its **name attribute**, placed **at that spot**.
2. A **second anchor links to it** with a **reference value made of the # symbol followed by that name**.

Clicking **scrolls the reader straight there** instead of hunting up and down the page.

## Sub lesson 2 — The practice shapes — a ring of pages, a sectioned page

The book's practice works both directions of travel: **three separate documents linked to each other in all directions**, then **a three-section page with links to the top and between section starts**. The guide solves both in full (teacher guide — evidence): three page files interlinked in a ring, and one long page with named sections and jump links between them.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the two moves of an in-page link — naming the location, then linking with # — and why the page must be long for the lesson to work.

## Do It Yourself
1. Build a three-section page with a link to the top and links between section starts.
2. Link three separate documents to each other in all directions.
3. Add a named location in the middle of a page and a # link that lands exactly on it.
