# HTML Headings and Paragraphs

## Do it first

A page of bare text is a wall. Headings break it into named parts; paragraphs break it into thoughts. Malaysia's Grade 7 HTML unit teaches the two tag families that do the breaking. In this lesson you mark a page's headings and paragraphs the HTML way.

## Sub lesson 1 — Headings — h1 to h6

HTML gives **six levels of heading**, written **<h1>** to **<h6>**: h1 is the biggest, most important heading — usually the page's title — and each level down is smaller and lower in rank. A heading is an **element**: an opening tag, the heading text, and a closing tag — <h1>My School</h1>. Browsers show headings bold and sized by level, with space around them. The levels are a **rank**, not just sizes: a page's headings should step down in order, h1, then h2, then h3, like a book's chapters and sections.

In your mind's eye: headings — h1 to h6.

## Sub lesson 2 — Paragraphs — the p element

Ordinary text is wrapped in the **paragraph element, <p>…</p>**. The browser shows each paragraph as a block with space after it, and — the rule beginners trip on — **line breaks and extra spaces typed inside the HTML are not shown**: the browser gathers running spaces into one and flows the text to the window's width. New paragraph, new <p> element — that, not the Enter key, is how HTML separates thoughts. With headings above them, paragraphs carry the page's actual reading.

In your mind's eye: paragraphs — the p element.
