# Checking Information Is Reliable — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) When is information reliable?**
**Answer: When it is true, current enough for the question, and from a source that knows and can be checked**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) Why does Egypt call searching a journey?**
**Answer: A search rarely arrives in one step — the searcher compares at each stage, like a traveller checking signs**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) Is unreliable information always a lie?**
**Answer: No — stale pages, dressed-up guesses and repeated opinion fail the same test**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What is the source check?**
**Answer: Who published it, and would they know — named, expert sources outrank unnamed copiers**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) What is the agreement check?**
**Answer: Whether another, independent source says the same**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What is one page, in evidence terms?**
**Answer: A claim — two independent pages are evidence**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What is the date check?**
**Answer: When it was made or updated, and whether the question needs newer**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) Name Egypt's three young-searcher checks**
**Answer: Source, agreement and date**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) What later course lessons grow from these checks?**
**Answer: Research reports and bibliographic work — the same checks grown up**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) What is this lesson's real product — a warning or a habit?**
**Answer: A habit — checking everything that will be used**
