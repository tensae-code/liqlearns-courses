# Blogs and Wikis — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What is a blog?**
a) A website of dated entries by one author, newest first, with readers' comments  b) Blogger  c) An account and the entry — no printing house and no permission  d) The author's voice
**Answer: a) A website of dated entries by one author, newest first, with readers' comments**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) Which service does Fiji name for blogs?**
a) An account and the entry — no printing house and no permission  b) Blogger  c) The author's voice  d) Readers' comments
**Answer: b) Blogger**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What does publishing a blog entry need?**
a) The author's voice  b) Readers' comments  c) An account and the entry — no printing house and no permission  d) A website whose pages visitors may edit — written by its readers together
**Answer: c) An account and the entry — no printing house and no permission**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What leads in a blog?**
a) Readers' comments  b) A website whose pages visitors may edit — written by its readers together  c) Wikipedia  d) The author's voice
**Answer: d) The author's voice**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) What gathers under a blog entry?**
a) Readers' comments  b) A website whose pages visitors may edit — written by its readers together  c) Wikipedia  d) The program Wikipedia runs on, free for any group to run its own wiki
**Answer: a) Readers' comments**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What is a wiki?**
a) Wikipedia  b) A website whose pages visitors may edit — written by its readers together  c) The program Wikipedia runs on, free for any group to run its own wiki  d) A blog gathers comments around one voice; a wiki merges many hands into one text
**Answer: b) A website whose pages visitors may edit — written by its readers together**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What is Fiji's great wiki example?**
a) The program Wikipedia runs on, free for any group to run its own wiki  b) A blog gathers comments around one voice; a wiki merges many hands into one text  c) Wikipedia  d) So readers can see who changed what — and check the page as travellers check a sign others wrote
**Answer: c) Wikipedia**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What is MediaWiki?**
a) A blog gathers comments around one voice; a wiki merges many hands into one text  b) So readers can see who changed what — and check the page as travellers check a sign others wrote  c) A website of dated entries by one author, newest first, with readers' comments  d) The program Wikipedia runs on, free for any group to run its own wiki
**Answer: d) The program Wikipedia runs on, free for any group to run its own wiki**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) What is the blog–wiki contrast?**
a) A blog gathers comments around one voice; a wiki merges many hands into one text  b) So readers can see who changed what — and check the page as travellers check a sign others wrote  c) A website of dated entries by one author, newest first, with readers' comments  d) Blogger
**Answer: a) A blog gathers comments around one voice; a wiki merges many hands into one text**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) Why do wiki pages carry histories?**
a) A website of dated entries by one author, newest first, with readers' comments  b) So readers can see who changed what — and check the page as travellers check a sign others wrote  c) Blogger  d) An account and the entry — no printing house and no permission
**Answer: b) So readers can see who changed what — and check the page as travellers check a sign others wrote**
