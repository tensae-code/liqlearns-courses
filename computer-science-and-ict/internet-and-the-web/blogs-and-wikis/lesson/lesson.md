# Blogs and Wikis

## Do it first

Two web forms changed who gets to publish: the blog, where one voice speaks to the world, and the wiki, where the world writes together. Fiji's Year 12 web-technologies section names them together — Blogger, Wikipedia, MediaWiki. This lesson takes both.

## Sub lesson 1 — Blogs — one voice, dated, answered

A **blog** is a website of **dated entries by one author** (or a small team), newest first, where readers answer in **comments**. Fiji names **Blogger** as the standing service: open an account, write an entry, publish — no printing house, no permission. The blog's bargain is its shape: the author's voice leads, the entries pile into an archive, and anyone may read — so a blog is publishing with the gate removed, for a school club, a teacher, or a pupil with something to say.

## Sub lesson 2 — Wikis — pages everyone may edit

A **wiki** is a website whose pages **visitors may edit** — not only read — so the text is **written by its readers together**. **Wikipedia** is the great example: an encyclopedia with no office of authors, corrected and grown by its users; **MediaWiki** is the program it runs on, free for any group to run its own wiki. Fiji's contrast is the lesson: a blog gathers *comments around* one voice; a wiki merges *many hands into* one text — which is why a wiki's pages carry histories, and why its readers check them the way travellers check a sign other travellers wrote.
