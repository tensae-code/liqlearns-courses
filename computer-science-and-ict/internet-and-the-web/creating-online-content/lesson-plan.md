**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Internet and the Web · **Lesson:** Creating Online Content
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Armenia (2026-11-14) — in the title Internet and the Web on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Armenia's Digital Literacy and Computer Science books (Armenia Grade 8 G8 student, Armenia Grade 8 G8 teacher), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree and live lesson content were checked for the slug and for near matches before building.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** creating online content — planning, making and publishing material for the web that is useful, honest and safe to share (Armenia Grade 8).
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Plan before making — audience, purpose, form** — **Online content** is anything published on the web: text, pictures, audio, video, pages. Good content starts with three decisions: the **audience** (who it is for), the **purpose** (what it should do — inform, teach, persuade, entertain), and the **form** that fits both (a post, a page, a short video, a gallery). Armenia's Grade 8 book teaches 'Creating online content' (§6) right after creating a blog — first one kind of making, then making in general.
2. **Publish responsibly — honesty, credit, safety** — Publishing carries duties: **honesty** (publish what you know, mark what you guess), **credit** (name the makers of anything you reuse, and reuse only what you may), **safety** (no private data — yours or others' — no addresses, no school-and-home details of children), and **care after publishing**: read responses, correct mistakes visibly, and take down what proves harmful. The creator's name travels with the content — Armenia's guide treats creating online content as a standard theme of its own (8-5), not a free-time extra.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Anything published on the web — text, pictures, audio, video, pages
2. Audience, purpose and form
3. What the content should do — inform, teach, persuade or entertain
4. The form must fit both — a how-to for children and a report for officials should not look the same
