# Lesson plan — The parts of a web address

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Internet and the Web · **Lesson:** The parts of a web address
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 8 — Internet and the Web in the Computer Science and ICT ladder (TITLES.md); stocked from Ethiopia's Grade 7 book, Unit 4 — Internet. The ladder is the recommended order, never a forced one.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a web address has four main parts — protocol (http://), www, domain name and domain extension — and the extension tells the site's type: .org, .edu, .gov, .net, and .et for Ethiopia.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the browser-window lesson — addresses are typed in the address bar that lesson named.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or in steps — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Break any address into its four parts.
2. Name what each part is: protocol, www, domain name, extension.
3. Say what .org, .edu, .gov, .net and .et each identify.
4. Read http://www.moe.gov.et part by part.

## What you need

## If the learner is stuck
- Reads the address as one word → always split at the marks: after ://, after www., and at the final dots.
- Mixes domain name and extension → the name says WHO (google, moe); the extension says WHAT KIND (.com, .gov.et).
