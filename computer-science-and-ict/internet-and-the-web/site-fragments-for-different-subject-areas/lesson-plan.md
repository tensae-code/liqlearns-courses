# Lesson plan — Site Fragments for Different Subject Areas

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Internet and the Web · **Lesson:** Site Fragments for Different Subject Areas
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Belarus (2026-11-14) — in the title Internet and the Web on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Belarus's *Інфарматыка* Grade 11, because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The web course ends by applying everything to real content: learners build fragments — working pieces — of sites for different subject areas, choosing each fragment's structure, text and images to fit its subject's own material.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the Belarus book's own order — it follows the book's sections in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What a site fragment is** — A **fragment** is a working piece of a site: not the whole finished site, but a complete, linked part of one — a page or small group of pages that already does its job. Building fragments is the practicum form of this course: design, page-making, prepared images and links are all exercised at once, on content that matters. A fragment is judged whole: it must open, read clearly, show its pictures, and link correctly — a beautiful page with a broken link is a failed fragment.
2. **Fitting the fragment to its subject** — **Different subject areas** give different material, and the fragment must fit it: a literature fragment is mostly text and portrait, a geography fragment leans on maps and tables, a biology fragment on labelled photographs. The maker's decisions repeat the course in miniature — who is this for, what structure serves it, which images must be prepared and how — but the answers change with the subject. That is the section's point: the web skills are not tied to one example; carried to a new subject, they still work.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
