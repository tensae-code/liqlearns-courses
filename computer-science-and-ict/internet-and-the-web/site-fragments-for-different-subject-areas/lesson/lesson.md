# Site Fragments for Different Subject Areas

## Do it first

Everything so far was practice on the course's own examples. Now the subjects themselves arrive — literature, geography, biology — each needing a piece of a site built its own way. In this lesson you build site fragments for different subject areas.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a site fragment is

A **fragment** is a working piece of a site: not the whole finished site, but a complete, linked part of one — a page or small group of pages that already does its job. Building fragments is the practicum form of this course: design, page-making, prepared images and links are all exercised at once, on content that matters. A fragment is judged whole: it must open, read clearly, show its pictures, and link correctly — a beautiful page with a broken link is a failed fragment.

## Sub lesson 2 — Fitting the fragment to its subject

**Different subject areas** give different material, and the fragment must fit it: a literature fragment is mostly text and portrait, a geography fragment leans on maps and tables, a biology fragment on labelled photographs. The maker's decisions repeat the course in miniature — who is this for, what structure serves it, which images must be prepared and how — but the answers change with the subject. That is the section's point: the web skills are not tied to one example; carried to a new subject, they still work.
