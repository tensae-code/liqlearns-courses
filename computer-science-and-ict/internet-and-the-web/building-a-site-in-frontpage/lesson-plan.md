# Lesson plan — Building a Site in FrontPage

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Internet and the Web · **Lesson:** Building a Site in FrontPage
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Belarus (2026-11-14) — in the title Internet and the Web on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Belarus's *Інфарматыка* Grade 11, because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** FrontPage builds a whole site, not single pages: its interface shows the site's pages and their links together, pages are made visually as in a word processor, and developing the site means growing the designed structure page by page and then publishing the whole.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the Belarus book's own order — it follows the book's sections in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The FrontPage way of working** — **FrontPage** is a site **editor**: where Notepad works on one HTML document at a time, FrontPage works on the **whole site** — its window shows the pages, the folders holding them, and the links between them, alongside the page being edited. The page itself is edited **visually**: text is typed and formatted, pictures and tables are placed, and hyperlinks are made by choosing their target — while the editor writes the HTML underneath, which can also be viewed and touched directly. It is the middle way of the book's ladder: more control than Word's conversion, less bare code than Notepad.
2. **Developing the website** — **Developing** the site (§ 5.4) is the build carried out in order: the design from the course's opening sections — purpose, audience, structure — becomes a real site skeleton first (the home page and the planned pages, linked as designed), then each page is filled with its prepared content — texts, the images prepared in § 4, tables where they help (§ 5.3) — and the links are checked by walking the site as a visitor. Only a site that walks cleanly is ready for the next step, publication (§ 5.5). The Belarus way is deliberate: the same site idea is carried from design, through Notepad by hand, and rebuilt here with the editor, so the learner sees what the editor does for them.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

Paper and a pencil, and a computer with the program this lesson uses, if available.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — You have designed a site, written a page of it by hand in Notepad, and prepared its pictures. Now you rebuild the whole site in FrontPage — pages, folders and links in one window. In this lesson you learn the editor's way of working, and you develop the full site in it. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of the FrontPage window with the site's pages and links on one side and the page being edited on the other — the student studies the picture and names what each part shows.
   - ADD GIF: a GIF of a site skeleton of empty linked pages filling up one by one with text and pictures — the student watches once, then says what changed and why.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the lesson's key names and definitions aloud, in the order the lesson gives them — the student listens once through, then reads the same lines aloud along with the voice. (Audio is a described slot in this build; no audio file is made.)
3. **Say it to your companion** — the companion block in lesson/lesson.md.
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the lesson in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-false, matching, short-answer — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the doing block at the end of lesson/lesson.md.
