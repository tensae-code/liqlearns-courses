# Lesson plan — The Value of Information — Timely, Complete and True

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** The Value of Information — Timely, Complete and True
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Mongolia (2026-11-14) — in the title First Steps on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Mongolia's student books (Ministry of Education, Culture and Science(s) of Mongolia, Ulaanbaatar), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree and live lesson content were checked for the slug and for near matches before building.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The value of information — information is worth what its qualities give it: it must be timely (arriving while it can still be used), complete (holding everything the decision needs) and true (matching the world)
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Timely and complete — value in time and in wholeness** — Information's value stands first on **timeliness**: information that arrives after its moment — the weather after the journey, the price after the purchase — is history, not help. It stands second on **completeness**: a report holding half the needed facts forces the decision it was meant to serve to be guessed at. Timely and complete are the value's outer walls: information late is useless however true; information partial is dangerous however fresh, because it invites confidence it has not earned.
2. **True — value in matching the world** — The third quality is **truth**: valuable information **matches the world** — its numbers are the real numbers, its statements the real state. False information is worse than none, because none sends you to look while false sends you onward, wrongly armed. Mongolia's triad is taught as a working test, the citizen's three questions of any information before it is used or passed on: Is it in time? Is it whole? Is it true? — the same three the property tables of earlier grades quietly required, here named as the value of information itself.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Grade 10's citizen book pauses on a question the younger books used without naming: what makes information valuable? Mongolia's answer is three qualities — and information missing any one of them fails in a different, nameable way. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of timely and complete — value in time and in wholeness as the lesson describes it — the student studies the picture and names what each part shows.
   - ADD GIF: a GIF of true — value in matching the world in action — the student watches once, then says what changed and why.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the lesson's key names and definitions aloud, in the order the lesson gives them — the student listens once through, then reads the same lines aloud along with the voice. (Audio is a described slot in this build; no audio file is made.)
3. **Say it to your companion** — the companion block in lesson/lesson.md.
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the lesson in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-false, matching, short-answer — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the doing block at the end of lesson/lesson.md.
