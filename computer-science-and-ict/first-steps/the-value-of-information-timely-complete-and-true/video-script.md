# Video script — The Value of Information — Timely, Complete and True

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Information's value stands first on **timeliness**: information that arrives after its moment — the weather after the journey, the price after the purchase — is history, not help. It stands second on **completeness**: a report holding half the needed facts forces the decision it was meant to serve to be guessed at. Timely and complete are the value's outer walls: information late is useless however true; information partial is dangerous however fresh, because it invites confidence it has not earned.
The third quality is **truth**: valuable information **matches the world** — its numbers are the real numbers, its statements the real state. False information is worse than none, because none sends you to look while false sends you onward, wrongly armed. Mongolia's triad is taught as a working test, the citizen's three questions of any information before it is used or passed on: Is it in time? Is it whole? Is it true? — the same three the property tables of earlier grades quietly required, here named as the value of information itself.
