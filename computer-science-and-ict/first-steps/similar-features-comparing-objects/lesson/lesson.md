# Similar Features — Comparing Objects

## Do it first

Hold up two pencils. Say how they are the same: both are long, both are thin, both are for writing. Those shared ways are their similar features. This lesson teaches you to find them.

## Similar Features — Comparing Objects

Objects have **features** — ways we can describe them, like their colour, shape, size and what they are made of.

When two objects share a feature, that feature is a **similar feature** of the two. Two pencils are similar: both are long and thin, and both are used for writing. A red ball and a red apple share a similar feature too: both are red, and both are round.

To compare objects, look at one feature at a time. Ask: is the colour the same? Is the shape the same? Is the size the same? Each 'the same' answer is a similar feature you have found.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Two objects can be alike in some ways: features they share — like colour, shape or size — are their similar features. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: say what a feature of an object is.
2. Show it, don't just say it: find at least two similar features shared by a pair of objects.
3. Show it, don't just say it: compare two objects one feature at a time: colour, then shape, then size.
