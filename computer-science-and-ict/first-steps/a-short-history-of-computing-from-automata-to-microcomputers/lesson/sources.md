Switzerland's own way (Grades 1–2, Décodage 1re–2e): Vaud's Romandie digital-education curriculum is taught in three axes — Science informatique (SI), Médias (M), Usages (U) — almost entirely UNPLUGGED at this age: algorithms are learned with the body (robot game, crane game, hand-washing, getting dressed, a crêpe recipe), a physical sorting machine, and the Blue-Bot floor robot; the class also builds a collective digital “charter book” of good digital habits across the whole cycle.

Switzerland's own way (Grades 3–4, Décodage 3e–4e): the 3e–4e volume repeats the 1re–2e unplugged activities at a higher level (loops and debugging join the robot and crane games and the sorting machine) and adds three new strands: Thymio robotics, a full ScratchJr scenario, and media “parcours” — Parcours des bestioles (how images influence emotions), Parcours du partage (image rights, digital traces, thinking before sharing) and Parcours de la pub (recognising and creating advertising). Pixel paravent introduces binary coding as transmitting a black-and-white image across a screen with an agreed code; Deviner la carte teaches the conditional through a magic trick and its logigramme.

Switzerland's own way (Grades 5–6, Décodage 5e–6e): Cycle 2 moves from unplugged games to real tools while keeping the game frame: the imaginary city “Square CT” teaches classification, decision trees, conditionals, indentation and flowcharts (logigrammes); ScratchJr tells emotion and climate stories; the Swiss Thymio robot is programmed in VPL; data is taught through binary pixel coding and colour coding; and “Enquêtes” (inquiries) teach addition-as-algorithm, debugging, sorting, the Nim winning strategy, Caesar ciphers and sensor experiments. Médias/Usages scenarios cover cyberbullying, screen practices, news and media stereotypes.


Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G6 Informatika): Grade 6 by the Almatykitap team drops the Scratch-game strand of the 2018 books: after systems/networks (including a lesson on Internet addiction) it teaches 3D modelling and 3D printing, a first Python course (IDE, syntax, data types, input/output, linear programs), and academic Word work — footnotes, hyperlinks, an automatic table of contents, copyright and plagiarism, and how to write a referat.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukhametzhanova G6 Informatika, 2018 edition): the 2018 Atamura Grade 6 course is built around making a real computer game across two whole parts — idea, scenario, stage and characters, implementation, sound accompaniment, a start page and improvements — with vector graphics in Inkscape before it and full Word documentation after it: footnotes, hyperlinks, an automatic contents and a formal game design document that teaches copyright and citing authors.

Kazakhstan's own way (Salgaraeva & Ilyasova G6 Informatika): the Arman-PV Grade 6 book runs the full 2018 programme in numbered paragraphs with a test after every part: systems and networks, presenting information (transmission, encryption, binary coding of text), vector graphics with a "Zhailau" drawing practicum, then the two-part game build in the Scratch environment, and a final Word part that adds references, copyright and plagiarism, a referat mini-project and the game document.

Kazakhstan's own way (Salgaraeva & Kopeeva G6 Informatika): the Arman-PV Grade 6 book follows the newer programme (systems and networks, 3D printing, Python, academic Word work) in 30 paragraphs with a test after each section; the 3D editor is SketchUp (the book's resource list points to gsketchup.ru and python.org), the history of computing is split into two paragraphs, and almost every topic is doubled with a "creating a project" paragraph.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukhametzhanova G6 Informatika, 2020 edition): the 2020 Atamura Grade 6 book drops the game project of its 2018 predecessor and follows the newer programme in four parts; its 3D strand is taught in Paint 3D (with Remix 3D models, the Mixed Reality Viewer and 3D-printer safety rules), and the Python part works in IDLE with type conversion (int/float) given its own space.
