# Embedded Computers in Everyday Devices

## Do it first

Your washing machine has no keyboard and no screen full of programs — yet something inside it decides the wash order, the timing and the spin. Sri Lanka's Grade 6 Reading Book names that something in its first chapter: a computer embedded — built in — to the device itself.

## Sub lesson 1 — An embedded computer — a computer built inside a device

An **embedded computer** is a computer **built inside another device**: a small processor put into a washing machine, a mobile phone, a car or a television **when the device was made** — not added later, and not used on its own. The user meets the **device**, never the computer by itself: you wash clothes, you do not 'use a computer'. The embedded computer is fitted to that one device — its size, its parts and its program all serve the device it lives in.

In your mind's eye: an embedded computer — a computer built inside a device.

## Sub lesson 2 — What the embedded computer controls — the device's own job

Inside the device, the embedded computer **controls the job**: in the washing machine it runs the **wash programmes** — the order of filling, washing, rinsing and spinning, and their timing; in the phone it runs the calls and the screen; in the car it helps control the engine as it runs; in the television it answers the remote control and shows the chosen channel. One device, one built-in brain: take the embedded computer out and the device stops being itself — a washing machine that cannot choose a programme is only a tub.
