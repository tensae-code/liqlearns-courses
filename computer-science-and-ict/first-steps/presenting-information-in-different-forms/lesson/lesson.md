# Presenting Information in Different Forms

## Do it first

Say 'There are five apples.' Now draw five apples. Now write the number 5. Three forms — one piece of information. This lesson teaches presenting information in different forms.

## Presenting Information in Different Forms

To **present** information is to show it to someone else. The same information can be presented in **different forms**.

Information can be presented as **speech** — telling it aloud. As **writing** — words written down. As a **picture** — drawing it. And with **numbers** — counting and writing the number. 'Five apples' can be spoken, written as words, drawn as five apples in a row, or written as the number 5.

Changing the form does not change the information. Five apples stay five apples in every form. A good presenter chooses the form that helps the listener most: a picture for someone who cannot read yet, writing for someone who must remember it later.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: The same information can be presented in different forms — spoken aloud, written as words, drawn as a picture, or shown with numbers — and the information stays the same. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: say what presenting information means.
2. Show it, don't just say it: present one piece of information in at least three forms: speech, writing, picture or number.
3. Show it, don't just say it: say why changing the form does not change the information.
