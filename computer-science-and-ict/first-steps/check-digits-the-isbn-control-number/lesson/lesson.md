# Check Digits — The ISBN Control Number

## Do it first

Copy a long book number by hand and swap two digits — the easiest copying error in the world. The bookshop's computer rejects your number instantly. Before reading on: how can one extra digit know you slipped?

## Sub lesson 1 — A digit computed from the others

An ISBN ends with a **check digit (chiffre de contrôle)** that is not free: it is **calculated from all the digits before it** by an agreed rule — for ISBN-10, multiply each digit by its position weight, add, and take the remainder against 11 (the check can even be the letter X, standing for 10). Type the number with one digit wrong and the calculation no longer agrees with the check digit: the number itself reports the error.

## Sub lesson 2 — Why check digits are everywhere

The same invention guards bank accounts, barcodes, identity numbers and card numbers: anywhere a human copies digits or a machine reads them, one computed digit rides along as a witness. The inquiry places the ISBN among “other similar systems for verifying strings of data” — parity's family: a little arithmetic spent at issue time buys error detection forever, at every till and terminal the number ever meets.
