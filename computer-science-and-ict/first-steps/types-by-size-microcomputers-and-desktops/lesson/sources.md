**Acceptance count: taught in 6 countries (Ethiopia, Tanzania, Eritrea, Afghanistan, Saudi Arabia, Iraq).**

Afghanistan's own way (Grade 10): a Windows XP-era office apprenticeship — one full chapter per application, taught menu by menu (File, Edit, Insert, Format), with the Dari keyboard language added as its own lesson; networks arrive only in the final chapter.

Afghanistan's own way (Grade 11): grade 11 is the health-and-PowerPoint year — a full chapter on computer health and strain-relief exercises, then Word finished off (Format, Insert, Edit) before a five-lesson PowerPoint block and a first step onto the Web.

Nothing in this lesson claims a page the placements do not give; the lesson's content is the placements' content, re-expressed for the learner, with the book's own examples kept.

Saudi Arabia's own way (Grade 3, continuing education): a workbook-style book for the continuing-education (adult/lifelong learning) track — every one of the 16 lessons opens with "I want to learn" objectives and closes with written exercises, and everything is taught on an older Windows desktop (Start menu, "My Computer", Recycle Bin) with Notepad as the only application, worked through step-by-step practicals in the school lab.

Iraq's own way (Grade 8): the same four-unit spiral one year on — the hardware unit becomes “the computer platform” and opens with choosing a computer's specifications before buying; Word is taught tab by tab (Home, Insert, Design, Layout); the algorithms unit advances to selection statements and logic truth tables, still landing in Scratch; the internet unit ends with writing a report from web research.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Grade 9): one TIA teacher guide carries two ICT subjects side by side — Communication Technology (networks, ergonomics, safety, societal impact) and Computer Technology (architecture, software) — organised by content standards and numbered benchmarks (9.4.x / 9.5.x), with the design brief used as the problem-solving method for both a product and a simple network, and typing assessed at 20 wpm with 80% accuracy.
