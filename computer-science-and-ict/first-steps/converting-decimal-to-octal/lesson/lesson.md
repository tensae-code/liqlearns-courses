# Converting Decimal to Octal

## Do it first

A decimal number must sometimes be written the octal way. Malaysia's Grade 8 book gives one method that always works: divide by eight, keep the remainders, read them upwards. In this lesson you work that method until it is yours.

## Sub lesson 1 — Divide by 8, keep the remainders

Write the decimal number and **divide it by 8**. Write the **remainder** — always a digit from 0 to 7 — to the side. Divide the new answer by 8 again, and keep going until the answer reaches **0**. For 25: 25 ÷ 8 = 3 rem **1**; 3 ÷ 8 = 0 rem **3**. The divisions are finished; the remainders, in order, are 1, 3 — but they are not yet the answer in that order.

## Sub lesson 2 — Read the remainders upwards — and check

The octal number is the remainders read **from last to first** — upwards: for 25, that gives **31₈**. The first remainder you found is the **last** digit of the octal number, its ones place; the last remainder is its first digit, the biggest place. Check by place value: 31₈ = 3×8 + 1 = 25. If the check fails, a division or the reading direction went wrong — the check is part of the method, not an extra.
