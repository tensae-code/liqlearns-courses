# ICT in education

## Do it first

Think of one thing you have learned outside a classroom — from a radio programme, a television lesson, or something you listened to. Hold it in mind: this lesson is about how ICT carries school itself to wherever the learner is.

## ICT in education

ICT improves education and widens it — it reaches learners the classroom walls would have kept out.

Ethiopia's own examples come first: radio and television school programmes, and plasma channels, carry lessons far beyond one school. 

[Picture 10113 — a classroom with an interactive board and a pupil listening to audio on headphones]

 Inside the classroom, computers, tablets, displays and interactive electronic boards put more in front of the learner's eyes than a chalkboard can.

And because a lesson on a device is not tied to a bell, learning can happen anytime and anywhere. One more widening matters most of all: audio lets a learner with a sight problem access information by listening. The lesson that cannot be seen can still be heard.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: ICT improves and widens education — Ethiopian radio and television school programmes and plasma channels, computers, tablets, displays and interactive boards — so learning can happen anytime and anywhere, and a learner with a sight problem can access information by listening. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
