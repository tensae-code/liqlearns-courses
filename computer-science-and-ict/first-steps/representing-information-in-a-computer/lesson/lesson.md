# Representing Information in a Computer

## Do it first

Look at a photo, a song and a page of writing. The computer stores none of them as paper or sound — first it must turn everything into a form it can hold. This lesson is how Iraq's Grade 7 book explains that turning.

## Sub lesson 1 — Everything becomes data the computer can hold

Before a computer can store or process anything, the information must be **represented** — turned into a form the machine can hold. Letters, numbers, pictures and sounds are all changed into **data** made of simple signals the computer's circuits can keep and move. That is why the same computer can hold a letter, a photo and a song: inside, all of them are represented the same way.

## Sub lesson 2 — Two states are enough

The computer's circuits work with **two states** — a signal is present or absent, on or off. Every piece of information is represented by **patterns of these two states**. The next lesson names the two-state digits themselves: the **binary numbers** that are the computer's own language.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Before a computer can store or process anything, the information must be represented — turned into a form the machine can hold. The computer's circuits work with two states — a signal is present or absent, on or off. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
