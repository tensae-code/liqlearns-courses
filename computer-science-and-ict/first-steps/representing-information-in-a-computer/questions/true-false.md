# Representing Information in a Computer — true-false

Say whether each statement is true or false.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "What must information be turned into before a computer can hold it?" is: Data made of simple signals**
**Answer: True**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "What is representing information?" is: In the computer's circuits**
**Answer: False** — Turning it into a form the machine can hold

**3. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "Which things does the book represent in this way?" is: Letters, numbers, pictures and sounds**
**Answer: True**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "Where is represented information kept and moved?" is: Two states**
**Answer: False** — In the computer's circuits

**5. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "Why can one computer hold a letter, a photo and a song?" is: Inside, all of them are represented the same way**
**Answer: True**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "How many states do the computer's circuits work with?" is: Patterns of the two states**
**Answer: False** — Two states

**7. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "What are the two states?" is: A signal present or absent — on or off**
**Answer: True**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "What is every piece of information represented by?" is: Every piece of information**
**Answer: False** — Patterns of the two states

**9. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "What does the next lesson name?" is: The binary numbers — the computer's own language**
**Answer: True**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "What can patterns of two states represent?" is: Turning it into a form the machine can hold**
**Answer: False** — Every piece of information
