# Representing Information in a Computer — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What must information be turned into before a computer can hold it?**
**Answer: Data made of simple signals**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What is representing information?**
**Answer: Turning it into a form the machine can hold**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) Which things does the book represent in this way?**
**Answer: Letters, numbers, pictures and sounds**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) Where is represented information kept and moved?**
**Answer: In the computer's circuits**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) Why can one computer hold a letter, a photo and a song?**
**Answer: Inside, all of them are represented the same way**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) How many states do the computer's circuits work with?**
**Answer: Two states**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What are the two states?**
**Answer: A signal present or absent — on or off**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What is every piece of information represented by?**
**Answer: Patterns of the two states**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) What does the next lesson name?**
**Answer: The binary numbers — the computer's own language**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) What can patterns of two states represent?**
**Answer: Every piece of information**
