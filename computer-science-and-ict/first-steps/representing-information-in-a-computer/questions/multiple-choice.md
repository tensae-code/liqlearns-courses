# Representing Information in a Computer — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What must information be turned into before a computer can hold it?**
a) Data made of simple signals  b) Turning it into a form the machine can hold  c) In the computer's circuits  d) Two states
**Answer: a) Data made of simple signals**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What is representing information?**
a) Letters, numbers, pictures and sounds  b) Turning it into a form the machine can hold  c) Inside, all of them are represented the same way  d) A signal present or absent — on or off
**Answer: b) Turning it into a form the machine can hold**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) Which things does the book represent in this way?**
a) In the computer's circuits  b) Two states  c) Letters, numbers, pictures and sounds  d) Patterns of the two states
**Answer: c) Letters, numbers, pictures and sounds**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) Where is represented information kept and moved?**
a) Inside, all of them are represented the same way  b) A signal present or absent — on or off  c) The binary numbers — the computer's own language  d) In the computer's circuits
**Answer: d) In the computer's circuits**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) Why can one computer hold a letter, a photo and a song?**
a) Inside, all of them are represented the same way  b) Two states  c) Patterns of the two states  d) Every piece of information
**Answer: a) Inside, all of them are represented the same way**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) How many states do the computer's circuits work with?**
a) A signal present or absent — on or off  b) Two states  c) The binary numbers — the computer's own language  d) Data made of simple signals
**Answer: b) Two states**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What are the two states?**
a) Patterns of the two states  b) Every piece of information  c) A signal present or absent — on or off  d) Turning it into a form the machine can hold
**Answer: c) A signal present or absent — on or off**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What is every piece of information represented by?**
a) The binary numbers — the computer's own language  b) Data made of simple signals  c) Letters, numbers, pictures and sounds  d) Patterns of the two states
**Answer: d) Patterns of the two states**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) What does the next lesson name?**
a) The binary numbers — the computer's own language  b) Every piece of information  c) Turning it into a form the machine can hold  d) In the computer's circuits
**Answer: a) The binary numbers — the computer's own language**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) What can patterns of two states represent?**
a) Data made of simple signals  b) Every piece of information  c) Letters, numbers, pictures and sounds  d) Inside, all of them are represented the same way
**Answer: b) Every piece of information**
