# Representing Information with Numbers

## Do it first

Before a computer can hold a letter, a sound or a picture, each must become numbers. This lesson is Uzbekistan Grade 8's bridge from number systems to coding: information written as digits.

## Sub lesson 1 — Everything becomes a number

A computer stores only numbers, so **information is represented with digits**: a quantity by its count, a yes or no by 1 or 0, a place on a grid by two numbers. Uzbekistan's Grade 8 book places this lesson between conversions and coding — the pupil first learns that numbers can *stand for* things, then learns the codes that do the standing.

## Sub lesson 2 — Counting the amount of information

Representing with numbers lets us **measure**: the amount of information in a message can be counted in its digits and symbols, and a bigger store holds more numbers than a smaller one. The same idea returns in the book's next lessons as volume of information and transfer speed — representation first, measurement second, code third.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: A computer stores only numbers, so **information is represented with digits**: a quantity by its count, a yes or no by 1 or 0, a place on a grid by two numbers. Representing with numbers lets us **measure**: the amount of information in a message can be counted in its digits and symbols, and a bigger store holds more numbers than a smaller one. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: Only numbers. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: 1 or 0. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: Measure the amount of information. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: In the message's digits and symbols. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
