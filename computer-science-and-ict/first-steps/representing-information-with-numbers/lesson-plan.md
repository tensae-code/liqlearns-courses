# Lesson plan — Representing Information with Numbers

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Representing Information with Numbers
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Uzbekistan (2026-11-14) — in the title First Steps on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Uzbekistan's informatics student books (Russian-language editions), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A computer stores only numbers, so **information is represented with digits**: a quantity by its count, a yes or no by 1 or 0, a place on a grid by two numbers.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Everything becomes a number** — A computer stores only numbers, so **information is represented with digits**: a quantity by its count, a yes or no by 1 or 0, a place on a grid by two numbers. Uzbekistan's Grade 8 book places this lesson between conversions and coding — the pupil first learns that numbers can *stand for* things, then learns the codes that do the standing.
2. **Counting the amount of information** — Representing with numbers lets us **measure**: the amount of information in a message can be counted in its digits and symbols, and a bigger store holds more numbers than a smaller one. The same idea returns in the book's next lessons as volume of information and transfer speed — representation first, measurement second, code third.
