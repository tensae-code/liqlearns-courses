# Lesson plan — How ICT Developed Over Time

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** How ICT Developed Over Time
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Yemen (2026-11-14) — in the title First Steps on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Yemen's *Computer* Grade 11, because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** ICT developed in stages: people first sent messages by hand and print, then instantly by electricity and radio, then through computers, and finally through the connected, pocket-sized networks of today — each stage faster and reaching further than the one before.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the Yemen book's own order — it follows the book's sections in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **From carried messages to instant signals** — For most of history, information travelled only as fast as a person could carry it: spoken news, written letters, printed books and newspapers. Electricity changed the speed: the telegraph, then the telephone, sent messages along wires almost instantly, and radio and television then carried voice and pictures through the air to many people at once. Communication was now fast — but the machines still only carried messages; they did not work on information themselves.
2. **Computers join, then connect** — The computer added the second half of ICT: a machine that stores and processes information, not only carries it. Computers shrank from room-sized machines to desks, then to laptops and phones, while growing faster and cheaper. The last stage joins the two halves: networks and the Internet connect the computers, so processing and communicating happen in the same pocket-sized device — the development Yemen's Grade 11 book traces before it turns to data itself.
