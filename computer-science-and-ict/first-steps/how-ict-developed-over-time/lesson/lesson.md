# How ICT Developed Over Time

## Do it first

A letter once took weeks. A message now crosses the world in a second. In this lesson you walk the road between those two — how information and communication technology developed over time.

## Sub lesson 1 — From carried messages to instant signals

For most of history, information travelled only as fast as a person could carry it: spoken news, written letters, printed books and newspapers. Electricity changed the speed: the telegraph, then the telephone, sent messages along wires almost instantly, and radio and television then carried voice and pictures through the air to many people at once. Communication was now fast — but the machines still only carried messages; they did not work on information themselves.

## Sub lesson 2 — Computers join, then connect

The computer added the second half of ICT: a machine that stores and processes information, not only carries it. Computers shrank from room-sized machines to desks, then to laptops and phones, while growing faster and cheaper. The last stage joins the two halves: networks and the Internet connect the computers, so processing and communicating happen in the same pocket-sized device — the development Yemen's Grade 11 book traces before it turns to data itself.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: ICT developed in stages: people first sent messages by hand and print, then instantly by electricity and radio, then through computers, and finally through the connected, pocket-sized networks of today — each stage faster and reaching further than the one before. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
