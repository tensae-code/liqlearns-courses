**Acceptance count: taught in 11 countries (Ethiopia, Tanzania, Mozambique, Yemen, Uzbekistan, Egypt, Libya, Mongolia, Rwanda, Greece, Indonesia).**

Yemen's own way (Grade 10): a skills ladder — every unit alternates short teaching topics with a named تدريب (guided training) and نشاط (activity), and Windows itself is taught as a school subject for a full unit before any application is opened.

The student book's text carries stray character runs typeset into it — runs of the letter k on printed pp. 2, 4 and 5 fall inside this lesson's pages; they are typesetting faults, carry no meaning, and are not taught. The teacher guide has the same fault family: a run of 'h' characters after the generations table (guide printed p. 4), also not taught. The guide's Unit 5 period figures contradict each other (its overview says 'seven (5) periods' while its sections add to 6); the road-map part of this lesson therefore names the guide's stated unit totals only, and files the Unit 5 contradiction as an open discrepancy, not a settled number. Nothing in this lesson claims a page the placements do not give; the lesson's content is the placements' content, re-expressed for the learner, with the book's own examples kept.

The Grade 8 teacher guide's Unit 1 answer key (printed p. 9) was used as the verified-answer check for this lesson's Grade 8 questions (teacher guide — evidence).

Papua New Guinea's own way (Grade 9): one TIA teacher guide carries two ICT subjects side by side — Communication Technology (networks, ergonomics, safety, societal impact) and Computer Technology (architecture, software) — organised by content standards and numbered benchmarks (9.4.x / 9.5.x), with the design brief used as the problem-solving method for both a product and a simple network, and typing assessed at 20 wpm with 80% accuracy. (teacher guide — evidence, PARTIAL)


Uzbekistan's own way (Grade 8): grade 8 is theory-first — number systems, coding and Boolean logic before the machine's own story — then a full operating-system chapter and a five-lesson Word course; safety rules return as a closing appendix.

Cuba's own way (Grade 2): Play-based (lúdico) introduction through the graficador (Paint-type drawing program) and educational software; correct workstation posture/ergonomics is taught explicitly in Grade 2, and files/folders are introduced intuitively through saving and opening drawings in a class-group folder. (teacher guide — evidence)

Egypt's own way (Grade 10, Term 1 Programming and Artificial Intelligence student book): A full Japanese-style "Information Study" course adopted for Egypt's new secondary track: 13 chapters running from information theory, ethics and security through number systems, data analysis (frequency, variance, correlation, regression in spreadsheets), simulation and queues, Python, and a final AI-programming project chapter building web pages, a quiz app and a block-breaker game with generative AI.

Libya's own way (Grade 8, Computer student book): a three-unit arc — computer history/generations and number systems first, then a whole practical unit built around the scanner (scan, save, copy, delete, scan via Paint), then Microsoft Excel 2007 taught step by step with screenshots. The basic-education line is الحاسوب (Computer) in Grades 8–9.

Mongolia's own way (Grade 12): the final book measures everything against Mongolia itself — the ITU index and Mongolia's slipping rank in it, 36,760 km of fibre to 299 soums, the Mongolsat-1 satellite, IoT chips on livestock, telemedicine at the State Central Hospital, the ХУР state data-exchange and ТҮЦ service machines, nano-products from horse bone and ger covers — and its chapter projects are real Mongolian systems: a school ICT-development survey, a study of the ebarimt VAT-receipt system, and a Small Basic "Bouncing Ball" game; programming examples draw the alkhan khee meander with a Turtle subprogram.

Rwanda's own way (S1 ICT Student Book, KLB): competency-based units each opening with a Key Unit Competency and keywords; a full GIS/ArcGIS unit and a Scratch music-making unit sit inside the first secondary year, and ICT use is grounded in Rwandan daily life.

Greece's own way (Grade 10, Efarmoges Pliroforikis A' Lykeiou): the older Lykeio applications book in four thematic units — hardware/software and applications, programming environments and creating applications (life cycle, modern IDEs, mobile-app and 3D object-oriented programming), communication and the internet (HTML5/CSS, learning online), and collaboration and safety online (cloud applications, telework, social networks, privacy).

Greece's own way (Grade 11 EPAL, Basika Themata Pliroforikis — student notes): the EPAL (vocational) informatics-sector notes go far deeper into digital technology than the general track — full data representation (number systems and base conversion, character encoding, compression, integer representation and overflow), image/sound/video representation and editing, animation, VR/AR, 3D printing and scanning, then the electronics ladder (Boolean algebra, logic gates, combinational and sequential circuits, adders, flip-flops, counters, A/D and D/A converters, integrated circuits and the CPU), ending with digital-technology applications sector by sector and ICT professions.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 10 G10 Informatika SMK Sem2): Semester 2 turns to algorithms in depth, the social side of informatics (history of IT, intellectual property, professions), and a full informatics project-management chapter — plan, monitor, evaluate.
