# Lesson plan — Computer generations — five periods of change

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Computer generations — five periods of change
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 1 — First Steps of the Computer Science and ICT ladder (TITLES.md); Ethiopia Grade 8, Unit 1 — Basics of Computer.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a computer generation is a period in the development of computers, marked by a major technological change in how computers operate — and there are five of them.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on nothing but the learner's own world: they have seen phones and computers change over the years. It is the first lesson of the course.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds, looping.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes up the order of the generations → say the key technologies in order as a chant: tubes, transistors, circuits on a chip, microprocessor, intelligence. Each new one replaced the one before.
- Thinks a generation is a model of one computer → a generation is a whole period of years, covering every computer made in that time.
