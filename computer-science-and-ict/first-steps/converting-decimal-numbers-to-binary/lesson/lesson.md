# Converting Decimal Numbers to Binary

## Do it first

Thirteen is 1101 to a computer. How do you get from one to the other without guessing? In this lesson you learn the divide-by-two method that always lands on the right binary number.

## Sub lesson 1 — Divide by two, keep the remainders

Write the decimal number and **divide it by 2**. Write the **remainder** — always 0 or 1 — to the side. Divide the new answer by 2 again, and keep going until the answer reaches **0**. For 13: 13 ÷ 2 = 6 rem **1**; 6 ÷ 2 = 3 rem **0**; 3 ÷ 2 = 1 rem **1**; 1 ÷ 2 = 0 rem **1**. The divisions are finished; the remainders, in order, are 1, 0, 1, 1 — but they are not yet the answer in that order.

[Picture — the divisions for 13 stacked up, remainders written down the side]

## Sub lesson 2 — Read the remainders upwards

The binary number is the remainders read **from last to first** — upwards: for 13, that gives **1101**. The first remainder you found is the **last** digit of the binary number, its ones place; the last remainder is its first digit, the biggest place. Check by place value: 1101 = 8 + 4 + 0 + 1 = 13. If the check fails, a division or a reading direction went wrong — the check is part of the method, not an extra.

[Picture — the remainders read upwards into 1101, checked by 8 + 4 + 0 + 1]

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: To convert a decimal number to binary, divide it by two again and again, write down each remainder, and read the remainders from last to first — that string of 0s and 1s is the binary number. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: By two. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: When the answer reaches 0. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: From last to first — upwards. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: As the last digit — the ones place of the binary number. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
