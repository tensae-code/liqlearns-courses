# Information — Questions and Tasks Review

## Do it first

Quick check: name one thing you saw, one you heard and one you read today. This lesson is the Information unit's own questions and tasks — its check-yourself review.

## Information — Questions and Tasks Review

This is the **review lesson** for the Information unit — the book's own 'questions and tasks' page. Each task set checks one lesson of the unit.

**Task set 1 — types and receiving.** Sort examples into information we see, hear and read. Then name the sense that receives a given piece of information: eyes, ears, nose, tongue or skin.

**Task set 2 — presenting and storing.** Present one fact in three different forms — speech, writing, picture or number — and say why the information stays the same. Name memory and two carriers, and say what each stores.

**Task set 3 — transmitting.** Tell how a piece of information travels from a sender to a receiver: who sends, what carries it on its way, and who receives it. If a task goes wrong, that task names the lesson to visit again.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: This review checks the whole Information unit: types of information, receiving it through the senses, presenting it in different forms, storing it, and transmitting it. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: sort information into see, hear and read, and match information to the receiving sense.
2. Show it, don't just say it: present one fact in three forms, and name memory and carriers as stores.
3. Show it, don't just say it: say how information is transmitted from a sender to a receiver.
