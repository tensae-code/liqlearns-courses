# Lesson plan — Information — Questions and Tasks Review

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Information — Questions and Tasks Review
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Azerbaijan (2026-11-14) — in the title First Steps on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Azerbaijan's *İnformatika 2, Grade 2*, because no existing lesson teaches this thing as its own lesson at this level.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** This review checks the whole Information unit: types of information, receiving it through the senses, presenting it in different forms, storing it, and transmitting it.
**This lesson's own level:** Grade 2 simple — it stands on the child's own everyday world and on the lessons named below; every new word is built inside the lesson from things the child can do at once.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill Grade 2 lesson; the 2026-11-09 ruling: sub lessons only where a lesson genuinely has distinct sequential parts).

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the Information lessons of Unit 2 in First Steps, from Types of Information to Communication in an ICT System. Recommended, never forced — a learner may land on this review directly, and each task names the lesson it checks.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Sort information into see, hear and read, and match information to the receiving sense.
2. Present one fact in three forms, and name memory and carriers as stores.
3. Say how information is transmitted from a sender to a receiver.

## What you need
A picture, a sound-maker, a page of words, a book and a photograph for the tasks. No computer is needed.

## If the learner is stuck
Mixes types with senses → types name how information is presented (seen/heard/read); senses name the organ that receives it — do task set 1 in two separate passes. Cannot name a carrier → revisit Storing Information — Memory and Carriers.
