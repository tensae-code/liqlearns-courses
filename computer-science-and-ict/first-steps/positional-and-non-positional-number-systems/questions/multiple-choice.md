# Positional and Non-Positional Number Systems — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) In a positional system, what does a digit's value depend on?**
a) Its position  b) Two hundreds, two tens and two ones  c) Decimal and binary  d) The base times the place before it
**Answer: a) Its position**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) In 222, what do the three twos stand for?**
a) Decimal and binary  b) Two hundreds, two tens and two ones  c) The base times the place before it  d) Because place multiplies a digit's value
**Answer: b) Two hundreds, two tens and two ones**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) Name two positional systems.**
a) The base times the place before it  b) Because place multiplies a digit's value  c) Decimal and binary  d) Nothing — it keeps the same value wherever it stands
**Answer: c) Decimal and binary**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) How is each place's worth fixed?**
a) Because place multiplies a digit's value  b) Nothing — it keeps the same value wherever it stands  c) 8 — five plus one plus one plus one  d) The base times the place before it
**Answer: d) The base times the place before it**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) Why can positional systems write any number with few digits?**
a) Because place multiplies a digit's value  b) Nothing — it keeps the same value wherever it stands  c) 8 — five plus one plus one plus one  d) Roman numerals
**Answer: a) Because place multiplies a digit's value**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) In a non-positional system, what does a sign's value depend on?**
a) 8 — five plus one plus one plus one  b) Nothing — it keeps the same value wherever it stands  c) Roman numerals  d) Big numbers grow heavy and rules do not carry them
**Answer: b) Nothing — it keeps the same value wherever it stands**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What is VIII in Roman writing?**
a) Roman numerals  b) Big numbers grow heavy and rules do not carry them  c) 8 — five plus one plus one plus one  d) Base two
**Answer: c) 8 — five plus one plus one plus one**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) Give an example of a non-positional system.**
a) Big numbers grow heavy and rules do not carry them  b) Base two  c) Its position  d) Roman numerals
**Answer: d) Roman numerals**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) Why are non-positional systems hard for arithmetic?**
a) Big numbers grow heavy and rules do not carry them  b) Base two  c) Its position  d) Two hundreds, two tens and two ones
**Answer: a) Big numbers grow heavy and rules do not carry them**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) What base is the computer's positional system?**
a) Its position  b) Base two  c) Two hundreds, two tens and two ones  d) Decimal and binary
**Answer: b) Base two**
