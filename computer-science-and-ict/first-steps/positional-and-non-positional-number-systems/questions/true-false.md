# Positional and Non-Positional Number Systems — true-false

Say whether each statement is true or false.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "In a positional system, what does a digit's value depend on?" is: Its position**
**Answer: True**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "In 222, what do the three twos stand for?" is: Decimal and binary**
**Answer: False** — Two hundreds, two tens and two ones

**3. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "Name two positional systems." is: Decimal and binary**
**Answer: True**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "How is each place's worth fixed?" is: Because place multiplies a digit's value**
**Answer: False** — The base times the place before it

**5. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "Why can positional systems write any number with few digits?" is: Because place multiplies a digit's value**
**Answer: True**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "In a non-positional system, what does a sign's value depend on?" is: 8 — five plus one plus one plus one**
**Answer: False** — Nothing — it keeps the same value wherever it stands

**7. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "What is VIII in Roman writing?" is: 8 — five plus one plus one plus one**
**Answer: True**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "Give an example of a non-positional system." is: Big numbers grow heavy and rules do not carry them**
**Answer: False** — Roman numerals

**9. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "Why are non-positional systems hard for arithmetic?" is: Big numbers grow heavy and rules do not carry them**
**Answer: True**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "What base is the computer's positional system?" is: Its position**
**Answer: False** — Base two
