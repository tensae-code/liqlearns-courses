# ICT in daily life — communication

## Do it first

Ask an older person in your family, or imagine asking: how did you send word to someone far away when you were my age? Hold their answer in mind. This lesson sets that old way beside today's way — and then walks ICT through health, farms and travel too.

## Sub lesson 1 — From letters to SMS, email and social media

ICT is not locked in a computer room. You meet it at home, at school, at work, in the street and in public places.

For communication, the change is easiest to see. Parents once wrote letters — paper, an envelope, days of waiting. Today the same message goes as an SMS, an email, or through social media, and arrives at once. 

[Picture 10111 — a handwritten letter beside a phone showing SMS, email and social media]

 Radio and television are also counted as communication ICT: one voice or one programme reaching many listeners and viewers at the same time.

## Sub lesson 2 — ICT in health, agriculture and transport (teacher guide — evidence)

The teacher guide works three more sectors. 

[Picture 10112 — three panels: a clinic record screen, a farmer reading a price SMS, a traveller checking flight information]



In **health**: patient records are kept in a computer database, so they can be retrieved easily when a patient returns. In **agriculture**: information flows better for rural development — a short SMS can give the day's market prices, and weather forecasts can reach farmers before they plant or harvest. In **transport**: travellers can access travel information — the guide teaches it through Ethiopian Airlines' travel information, and looks ahead to driverless cars.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: ICT is met at home, school, work, in the street and in public places — and for communication it has replaced the letters parents once wrote with SMS, email and social media, with radio and television also counted as communication ICT. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: name where ICT is met: home, school, work, the street, public places. Do this with a real example from your own home, school or street, and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: tell the book's contrast: letters then; SMS, email and social media now; radio and television counted too. Do this with a real example from your own home, school or street, and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: give the guide's worked examples for health (records in a database), agriculture (price SMS, weather forecasts) and transport (travel information). Do this with a real example from your own home, school or street, and write or say how you know it is right.
