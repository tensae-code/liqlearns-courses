**Acceptance count: taught in 11 countries (Ethiopia, Turkmenistan, Uganda, North Macedonia, Iraq, Sudan, Cuba, Bangladesh, Jordan, Indonesia, South Sudan).**

The student book's printed p. 7 — inside this lesson's page range — carries a garbled sidebar block ending in nonsense characters ("dddddd dsdfffdf"), a typesetting fault; it is not reproduced and nothing here rests on it.

North Macedonia's own way (Year 1): five themes move from modern digital technology and online living into a full structured-programming course taught in C++ (with Scratch/C++/Java/Python/PHP/Lisp compared), then advanced Word work (styles, contents, templates) and spreadsheets up to pivot tables.

Iraq's own way (Grade 8): the same four-unit spiral one year on — the hardware unit becomes “the computer platform” and opens with choosing a computer's specifications before buying; Word is taught tab by tab (Home, Insert, Design, Layout); the algorithms unit advances to selection statements and logic truth tables, still landing in Scratch; the internet unit ends with writing a report from web research.

Sudan's own way (Grade 4): a gentle first ICT book in six short units that ends not with applications but with the keyboard and mouse themselves — hand skills before software. The Bakht Al-Ruda ICT series runs in the same pattern from Grade 4 to Grade 9.

Cuba's own way (Grade 2): Play-based (lúdico) introduction through the graficador (Paint-type drawing program) and educational software; correct workstation posture/ergonomics is taught explicitly in Grade 2, and files/folders are introduced intuitively through saving and opening drawings in a class-group folder. (teacher guide — evidence)

Cuba's own way (Grade 5): The Grade 5 programme is built around extracting information from Cuban educational software, encyclopedias and EcuRed into word-processor documents, then Scratch — drawing/animation first (Unit 3), control/movement/appearance blocks in Unit 4. (teacher programme — evidence)

Cuba's own way (Grade 5): Same Grade 5 content as the Programa, recast as per-unit teaching tables with hour allocations; Unit 4 is renamed “Aprendiendo y creando” and taught purely as Scratch control, movement and appearance blocks. (teacher guide — evidence)

Cuba's own way (Grade 5): Student text mirrors the programme in four chapters; word-processing is taught as a research workflow (synonyms, symbols, searching, copying texts and images from sources), and Scratch is introduced through its paint editor before movement/appearance/control blocks.

Bangladesh's own way (HSC ICT, Classes XI–XII and Alim): one compulsory ICT book for every HSC and Alim student in the country, saturated with Bangladesh itself: Bangabandhu Satellite-1 and its ground stations, bdjobs and chakri.com job portals, Daraz and Bikroy e-commerce, the Walton factory's automated production line, the National ID card used to teach primary keys and its database used to teach RDBMS at work, a full Bangla Unicode table and a worked example of putting Bangla text on a web page with meta charset, the national letter-grade scale (A+ from 80) as the else-if programme, "I Love Bangladesh" as the loop example text, an algorithm for reaching college by bicycle or rickshaw, and the Digital Bangladesh programme — National Data Centre, hi-tech parks, Startup Bangladesh and Bangladesh Bank's QR-based Cashless Bangladesh. C is the HSC programming language, taught with scanf/printf in Code::Blocks.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 7 G7T1 Student): Grade 7 starts the whole subject from zero — what a computer is and how it works, hardware inside and out, software, operating systems and human-computer interaction — then networks from definition through topologies to secure communication and network performance.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 7 G7 Informatika student): Kurikulum Merdeka Informatika built on four elements (Berpikir Komputasional, Literasi Digital, Analisis Data, Algoritma dan Pemrograman), mixing plugged and unplugged activities with coded activity IDs (e.g. VII-BK, VII-LD) and QR-linked resources; Grade 7 covers only BK + Literasi Digital + data handling.

South Sudan's own way (Grade 9 S1 Student): Opens ICT at secondary level entirely through doing — the whole first year is Microsoft Office application skills (Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Publisher, Paint) taught as step-by-step keyboard/mouse procedures with local data (South Sudan state populations, local disease statistics for charts), then internet use, hands-on troubleshooting, and Windows access control/security settings.
