# Storage — keeping data, and the four functions together

## Do it first

You take a photo on a phone, look at it, and switch the phone off. Tomorrow you switch on — the photo is still there. Where was it all night? Say your answer, then read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — Storage — keeping data

Storage is keeping data on the computer's internal or external storage — a hard drive inside the machine, or a flash drive or compact disk outside it. The book's examples: a photo kept on phone memory, videos kept on CD, and the class roster kept on the hard drive.

## Sub lesson 2 — The four functions together

Now the whole cycle stands together: data is put in (input), worked on (process), shown as a result (output), and kept for later (storage). Watch the GIF: one piece of data travels round all four steps and comes back to the start, ready to be used again. The teacher guide's keyed answer to 'how does a computer work' says the same thing in brief: a computer is hardware and software together; it takes data in through an input unit under the instructions it is given, processes that data, and sends the result back out through an output device (teacher guide — evidence).
