# What a computer cannot do — no IQ, no feelings

## Do it first

A computer holds a whole library and never tires. Could it decide, by itself, that today it would rather write a poem than do your sums? Before reading on, say what the machine is missing that you have.

## The power of remembering

A computer has the power of remembering: any amount of information can be stored in it and recalled for as long as required. Closely tied to this is storage capacity: a large amount of data can be held in its in-built memory, ready to come back the moment it is asked for.

[Picture 10925 — remembering]

## No IQ, no feelings

But a computer has no IQ: it cannot do any work without instructions from the user. It does not think for itself. And it has no feeling: no emotions, no taste, no knowledge or experience of its own — and, as the diligence characteristic already showed, it does not tire after long hours. Every clever thing a computer does was instructed by a person.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the two strengths in this lesson (remembering, storage) and the two absences (no IQ, no feelings). What is the one thing every computer task needs from a person?
