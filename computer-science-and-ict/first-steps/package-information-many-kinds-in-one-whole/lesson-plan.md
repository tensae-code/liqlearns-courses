## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The package — kinds gathered about one subject** — **Package information** is **many kinds of information gathered into one whole** about one subject: the text tells, the numbers measure, the pictures show, the sounds and recordings witness — bound together by the subject they all serve. A package is more than a pile: its kinds **complete and check each other** — the caption explains the photograph, the table corrects the memory the text relied on. Mongolia's term names what every real task already handles: nobody knows a place by its map alone or its story alone, but by the package.
2. **The example — a subject packaged whole** — The book then **works an example**: one subject taken and its information packaged — the kinds collected, ordered, and held together so the whole can be used, stored and presented as one thing. The example teaches the package's discipline: every kind admitted must earn its place by adding what the others cannot say, and the whole must stay **one** — a reader who takes the package takes the subject entire, not whichever kind happened to be nearest. The chapters after this one are the package's working life: how it is processed, stored, and merged with other information into finished work.
