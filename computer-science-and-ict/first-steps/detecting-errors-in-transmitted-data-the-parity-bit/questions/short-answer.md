# Detecting Errors in Transmitted Data — The Parity Bit — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What is a parity bit?**
**Answer: One extra bit sent with each group of data bits**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) In even parity, what must the count of 1s be?**
**Answer: Even**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) How does the receiver use parity?**
**Answer: It counts again — wrong parity means something flipped**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What must the two sides agree beforehand?**
**Answer: Even or odd parity**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) What does the inquiry do in secret?**
**Answer: Flips one bit to watch the count expose it**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) Can parity correct an error?**
**Answer: No — it detects, but cannot say which bit flipped**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What slips past parity unseen?**
**Answer: Two flips — they restore the parity**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What is parity, in one phrase?**
**Answer: A cheap alarm, not a guard**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) What do stronger systems add?**
**Answer: More check bits and cleverer counts**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) What is every error-detecting code?**
**Answer: Parity's descendant — spare data watching the rest**
