# Describing an Object by Its Features — Riddles

## Do it first

Listen: 'I am round. I am red. You can eat me. What am I?' An apple! You guessed it from its features alone. This lesson teaches riddles: describing an object by its features so a listener can guess it.

## Describing an Object by Its Features — Riddles

A **riddle** is a guessing game. One person describes an object by its **features** — its colour, shape, size and what it is for — but does **not** say its name. The listener uses the features to guess the object.

A good riddle gives features one at a time, and each feature cuts the guesses down. 'It is round' — many things are round. 'It is red' — fewer. 'You can eat it' — now the answer is near: an apple, a tomato. The last feature should make only one object fit.

To make your own riddle, choose an object in secret, list three or four of its true features, and say them without its name. If a feature is not true of the object, the riddle is broken — every feature in a riddle must be true.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: A riddle describes an object by its features without naming it — and the listener guesses the object from the features. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: say what a riddle is.
2. Show it, don't just say it: guess an object from three features given one at a time.
3. Show it, don't just say it: make a riddle of your own: three or four true features, and no name.
