# Converting Binary to Octal — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) How are the bits grouped?**
**Answer: In threes**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) From which end does grouping start?**
**Answer: From the right — the places run from the right**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) Why must grouping start there?**
**Answer: Grouping from the left would misplace every value**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What if the last group has fewer than three bits?**
**Answer: It still counts — think of it as filled with leading zeros**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) What does the group 101 read as?**
**Answer: 5**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What is 100111₂ in octal?**
**Answer: 47₈ — groups 100 and 111**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What is 101010₂ in octal?**
**Answer: 52₈ — groups 101 and 010**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What is 111₂ in octal?**
**Answer: 7₈ — one full group**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) How are the two octal–binary roads related?**
**Answer: They are inverses — spreading digits into bits, and gathering bits into digits**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) What is 1101101₂ in octal?**
**Answer: 155₈ — groups 1, 101, 101 read as 001, 101, 101**
