# Lesson plan — Sources of Information and Who Receives It

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Sources of Information and Who Receives It
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Turkmenistan (2026-11-14) — in the title First Steps on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Turkmenistan's *Basics of Informatics, Grade 1*, because no existing lesson teaches this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Information always comes from somewhere and goes to someone: the one who gives the information is its source, and the one who takes it in is the receiver — a teacher speaking is a source, the pupils listening are receivers, and the same person can be a source at one moment and a receiver the next.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the Grade 1 idea of information itself — what information is — and on nothing else; source and receiver are built inside the lesson, in the child's own classroom.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The source — where information comes from** — A source gives information: a teacher telling, a book showing words and pictures, a radio playing, a sign on the road. If you can point to where the information started, you have found its source.
2. **The receiver — who takes the information in** — A receiver takes information in: the pupils listening, the reader, the person who hears the radio. The same person swaps jobs all day — receiving when listening, a source when speaking.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: From Data to Information in First Steps. Recommended, never forced — a learner may land on this lesson directly, and it stands alone.
- Turkmenistan's own way is kept: in Turkmenistan's Grade 1 book this comes in Chapter III, Information around us, straight after the concept of information — information is first met as something that comes from a source to a receiver, before any machine is needed.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a source of information is, and name sources: a teacher, a book, a radio, a sign.
2. Say what a receiver is, and point to the receiver in a small scene.
3. Say how the same person can be a receiver when listening and a source when speaking.

## What you need
Nothing but the people and things in the room — a teacher's voice, a book and a friend to talk with are the examples.

## If the learner is stuck
Thinks the receiver is a machine → the book's word is for the one who takes information in, and that is very often a person: the listener, the reader, the watcher. Mixes the two ends up → ask the two questions in order: where did it start (source)? who took it in (receiver)?
