# Minicomputers and mainframes

## Do it first

One till in a small shop serves one customer at a time. A bank's system serves thousands of customers at the same moment, in many branches. Before reading on: what must be different about the bank's computer?

## The minicomputer — the middle machine

A minicomputer sits between the microcomputer and the mainframe: larger and more powerful than most microcomputers, smaller and less powerful than a mainframe. It is a small general-purpose machine — 'general-purpose' is taken up properly in the lesson on general-purpose computers.

## The mainframe — many users at once

Mainframes are large, powerful machines. They process data at millions of instructions per second, serve many users at the same time, and handle vast amounts of data quickly. The typical users are banks, insurance companies, manufacturers and airlines — organisations where hundreds of people and huge amounts of data meet one system.
