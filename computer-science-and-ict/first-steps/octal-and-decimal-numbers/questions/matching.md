10. (Sub lesson 2) A number contains the digit 8. Can it be octal?

a) Only 0 to 7 — 8 and 9 never appear
b) 21 — 2×8 + 5×1
c) 8 — octal counts in eights
d) So the difference between the systems is stated plainly before conversions begin
e) 1, 8, 64, 512 — powers of eight
f) 1, 10, 100, 1000 — powers of ten
g) With a small subscript base — 25₈ against 25₁₀
h) 10 — decimal counts in tens
i) No — octal has no digit 8
j) Yes — 25 is 25 in decimal but 21 when read as octal
