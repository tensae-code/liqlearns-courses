# Octal and Decimal Numbers — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What is the base of the octal system?**
a) 8 — octal counts in eights  b) Only 0 to 7 — 8 and 9 never appear  c) 1, 8, 64, 512 — powers of eight  d) 21 — 2×8 + 5×1
**Answer: a) 8 — octal counts in eights**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) Which digits does octal use?**
a) 1, 8, 64, 512 — powers of eight  b) Only 0 to 7 — 8 and 9 never appear  c) 21 — 2×8 + 5×1  d) So the difference between the systems is stated plainly before conversions begin
**Answer: b) Only 0 to 7 — 8 and 9 never appear**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What are the first octal place values, from the right?**
a) 21 — 2×8 + 5×1  b) So the difference between the systems is stated plainly before conversions begin  c) 1, 8, 64, 512 — powers of eight  d) 10 — decimal counts in tens
**Answer: c) 1, 8, 64, 512 — powers of eight**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What is octal 25 in decimal?**
a) So the difference between the systems is stated plainly before conversions begin  b) 10 — decimal counts in tens  c) 1, 10, 100, 1000 — powers of ten  d) 21 — 2×8 + 5×1
**Answer: d) 21 — 2×8 + 5×1**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) Why does Malaysia teach octal beside decimal first?**
a) So the difference between the systems is stated plainly before conversions begin  b) 10 — decimal counts in tens  c) 1, 10, 100, 1000 — powers of ten  d) Yes — 25 is 25 in decimal but 21 when read as octal
**Answer: a) So the difference between the systems is stated plainly before conversions begin**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What is the base of the decimal system?**
a) 1, 10, 100, 1000 — powers of ten  b) 10 — decimal counts in tens  c) Yes — 25 is 25 in decimal but 21 when read as octal  d) With a small subscript base — 25₈ against 25₁₀
**Answer: b) 10 — decimal counts in tens**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What are the first decimal place values, from the right?**
a) Yes — 25 is 25 in decimal but 21 when read as octal  b) With a small subscript base — 25₈ against 25₁₀  c) 1, 10, 100, 1000 — powers of ten  d) No — octal has no digit 8
**Answer: c) 1, 10, 100, 1000 — powers of ten**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) Can the marks 25 belong to both systems?**
a) With a small subscript base — 25₈ against 25₁₀  b) No — octal has no digit 8  c) 8 — octal counts in eights  d) Yes — 25 is 25 in decimal but 21 when read as octal
**Answer: d) Yes — 25 is 25 in decimal but 21 when read as octal**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) How does Malaysia mark which system a number is in?**
a) With a small subscript base — 25₈ against 25₁₀  b) No — octal has no digit 8  c) 8 — octal counts in eights  d) Only 0 to 7 — 8 and 9 never appear
**Answer: a) With a small subscript base — 25₈ against 25₁₀**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) A number contains the digit 8. Can it be octal?**
a) 8 — octal counts in eights  b) No — octal has no digit 8  c) Only 0 to 7 — 8 and 9 never appear  d) 1, 8, 64, 512 — powers of eight
