# Video script — Number Systems — Positional Systems and the Binary System

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

In a **positional number system**, a digit's value depends on its **position**: in 222, the three identical digits are worth two hundred, twenty and two. The system's **base** is its number of different digits and the weight each step of position multiplies by: decimal has base **10** (digits 0–9), and each place is worth ten times the place to its right. A number's value is the sum of its digits times their place weights — the rule every conversion in this course spends.
The **binary system** is positional with base **2**: only two digits, **0 and 1**, and places worth 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 — doubling each step. So 101 in binary is 4 + 0 + 1 = **5** in decimal. The computer uses binary because its parts have two stable states — current or none, charged or not — and one digit matches one such state: a **bit**. Grade 11 returns to the system to build conversions and binary arithmetic on this Grade 7 foundation.
