# What ICT is

## Do it first

Look around your home or classroom in your mind. Name every thing you can see that sends, shows or stores a message or a picture: a phone, a television, a radio, a computer. Keep your list — this lesson gives all of those things one shared name.

## Sub lesson 1 — The road map of this course, and its brainstorm-first rhythm

This whole course walks one road, in six steps. First, what ICT is. Then the machine itself: hardware, the part you can touch, and software, the instructions that make it work. Then the internet. Then keeping computers safe and secure. Last, a first program, drawn with LOGO. 

[Picture 10100 — the six-unit road map]



Every unit on this road starts the same way: with a brainstorm. Before any new word is defined, you first say what you already know and have already seen. That is not wasted time — the new idea hangs on what you already know, the way a new picture hangs on a nail that is already in the wall. So in this lesson, and in every lesson after it, we start by thinking first.

## Sub lesson 2 — What ICT is

ICT stands for Information and Communication Technology. It is not one machine. It is all the communication technologies together: the internet, mobile phones, television, computers, tablets and cameras. 

[Picture 10101 — the named devices around the letters ICT]



ICT joins two kinds of networks together: telephone and audio-visual networks — the networks of voices, pictures and sound — with computer networks. Joined together, they let people do five things with information in digital form: access it, retrieve it, store it, transmit it and change it. When you send a message on a phone, watch a lesson on television, or save a photo on a tablet, you are using ICT.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: ICT is all the communication technologies together — internet, mobile phones, television, computers, tablets and cameras — joining telephone and audio-visual networks with computer networks so people can access, retrieve, store, transmit and change information in digital form. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
