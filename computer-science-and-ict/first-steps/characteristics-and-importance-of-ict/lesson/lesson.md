# Characteristics and Importance of ICT

## Do it first

A phone call crosses the country in a second; a lesson recorded once is watched in a hundred villages. Laos's Grade 6 book, after defining ICT, stops to name what ICT is like and why it matters. This lesson is that naming.

## Sub lesson 1 — The characteristics of ICT

**ICT** is characterised first by **speed**: information travels and is processed almost at once. It reaches **far and wide** — one message can cross the country or the world. It stores **huge amounts** in small spaces, works **accurately** the same way every time, and joins **many forms together** — text, picture, sound and moving image carried on the same road. Laos names these characteristics before any machine is touched: the technology is judged by what it does to information.

## Sub lesson 2 — Why ICT is important

The **importance** follows from the characteristics. Because ICT is fast and far-reaching, it shrinks distance in **communication**; because it stores and retrieves, it serves **education, work, trade and government**; because it handles many forms, it carries entertainment and news as easily as figures. The book's examples are Lao daily life — the TV remote and the household appliance are its first pictures of technology at work — and its rule is judgement: a characteristic is only good when the use made of it is good.
