# Digital computers — data as digits

## Do it first

Count your fingers one by one: 1, 2, 3 — separate values, nothing in between. That stepping is the digital way. Before reading on, name three machines around you that work with numbers like that.

## Data as digits

A digital computer works on discrete values — separate values that can be counted. It takes raw data in as digits, and processes it with programs stored in its memory. Almost every computer you will ever touch — phone, laptop, school computer — is digital.

[Picture 10919 — analog against digital (teacher guide table)]

## What it does with the digits

A digital computer produces output from the processed digits, performing calculations and logical operations at high speed. The teacher guide sets analog and digital side by side (teacher guide — evidence): analog works with continuous values and is slower, with very low, limited memory and comparatively low performance; digital works with discrete values and is faster, with very large memory and very high performance.
