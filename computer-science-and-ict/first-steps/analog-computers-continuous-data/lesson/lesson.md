# Analog computers — continuous data

## Do it first

Watch a car's speedometer as it speeds up: the needle glides smoothly, never jumping in steps. Now watch a digital clock: the numbers hop from one value to the next. Before reading on, say which movement is 'continuous' and which is in separate steps.

## Continuous data

By method of operation there are three types of computer. An analog computer processes continuous data — data that flows smoothly without steps: speed, temperature, pressure, current. It takes the data straight from a measuring device, without first converting it into numbers and codes.

## Answers on a dial

An analog computer gives its output as a reading on a dial or scale — like the sweeping needle in the GIF. The everyday examples are the speedometer and the mercury thermometer. The type was used in the 1950s and 1960s, and it has limited memory.
