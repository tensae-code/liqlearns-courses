No book error touches this lesson's content.

Saudi Arabia's own way (Grade 3, continuing education): a workbook-style book for the continuing-education (adult/lifelong learning) track — every one of the 16 lessons opens with "I want to learn" objectives and closes with written exercises, and everything is taught on an older Windows desktop (Start menu, "My Computer", Recycle Bin) with Notepad as the only application, worked through step-by-step practicals in the school lab.

Iraq's own way (Grade 7): a four-unit spiral repeated at every grade — computer fundamentals, computer software (here: the operating system plus an unusually deep treatment of Windows right-click shortcut menus), algorithms and programming principles (ending in Scratch), and information technology and the internet — every lesson built on the Iraqi five-step learning cycle with opening activity, main idea, learning outcomes, vocabulary and lesson review.

Sudan's own way (Grade 5): a short, highly practical Arabic primary series that revises the previous grade first, then works entirely inside Windows and its built-in apps (Calculator, Paint) before reaching Word — every lesson ends with hands-on activities and each unit with an assessment.

Sudan's own way (Grade 11): a university-style secondary text (single named author, Dr Awad Haj Ali of the University of Gezira) that teaches systems work before code — file types and file security in a floppy-disk/UPS world, a complete spare-parts-factory system as the running example, the algorithm named for al-Khwarizmi, desk-checking with real sales data, and then a full BASIC course (numbered lines, READ/DATA, GOSUB, DEF FN) with payroll and mean/variance programs — data processing taught as a school subject in its own right.

Cuba's own way (Grade 6): Grade 6 adds the three productivity firsts in one year — digital presentations, Scratch animation with event/control blocks, and spreadsheets with formulas and charts — closing with a TIC chapter on searching and processing digital information; computer generations are taught inside Chapter 1.

Fiji's own way (Year 11 Computer Education student book): The first locally written text of its kind (the book's Preface): written by a panel of Computer Studies officers and University of the South Pacific lecturers for the new Year 11 Computer Education syllabus trialled in 2014; each chapter uses "Discovery Learning" prompts, and the database chapter is a full Access 2007 practical building a Student database in a "Lab Chapter 3" folder.

Mongolia's own way (Grade 6): ICT is built on Mongolian examples throughout — information and objects taught through the Mazaalai (Gobi bear), the ger (with a paper origami model), Mongolian pattern (khee) motifs and the national flag; touch typing is taught on the Mongolian Cyrillic keyboard layout with local tutor programs (MonTT, Fast type); web work uses Mongolian sites (surguuli.edu.mn, kids.gogo.mn, tsag-agaar.mn).

Rwanda's own way (S1 ICT Student Book, KLB): competency-based units each opening with a Key Unit Competency and keywords; a full GIS/ArcGIS unit and a Scratch music-making unit sit inside the first secondary year, and ICT use is grounded in Rwandan daily life.

Kuwait's own way (Grade 4 Digital Explorer Term 2, printed title — records name it 'Our Digital World'): the Digital World unit mixes identity (pride in Arabic language and identity online), cloud storage on the national platform (OneDrive inside Teams), and a three-lesson AI arc (data → training a machine → AI decisions) before the tool unit; PowerPoint is taught as a five-step 'Exciting Presentation' journey ending in presentations about Kuwait itself.

Greece's own way (Grade 11 EPAL, Basika Themata Pliroforikis — student notes): the EPAL (vocational) informatics-sector notes go far deeper into digital technology than the general track — full data representation (number systems and base conversion, character encoding, compression, integer representation and overflow), image/sound/video representation and editing, animation, VR/AR, 3D printing and scanning, then the electronics ladder (Boolean algebra, logic gates, combinational and sequential circuits, adders, flip-flops, counters, A/D and D/A converters, integrated circuits and the CPU), ending with digital-technology applications sector by sector and ICT professions.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 2 G2 student): A story-led primary book built around two Grade 2 characters (Hayk and Arpi); every lesson uses fixed activity "rooms" (key knowledge, key words, skills/tasks) and QR codes link to audio/video material; safety and health come before any machine skills.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 2 G2 teacher): Companion to Book 1 in the Zangak guide template: Grade 2 standard outcomes, an exemplary programme, a 32-hour thematic plan covering the student book's twelve themes with practical works and two summary tests, sample lesson plans (the first teaches the "Important commands" lesson — select, cut, copy, paste, undo, redo), and worked explanations of the book's electronic tasks, opening with correct sitting posture at the computer.

Vietnam's own way (Grade 3 Tin học 3): Informatics starts in Grade 3 not with the machine but with information in daily life — an alarm clock ringing is "information" that leads to a decision — and only then meets the computer; problem-solving (Chủ đề 6) is taught unplugged as ordering the steps of everyday jobs, with and without conditions, before any code exists.

Vietnam's own way (Grade 10 Tin học 10): the Grade 10 book is one shared book for both orientations (ICT-coloured and CS-coloured lesson title bands tell each track its own lessons, per its preface); its data strand is unusually deep for school level — binary integers, logic data, sound and image data — and its programming strand is a complete first Python course from variables to functions, scope, error recognition and testing.


Pakistan's own way (Grade 6 Computer Science 6): every unit opens with Students Learning Outcomes, is dotted with "Do you Know?", "Extra Bit!" and "Tips" boxes, and closes with Summary, MCQ/short/detailed exercises, project-based and activity-based questions, with an answers key at the back. Digital skills (Windows desktop, file management, Paint 3D, a typing tutor) are taught hands-on BEFORE programming; digital citizenship is framed through Pakistani citizenship first; the entrepreneurship unit teaches from Pakistani founders (Foodpanda/EatOye, Shopsy.pk, Zero Makeup, Investors Lounge).

Kazakhstan's own way (Grade 1 teacher guide «Цифровая грамотность. Методическое руководство» 2023 (Russian + Kazakh editions)): the 2023 guide restructures Grade 1 against the 2021 student book (Book 5): robotics disappears, replaced by a drawing section («Мой первый рисунок», taught in Tux Paint in the Kazakh plans) and a separate algorithms section (concept of algorithm, executors and their command systems, kinds of algorithms) before Scratch programming.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kopeeva G5 «Информатика»): the year runs safety → internet safety → information and its processing → a long Scratch chapter, and it closes not with a test but with two production projects: a printed newspaper page made in a word processor (page setup, columns, WordArt, page numbers) and a defended Scratch project published to the Scratch community site.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kopeeva G3 «Ақпараттық-коммуникациялық технологиялар»): Grade 3 ICT is a tools year organised by cross-cutting themes («Менің мектебім», «Сәулет», «Өнер»…): text, then sound, then computer pictures in a graphics editor, then a mixed document that combines them, then internet safety/search and presentations — with a practicum closing almost every section.

Kazakhstan's own way (Vyushkova/Zhurbenko/Orazbekov G7 «Информатика» (NIS student book, Russian edition)): the NIS Grade 7 book is organised around data: measuring and representing information (including the von Neumann architecture), business documents, spreadsheets that grow into databases (error checking, sorting/filtering, protecting data from change), raster graphics and logo design, a whole modelling section (modelling games, school life and social problems, forecasting from data), networks and information systems, and linear-structure programming that ends with building your own calculator.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G5 «Информатика»): Grade 5 in the Almatykitap line is a robotics year: after information representation (encryption, binary, binary-coded graphics) and raster/vector graphics, two full sections run LEGO robots — gyro sensor, turns, line following and robo-sumo competitions — before a closing safety section that ends with shared folders and mini-projects. Every term is glossed in three languages (Kazakh/Russian/English) in the margin.
