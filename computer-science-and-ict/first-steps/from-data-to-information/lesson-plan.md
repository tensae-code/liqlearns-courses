# Lesson plan — From data to information

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** From data to information
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — Introduction to Information & Communication Technology — in the title First Steps on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Information is processed data — data collected, put into context and given meaning; interpreting data is reviewing it through a process to reach a conclusion.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on Data — raw facts with no meaning yet: the same list from that lesson is given its title and its meaning here.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Processed data** — Information as data collected, put into context and given meaning; interpreting data to reach a conclusion.
2. **Titling data yourself** — The worked example — the same list become "names of Grade 7 students" and "their ages, ranging 14 to 20" — and titling given data sets yourself, where many fair titles are acceptable.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the lessons before this one in First Steps on the ladder. Recommended, never forced — a learner may land on this lesson directly, and it stands alone.
- The book's own rhythm is kept: this lesson opens with a brainstorm — the learner first says what they already know — before any definition is given.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what information is: processed data, put into context and given meaning.
2. Turn the book's name-and-number list into information by titling it.
3. Title a given data set yourself, and accept that another fair title can also be right.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil.

## If the learner is stuck
Thinks the data itself changed → nothing in the list changed; the context around it changed. Insists there is exactly one right title → the guide's marking guidance says many titles are acceptable where the data truly fits them.
