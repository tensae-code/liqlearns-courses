# Checking Data — Verification and Validation

## Do it first

A mark typed as 850 instead of 85 is wrong data, and every decision built on it is wrong too. Laos's Grade 12 data-handling chapter gives checking the correctness of data its own section, with two different checks. This lesson tells them apart.

## Sub lesson 1 — Verification — was it copied right?

**Verification** checks that data was **transferred correctly** — that what entered the system is exactly what was on the source. Its classic methods are **double entry** (the same data typed twice, by two people or at two passes, and the two compared) and **proofreading / visual checking** (reading the entered data against the original document). Verification catches the typing slip: the digit dropped, the name misspelled in copying — errors made between the paper and the machine.

## Sub lesson 2 — Validation — can it be right at all?

**Validation** checks data against **rules** — whether it is the kind of thing that could be correct. A **range check** asks whether a number sits inside its allowed span (a mark between 0 and 100); a **format check** asks whether it has the right shape (a date, a code); a **presence check** refuses an empty field that must be filled; a **type check** asks whether letters stand where numbers must be. Validation cannot know the true value — a mark of 58 passes every rule and may still be the wrong mark — which is why the book teaches the two checks together: one guards the copying, the other guards the sense.
