# IT and ICT — telling them apart

## Do it first

Say the two short names out loud: IT. ICT. One letter is different. In your notebook, write one sentence about computers working with information, and one sentence about people sending messages to each other. This lesson teaches you which name belongs to which sentence.

## IT, and how IT differs from ICT

IT — Information Technology — is the industry and the process of creating, maintaining and using computer software, hardware and networks to manage information. Wherever people build computers, keep them working, write their programs and look after information with them, that is IT.

ICT — Information and Communication Technology — is the communication side: the technologies that carry messages, voices, pictures and information from person to person and place to place.

The book states the difference plainly: IT is the broader computing-data concept; ICT is the communication-related technologies. 

[Picture 10102 — two overlapping circles, computing on one side, communication on the other]

 The two circles overlap, because a phone sending a message is both a computer working with data and a communication technology at once. Your job in this lesson is to be able to say the difference in your own words — not the book's words copied back, but your own.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: IT is the broader computing-data concept — the industry and process of creating, maintaining and using computer software, hardware and networks to manage information — while ICT is the communication-related technologies. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: say what IT is: creating, maintaining and using software, hardware and networks to manage information. Do this with a real example from your own home, school or street, and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: say the book's difference in your own words: IT is the broader computing-data concept; ICT is the communication-related technologies. Do this with a real example from your own home, school or street, and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: sort examples: a technician repairing a school computer (IT work); a message travelling between two phones (ICT at work). Do this with a real example from your own home, school or street, and write or say how you know it is right.
