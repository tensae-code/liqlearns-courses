# The Information Model of an Object — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What is an object's information model?**
**Answer: The organised information that stands for the object**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What form does the book choose for it?**
**Answer: A table of properties**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What stands at the table's head, and what fills its rows?**
**Answer: The object's name; its properties**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) Why is the table a model?**
**Answer: It keeps what the purpose needs and drops the rest**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) What can a table do that the living animal cannot?**
**Answer: Be compared, sorted and studied at a desk**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What are the five-snout livestock?**
**Answer: Horse, camel, cow, sheep and goat**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What does modelling all five in one form allow?**
**Answer: The tables can be laid side by side and read against each other**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) Name questions the five tables answer together.**
**Answer: Which gives milk, which carries, which survives the cold (any two)**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) Name the book's four opening examples.**
**Answer: Cat, bird, human and snakes**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) What is the table's power, in the closing phrase?**
**Answer: One form makes unlike things comparable**
