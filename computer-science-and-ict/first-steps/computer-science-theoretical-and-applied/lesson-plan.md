## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Theoretical computer science — what can be computed, and at what cost** — **Theoretical computer science** asks the questions **beneath** any machine: what is a **problem**, what is an **algorithm**, which problems **can** be solved by algorithm at all, and what a solution **costs** in steps and memory as its data grows. Its tools are definitions and proofs, not devices — the book's whole second unit (algorithms, complexity and computability, data structures, paradigms) is this science at school level.
2. **Applied computer science — systems that must work in the world** — **Applied computer science** builds the **working systems**: operating systems, databases and information systems, networks, the applications people use — Unit 3 of the book, chapter by chapter. Its questions are the engineer's: does it **work**, is it **usable**, does it stay **correct and safe** under real hands and real data? The book's balance is the lesson: neither half is the other's servant — theory tells practice what is possible and what it costs; practice gives theory its problems.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
