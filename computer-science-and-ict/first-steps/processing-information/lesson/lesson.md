# Processing Information

## Do it first

Grade 7's information unit walks the whole road of information: receiving, then this lesson — processing. Mongolia teaches processing as real work with a real risk: the work can go wrong, so processing includes its own checking.

## Sub lesson 1 — Processing — old information in, new information out

To **process** information is to work on what was received and produce **new information** from it: sounds heard become words understood, numbers gathered become a total, observations become a conclusion. Processing is not copying — the output says something the input did not say alone. A pupil listening and then answering, a herder reading the sky and then deciding, a computer calculating — all are processing, and Mongolia's definition fits them all because it names the act, not the tool.

In your mind's eye: processing — old information in, new information out.

## Sub lesson 2 — Checking and correcting — processing's second half

Processed information can carry **errors**: a misheard word, a wrong total, a hasty conclusion. So processing includes **checking** — comparing the result against the source, a second method, or plain sense — and **correcting** what the check condemns. The book pairs them in one lesson on purpose: processing without checking manufactures confident mistakes, and the information road's next station, storing, will faithfully keep whatever processing hands it — errors included, unless this lesson's half of the work is done.

In your mind's eye: checking and correcting — processing's second half.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: Working on received information to produce new information from it. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: The output says something the input did not say alone. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: Numbers gathered becoming a total (any one of: sounds heard becoming words understood). Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: A misheard word, a wrong total, a hasty conclusion (any one). Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
