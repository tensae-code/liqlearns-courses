## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The five forms — text, number, picture, sound, video** — Information is **represented** — given a shape the senses can take in — in five main forms. **Text**: information in written signs, as in this book. **Number**: information in digits, as in a price or a mark. **Picture**: information in images, as in a photograph or a drawing. **Sound**: information in what is heard, as in speech or music. **Video**: moving pictures, usually with sound. Naming the form tells you at once how the information must be taken in — read, counted, seen, heard, or watched.
2. **Mixed forms — several forms in one whole** — Real information rarely travels in one form alone. A web page joins **text, pictures and sound**; a film joins **video, sound and text** in its titles. Mongolia calls these **mixed forms** — several forms combined in one whole, each doing the job it does best: the picture shows, the text explains, the number measures. The computer's power, met later in the course, is exactly this: it can hold and join every form in one machine.
