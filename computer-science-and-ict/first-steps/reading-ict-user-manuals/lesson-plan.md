# Lesson plan — Reading ICT User Manuals

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Reading ICT User Manuals
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Uganda (2026-11-14) — in the title First Steps on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Uganda's *Information Communication Technology Textbook — Senior One* (NCDC Prototype, Lower Secondary Curriculum), because no existing lesson teaches this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** every ICT tool comes with a user manual — the instruction book for that tool — and a learner who can read a manual can find what a tool is for, set it up, use it step by step, keep safe around it and fix simple problems, without waiting for someone else to show them.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the learner's own ICT tools at home and at school and on knowing what ICT tools are; what a manual is, where its answers live and how to follow it are built inside the lesson from Activity 1.9 itself.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: What ICT Is in First Steps. Recommended, never forced — a learner may land on this lesson directly, and it stands alone.
- Uganda's own way is kept: in Uganda's Senior One prototype every topic is an "Activity" learners do, under the revised Lower Secondary Curriculum — this lesson is taught as a doing activity, not as notes to copy.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a user manual is and what it is for.
2. Find the part of a manual you need, using its contents list, headings or index.
3. Follow a manual's steps in order to do a job or answer a question about a tool.
4. Say why a manual's safety warnings must be read before you touch, and why the manual is kept with its tool.

## What you need
An ICT tool's user manual — the printed book that came with a tool, or the help/manual on a device itself — paper and a pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)

## If the learner is stuck
Thinks a manual is only for experts or repair people → the manual is written for the user; that is its name. Reads from page one and gives up → a manual is a place to find answers: question first, contents next, only that part. Skips a step and the job fails → steps are in doing order; go back to the step that was skipped.
