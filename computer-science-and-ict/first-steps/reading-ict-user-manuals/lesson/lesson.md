# Reading ICT User Manuals

## Do it first

A new ICT tool is in front of you — a phone, a printer, a computer part. It does something you do not yet know how to do, and no teacher is free to show you. Somewhere with that tool there is a book, or a help file on the tool itself, that was written to answer exactly your question. This lesson teaches you to use it.

## The manual — the tool's own instruction book

A **user manual** is the instruction book that comes with an ICT tool. The people who made the tool wrote it to teach the person who will use it: what the tool is, what each part is called, how to set it up, how to do each job with it, how to keep safe while using it, and what to do when something goes wrong. A manual is not a story to read from the first page to the last. It is a **place to find answers**: you go to the part that matches your question, follow it, and close the book again. Uganda's book makes this a doing activity — Activity 1.9 — because the skill is in the using: find the tool's manual, find the right part of it, and do what it says.

## Finding your answer and following it

Work a manual the same way every time. **First, name your question** in plain words: 'How do I connect this?' or 'Why did it stop?' **Second, find the place**: use the contents list at the front to find the chapter or heading for that job; many manuals also have an index at the back, and a manual on a screen can be searched by word. **Third, follow the steps in order** — manual steps are written in the order they must be done, and skipping a step is the commonest reason a job fails. **Fourth, obey the warnings**: the parts marked as warnings or cautions are there because someone could be hurt or the tool damaged; read them before you touch, not after. **Last, keep the manual with the tool** — a manual stored far away helps nobody on the day it is needed. A learner who can do this owns every new tool that arrives, because the tool arrives with its own teacher.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: a user manual is the tool's own instruction book — name your question, find its place by the contents or index, follow the steps in order, obey the warnings, and keep the manual with the tool. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: say what a user manual is and what it is for. Do this with a real example from your own home, school or street, and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: find the part of a manual you need, using its contents list, headings or index. Do this with a real example from your own home, school or street, and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: follow a manual's steps in order to do a job or answer a question about a tool. Do this with a real example from your own home, school or street, and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: say why a manual's safety warnings must be read before you touch, and why the manual is kept with its tool. Do this with a real example from your own home, school or street, and write or say how you know it is right.
