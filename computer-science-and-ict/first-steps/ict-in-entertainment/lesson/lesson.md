# ICT in entertainment

## Do it first

Think of your last free hour at home. Did a screen or a speaker fill any of it — a film, a song, a game? This short lesson names that part of daily life: ICT at play.

## ICT in entertainment

Not all ICT is work or school. Entertainment and leisure come through ICT at home. 

[Picture 10114 — a TV film, a music note and an online game on one screen wall]



Films and music arrive through television. Games are played over the internet, against or alongside other players, in free time. The same networks and devices that carry lessons and messages in the day carry the film in the evening — the technology is the same; the purpose is rest and play.

One honest note, which the book itself makes two lessons from now: enjoyment has a limit. Too much gaming brings eyestrain and sore wrists — the harm is named in the lesson on what computers are good at, and it belongs to this picture too.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Entertainment and leisure come through ICT at home — films and music from television, and games played over the internet in free time. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: name the book's entertainment examples: films and music from television; games played over the internet. Do this with a real example from your own home, school or street, and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: say where this entertainment happens: at home, in free time. Do this with a real example from your own home, school or street, and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: say that the same ICT carries work, school and play — the purpose changes, not the technology. Do this with a real example from your own home, school or street, and write or say how you know it is right.
