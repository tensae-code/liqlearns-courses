# Getting Information Through the Senses

## Do it first

Close your eyes and listen. Open them and look. Sniff the air. Touch your desk. In ten seconds your body took in sounds, colours, smells and feelings — without you even trying. This lesson names the five ways that happens: your senses.

## Getting Information Through the Senses

Close your eyes for a moment. You can still hear. Open them. Now you can see, too. Every moment, your body is taking in information — and it does it through your **senses**.

Your **eyes** take in colours, shapes, sizes and light. With your eyes you know that an apple is red and round, and that a friend is smiling.

Your **ears** take in sounds. With your ears you hear a voice, a bell, a bird, and music.

Your **nose** takes in smells. With your nose you know bread is baking, or that a flower is near.

Your **tongue** takes in tastes. With your tongue you know sweet, sour and salty.

Your **skin** takes in touch. With your skin you feel hot and cold, soft and hard, smooth and rough.

[Picture — a child with five small pictures around them: an eye and a red apple, an ear and a bell, a nose and a flower, a tongue and a sweet, a hand touching a soft ball]

Five senses, five ways of receiving information. Each sense organ is a receiver — and together they are how information from the whole world first reaches you.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: we receive information through five senses — eyes for colour and shape, ears for sound, nose for smells, tongue for tastes, and skin for touch. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: point to each of your five sense organs in turn and say what information it takes in.
2. Show it, don't just say it: close your eyes, let a friend hold up a safe object, and name it using only touch and smell — then say which senses were your receivers.
3. Show it, don't just say it: find one piece of information in the room for each sense, and make a list of five.
