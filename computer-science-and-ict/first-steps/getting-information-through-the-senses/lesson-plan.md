# Lesson plan — Getting Information Through the Senses

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Getting Information Through the Senses
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Turkmenistan (2026-11-14) — in the title First Steps on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Turkmenistan's *Basics of Informatics, Grade 1*, because no existing lesson teaches this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** We receive information through our sense organs: the eyes take in colours and shapes, the ears take in sounds, the nose takes in smells, the tongue takes in tastes, and the skin takes in touch — hot and cold, soft and hard.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the Grade 1 idea of information and its sources and receivers, and on the child's own body; every sense is built inside the lesson from things the child can do at once.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: From Data to Information and Sources of Information and Who Receives It in First Steps. Recommended, never forced — a learner may land on this lesson directly, and it stands alone.
- Turkmenistan's own way is kept: in Turkmenistan's Grade 1 book this comes in Chapter III, Information around us — the ways of receiving information are the child's own sense organs, years before any input device is named.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the five senses and the sense organ for each one.
2. Say what kind of information each sense takes in: colour and shape, sound, smell, taste, touch.
3. Match a piece of information to the sense that received it.

## What you need
Your own body, and safe things in the room to look at, listen to, smell, taste and touch as the teacher allows.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Close your eyes and listen. Open them and look. Sniff the air. Touch your desk. In ten seconds your body took in sounds, colours, smells and feelings — without you even trying. This lesson names the five ways that happens: your senses. (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
Mixes up the organ and the information → match them with the body, not with words: point to the eyes and name colour and shape; point to the ears and name sound; do the same for nose, tongue and skin. Thinks only eyes and ears receive → close the eyes and feel the desk: the skin has just received information too.
