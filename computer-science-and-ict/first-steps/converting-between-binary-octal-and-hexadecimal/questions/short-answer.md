# Converting Between Binary, Octal and Hexadecimal — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) Why do binary and octal convert by grouping?**
**Answer: Because 8 = 2³ — every octal digit is exactly three binary digits**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) How is binary grouped for octal?**
**Answer: In threes from the right, the last group padded with leading zeros**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) Convert 101110₂ to octal.**
**Answer: 56₈ (101 | 110)**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What arithmetic does the conversion need?**
**Answer: None — only grouping**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) Why do programmers read bits in octal or hex?**
**Answer: Long bit strings are shorter and safer to read grouped**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) Why groups of four for hexadecimal?**
**Answer: Because 16 = 2⁴ — every hex digit is exactly four binary digits**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) Convert 101110₂ to hexadecimal.**
**Answer: 2E₁₆ (0010 | 1110)**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What letters does hexadecimal use, and for what?**
**Answer: A–F, for the values ten to fifteen**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) How is octal converted to hexadecimal?**
**Answer: Through binary — expand by threes, regroup by fours**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) What are the bases, in the lesson's closing phrase?**
**Answer: Views — some group the bits for the hand and eye**
