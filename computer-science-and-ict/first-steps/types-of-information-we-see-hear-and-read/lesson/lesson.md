# Types of Information — We See, Hear and Read

## Do it first

Look at a picture: that information came through your eyes. Listen to the bell: that came through your ears. Read one word on the board: that came as writing. This lesson sorts information into its types.

## Types of Information — We See, Hear and Read

**Information** is what we learn about the world around us. It does not all come the same way — it comes in **types**.

Some information we **see**: pictures, colours, shapes, signs and faces. Some information we **hear**: sounds, music, speech and the bell. Some information we **read**: words, letters and numbers written down. A story book gives information we read; a song gives information we hear; a drawing gives information we see.

The same thing can give more than one type. A teacher speaking gives information we hear — and if the teacher writes on the board, the same lesson also gives information we read. Naming the type means naming how the information reaches us.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Information comes in types: some we see (pictures and shapes), some we hear (sounds and speech), and some we read (words and numbers). Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: say what information is, in your own words.
2. Show it, don't just say it: sort examples into information we see, information we hear and information we read.
3. Show it, don't just say it: name the type of a new example and say how it reaches us.
