# Converting Numbers Between Systems

## Do it first

The same quantity can be written in decimal, in binary, or in another system — and a computer worker must be able to carry a number from one writing to the other without changing its value.

## Sub lesson 1 — From another system into decimal

To carry a number **into decimal**, write it out by places: each digit times the value of its place — the base raised to the digit's position — and add the parts. The binary number 101 is 1×4 + 0×2 + 1×1 = 5 in decimal. Uzbekistan's Grade 8 book teaches conversion as its own lesson, after the systems themselves, because every later coding lesson spends it.

## Sub lesson 2 — From decimal into another system

To carry a decimal number **into another system**, divide it by the new base again and again, write down each remainder, and read the remainders in reverse — last first — to get the new writing. Thirteen divided by 2 leaves remainders 1, 0, 1, 1 read backwards: 1101 in binary. Check by converting back: a conversion you cannot undo is a conversion you have not proved.

Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: Its value. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: Each digit times its place value, then the parts are added. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: Divide by the new base again and again and keep the remainders. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: In reverse — last first. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
