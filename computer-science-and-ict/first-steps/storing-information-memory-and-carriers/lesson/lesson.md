# Storing Information — Memory and Carriers

## Do it first

You remember your friend's name without being told again — your memory stored it. Yesterday's lesson is in your book — the book stored it too. This lesson teaches storing: memory inside us, and carriers outside us.

## Storing Information — Memory and Carriers

To **store** information is to keep it so you can use it again later. Information that is not stored is soon forgotten.

People store information in their **memory**. Your memory keeps your name, your way home, and the words of a song. Memory is a wonderful store — but it can forget, and it cannot be handed to a friend.

So people also store information on **carriers** — things outside the body that carry information and keep it: **paper** and **books** carry written words and pictures, a **photograph** carries the look of a moment, a drawing carries what a place looked like. A carrier can be kept, shown to others, and read long after. Later in this course, the computer will appear as another carrier of information — but paper, books and memory come first.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Information can be stored so it is not forgotten: people store it in their memory, and outside the body it is kept on carriers — paper, books and photographs. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: say what storing information means.
2. Show it, don't just say it: name memory as the store inside a person, and say one thing it keeps.
3. Show it, don't just say it: name carriers — paper, books, photographs — and say what each carries.
