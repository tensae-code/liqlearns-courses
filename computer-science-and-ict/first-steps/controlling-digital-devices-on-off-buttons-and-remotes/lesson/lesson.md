# Controlling Digital Devices — On-Off Buttons and Remotes

## Do it first

Every digital device in the house answers to a hand. Moldova's Grade 1 lesson on controlling devices teaches the two controllers a six-year-old actually uses: the button on the device itself, and the remote that speaks to it across the room.

## Sub lesson 1 — The on/off button — starting and stopping

The **on/off button** is the first control of almost every digital device: press it once and the device wakes and starts its work; press it again and the device stops and sleeps. The button carries a sign known everywhere — a circle with a line through its top. The book's rule is a control rule, not only a safety one: a device that is off takes no commands, so **switching on comes before every other command**, and switching off is how a piece of work is properly ended.

In your mind's eye: the on/off button — starting and stopping.

## Sub lesson 2 — The remote control — commanding from a distance

A **remote control** commands a device **from a distance**: its buttons send the command through the air, and the device — television, air conditioner, toy car — receives it and obeys. Each button has its job: numbers choose the channel, arrows move, the volume buttons raise and lower the sound. The remote must **point at the device** and nothing may block the way, because the command travels in a straight line. Controlling from a distance is still controlling: the same commands, sent from further away.

In your mind's eye: the remote control — commanding from a distance.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: It starts the device with one press and stops it with another. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: A circle with a line through its top. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: No — switching on comes before every other command. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: By switching the device off. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
