# Objects and Their Names

## Do it first

Look around the room. Point to one thing at a time and say its name out loud: book. Chair. Bag. Window. You have just named objects — this lesson says what an object is.

## Objects and Their Names

Look around you. Everything you can point to has a name: a **book**, a **chair**, a **pencil**, a **tree**, a **dog**. Each one of these things is an **object**.

An object is a thing we can see, point to and name. The name tells us which thing we mean. When you say 'pencil', everyone knows you mean the pencil — not the book and not the chair.

Objects are everywhere: at home, at school and outside. A cup at home is an object. A desk at school is an object. A stone on the road is an object. Living things are objects too — a cat, a flower and a bird all have names.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Everything around us that we can point to and name is an object — a book, a chair, a tree, a dog — and every object has its own name. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: say what an object is, in your own words.
2. Show it, don't just say it: name at least five objects in the room, pointing to each one.
3. Show it, don't just say it: tell an object from a thing that is not one single named thing, and say why.
