# Early Counting Devices

## Do it first

Before there was a computer there was a hand. Laos's Grade 6 history chapter begins not with a machine but with the counting tools of the ancient era — the tools every later machine grew from. This lesson counts with them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Counting on the body and with things

The first counting device is the **fingers**: ten of them, always carried, and the reason counting so often goes in tens. Beyond the fingers, people counted with **stones and pebbles** — one stone laid down for each animal or sack — and with **sticks**, each stick standing for a thing counted. These tools share one idea, the idea all computing still stands on: let one thing stand for another, and keep the stand-ins in order.

## Sub lesson 2 — Marks and knots — counting that lasts

Fingers and loose stones forget: a count that must **last** is recorded. A **notch cut in a stick** keeps the count after the flock has gone; **knots tied in a rope** do the same work — one knot per item, the rope kept as the record. Laos places these devices at the head of its computer history for a reason: the movement from counting in the moment to storing a count is the first step on the road that ends at the computer's memory.
