## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Counting on the body and with things** — The first counting device is the **fingers**: ten of them, always carried, and the reason counting so often goes in tens. Beyond the fingers, people counted with **stones and pebbles** — one stone laid down for each animal or sack — and with **sticks**, each stick standing for a thing counted. These tools share one idea, the idea all computing still stands on: let one thing stand for another, and keep the stand-ins in order.
2. **Marks and knots — counting that lasts** — Fingers and loose stones forget: a count that must **last** is recorded. A **notch cut in a stick** keeps the count after the flock has gone; **knots tied in a rope** do the same work — one knot per item, the rope kept as the record. Laos places these devices at the head of its computer history for a reason: the movement from counting in the moment to storing a count is the first step on the road that ends at the computer's memory.
