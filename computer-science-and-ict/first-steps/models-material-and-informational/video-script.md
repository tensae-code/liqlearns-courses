# Video script — Models — Material and Informational

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

A **model** is a stand-in made to study an object instead of the object itself: it keeps the features that matter for the maker's purpose and leaves the rest out. The book's own Mongolian examples carry the idea — a paper **origami ger**, a drawing of the **Mazaalai**, a pattern of the national flag. A model is always simpler than its object, and that simplicity is its use: what can be held, turned and tested teaches what the real thing, too big or too far, cannot.
Models come in two kinds. A **material model** is a physical stand-in: the origami ger, a globe, a toy car — it copies the object's shape and size in another material. An **informational model** is made of information: a description in words, a table of properties, a drawing, a formula — it copies what is known about the object, not its body. The computer works only with informational models: every file in the later lessons is one — information standing in for a thing in the world.
