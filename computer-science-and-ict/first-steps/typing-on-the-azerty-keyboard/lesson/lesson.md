# Typing on the AZERTY Keyboard

## Do it first

Look at the top row of letters on your keyboard and read the first six letters aloud. On many keyboards they say Q W E R T Y. On the keyboard of French-language schooling they say A Z E R T Y. Find out which one is in front of you — this lesson is for the AZERTY one.

## Sub lesson 1 — The AZERTY layout — where the letters sit

A keyboard is named by the first letters of its top letter row. AZERTY begins A, Z, E, R, T, Y — so the name itself tells you where to look first.

[Picture — the AZERTY keyboard with its top letter row highlighted]

Compared with QWERTY, some letters have traded places. A sits where Q sits on a QWERTY keyboard, and Z sits where W sits. M has moved too: on AZERTY it sits at the end of the home row, beside the punctuation. If your fingers learned one layout and your eyes meet the other, the letters come out wrong — not because you cannot type, but because the map changed. Learn this map.

The top row above the letters is shared by figures and signs. On AZERTY, pressing a number-row key on its own gives an accented letter or a symbol — the letters French writing needs, like é and è. To type the figure itself, hold Shift while you press the key. That is the AZERTY way, and it is worth knowing before it surprises you.

## Sub lesson 2 — Typing with the right fingers

Good typing is not fast searching. It is each finger knowing its own keys so well that your eyes never need to help.

Rest your fingers lightly on the home row — the middle row, where A sits at the left end on AZERTY. Each finger owns the keys above, below and beside its home key, and reaches for them and returns. Your thumbs rest on the space bar.

[Picture — hands on the home row, each finger matched to the keys it owns]

Now type slowly. Copy a short line, letter by letter. Keep your eyes on the line you are copying, not on your hands. When a letter comes out wrong, do not speed up — slow down, find the right key with the right finger, and type it again. Accuracy first: a slow line with no errors teaches your fingers more than a fast line full of them. A little practice every day builds the map into your hands, and speed arrives on its own.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: the keyboard is called AZERTY because its top letter row begins A Z E R T Y; A and Z trade places with Q and W compared with QWERTY; figures on the number row need Shift; and good typing rests the fingers on the home row, each finger owning its keys, eyes on the copy. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: point to A, Z, E, R, T and Y on a real or drawn AZERTY keyboard, in order, without hesitating. Write or say how you know each one is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: sit in the home position and type a short line of copy slowly, eyes on the copy, and count your errors. Type it again and beat your own count.
3. Show it, don't just say it: type the figures 1 to 0 on the number row using Shift, then type the same row without Shift and say what changed.
