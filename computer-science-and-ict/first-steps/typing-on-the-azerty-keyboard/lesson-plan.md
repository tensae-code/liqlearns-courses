# Lesson plan — Typing on the AZERTY Keyboard

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Typing on the AZERTY Keyboard
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Djibouti (2026-11-14) — in the title First Steps on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Djibouti's *Guide manuel — Informatique, 6ème année*, because no existing lesson teaches typing on the AZERTY keyboard as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The AZERTY keyboard puts its letters in a different order from QWERTY — its top letter row begins A Z E R T Y — and a learner types well on it by learning where its letters, accents and number row sit, resting the fingers in the home position, and practising letter by letter without looking down.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on knowing what a keyboard is and that pressing a key puts a character on the screen, and on nothing else; the AZERTY layout itself is built inside the lesson.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The AZERTY layout — where the letters sit** — The top letter row begins A, Z, E, R, T, Y, which is where the keyboard gets its name; A and Z change places with Q and W compared with QWERTY, M sits on the home row side, and the number row gives its figures with Shift while its unshifted side carries the accented letters and symbols French writing needs.
2. **Typing with the right fingers** — Rest the fingers on the home row, one finger owning its own keys, thumbs on the space bar; type slowly letter by letter, eyes on the copy and not on the hands, and let speed come from accuracy practised daily.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say why the keyboard is called AZERTY and point to where A, Z, E, R, T and Y sit.
2. Name how AZERTY differs from QWERTY for A, Z, Q, W and M.
3. Sit in the home position, with each finger owning its keys and thumbs on the space bar.
4. Type a short line slowly and accurately without looking down at the hands.

## What you need
A computer or tablet with an AZERTY keyboard if one is available; otherwise a printed or drawn copy of the AZERTY layout, plus paper and a pencil.

## If the learner is stuck
Keeps looking for Q where A sits → their hands remember QWERTY; cover the hands with a sheet of paper for short practice so the eyes cannot hunt, and go letter by letter. Types fast with many errors → slow right down: accuracy first, speed follows practice. Cannot find a figure → figures on AZERTY need Shift on the number row; the unshifted side is for accents and symbols.
