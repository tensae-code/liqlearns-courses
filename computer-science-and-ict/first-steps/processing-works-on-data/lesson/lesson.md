# Processing — the computer works on the data

## Do it first

Your teacher collects the class's test marks on paper. Before a roster can be shown, someone must add the marks, work out averages, and order who came top. Who — or what — does that work in a computer? Say your answer, then read on.

## Processing is the work done on data

Processing is doing operations on the entered data. Adding two numbers is processing. Changing a photo's colours, borders and brightness is processing — the picture that comes out is not the picture that went in; work has been done on it.

## The book's example — the class roster

When preparing a roster, the computer takes students' marks and works out totals, averages, and who came top. The marks are the input; the totalling, averaging and ranking are the processing; the roster you will see in the next lesson is the output.

## Say it to your companion

Explain processing to your companion using the roster: what goes in, what work is done, and what is then ready to be shown?
