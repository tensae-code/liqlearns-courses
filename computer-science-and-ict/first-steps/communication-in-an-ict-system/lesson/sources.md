**Acceptance count: taught in 10 countries (Ethiopia, Cambodia, Azerbaijan, Afghanistan, Egypt, Rwanda, Moldova, Bangladesh, Armenia, Kazakhstan).**

Afghanistan's own way (Grade 12): the final year turns outward — computers in society (e-commerce, communications, even military systems), system software and virus protection, then Excel for the first time and a closing block on the Web, e-mail and browsers (taught through Internet Explorer).

No book error touches this lesson's content.

Egypt's own way (Grade 10, Term 1 Programming and Artificial Intelligence student book): A full Japanese-style "Information Study" course adopted for Egypt's new secondary track: 13 chapters running from information theory, ethics and security through number systems, data analysis (frequency, variance, correlation, regression in spreadsheets), simulation and queues, Python, and a final AI-programming project chapter building web pages, a quiz app and a block-breaker game with generative AI.

Rwanda's own way (S1 ICT Student Book, KLB): competency-based units each opening with a Key Unit Competency and keywords; a full GIS/ArcGIS unit and a Scratch music-making unit sit inside the first secondary year, and ICT use is grounded in Rwandan daily life.

Moldova's own way (Grade 1, Educație Digitală): digital education starts at age 6–7 with no keyboard skills assumed — information, devices and staying safe first, told through two child guides (Ana and Ștefan), and programming introduced unplugged (instructions, algorithm, a first maze program, debugging) before any real software. The Digital Education module sits inside Technological Education in the early grades.

Bangladesh's own way (Grade 6, Digital Technology): the Competency-based National Curriculum 2022 book is organised as nine project "Learning Experiences" done in groups through stories, games and fieldwork — no conventional chapters; even learners without a computer at home learn technology through experience around school and home. The NCTB Digital Technology line runs in parallel English and Bangla editions; placements are cited from the English Version with the Bangla Version parallel spot-checked.

Bangladesh's own way (Grade 7, Digital Technology): the Grade 7 book continues the Learning-Experience project format — intellectual property, virtual identity, cybercrime, robotics-through-pseudocode and e-services are each learned by doing a class project with the recurring robot character Nano. The NCTB Digital Technology line runs in parallel English and Bangla editions; placements are cited from the English Version with the Bangla Version parallel spot-checked.

Bangladesh's own way (HSC ICT, Classes XI–XII and Alim): one compulsory ICT book for every HSC and Alim student in the country, saturated with Bangladesh itself: Bangabandhu Satellite-1 and its ground stations, bdjobs and chakri.com job portals, Daraz and Bikroy e-commerce, the Walton factory's automated production line, the National ID card used to teach primary keys and its database used to teach RDBMS at work, a full Bangla Unicode table and a worked example of putting Bangla text on a web page with meta charset, the national letter-grade scale (A+ from 80) as the else-if programme, "I Love Bangladesh" as the loop example text, an algorithm for reaching college by bicycle or rickshaw, and the Digital Bangladesh programme — National Data Centre, hi-tech parks, Startup Bangladesh and Bangladesh Bank's QR-based Cashless Bangladesh. C is the HSC programming language, taught with scanf/printf in Code::Blocks.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 7 G7 student): A new author team (Topchyan & Melkonyan, 2025) rebuilds the course around the "virtual world": data and encoding, networks, e-security and e-communication, then a full Python chapter — decisions, lists, loops, functions, objects and classes — taught as making the real world virtual.


Kazakhstan's own way (Grade 1 teacher guide «Цифровая грамотность. Методическое руководство» 2023 (Russian + Kazakh editions)): the 2023 guide restructures Grade 1 against the 2021 student book (Book 5): robotics disappears, replaced by a drawing section («Мой первый рисунок», taught in Tux Paint in the Kazakh plans) and a separate algorithms section (concept of algorithm, executors and their command systems, kinds of algorithms) before Scratch programming.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukametzhanova G6 «Информатика»): the whole second half of Grade 6 is one long game-development arc — idea, scenario, drawing scenes and characters (vector editor), realising the scenario, sound, a title screen, improving the project — and then documenting the game properly with footnotes, hyperlinks and an automatically built table of contents.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G5 «Информатика»): Grade 5 in the Almatykitap line is a robotics year: after information representation (encryption, binary, binary-coded graphics) and raster/vector graphics, two full sections run LEGO robots — gyro sensor, turns, line following and robo-sumo competitions — before a closing safety section that ends with shared folders and mini-projects. Every term is glossed in three languages (Kazakh/Russian/English) in the margin.

Kazakhstan's own way (Salgaraeva G6 «Информатика»): the Arman-PV Grade 6 book is a game-making year wrapped in document skills: ergonomics and wireless networks first, then binary coding and encryption, vector graphics (drawing a «Жайлау» summer pasture), a Scratch game designed, built and given a sound track and title screen — and the year ends with footnotes, hyperlinks, copyright and plagiarism, a table of contents and the written documentation for the game.

Kazakhstan's own way (Atayeva/Vyushkova/Zhurbenko G8 «Информатика» NIS): the NIS Grade 8 book is the widest of the set: a whole section on vector graphics and geographic information systems (planning an excursion, making maps, visualising data in GIS), octal and hexadecimal coding, loops applied separately to mathematics, physics, business and animation, strings and subroutines, and a nine-step project method ending in a mini-project on artificial intelligence.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukametzhanova G5 «Информатика» 2020 edition): the 2020 Atamura revision of the Grade 5 book is rebuilt on the renewed programme — the same five sections as the Almatykitap book (Book 18): presenting information with encryption and binary, raster and vector graphics with curves, robotics with the gyroscope sensor and turns, robot competitions (line following and robo-sumo), and computer safety moved to the end.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kobdikova G5 «Информатика»): the Arman-PV Grade 5 book is the practicum twin of the same programme as Books 18 and 35 — nearly every unit comes in a theory + practice pair, and it adds a block the others lack: sharing files (placing, changing and downloading shared files), setting a password on documents, and organising a document effectively for print, taught as "mini-project works".
