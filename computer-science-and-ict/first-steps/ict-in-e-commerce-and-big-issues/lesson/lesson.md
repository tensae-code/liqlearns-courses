# ICT in e-commerce — and ICT in big issues

## Do it first

Imagine buying something without walking to a shop: you choose it on a phone, money moves without any hand touching it, and the thing comes to you. That is the first half of this lesson. The second half asks a harder question: what can ICT do about the biggest problems a country faces?

## Sub lesson 1 — E-commerce

E-commerce — electronic commerce — is buying and selling goods and services through the internet. It also covers transferring funds and data through the internet: the money and the information move even when the buyer never leaves home. 

[Picture 10115 — a phone shopping basket with a payment arrow]



The shop, in other words, can live inside the phone. The basket, the payment and the receipt are all ICT at work — the same communication technologies from the first lessons, pointed at trade.

## Sub lesson 2 — ICT in big issues (teacher guide — evidence)

The student book sets groups a discussion: what is ICT's role in child trafficking, in COVID-19, in HIV/AIDS, and in climate change and agriculture? The teacher guide keys the discussion. 

[Picture 10116 — a phone carrying a health-ministry prevention message]



Against **child trafficking**: awareness-raising, reaching stakeholders through channels such as social media. For **COVID-19 and HIV/AIDS**: trusted prevention information, delivered by bodies such as the Ministry of Health through web pages and media outlets. For **climate change and agriculture**: weather forecasting. For **gender discrimination**: equal opportunity for men and women to access information — women's empowerment. On all four, the guide tells the teacher to accept varied group answers: these are discussion questions, and thinking them through is the work.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: E-commerce is buying and selling goods and services, or transferring funds and data, through the internet — and ICT also has a role in the big issues groups discuss: child trafficking, COVID-19, HIV/AIDS, and climate change and agriculture. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

2. Show it, don't just say it: give the guide's keyed ICT roles: awareness against trafficking; trusted prevention information for COVID-19 and HIV/AIDS; weather forecasting for climate and agriculture. Do this with a real example from your own home, school or street, and write or say how you know it is right.
