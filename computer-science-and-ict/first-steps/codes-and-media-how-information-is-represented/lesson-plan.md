# Lesson plan — Codes and Media — How Information Is Represented

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Codes and Media — How Information Is Represented
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Cambodia (2026-11-14) — in the title First Steps on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Cambodia's *Information and Communication Technology, Grade 10*, because no existing lesson teaches this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Information never travels naked: it is put into a code — a shared system of signs that stands for it — and carried by a medium such as text, a picture, sound or video; and the right code and medium are chosen from the context — who must receive the information, where, and for what.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on what information is and on everyday signs the learner already reads — words, pictures, traffic lights, a school bell; context, code and medium are built inside the lesson from those.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. ## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Context and codes — information put into signs** — Context is the whole situation a message lives in: who sends it, who must receive it, where and why. A code is a system of signs with shared meanings — letters for sounds and words, digits for numbers, colours for go and stop — that information is put into so another person (or a machine) who knows the code can read it back.
2. **Media — the carriers, and choosing by context** — A medium is what carries the coded information: printed text, a picture, spoken sound, a video, a file on a screen. The same information can ride in different media, and the context chooses: a warning that must be seen at a distance becomes a big coloured sign, news for someone who cannot see the screen becomes sound, a long official message becomes written text that can be kept and checked.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a code is, and give two codes they already read every day.
2. Say what a medium is, and name the media carrying one message.
3. Say what context means for a message: sender, receiver, place and purpose.
4. Take one piece of information and represent it in two different codes or media, and say which fits a given context better, and why.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Things around the room that carry messages — a book, a sign, a phone, a bell or a whistle — are the examples.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Look at one message in the room — words in a book, a picture on the wall, the sound of a bell. The message itself is invisible; what you can point to is signs on a carrier. Ask: what signs? carried by what? meant for whom? Those three questions are this whole lesson. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of one message — "school is closed tomorrow" — shown three ways: as written text, as a spoken announcement with sound waves, and as a picture sign — the student names the code and the medium in each of the three.

## If the learner is stuck
Thinks the code is the thing itself → the sign stands for the thing; the letters of a name are not the person. Mixes code and medium → ask two questions: what are the signs (code)? what are they riding on (medium)? A letter is a code; the paper, the screen and the spoken voice are media.
