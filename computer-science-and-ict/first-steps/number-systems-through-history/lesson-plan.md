## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Counting among the ancient nations** — Every ancient nation built its own way of **counting and writing numbers**: tallies and counting stones for small amounts, then written signs for larger ones. The systems differed — different signs, different groupings — but all faced the same limits: big numbers were heavy to write, and arithmetic was hard. Libya teaches this history first, so the number systems that follow are seen as inventions people made to solve a real problem, not rules that fell from the sky.
2. **The discovery of zero** — The turning point is **zero**. As a sign for **nothing**, zero lets a counting system say 'none of these'; as a **place holder**, it lets the same digit stand for different values by its place — the difference between 5, 50 and 500 is zero at work. With zero, a positional system needs only a few digits to write any number, however large, and arithmetic by written rules becomes possible — the road that leads, in the end, to the binary system of the computer.
