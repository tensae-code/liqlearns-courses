# Video script — Number Systems Through History

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Every ancient nation built its own way of **counting and writing numbers**: tallies and counting stones for small amounts, then written signs for larger ones. The systems differed — different signs, different groupings — but all faced the same limits: big numbers were heavy to write, and arithmetic was hard. Libya teaches this history first, so the number systems that follow are seen as inventions people made to solve a real problem, not rules that fell from the sky.
The turning point is **zero**. As a sign for **nothing**, zero lets a counting system say 'none of these'; as a **place holder**, it lets the same digit stand for different values by its place — the difference between 5, 50 and 500 is zero at work. With zero, a positional system needs only a few digits to write any number, however large, and arithmetic by written rules becomes possible — the road that leads, in the end, to the binary system of the computer.
