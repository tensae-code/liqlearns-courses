# Lesson plan — Fourth generation — microprocessors and the PC

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** Fourth generation — microprocessors and the PC
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 1 — First Steps of the Computer Science and ICT ladder (TITLES.md); Ethiopia Grade 8, Unit 1 — Basics of Computer.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the fourth generation (1971–present) packs hundreds of thousands of transistors onto one microchip — the microprocessor — and that is what brought the personal computer.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the third-generation lesson: chips held many components; VLSI chips hold hundreds of thousands of transistors.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds, looping.

1. Say the one thing this lesson teaches: the fourth generation (1971–present) packs hundreds of thousands of transistors onto one microchip — the microprocessor — and that is what brought the personal computer.

3. Explain, in their own words: the microprocessor and the pc revolution.
4. Answer this lesson's questions (10 items) from what the lesson itself teaches.
