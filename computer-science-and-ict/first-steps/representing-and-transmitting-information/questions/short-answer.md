# Representing and Transmitting Information — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What two choices make a representation?**
**Answer: A form (text, number, image, sound) and a code turning it into signals**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What are signals?**
**Answer: What a channel can carry — the represented information in travelling form**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What does digitising a voice change?**
**Answer: It can then be copied without loss, stored and resent**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What stands on the choice of representation?**
**Answer: Every later system — networks, the Internet**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) When are the journey's possibilities decided?**
**Answer: At representation — before the journey begins**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What is transmission?**
**Answer: Sending signals over a channel from transmitter to receiver, which decodes them back**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) Name three channels.**
**Answer: Wire, air (radio), and fibre**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What is interference?**
**Answer: Anything in the channel that alters signals on the way — noise**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) Name a defence belonging to representation.**
**Answer: Redundancy and checking codes that notice damage**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) State the chapter's closing model in order.**
**Answer: Source, code, channel, noise, decode, destination**
