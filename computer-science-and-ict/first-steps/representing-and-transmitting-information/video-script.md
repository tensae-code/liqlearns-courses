# Video script — Representing and Transmitting Information

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

To travel, information must be **represented**: given a **form** (text, number, image, sound) and a **code** that turns that form into **signals** a channel can carry. Representation decides the journey's possibilities before it begins: a voice represented as a continuous wave travels one way; the same voice **digitised** — sampled, quantised, coded in bits — travels another, can be copied without loss, stored, and resent. Every later system in the course (networks, the Internet) stands on this choice of representation.
**Transmission** is the sending of signals over a **channel** (wire, air, fibre) from a **transmitter** to a **receiver**, which **decodes** the signals back into information. The road has an enemy: **interference (noise)** — anything in the channel that alters signals on the way, from a crackling line to a storm over a radio path. The defences belong to the representation: redundancy, checks, and codes that notice damage. The chapter's closing model — source, code, channel, noise, decode, destination — is the map the networks unit will now walk with machines.
