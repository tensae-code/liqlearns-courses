# Lesson plan — What a computer is — input, process, store, output

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** First Steps · **Lesson:** What a computer is — input, process, store, output
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — Introduction to Information & Communication Technology — in the title First Steps on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A computer is no longer only a calculating machine — it processes numbers, text and pictures — and its four basic functions are entering (input), processing, storing, and producing processed data as output.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the whole daily-life walk: the learner has met ICT all around them; now they look at the machine at its centre.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What a computer is** — No longer only a calculating machine: a computer processes numbers, text and pictures.
2. **The four basic functions** — Entering (input), processing, storing, and producing processed data as output — and one piece of data travelling round all four.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a computer processes: numbers, text and pictures — not numbers alone.
2. Name the four basic functions in order: input, processing, storing, output.
3. Follow one real task through all four functions.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Watch any computer task in your mind — typing your name and seeing it appear saved on the screen. Break it into steps: something went in, something was worked on, something was kept, something came out. Four steps. This lesson names them, in order. (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
Names only three functions → count the GIF's stops: in, work, keep, out. Thinks storing comes last → the book's order stores before output: results are kept, then produced.
