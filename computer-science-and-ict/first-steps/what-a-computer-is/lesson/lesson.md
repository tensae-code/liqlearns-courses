# What a computer is — input, process, store, output

## Do it first

Watch any computer task in your mind — typing your name and seeing it appear saved on the screen. Break it into steps: something went in, something was worked on, something was kept, something came out. Four steps. This lesson names them, in order.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a computer is

A computer was once only a calculating machine — a machine for numbers. Not any more. A computer processes numbers, yes — but also text, and also pictures. The letter you type, the photo you open, the sum you check: all of it is the computer's work.

## Sub lesson 2 — The four basic functions

Every computer task, however large, is built from four basic functions, in this order:

1. **Entering (input)** — data goes in.
2. **Processing** — the computer works on the data.
3. **Storing** — data and results are kept.
4. **Output** — the processed data is produced for the user to see, hear or read.

Watch one piece of data make the whole journey. 

[GIF 10118 — a piece of data travelling round the four steps]

 In as a keystroke, worked on inside, stored away, and out onto the screen as a finished letter. Input, process, store, output — the cycle every task follows.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: A computer is no longer only a calculating machine — it processes numbers, text and pictures — and its four basic functions are entering (input), processing, storing, and producing processed data as output. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
