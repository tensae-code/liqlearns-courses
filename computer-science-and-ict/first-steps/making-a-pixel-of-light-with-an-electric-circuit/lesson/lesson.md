# Making a Pixel of Light With an Electric Circuit

## Do it first

A screen is millions of tiny lights switching on and off. Before reading on, ask the smallest question: what does it take to light ONE? This lesson builds that one — a pixel of light — with your own hands.

## Sub lesson 1 — The circuit — battery, wires, lamp

A **circuit** is a closed loop the current can travel: from the **battery (pile)**, along a wire, through the **lamp (ampoule)**, and back along another wire to the battery. Break the loop anywhere and the lamp dies — the loop must be whole. The scenario's first builds (montages avec pile et ampoules) are exactly this: one battery, one lamp, one unbroken ring, and the discipline of finding the break when the light refuses.

## Sub lesson 2 — One lamp, one pixel

Light the lamp and you hold a **pixel**: one point of light that can be on or off — the two states all screens are built from. A screen is this build multiplied: millions of such points, each switchable, each small enough that together they draw pictures. The scenario climbs from your single lamp to that idea: an image is a crowd of lights, and every light obeys the same simple loop you just closed.
