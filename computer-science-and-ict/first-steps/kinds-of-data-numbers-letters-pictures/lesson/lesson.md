# Kinds of Data — Numbers, Letters, Pictures

## Do it first

A mark in a register is a number. A name is letters. A photo on a phone is a picture. All three are data. In this lesson you learn to tell the kinds of data apart and name the kind in front of you.

## Sub lesson 1 — Numbers and letters — data you can read as symbols

**Numeric data** is made of numbers: a mark of 85, a price, a count of students — data the computer can calculate with. **Alphabetic data** is made of letters and words: a name, an address, a sentence — data the computer stores and shows, but does not add up. The two kinds are often mixed in one record — a student's name beside the student's mark — and a good user keeps them straight, because a number stored as letters cannot be calculated with.

## Sub lesson 2 — Pictures — data you see

**Picture data** — photographs, drawings, scanned pages — is a third kind: data you take in by seeing, not by reading symbols. The computer stores a picture as its own kind of data, in far more storage than a name or a number takes, and shows it back on the screen or in print. Sound and moving pictures stand beside still pictures as further kinds, but the book's first classification is these three: numbers, letters, pictures.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Data comes in kinds: numbers, letters and words, and pictures are the three kinds Yemen's Grade 11 book classifies first — and the computer takes in, stores and works with every one of them. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: Numbers, letters, and pictures. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: Calculate with it. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: Photographs, drawings and scanned pages — data you take in by seeing. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: Far more storage. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
