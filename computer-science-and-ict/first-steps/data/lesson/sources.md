**Acceptance count: taught in 12 countries (Ethiopia, Tanzania, China, Yemen, Iraq, Cuba, Libya, Rwanda, Laos, Greece, Moldova, Armenia).**

Yemen's own way (Grade 11): the book pivots from operating the machine to understanding information itself — data kinds, number systems and coding get full units before the applications (presentations, Excel) and a networks/Internet finale; every unit closes with a formal تقويم (assessment).

No book error touches this lesson's content.

Iraq's own way (Grade 7): a four-unit spiral repeated at every grade — computer fundamentals, computer software (here: the operating system plus an unusually deep treatment of Windows right-click shortcut menus), algorithms and programming principles (ending in Scratch), and information technology and the internet — every lesson built on the Iraqi five-step learning cycle with opening activity, main idea, learning outcomes, vocabulary and lesson review.

Cuba's own way (Grade 9): Grade 9 is the formal programming-foundations year — algorithms, data, variables, counters/accumulators, flowcharts and pseudocode, taught language-independently — paired with a full cybersecurity chapter (passwords/MFA, safe internet, safe social networks).

Libya's own way (Grade 8, Computer student book): a three-unit arc — computer history/generations and number systems first, then a whole practical unit built around the scanner (scan, save, copy, delete, scan via Paint), then Microsoft Excel 2007 taught step by step with screenshots. The basic-education line is الحاسوب (Computer) in Grades 8–9.

Rwanda's own way (S1 ICT Student Book, KLB): competency-based units each opening with a Key Unit Competency and keywords; a full GIS/ArcGIS unit and a Scratch music-making unit sit inside the first secondary year, and ICT use is grounded in Rwandan daily life.

Laos's own way (Grade 6 ICT student book): a theory-only foundation year — no hands-on computer practicals at all; ICT is introduced through Lao daily-life examples (TV remote, household appliances) and every chapter opens with objectives, guiding questions and a discussion activity (ກິດຈະກໍາ) before the exposition. The RIES ICT line runs ມ.1–ມ.7 (Grades 6–12).

Laos's own way (Grade 11 ICT student book): a single-topic year — databases from first principles (the data hierarchy is taught as a Lao glossary table: Bit, Byte/Character, Field, Record, Files, Database) into a complete MS Access 2007 build: tables, relationships, forms, queries, reports.

Greece's own way (Grade 11, Eisagogi stis Arches tis Epistimis ton H/Y B' Lykeiou): a theory-first introduction to computer science in three units — basic concepts (theoretical vs applied CS), theoretical CS (problem, algorithms in depth including complexity/computability, data structures operations, debugging logic errors and documentation, programming paradigms and structured programming, software life cycle), and applied CS (operating systems in depth, information systems with databases and SQL/XML, networks, AI).

Greece's own way (Grade 12, Anaptyxi Efarmogon — AEPP): Greece's national-exam programming book — problem analysis, algorithms, data structures (arrays, stack, queue, lists, trees, graphs; search and sort; recursion), algorithm design methods (divide-and-conquer, dynamic programming, greedy), algorithm performance/complexity/correctness, then programming in Greece's own school pseudocode language ΓΛΩΣΣΑ (selection/repetition commands, one- and multi-dimensional arrays, subprograms with parameters and scope, recursion), object-oriented and event-driven programming, GUI elements, user-interface design, debugging and runtime errors, and program evaluation/documentation/software life cycle.

Moldova's own way (Grade 1, Educație Digitală): digital education starts at age 6–7 with no keyboard skills assumed — information, devices and staying safe first, told through two child guides (Ana and Ștefan), and programming introduced unplugged (instructions, algorithm, a first maze program, debugging) before any real software. The Digital Education module sits inside Technological Education in the early grades.

Switzerland's own way (Grades 1–2, Décodage 1re–2e): Vaud's Romandie digital-education curriculum is taught in three axes — Science informatique (SI), Médias (M), Usages (U) — almost entirely UNPLUGGED at this age: algorithms are learned with the body (robot game, crane game, hand-washing, getting dressed, a crêpe recipe), a physical sorting machine, and the Blue-Bot floor robot; the class also builds a collective digital “charter book” of good digital habits across the whole cycle.

Switzerland's own way (Grades 3–4, Décodage 3e–4e): the 3e–4e volume repeats the 1re–2e unplugged activities at a higher level (loops and debugging join the robot and crane games and the sorting machine) and adds three new strands: Thymio robotics, a full ScratchJr scenario, and media “parcours” — Parcours des bestioles (how images influence emotions), Parcours du partage (image rights, digital traces, thinking before sharing) and Parcours de la pub (recognising and creating advertising). Pixel paravent introduces binary coding as transmitting a black-and-white image across a screen with an agreed code; Deviner la carte teaches the conditional through a magic trick and its logigramme.

Switzerland's own way (Grades 5–6, Décodage 5e–6e): Cycle 2 moves from unplugged games to real tools while keeping the game frame: the imaginary city “Square CT” teaches classification, decision trees, conditionals, indentation and flowcharts (logigrammes); ScratchJr tells emotion and climate stories; the Swiss Thymio robot is programmed in VPL; data is taught through binary pixel coding and colour coding; and “Enquêtes” (inquiries) teach addition-as-algorithm, debugging, sorting, the Nim winning strategy, Caesar ciphers and sensor experiments. Médias/Usages scenarios cover cyberbullying, screen practices, news and media stereotypes.

Switzerland's own way (Grades 7–8, Décodage 7e–8e): at 7e–8e the Décodage course becomes a real computer-science course under the Plan d'études romand EN 22/23: Thymio VPL missions (including Morse communication and self-driving-car autonomy levels), a full Scratch discovery course (movement, shortening programs, sub-programs as own blocks, remixing), a Scratch video-game project run in “studios” with a cahier des charges, data work (Huffman-style compression via Zip la marmotte, binary/octal/hex/ASCII codings, file sizes), access security (passwords, file encryption, phishing, authentication), home networks at two levels (IP addresses; message routing, packets, TCP and encryption), and inquiries into sorting, sorting networks, Caesar decryption and parity-bit error detection. Médias/Usages covers social networks and digital identity/footprint, information sources, cyberbullying and the environmental cost of digital technology.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 2 G2 student): A story-led primary book built around two Grade 2 characters (Hayk and Arpi); every lesson uses fixed activity "rooms" (key knowledge, key words, skills/tasks) and QR codes link to audio/video material; safety and health come before any machine skills.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 3 G3 student): Grade 3 (Stepanyan, Amirkhanyan, Matinyan) keeps the Hayk-and-Arpi story frame and QR-linked media: device safety, technology around us, picture and text editors, e-mail with attachments, input/output devices, keyboard keys, data collection with programs, then a Scratch strand — environment, first program, pictures, repeated actions, and game scores as a first step into variables.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 5 G5 student (Antares)): One of two different Grade 5 books in the set (this one by Velijanyan & Ayvazyan, Antares); it runs as numbered § lessons mixing information theory, careful judgement of fake vs true information, and a long practical spine — text editor, spreadsheets, algorithms and variables — ending in teamwork principles.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 5 G5 teacher): The guide adds a strand the student book does not print — touch-typing with ten fingers — plus per-theme outcome/source tables (including the KuMir programming environment for variables), sample lesson plans, and full assessment rubrics for projects and slide presentations.
