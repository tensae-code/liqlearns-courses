# Data — raw facts with no meaning yet

## Do it first

Read this list: Selam — 14 — Hana — 15 — 7 — 20. What is it about? Who are these people? What do the numbers count? You cannot tell — and that is exactly the point of this lesson. What you are holding is data.

## Data — raw facts with no meaning yet

Data is any representation of an object or an event. It can be numbers. It can be text. It can be symbols, speech, or images. 

[Picture 10104 — a jumbled list of names and numbers with a question mark over it]



By itself, data has no meaning. A list of names and numbers, with nothing to say what the list is for, means nothing — are the numbers ages? Marks? Prices? House numbers? Nobody can tell from the data alone.

That is not a fault in the data. Data is the raw material — the facts before anyone has put them into context. The next lesson shows what happens when someone does.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Data is any representation of an object or event — numbers, text, symbols, speech, images — which by itself has no meaning. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: say what data is, and name its forms: numbers, text, symbols, speech, images. Do this with a real example from your own home, school or street, and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: explain why a bare list of names and numbers has no meaning by itself. Do this with a real example from your own home, school or street, and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: give an example of data from your own school day. Do this with a real example from your own home, school or street, and write or say how you know it is right.
