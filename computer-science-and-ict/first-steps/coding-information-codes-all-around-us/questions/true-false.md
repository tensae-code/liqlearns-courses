# Coding Information — Codes All Around Us — true-false

Say whether each statement is true or false.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "What is a code?" is: A system of signs with agreed meanings, representing information in another form**
**Answer: True**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "How does the alphabet-position code work?" is: Short and long signals — dots and dashes**
**Answer: False** — Each letter is replaced by its number in the alphabet

**3. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "What does Morse code turn letters into?" is: Short and long signals — dots and dashes**
**Answer: True**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "By what can Morse travel?" is: Sounds — fixing pitch and length so a song can be stored and read back**
**Answer: False** — Light, sound or wire

**5. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "What does music notation code?" is: Sounds — fixing pitch and length so a song can be stored and read back**
**Answer: True**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "What do a road sign's shape and colour announce?" is: A picture-code — a small standard figure understood at a glance**
**Answer: False** — The kind of message — warning, forbidding, directing — before the symbol is read

**7. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "What is a pictogram?" is: A picture-code — a small standard figure understood at a glance**
**Answer: True**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "Why do pictograms work across languages?" is: Only when sender and receiver share it**
**Answer: False** — They are read by the picture, not by words

**9. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "When does a code work at all?" is: Only when sender and receiver share it**
**Answer: True**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "What is coding, in the lesson's closing phrase?" is: A system of signs with agreed meanings, representing information in another form**
**Answer: False** — Agreement, written in signs
