# Editing Pictures with Filters — White Balance, Colour Grading and Sepia

## Do it first

The film's pictures are in, but some look too yellow and some look flat. In this lesson you fix and restyle pictures with three filters. (teacher guide — evidence)

## Sub lesson 1 — The white-balance filter — repairing colour

A camera can record a scene with the wrong **white balance**, so whites come out yellow or blue and every colour shifts with them. The **white-balance filter** repairs this: it tells the editor what should be truly white, and the whole picture's colours settle back to natural. It is the fixing filter — used first, before any styling.

## Sub lesson 2 — Colour grading and sepia — choosing a look

**Colour grading** deliberately shifts a picture's colours and tones toward a chosen look — warmer, colder, stronger, softer — the way a film's mood is set by its colours. The **sepia tone** is a ready-made grading of one special kind: it washes the picture in warm browns, giving a modern photo the look of an old one. Both are choices of style, made after the picture is correct.
