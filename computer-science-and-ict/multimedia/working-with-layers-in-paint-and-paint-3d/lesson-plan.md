## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Layers thinking in MS Paint — the two-colour technique** — Classic **MS Paint** has no layer panel, so the book teaches the **two-colour line-art technique** as layer thinking by hand: draw the **line art in one colour**, then work the **second colour** under and around the lines — colouring *behind* the drawing by keeping the line colour untouched, so lines and fills behave like **two layers** even on one canvas. The discipline is the lesson: decide what belongs to the line layer and what to the colour layer *before* the brush moves.
2. **Paint 3D — objects that really stack** — **Microsoft Paint 3D** gives real stacking: **3D objects** can be placed, turned and layered in depth; **brushes** paint on canvas and on objects; **effects** change the light and mood of the scene; and **text** can stand flat or as a 3D object. Each element stays **separately selectable** — the layer idea made literal: pick the object, move it, and the rest of the scene does not tear. Rwanda's S3 book pairs the two programs so the learner feels the difference between *simulating* layers and *having* them.
