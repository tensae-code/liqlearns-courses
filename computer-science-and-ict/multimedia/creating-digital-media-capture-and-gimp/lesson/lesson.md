# Creating Digital Media — Capture and GIMP

## Do it first

Until now the files were given. Rwanda's S3 multimedia unit turns the learner into the maker: capture the image, work it in GIMP, record the sound. This lesson is that making.

## Sub lesson 1 — Capture — making the image and sound files

A digital media file begins at **capture**. A **digital camera or smartphone** turns a scene into an **image file** the moment the shutter is pressed — framed, lit and held steady by the photographer, because capture faults are the hardest to repair later. Sound is captured the same way: the book records an **audio file with Voice Recorder**, the microphone standing where the ears would be. Capture is a decision, not a click: what enters the file is what the maker chose to stand in front of.

In your mind's eye: capture — making the image and sound files.

## Sub lesson 2 — Manipulate — working the image in GIMP

The captured image is finished in **GIMP**, the book's image manipulation program: **crop** away the waste, **resize** for its destination, adjust **brightness and colour**, and repair small faults — the same editing family taught in the Photoshop lessons, in the free program. The worked image is **exported** in the format its use demands. Capture honestly, manipulate openly: the unit's maker ethic is that editing improves a picture's clarity, never its truth.

In your mind's eye: manipulate — working the image in GIMP.
