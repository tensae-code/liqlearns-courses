# Lesson plan — Creating Digital Media — Capture and GIMP

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Multimedia · **Lesson:** Creating Digital Media — Capture and GIMP
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Rwanda (2026-11-14) — in the title Multimedia on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Rwanda student books (Kenya Literature Bureau / Fountain Publishers Rwanda, approved by the Rwanda Education Board), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree and live lesson content were checked for the slug and for near matches before building.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Creating digital media files yourself: capturing a photo with a camera or smartphone, manipulating an image in GIMP, and recording an audio file with Voice Recorder
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Capture — making the image and sound files** — A digital media file begins at **capture**. A **digital camera or smartphone** turns a scene into an **image file** the moment the shutter is pressed — framed, lit and held steady by the photographer, because capture faults are the hardest to repair later. Sound is captured the same way: the book records an **audio file with Voice Recorder**, the microphone standing where the ears would be. Capture is a decision, not a click: what enters the file is what the maker chose to stand in front of.
2. **Manipulate — working the image in GIMP** — The captured image is finished in **GIMP**, the book's image manipulation program: **crop** away the waste, **resize** for its destination, adjust **brightness and colour**, and repair small faults — the same editing family taught in the Photoshop lessons, in the free program. The worked image is **exported** in the format its use demands. Capture honestly, manipulate openly: the unit's maker ethic is that editing improves a picture's clarity, never its truth.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
