# Sprites and Costumes in Scratch

## Do it first

Think of an actor in a play who changes clothes between scenes: same actor, new look. A Scratch sprite is the actor; its clothes are its costumes.

## Sprites and Costumes in Scratch

This double lesson builds the pair of ideas every Scratch story needs. A **sprite (герой)** is a character or thing that acts on the stage — it can move, speak and change. Its **costumes (костюми)** are its looks: the same sprite drawn differently — walking and standing, happy and surprised, wings up and wings down. One sprite holds **several costumes**, and a script can **switch costume** to change the look in a moment, which is also the seed of animation: switch looks fast, one after another, and the sprite seems to move its own body. The learner adds sprites, opens their costumes, adds and switches costumes, and names which costume a story moment needs.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: A sprite is a character or object in Scratch, and a costume is one of its looks: one sprite can hold many costumes and change its look by switching costume. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: say what a sprite is and what a costume is. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: add a costume to a sprite and switch to it. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: choose the costume a story moment needs and say why. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
