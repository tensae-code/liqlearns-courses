# Lesson plan — Multimedia and hypermedia — linear vs linked

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Multimedia · **Lesson:** Multimedia and hypermedia — linear vs linked
**Place in the curriculum:** Ethiopia Grade 10, Unit 4 — Image Processing and Multimedia (printed pp. 139–181).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** multimedia is the broad digital integration of text, pictures, graphics, audio, animation and video — hypermedia is its non-linear extension, the same material linked by a hypertext program, with the World Wide Web as its best example.
**This lesson's own level:** it closes the unit: every component, tool and editing skill of the unit is gathered into the one distinction the unit ends on.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds, looping.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The whole of Unit 4 — components, authoring, editing and animation. Recommended, never forced. A learner can land here directly; the lesson stands alone.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define multimedia and hypermedia, and the difference between them.
2. Name the World Wide Web as hypermedia's best example.
3. Define computer animation as the unit summary gives it.
## What you need

## If the learner is stuck
- Treats hypermedia as 'more media' → the difference is not amount but movement: links make the journey non-linear.
- Calls every website page multimedia only → the Web's linked structure is exactly what makes it hypermedia.
