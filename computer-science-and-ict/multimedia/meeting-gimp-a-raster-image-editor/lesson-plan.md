## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **GIMP — the window and the toolbox** — **GIMP** (GNU Image Manipulation Program) is a **free raster image editor**: it works on images made of **pixels**, changing them dot by dot. Its window gathers the **toolbox** (selection, paint, erase, fill, text and transform tools), the **canvas** where the image stands, and panels for the tool's options, colours and **layers**. Every tool is chosen, given its options (size, hardness, colour), and applied to the canvas — the same choose-set-apply rhythm as Paint's, at professional depth.
2. **Layers and practical raster work** — GIMP's central idea is the **layer**: the image is a stack of transparent sheets, each holding part of the picture, edited without touching the others — text on its own layer, a pasted photo on another. The practical section drills the raster crafts: **open and crop** a photo, **correct** its colours, **erase and retouch** a flaw, **compose** layers into one picture, and **save** in the working format (keeping layers) versus **export** flat (PNG, JPEG) for use. Layer discipline — name layers, edit the right one — is the practical's real syllabus.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
