**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Multimedia · **Lesson:** Making a Photo Collage with Layers and Transparency
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Mongolia (2026-11-14) — in the title Multimedia on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). **The one thing this lesson teaches:** Making a photo collage in Paint.NET — pasting pictures together on layers, cutting with the Magic Wand, blending by transparency, keeping perspective in the drawing, and choosing colours with a colour calculator (RGB, CMYK, hex codes, the colour wheel)

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Layers, Magic Wand, transparency — the collage's craft** — A **collage** pastes pictures together into one image, and in the layered editor each pasted part lands on **its own layer**. The **Magic Wand** selects by colour likeness — click a sky and the wand takes the sky — so a photographed animal can be lifted out of its background in one selection. **Transparency** then does the blending: a layer's opacity lowered lets it melt into what is beneath, edges and skies soften into each other, and five photographs stop looking pasted and start looking present. Cut cleanly, stack honestly, blend patiently — the collage's order.
2. **Perspective and the colour calculator** — Two knowledges keep the made scene true. **Perspective**: the pasted parts must obey one space — far animals smaller, all feet on one ground line, one light over all. And **colour choice** is done with instruments, not wishes: the **colour calculator** gives a colour its numbers — **RGB** for the screen, **CMYK** for print, its **hex code** for the web — and the **colour wheel** shows which colours marry and which fight. Mongolia's collage is judged as the composition lesson judged: one picture, one land, one light — the livestock standing in a steppe that never existed, believed because every craft in the lesson was obeyed.
