## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Shapes and curves, layer by layer** — Drawing in the layered way means choosing the **layer** before the tool: **shapes and curves** for one part of the picture are drawn on that part's own layer, so a mistake costs one sheet, not the picture. A finished layer can be **duplicated** — the same part twice, as a row of trees from one tree — and **rotated** as a whole, the sheet turning under the drawing. Editing's question changes in a layered editor: not 'how do I fix this mark?' but 'which layer is the mark on?'
2. **Finding and saving pictures — with the copyright note** — Not every part is drawn: parts are also **found**. The book teaches **Google image search** as the finding tool — search by words, judge the results, and **save** the picture into the work. With the finding comes the book's **copyright note**, taught in the same breath: a found picture was made by someone; using it carries the duties of naming its source and respecting its maker's rights, in a school picture as in a printed book. Finding, saving and owing — Mongolia binds the three together so the skill and its ethics arrive as one habit.
