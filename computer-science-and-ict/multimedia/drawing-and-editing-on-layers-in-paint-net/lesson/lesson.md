# Drawing and Editing on Layers in Paint.NET

## Do it first

The layers exist; now they earn their keep. Mongolia's second Paint.NET lesson draws the way the layered editor invites: every part on its own sheet, whole sheets copied and turned, and the world's pictures fetched — carefully — to join the work.

## Sub lesson 1 — Shapes and curves, layer by layer

Drawing in the layered way means choosing the **layer** before the tool: **shapes and curves** for one part of the picture are drawn on that part's own layer, so a mistake costs one sheet, not the picture. A finished layer can be **duplicated** — the same part twice, as a row of trees from one tree — and **rotated** as a whole, the sheet turning under the drawing. Editing's question changes in a layered editor: not 'how do I fix this mark?' but 'which layer is the mark on?'

In your mind's eye: shapes and curves, layer by layer.

## Sub lesson 2 — Finding and saving pictures — with the copyright note

Not every part is drawn: parts are also **found**. The book teaches **Google image search** as the finding tool — search by words, judge the results, and **save** the picture into the work. With the finding comes the book's **copyright note**, taught in the same breath: a found picture was made by someone; using it carries the duties of naming its source and respecting its maker's rights, in a school picture as in a printed book. Finding, saving and owing — Mongolia binds the three together so the skill and its ethics arrive as one habit.

In your mind's eye: finding and saving pictures — with the copyright note.
