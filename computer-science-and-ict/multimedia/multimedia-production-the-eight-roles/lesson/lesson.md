# Multimedia production — the eight roles

## Do it first

Think of a short school video or presentation you have seen. Before reading on, list every job somebody must have done to make it — filming, writing, drawing, recording, organising. This lesson names the team.

## What production is

**Multimedia production** is any work using imagery plus a combination of text, audio and graphics to tell a story — selling a product, narrating an event, educating, making art. It begins by **gathering** content elements — text, photo, video — and **integrating** them into a meaningful presentation.

## The eight roles

The team has eight roles. The **production manager** defines, coordinates and facilitates the project. The **content specialist** researches the content. The **scriptwriter** visualises the environment and its virtual-reality integration. The **text editor** revises all text. The **multimedia architect / authoring specialist** integrates every element with authoring programs. The **computer graphic artist** makes backgrounds, buttons, collages, edited pictures, 3D objects and logos. The **audio-video specialist** records and edits narration, effects and music, and captures, edits and digitises video. The **computer programmer** writes the code and scripts in the authoring language.

One person can hold two roles on a small project — but no role's work can simply be skipped.

## Say it to your companion

Walk your companion around the table: name each role and its job. Then say which two roles you would take on a class project, and why.

## Do It Yourself

1. Match the job to the role: revises all text; researches content; writes code in the authoring language; makes logos and 3D objects.
2. For a one-minute video about your school, write which role does what — one line per role.
3. Say what production begins by doing, in the book's two verbs.
