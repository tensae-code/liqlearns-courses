# Audio File Formats — Size and Quality

## Do it first

The same song is 30 MB in one format and 3 MB in another — and sounds almost the same. Malaysia's Grade 7 book closes its media unit with that puzzle. In this lesson you learn what audio formats do, and how to choose between size and quality.

## Sub lesson 1 — Lossless and lossy keeping

**WAV** keeps the recorded samples **exactly** — uncompressed or losslessly kept, full quality, full size. **MP3** and similar formats are **lossy**: they discard parts of the sound the ear is least likely to miss, and keep the rest at a chosen **bit rate**. What is discarded cannot be recovered — saving lossy is a one-way door. Other formats (such as AAC) make the same trade with different discarding rules.

## Sub lesson 2 — The same audio in several formats

Saved across formats, **the same audio** comes out **smallest and slightly thinned in lossy formats**, and **largest and exact in WAV**. Malaysia's comparison is made by listening as well as measuring: at a generous bit rate the lossy copy fools most ears; at a stingy one, the thinning is audible — cymbals smear, voices flatten. The maker's rule follows: keep the master lossless, publish lossy at a bit rate the purpose justifies — size is bought with quality, knowingly.
