## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Lossless and lossy keeping** — **WAV** keeps the recorded samples **exactly** — uncompressed or losslessly kept, full quality, full size. **MP3** and similar formats are **lossy**: they discard parts of the sound the ear is least likely to miss, and keep the rest at a chosen **bit rate**. What is discarded cannot be recovered — saving lossy is a one-way door. Other formats (such as AAC) make the same trade with different discarding rules.
2. **The same audio in several formats** — Saved across formats, **the same audio** comes out **smallest and slightly thinned in lossy formats**, and **largest and exact in WAV**. Malaysia's comparison is made by listening as well as measuring: at a generous bit rate the lossy copy fools most ears; at a stingy one, the thinning is audible — cymbals smear, voices flatten. The maker's rule follows: keep the master lossless, publish lossy at a bit rate the purpose justifies — size is bought with quality, knowingly.
