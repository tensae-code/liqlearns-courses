**Acceptance count: taught in 5 countries (Ethiopia, North Macedonia, Uzbekistan, Mongolia, Kazakhstan).**

Nothing in this lesson claims a page the placements do not give; the lesson's content is the placements' content, re-expressed for the learner, with the book's own step orders, examples and Ethiopian practicals kept. Procedures are taught exactly as the book teaches them — Photoshop first, with the GIMP alternative where the book notes it.

North Macedonia's own way (Year 2): a strikingly current first theme (cybersecurity, cryptography, blockchain, robotics, AI/ML and large language models) precedes deep C++ (data representation, functions, one-dimensional arrays), a full databases theme ending in SQL, multimedia with generative AI for graphics, and HTML web authoring.


Uzbekistan's own way (Grade 9): grade 9 is the capstone — computer graphics, Access, Excel, then a nine-lesson algorithms block and a 28-lesson Turbo Pascal 7.0 course that runs from the alphabet of the language to files, procedures and graphics mode, closing with the Internet. Every chapter ends with a dedicated repetition lesson, placed here to that chapter's main lesson.

Mongolia's own way (Grade 8): Grade 8 is built around two long projects — a folded leaflet on Ulaanbaatar's air pollution (Word templates, styles, WordArt, charts and SmartArt) that then becomes a PowerPoint presentation with its own master slide and hyperlink buttons, and a Paint.NET collage of the five-snout livestock (таван хошуу мал) in a steppe landscape. Models are taught on Mongolian material (the livestock, Rutherford's atom, a virus-growth formula); spreadsheets open with the chessboard-and-wheat legend and count Mongolia's real 2014 livestock by aimag; social networks are taught on Facebook and Twitter ("жиргээ") with Mongolian examples.


Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G5 «Информатика»): Grade 5 in the Almatykitap line is a robotics year: after information representation (encryption, binary, binary-coded graphics) and raster/vector graphics, two full sections run LEGO robots — gyro sensor, turns, line following and robo-sumo competitions — before a closing safety section that ends with shared folders and mini-projects. Every term is glossed in three languages (Kazakh/Russian/English) in the margin.
