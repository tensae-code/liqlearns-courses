## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Digital design and its principles** — **Digital design** is making visual things — posters, publications, images — **on the computer, on purpose**: every element placed because it serves what the design must say. The **principles of design** are its standing rules of judgement: balance (the design does not tip), contrast (what matters stands out), alignment (parts line up as if they meant it), and repetition and space used deliberately. Kuwait teaches the principles before GIMP because tools change and principles judge every tool's work.
2. **Colour systems — the colours of screens and of print** — Colour on a computer is counted, not just named, and the **colour systems** are its countings: the screen's system mixes **light** (red, green and blue added toward white), while print's system mixes **ink** (cyan, magenta, yellow and black laid toward darkness). A colour that glows on screen may print dull because the systems reach it differently — the designer chooses colours knowing which world the design will live in, screen or paper.
