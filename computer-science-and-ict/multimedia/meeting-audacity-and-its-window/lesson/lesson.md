# Meeting Audacity and Its Window

## Do it first

Every craft begins by learning the bench. Class VIII meets Audacity — the free audio editor its whole unit runs on — and learns its window part by part, so hands know where everything is before any sound is touched.

## Sub lesson 1 — The program — a free editor for recorded sound

**Audacity** is a **free program for recording and editing audio**: it records through the microphone, opens audio files, and works on their sound — cutting, changing and mixing — before saving the result. Schools use it because it costs nothing and runs on ordinary computers; Bhutan's Class VIII unit is built on it from this first meeting to the final shared piece.

## Sub lesson 2 — The window — tracks, tools and the waveform

Audacity's window is read in three parts. The **toolbar** carries the transport buttons — play, stop, record — and the editing tools. The **track area** holds each sound as a **track**, drawn as a **waveform**: the picture of the sound, tall where it is loud and flat where it is silent. The **timeline** above the tracks counts the seconds, so any point in the sound can be found by its time.
