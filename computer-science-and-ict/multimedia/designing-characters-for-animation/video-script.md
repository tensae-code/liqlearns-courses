# Video script — Designing Characters for Animation

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

An animated character is not one good drawing — it is a drawing that can be **made again, in any pose, hundreds of times**. So design starts with **simple shapes**: a body built from circles and ovals, a **silhouette** readable at a glance (cover the character in black — is it still itself?), and **proportions** chosen on purpose — a big head reads young and comic, long limbs read elegant or comic-gangly. Vietnam's specialist lesson designs the character before any film is made, on paper first.
The design is fixed on a **model sheet**: the character from the front and side, its **expressions** (happy, surprised, cross — drawn with the same shapes), and its **colours**, few and constant. Personality is drawn, not described: posture, the size of the eyes, a signature item of clothing tell the audience who this is before it speaks. Every later animator — including yourself in scene twelve — draws from the model sheet, which is what keeps one character one character through a whole film.
