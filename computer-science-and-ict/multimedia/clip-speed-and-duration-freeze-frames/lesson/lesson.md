# Clip speed and duration — freeze frames

## Do it first

Act out walking across the room in slow motion, then at double speed. Before reading on, say what changed: the distance, or the time it took? A clip's frames are its steps — this lesson speeds them, slows them, and stops one dead.

## Sub lesson 1 — Speed and duration — tied together

**Speed and duration are tied**: speeding a clip **removes frames and shortens it** — audio pitch rises with speed unless the time-stretch option to **maintain original pitch** is used, and interlaced-field handling may need adjusting below 100% speed — while slowing **repeats frames and lengthens it**. Speed is changed **from the Timeline only**.

**Duration** — first frame (**In point**) to last frame (**Out point**) — starts as imported, is usually changed by trimming either end, but can also be **set directly** to a specific length. **Still images**, unlike video, can be set to **any duration at all**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Freeze frames — holding one frame

A single frame can be **exported as a still image** for use in the movie or elsewhere — for example, a character frozen mid-action.

A freeze is played by pressing **Freeze Frame in the Monitor panel** to make the still, then **inserting it at the original frame's location**, so the video **starts as a still and then begins to move**.
