# The Parts an Image Is Made Of

## Do it first

Before Grade 9 pupils process an image, the book makes them take one apart: what pieces is a picture on a computer actually made of?

## Sub lesson 1 — Points, lines and areas

An image is made of **parts**: single **points** (pixels), **lines** joining points, and filled **areas**. A drawing program builds every picture from these — a house is lines and areas, a face is areas and points of colour. Naming the parts is the first step of the Grade 9 graphics chapter's acquaintance lesson: you cannot edit what you cannot take apart in words.

## Sub lesson 2 — Foreground, background and colour

An image also divides into **foreground** — the thing shown — and **background** — what stands behind it — and every part carries a **colour**. Editors keep these divisions real: select the foreground, change the background, recolour one area. The parts named in this lesson are the handles every later editing lesson grabs.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: An image is made of **parts**: single **points** (pixels), **lines** joining points, and filled **areas**. An image also divides into **foreground** — the thing shown — and **background** — what stands behind it — and every part carries a **colour**. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
