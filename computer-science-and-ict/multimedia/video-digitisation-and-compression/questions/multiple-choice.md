# Video Digitisation and Compression — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What is a video, digitally?**
a) A fast sequence of still images — frames — shown quickly enough that the eye reads movement  b) Like a photograph — pixels with colour depth  c) How many frames one second of video holds  d) Resolution × colour depth × frame rate
**Answer: a) A fast sequence of still images — frames — shown quickly enough that the eye reads movement**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) How is each frame digitised?**
a) How many frames one second of video holds  b) Like a photograph — pixels with colour depth  c) Resolution × colour depth × frame rate  d) Uncompressed video is so large that storage and transmission cannot carry it
**Answer: b) Like a photograph — pixels with colour depth**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What does frame rate decide?**
a) Resolution × colour depth × frame rate  b) Uncompressed video is so large that storage and transmission cannot carry it  c) How many frames one second of video holds  d) What the viewer will not miss
**Answer: c) How many frames one second of video holds**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What product gives a video's raw data rate?**
a) Uncompressed video is so large that storage and transmission cannot carry it  b) What the viewer will not miss  c) Store the changes — most of a frame is the same as the frame before  d) Resolution × colour depth × frame rate
**Answer: d) Resolution × colour depth × frame rate**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) Why is compression essential for video, not optional?**
a) Uncompressed video is so large that storage and transmission cannot carry it  b) What the viewer will not miss  c) Store the changes — most of a frame is the same as the frame before  d) Fine detail the eye forgives
**Answer: a) Uncompressed video is so large that storage and transmission cannot carry it**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What does compression remove?**
a) Store the changes — most of a frame is the same as the frame before  b) What the viewer will not miss  c) Fine detail the eye forgives  d) Softer — some detail is gone
**Answer: b) What the viewer will not miss**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What is the video-only compression trick between frames?**
a) Fine detail the eye forgives  b) Softer — some detail is gone  c) Store the changes — most of a frame is the same as the frame before  d) Sound, then images, then video with compression
**Answer: c) Store the changes — most of a frame is the same as the frame before**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What does lossy compression discard?**
a) Softer — some detail is gone  b) Sound, then images, then video with compression  c) A fast sequence of still images — frames — shown quickly enough that the eye reads movement  d) Fine detail the eye forgives
**Answer: d) Fine detail the eye forgives**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) How does a lossy stream look compared with the original?**
a) Softer — some detail is gone  b) Sound, then images, then video with compression  c) A fast sequence of still images — frames — shown quickly enough that the eye reads movement  d) Like a photograph — pixels with colour depth
**Answer: a) Softer — some detail is gone**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) In what order does Egypt's book teach digitisation?**
a) A fast sequence of still images — frames — shown quickly enough that the eye reads movement  b) Sound, then images, then video with compression  c) Like a photograph — pixels with colour depth  d) How many frames one second of video holds
**Answer: b) Sound, then images, then video with compression**
