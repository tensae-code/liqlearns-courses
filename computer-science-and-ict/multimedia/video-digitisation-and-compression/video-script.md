# Video script — Video Digitisation and Compression

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

A **video** is a fast sequence of still images — **frames** — shown quickly enough that the eye reads movement. Digitising video therefore multiplies the image problem by time: each frame is digitised like a photograph (pixels, colour depth), and the **frame rate** — frames per second — decides how many of them a second of video holds. The arithmetic is sobering: resolution × colour depth × frame rate gives a raw data rate so large that a minute of uncompressed video would swallow a school computer's storage. That size is not a detail; it is the reason the second half of this lesson exists.
**Compression** shrinks video by removing what the viewer will not miss. Inside one frame it squeezes like image compression; between frames it does the video-only trick — most of a frame is the **same as the frame before**, so the file stores the **changes** instead of the whole picture again. **Lossy** compression goes further, discarding fine detail the eye forgives, which is why a streamed match looks softer than the original recording. Egypt's book places video last in its digitisation run — sound, images, then video with compression — because each step reuses the last: samples, pixels, frames, and then the judgement of what may safely be thrown away.
