# Generative AI for Images

## Do it first

Type a sentence, receive a picture. This lesson looks at what the machine is actually doing when that happens — and what the human must still do.

## Sub lesson 1 — From prompt to picture

A **generative-AI image model** has been **trained** on huge collections of images paired with text, learning how descriptions and pictures correspond. Given a **prompt** — a written description — it **generates** a new image that fits: subject, style, colours, composition can all be steered by the words. The image is **new**, not fetched: no such photo existed before, which is both the power and the problem.

## Sub lesson 2 — Directing and judging the result

Good results need **precise prompts** (subject + style + setting + details) and **iteration** — generate, judge, sharpen the prompt, repeat. Judge hard: generators famously fumble **hands, written text, and factual details**, and a convincing image can still be a false one. North Macedonia's 2026 book already teaches this in school: the standing rules are to **credit** machine-made work honestly and to respect **copyright and people's likenesses** — a generated picture is a tool's output, not a photograph of reality.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Generative AI makes new images from a written prompt: a model trained on vast numbers of images and captions produces a picture matching the description — fast and flexible, but the user must direct it with a precise prompt, judge the result critically (hands, text and facts often come out wrong), and respect ownership and honesty about what is machine-made. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: A new image matching the description. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: No — it is new; no such photo existed. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: Precision — subject, style, setting and details. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: Hands and written text (factual details also counts). Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
