Malaysia's own way (Grade 7, ASK Tingkatan 1): the KSSM Asas Sains Komputer (ASK — Foundations of Computer Science) course is built on the four computational-thinking techniques (decomposition, pattern recognition, abstraction, generalisation) introduced through a local food example (making popia basah); programming is done in Scratch 2.0, then learners build a real interactive website in HTML in the same year; every chapter ends with a mind map, self-check list and self-practice.


Kazakhstan's own way (Adekenova & Pryanichnikova G9 Informatics, NIS): the NIS Grade 9 course is built around databases and object-oriented programming: computer architecture with logic circuits, coding raster images and sound, single-table then multi-table databases in Access (forms, queries, reports), five lessons introducing OOP, nine lessons developing applications in an OOP environment (C#), and a thirteen-lesson capstone — choosing a problem, designing the interface, programming, making a video instruction, testing, documenting and evaluating the project.

Kazakhstan's own way (Vyushkova/Zhurbenko/Orazbekov G7 «Информатика» (NIS student book, Russian edition)): the NIS Grade 7 book is organised around data: measuring and representing information (including the von Neumann architecture), business documents, spreadsheets that grow into databases (error checking, sorting/filtering, protecting data from change), raster graphics and logo design, a whole modelling section (modelling games, school life and social problems, forecasting from data), networks and information systems, and linear-structure programming that ends with building your own calculator.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G5 «Информатика»): Grade 5 in the Almatykitap line is a robotics year: after information representation (encryption, binary, binary-coded graphics) and raster/vector graphics, two full sections run LEGO robots — gyro sensor, turns, line following and robo-sumo competitions — before a closing safety section that ends with shared folders and mini-projects. Every term is glossed in three languages (Kazakh/Russian/English) in the margin.

Kazakhstan's own way (Pryanichnikova team G9 «Информатика» NIS): the NIS Grade 9 book is a step up in level: logic expressions, truth tables and logic circuits inside "computer architecture"; sound encoding beside raster encoding; single-table then multi-table databases in Access with forms, queries and reports; and a full object-oriented programming course in C# — components of OOP, multimedia applications, and a software-solution project with a video instruction and a slide show.
