## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Measuring an image — pixels and resolution** — A digital image is a grid of **pixels** — single-colour dots. It is measured by its **dimensions** in pixels (width × height, such as 1920 × 1080) and by its **resolution** — how finely the pixels are packed, counted in pixels per inch (ppi) when printed. The total pixel count (width × height) is the image's size in pixels; a megapixel is a million pixels. Malaysia teaches the units first because every later claim about image quality or file size is built from them.
2. **Measuring audio — sample rate and bit depth** — Digital audio is measured by how often the sound is sampled and how exactly each sample is kept: the **sample rate** counts samples per second in **hertz (Hz)** — CD audio uses 44,100 Hz — and the **bit depth** counts the bits kept per sample. Duration is measured in seconds as usual. Together, sample rate × bit depth × seconds gives the audio's data size — the measuring units of this lesson are the factors of every audio file-size sum that follows.
