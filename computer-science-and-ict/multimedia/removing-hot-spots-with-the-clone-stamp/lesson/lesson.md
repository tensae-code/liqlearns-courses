# Removing hot spots with the Clone Stamp

## Do it first

Flash does a third kind of damage: a shiny white patch on a face or a car where the light bounced back. Before reading on, say how you could cover a shiny patch using clean skin or clean paint from nearby — that is exactly this lesson's tool.

## The Clone Stamp fix — the book's order

Flash also causes shiny **hot spots** on faces, or reflections on shiny surfaces. The worked example is a shiny patch on a car:

1. Open the photo.
2. Duplicate the layer and work on the copy, not the background.
3. Select the **Clone Stamp Tool**.
4. In the Options bar, change Blend Mode from Normal to **Darken** — so only pixels lighter than the sample will change, and hot spots are light pixels.
5. Choose a soft-edged brush of diameter **40–50**.
6. **Alt-click** a clean, non-hot-spot area to set the sample point (the cursor becomes an eyedropper).
7. Paint over the hot spot — the light area gradually darkens.
