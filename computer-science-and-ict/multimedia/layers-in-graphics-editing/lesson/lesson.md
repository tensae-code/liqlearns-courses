# Layers in Graphics Editing

## Do it first

Painting everything on one canvas means one mistake ruins everything beneath it. Layers are how editors keep every part of a picture separately in hand.

## Sub lesson 1 — The stack — sheets of glass

Think of **layers** as **transparent sheets** stacked in order: paint on a sheet shows only where it is painted, and the final image is what you see **looking through the stack**. The **top** layer covers what is below where its paint is opaque; reorder the stack and the picture changes. Each layer can be **shown or hidden** with its eye toggle — the fastest way to test alternatives.

## Sub lesson 2 — Working layer by layer

The working habit: **one element per layer** (background, person, text), **name** layers so the stack stays readable, edit the **active** layer only — most 'the wrong thing changed' accidents are the wrong layer being active. **Opacity** fades a whole layer; **flattening** merges everything into one layer for final export and should be the last step, on a copy, because it cannot be undone into parts.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Layers are transparent sheets stacked over an image, each holding part of the picture — background, objects, text — so each part can be drawn, moved, hidden or deleted without touching the others; the visible picture is the stack seen from above, and flattening merges the stack into one layer for good. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
