## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Message first** — A **prevention video** is an audiovisual message with a protective job: make viewers recognise a danger and know the move that answers it. The scenario's prolongement plans it as message first: the **situation** (a cyberbullying scene viewers recognise), the **turn** (what the victim or a bystander can do), and the **sentence that stays** (the line the video exists to plant). Camera work serves these three; a video with no turn teaches only fear.
2. **Making and showing it** — Making follows the class's audiovisual craft: script the scenes, film simply and clearly, edit to the essential, and check the result against the plan — does a fresh viewer catch the situation, the turn and the sentence? Showing is part of prevention: the video is presented to its real audience (the class, the school), and its makers answer questions. A prevention video succeeds in the audience's memory a week later, not in the filming.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — A warning that nobody watches protects nobody. Your class must make ninety seconds that pupils will actually watch to the end — about cyberbullying. Before reading on: what is the one sentence your video exists to plant? (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of message first as the lesson describes it — the student studies the picture and names what each part shows.
