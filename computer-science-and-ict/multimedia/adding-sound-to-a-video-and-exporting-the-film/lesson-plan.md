# Lesson plan — Adding Sound to a Video and Exporting the Film

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Multimedia · **Lesson:** Adding Sound to a Video and Exporting the Film
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Saudi Arabia (2026-11-14) — in the title Multimedia on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Saudi Arabia's Grade 8 teacher guide, because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson. This lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence only (Saudi Arabia), named in lesson/sources.md, counted nowhere.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A silent film is finished by its sound: effects and music are added on their own tracks, the audio is edited with filters and its volume balanced — and then the whole project is exported as a single .mp4 file that can be shared and played anywhere.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the Saudi book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Adding and editing the sound** — **Sound effects** and **music** are added to the video on their own audio tracks, placed where the pictures need them: an effect lands on its moment, music runs under a scene. The audio itself is then **edited**: **filters** shape the sound, and the **volume** is balanced so speech is heard clearly and music supports instead of drowning it.
2. **Exporting the film** — Editing works on a **project**; the audience needs a **film**. **Exporting** renders the whole project — pictures, text, transitions and sound together — into one finished file. The guide exports as **.mp4 for sharing**: a single file that plays on other computers and phones, ready to present and send.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
