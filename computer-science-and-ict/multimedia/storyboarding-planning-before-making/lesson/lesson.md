# Storyboarding — planning before making

## Do it first

Before reading on, plan a 30-second video in your head — teaching a small child the letter A. How many scenes would it have? What would each scene show? Hold your answer; this lesson gives the tool filmmakers use for exactly this.

## Sub lesson 1 — The storyboard — a story told scene by scene

Before producing a multimedia product — an animation, a film, a movie, a book — a planning strategy is used: a **storyboard**.

A storyboard is a graphic layout sequencing illustrations and images to tell a story visually, **scene by scene** — the way filmmakers move ideas from mind to screen.

Storyboarding also delineates the program's navigation scheme — how the user moves through the product. A storyboard can include video, sketch, text, audio, photos and physical prototypes for pre-visualisation. It is made in software, or as a rough paper-and-pencil sketch.

The book's practical: prepare a storyboard for a video teaching the English alphabet to Ethiopian kindergarten students — on a chart board, in PowerPoint, or on A3 paper.

## Sub lesson 2 — Planning a production in six steps (teacher guide — evidence)

The full production-planning sequence runs in six steps:

1. Define the goals and objectives of the proposed title.
2. Describe its content.
3. Develop the application script.
4. Translate the script into an outline.
5. Translate the outline into a logic flow chart.
6. Develop the storyboard.

Beyond paper and PowerPoint, the named storyboarding software alternatives are Storyboarder, StudioBinder, FrameForge Storyboard Studio, Moviestorm, PowerProduction Software, Storyboard Pro and Storyboard Composer — with phone software as the no-lab fallback.
