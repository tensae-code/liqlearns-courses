Nothing in this lesson claims a page the placements do not give; the lesson's content is the placements' content, re-expressed for the learner, with the book's own step orders, examples and Ethiopian practicals kept. Procedures are taught exactly as the book teaches them — Photoshop first, with the GIMP alternative where the book notes it.

Saudi Arabia's own way (Grade 8, Digital Skills): the Binary Logic series is taught in fixed periods (33 periods this term) with the student book mirrored by this guide — unit/lesson description, learning outcomes, "important points", lesson steps, worked answers and group unit projects, with all practice files delivered through the national Ain enrichment platform (ien.edu.sa). The term is three applied units: video production in Shotcut, charts in Excel, and programming a virtual robot in VEXcode VR where pupils read their own blocks as Python. (teacher guide — evidence)

Malaysia's own way (Grade 7, ASK Tingkatan 1): the KSSM Asas Sains Komputer (ASK — Foundations of Computer Science) course is built on the four computational-thinking techniques (decomposition, pattern recognition, abstraction, generalisation) introduced through a local food example (making popia basah); programming is done in Scratch 2.0, then learners build a real interactive website in HTML in the same year; every chapter ends with a mind map, self-check list and self-practice.

Bangladesh's own way (Grade 6, Digital Technology): the Competency-based National Curriculum 2022 book is organised as nine project "Learning Experiences" done in groups through stories, games and fieldwork — no conventional chapters; even learners without a computer at home learn technology through experience around school and home. The NCTB Digital Technology line runs in parallel English and Bangla editions; placements are cited from the English Version with the Bangla Version parallel spot-checked.
