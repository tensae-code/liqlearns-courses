# Video script — Animated GIFs — Timing and Moving Text

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

An animated GIF is a row of still frames shown one after another; the **delay** set on each frame — in hundredths of a second — is its clock. **Short delays** make fast, smooth motion; **long delays** make steps and pauses. Delays need not be equal: a moving object may rush, then **hold** on one frame to let the eye rest. Vietnam's specialist lesson teaches timing as a design choice, set frame by frame in the editor, then previewed and re-set — animation is judged by watching, not by numbers alone.
Text joins the animation by **changing position frame by frame**: the same text layer is copied across frames, shifted a little each time, so the words appear to **glide** — entering from an edge, crossing, or rising. The craft rules are a animator's: move in **small equal steps** for smoothness, keep the text **readable** (it must stay long enough to be read — its frames get longer delays), and let the text's motion serve the message, not fight it.
