# Lesson plan — Making an Animated Film

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Multimedia · **Lesson:** Making an Animated Film
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Vietnam (2026-11-14) — in the title Multimedia on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Vietnam's Tin học books (Vietnam Grade 11 Tin học 11 Tin học ứng dụng, Vietnam Grade 11 Chuyên đề Tin học 11 Ứng dụng), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree and live lesson content were checked for the slug and for near matches before building.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** making an animated film — producing a cartoon from its script and characters through animated scenes and sound, to the finished film and its release (Vietnam Grade 11, Applied and specialist).
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Production — scenes built from the plan** — The animated film is **produced** from its plan: the script and storyboard name the **scenes**, the model sheets fix the characters, and the software's timeline receives them scene by scene. Each scene is built the animator's way — background layer, character layers, the frame-by-frame changes — and **played back at once**, while fixing is cheap. Vietnam teaches this production twice over: in the Applied Grade 11 book as a film-making practical, and in the specialist volume as the cartoon craft's centre.
2. **Sound, export and release — the film leaves the workshop** — A film is finished in its last crafts: **sound** is added — voices, music and effects laid under the scenes they belong to; the project is **exported** as one video file any player can show; and the film is **released** — presented to its audience, in the specialist book with a premiere for the class ('Releasing my animated film'). Export and release are part of the lesson, not an afterthought: a film that cannot be shown is a workshop exercise, not a film.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
