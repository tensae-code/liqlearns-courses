# Lesson plan — Where multimedia is used — five fields

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Multimedia · **Lesson:** Where multimedia is used — five fields
**Place in the curriculum:** Ethiopia Grade 10, Unit 4 — Image Processing and Multimedia (printed pp. 139–181).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** multimedia works in five fields — education, entertainment, public places, collaboration and healthcare — and each field uses it in its own way.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on What multimedia is (the grown opening lesson of this title): the learner knows the five media forms; this lesson shows where the combination is put to work.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds, looping.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: What multimedia is — the five forms and why the combination matters. Recommended, never forced. A learner can land here directly; the lesson stands alone.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the five fields where multimedia is used.
2. Give one book example for each field.
3. Explain virtual learning, the virtual meeting, telemedicine and virtual surgery.
## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes virtual learning with any video → virtual learning needs teacher and learners separated by place or time, learning together online.
- Puts the virtual meeting under education → a meeting's job is people working together across regions; that is collaboration, even when the topic is schoolwork.
