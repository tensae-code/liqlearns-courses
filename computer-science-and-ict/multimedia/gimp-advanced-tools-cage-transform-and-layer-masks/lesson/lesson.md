# GIMP — Advanced Tools: Cage Transform and Layer Masks

## Do it first

For pupils who finish the GIMP basics hungry, Kuwait's enrichment projects open the advanced shelf. Three of its tools are taught by name. This lesson is that shelf.

## Sub lesson 1 — Cage Transform — bending an image inside a cage

The **Cage Transform** tool wraps a part of the image in a **cage** — a frame of points the maker drags — and the image inside **bends to follow the cage**: a flag re-draped, a label wrapped around a curve, a character's pose adjusted. It is transformation beyond move, scale and rotate: the picture's own fabric is reshaped, which is why it is an enrichment tool — powerful exactly where it can also tear a design's believability.

## Sub lesson 2 — Layer Masks, Curves and Channels — hiding without deleting, light by numbers

A **layer Mask** removes a background the reversible way: the mask **hides** parts of the layer (painted black) and **shows** them (painted white) — nothing is deleted, so a cut can be refined or undone at any moment; the enrichment uses it for background removal finer than any eraser. **Curves** adjust an image's light by a drawn graph — shadows, midtones and highlights pulled separately — and **Channels** are the colour system's separate plates (the RGB plates of the foundations lesson), worked on one at a time. Three tools, one theme: control that does not destroy.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: Wraps part of an image in a draggable cage; the image bends to follow it. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: Re-draping a flag or wrapping a label around a curve (any sensible use). Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: It reshapes the picture's fabric — powerful where it can also tear believability. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: Hides (black) and shows (white) parts of a layer — without deleting anything. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
