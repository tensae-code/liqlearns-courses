## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Framing — what the edges choose** — **Framing (cadrage)** is the photographer's first decision: what is inside the picture and what is cut away. Close framing fills the picture with the animal's face — you meet its eyes and feel with it; wide framing shrinks it in a large world — you feel its smallness. The Parcours des bestioles starts here because a picture's emotion is built, not found: the same creature, framed differently, tells two different truths.
2. **Colour and light — the feeling's steering wheel** — **Colour** steers feeling: warm, saturated colours excite and cheer; cold, grey ones quieten or sadden. **Light** steers it too: soft light soothes, hard contrast dramatises, a face lit from below turns strange. Reading a picture means naming these choices before surrendering to the feeling — the animal has not changed; the steering has. A reader who can name the steering can also use it, and resist it.

1. **Do it first** — Two photos of the same animal: one makes you laugh, one makes your heart squeeze. Same animal. Before reading on, find what the photographer changed — distance, colour, light — to change your feeling. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of framing — what the edges choose as the lesson describes it — the student studies the picture and names what each part shows.
