# Video script — Editing Photos in Paint.NET — Red Eye and Brightness

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

**Red eye** is the flash photograph's famous fault: eyes staring red where the flash lit the blood of the eye itself. Paint.NET's correction is applied where the fault sits — the eye's area is selected and the red is taken out, restoring the eye's own colour, the rest of the face untouched. The lesson's discipline is the editor's standing one: correct the fault at its place, on a copy or a layer, checking at true size — a repair that shows is a second fault.
A photograph's light is corrected in steps of strength. **Brightness** lifts or lowers the whole picture's light at once; **contrast** deepens or softens the distance between its lights and darks. **Levels** is the finer instrument: it remaps the picture's tones — where its blacks begin, where its whites peak, the balance between — rescuing photographs that brightness alone would wash or bury. Mongolia's order is the professional's: judge the fault first (too dark? too flat? too harsh?), then choose the smallest instrument that reaches it.
