## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Red eye — the flash's fault, removed** — **Red eye** is the flash photograph's famous fault: eyes staring red where the flash lit the blood of the eye itself. Paint.NET's correction is applied where the fault sits — the eye's area is selected and the red is taken out, restoring the eye's own colour, the rest of the face untouched. The lesson's discipline is the editor's standing one: correct the fault at its place, on a copy or a layer, checking at true size — a repair that shows is a second fault.
2. **Brightness, contrast and Levels — the light corrected** — A photograph's light is corrected in steps of strength. **Brightness** lifts or lowers the whole picture's light at once; **contrast** deepens or softens the distance between its lights and darks. **Levels** is the finer instrument: it remaps the picture's tones — where its blacks begin, where its whites peak, the balance between — rescuing photographs that brightness alone would wash or bury. Mongolia's order is the professional's: judge the fault first (too dark? too flat? too harsh?), then choose the smallest instrument that reaches it.
