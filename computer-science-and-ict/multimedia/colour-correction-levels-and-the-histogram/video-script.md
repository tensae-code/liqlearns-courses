Today: **Colour correction — Levels and the histogram**.

First, what we are doing: Levels corrects colour by adjusting shadows, highlights and midtones against the histogram: pull the white slider to the data's right edge, the black slider only if shadows are too bright, and compare by toggling the adjustment layer.

Colour correction fixes colours that make an image look unnatural — lost colour, faded skin. Every image mixes shadows (the darkest parts), highlights (the brightest) and midtones (everything between). Levels adjusts these tones and is much more powerful than brightness/contrast.

1. Open the photo in Photoshop.
2. Add a Levels adjustment layer from the Layers panel — non-destructive: it changes nothing in the original and can be re-tweaked. (In GIMP, Levels is under the Colour menu.)
3. Read the histogram in the Properties panel — a gap with no information on the right means the image is underexposed.
4. Drag the white Input Levels slider left to the histogram's right-most edge. (Dragging past it loses detail — clipping.)
5. Drag the black slider right only if the shadows are too bright.
6. Optionally move the grey midtone slider — left brightens.
7. Toggle the adjustment layer off to compare with the original.
