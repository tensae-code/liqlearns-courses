# Effects for Animated Images

## Do it first

creating effects for animated images — fades, blurs, glows and transitions applied across a GIF's frames so the effect itself moves (Vietnam Grade 11 specialist, Applied)

## Sub lesson 1 — An effect is a change spread over frames

A still-image effect changes one picture; an **animated effect** changes a picture **gradually, frame by frame**. A **fade** raises or lowers opacity step by step until a thing appears or vanishes. A **blur** softens more and more, as if the eye lost focus. A **glow** grows a bright edge around an object. The method is always the same: choose the **first state** and the **last state**, and build the in-between frames in **equal steps** — the eye reads the steps as one smooth change.

In your mind's eye: an effect is a change spread over frames.

## Sub lesson 2 — Using effects with restraint — timing and purpose

Effects obey the animation's other laws: their **timing** is set by frame delays (a slow fade gets many frames, a flash gets few), and their **purpose** must be a story purpose — the glow marks the magic object, the fade ends the scene. Vietnam's specialist lesson warns by example: an animation where everything glows, blurs and flashes has no effects at all, because the eye stops believing any of them. **One effect, well placed, beats five competing.**

In your mind's eye: using effects with restraint — timing and purpose.
