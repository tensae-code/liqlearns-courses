**Acceptance count: taught in 13 countries (Ethiopia, Yemen, North Macedonia, Cuba, Fiji, Bhutan, Libya, Sri Lanka, Rwanda, Laos, Greece, Moldova, Bangladesh).**

Yemen's own way (Grade 11): the book pivots from operating the machine to understanding information itself — data kinds, number systems and coding get full units before the applications (presentations, Excel) and a networks/Internet finale; every unit closes with a formal تقويم (assessment).

Nothing in this lesson claims a page the placements do not give; the lesson's content is the placements' content, re-expressed for the learner, with the book's own step orders, examples and Ethiopian practicals kept. Procedures are taught exactly as the book teaches them — Photoshop first, with the GIMP alternative where the book notes it.

Grown 2026-11-12 with the Grade 10 Unit 4 opening (printed pp. 139–140): the Grade 10 book teaches the same combination one level deeper — impact, retention, the human interface — and this lesson grew by those parts rather than being duplicated. Book error taught corrected: the Unit 4 overview (printed p. 139) prints "contract some useful information" for "extract" — the word is taught as **extract** wherever the overview's content is taught (the image-processing meaning: to extract useful information from an image), and the slip is named here, exactly as the placements flag it.

North Macedonia's own way (Year 2): a strikingly current first theme (cybersecurity, cryptography, blockchain, robotics, AI/ML and large language models) precedes deep C++ (data representation, functions, one-dimensional arrays), a full databases theme ending in SQL, multimedia with generative AI for graphics, and HTML web authoring.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Grade 9): one TIA teacher guide carries two ICT subjects side by side — Communication Technology (networks, ergonomics, safety, societal impact) and Computer Technology (architecture, software) — organised by content standards and numbered benchmarks (9.4.x / 9.5.x), with the design brief used as the problem-solving method for both a product and a simple network, and typing assessed at 20 wpm with 80% accuracy. (teacher guide — evidence, PARTIAL)

Papua New Guinea's own way (Grade 11): the senior guide turns learners into junior system administrators and builders — computer administration, datasheets ("data books") and timing technology, documenting server and printer configurations, data science, pseudocode, Green IT, and building static web pages with tools like Canva and Figma; typing rises to 40 wpm at 85–100% accuracy. (teacher guide — evidence)

Cuba's own way (Grade 7): Secondary Informática opens by contrasting proprietary Windows with Cuba's free operating system Nova; programming continues the primary Scratch line, here centred on the Scratch pen tool for drawing geometric figures and the linear function; word-processor work ends by exporting documents to HTML for the web.

Fiji's own way (Years 7–8 Computer Education student book): A practical, exercise-driven primary book built around a class "students folder" of set files (work is saved as U<unit>-Ex<n>-<FirstName>); local Fijian names and schools throughout (e.g. Babasiga Secondary School), Internet searching is practised on the "Map of Fiji" with kids' search engines, and a full unit covers computers in Fijian society including Braille keyboards for blind learners. PARTIAL: the PDF as received has no cover, title page, contents or imprint — the PARTIAL label travels with every citation of this book.

Bhutan's own way (Class VII, Literacy with ICT student book): Social media is taught hands-on — learners create a Facebook account, post with audience settings, and follow netiquette and an Acceptable Use Policy; backup is taught to Google Drive, and the end-of-year experience is shared on Facebook.

Libya's own way (Grade 12 Humanities, Information Technology student book): the Humanities final year pairs the social/legal side of ICT (data security, e-government, computer law and software licences) with a long multimedia-and-PowerPoint practical; the closing projects are Libyan — a deck about Libyan dishes and a deck about Libya's archaeological cities and landmarks.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 11, Tamil): a full Visual Basic 6.0 programming course inside the O/L book (IDE, controls, data types, Select Case, arrays), an SDLC chapter, Photoshop-based multimedia, hand-coded HTML with Sri Lankan examples (the Sinharaja rainforest page, ucsc.cmb.ac.lk addresses, sltnet.lk domains), and sector chapters (agriculture, industry, government, commerce) before malware and health.

Rwanda's own way (S3 ICT Learner's Book, Fountain): the same ten units as the S3 guide taught as long screenshot-by-screenshot practicals rooted in Rwandan school life (a GS Bumbogo parents'-meeting mail merge, an administration map of Rwanda built in ArcGIS, an environmental-protection presentation, 'Rwanda Nziza' hyperlink decks), closing with a complete Scratch platform game, Hard Journey.

Rwanda's own way (S3 ICT Teacher Guide, Fountain): the S3 guide (a different publisher from the KLB S1/S2 set) organises the year into four study areas — application software, GIS/spreadsheets/presentations, graphics and multimedia, networks and programming — ending in platform game programming in Scratch. (teacher guide — evidence)

Laos's own way (Grade 6 ICT student book): a theory-only foundation year — no hands-on computer practicals at all; ICT is introduced through Lao daily-life examples (TV remote, household appliances) and every chapter opens with objectives, guiding questions and a discussion activity (ກິດຈະກໍາ) before the exposition. The RIES ICT line runs ມ.1–ມ.7 (Grades 6–12).

Greece's own way (Grade 10, Efarmoges Pliroforikis A' Lykeiou): the older Lykeio applications book in four thematic units — hardware/software and applications, programming environments and creating applications (life cycle, modern IDEs, mobile-app and 3D object-oriented programming), communication and the internet (HTML5/CSS, learning online), and collaboration and safety online (cloud applications, telework, social networks, privacy).

Moldova's own way (Grade 7, Informatică): lower-secondary Informatica is a real textbook discipline, not computer-skills practice — number systems, text/image/sound encoding and data types in Pascal sit alongside GUI literacy, and two full chapters are given to behaviour, law and information culture in virtual spaces.

Bangladesh's own way (Grade 6, Digital Technology): the Competency-based National Curriculum 2022 book is organised as nine project "Learning Experiences" done in groups through stories, games and fieldwork — no conventional chapters; even learners without a computer at home learn technology through experience around school and home. The NCTB Digital Technology line runs in parallel English and Bangla editions; placements are cited from the English Version with the Bangla Version parallel spot-checked.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 7 G7 teacher): SERIES MISMATCH NOTED — this guide (Velijanyan & Aslanyan) is the teacher guide for the OTHER Grade 7 series, whose student book (§1 Electronic security … §23 Computer hardware maintenance, Python-based) is NOT among the records; it does not match the Topchyan & Melkonyan Grade 7 student book filed as Book 12. The guide is complete in itself: 20 themes, a thematic plan, the student book's questions/tasks with answers for §1–§23, worked Python solutions, and assessment rubrics.
