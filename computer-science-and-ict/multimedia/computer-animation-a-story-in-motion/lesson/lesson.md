# Computer Animation — a Story in Motion

## Do it first

Flip a notebook with a small drawing in the corner of each page, changed a little page by page. The drawing seems to move. Animation is that trick, done by the computer with scenes and costumes.

## Computer Animation — a Story in Motion

Grade 3 closes its Scratch story work with **computer animation (Компютърна анимация)**: making a story move on the screen. The book's example is the story **"The lost teddy bear"** — a small tale told as a row of moments: backdrop changes carry the story from place to place, costume switches move the characters' bodies, say blocks carry the words, and movement blocks carry the action, all **in story order**. Animation here is not a new block; it is all the earlier skills aimed at one thing — a story an audience can follow from its first scene to its last. The craft is order and small steps: each moment changes a little from the one before, so the eye reads the row as one living story.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Computer animation makes a story move: a row of scenes and costume changes, each a little different, played in order — as in the book's story "The lost teddy bear". Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: say what computer animation is, in your own words. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: build a short animated story as a row of moments in order. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: use backdrop changes, costume switches and movement to carry a story. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
