# Reading an Audiovisual Message — Point of View

## Do it first

A schoolyard quarrel, filmed twice: once over one child's shoulder, once over the other's. Same quarrel, two different villains. Before reading on, say why the camera's place is an opinion.

## Sub lesson 1 — The elements of the moving image

An audiovisual message is built from elements a reader can name: the **image** (framing, angle, movement), the **sound** (words, music, silence), the **editing** (what follows what, what is cut), and the **text** it carries (titles, captions). The activity's analysis takes a message apart element by element, because a message you can dismantle is a message you can judge — nothing in it is “just there”; every element was placed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Point of view — whose eyes?

Every story is told from a **point of view (point de vue)**: the camera stands somewhere, the narration speaks as someone, and the viewer is lent those eyes. Change the point of view and sympathies move — the activity uses this on a message about bullying between pupils, where whose side the camera takes decides who the audience blames. The reader's standing question: whose eyes am I watching through, and what would this look like from the other side?
