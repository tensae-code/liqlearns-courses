**Acceptance count: taught in 3 countries (North Macedonia, Mongolia, Kazakhstan).**

Placement choice: this lesson was built new for the ICT expansion (North Macedonia, 2026-11-14) because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree was checked for the slug and for near matches before building. The placements file named this slug directly (NEW LESSON NEEDED — multimedia/vector-and-raster-graphics). It sits in the Multimedia title (multimedia/), following the book's own order.

North Macedonia's own way (Year 2): a strikingly current first theme (cybersecurity, cryptography, blockchain, robotics, AI/ML and large language models) precedes deep C++ (data representation, functions, one-dimensional arrays), a full databases theme ending in SQL, multimedia with generative AI for graphics, and HTML web authoring.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Grade 12): the final year is design-and-build — simulation models, a seven-method technological problem-solving process, biotechnology with a DNA-technology case study, CAD, the software development process, IT career pathways, and GUI work with client-side scripting and interactive menus. (teacher guide — evidence)

Mongolia's own way (Grade 9): Grade 9 turns outward to society and real tools — the information society taught through Mongolia's own shift, open-source software through Soyombo Linux (Mongolia's own Linux) and Mongolian OpenOffice, copyright through Mongolia's 2006 copyright law; algorithms are drawn as flowcharts and run in Turtle Art; the long spreadsheet unit grades pupils with nested IFs, builds Fibonacci sequences, grows a 500,000-tögrög bank deposit, and charts the depth and area of Mongolia's great lakes (Khuvsgul, Uvs, Khar-Us); e-services are the real Mongolian ones — the ТҮЦ service kiosk, bookstore.mn and internet banking.


Kazakhstan's own way (Mukametzhanova G6 «Информатика»): the whole second half of Grade 6 is one long game-development arc — idea, scenario, drawing scenes and characters (vector editor), realising the scenario, sound, a title screen, improving the project — and then documenting the game properly with footnotes, hyperlinks and an automatically built table of contents.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G5 «Информатика»): Grade 5 in the Almatykitap line is a robotics year: after information representation (encryption, binary, binary-coded graphics) and raster/vector graphics, two full sections run LEGO robots — gyro sensor, turns, line following and robo-sumo competitions — before a closing safety section that ends with shared folders and mini-projects. Every term is glossed in three languages (Kazakh/Russian/English) in the margin.

Kazakhstan's own way (Salgaraeva G6 «Информатика»): the Arman-PV Grade 6 book is a game-making year wrapped in document skills: ergonomics and wireless networks first, then binary coding and encryption, vector graphics (drawing a «Жайлау» summer pasture), a Scratch game designed, built and given a sound track and title screen — and the year ends with footnotes, hyperlinks, copyright and plagiarism, a table of contents and the written documentation for the game.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukametzhanova G5 «Информатика» 2020 edition): the 2020 Atamura revision of the Grade 5 book is rebuilt on the renewed programme — the same five sections as the Almatykitap book (Book 18): presenting information with encryption and binary, raster and vector graphics with curves, robotics with the gyroscope sensor and turns, robot competitions (line following and robo-sumo), and computer safety moved to the end.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kobdikova G5 «Информатика»): the Arman-PV Grade 5 book is the practicum twin of the same programme as Books 18 and 35 — nearly every unit comes in a theory + practice pair, and it adds a block the others lack: sharing files (placing, changing and downloading shared files), setting a password on documents, and organising a document effectively for print, taught as "mini-project works".

Kazakhstan's own way (Express Publishing G10 «ICT»): alongside the national publishers, Kazakhstan's English-medium Grade 10 classes use this imported Express Publishing ICT course — ten self-contained modules that include two topics the national books do not teach at this level: expert and control systems, and developing a mobile app.
