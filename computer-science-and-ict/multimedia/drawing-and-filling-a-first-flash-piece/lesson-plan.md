# Lesson plan — Drawing and filling a first Flash piece

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Multimedia · **Lesson:** Drawing and filling a first Flash piece
**Place in the curriculum:** Ethiopia Grade 10, Unit 4 — Image Processing and Multimedia (printed pp. 139–181).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a first Flash piece is drawn with the Rectangle Tool and filled with the Paint Bucket Tool and a colour from Color Swatches — in that order — then saved with File → Save or Ctrl+S.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Authoring tools — naming the Flash screen: the learner can find the toolbox, the work area and the menus this lesson clicks.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds, looping.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Authoring tools — naming the Flash screen. Recommended, never forced. A learner can land here directly; the lesson stands alone.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Start a new Flash project by the book's route.
2. Draw a rectangle and fill it in the book's exact order.
3. Save the piece by File → Save or Ctrl+S.
## What you need
A computer with Flash for the real doing; paper and pencil for the order work.

## If the learner is stuck
- Picks the colour before drawing → the book's order is tool, draw, bucket, pick colour, click inside. The swatch is picked at the bucket step.
- Clicks outside the rectangle → the fill floods the area clicked; the click must land inside the outline.
