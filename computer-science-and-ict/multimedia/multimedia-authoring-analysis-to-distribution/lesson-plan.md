# Lesson plan — Multimedia authoring — analysis to distribution

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Multimedia · **Lesson:** Multimedia authoring — analysis to distribution
**Place in the curriculum:** Ethiopia Grade 10, Unit 4 — Image Processing and Multimedia (printed pp. 139–181).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** authoring joins media contents into a single stream of information through five stages in order: Analysis, Design, Development, Evaluation, Distribution.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Multimedia production — the eight roles: authoring is the road that team walks, stage by stage.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds, looping.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Analysis — decide what to do** — decide what to do and what to use.
2. **Design — storyboard the story** — storyboard the project's story.
3. **Development — build the prototype** — incorporate the data and set it up as a prototype.
4. **Evaluation — test and fine-tune** — test the working prototype, fine-tune, make it attractive, review.
5. **Distribution — package and hand out** — package the finished work and distribute it.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Multimedia production — the eight roles — who does the work at each stage. Recommended, never forced. A learner can land here directly; the lesson stands alone.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define authoring.
2. Say the five stages in order with each stage's job.
3. Apply the five stages to a small project of their own.
