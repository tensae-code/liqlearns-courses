# Multimedia authoring — analysis to distribution

## Do it first

Before reading on, imagine your class must produce a short multimedia lesson for younger students. Write what you would do first, next, and last. This lesson gives the five stages every such project walks.

## Sub lesson 1 — Analysis — decide what to do

**Authoring** assembles different media contents — text, audio, image, animation, video — into a **single stream of information** using software tools. Its first stage is **Analysis**: decide **what to do** and **what to use**. A project that skips analysis builds the wrong thing beautifully.

## Sub lesson 2 — Design — storyboard the story

The second stage is **Design**: **storyboard the project's story**. The storyboard fixes scene by scene what the learner will see and hear, before any building starts — the same planning tool the storyboarding lesson of this title teaches in full.

## Sub lesson 3 — Development — build the prototype

The third stage is **Development**: **incorporate the data** and set it up as a **prototype** — a working first version of the whole project, not a finished polish of one corner.

## Sub lesson 4 — Evaluation — test and fine-tune

The fourth stage is **Evaluation**: **test** the working prototype, **fine-tune** it, make it **attractive**, and **review** it. Evaluation is where the audience's eyes enter the project: what only the makers understood gets fixed here.

## Sub lesson 5 — Distribution — package and hand out

The fifth stage is **Distribution**: **package** the finished work and **distribute** it. A project that is never packaged and handed out has not finished — the stream of information must reach its learners.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: keep the prototype from Development even after Distribution. When a user reports a fault, the prototype is where you test the fix first.

## Say it to your companion

Say the five stages in order to your companion, with each stage's job in three words or fewer. Your companion stops you if the order slips.

## Do It Yourself

1. Put the stages in order from a shuffled list, then write each stage's job beside it.
2. For the class lesson you imagined in Do it first, write one line per stage saying what your class would actually do.
3. Say why Evaluation comes before Distribution, not after.
