# Lesson plan — Adding a Sound to a Scratch Script

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Multimedia · **Lesson:** Adding a Sound to a Scratch Script
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Saudi Arabia (2026-11-14) — in the title Multimedia on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Saudi Arabia's Grade 4 teacher guide, because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson. This lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence only (Saudi Arabia), named in lesson/sources.md, counted nowhere.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A Scratch program can be heard as well as seen: the Sounds panel holds the project's sound clips — a bird sound and its fellows — and sound blocks play them from the script, with effects to try on the voice of the program.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the Saudi book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The Sounds panel — the project's clips** — Every Scratch project has a **Sounds panel (tab)** where its **sound clips** live. A project starts with its default clip; **adding a sound** brings a new clip — the guide adds the **bird sound** — into the panel, ready for the script to play. Clips in the panel belong to the sprite whose panel it is.
2. **Playing sounds from the script — and effects** — **Sound blocks** in the script **play the clips**: put a play block where the story needs the sound — the bird sings when it appears. The panel also lets you **try sound effects** on a clip, changing how it sounds before the audience hears it. Sound is placed like every block: deliberately, on its moment.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
