# Lesson plan — Preparing Images for the Web

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Multimedia · **Lesson:** Preparing Images for the Web
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Belarus (2026-11-14) — in the title Multimedia on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Belarus's *Інфарматыка* Grade 11, because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** An image for the web must be prepared before it is published: sized to the place it will fill, saved in a web-suitable format, and light enough to load quickly — a photograph straight from a camera is almost always too big for its page.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the Belarus book's own order — it follows the book's sections in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Size and weight** — A camera photograph is made to be printed and kept; a web page needs it **no bigger than the place it fills**. Preparation starts with **size**: shrink the image's dimensions to the display size — a picture shown small but stored huge wastes loading time on detail nobody sees. Then **weight**: an image file's size in bytes decides how long a visitor waits. Preparation is the trade between looking right and loading fast, and for the web the balance always leans to the visitor's wait.
2. **Choosing the format** — The saving **format** is the second decision. Photographs, with their millions of shades, belong in a format made for photographs, which squeezes them by dropping detail the eye barely misses. Drawings, logos and screenshots with flat colours and sharp edges belong in a format that keeps edges exact, even at few colours. Saving in the wrong format shows at once: a photograph bloated far beyond its need, or a sharp diagram gone fuzzy. The finished preparation is checked on the page itself: right size on screen, small weight in the folder, and nothing the eye needed lost.
