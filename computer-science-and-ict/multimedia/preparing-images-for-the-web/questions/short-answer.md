# Preparing Images for the Web — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What must happen to an image before it is published on the web?**
**Answer: It must be prepared — sized, formatted, and made light enough to load quickly**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What size should a web image be stored at?**
**Answer: No bigger than the place it will fill on the page**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What is an image's weight?**
**Answer: Its file size in bytes, which decides how long a visitor waits**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What is preparation a trade between?**
**Answer: Looking right and loading fast**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) Which way does the balance lean for the web?**
**Answer: Toward the visitor's wait — loading fast**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What format suits photographs?**
**Answer: One made for photographs, which squeezes by dropping detail the eye barely misses**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What format suits logos and sharp diagrams?**
**Answer: One that keeps edges exact, even at few colours**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What does the wrong format show as?**
**Answer: A bloated photograph, or a sharp diagram gone fuzzy**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) Where is finished preparation checked?**
**Answer: On the page itself — size on screen, weight in the folder, nothing needed lost**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) Why is a camera photo too big for its page?**
**Answer: It is made for printing and keeping, far beyond display size and weight**
