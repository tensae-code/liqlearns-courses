# Lesson plan — Adding Sounds and a Music Loop in Scratch

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Multimedia · **Lesson:** Adding Sounds and a Music Loop in Scratch
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Finland (2026-11-14) — in the title Multimedia on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Finland's *Ensiaskeleet ohjelmointiin* (student book, Oppilaan kirja, Versio 2.0), because no existing lesson teaches this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Scratch projects get sound from the sound library, and the two play blocks behave differently: play sound starts the sound and the script moves on, while play sound until done makes the script wait until the sound ends — and a loop of the second kind makes background music that never stops.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on building Scratch scripts; every sound block and the difference between the two play blocks are built inside the lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Add a sound to a project from the sound library.
2. Say the difference between play sound and play sound until done, and choose the right one.
3. Make background music loop through a whole project.

## What you need
A computer with Scratch and its sound turned on.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Your story is silent. A door should creak, a character should cheer, and music should play under the whole story. Scratch keeps sounds ready for you — but two of its sound blocks do different jobs, and picking the wrong one either cuts the story's speech or jams the action. (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
Speech is cut off by the next action → the script used play sound; switch to play sound until done. Project seems frozen → a long sound with until done waits a long time; use play sound for effects. Music stops mid-project → it is not in a loop, or its script ended.
