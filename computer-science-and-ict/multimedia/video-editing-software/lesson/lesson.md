# Video-Editing Software

## Do it first

A video is processed in a video editor, and the citizen book names its benches honestly: one professional and dear, one free and present. This short lesson is the comparison the next lesson's practice stands on.

## Sub lesson 1 — The editor's workplace — what any video editor must do

A **video editor** is the program where raw clips become a finished film: clips are **imported** to its shelf, **cut and joined** on its timeline, given **titles, transitions and sound**, and **exported** as one file in a chosen format. Whatever the editor's price, those four rooms — shelf, timeline, dressing, export — are the workplace, and a pupil who can name them can walk into any editor, including ones not yet written, and find where each job lives.

## Sub lesson 2 — Premiere and MovieMaker — the two benches

The book's pair spans the world's range. **Premiere** is the professional editor: many tracks, fine control, effects and formats without practical limit — bought, heavy, and taught in studios. **MovieMaker** is the system's own free editor of the book's era: one simple timeline, the household jobs — cut, join, title, narrate, music — and nothing to buy or install. Mongolia's comparison carries no snobbery: the bench is judged by the job — MovieMaker carries the book's own film projects in the next lesson, whole, because the citizen's films fit the free bench, and knowing when the free bench suffices is itself the professional knowledge.
