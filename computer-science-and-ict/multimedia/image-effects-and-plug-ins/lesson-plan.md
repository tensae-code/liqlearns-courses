# Lesson plan — Image Effects and Plug-Ins

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Multimedia · **Lesson:** Image Effects and Plug-Ins
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Mongolia (2026-11-14) — in the title Multimedia on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Mongolia's student books (Ministry of Education, Culture and Science(s) of Mongolia, Ulaanbaatar), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree and live lesson content were checked for the slug and for near matches before building.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Image effects in Paint.NET — the Effects menu at work on real photographs, installing plug-ins that add effects the program does not ship, and the book's 3D text-effect project
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The Effects menu — on photographs now** — Applied to **photographs**, the **Effects menu** changes its meaning: artistic effects turn a photo toward painting for a poster; distortions bend it for a joke or a diagram; the adjustments' neighbours sharpen and soften. The Grade 9 judgement is the photo's purpose again — an effect on a photograph is a claim about it, and the pupil who effects a documentary photo without saying so has crossed the ethics line the course drew before. Effects are chosen, placed on their own layer, and owned.
2. **Plug-ins — extending the editor, and the 3D text project** — A **plug-in** is an added module that gives the editor new powers: **installed** once — the plug-in file placed where Paint.NET reads its extensions and the program restarted — its effects join the menu as citizens. Mongolia's set project spends them on **3D text**: letters given depth, light and shadow by plug-in effects, for the titles photographs alone cannot make. The lesson's standing rule closes both halves: an editor is not a fixed shelf but a growing bench — what it cannot do today, a plug-in may teach it tomorrow, and the pupil is responsible for every tool on the bench.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
