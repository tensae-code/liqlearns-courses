# Creating Pixel Art in Excel

## Do it first

A spreadsheet is a grid — and a grid of coloured cells is a picture waiting to be planned. This lesson turns Excel into a pixel canvas, cell by cell.

## Sub lesson 1 — Pixels in a grid — the cell as a dot

A digital picture is **pixels** — tiny coloured dots in a grid. Excel's grid can play the pixel grid: make the columns **narrow and equal** so cells are square, then **fill cells** with colours, one cell per pixel, following a plan (a small drawing counted out on paper first — so many across, so many down). The picture grows cell by cell: a flag, a flower, a character — the Grade 6 project's charm is that the spreadsheet's most serious grid becomes a drawing board without any new tool at all.

## Sub lesson 2 — Controlling the picture's dimensions

The picture's **dimensions** are controlled deliberately: the **number of cells** across and down sets its size and fineness (more cells — a finer, bigger picture; fewer — a chunkier one), the **cell size** sets its printed size, and the plan's counting keeps the parts in proportion. Jordan's workbook teaches dimensions here because a pixel artist who cannot say how wide their picture is, in cells and in centimetres, is decorating by accident — the project's craft is the counting: plan the grid, fill the grid, check the picture against the plan.
