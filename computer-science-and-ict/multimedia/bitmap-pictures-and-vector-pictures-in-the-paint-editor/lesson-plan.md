# Lesson plan — Bitmap Pictures and Vector Pictures in the Paint Editor

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Multimedia · **Lesson:** Bitmap Pictures and Vector Pictures in the Paint Editor
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Finland (2026-11-14) — in the title Multimedia on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Finland's *Ensiaskeleet ohjelmointiin* (teacher guide, Opettajan opas, Versio 2.0 — teacher notes only), because no existing lesson teaches this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The Scratch paint editor draws in two kinds of picture: vector pictures, built from shapes with reshape points that stay editable and sharp at any size, and bitmap pictures, built from coloured dots — and converting a vector picture to bitmap gives up the editable parts and the sharpness for good. This lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence only.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on using the paint editor's tools; the two picture kinds, reshape points and grouping are built inside the lesson from the teacher guide's notes.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a vector picture is made of, and what a bitmap picture is made of.
2. Say what is lost when a vector picture is converted to bitmap.
3. Move a shape's reshape points to change it.
4. Group shapes so they move as one, and ungroup them again.

## What you need
A computer with Scratch.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Draw a circle. Make it ten times bigger: still perfectly round and smooth. Now take a tiny photo and make it ten times bigger: squares and blur. Your drawing and the photo are not the same kind of picture at all — and Scratch's paint editor works with both kinds. (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
Enlarged picture is blocky → it is a bitmap (or was converted); shapes cannot come back from dots — redraw or return to a kept vector copy. Cannot bend a shape → it may be bitmap, or grouped; ungroup first, then look for reshape points.
