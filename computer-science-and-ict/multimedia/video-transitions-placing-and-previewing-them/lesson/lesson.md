# Video transitions — placing and previewing them

## Do it first

Imagine two scenes: a market, then a classroom. How would the film get from one to the other — a hard jump, a soft fade, a page turning? Before reading on, pick the gentlest and the boldest change. This lesson teaches both, and when to use neither.

## Sub lesson 1 — Transitions and how they sit on clips

The editing work now moves to **Adobe Premiere Elements 9** — opened **Start → Programs → Adobe Premiere Elements 9**. A **transition** phases one clip out and the next in — from a subtle **cross dissolve** to an emphatic **page turn** or **spinning pinwheel** — and can be customised and previewed before applying.

A transition normally sits **on the cut between two clips (double-sided)**, but may sit at one clip's start or end alone (**single-sided**, such as a fade to black). It overlaps frames from both clips, using **frames previously trimmed** from the clips where they exist — trimming never deletes frames; the In and Out points merely frame a window over the original clip — or **repeating frames** where they do not.

## Sub lesson 2 — Choosing and setting a transition

A transition's kind is checked by selecting it and opening **Edit Transition** in the Task panel's Transition view. Selection is **Edit → Transitions** — with the book's craft warning: first-time filmmakers overuse them. **Add a transition only where it makes sense, after editing and before the soundtrack.**

Objects' transform properties — **position, rotation, scale, opacity** — are adjusted by dragging in the **Monitor panel** or from the title menu. Transition position types — **Start, End, Centre** — are set in the Transition properties window. Motion paths can be drawn with the **Line, Oval, Rectangle and Lasso** tools; a zig-zag path is pictured in the book.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: preview the transition at full size, not only in the small panel. A dissolve that looks gentle small can look muddy big.
