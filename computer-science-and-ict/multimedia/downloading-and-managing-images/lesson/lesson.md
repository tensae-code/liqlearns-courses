# Downloading and Managing Images

## Do it first

Your computer fills with pictures named like codes and scattered everywhere. This lesson gives images a proper arrival: a checked source, a chosen folder, a clear name.

## Sub lesson 1 — Downloading an image

To **download an image**, find it on a page you may rightly use, right-click it and choose **Save image as**, then choose the folder and name before saving. The image's **format** and size are shown as you save: a photograph arrives as JPEG, a diagram often as PNG. An image found online belongs to its maker — for school work you may use it with the source named; for anything public, permission is the rule.

## Sub lesson 2 — Managing your images

**Managing** images means they can be found again: keep them in folders by subject or project, name them for their content — `tej-market-stall.jpg`, not `IMG_2041.jpg` — and sort by date or name when a folder grows. Delete the blurred and the doubled, keep the best copy, and back up the ones that matter. A well-kept image folder is a library; an unmanaged one is a pile.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Getting an image from the internet onto your computer is only half the job: the other half is managing it — saving it in the right folder, with a name that says what it is, and respecting the rights of the person who made it. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
