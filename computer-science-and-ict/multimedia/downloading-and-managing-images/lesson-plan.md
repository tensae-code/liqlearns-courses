**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Getting an image from the internet onto your computer is only half the job: the other half is managing it — saving it in the right folder, with a name that says what it is, and respecting the rights of the person who made it.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Downloading an image** — To **download an image**, find it on a page you may rightly use, right-click it and choose **Save image as**, then choose the folder and name before saving. The image's **format** and size are shown as you save: a photograph arrives as JPEG, a diagram often as PNG. An image found online belongs to its maker — for school work you may use it with the source named; for anything public, permission is the rule.
2. **Managing your images** — **Managing** images means they can be found again: keep them in folders by subject or project, name them for their content — `tej-market-stall.jpg`, not `IMG_2041.jpg` — and sort by date or name when a folder grows. Delete the blurred and the doubled, keep the best copy, and back up the ones that matter. A well-kept image folder is a library; an unmanaged one is a pile.
