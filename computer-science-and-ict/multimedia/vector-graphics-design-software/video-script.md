The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

A **bitmap** picture is a grid of coloured dots (pixels): enlarge it and it tears into squares. A **vector** picture is a **description** — shapes, lines and curves held as objects with positions and properties — so it can be enlarged **without loss**, from a stamp to a banner. **Design software** such as **Inkscape** (free and open-source) works the vector way, and Vietnam meets it twice: in Grade 10's design lessons, and opening the Grade 11 specialist decoration-design volume with bitmap against vector as the first idea.
In the vector editor, everything is an **object**: a shape, a path, a piece of text. Each object can be **selected, moved, resized, rotated, recoloured and layered** over others, and it stays **editable** — a circle is still a circle, with a fill and an outline you can change tomorrow. Designing is arranging these objects into the finished product — in the specialist book, a decorative postcard for a national tourism-design contest; in Grade 10, the objects a poster or logo is built from.
