# Video script — Digital Photography — Cameras and Photo Files

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

A **digital camera** makes its picture on a **sensor**: a grid of light-sensitive elements, one per **pixel**, each measuring the light that falls on it during the exposure. The picture's **resolution** is the grid's size — width × height in pixels — counted in **megapixels** (millions of pixels). More pixels mean finer detail and bigger files; the **lens** gathers and focuses the light, the **shutter's time** and the sensor's **sensitivity (ISO)** trade brightness against noise — the photographer's three controls over the sensor's meal of light.
The sensor's measurements become a **file**. **JPEG** compresses the picture — discarding fine detail the eye barely misses — into a small, universal file: the format of sharing. **RAW** keeps the sensor's measurements **nearly untouched**: a large file holding everything, for the photographer to develop afterwards in an editor — the format of working. The file also carries **data about the photo (metadata/EXIF)**: the camera, the date, the settings — the photograph's own label, travelling inside it. Choosing a format is choosing what to keep: convenience, or everything.
