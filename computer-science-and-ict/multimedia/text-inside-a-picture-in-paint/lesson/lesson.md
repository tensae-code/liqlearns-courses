# Text Inside a Picture in Paint

## Do it first

Draw a house in Paint. Now write 'My house' under it, inside the same picture. The picture now has text in it. This lesson teaches putting text inside a picture in Paint.

## Text Inside a Picture in Paint

A picture in **Paint** can carry **words** as well as drawings. A picture with words in it — a name under a drawing, a word on a poster, 'Happy birthday' on a card — is a **picture with text**.

To put text in a picture: open Paint, make or open your drawing, choose the **text tool** (the tool marked with a letter), click on the place in the picture where the words should go, and **type** the words. The letters appear inside the picture, in the place you clicked.

Choose the place before you click: words under the drawing for a name or caption, words in an empty space so they can be read clearly. Once text is placed and you click away, it becomes part of the picture itself — so think first, then type. A good picture-with-text can be read at a glance: short words, clear place, nothing covering the drawing's important parts.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: In Paint you can put text inside a picture: choose the text tool, click in the picture, and type — so a drawing can carry words, like a name or a caption. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: say what a picture with text is.
2. Show it, don't just say it: put text into a Paint picture: text tool, click the place, type.
3. Show it, don't just say it: choose a clear place for the words and say why placement matters before clicking away.
