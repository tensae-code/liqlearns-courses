# Composing a Picture from Several Images

## Do it first

One picture is a drawing; several pictures joined with judgement are a composition. Mongolia's Grade 6 Paint unit closes with composition: the pupil stops drawing single things and starts arranging a world.

## Sub lesson 1 — Combining pictures — one world from many parts

A **composition** is a new picture made by **combining pictures**: parts drawn or copied separately — a ger, a horse, mountains, the sky — are placed together on one canvas until they read as a single scene. The parts must agree: light from the same side, colours that sit together, sizes that fit their places. Composition is where the earlier Paint lessons — drawing, selecting, copying, resizing — stop being exercises and become one act of making.

## Sub lesson 2 — Near and far, and adding text

A flat row of same-sized things looks wrong because the eye expects **perspective**: things **near** are drawn **larger**, things **far** smaller — the mountains shrink as they retreat, the near horse stands tall. Resizing is the tool that builds the depth. Last, **text** is added to the picture — a title or a label — placed where it finishes the scene without covering it. Mongolia's closing rule: a composition is judged as one picture, not as the sum of its parts.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: A new picture made by combining several pictures into one scene. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: Light from the same side, colours that sit together, sizes that fit their places. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
