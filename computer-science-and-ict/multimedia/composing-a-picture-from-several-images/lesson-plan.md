## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Combining pictures — one world from many parts** — A **composition** is a new picture made by **combining pictures**: parts drawn or copied separately — a ger, a horse, mountains, the sky — are placed together on one canvas until they read as a single scene. The parts must agree: light from the same side, colours that sit together, sizes that fit their places. Composition is where the earlier Paint lessons — drawing, selecting, copying, resizing — stop being exercises and become one act of making.
2. **Near and far, and adding text** — A flat row of same-sized things looks wrong because the eye expects **perspective**: things **near** are drawn **larger**, things **far** smaller — the mountains shrink as they retreat, the near horse stands tall. Resizing is the tool that builds the depth. Last, **text** is added to the picture — a title or a label — placed where it finishes the scene without covering it. Mongolia's closing rule: a composition is judged as one picture, not as the sum of its parts.
