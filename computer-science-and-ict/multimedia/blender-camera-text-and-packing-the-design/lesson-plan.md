## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The camera — framing, render size and quality** — The scene's **camera** is its official eye: what the camera sees is what the **render** — the finished picture — shows. Framing a shot is placing the camera (and aiming it) so the model fills its story; **render size** sets the picture's dimensions and **quality** its fineness — bigger and finer cost time, so the designer chooses them for the picture's destination: a draft small, a poster large. Kuwait teaches the trade-off plainly: the render is a decision, not a default.
2. **Text in the design — and packing it to move** — Blender adds **text** as a 3D object in the scene — letters with depth, given a material like any solid — for titles and labels that belong to the design itself. And when the design must **move** — to another computer, another program, the 3D printer of the next lesson — it is **packed**: its textures and parts gathered **into** the file, so the design arrives whole instead of pointing at files left behind. Packing is the designer's suitcase check: everything the work needs, in the case.
