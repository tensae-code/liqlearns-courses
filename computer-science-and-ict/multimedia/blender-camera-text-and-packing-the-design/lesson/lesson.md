# Blender — Camera, Text, and Packing the Design

## Do it first

A model nobody frames is a workshop secret. The Grade 8 modelling course ends its craft lessons with showing and sharing: the camera that frames the model for rendering, text that titles the design, and packing that lets the work travel. This lesson is the showing and the sending.

## Sub lesson 1 — The camera — framing, render size and quality

The scene's **camera** is its official eye: what the camera sees is what the **render** — the finished picture — shows. Framing a shot is placing the camera (and aiming it) so the model fills its story; **render size** sets the picture's dimensions and **quality** its fineness — bigger and finer cost time, so the designer chooses them for the picture's destination: a draft small, a poster large. Kuwait teaches the trade-off plainly: the render is a decision, not a default.

## Sub lesson 2 — Text in the design — and packing it to move

Blender adds **text** as a 3D object in the scene — letters with depth, given a material like any solid — for titles and labels that belong to the design itself. And when the design must **move** — to another computer, another program, the 3D printer of the next lesson — it is **packed**: its textures and parts gathered **into** the file, so the design arrives whole instead of pointing at files left behind. Packing is the designer's suitcase check: everything the work needs, in the case.
