# Trimming video clips — smart trim

## Do it first

Tell a partner about your journey to school, but take twice as long as needed — add every small wait and detour. Now retell it in half the time, keeping only what matters. Before reading on — you have just trimmed. This lesson trims film the same way.

## Sub lesson 1 — What trimming is

**Editing a clip** means **previewing** it and **trimming away unnecessary material** and **revising its properties** — speed, direction, duration. Clips made in other programs can be edited in their **original applications** or inside **Premiere Elements**, which is aimed at **blurred, shaky, low-quality and low-interest** sections.

## Sub lesson 2 — Smart trim — manual and automatic

**Smart trimming** finds those trimmable sections two ways. **Manual smart trim** — the default — is where **you set the options** deciding what counts as trimmable. **Automatic smart trim** is where **the program decides** and you keep or delete its choices.

Trimming is possible from **Timeline** view, and **one clip at a time** from the **Sceneline** view.

The guide widens the tool shelf for this work: for the school editing practical, the named editors include Lightworks, Adobe Premiere Rush, HitFilm Express, iMovie, OpenShot and Shotcut, with professional editors Premiere Pro, Final Cut Pro and Lightworks and consumer editors iMovie, PowerDirector, Roxio Creator and Windows Movie Maker named beside them (teacher guide — evidence).
