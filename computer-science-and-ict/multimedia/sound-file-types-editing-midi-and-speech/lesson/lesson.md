# Sound — File Types, Editing, MIDI, and Speech

## Do it first

Grade 11 EPAL gives sound a chapter as full as the image's: how sound is filed, how it is cut, how a keyboard speaks to a computer in MIDI, and how machines learned to speak and to listen.

## Sub lesson 1 — Sound file types, compression and editing

Recorded sound is filed in **types that trade size against quality** — the uncompressed keeping every sample, the **compressed** types discarding what the ear forgives, as the formats lessons teach — and **sound editing** works the recording itself: **cut and join** passages, **fade** ends in and out, **mix** tracks together, clean noise away. The editor's timeline is the sound's picture: what you see (the wave) is what you cut.

## Sub lesson 2 — MIDI, and machines that speak and listen

**MIDI** is not sound at all: it is the **standard language of musical instructions** — which note, how hard, how long — passed between instruments and computers, so a performance can be stored as directions and played by any instrument that reads them. Beyond music stand **speech synthesis (TTS)**, turning written text into spoken voice, and **speech recognition (STT)**, turning voice into text and commands — the pair that lets machines talk with people in both directions, and the chapter closes with sound **transmitted over the internet**, streaming to listeners as it plays.
