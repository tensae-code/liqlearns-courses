## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Sound file types, compression and editing** — Recorded sound is filed in **types that trade size against quality** — the uncompressed keeping every sample, the **compressed** types discarding what the ear forgives, as the formats lessons teach — and **sound editing** works the recording itself: **cut and join** passages, **fade** ends in and out, **mix** tracks together, clean noise away. The editor's timeline is the sound's picture: what you see (the wave) is what you cut.
2. **MIDI, and machines that speak and listen** — **MIDI** is not sound at all: it is the **standard language of musical instructions** — which note, how hard, how long — passed between instruments and computers, so a performance can be stored as directions and played by any instrument that reads them. Beyond music stand **speech synthesis (TTS)**, turning written text into spoken voice, and **speech recognition (STT)**, turning voice into text and commands — the pair that lets machines talk with people in both directions, and the chapter closes with sound **transmitted over the internet**, streaming to listeners as it plays.
