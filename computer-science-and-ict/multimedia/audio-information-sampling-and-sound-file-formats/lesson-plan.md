## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Sampling — the wave measured into numbers** — Sound in the air is a **continuous wave**; the computer holds only numbers. **Sampling** is the bridge: the wave is **measured at regular instants**, each measurement kept as a number, and the row of numbers stored as the sound. Played back, the numbers rebuild a wave near enough to the original for the ear. Sample more often and keep the measurements finer, and the copy stands closer — at the price of a larger file: every audio quality question in the course is this trade, struck at the moment of sampling.
2. **WAV, AIF, MP3 and MID — what each format holds** — The sampled sound is kept in **formats** that differ in what they hold. **WAV** and **AIF** hold the samples themselves, full and uncompressed — the masters, large and true. **MP3** holds a **compressed** account: the samples squeezed by discarding what the ear least misses — small files, near-enough sound. **MID (MIDI)** holds no sound at all: it holds **instructions** — which notes, which instruments, how long — from which the sound is rebuilt at playing. Mongolia's row teaches the formats as answers to one question: what, exactly, did you keep — the wave, a squeeze of it, or the recipe?
