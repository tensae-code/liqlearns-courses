# Lesson plan — Multimedia Programming — Sound, Image and Animation in Code

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Multimedia · **Lesson:** Multimedia Programming — Sound, Image and Animation in Code
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Mongolia (2026-11-14) — in the title Multimedia on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Mongolia's student books (Ministry of Education, Culture and Science(s) of Mongolia, Ulaanbaatar), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree and live lesson content were checked for the slug and for near matches before building.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Multimedia programming in Small Basic — the Shapes object (add, move, rotate, zoom, opacity), the Sound object and its PlayMusic note notation, the Mouse and Program objects, a complete 'Bouncing Ball' paddle game, and the book's closing survey of programming languages
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Shapes and Sound — multimedia's two objects** — Programmed pictures that **move** are the **Shapes object's** work: a shape is **added** once, then **moved, rotated and zoomed** by orders, and faded by its **opacity** — animation as the repeated re-placing of kept shapes, smoother than redrawing. Programmed music is the **Sound object's**: **PlayMusic** takes music written as **note notation** — a string of note names and lengths — and plays the tune the string describes, text become sound by the string lesson's own tools. The **Mouse object** reports the pointer's place and clicks, and the **Program object** governs the program itself — its window, its delays — the stage manager of the show the other objects perform.
2. **The 'Bouncing Ball' game — and the survey of languages** — The chapter's crown artefact is complete: the **'Bouncing Ball' paddle game** — a ball in flight, a paddle under the player's mouse, walls, bounces, misses — every object and every earlier doctrine (loops, conditions, graphics, sound) serving inside one playable program. Then the book closes its whole programming course with a **survey of languages** — C/C++/C#, Java, Python, App Inventor, Swift, Scratch, Flowgorithm, Logo, Alice — Small Basic's family portrait: the pupil who leaves Mongolia's course knows one language well and can place a dozen by their shapes and homes, which is what 'knowing a language' honestly means at school's end.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
