# Multimedia hardware and software requirements

## Do it first

Before reading on, list what a computer must have before it can show you a film with sound, words and moving drawings all at once. Keep your list — this lesson gives the stated floor, part by part.

## What a multimedia system is — and its hardware floor

A **multimedia system** integrates two or more media types for generating, storing, representing, manipulating and accessing multimedia information.

The stated hardware floor is: a **Pentium-class CPU**, **1 GB RAM**, an **SVGA monitor**, a **video card**, a **sound card** and a **CD-ROM drive**. Each part serves a component: the sound card serves audio, the video card and monitor serve video, graphics and animation, and the CD-ROM drive carried the large files multimedia arrives on.

## The software set

Five kinds of software are named: a **word processor** for text; **image processing software** — Photoshop and CorelDraw are named — for images; **animation creators** — 3D Max and Flash; **sound software** — Sound Forge; and **video and authoring software** — Video for Windows and Authorware are named.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: the hardware floor in this lesson is the floor as printed in the book. Machines you meet today are usually stronger; the lesson's point is which part serves which medium, not the numbers alone.

## Three key concepts — 2D, 3D, virtual reality

**2D animation** is animation in two dimensions — flat movement across a screen. **3D animation** adds depth, so objects can turn and be viewed as if solid. **Virtual reality** is an immersive computer-generated 3D environment, worked through stereo headphones, a head-mounted display (goggles) and data gloves — the user feels inside the scene, not in front of it.
