**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Multimedia · **Lesson:** Creating a Video with PowerPoint and Movie Maker

**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Creating a video two ways: exporting a PowerPoint presentation as a video with narration and timings, and building a video in Windows Movie Maker

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **PowerPoint as a video — narration and timings** — A presentation becomes a video by **exporting it**: record a **narration** over the slides, set the **timings** — how long each slide stays — and PowerPoint renders the deck as a **video file** that plays itself anywhere, no presenter and no PowerPoint needed. The slide show stops being an event and becomes an **object** that can be sent, stored and replayed. Rwanda's S3 practical uses it for exactly that: a deck that must travel without its maker.
2. **Movie Maker — building the video from pieces** — In **Windows Movie Maker** the video is assembled: **videos, photos and music are added** to the project, then shaped — **fade** between pieces, **trim** a clip's ends, **split** a clip to remove or move its middle, and change a clip's **speed**. The book's order is the editor's order everywhere: gather, arrange, trim, then polish — effects last, because a fade cannot fix a wrong order.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
