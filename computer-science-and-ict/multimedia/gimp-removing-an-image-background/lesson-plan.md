## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Cutting out — selecting the subject, refusing the rest** — **Removing a background** means selecting the **subject** — a person, an object — so exactly that it can be separated from everything around it, and the rest **deleted or masked away**. The tools are the course's own: **selection tools** for clean-edged subjects, the **Paths tool** for edges a marquee cannot follow. The craft lives at the boundary: zoom in, because a cut is judged at its worst centimetre — a halo of the old background clinging to an edge betrays the whole move.
2. **Merging into a new scene** — The freed subject is **merged into a new scene**: placed on its layer over a new background, scaled to belong (the blending lesson's craft), and seated so light and ground agree. Removing is the noun, merging is the verb's completion — a cut-out that never lands anywhere is only an exercise. Kuwait sequences it after blending deliberately: pupils who have blended know exactly what a good cut must survive.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
