# Making an Awareness Infographic

## Do it first

A poster about cybercrime that nobody reads prevents nothing. An infographic is the opposite craft: one page, mostly pictures, a handful of words, and a stranger understands the danger and the defence in twenty seconds. Grade 9's cyber-safety experience ends by making one — this lesson is how.

## Sub lesson 1 — The infographic — information as a picture

An **infographic** presents information **graphically**: icons, simple charts, short labels and a clear visual path replace paragraphs. Its rules are compression rules: one message only (here, *prevent this cybercrime*); every element earning its place; numbers shown as pictures (a bar, a pie, ten figures with three coloured) rather than digits in a sentence. The reader's eye must travel in the maker's order — title, danger, defence, action — without a guide standing beside it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Making it — from the unit's knowledge to the public's page

The Grade 9 teams build theirs from the whole cyber-threats experience: choose the **one crime** their audience actually meets (the audience lesson's discipline), take its **true facts** from the unit's verified learning, and design the page — draft, test on a stranger from the target audience, and fix what the stranger missed or misread. The infographic is finished when the stranger can repeat its message unaided. Awareness that cannot be repeated was decoration, and the unit's standard is stricter: a page that changes what its reader does next.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: Information presented graphically on one page. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: Icons, simple charts and short labels. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: One. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: As pictures — bars, pies, coloured figures. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
