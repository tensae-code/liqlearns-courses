# Lesson plan — Making an Awareness Infographic

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Multimedia · **Lesson:** Making an Awareness Infographic
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Bangladesh (2026-11-14) — in the title Multimedia on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Bangladesh's NCTB student books (Bangladesh Grade 9 Digital Technology), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree and live lesson content were checked for the slug and for near matches before building.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** making an awareness infographic: compressing a public-awareness message — here, preventing cybercrime — into one visual page where picture, chart and few words carry the whole warning (Bangladesh Grade 9 Digital Technology, Learning Experience 2, Sessions 9–10).
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The infographic — information as a picture** — An **infographic** presents information **graphically**: icons, simple charts, short labels and a clear visual path replace paragraphs. Its rules are compression rules: one message only (here, *prevent this cybercrime*); every element earning its place; numbers shown as pictures (a bar, a pie, ten figures with three coloured) rather than digits in a sentence. The reader's eye must travel in the maker's order — title, danger, defence, action — without a guide standing beside it.
2. **Making it — from the unit's knowledge to the public's page** — The Grade 9 teams build theirs from the whole cyber-threats experience: choose the **one crime** their audience actually meets (the audience lesson's discipline), take its **true facts** from the unit's verified learning, and design the page — draft, test on a stranger from the target audience, and fix what the stranger missed or misread. The infographic is finished when the stranger can repeat its message unaided. Awareness that cannot be repeated was decoration, and the unit's standard is stricter: a page that changes what its reader does next.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — A poster about cybercrime that nobody reads prevents nothing. An infographic is the opposite craft: one page, mostly pictures, a handful of words, and a stranger understands the danger and the defence in twenty seconds. Grade 9's cyber-safety experience ends by making one — this lesson is how. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of the infographic — information as a picture as the lesson describes it — the student studies the picture and names what each part shows.
   - ADD GIF: a GIF of making it — from the unit's knowledge to the public's page in action — the student watches once, then says what changed and why.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the lesson's key names and definitions aloud, in the order the lesson gives them — the student listens once through, then reads the same lines aloud along with the voice. (Audio is a described slot in this build; no audio file is made.)
