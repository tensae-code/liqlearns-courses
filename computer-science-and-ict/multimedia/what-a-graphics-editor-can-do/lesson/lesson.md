# What a Graphics Editor Can Do

## Do it first

Grade 9's second graphics lesson asks what the tool is *for*: the tasks and possibilities of a graphics editor — what jobs it is given, and what it can do with a picture.

## Sub lesson 1 — The tasks of a graphics editor

A **graphics editor** is given three tasks: **create** new pictures, **edit** pictures that exist — fix, crop, recolour, combine — and **prepare** pictures for use: size them, save them in the needed form. Uzbekistan's Grade 9 book states the tasks before the interface, so the pupil knows the job before the tools.

## Sub lesson 2 — The possibilities — what can be done

The editor's **possibilities** reach further than drawing: work with photographs, join pieces into one picture, add text to an image, and save the result for print or screen. The lesson's measure is practical — a possibility counts when the pupil can say which task it serves and try it on a real picture.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: A **graphics editor** is given three tasks: **create** new pictures, **edit** pictures that exist — fix, crop, recolour, combine — and **prepare** pictures for use: size them, save them in the needed form. The editor's **possibilities** reach further than drawing: work with photographs, join pieces into one picture, add text to an image, and save the result for print or screen. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: Create, edit and prepare pictures. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: Fix and crop (or recolour, combine). Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: Work with photographs; join pieces into one picture (or add text). Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: Print or screen. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
