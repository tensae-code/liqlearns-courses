## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Bitmap images — made of pixels** — A **bitmap** (or raster) image is built from **pixels** — tiny coloured squares in a grid — the way a camera or Paint records a picture. It holds photographs beautifully, but it has a fixed number of pixels: stretch it bigger and the squares show, and the picture turns blocky and blurred. The common bitmap formats are **JPEG** for photographs, **PNG** for pictures needing sharp edges or transparency, and **GIF** for simple pictures and short animations.
2. **Vector images — made of shapes and rules** — A **vector** image stores no pixels: it stores the **rules** for its shapes — lines, curves and fills described exactly — so the computer redraws it perfectly at any size, from a stamp to a banner. That is why logos, diagrams and lettering are drawn as vectors. Editors such as GIMP and Photoshop work mainly with bitmaps, while drawing programs work with vectors; a vector can always be turned into a bitmap at a chosen size, but a bitmap cannot truly be turned back.
