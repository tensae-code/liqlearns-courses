# Choosing Sounds and Changing Backdrops

## Do it first

Watch a scene of a forest with seaside sounds and a bedroom picture: everything fights. Now give it forest sounds and a forest backdrop: the scene agrees with itself. Choosing well is this lesson's skill.

## Choosing Sounds and Changing Backdrops

This double lesson joins two finishing skills. **Choosing a sound**: the Scratch sound gallery holds many sounds, and the craft is picking the one that **fits the moment** — a pop for a balloon, a cheer for a goal — and testing it in the scene, not just in the gallery. **Changing the backdrop**: when the story moves to a new place, the backdrop changes with it, at the right moment, so place and action agree. Together they are the polish of every earlier project: the right sound at the right second, the right place behind the right action. The learner practises judging fit — trying a sound, rejecting it, choosing better — which is real multimedia judgement, not just block knowledge.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: A scene is finished by its sound and its place: choose the sound that fits the moment from the gallery, and change the backdrop when the story moves — sound and place working together. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: choose a sound from the gallery that fits a scene and say why it fits. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: change the backdrop at the moment the story changes place. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: reject a sound or backdrop that fights the scene and choose a better one. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
