# Sharpening — what it does and when to stop

## Do it first

A photo can be in focus yet look soft — edges melt into each other. Before reading on, guess how a program could make edges stand out without adding any real detail — and why pushing that trick too far would ruin the picture.

## Edges, contrast, and the stop line

**Sharpening** makes an image look crisp and clear by **enhancing the edges of objects** — increasing contrast along edges brings out detail. But too much sharpness makes an image worse and loses detail.

Three methods are announced: **Unsharp Mask, Smart Sharpen and High Pass** — one lesson each follows this one.

## The Unsharp Mask and its three controls

The Unsharp Mask's curious name: a blurred negative mask is made, subtracted to detect edges, then contrast is raised along those edges. Its three controls:

- **Amount** — how much sharpness. Experiment; it depends on image size.
- **Radius** — the size of details sharpened: **0.3–0.5** for most images, **1–1.5** for high-resolution ones.
- **Threshold** — tells the mask to ignore parts of the image, reducing sharpening noise: keep at **0** unless noise appears.
