# Video script — Layers and Colour Channels in Image Editing

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

A digital image in an editor is a **stack of layers**: transparent sheets, each holding part of the picture — background on the bottom sheet, objects above, text near the top. The visible image is the stack **seen through**, top to bottom. Layers can be **shown or hidden, reordered, made partly transparent (opacity), edited or deleted** — each without harming the others. That separateness is the editor's safety: a mistake lives on one layer and dies with it, not with the whole picture.
A colour image is also kept **by channels**: in the RGB model, three **grayscale channels** hold how much **red, green and blue** each pixel carries, and the colour picture is their sum. Looking at one channel shows that colour's map of the image — the red channel is bright where the picture is reddish. Editors let you **view and even edit channels separately**, the road to fine colour correction and effects. Layers divide the image by **place**; channels divide it by **colour** — the two cuts every raster editor is built from.
