# Lesson plan — Making a Film on the Shotcut Timeline

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Multimedia · **Lesson:** Making a Film on the Shotcut Timeline
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Saudi Arabia (2026-11-14) — in the title Multimedia on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Saudi Arabia's Grade 8 teacher guide, because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson. This lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence only (Saudi Arabia), named in lesson/sources.md, counted nowhere.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** In Shotcut a film is built on the Timeline: you import your clips, add them to tracks in the order of the story, delete what does not fit, lock finished tracks so they cannot be disturbed, undo mistakes — and save the project so it can be reopened and finished later.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the Saudi book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The Shotcut screen and importing files** — Shotcut's **interface** centres on the **Timeline** — the tracks where the film is assembled — with a preview that shows the film as it grows. Making begins by **importing files**: bring the video clips, pictures and sounds into the project first, so everything the film needs sits ready before any editing starts.
2. **Building on the Timeline — and saving the project** — **Add clips** to the Timeline in the order the story needs them; **delete** a clip that does not fit. **Lock a track** when its work is finished, so later edits cannot disturb it. A mistake is not a disaster: **Undo** steps back over it. **Save the project** as you work, and **reopen** it next time to carry on exactly where you stopped — the project file remembers the whole arrangement.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
