## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **File types, codecs and compression** — A video **file type** is a container; its **codec** is the method that **codes and decodes** the frames inside it — compression being unavoidable, since raw video's size (the digitisation lesson's arithmetic) no machine or network can carry kindly. Codecs trade **quality against size and speed**: the same film kept near-perfect for the archive, squeezed hard for the phone. Choosing a type is choosing that trade for a named audience, never a matter of taste alone.
2. **Editing, encoding and streaming** — **Video editing** assembles the film: **cut and order clips** on the timeline, join them with **transitions**, lay **sound** under them, add **titles**, and **encode (export)** the finished piece in the type and codec its audience needs — encoding, distribution and sending being one road with three names for its stages. Sent over the internet, video is **streamed**: played as it arrives, the network's speed setting the quality the player can honestly show — the chapter's closing link between the codec's arithmetic and the watcher's experience.
