# What Animation Is — Frames and Motion — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) Two students draw a bouncing ball. One uses six frames with the ball a little further along in each; the other uses two frames — ball on the ground, ball in the air. Whose will look like bouncing, and why?**
**Answer:** The six-frame one. Small changes between frames are what the eye joins into movement; two frames with a big jump read as the ball vanishing and appearing, not bouncing.

**2. (Sub lesson 2) An animation plays at a very low frame rate. Describe what you see and name the fix.**
**Answer:** You see separate pictures flickering with gaps — jerky movement, not smooth motion. The fix is to show more frames per second, filling in the in-between positions.

**3. (Sub lesson 2) A camera video of a real bouncing ball also shows frames. How is animation different from that video?**
**Answer:** The camera *recorded* real movement frame by frame; in animation every frame is *created* — drawn or set by the animator (with the computer's help for in-betweens) — no real ball ever bounced.
