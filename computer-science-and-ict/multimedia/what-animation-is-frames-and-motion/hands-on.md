# Hands-on — What Animation Is — Frames and Motion

The lesson with your hands, not only your eyes.

## Make a flip book

Take the corner of a notebook — at least ten pages. On the last page draw a simple figure: a ball, a stick person, a bird. On each page going forward, draw it again, changed by one small step — the ball higher, the wings lower. Flip the pages with your thumb and watch it move. Then flip slowly and watch the movement fall apart into single frames.

## Slow it down, speed it up

Flip the same flip book at three speeds: very slow, medium, as fast as your thumb can go. Write one line for each speed: what did the movement look like? Point to the speed where it stopped being separate pictures and became movement — that change is frame rate doing its work.
