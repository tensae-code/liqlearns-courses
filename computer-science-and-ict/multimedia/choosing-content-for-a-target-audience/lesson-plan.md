# Lesson plan — Choosing Content for a Target Audience

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Multimedia · **Lesson:** Choosing Content for a Target Audience
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Bangladesh (2026-11-14) — in the title Multimedia on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Bangladesh's NCTB student books (Bangladesh Grade 6 Digital Technology), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree and live lesson content were checked for the slug and for near matches before building.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** choosing content for a target audience: naming who a digital gift or message is for, and choosing its content — text, image, video, music — to fit that audience, as the opening sessions of Bangladesh's Grade 6 digital-gifts experience teach (Grade 6 Digital Technology, Learning Experience 2, Sessions 1–2).
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Content and its target group** — **Digital content** comes in forms — **text, image, video, music** and their mixes (the multimedia companion lesson) — and every piece of content has a **target group**: the people it is made for. A target group is named by real traits: age, language, place, what they know, what they love, what they can use. 'Everyone' is not a target group; a gift 'for everyone' fits no one in particular, and its maker chooses blindly.
2. **Planning the gift for the named audience** — With the audience named, the **planning** follows: choose the form they receive best (a song for the friend who never reads, large print for the grandfather), the language they dream in, the length their patience holds, and the examples from *their* world, not the maker's. The Grade 6 teams plan a **digital gift** for a named person or group and defend each choice by the audience: not 'I like video' but 'she watches video with her daughter, so video'. The audience is the plan's ruler — every later choice in the making lessons is measured against it.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — A birthday message for your grandmother and one for your best friend may carry the same love — and not one same word, picture or song. The difference is not the feeling; it is the audience. Grade 6's digital-gifts project begins here: before any making, name who it is for. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of content and its target group as the lesson describes it — the student studies the picture and names what each part shows.
   - ADD GIF: a GIF of planning the gift for the named audience in action — the student watches once, then says what changed and why.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the lesson's key names and definitions aloud, in the order the lesson gives them — the student listens once through, then reads the same lines aloud along with the voice. (Audio is a described slot in this build; no audio file is made.)
3. **Say it to your companion** — the companion block in lesson/lesson.md.
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the lesson in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-false, matching, short-answer — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the doing block at the end of lesson/lesson.md.
