## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **3D design — and its file formats** — **3D design** builds objects with **length, width and depth** — models that can be turned, lit and finally printed, not pictures of objects but the objects' digital selves. Its work travels in named **file formats**: the program's own project format, which keeps everything editable, and exchange formats that carry the model to other programs and to printers. Knowing which format a moment needs — keeping, sharing, printing — is the designer's first filing skill.
2. **Meeting Blender — Viewport, Outliner, moving and handling** — **Blender** is the free 3D program. Its home room is the **3D Viewport**: the space where the model stands and is seen — from any side, as the designer **moves around** it (orbit, pan, zoom), because a solid thing cannot be judged from one view. Beside it the **Outliner** lists every object in the scene by name — the scene's table of contents. **Handling an object** — selecting it, moving it in space — is the first manual skill, practised before any modelling: you cannot build in a room you cannot walk around.
