# Learning a Craft From a Tutorial Video

## Do it first

learning a craft from a tutorial video — watching a maker's video in steps, pausing to do each stage with your own hands, until a real craft or household product stands finished (Vietnam Grade 5, track B)

## Sub lesson 1 — The video is the teacher — watch in steps, not all at once

In Grade 5's track B, the teacher of a craft is a **tutorial video**: a maker shows, step by step, how a real thing is made — a paper craft, a household item. The learning way is not to watch it through like a story, but to **watch a step, pause, and do that step** with your own materials, then watch the next. Before starting, the class gathers **everything the video's maker lists** — materials and tools — because a craft stopped halfway by a missing piece teaches mostly frustration.

In your mind's eye: the video is the teacher — watch in steps, not all at once.

## Sub lesson 2 — Hands follow eyes — checking, fixing, finishing

Doing the step is its own skill: **compare** your piece with the video's piece at every pause — same fold, same join, same size? Where yours differs, **rewind and watch that step again** before going on; a small error carried forward becomes a big one. At the end stands a **finished, usable product** — and the track's real lesson beside the craft: a video can teach a pair of hands almost anything, if the hands work while the eyes watch.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: A tutorial video — a maker showing how a real thing is made, step by step Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: In steps — watch one step, pause, and do that step yourself Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: Everything the maker lists — all materials and tools Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: Real physical products — a craft product, then a household item Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
