**Acceptance count: taught in 8 countries (Ethiopia, Nepal, Uzbekistan, Egypt, Mongolia, Sri Lanka, Rwanda, Greece).**

Nothing in this lesson claims a page the placements do not give; the lesson's content is the placements' content, re-expressed for the learner, with the book's own step orders, examples and Ethiopian practicals kept. Procedures are taught exactly as the book teaches them — Photoshop first, with the GIMP alternative where the book notes it.

Mauritania's own way (Grade 8): The second year is organised as five applied units of 35 thirty-minute fiches — image processing first (Paint/GIMP/Photoshop, bitmap vs vector), then internet ethics, a full Excel data-processing unit ending in a practical project, web source-checking and responsible downloading, and finally email — each unit closed by evaluation and support fiches with full answer keys at the end.

Saudi Arabia's own way (Grade 8, Digital Skills): the Binary Logic series is taught in fixed periods (33 periods this term) with the student book mirrored by this guide — unit/lesson description, learning outcomes, "important points", lesson steps, worked answers and group unit projects, with all practice files delivered through the national Ain enrichment platform (ien.edu.sa). The term is three applied units: video production in Shotcut, charts in Excel, and programming a virtual robot in VEXcode VR where pupils read their own blocks as Python. (teacher guide — evidence)

Papua New Guinea's own way (Grade 11): the senior guide turns learners into junior system administrators and builders — computer administration, datasheets ("data books") and timing technology, documenting server and printer configurations, data science, pseudocode, Green IT, and building static web pages with tools like Canva and Figma; typing rises to 40 wpm at 85–100% accuracy. (teacher guide — evidence)


Uzbekistan's own way (Grade 9): grade 9 is the capstone — computer graphics, Access, Excel, then a nine-lesson algorithms block and a 28-lesson Turbo Pascal 7.0 course that runs from the alphabet of the language to files, procedures and graphics mode, closing with the Internet. Every chapter ends with a dedicated repetition lesson, placed here to that chapter's main lesson.

Cuba's own way (Grade 5): The Grade 5 programme is built around extracting information from Cuban educational software, encyclopedias and EcuRed into word-processor documents, then Scratch — drawing/animation first (Unit 3), control/movement/appearance blocks in Unit 4. (teacher programme — evidence)

Cuba's own way (Grade 5): Same Grade 5 content as the Programa, recast as per-unit teaching tables with hour allocations; Unit 4 is renamed “Aprendiendo y creando” and taught purely as Scratch control, movement and appearance blocks. (teacher guide — evidence)

Egypt's own way (Grade 10, Term 1 Programming and Artificial Intelligence student book): A full Japanese-style "Information Study" course adopted for Egypt's new secondary track: 13 chapters running from information theory, ethics and security through number systems, data analysis (frequency, variance, correlation, regression in spreadsheets), simulation and queues, Python, and a final AI-programming project chapter building web pages, a quiz app and a block-breaker game with generative AI.

Mongolia's own way (Grade 10): Grade 10 is the "citizen" book — ICT ethics, law and intellectual property first, taught with Mongolian digital content as the examples (the Nogun comic, the Chinggis Khaan animation, GoGo Kids, Maamuu and YoLo; the CRC's black-list.mn), then a distinctive "package information" chapter — collecting, storing, merging and presenting information as one whole, worked through a 24-slide presentation on the sights of Bayankhongor aimag — multimedia production with free tools (Pencil2D, Pivot Animator, Unfreez, Audacity), and a full MS Access database chapter built around a Mongolian phone-book database and the state's unified registration system.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 11, Tamil): a full Visual Basic 6.0 programming course inside the O/L book (IDE, controls, data types, Select Case, arrays), an SDLC chapter, Photoshop-based multimedia, hand-coded HTML with Sri Lankan examples (the Sinharaja rainforest page, ucsc.cmb.ac.lk addresses, sltnet.lk domains), and sector chapters (agriculture, industry, government, commerce) before malware and health.

Rwanda's own way (S3 ICT Learner's Book, Fountain): the same ten units as the S3 guide taught as long screenshot-by-screenshot practicals rooted in Rwandan school life (a GS Bumbogo parents'-meeting mail merge, an administration map of Rwanda built in ArcGIS, an environmental-protection presentation, 'Rwanda Nziza' hyperlink decks), closing with a complete Scratch platform game, Hard Journey.

Rwanda's own way (S3 ICT Teacher Guide, Fountain): the S3 guide (a different publisher from the KLB S1/S2 set) organises the year into four study areas — application software, GIS/spreadsheets/presentations, graphics and multimedia, networks and programming — ending in platform game programming in Scratch. (teacher guide — evidence)

Greece's own way (Grade 11 EPAL, Basika Themata Pliroforikis — student notes): the EPAL (vocational) informatics-sector notes go far deeper into digital technology than the general track — full data representation (number systems and base conversion, character encoding, compression, integer representation and overflow), image/sound/video representation and editing, animation, VR/AR, 3D printing and scanning, then the electronics ladder (Boolean algebra, logic gates, combinational and sequential circuits, adders, flip-flops, counters, A/D and D/A converters, integrated circuits and the CPU), ending with digital-technology applications sector by sector and ICT professions.
