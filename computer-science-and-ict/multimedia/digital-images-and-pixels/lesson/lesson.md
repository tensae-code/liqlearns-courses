# Digital images — made of pixels

## Do it first

Open any photo on a phone or computer and zoom in as far as it will go, again and again. Before reading on, say what the smooth picture turns into when you zoom far enough.

## Sub lesson 1 — Pixels — the smallest element of an image

A computer does everything in binary digits — bits, series of 0s and 1s.

When you take a picture with a digital camera, the picture is **digitised** — turned into bits. Scanning a photo or artwork into Photoshop digitises it too.

A **digital image** is an image represented digitally, in combinations of bits called **pixels**. A pixel is the smallest element of an image — one tiny square of one colour. Stand back and the squares join into a smooth picture; zoom in and you see the squares themselves.

## Sub lesson 2 — Colour from three basics — and what a logo is made of (teacher guide — evidence)

Every colour in a digital image is mixed from three basics: **red, blue and green**. Mix the three lights in different amounts and every other colour is obtained.

[Picture 20503 — RGB mixing and the parts of a logo]

[GIF 20504 — the three basics overlapping]

A **logo** is a symbol made of text and images that identifies a business. It is usually built from a symbol or brand mark plus a logotype — the name written in letters — often with a tagline under it. The Ministry of Education's own logo is the worked example of text and image combined.

And this is why images matter: a single still image can convey complex, multiple ideas more effectively than a verbal description.
