# Lesson plan — Gradients and Shadows That Make Depth in a Game

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Multimedia · **Lesson:** Gradients and Shadows That Make Depth in a Game
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Finland (2026-11-14) — in the title Multimedia on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Finland's *Ensiaskeleet ohjelmointiin* (student book, Oppilaan kirja, Versio 2.0), because no existing lesson teaches this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A flat-looking game gains depth from shadow: draw shadow shapes filled with a gradient, and send them behind the walls, so the maze looks raised and three-dimensional instead of flat on the page.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on drawing in the paint editor with gradient fills; the shadow shape and the send-behind ordering are built inside the lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a gradient fill does in a shadow shape.
2. Draw a gradient-filled shadow shape for a wall.
3. Send the shadow behind the walls so the depth effect works.
4. Say why depth is an advanced finishing touch, added after the game works.

## What you need
A computer with Scratch, with a maze project from the earlier lessons.

## If the learner is stuck
Shadow looks like a dirty smear on the wall → the shadow shape sits in front; send it behind the walls. Shadow edge is hard and fake → the fill is flat colour; use a gradient that fades. Game broke → this tip changes looks only; a play fault came from elsewhere.
