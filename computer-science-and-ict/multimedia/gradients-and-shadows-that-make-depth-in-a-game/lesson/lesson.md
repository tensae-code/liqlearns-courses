# Gradients and Shadows That Make Depth in a Game

## Do it first

Two mazes, same walls, same game. One looks like a flat diagram; the other looks like walls you could stub a toe on. The difference is not the game at all — it is light and shadow, drawn in the right order.

## Shadows with gradients, sent behind

Depth is drawn, not programmed. Along a wall's edge, draw a **shadow shape** — a dark strip the shape of the shadow the wall would cast — and fill it with a **gradient**, dark at the wall and fading as it leaves it, the way real shadows soften at their edges. Then comes the ordering that makes or breaks the effect: **send the shadow behind the walls** (the shadow belongs under/behind the wall layer, showing only where a shadow would fall). In front, the 'shadow' covers the wall itself and looks like dirt; behind, it peeks out exactly as shadow should and the wall stands up from the floor. The book files this among the advanced project tips: it changes nothing about how the game plays, and everything about how the game feels — which is why it comes after the game works, as a finishing touch.
