# The five components of multimedia

## Do it first

Open any lesson or app screen you know. Before reading on, point at everything on it and sort what you point at: words, pictures, sounds, moving film, moving drawings. How many sorts did you make?

## Text and graphics

**Text** — headlines, subtitles, captions, directions, menus and buttons, in varied fonts and sizes. Text is still the backbone of electronic books and reference works: when everything else is stripped away, the words carry the information.

**Graphics** — visual elements that point to information and make concepts clearer and more interesting, spanning clipart, charts and photographs. A graphic becomes **hypermedia** when the graphic itself links to further information: click the picture, and more opens.

## Audio and video

**Audio** — digitised speech, music and sound effects. Sound is captured by microphone, or played from a synthesizer or keyboard through a MIDI port.

**Video** — photographic images played at speed so they appear in full motion. Because every second holds many images, video makes very large files. In interactive multimedia the user does not merely receive video — they respond to it and control elements of it.

## Animation

**Animation** — graphic images that change or move. Animation conveys information, adds interest and pulls attention, and it is a strong teaching aid for techniques and complex procedures: a process too fast, too slow or too dangerous to film can be drawn moving, step by step.

Hold the five jobs apart: text says, graphics show, audio is heard, video films the real, animation draws the moving.

## Say it to your companion

Give your companion the five components and each one's job in one short sentence each. Finish with the memory line: text says, graphics show, audio is heard, video films the real, animation draws the moving.
