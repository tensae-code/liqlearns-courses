# Lesson plan — The five components of multimedia

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Multimedia · **Lesson:** The five components of multimedia
**Place in the curriculum:** Ethiopia Grade 10, Unit 4 — Image Processing and Multimedia (printed pp. 139–181).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** each of multimedia's five components — text, graphics, audio, video and animation — has its own job in a presentation.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on What multimedia is: the learner can name the five forms; this lesson gives each form its job, in depth.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds, looping.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: What multimedia is — the combination itself. Recommended, never forced. A learner can land here directly; the lesson stands alone.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the five components and state each one's job.
2. Explain hypermedia at component level: a graphic that links to further information.
3. Say how audio is captured and why video files are large.
## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes video and animation → video is photographic images played at speed; animation is drawn graphic images that change or move.
- Calls every picture a graphic only → a graphic that links onward is doing the hypermedia job; name both jobs.
