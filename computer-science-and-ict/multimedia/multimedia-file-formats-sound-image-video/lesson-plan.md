# Lesson plan — Multimedia file formats — sound, image, video

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Multimedia · **Lesson:** Multimedia file formats — sound, image, video
**Place in the curriculum:** Ethiopia Grade 10, Unit 4 — Image Processing and Multimedia (printed pp. 139–181).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** multimedia files come in named formats, sorted by medium: sound WAV/MIDI/WMA, image TIFF/JPEG/GIF/PNG/BMP, video AVI/MPEG.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The five components of multimedia and on the file-extension lessons of this course: a format is the structure a file's information is stored in.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds, looping.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The five components of multimedia; file name extensions — reading a file's ending. Recommended, never forced. A learner can land here directly; the lesson stands alone.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define file format.
2. Sort all ten named formats under sound, image and video.
3. State that images are inserted into or linked to a document.
## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.
