# Lesson plan — AR and VR at work — education, health, entertainment

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** New Technologies · **Lesson:** AR and VR at work — education, health, entertainment
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Emerging Technologies (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** in education AR/VR let learners travel in time and space and handle 3-D ideas; in healthcare they train surgeons safely and help in operations; in entertainment they bring museums, cinema, parks and games alive.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Grade 11 Emerging Technologies unit around it. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or in steps — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **In education** — time-and-space travel, 3-D concepts, scarce-lab stand-ins.
2. **In healthcare** — risk-free surgery training, tele-surgery, calmer patients.
3. **In entertainment** — museums, VR cinema, parks, AR games.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the other lessons of this unit. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name four education uses, including the scarce-laboratory point.
2. Name four healthcare uses, including risk-free surgery training.
3. Name the four entertainment uses.
4. Add two guide-only education examples (teacher guide — evidence).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Imagine learning the human heart by walking around a beating 3-D heart, instead of reading one flat picture. Hold that. Then read what AR and VR do in classrooms, hospitals and places of fun. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the parts below, in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 16514]: a picture of three panels — a history scene in a classroom, a simulated operation, a museum artefact coming alive — the student names one use per panel.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 16519]: a picture of a pilot in a VR flight simulator beside a pupil walking inside a giant cell — the student says why each scene must be simulated, not real.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading this lesson's key words aloud — the student listens, then says each word and what it means.
3. **Say it to your companion** — explain the lesson back in your own words (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the lesson retold in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-or-false, fill-in-the-blank, short-answer — served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the doing block at the end of lesson/lesson.md.
