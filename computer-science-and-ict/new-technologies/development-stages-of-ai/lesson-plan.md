## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The early stages — birth and first winter** — AI begins as a named field in the **1950s** (the Dartmouth workshop, 1956), with early triumphs — programs proving theorems and playing checkers — and large promises. The promises outran the machines: by the **1970s** came the first **AI winter**, funding and faith withdrawn. A second flowering in the **1980s** — expert systems in real companies — met a second winter when their brittleness and upkeep showed. The pattern of the stages is itself the lesson: capability and claim must be told apart, in every decade.
2. **The modern stages — data, learning, everywhere** — The return came on three legs: **data** (the internet's oceans of it), **computing power** (chips that could train large models), and **machine learning** methods that improved with both. Landmarks followed — a chess champion beaten (1997), speech and images recognised reliably (2010s), language models conversing (2020s). Jordan's Grade 9 history ends with the pupil inside the story: the current stage is theirs to judge, with the winters as the standing warning against both despair and hype.
