## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What a simulation is — a world that obeys rules** — A **simulation** is a program that imitates a real system and **obeys its rules**: change a value, and the world answers as the real one would (within the model's honesty). **PhET** is a library of such worlds for science and maths — free, visual, built for exploring: the pupil does not read about the circuit; the pupil **builds it**, closes the switch, and watches the bulb. Jordan's Grade 4 guide sets PhET inside a science environment (electricity): the simulation is the laboratory the school can always afford.
2. **Exploring and designing — the pupil as experimenter** — Learning with a simulation follows the experimenter's loop: **predict** (what will happen if…), **change one thing**, **observe**, **explain** — and the guide's project spends it fully: pupils **design a circuit** in the PhET electricity world, test it, and carry the design's understanding to their presentation work. The lesson's discipline is the scientist's: a simulation is believed **as far as its rules are the real rules** — the pupil who can say where the model simplifies is using the simulation; the pupil who cannot is being used by it.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
