## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Connectivity and heterogeneity** — An IoT system is first defined by **connectivity**: its things are linked — to each other, to gateways and to the internet — by wired or wireless means, and the link is the system's lifeline. Second, **device heterogeneity**: the things differ — different makers, sensors, processors, protocols and power — yet must work as one system. Designing for difference is not an extra in IoT; it is the normal condition, and standards and gateways exist to translate between the unlike parts.
2. **Scalability and security** — **Scalability** means the system can grow — from ten sensors to ten thousand — without being rebuilt: addresses, networks and data handling must stretch. **Security** is a characteristic, not an afterthought: every connected thing is a door, so identity, encryption and updates are designed in from the start. Jordan's list closes the definition of IoT itself: a system that cannot connect, mix, grow and protect is not yet an IoT system, however smart its parts.
