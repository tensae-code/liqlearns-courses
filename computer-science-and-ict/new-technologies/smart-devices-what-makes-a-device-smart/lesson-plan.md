# Lesson plan — Smart Devices — What Makes a Device Smart

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** New Technologies · **Lesson:** Smart Devices — What Makes a Device Smart
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Iraq (2026-11-14) — in the title New Technologies on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Iraq's Grade 11 Computer student book, because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A smart device is a device that does more than its plain job: it senses, processes and responds — taking in information, working on it with its own programs, and acting or answering back. Smart devices run on their own operating systems and applications — the next lessons' topics — and they connect: to the internet and to each other, which is what leads the book straight into the Internet of Things.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The concept of smart devices** — A **smart device** is a device that does more than its plain job: it **senses, processes and responds** — taking in information, working on it with its own programs, and acting or answering back. Grade 11's first unit builds the concept from the devices pupils already carry: the **smartphone** above all.
2. **Smart devices around us** — Smart devices run on their own **operating systems** and **applications** — the next lessons' topics — and they **connect**: to the internet and to each other, which is what leads the book straight into the **Internet of Things**. A device earns “smart” not by having a screen, but by processing, responding and connecting.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — A phone reminds you, a watch counts your steps, a television connects to the internet by itself. We call all of them smart — but what earns the word? Iraq's Grade 11 book opens its final year with that question. This lesson is its answer. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
