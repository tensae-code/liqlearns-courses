**Acceptance count: taught in 13 countries (Ethiopia, Mozambique, Nepal, Iraq, Sudan, Egypt, Fiji, Mongolia, Kuwait, Greece, Bangladesh, Jordan, Indonesia).**

The student book §1.4 points readers to “Unit 2 Section 2.3” for cloud computing; cloud is §2.2 and §2.3 is fog computing. This lesson follows the book's own Unit 2 order (§2.2 cloud, §2.3 fog) and names the wrong cross-reference here, as the placements flag it.

Iraq's own way (Grade 11): the final preparatory year is the most forward-looking of the series — smart devices and the Internet of Things (with a lesson on choosing a computer or smartphone by its specifications), a full Excel unit ending in AutoSum, one- and two-dimensional arrays with nested loops (the series' first data structures), and electronic services: e-government, email and cloud computing with its applications.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Grade 11): the senior guide turns learners into junior system administrators and builders — computer administration, datasheets ("data books") and timing technology, documenting server and printer configurations, data science, pseudocode, Green IT, and building static web pages with tools like Canva and Figma; typing rises to 40 wpm at 85–100% accuracy. (teacher guide — evidence)

Sudan's own way (Grade 6): the final primary book is built around real Sudanese systems — networks and cloud first, then a full security unit taught through named children's everyday internet dilemmas, and a worked example of the ipSchools school-management system (with a real Bakht Al-Ruda school results table) before closing with computers in banks and police work.

Egypt's own way (Grade 7, Term 1 student book): One chapter ("Digital Programs and Applications") of 10 self-assessed lessons, each opening with "I can…" objectives and closing with Learn-by-doing; national platforms are the examples throughout — Digital Egypt platform, InstaPay, IDSC — and a separate Arabic activity booklet mirrors every lesson.

Egypt's own way (Grade 7, Term 1 workbook): A printed activity booklet is issued alongside the student book; the student book directs pupils to it by activity number after each sub-topic. The workbook is counted as its own book and mirrors the student book lesson by lesson (Lessons 1–9; no activity set for the Lesson 10 project).

Fiji's own way (Year 12 Computer Studies student book): Written locally by the same TEST/USP panel for the trialled Year 12 syllabus; the Internet chapter is grounded in Fiji (fiji.gov.fj URL anatomy with the .fj country code, and Fiji ISPs Connect Fiji, Unwired Fiji, Vodafone, Digicel and Kidanet by Fintel), programming is a full Visual Basic course with hands-on examples, and data management is taught as a move to cloud storage (Google Drive, Dropbox, SpiderOak).

Mongolia's own way (Grade 12): the final book measures everything against Mongolia itself — the ITU index and Mongolia's slipping rank in it, 36,760 km of fibre to 299 soums, the Mongolsat-1 satellite, IoT chips on livestock, telemedicine at the State Central Hospital, the ХУР state data-exchange and ТҮЦ service machines, nano-products from horse bone and ger covers — and its chapter projects are real Mongolian systems: a school ICT-development survey, a study of the ebarimt VAT-receipt system, and a Small Basic "Bouncing Ball" game; programming examples draw the alkhan khee meander with a Turtle subprogram.

Kuwait's own way (Grade 4 Digital Explorer Term 2, printed title — records name it 'Our Digital World'): the Digital World unit mixes identity (pride in Arabic language and identity online), cloud storage on the national platform (OneDrive inside Teams), and a three-lesson AI arc (data → training a machine → AI decisions) before the tool unit; PowerPoint is taught as a five-step 'Exciting Presentation' journey ending in presentations about Kuwait itself.

Greece's own way (Grade 10, Efarmoges Pliroforikis A' Lykeiou): the older Lykeio applications book in four thematic units — hardware/software and applications, programming environments and creating applications (life cycle, modern IDEs, mobile-app and 3D object-oriented programming), communication and the internet (HTML5/CSS, learning online), and collaboration and safety online (cloud applications, telework, social networks, privacy).

Bangladesh's own way (HSC ICT, Classes XI–XII and Alim): one compulsory ICT book for every HSC and Alim student in the country, saturated with Bangladesh itself: Bangabandhu Satellite-1 and its ground stations, bdjobs and chakri.com job portals, Daraz and Bikroy e-commerce, the Walton factory's automated production line, the National ID card used to teach primary keys and its database used to teach RDBMS at work, a full Bangla Unicode table and a worked example of putting Bangla text on a web page with meta charset, the national letter-grade scale (A+ from 80) as the else-if programme, "I Love Bangladesh" as the loop example text, an algorithm for reaching college by bicycle or rickshaw, and the Digital Bangladesh programme — National Data Centre, hi-tech parks, Startup Bangladesh and Bangladesh Bank's QR-based Cashless Bangladesh. C is the HSC programming language, taught with scanf/printf in Code::Blocks.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 11 G11T2 Student): Cloud services are taught through the two commercial providers students can actually open — Microsoft Azure and Google Cloud — before data security in the cloud; the AI unit goes from expert systems through machine learning and NLP to modelling and simulation.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 7 G7 Informatika student): Kurikulum Merdeka Informatika built on four elements (Berpikir Komputasional, Literasi Digital, Analisis Data, Algoritma dan Pemrograman), mixing plugged and unplugged activities with coded activity IDs (e.g. VII-BK, VII-LD) and QR-linked resources; Grade 7 covers only BK + Literasi Digital + data handling.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 12 G12 Informatika student): Grade 12 (original Kurikulum Merdeka edition) is built around making things: single-board computers/controllers and a full Arduino C course (IDE, UnoArduSim simulator, sensors, S.O.S. LED and melody projects), then networks with cyber security and data governance, social impact with the UU ITE law, and a cross-field group project (smart trash bin / waste-bank apps) with journals, reports and peer assessment.
