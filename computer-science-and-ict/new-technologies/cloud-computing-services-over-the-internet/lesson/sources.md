**Acceptance count: taught in 6 countries (Ethiopia, Mozambique, Nepal, Iraq, Sudan, Egypt).**

The student book §1.4 points readers to “Unit 2 Section 2.3” for cloud computing; cloud is §2.2 and §2.3 is fog computing. This lesson follows the book's own Unit 2 order (§2.2 cloud, §2.3 fog) and names the wrong cross-reference here, as the placements flag it.

Iraq's own way (Grade 11): the final preparatory year is the most forward-looking of the series — smart devices and the Internet of Things (with a lesson on choosing a computer or smartphone by its specifications), a full Excel unit ending in AutoSum, one- and two-dimensional arrays with nested loops (the series' first data structures), and electronic services: e-government, email and cloud computing with its applications.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Grade 11): the senior guide turns learners into junior system administrators and builders — computer administration, datasheets ("data books") and timing technology, documenting server and printer configurations, data science, pseudocode, Green IT, and building static web pages with tools like Canva and Figma; typing rises to 40 wpm at 85–100% accuracy. (teacher guide — evidence)

Sudan's own way (Grade 6): the final primary book is built around real Sudanese systems — networks and cloud first, then a full security unit taught through named children's everyday internet dilemmas, and a worked example of the ipSchools school-management system (with a real Bakht Al-Ruda school results table) before closing with computers in banks and police work.

Egypt's own way (Grade 7, Term 1 student book): One chapter ("Digital Programs and Applications") of 10 self-assessed lessons, each opening with "I can…" objectives and closing with Learn-by-doing; national platforms are the examples throughout — Digital Egypt platform, InstaPay, IDSC — and a separate Arabic activity booklet mirrors every lesson.

Egypt's own way (Grade 7, Term 1 workbook): A printed activity booklet is issued alongside the student book; the student book directs pupils to it by activity number after each sub-topic. The workbook is counted as its own book and mirrors the student book lesson by lesson (Lessons 1–9; no activity set for the Lesson 10 project).
