# Cloud computing — services over the internet, not on your PC

## Do it first

Think of your email. Where are those messages right now — inside your phone, or somewhere else entirely? You can read them from any phone, any computer, any town. They do not live on your device. They live in the cloud — and this lesson says exactly what that means.

## Sub lesson 1 — What cloud computing is

**Cloud computing** is **storing and accessing data and computing services over the internet instead of on your personal computer**.

What is on offer there: **servers, storage, networking and databases, available on demand**, with **many users served from a single data centre** and **no location limit on access** — the service is where the internet is, not where your desk is.

[Picture 30107 — cloud computing, not on your PC]

Everyday examples: **Gmail, Google Maps, Amazon, Netflix, PayPal, Spotify, Adobe Creative Cloud and Kindle**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Where the word “cloud” comes from (teacher guide — evidence) — and saving a file there

Why is it called a cloud at all? The guide gives the origin the student text does not: **network engineers drew networks as a cloud shape, to stand for the location of the various network devices and how they interconnect — and the name stuck** to the computing model.

[Picture 30123 — where the word cloud comes from]

The practised skill that goes with it: **save a file from a school computer up into a cloud folder**, so it can be reached from anywhere.
