# Lesson plan — Cloud computing — services over the internet, not on your PC

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** New Technologies · **Lesson:** Cloud computing — services over the internet, not on your PC
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Emerging Technologies (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** cloud computing is storing and accessing data and computing services over the internet instead of on your personal computer — and the guide tells where the word 'cloud' itself came from.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the emerging-technologies doorway. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or in steps — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **What cloud computing is** — data and services over the internet, not on your PC; servers, storage, networking and databases on demand, many users from one data centre, no location limit; the everyday examples.
2. **Where the word “cloud” comes from (teacher guide — evidence) — and saving a file there** — engineers drew the network as a cloud; the name stuck. Then the practised skill: save a file from your computer up into a cloud folder.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the other lessons of this unit. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define cloud computing.
2. Name what the cloud offers: servers, storage, networking, databases, on demand.
3. Name everyday cloud examples.
4. Retell where the word 'cloud' comes from (teacher guide — evidence).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the cloud is the sky or the weather → it is data centres reached over the internet; 'cloud' is the engineers' drawing, kept as a name. Thinks files in the cloud are 'nowhere' → they sit on the provider's servers, in a data centre.
