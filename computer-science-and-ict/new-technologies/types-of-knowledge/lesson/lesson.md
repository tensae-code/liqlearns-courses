# Types of Knowledge

## Do it first

Before a machine can hold knowledge, the knowledge must be sorted: knowing that, knowing how, and knowing that you know are different goods. This lesson sorts them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Facts and procedures — knowing that, knowing how

**Declarative knowledge** is knowing **that**: facts and relations — Paris is in France, fever accompanies flu. **Procedural knowledge** is knowing **how**: the steps and skills — how to take a temperature, how to prove a theorem. A knowledge base needs both, held differently: facts as statements it can check, procedures as methods it can run. Confusing them is a beginner's fault — a system stuffed with facts about swimming cannot swim.

In your mind's eye: facts and procedures — knowing that, knowing how.

## Sub lesson 2 — Deeper types — heuristics, meta, tacit

Beyond the pair: **heuristic knowledge** — rules of thumb from experience ('a cough with this sound is usually…'); **meta-knowledge** — knowledge about knowledge: what the system knows, and what it doesn't; and **tacit knowledge** — what the expert does but cannot say, the kind knowledge engineers struggle most to capture. Jordan's lesson gives the pupil the sorter's eye: any knowledge a system is to hold must first be met in its own kind, because each kind has its own way of being written down.

In your mind's eye: deeper types — heuristics, meta, tacit.
