## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Facts and procedures — knowing that, knowing how** — **Declarative knowledge** is knowing **that**: facts and relations — Paris is in France, fever accompanies flu. **Procedural knowledge** is knowing **how**: the steps and skills — how to take a temperature, how to prove a theorem. A knowledge base needs both, held differently: facts as statements it can check, procedures as methods it can run. Confusing them is a beginner's fault — a system stuffed with facts about swimming cannot swim.
2. **Deeper types — heuristics, meta, tacit** — Beyond the pair: **heuristic knowledge** — rules of thumb from experience ('a cough with this sound is usually…'); **meta-knowledge** — knowledge about knowledge: what the system knows, and what it doesn't; and **tacit knowledge** — what the expert does but cannot say, the kind knowledge engineers struggle most to capture. Jordan's lesson gives the pupil the sorter's eye: any knowledge a system is to hold must first be met in its own kind, because each kind has its own way of being written down.
