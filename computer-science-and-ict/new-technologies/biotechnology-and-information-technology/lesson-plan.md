## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **From fermentation to genetic engineering** — **Biotechnology** uses living things and their processes to make products, and it is as old as its first product: **ancient fermentation** — bread, yoghurt, and Mongolia's own airag — was biotechnology before the word. The modern turn works at the level of **genes**: **genetic engineering** reads and edits the instructions of life itself, breeding and curing by design rather than by patience. Between the churn and the gene editor stands one continuous craft: persuading life to work for people, with rising precision.
2. **Fields, bioinformatics, and Mongolia's policy** — The craft serves named **fields**: **medicine** (vaccines, insulin, diagnostics), **food** (fermentation and improved strains), **agriculture** (hardier crops and livestock) and the **environment** (living clean-up of wastes). Its meeting with this course is **bioinformatics**: life's information — gene sequences grown past any human reading — is stored, searched and compared by computers; biology's data became an information science. Mongolia's stake is dated: a national **biotech policy followed since 1986**, in a livestock-and-steppe country whose wealth is substantially alive — the lesson's close is that for Mongolia, biotechnology is not an imported fashion but a sector of the national household, and its information half is taught in this book because its other half grazes outside.
