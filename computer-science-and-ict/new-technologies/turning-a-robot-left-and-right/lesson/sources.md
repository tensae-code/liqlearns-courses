Provenance scope note: the placements stocked here are from Kazakhstan's okulyk.kz (OKULYK) textbooks, recommended by the Ministry of Education and Science of the Republic of Kazakhstan — the Digital Literacy and Informatika lines of the Almatykitap, Arman-PV and Atamura teams and the Nazarbayev Intellectual Schools (NIS) books, all student books except the two Kadirkulov teacher guides named in the entries below, whose placements are stocked exactly as filed. Many books exist in parallel language editions (Kazakh / Russian / Uzbek / Uyghur / English) of the same textbook: each distinct book is taught once, and every distinct edition is named and cited in the book entry. Kazakhstan's own ways are kept: one textbook line in up to five parallel language editions, the okulyk.kz digital library as the books' home, Scratch from Grade 1 and robotics strands (LEGO WeDo 2.0 and Mindstorms EV3) from Grade 1–2, and every keyword given in Kazakh–Russian–English in the early grades. Media slots in lesson/lesson.md are described slots only ([Picture — …]); no picture numbers are invented.


Kazakhstan's own way (Kobdikova G1 Digital Literacy): every keyword is given in three languages (Kazakh–Russian–English); the same national curriculum frame as the Almatykitap Grade 1 book (information etiquette / programming / robots, themes "Journey" and "Traditions and folklore") but this Arman-PV team teaches robotics on LEGO Mindstorms Education EV3 instead of WeDo 2.0, and Scratch through Kazakh folklore heroes (Aldar Kose, Makta Kyz) and national instruments (dombyra, kobyz, zhetigen); each project lesson is tiered A/B/C by difficulty.

Kazakhstan's own way (Sagimbaeva G1 Digital Literacy): Grade 1 digital literacy is organised by cross-cutting themes ("Journey"; "Traditions and folklore" — a linear algorithm is taught through setting up a yurt); Scratch programming and Lego Education WeDo 2.0 robotics already start in Grade 1; lessons carry QR codes to videos.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G5 Informatika): Grade 5 Informatika by the Almatykitap team drops Scratch entirely: information theory first (transmission, encryption, binary, graphics in binary code), then raster work in Paint against vector work with curves, and half the year is EV3 robotics beyond simple movement — the gyroscopic sensor, line following and robo-sumo competitions.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kobdikova G5 Informatika): the Arman-PV Grade 5 book teaches the same five national sections as the other two publishers, but every second paragraph is a practicum or a "creating a project" lesson, and the safety section ends in cloud skills — placing, changing and downloading shared-access files — plus mini-projects on setting passwords on documents and preparing a document for print. The vector editor is Inkscape (named in the glossary), the raster editor Paint.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukhametzhanova G5 Informatika, 2020 edition): the Atamura team's 2020 Grade 5 book follows the same five national sections in numbered-paragraph form (1.1–5.5) with end-of-section final tasks; its graphics strand works in Paint for raster and Inkscape for vector (canvas size, SVG, Bezier pen), and the introduction also promises a first taste of 3D technologies (3D model, 3D editor, 3D printing) inside the graphics work.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G2 teacher guide «Цифровая грамотность. Методическое руководство» (Russian + Kazakh editions)): the guide teaches the same Grade 2 year as full scripted lesson plans (objectives referenced to the state programme codes, e.g. 2.1.3.1, differentiated tasks for all/most/some pupils, formative-assessment notes); its section names differ slightly from the student books («Текст и мультимедиа», «Роботы в нашей жизни»), and it adds lessons the student books fold together — robot wheel and movement blocks, the touch sensor, and exchanging data between programmes.

Kazakhstan's own way (Sagimbaeva G1 «Цифровая грамотность»): two cartoon guides (Arman and Saule, themselves first-graders) lead the year; lessons are told as a journey, QR codes open lesson videos, and from lesson 10 the class builds and programmes LEGO-style robots (windmill model, labyrinth runs).

Kazakhstan's own way (Kobdikova G1 «Цифрлық сауаттылық»): the Arman-PV Grade 1 book teaches programming through Scratch from §6 (creating a project, the «Қош келдің, Наурыз!» project, saving/opening a project, a «Музыкалық аспаптар» project) where the Almatykitap book uses an unnamed game environment, and its safety section adds a lesson on unwanted contacts online.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kobdikova G4 «Ақпараттық-коммуникациялық технологиялар»): Grade 4 ICT is five project-driven sections with cross-cutting patriotic/cultural themes: a Scratch game the pupil designs from scenario to finished game, LEGO robotics with colour and ultrasonic sensors ending in labyrinth and kegelring challenges, video making in VideoPad, presentations with sound/video/animation, and a closing «Компьютеры будущего» section that is really about sending data, e-mail attachments and password strength; AR (augmented reality) tasks run through the book via a companion CD/app.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G5 «Информатика»): Grade 5 in the Almatykitap line is a robotics year: after information representation (encryption, binary, binary-coded graphics) and raster/vector graphics, two full sections run LEGO robots — gyro sensor, turns, line following and robo-sumo competitions — before a closing safety section that ends with shared folders and mini-projects. Every term is glossed in three languages (Kazakh/Russian/English) in the margin.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukametzhanova G5 «Информатика» 2020 edition): the 2020 Atamura revision of the Grade 5 book is rebuilt on the renewed programme — the same five sections as the Almatykitap book (Book 18): presenting information with encryption and binary, raster and vector graphics with curves, robotics with the gyroscope sensor and turns, robot competitions (line following and robo-sumo), and computer safety moved to the end.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kobdikova G5 «Информатика»): the Arman-PV Grade 5 book is the practicum twin of the same programme as Books 18 and 35 — nearly every unit comes in a theory + practice pair, and it adds a block the others lack: sharing files (placing, changing and downloading shared files), setting a password on documents, and organising a document effectively for print, taught as "mini-project works".
