# Data Visualisation

## Do it first

The eye is the fastest analyst ever built — if the data is drawn honestly. This lesson is visualisation: the craft of turning tables into seeing.

## Sub lesson 1 — What visualisation does

**Data visualisation** represents data **graphically** — charts, maps, diagrams — so patterns the table hides become visible: the trend in a line, the outlier standing off its group, the season rising and falling. A good visualisation is not decoration on the analysis; it **is** analysis — the moment of seeing is often the moment of finding. Its first rule is the match: the picture's form (line, bar, pie, map) must fit the data's shape and the question asked, as the chart lessons teach.

## Sub lesson 2 — Honest pictures — the craft's rules

The craft's rules guard the eye's trust: **scales start honestly** (a bar's axis from zero), **labels and units** say what is drawn, **one picture, one message** (a chart arguing three things argues none), and **nothing drawn that the data does not hold** — no 3D tilts fattening one slice, no smoothed line through wild data. Jordan's Grade 10 visualisation lesson is the big-data unit's conscience: data too big to read is data too easy to draw falsely, so the rules are taught with the tools, in the same lesson.
