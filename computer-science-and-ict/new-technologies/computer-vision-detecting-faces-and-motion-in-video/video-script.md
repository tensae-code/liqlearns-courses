# Video script — Computer Vision — Detecting Faces and Motion in Video

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

The first experiment is **detection** alone, practised as a skill: run the detector over **images** — class photos, portraits, crowds — and read its results honestly: faces found, faces missed (turned, shadowed, tiny), and false faces (patterns that are not people). Kuwait runs it as an experiment, with results recorded, because a detector's worth is not its best picture but its **rate** — and a pupil who has counted the misses understands every vision claim made to them afterwards.
The second experiment turns to **video**: **motion detection** compares each frame with the one before, and where the picture has **changed enough**, reports **movement** — a person crossing, a door opening — without knowing what moved. It is vision at its most primitive and most useful: the camera that watches a corridor does not need to recognise anyone; it needs to know the corridor changed. The pair of experiments teaches the spectrum: detection names a thing, motion notices an event.
