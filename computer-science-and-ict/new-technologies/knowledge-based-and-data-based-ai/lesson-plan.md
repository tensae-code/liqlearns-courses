# Lesson plan — Knowledge-Based and Data-Based AI

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** New Technologies · **Lesson:** Knowledge-Based and Data-Based AI
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Germany (2026-11-14) — in the title New Technologies on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Germany's *Informatik 5 (Bayern NTG)*, because no existing lesson covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** AI is built two ways: knowledge-based systems hold a person's expertise as explicit rules and facts and reason over them, while data-based systems are given no rules at all — they find their own patterns in large amounts of data — and knowing which way a system was built tells you what it is good at and how it fails.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the AI opening of Germany's fourth strand (what AI is, taught in this course's Emerging Technologies lesson) and leads straight into the machine-learning lessons around it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. ## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Knowledge-based AI — expertise written as rules** — The expert-system shape: a knowledge base of facts and IF–THEN rules gathered from human experts, plus an inference engine that chains rules over new facts to a conclusion, and can show its reasoning. Strengths: exact, explainable. Weaknesses: collecting the knowledge is slow, and the system knows nothing that was not written down.
2. **Data-based AI — patterns found, not written** — The data-based shape: no rules are given; a learning procedure adjusts an internal model over many examples until its answers fit the data (the machine-learning idea, opened fully in the neighbouring lesson). Strengths: reaches problems nobody can write rules for (faces, speech). Weaknesses: hungry for data, silent about its reasons, and only as good as its examples.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the New Technologies lesson Emerging Technologies — and What AI Is in this course. Recommended, never forced — a learner may land on this lesson directly, and it stands alone.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Describe how a knowledge-based system stores and uses expertise, with the expert-system shape.
2. Describe how a data-based system learns its behaviour from examples instead.
3. Choose the right approach for a stated job, and give the reason.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. The hands-on asks you to write real rules for a small expert — and to feel where rule-writing runs out.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — A doctor with thirty years of experience knows thousands of small rules: this rash plus that fever means this illness. One kind of AI writes those rules down and lets the machine apply them. Another kind shows the machine ten thousand cases and lets it find its own rules. This lesson opens both machines and looks inside. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of a wall of IF–THEN rule cards and a small engine walking from card to card along a glowing chain — the student follows the glowing chain aloud and states the conclusion it reaches.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of a river of labelled example photographs flowing into a machine whose inner dials settle a little with each one — the student says what the dials stand for and why nobody can read them as rules.

## If the learner is stuck
Calls every AI 'data-based' because it is modern → the question is how the behaviour got in: written rules, or found patterns. Asks which approach is 'better' → better for what: an auditable tax decision and a face in a photograph are opposite kinds of job.
