# Expert systems — a program that imitates an expert

## Do it first

Imagine the only doctor for a rare illness lives far away. How could her knowledge still help a patient here? Jot your idea. Then read how an expert system carries an expert's knowledge inside a program.

## Sub lesson 1 — The user interface

An **expert system** is a program that **imitates a human expert** to solve problems experts normally solve. Its fields include **medical diagnosis, petroleum engineering and financial investing**. **The user interface** is its door: it takes the **user's query**, passes it on, and shows the **recommendation**.

[Picture 16506 — expert system — three parts]

## Sub lesson 2 — The knowledge base

**The knowledge base** holds the experts' knowledge stored as **if–then–else rules** — both **factual and heuristic** (rule-of-thumb) knowledge. Its **completeness and accuracy decide how well the system performs**: a missing or wrong rule means a missing or wrong answer.

[GIF 16507 — a query travelling through the system]

## Sub lesson 3 — The inference engine — and MYCIN

**The inference engine** is the **'brain'** that produces the answer from the knowledge base — it reasons over the rules to reach a recommendation. **MYCIN** is the famous early expert system: it **identifies infection-causing bacteria** and **recommends antibiotics** — a doctor's expertise, carried in rules, reaching patients the doctor never meets.
