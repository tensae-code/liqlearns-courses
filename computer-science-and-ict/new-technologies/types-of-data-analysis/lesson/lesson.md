# Types of Data Analysis

## Do it first

Four questions, four kinds of analysis: What happened? Why did it happen? What will happen? What should we do? This lesson sorts analysis by the question it answers.

## Sub lesson 1 — Descriptive and diagnostic — the past, explained

**Descriptive analysis** answers **what happened**: summaries of the past — totals, averages, charts of what was. **Diagnostic analysis** answers **why it happened**: drilling into the descriptive facts, comparing groups and periods, hunting the cause behind the number. The two are the foundation pair: no prediction stands on an unexplained past, and Jordan's unit order — description before diagnosis before prediction — is the discipline itself.

## Sub lesson 2 — Predictive and prescriptive — the future, advised

**Predictive analysis** answers **what will happen**: patterns projected forward — forecasts, risks, likely outcomes, with their uncertainty stated. **Prescriptive analysis** answers **what should we do**: it weighs possible actions against predicted outcomes and recommends — the most demanding type, and the one that spends all the others. The four types are a ladder of ambition and of risk: each rung is worth more and can be wrong in more expensive ways, which is why the ladder is climbed in order.
