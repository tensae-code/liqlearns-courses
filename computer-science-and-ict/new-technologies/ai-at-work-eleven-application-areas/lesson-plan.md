# Lesson plan — AI at work — eleven application areas

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** New Technologies · **Lesson:** AI at work — eleven application areas
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Emerging Technologies (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** AI already works in eleven named fields — shopping, autonomous cars, social media, surveillance, agriculture, customer service, healthcare, space, smart homes, banks and search engines.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Grade 11 Emerging Technologies unit around it. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or in steps — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Shopping, social media, search and customer service** — recommendations, feeds, RankBrain, chatbots.
2. **Cars, surveillance, farming and banks** — autopilot, hazard sharing, precision farming, fraud patterns.
3. **Healthcare, space and smart homes** — diagnosis and surgery, Robonaut, smoke alerts.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the other lessons of this unit. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name all eleven fields.
2. Give the named examples: Tesla autopilot, RankBrain, Robonaut.
3. Explain chatbots, precision farming and fraud-pattern spotting in one line each.
4. Explain social-media targeting and personalisation (teacher guide — evidence).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Count today: how many times did a machine recommend, recognise, translate or decide something for you? List them. Then read the eleven fields where AI works — and check your list against it. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the parts below, in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 16508]: a picture of an eleven-panel wall, one icon per named field — the student points to three panels and says the AI job in each.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 16518]: a picture of a social-media feed splitting into two different personalised feeds for two users — the student explains why the two feeds differ.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading this lesson's key words aloud — the student listens, then says each word and what it means.
3. **Say it to your companion** — explain the lesson back in your own words (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the lesson retold in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-or-false, fill-in-the-blank, short-answer — served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the doing block at the end of lesson/lesson.md.
