# Nanotechnology — What It Is

## Do it first

Every strand of Grade 12's technology survey changes one variable. Nanotechnology changes the smallest one there is: size itself. Mongolia teaches it — unusually — through products made from Mongolia's own materials.

## Sub lesson 1 — The nanometre scale — small enough to be different

**Nanotechnology** works at the **nanometre scale — 1 to 100 nanometres**, a thousand times finer than a hair's width, where matter stops behaving only by its kind and starts behaving by its size: particles gain new strengths, colours and reactivities at the nano scale. Its emblem material is the **carbon nanotube** — carbon rolled into tubes of atomic walls, stronger and lighter than the same element has any bulk right to be. Beside it stands **Moore's law**, the chip-maker's long doubling of transistors — the electronics road that itself arrives at nano dimensions, which is why this lesson lives in an IT book.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mongolia's nano products — the steppe at the nanoscale

The book's pride is local: Mongolian nano work turns the country's own materials. **Waterproof ger covers**, nano-treated, shed the rain the felt alone drinks. **Horse-bone calcium** is milled to nano fineness into a food of the bones Mongolia has in abundance. **Fireproof insulation is made from wool waste** — the steppe's surplus fibre, nano-worked into the building material a ger-and-apartment country needs against fire and winter. The lesson's claim generalises the whole Grade 12 survey: a new technology is not developed for a country when its flag is on the product, but when its products are made from the country's own ground — Mongolia's nano lesson can name three.
