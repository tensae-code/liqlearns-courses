# Lesson plan — Nanotechnology — What It Is

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** New Technologies · **Lesson:** Nanotechnology — What It Is
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Mongolia (2026-11-14) — in the title New Technologies on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Mongolia's student books (Ministry of Education, Culture and Science(s) of Mongolia, Ulaanbaatar), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree and live lesson content were checked for the slug and for near matches before building.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The development and use of nanotechnology — working at the nanometre scale of 1–100 nm, carbon nanotubes, Moore's law as its neighbour, and Mongolia's own nano products: waterproof ger covers, horse-bone calcium, fireproof insulation from wool waste
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The nanometre scale — small enough to be different** — **Nanotechnology** works at the **nanometre scale — 1 to 100 nanometres**, a thousand times finer than a hair's width, where matter stops behaving only by its kind and starts behaving by its size: particles gain new strengths, colours and reactivities at the nano scale. Its emblem material is the **carbon nanotube** — carbon rolled into tubes of atomic walls, stronger and lighter than the same element has any bulk right to be. Beside it stands **Moore's law**, the chip-maker's long doubling of transistors — the electronics road that itself arrives at nano dimensions, which is why this lesson lives in an IT book.
2. **Mongolia's nano products — the steppe at the nanoscale** — The book's pride is local: Mongolian nano work turns the country's own materials. **Waterproof ger covers**, nano-treated, shed the rain the felt alone drinks. **Horse-bone calcium** is milled to nano fineness into a food of the bones Mongolia has in abundance. **Fireproof insulation is made from wool waste** — the steppe's surplus fibre, nano-worked into the building material a ger-and-apartment country needs against fire and winter. The lesson's claim generalises the whole Grade 12 survey: a new technology is not developed for a country when its flag is on the product, but when its products are made from the country's own ground — Mongolia's nano lesson can name three.
