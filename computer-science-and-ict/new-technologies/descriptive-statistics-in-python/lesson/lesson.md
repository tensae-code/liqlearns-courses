# Descriptive Statistics in Python

## Do it first

Four hundred numbers become five: the average, the middle, the most common, the lowest, the spread. This lesson computes the five in Python — and learns what each can hide.

## Sub lesson 1 — Centre — mean, median, mode

Python's data library computes the centre in a line each: the **mean** (the total shared equally — sensitive to every value, dragged by extremes), the **median** (the middle value — unmoved by extremes), and the **mode** (the most frequent value — the only centre a kind can have). Choosing among them is the analyst's judgement, not the library's: incomes take the median, shoe sizes the mode, and reporting the mean alone for dragged data is the oldest statistical sleight.

In your mind's eye: centre — mean, median, mode.

## Sub lesson 2 — Spread and shape — range, deviation, the summary

Centre without spread lies politely: two classes can share a mean and differ in everything else. The spread measures: **range** (max − min), **standard deviation** (the typical distance from the mean — the spread with a unit and a meaning), and the quartiles. Python's `describe()` gathers the family into one summary table — count, mean, deviation, min, quartiles, max — which Jordan teaches as the analyst's first page on any new column: read it whole before quoting any one number from it.

In your mind's eye: spread and shape — range, deviation, the summary.
