## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The early stages — marking and connecting** — The story begins with **identification**: barcodes and **RFID** tags let objects carry an identity a machine could read. Then came **machine-to-machine (M2M)** communication — devices talking to devices over networks without a person in between — and the spread of **wireless** links and **IPv6**, whose vast address space meant every thing could have its own address. Each stage removed a limit: first things could be named, then they could talk, then there were addresses enough for all of them.
2. **The later stages — sensing things everywhere** — With cheap **sensors**, small processors and cloud computing, things began to **sense and report** on their own — the smart meter, the tracked lorry, the wearable. The newest stage joins IoT to **artificial intelligence**: things do not only send data, their data is analysed for prediction and automatic response. Jordan's book reads the evolution as growth in three things together — the number of connected things, what each thing can do, and how independently it can act.
