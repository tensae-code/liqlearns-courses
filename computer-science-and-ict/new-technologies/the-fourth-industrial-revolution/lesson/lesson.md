# The Fourth Industrial Revolution

## Do it first

The final book places Mongolia's future inside world history's largest frame: the revolutions that remake work. This lesson is the fourth of them — and Mongolia's stated intention not to be its spectator.

## Sub lesson 1 — Four revolutions — steam, electricity, computers, fusion

Industry has been revolutionised in waves. The **first** revolution mechanised production with **steam**. The **second** electrified it into **mass production**. The **third** automated it with **computers and electronics**. The **fourth** — **Industry 4.0** — fuses the physical, digital and biological worlds: systems of sensors, data and intelligent machines that sense, decide and act together. Each wave moved the worker's value: from muscle, to attendance, to supervision — toward judgement, design and care, the works machines absorb last.

## Sub lesson 2 — The 47% forecast — and Mongolia's Three Pillars

The book states the fourth wave's famous warning plainly: a forecast that **47% of jobs** stand at risk of automation — a photograph of exposure, not a sentence, since every past wave destroyed trades and raised new ones no forecaster had named. Mongolia's answer is policy: the **Three Pillars** development policy, by which the state intends to **start Industry 4.0 in Mongolia** rather than import its results. The Grade 12 pupil is addressed as the wave's worker either way: the lesson's question is not whether the fourth revolution comes to Mongolia, but whether Mongolians arrive in it as its operators or its audience.
