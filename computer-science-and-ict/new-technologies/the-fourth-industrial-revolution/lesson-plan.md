# Lesson plan — The Fourth Industrial Revolution

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** New Technologies · **Lesson:** The Fourth Industrial Revolution
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Mongolia (2026-11-14) — in the title New Technologies on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Mongolia's student books (Ministry of Education, Culture and Science(s) of Mongolia, Ulaanbaatar), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree and live lesson content were checked for the slug and for near matches before building.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The Fourth Industrial Revolution — the four industrial revolutions in sequence, the forecast that 47% of jobs will be automated, and Mongolia's Three Pillars policy to start its own Industry 4.0
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Four revolutions — steam, electricity, computers, fusion** — Industry has been revolutionised in waves. The **first** revolution mechanised production with **steam**. The **second** electrified it into **mass production**. The **third** automated it with **computers and electronics**. The **fourth** — **Industry 4.0** — fuses the physical, digital and biological worlds: systems of sensors, data and intelligent machines that sense, decide and act together. Each wave moved the worker's value: from muscle, to attendance, to supervision — toward judgement, design and care, the works machines absorb last.
2. **The 47% forecast — and Mongolia's Three Pillars** — The book states the fourth wave's famous warning plainly: a forecast that **47% of jobs** stand at risk of automation — a photograph of exposure, not a sentence, since every past wave destroyed trades and raised new ones no forecaster had named. Mongolia's answer is policy: the **Three Pillars** development policy, by which the state intends to **start Industry 4.0 in Mongolia** rather than import its results. The Grade 12 pupil is addressed as the wave's worker either way: the lesson's question is not whether the fourth revolution comes to Mongolia, but whether Mongolians arrive in it as its operators or its audience.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
