## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What logic is — the study of arguments** — **Logic** is the study of **arguments**: when do conclusions truly follow from premises? It examines **indicators** — the words that signal premises ('because', 'since') and conclusions ('therefore', 'so') — and separates arguments whose conclusions **must** follow (valid) from those whose merely might. Logic does not ask whether premises are true; it asks the colder question: **if** they were true, would the conclusion have to be?
2. **Logic in AI — knowledge that can conclude** — AI needs logic because a machine that knows must also **conclude**: from 'all birds fly' and 'this is a bird', the new fact must be produced by rule, not by magic. Logic gives AI its **knowledge representation** (facts as statements), its **inference** (rules for deriving new statements), and its honesty check — a conclusion drawn by valid steps from true premises cannot be false. Jordan's book walks from logic to its two working forms — propositional and predicate — in the next lessons.
