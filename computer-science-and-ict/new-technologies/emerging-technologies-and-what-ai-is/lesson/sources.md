North Macedonia's own way (Year 1): five themes move from modern digital technology and online living into a full structured-programming course taught in C++ (with Scratch/C++/Java/Python/PHP/Lisp compared), then advanced Word work (styles, contents, templates) and spreadsheets up to pivot tables.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Grade 10): Grade 10 jumps from using computers to building with them — simple electronic circuits, programming that controls an external device, robotics and artificial intelligence sit inside "Computer Architecture", while the software unit pairs operating-system installation with machine learning; typing rises to 30 wpm. (teacher guide — evidence, PARTIAL)

Egypt's own way (Grade 7, Term 2 student book): Term 2 pivots from applications to computing itself — AI, sensors and robots, then a Scratch-to-Python bridge in the same term.

Egypt's own way (Grade 8, Term 2 student book (Arabic)): Term 2 teaches AI from the neuron up — artificial neuron, its role in AI — then Python for data analysis and even encryption with XOR, a depth most countries save for secondary school.

Kuwait's own way (Grade 4 Digital Explorer Term 2, printed title — records name it 'Our Digital World'): the Digital World unit mixes identity (pride in Arabic language and identity online), cloud storage on the national platform (OneDrive inside Teams), and a three-lesson AI arc (data → training a machine → AI decisions) before the tool unit; PowerPoint is taught as a five-step 'Exciting Presentation' journey ending in presentations about Kuwait itself.

Kuwait's own way (Grade 10 Information Technology Term 2): the Grade 10 year is a Python-to-AI arc — Term 2 moves from Python data structures and functions straight into AI concepts and a hands-on OpenCV face-recognition project, with the ministry's Hamad AI assistant threaded through the front matter.

Kuwait's own way (Grade 5 Digital Explorer Term 1): Grade 5 leaves ScratchJr for full Scratch (installed and online), but first comes a values-and-AI unit — citizenship, smart vs non-smart devices, what AI is, robots and their parts — and programming itself is taught as a four-stage craft (set the goal, the smart plan, the flowchart, then the code) before a single block is dragged.

Laos's own way (Grade 10 ICT student book): the upper-secondary course turns conceptual — a full information-systems syllabus (organisations, TPS/MIS/DSS/EIS/expert/office systems, the development life cycle), then the series' deepest security chapter (firewalls, cookies, spyware, spam, employee monitoring, privacy law), closing with artificial intelligence, with DOS and Unix taught alongside Windows in the senior years' background.

Greece's own way (Grade 8, Pliroforiki B' Gymnasiou, 2024): Grade 8 moves from bits/bytes and data rates into networks, then data analysis in spreadsheets and presentations, then modular Scratch programming (own blocks with parameters, realistic character motion), list data structures, a full AI unit training a neural network, and ICT's changes to the economy (e-commerce, Industry 4.0, new jobs, sustainability).

Greece's own way (Grade 11, Eisagogi stis Arches tis Epistimis ton H/Y B' Lykeiou): a theory-first introduction to computer science in three units — basic concepts (theoretical vs applied CS), theoretical CS (problem, algorithms in depth including complexity/computability, data structures operations, debugging logic errors and documentation, programming paradigms and structured programming, software life cycle), and applied CS (operating systems in depth, information systems with databases and SQL/XML, networks, AI).

Bangladesh's own way (HSC ICT, Classes XI–XII and Alim): one compulsory ICT book for every HSC and Alim student in the country, saturated with Bangladesh itself: Bangabandhu Satellite-1 and its ground stations, bdjobs and chakri.com job portals, Daraz and Bikroy e-commerce, the Walton factory's automated production line, the National ID card used to teach primary keys and its database used to teach RDBMS at work, a full Bangla Unicode table and a worked example of putting Bangla text on a web page with meta charset, the national letter-grade scale (A+ from 80) as the else-if programme, "I Love Bangladesh" as the loop example text, an algorithm for reaching college by bicycle or rickshaw, and the Digital Bangladesh programme — National Data Centre, hi-tech parks, Startup Bangladesh and Bangladesh Bank's QR-based Cashless Bangladesh. C is the HSC programming language, taught with scanf/printf in Code::Blocks.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 12 G12T2 Student): Arabic text with every technical term also given in English in parentheses; each lesson opens with main idea, key concepts and learning outcomes and closes with self-assessment; each unit ends with unit questions and a self-checklist. Draft copy (نسخة قيد الإعداد) watermark.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 9 G9T2 Student): Cyber security is built from the CIA pillars outwards to encryption taught hands-on with the Caesar and Rail Fence ciphers; robotics is taught in a virtual simulator playground before any physical robot.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 10 G10 student): The senior-school series (Stepanyan, Torosyan, Martirosyan, Amirkhanyan, Matinyan) is built on four ideas around digital tools and collaboration; Grades 10–12 switch the programming language to C++ and go deep: Boolean logic, translators, types, code quality, debugging, procedures, recursion, divide-and-conquer, testing, development stages, AI and data.
