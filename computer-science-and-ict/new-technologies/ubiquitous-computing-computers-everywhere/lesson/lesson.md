# Ubiquitous Computing — Computers Everywhere

## Do it first

Every society lesson so far described the present. Mongolia's Grade 9 book ends its opening unit looking forward: if the information society keeps spreading, where does it stop? Its answer is a word — ubiquitous: everywhere.

## Sub lesson 1 — Computing that disappears into the world

**Ubiquitous computing** is computing **everywhere**: processors and sensors woven into objects, rooms, vehicles and streets, serving without being sat at. The computer as a box on a desk is the temporary form; the lasting form is computation dissolved into the environment — the door that knows its holder, the road that reports its traffic, the herd's collar that reports the animal. The technology's success is measured by its invisibility: when computing is truly everywhere, nobody says they are 'using a computer' at all.

In your mind's eye: computing that disappears into the world.

## Sub lesson 2 — The future it makes — promise and question

Mongolia places the vision inside its information-society unit as the society's **future**: services that arrive before they are asked for, environments that adapt to their people. The lesson is filed as foresight, not fact — the same book teaches the society's protections (the CIA triad follows in its own pages) because a world of everywhere-computing is also a world of everywhere-watching, and the future citizen of the earlier lesson will need both the vision and the guard. The book holds the two in one unit deliberately: tomorrow's technology and tomorrow's caution, taught together.

In your mind's eye: the future it makes — promise and question.
