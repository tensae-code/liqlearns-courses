# Lesson plan — Ubiquitous Computing — Computers Everywhere

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** New Technologies · **Lesson:** Ubiquitous Computing — Computers Everywhere
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Mongolia (2026-11-14) — in the title New Technologies on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Mongolia's student books (Ministry of Education, Culture and Science(s) of Mongolia, Ulaanbaatar), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree and live lesson content were checked for the slug and for near matches before building.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The future of the information society as Mongolia's Grade 9 book sees it — ubiquitous computing: computation woven into everyday objects and places until it is everywhere and nowhere noticed
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Computing that disappears into the world** — **Ubiquitous computing** is computing **everywhere**: processors and sensors woven into objects, rooms, vehicles and streets, serving without being sat at. The computer as a box on a desk is the temporary form; the lasting form is computation dissolved into the environment — the door that knows its holder, the road that reports its traffic, the herd's collar that reports the animal. The technology's success is measured by its invisibility: when computing is truly everywhere, nobody says they are 'using a computer' at all.
2. **The future it makes — promise and question** — Mongolia places the vision inside its information-society unit as the society's **future**: services that arrive before they are asked for, environments that adapt to their people. The lesson is filed as foresight, not fact — the same book teaches the society's protections (the CIA triad follows in its own pages) because a world of everywhere-computing is also a world of everywhere-watching, and the future citizen of the earlier lesson will need both the vision and the guard. The book holds the two in one unit deliberately: tomorrow's technology and tomorrow's caution, taught together.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Every society lesson so far described the present. Mongolia's Grade 9 book ends its opening unit looking forward: if the information society keeps spreading, where does it stop? Its answer is a word — ubiquitous: everywhere. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of computing that disappears into the world as the lesson describes it — the student studies the picture and names what each part shows.
   - ADD GIF: a GIF of the future it makes — promise and question in action — the student watches once, then says what changed and why.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the lesson's key names and definitions aloud, in the order the lesson gives them — the student listens once through, then reads the same lines aloud along with the voice. (Audio is a described slot in this build; no audio file is made.)
3. **Say it to your companion** — the companion block in lesson/lesson.md.
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the lesson in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-false, matching, short-answer — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the doing block at the end of lesson/lesson.md.
