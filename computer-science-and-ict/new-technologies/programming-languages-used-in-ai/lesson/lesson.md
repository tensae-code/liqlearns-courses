# Programming Languages Used in AI

## Do it first

Grade 11's AI chapter ends with the workshop question: in what languages is this work actually written? The answer has a history — languages invented FOR intelligence, and the general languages that adopted it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The classic AI languages — Lisp and Prolog

AI's founding languages were built for its own kind of data. **Lisp** (from *list processing*) treats **lists** as its native material and programs as lists themselves — the language of the first AI decades, still the ancestor of how AI represents knowledge and rules. **Prolog** is the **logical** language: the programmer states **facts and rules**, and the system **answers questions by reasoning** over them — the expert-systems work of the AI-at-work lessons written in its own tongue.

## Sub lesson 2 — The modern working set — Python and the data languages

Today's AI is largely written in **general languages with great AI libraries**: **Python** above all — the course's own language grown up, carrying the machine-learning and data work of the new-technologies strand — with languages of **data and statistics** beside it and the fast systems languages underneath, where the libraries' heavy arithmetic actually runs. The chapter's judgement is the durable one: languages rise and fall; what lasts is knowing **which kind of language a problem wants** — symbolic for reasoning, data-rich for learning.
