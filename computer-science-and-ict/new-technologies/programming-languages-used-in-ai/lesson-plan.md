## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The classic AI languages — Lisp and Prolog** — AI's founding languages were built for its own kind of data. **Lisp** (from *list processing*) treats **lists** as its native material and programs as lists themselves — the language of the first AI decades, still the ancestor of how AI represents knowledge and rules. **Prolog** is the **logical** language: the programmer states **facts and rules**, and the system **answers questions by reasoning** over them — the expert-systems work of the AI-at-work lessons written in its own tongue.
2. **The modern working set — Python and the data languages** — Today's AI is largely written in **general languages with great AI libraries**: **Python** above all — the course's own language grown up, carrying the machine-learning and data work of the new-technologies strand — with languages of **data and statistics** beside it and the fast systems languages underneath, where the libraries' heavy arithmetic actually runs. The chapter's judgement is the durable one: languages rise and fall; what lasts is knowing **which kind of language a problem wants** — symbolic for reasoning, data-rich for learning.
