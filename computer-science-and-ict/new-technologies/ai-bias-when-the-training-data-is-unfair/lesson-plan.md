## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Bias comes from the data** — A learning system is built from its **training data**: it finds the patterns the data contains — including the data's unfairness. If the examples under-represent a group, skew toward one place or period, or encode yesterday's prejudices, the system learns a **bias (biais)**: a systematic tilt in its answers. Every Décodage introduction carries the same warning from age six upward: attention aux biais — the machine's mistakes often begin as our data's mistakes.
2. **Correlation is not causation** — The introductions pair bias with its twin trap: **correlation is not causation (corrélation n'est pas causalité)**. A system may learn that two things rise together and treat one as the other's cause — ice-cream sales and drowning both rise in summer, caused by a third thing neither system nor child should miss: the heat. A correlation is a hint to investigate, never a proof; acting on it as a cause builds unfair and foolish systems.
