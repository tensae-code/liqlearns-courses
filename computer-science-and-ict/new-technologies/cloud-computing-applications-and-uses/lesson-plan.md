**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. ## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The everyday uses** — The cloud's common applications: **storage and backup** (files safe beyond one device), **email and office work** (documents shared and edited together), **hosting** (websites and apps without owning servers), **streaming** (music and film delivered, not possessed), and **software by subscription** (the SaaS of daily life). Each use spends the cloud's same gifts — anywhere access, stretching capacity, and someone else's machinery maintained for you.
2. **Uses in the fields** — Field by field: **education** (classes, files and learning platforms reaching any home), **health** (records shared between clinics, with the strictest privacy duties), **business** (shops and banks scaling at their busy seasons), **government** (services online for every citizen), and **science** (renting a thousand computers for a week's calculation). Jordan's closing point: the fields differ in their duties — health's privacy is not a shop's — but the cloud's shape serves them all, which is why it became the default ground of new systems.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
