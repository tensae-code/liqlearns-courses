# My Robot Draws Shapes

## Do it first

Walk a square on the floor counting your steps: same steps each side, same corner turn each time. Now walk a long thin rectangle: only the side lengths changed. A robot draws exactly this way.

## My Robot Draws Shapes

**"Моят робот и фигурите" (My robot and the shapes)** gives the Blue-Bot a drawing job: drive the **shapes (фигури)**. A square is four equal **steps** and four equal **quarter-turns** — the robot-artist lesson and the pen-drawing lesson, now with wheels on a mat. A rectangle keeps the turns and changes two step-counts; a shape that will not close has a step or a turn that disagrees with its opposite — and the fix is found by **reading the program back**, comparing side with side, turn with turn. Planning happens in robot language before the robot moves: steps and turns written in order, the whole shape walked by a finger on the mat first. The pride of the lesson is exactness you can see: a closed shape, drawn by a machine, from a program you wrote.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: A floor robot draws shapes by driving them: equal steps and equal quarter-turns make a square; change the steps and turns deliberately, and new shapes come — planned in robot language first. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: program the robot to drive a square: equal steps, equal quarter-turns. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: change step-counts deliberately to drive a rectangle. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: find why a shape will not close by reading the program back. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
