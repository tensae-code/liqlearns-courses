# Working with a Programmable Device

## Do it first

Press the buttons of a toy that beeps and moves: you gave it instructions, and the thing itself obeyed. That is a programmable device — a program you can touch.

## Working with a Programmable Device

Scratch sprites live on a screen. This lesson leaves the screen: a **programmable device (програмируемо устройство)** is a physical machine built to receive a **program of commands** and act on them in the real world — moving, turning, beeping, lighting. Working with one follows the same rhythm as all the programming before it, with real-world weight: plan the commands, enter them in order, run them, watch what the device **actually does**, and correct the program — not the device — when the action is wrong. The book treats this as the learner's first robotics ground: what such a device is, how a program gets into it, and how carefully commands must be given when a mistake moves a real object into a real chair leg.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: A programmable device is a real machine that does what a program tells it: give it commands in order, and it acts in the real world — not on a screen. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: say what a programmable device is. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: give a device a short program of commands in order. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: correct the program — not the device — when the action is wrong. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
