**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** New Technologies · **Lesson:** Electronics — Development and Use
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Mongolia (2026-11-14) — in the title New Technologies on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). **The one thing this lesson teaches:** The development and use of electronics — from Edison's lamp to the analogue-against-digital signal divide, what an electronics engineer does, and robots assembling the motherboards every other lesson in the course depends on
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Edison's lamp to the signal divide** — **Electronics** began as light and heat in wires: **Edison's lamp** stands at the head of the book's road — the first mass electric device, and the valve technology that followed it learned to control currents, the act every electronic device still performs. The road's great fork is the **signal divide**: **analogue** signals vary continuously, a wave copying the world; **digital** signals step between states, a code standing for the world. The whole course lives on the digital side of that fork — this lesson is where the fork itself is finally named, in the book's last technology chapter.
2. **The electronics engineer — and the robots on the line** — The field's human face is the **electronics engineer**: designing circuits, choosing components, testing signals — the profession that stands behind every device the course has touched. Its modern face is stranger: **robots assembling motherboards**, machines placing the components of new machines at speeds and accuracies no hand holds — electronics manufacturing electronics. Mongolia closes the survey with the loop made visible: the course began with information in the abstract and ends with the physical line where information's machines are born, built by their own kind.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
