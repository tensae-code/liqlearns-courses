# Propositional Logic and Predicate Logic

## Do it first

Logic has two toolboxes: one treats whole statements as atoms; the other opens the atom and finds objects and properties inside. This lesson learns both, and why AI needs the second.

## Sub lesson 1 — Propositional logic — whole statements, joined

**Propositional logic** works with **propositions** — whole statements that are true or false — joined by **connectives**: AND, OR, NOT, IF–THEN. 'It rains AND the ground is wet' is built from two propositions and a connective, and its truth is computed from theirs. Its limit is its blindness inside statements: 'all birds fly' and 'some birds fly' are, to it, just two unrelated atoms.

## Sub lesson 2 — Predicate logic — inside the statement

**Predicate logic** opens the statement: **predicates** say things about **objects** — Bird(x), Flies(x) — and **quantifiers** speak of all (∀) or some (∃): ∀x (Bird(x) → Flies(x)). Now a machine can reason about every bird at once and about this bird in particular — the representation AI's knowledge bases actually use. Jordan's pair closes with the trade: propositional logic is simpler and decidable; predicate logic is expressive enough for real knowledge, and its reasoning costs more.
