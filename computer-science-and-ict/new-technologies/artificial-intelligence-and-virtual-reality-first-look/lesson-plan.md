**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Artificial intelligence — machines that imitate understanding** — **Artificial intelligence (AI)** is the building of computer systems that perform jobs which, done by a person, would be called intelligent — **understanding language, recognising pictures, learning from examples, making decisions**. Fiji's first look is careful: the machine imitates the *result* of understanding by working on patterns in great amounts of data — it does not understand the way a person does. Even at this depth the learner takes away the shape of the field: AI is software, built by people, strong at pattern jobs, blind outside them.
2. **Virtual reality — a world made of software** — **Virtual reality (VR)** is a **computer-made world** a person steps into — usually through a headset that fills the eyes and ears — and can **look around and act in**, as if it were a place. Its school uses are practice without danger: flying a plane that cannot crash, walking a building not yet built, visiting a reef from a classroom in Suva. Fiji pairs AI and VR as the advanced horizon of Year 11 for one reason: both stretch what 'application software' means — one stretches what the program can *do*, the other stretches where the user can *be*.

This lesson is a first look at both, at the depth a first look should have. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
