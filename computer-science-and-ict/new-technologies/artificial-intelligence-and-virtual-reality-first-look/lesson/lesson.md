# Artificial Intelligence and Virtual Reality — First Look

## Do it first

Two names end Fiji's Year 11 advanced applications — the two that point past the course's own time: artificial intelligence, machines that act as if they understand, and virtual reality, places that exist only inside the machine. This lesson is a first look at both, at the depth a first look should have.

## Sub lesson 1 — Artificial intelligence — machines that imitate understanding

**Artificial intelligence (AI)** is the building of computer systems that perform jobs which, done by a person, would be called intelligent — **understanding language, recognising pictures, learning from examples, making decisions**. Fiji's first look is careful: the machine imitates the *result* of understanding by working on patterns in great amounts of data — it does not understand the way a person does. Even at this depth the learner takes away the shape of the field: AI is software, built by people, strong at pattern jobs, blind outside them.

## Sub lesson 2 — Virtual reality — a world made of software

**Virtual reality (VR)** is a **computer-made world** a person steps into — usually through a headset that fills the eyes and ears — and can **look around and act in**, as if it were a place. Its school uses are practice without danger: flying a plane that cannot crash, walking a building not yet built, visiting a reef from a classroom in Suva. Fiji pairs AI and VR as the advanced horizon of Year 11 for one reason: both stretch what 'application software' means — one stretches what the program can *do*, the other stretches where the user can *be*.
