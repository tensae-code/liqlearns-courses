# Big data challenges — growth, people, security, mixing sources

## Do it first

The last lessons sounded like winning. Now argue the other side. You run the data store. It doubles every few months. Nobody on your team is trained for this work. The security update is still on next month's list — and head office just sent you five new data sources to merge in. You have just met this lesson's four challenges.

## Sub lesson 1 — The four challenges

**Managing growth** — data sets **grow quickly and are hard to store and manage** in data centres and databases.

**Lack of data professionals** — **data scientists, analysts and engineers are in demand**. The book gives pupils the career hint directly: these are jobs the world is short of.

**Securing data** — security is **often postponed** while organisations focus on storing and analysing, **leaving repositories open to intruders**.

**Integrating data from many sources** — social pages, company software, log files, transactions, email, presentations, reports. The work is **choosing what is most relevant, to save time and money** — with **data quality and validation** alongside.

## Sub lesson 2 — The guide's limits — when data misleads (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide keys the limits just as concretely: **storing huge volumes on traditional storage is costly**; analysing massive data **can violate privacy standards and can alter client records**; it **must be studied for a long time before the benefits come**; its **results are sometimes false**; and **decision-makers who lean on it too heavily and set aside experience and judgement make bad choices**.
