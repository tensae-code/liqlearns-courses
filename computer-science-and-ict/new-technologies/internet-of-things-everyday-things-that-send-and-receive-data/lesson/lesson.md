# Internet of Things — everyday things that send and receive data

## Do it first

Walk your kitchen and your street in your mind. A fridge that reports it is nearly empty. A water meter that reads itself. A cow whose collar reports where she grazes. None of these is a computer in the usual sense — yet all of them could collect and exchange data. That is this lesson's world.

## What IoT is — and what counts as a “thing”

The **Internet of Things (IoT)** is a **network of physical objects or people — “things” — equipped with software, electronics, networks and sensors to collect and exchange data**.

It **extends internet connectivity beyond computers and smartphones** to **home and business devices that can be controlled remotely across a network**, **cutting human effort** and — through **data collection, AI algorithms and networks** — making things **virtually smart**.

A thing can be **a toaster, a person with a diabetes monitor implant, an animal with a tracking device, an AC sensor adjusting temperature, or a smart wearable**.

And the book's rule decides every borderline case: **when something can send and/or receive information over the internet, it becomes smart**.
