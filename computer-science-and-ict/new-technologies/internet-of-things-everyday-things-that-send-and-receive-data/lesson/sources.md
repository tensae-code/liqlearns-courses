**Acceptance count: taught in 12 countries (Ethiopia, Nepal, Iraq, Sudan, Egypt, Sri Lanka, Kuwait, Greece, Bangladesh, Jordan, Indonesia, Vietnam).**

Printed p. 48 frames the Internet as “created in the late 1990s and is now more than 30 years old” — the book's own inconsistent framing (a late-1990s creation would not yet be 30 years old at the book's writing, and the framing sits oddly against the internet's longer history). This lesson teaches the IoT topics as the book teaches them and flags the inconsistency here rather than silently rewriting the book, exactly as the placements direct.

Iraq's own way (Grade 11): the final preparatory year is the most forward-looking of the series — smart devices and the Internet of Things (with a lesson on choosing a computer or smartphone by its specifications), a full Excel unit ending in AutoSum, one- and two-dimensional arrays with nested loops (the series' first data structures), and electronic services: e-government, email and cloud computing with its applications.

Sudan's own way (Grade 7): the middle-stage book spends half its length on one database built step by step in Microsoft Access (tables, relationships, forms, queries, reports on a pupils-and-subjects database), then teaches the internet through Sudanese examples — the DNS lesson resolves the Khartoum Ministry of Education site (moekh.gov.sd).

Egypt's own way (Grade 8, Term 1 student book): Two units — "Artificial Intelligence and Protecting Our Digital Data" and "Website Design and Creation" — ending with pupils creating their own school website with AI help.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 9 Workbook): a companion workbook mirroring the Reading Book chapter-for-chapter.

Kuwait's own way (Grade 10 Information Technology Term 2): the Grade 10 year is a Python-to-AI arc — Term 2 moves from Python data structures and functions straight into AI concepts and a hands-on OpenCV face-recognition project, with the ministry's Hamad AI assistant threaded through the front matter.

Greece's own way (Grade 7, Pliroforiki A' Gymnasiou, 2024): the new Gymnasium informatics course is built around algorithms from the first page (a Python binary example in Unit 1), then hardware/software, the internet with a full copyright/Creative Commons/AI-copyright sequence, cybersecurity with cryptography, word processing, algorithmics, Scratch storytelling/chatbot projects, and a long society unit (robots, drones, autonomous cars, e-government, AI in education/medicine).

Switzerland's own way (Grades 7–8, Décodage 7e–8e): at 7e–8e the Décodage course becomes a real computer-science course under the Plan d'études romand EN 22/23: Thymio VPL missions (including Morse communication and self-driving-car autonomy levels), a full Scratch discovery course (movement, shortening programs, sub-programs as own blocks, remixing), a Scratch video-game project run in “studios” with a cahier des charges, data work (Huffman-style compression via Zip la marmotte, binary/octal/hex/ASCII codings, file sizes), access security (passwords, file encryption, phishing, authentication), home networks at two levels (IP addresses; message routing, packets, TCP and encryption), and inquiries into sorting, sorting networks, Caesar decryption and parity-bit error detection. Médias/Usages covers social networks and digital identity/footprint, information sources, cyberbullying and the environmental cost of digital technology.

Bangladesh's own way (HSC ICT, Classes XI–XII and Alim): one compulsory ICT book for every HSC and Alim student in the country, saturated with Bangladesh itself: Bangabandhu Satellite-1 and its ground stations, bdjobs and chakri.com job portals, Daraz and Bikroy e-commerce, the Walton factory's automated production line, the National ID card used to teach primary keys and its database used to teach RDBMS at work, a full Bangla Unicode table and a worked example of putting Bangla text on a web page with meta charset, the national letter-grade scale (A+ from 80) as the else-if programme, "I Love Bangladesh" as the loop example text, an algorithm for reaching college by bicycle or rickshaw, and the Digital Bangladesh programme — National Data Centre, hi-tech parks, Startup Bangladesh and Bangladesh Bank's QR-based Cashless Bangladesh. C is the HSC programming language, taught with scanf/printf in Code::Blocks.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 12 G12T2 Student): Arabic text with every technical term also given in English in parentheses; each lesson opens with main idea, key concepts and learning outcomes and closes with self-assessment; each unit ends with unit questions and a self-checklist. Draft copy (نسخة قيد الإعداد) watermark.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 10 G10T2 Student): IoT is taught at Grade 10 through the smart home — components and functions first, then network design, data collection and IoT security with practical network-design applications; the AI unit approaches AI through knowledge bases and formal logic (propositional/predicate logic, truth tables) before nanotechnology and holograms.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 12 G12 Informatika student): Grade 12 (original Kurikulum Merdeka edition) is built around making things: single-board computers/controllers and a full Arduino C course (IDE, UnoArduSim simulator, sensors, S.O.S. LED and melody projects), then networks with cyber security and data governance, social impact with the UU ITE law, and a cross-field group project (smart trash bin / waste-bank apps) with journals, reports and peer assessment.

Vietnam's own way (Grade 9 Tin học 9): Grade 9 teaches simulation software (PhET, Geometer's Sketchpad) as a way to learn by experiment, and its applied fork is advanced Excel (data validation, COUNTIF/SUMIF/IF, a family-finance workbook project) versus a full video-production track in Video Editor from storyboard to export.
