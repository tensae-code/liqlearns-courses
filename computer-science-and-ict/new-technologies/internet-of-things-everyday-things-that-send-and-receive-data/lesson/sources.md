**Acceptance count: taught in 4 countries (Ethiopia, Nepal, Iraq, Sudan).**

Printed p. 48 frames the Internet as “created in the late 1990s and is now more than 30 years old” — the book's own inconsistent framing (a late-1990s creation would not yet be 30 years old at the book's writing, and the framing sits oddly against the internet's longer history). This lesson teaches the IoT topics as the book teaches them and flags the inconsistency here rather than silently rewriting the book, exactly as the placements direct.

Iraq's own way (Grade 11): the final preparatory year is the most forward-looking of the series — smart devices and the Internet of Things (with a lesson on choosing a computer or smartphone by its specifications), a full Excel unit ending in AutoSum, one- and two-dimensional arrays with nested loops (the series' first data structures), and electronic services: e-government, email and cloud computing with its applications.

Sudan's own way (Grade 7): the middle-stage book spends half its length on one database built step by step in Microsoft Access (tables, relationships, forms, queries, reports on a pupils-and-subjects database), then teaches the internet through Sudanese examples — the DNS lesson resolves the Khartoum Ministry of Education site (moekh.gov.sd).
