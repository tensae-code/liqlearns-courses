# Lesson plan — Internet of Things — everyday things that send and receive data

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** New Technologies · **Lesson:** Internet of Things — everyday things that send and receive data
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Emerging Technologies (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** IoT is a network of physical things — objects or people with software, electronics, networks and sensors — that collect and exchange data; when a thing can send and/or receive information over the internet, it becomes smart.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the emerging-technologies doorway. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or in steps — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the other lessons of this unit. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define IoT.
2. Name what things are equipped with: software, electronics, networks, sensors.
3. Give the book's examples of things.
4. Apply the smart rule: send and/or receive over the internet.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Walk your kitchen and your street in your mind. Which ordinary things could report what they sense — the fridge, the water meter, a cow's collar? You are already thinking in IoT. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the parts below, in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 30118]: a picture of a toaster, a wearable, an animal tracker and a temperature sensor all connected to one network — the student names each thing and the data each one sends or receives.

3. **Say it to your companion** — explain the lesson back in your own words (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the lesson retold in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-or-false, fill-in-the-blank, short-answer — served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks IoT is only phones and computers → its whole point is connectivity BEYOND computers and smartphones, into ordinary things. Thinks 'smart' is about intelligence → the book's rule is mechanical: can it send and/or receive information over the internet?
