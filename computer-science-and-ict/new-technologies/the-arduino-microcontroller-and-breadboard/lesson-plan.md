## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The Arduino — a microcontroller you wire yourself** — **Arduino** is a **microcontroller board** — the small computer-on-a-chip of the microcontrollers lesson, mounted on a board that exposes its **pins**: connections where the outside world (lights, buttons, sensors, motors) is **wired to the chip**. The program, written on the computer, is **uploaded** to the board, which then runs it **alone**, reading its pins and driving its pins — the pattern of every embedded machine, learned with the wires visible.
2. **The breadboard — circuits without solder** — The **breadboard** is the **solderless board of connected holes** where Arduino circuits are **tried**: components and jumper wires **push in and pull out**, rows inside the board carry the connections (power rails along the edges, joined strips across the middle), so a circuit can be built, tested, corrected and rebuilt in minutes with nothing melted and nothing wasted. Connecting the board to the computer — cable, the environment's board and port chosen — completes the bench: build on the breadboard, upload from the computer, watch the circuit answer.
