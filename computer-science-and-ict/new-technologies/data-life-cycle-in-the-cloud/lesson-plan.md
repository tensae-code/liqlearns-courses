## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The early stages — create, store, use, share** — The cycle begins with **creation** (data made or collected, and classified — public, internal, secret), then **storage** (placed in the cloud, encrypted and copied), then **use** (processed by services and people, under permissions), then **sharing** (sent to partners and users — the stage where control is hardest, because shared data travels beyond the owner's roof). At every stage the questions repeat: who may touch it, is it encrypted, is its touching logged?
2. **The late stages — archive and destroy** — When data's working life ends it is **archived** — kept cold for the years law or history demands — and finally **destroyed**: truly erased, from live copies and backups alike, with the erasure provable. In the cloud, destruction is a duty the customer must demand and the provider must perform, because copies live in places the customer never sees. Jordan's lesson closes the security unit's frame: protecting data means guarding a whole life, not a moment.
