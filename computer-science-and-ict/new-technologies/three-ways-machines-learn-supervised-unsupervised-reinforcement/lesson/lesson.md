# Three Ways Machines Learn — Supervised, Unsupervised, Reinforcement

## Do it first

You can learn a game three ways: a coach shows you the right moves, you watch a hundred games and spot the patterns yourself, or you play and get points for what works. Machines learn the same three ways. Name which is which as you read.

## Sub lesson 1 — Supervised and unsupervised learning

In **supervised learning (apprentissage supervisé)**, the system trains on examples **with their answers**: photos labelled cat or dog, sentences marked correct or wrong — it learns to produce the answer for new cases. In **unsupervised learning (apprentissage non supervisé)**, there are no answers: the system **finds structure itself** — grouping similar customers, words or sounds into families nobody named in advance.

## Sub lesson 2 — Reinforcement learning

In **reinforcement learning (apprentissage par renforcement)**, the system acts, and the world answers with **reward or penalty**: a game program tries moves, keeps what scores, abandons what loses, and improves by trial over enormous numbers of attempts. The three forms differ in their feedback: an answer key, no key but structure, or a score. Naming the feedback names the learning — the introductions' tidy map of how machines learn.
