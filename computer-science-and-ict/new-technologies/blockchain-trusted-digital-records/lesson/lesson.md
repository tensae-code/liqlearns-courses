# Blockchain — Trusted Digital Records

## Do it first

A record one person keeps can be quietly changed by that person. This lesson is about a record book designed so that nobody can quietly change anything.

## Sub lesson 1 — Blocks, hashes and the chain

Records are packed into **blocks**. Each block carries a **hash** — a short fingerprint computed from its contents — and the **hash of the previous block**, linking them into a **chain**. Alter any old record and its block's hash changes, which no longer matches the link stored in the next block: the tampering shows. New blocks are added by agreed rules, never by editing old ones.

## Sub lesson 2 — Many copies, no single keeper

The chain is **distributed**: many computers hold a full copy and compare notes, so there is no single keeper to bribe, hack or lose the book. That is the trust idea in the book's title — **trusted digital records**: trust comes from the structure, not from trusting a person. The famous use is cryptocurrency, but the same structure serves land records, certificates and supply chains.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: A blockchain is a shared record book kept as a chain of blocks — each block holds records plus a fingerprint (hash) of the block before it — copied onto many computers at once, so changing an old record would break the chain and be seen by everyone: records that no single keeper can quietly rewrite. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
