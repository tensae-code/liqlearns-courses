# Lesson plan — Big data in healthcare and education

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** New Technologies · **Lesson:** Big data in healthcare and education
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Emerging Technologies (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** in healthcare big data improves prevention, care and cost; in education it fits learning plans to each learner and backs decisions with evidence — and the guide adds personalised lesson plans and college matching at school.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the benefits lesson before it. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or in steps — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the other lessons of this unit. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name healthcare's big-data sources.
2. Name the five things big data improves in healthcare.
3. Say what big data does in education, including the guide's school-level uses.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — A hospital holds records for a whole city. A school holds results for thousands of learners. What could each learn from all that data together, that no single file could tell? (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the parts below, in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 30104]: a picture of a hospital record and a learner's plan both feeding one analytics chart — the student points to each side and names what that field gains.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading this lesson's key words aloud — the student listens, then says each word and what it means.
3. **Say it to your companion** — explain the lesson back in your own words (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the lesson retold in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-or-false, fill-in-the-blank, short-answer — served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the doing block at the end of lesson/lesson.md.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes the two fields' sources → hospital records and testing machines are healthcare; grades and attention spans are education. Thinks 'cost' improving means care gets worse → the book lists patient experience AND cost improving together.
