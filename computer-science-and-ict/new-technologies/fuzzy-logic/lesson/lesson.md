# Fuzzy Logic

## Do it first

Is the water warm? Not yes, not no — warm-ish. Ordinary computer logic knows only two answers, but the world speaks in degrees. Laos's Grade 10 AI chapter gives the in-between logic its own branch: Fuzzy Logic. This lesson thinks in degrees.

## Sub lesson 1 — From true/false to degrees

Ordinary (crisp) logic sorts every statement as **true or false**, every thing as in the set or out — a person of 179 cm is simply 'not tall' if tall begins at 180. **Fuzzy logic** replaces the hard edge with a **degree of membership**: a value between **0 and 1** saying how much a thing belongs — 179 cm is tall to degree 0.9, warm water is warm to degree 0.7. The computer can then reason the way people speak: *rather* hot, *quite* far, *almost* full — judgements the two-answer logic cannot even write down.

In your mind's eye: from true/false to degrees.

## Sub lesson 2 — Fuzzy logic at work — control that feels human

Fuzzy logic's home is **control**: a fuzzy controller takes readings stated in degrees (the room is *rather* warm; the load is *quite* heavy) and rules stated as people state them (**if** the room is rather warm **then** cool it gently) and produces a **graded action** — the air conditioner's fan easing instead of snapping on and off, the washing machine choosing its wash by how dirty, the camera steadying its focus. Laos places Fuzzy Logic among the branches of AI — beside neural networks and intelligent agents — because it is intelligence of a particular kind: not learning, but **judgement in degrees**, the machine handling the world's vagueness without pretending it is precise.

In your mind's eye: fuzzy logic at work — control that feels human.
