## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The describing techniques** — The first family **describes and compares**: **statistical summaries** (averages, spread, percentiles — the descriptive-statistics lesson's craft), **grouping and cross-tabulation** (splitting by kind and reading the cells), **correlation** (do two measures move together — and how strongly), and **visualisation** itself, the technique of letting the eye analyse. These techniques answer 'what' and begin 'why'; every analysis, however advanced, opens with them because they are how the analyst first meets the data honestly.
2. **The modelling techniques** — The second family **models**: **regression** (a line or rule relating measures — predicting one from others), **classification** (sorting cases into kinds by their measures — the ML unit's bridge), **clustering** (finding groups no one named in advance), and **time-series methods** (reading a measure's own history for trend and season). Jordan's techniques lesson is a shelf with labels: a technique is chosen by the question's type and the data's shape — and the analyst who knows only one technique sees every problem as its shape, which the book names as the beginner's trap.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
