# Data Analysis Techniques

## Do it first

Types name the questions; techniques are the hands that answer them. This lesson learns the analyst's standing techniques and when each is taken down from the shelf.

## Sub lesson 1 — The describing techniques

The first family **describes and compares**: **statistical summaries** (averages, spread, percentiles — the descriptive-statistics lesson's craft), **grouping and cross-tabulation** (splitting by kind and reading the cells), **correlation** (do two measures move together — and how strongly), and **visualisation** itself, the technique of letting the eye analyse. These techniques answer 'what' and begin 'why'; every analysis, however advanced, opens with them because they are how the analyst first meets the data honestly.

## Sub lesson 2 — The modelling techniques

The second family **models**: **regression** (a line or rule relating measures — predicting one from others), **classification** (sorting cases into kinds by their measures — the ML unit's bridge), **clustering** (finding groups no one named in advance), and **time-series methods** (reading a measure's own history for trend and season). Jordan's techniques lesson is a shelf with labels: a technique is chosen by the question's type and the data's shape — and the analyst who knows only one technique sees every problem as its shape, which the book names as the beginner's trap.
