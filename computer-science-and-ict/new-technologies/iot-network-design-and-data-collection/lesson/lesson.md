# IoT Network Design and Data Collection

## Do it first

Before a single sensor is screwed to a wall, someone must answer: who talks to whom, how often, and where does the data sleep? This lesson is that answering.

## Sub lesson 1 — Communication scenarios — who talks to whom

An IoT system's **communication scenario** is the pattern of its conversations. Jordan's book walks the standing patterns: **device to device** (things coordinate directly), **device to gateway** (things report to a local collector), **device to cloud** (things report to internet services), and **cloud to application** (services feed the apps people use). A design chooses and mixes scenarios per job — the fire alarm must not wait for a distant cloud; the monthly meter reading can.

## Sub lesson 2 — Designing and collecting — the data's journey planned

**Network design** fixes the rest: which links (short or long range), where gateways stand, how addresses and security are handled, and what happens when a link fails. **Data collection** is designed with it: what is collected, at what rhythm, kept where, for how long. The book's rule: data collected without a designed journey becomes a swamp — expensive to keep, impossible to trust, dangerous to hold.
