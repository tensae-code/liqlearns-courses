## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Communication scenarios — who talks to whom** — An IoT system's **communication scenario** is the pattern of its conversations. Jordan's book walks the standing patterns: **device to device** (things coordinate directly), **device to gateway** (things report to a local collector), **device to cloud** (things report to internet services), and **cloud to application** (services feed the apps people use). A design chooses and mixes scenarios per job — the fire alarm must not wait for a distant cloud; the monthly meter reading can.
2. **Designing and collecting — the data's journey planned** — **Network design** fixes the rest: which links (short or long range), where gateways stand, how addresses and security are handled, and what happens when a link fails. **Data collection** is designed with it: what is collected, at what rhythm, kept where, for how long. The book's rule: data collected without a designed journey becomes a swamp — expensive to keep, impossible to trust, dangerous to hold.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
