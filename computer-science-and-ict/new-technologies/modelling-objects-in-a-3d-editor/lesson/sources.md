Placement choice: this lesson was built new for the ICT expansion (Kazakhstan, 2026-11-14) because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree was checked for the slug and the live lesson content was checked for near matches before building. It sits in the New Technologies title (new-technologies/), following the book's own order. Provenance scope note: the placements stocked here are from Kazakhstan's okulyk.kz (OKULYK) textbooks, recommended by the Ministry of Education and Science of the Republic of Kazakhstan — the Digital Literacy and Informatika lines of the Almatykitap, Arman-PV and Atamura teams and the Nazarbayev Intellectual Schools (NIS) books, all student books except the two Kadirkulov teacher guides named in the entries below, whose placements are stocked exactly as filed. Many books exist in parallel language editions (Kazakh / Russian / Uzbek / Uyghur / English) of the same textbook: each distinct book is taught once, and every distinct edition is named and cited in the book entry. Kazakhstan's own ways are kept: one textbook line in up to five parallel language editions, the okulyk.kz digital library as the books' home, Scratch from Grade 1 and robotics strands (LEGO WeDo 2.0 and Mindstorms EV3) from Grade 1–2, and every keyword given in Kazakh–Russian–English in the early grades. Media slots in lesson/lesson.md are described slots only ([Picture — …]); no picture numbers are invented.


Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G6 Informatika): Grade 6 by the Almatykitap team drops the Scratch-game strand of the 2018 books: after systems/networks (including a lesson on Internet addiction) it teaches 3D modelling and 3D printing, a first Python course (IDE, syntax, data types, input/output, linear programs), and academic Word work — footnotes, hyperlinks, an automatic table of contents, copyright and plagiarism, and how to write a referat.

Kazakhstan's own way (Salgaraeva & Kopeeva G6 Informatika): the Arman-PV Grade 6 book follows the newer programme (systems and networks, 3D printing, Python, academic Word work) in 30 paragraphs with a test after each section; the 3D editor is SketchUp (the book's resource list points to gsketchup.ru and python.org), the history of computing is split into two paragraphs, and almost every topic is doubled with a "creating a project" paragraph.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukhametzhanova G6 Informatika, 2020 edition): the 2020 Atamura Grade 6 book drops the game project of its 2018 predecessor and follows the newer programme in four parts; its 3D strand is taught in Paint 3D (with Remix 3D models, the Mixed Reality Viewer and 3D-printer safety rules), and the Python part works in IDLE with type conversion (int/float) given its own space.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukhametzhanova G7 Informatika, 2017 edition): the 2017 Atamura Grade 7 book adds two things its rivals lack — file archiving (creating archives and extracting them) in the first part, and a fifth part on 3D modelling of objects and events; its programming part works with programming languages and systems in general (the glossary's syntax examples are C++: main, cout, cin) and includes designing the project's interface.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G7 Informatika, 2019 edition): Almatykitap's other Grade 7 book (2019, printed in Turkey) follows the same five-part frame as the Atamura 2017 book — units and memory, networks and antivirus, spreadsheets with conditional formatting and modelling, programming solutions with interface design (its bibliography is Lazarus/Delphi-based), and 3D modelling of objects and events in SketchUp.

Kazakhstan's own way (Adekenova & Pryanichnikova G9 Informatics, NIS): the NIS Grade 9 course is built around databases and object-oriented programming: computer architecture with logic circuits, coding raster images and sound, single-table then multi-table databases in Access (forms, queries, reports), five lessons introducing OOP, nine lessons developing applications in an OOP environment (C#), and a thirteen-lesson capstone — choosing a problem, designing the interface, programming, making a video instruction, testing, documenting and evaluating the project.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G6 «Информатика»): the Almatykitap Grade 6 book follows the same state programme as the Arman-PV book (Book 24) — ergonomics, wireless networks, footnotes and hyperlinks, copyright, contents, report — but swaps vector graphics for a whole 3D-printing section in a SketchUp-style editor (cone, cylinder and sphere models, print setup) and swaps the Scratch game for a first Python course: IDE, alphabet and syntax, data types, arithmetic expressions, number input and output, linear algorithms.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G7 «Информатика» 2019 edition): the 2019 Almatykitap Grade 7 book is not the 2023 Python book (Book 15): after measurement, networks and spreadsheets it teaches programming through the Lazarus project interface — interface elements, data types, branching, nested and compound conditions — and ends with 3D modelling of objects and events (house, car, table, chair, window, glass as the final tasks).

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukametzhanova G7 «Информатика» 2017 edition): the Atamura Grade 7 book runs the same state programme as the Almatykitap one (Book 27) but adds a unit on converting between units of information, teaches the programming in C++, and likewise ends in 3D modelling of objects and events.

Kazakhstan's own way (Pryanichnikova team G9 «Информатика» NIS): the NIS Grade 9 book is a step up in level: logic expressions, truth tables and logic circuits inside "computer architecture"; sound encoding beside raster encoding; single-table then multi-table databases in Access with forms, queries and reports; and a full object-oriented programming course in C# — components of OOP, multimedia applications, and a software-solution project with a video instruction and a slide show.

Kazakhstan's own way (Salgaraeva/Kopeeva G6 «Информатика»): this Arman-PV Grade 6 book (a third distinct Grade 6 book in this half) opens with an explicit discussion of internet addiction, and its text-document section treats hyperlinks, copyright and plagiarism, the table of contents and the school report (реферат) as project work — the document-skills tail of the Grade 6 programme.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukametzhanova G6 2020 «Информатика»): the 2020 Atamura Grade 6 revision drops the spreadsheet tail of its 2018 edition and follows the new programme instead — ergonomics, computer history, wireless networks, 3D printing, Python and a text-document section (instructions and links, hyperlinks, table of contents, report) — in four compact sections.
