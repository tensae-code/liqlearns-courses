# Video script — Data Security in Cloud Computing

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Cloud data is protected by the course's standing crafts, arranged for a rented home: **encryption** in transit and at rest (so stolen disks and tapped lines yield noise), **access control** (accounts, permissions, multi-factor sign-in — the doors and their keys), **backup and replication** (copies in other buildings against fire and failure), and **monitoring** (logs watched for the intruder's footsteps). The provider operates most of this machinery — but the customer switches much of it on, or fails to.
Cloud security is a **shared responsibility**: the provider secures the cloud itself — buildings, machines, the platform's soundness; the customer secures what they put **in** the cloud — their accounts, their permissions, their data's classification, their people's habits. Most cloud breaches walk through the customer's half: a public bucket, a phished password, a permission given to everyone. Jordan's lesson ends with the division memorised, because blaming the provider for the customer's half is the field's most expensive misunderstanding.
