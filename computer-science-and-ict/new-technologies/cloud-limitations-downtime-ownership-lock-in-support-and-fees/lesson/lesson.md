# Cloud limitations — downtime, ownership, lock-in, support and fees

## Do it first

Now argue against the cloud for two minutes. The internet in your area drops twice a week. Your files sit on a stranger's server, in the stranger's own formats. When it breaks at midnight, who do you call? Every weakness you just named is in this lesson — six limitations, named plainly.

## Six limitations

**Server downtime** — **no provider guarantees no interruption**, and the cloud **depends on the internet connection: if it goes down, so does the service**.

**Security and privacy risk** — **intruders keep trying new ways in, so the cloud is not completely safe**.

**Data ownership and transparency** — whether **uploaded data becomes the provider's property**, and whether **its handling is open** for you to see.

**Inflexibility** — a provider may **force its own applications and formats**, and **ultimately controls the infrastructure** — the lock-in problem.

**Lack of support** — **minimal to no customer assistance**; users **turn to internet forums** instead.

**Cost — unknown fees**: paying for **every feature an organisation needs** can make the service **costly, despite saving on-site infrastructure**.
