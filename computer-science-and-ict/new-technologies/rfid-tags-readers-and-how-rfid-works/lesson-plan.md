## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The three parts — tag, reader, system** — An **RFID** system has three parts. The **tag** (chip with a small antenna) is fixed to the object and holds its identity data. The **reader** sends out radio waves and receives the tag's answer. The **application system** — the computer and software behind the reader — records and uses the identity: stock, entry, payment. Tags may be **passive** (powered by the reader's wave) or **active** (carrying their own battery, answering from farther away).
2. **Writing and reading — how the data moves** — In the **write process**, data is stored onto the tag — the object's identity written once or updated. In the **read process**, the reader's wave wakes the tag, the tag replies with its data, and the application system matches the reply against its records and acts. Reading needs no line of sight and can take many tags at once — the two advantages over barcodes that explain RFID's spread through shops, libraries, roads and warehouses.
