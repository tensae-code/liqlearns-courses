# Video script — Physical Computing — Coding for the International Space Station

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

The station's computer (the **Astro Pi**, with its Sense HAT) feels its world through **sensors** the program must **handle**: temperature, humidity, pressure, movement and orientation, light and colour. Handling a sensor means the program's standing pattern — **read the value, decide by it, act on it** — run against readings that arrive from a real, moving, orbiting room, with experiments the book designs around them (what is the station's air like, right now, above our heads?).
The station computer's face is an **8×8 LED matrix** — sixty-four lights in an 8-by-8 grid — and the unit's creative half is **creating images on it**: setting **pixels by their coordinates and colours**, building pictures, letters, icons and small animations light by light. Its discipline is the grid's smallness: with 64 lights, every pixel is a decision — the same economy the Scratch stage taught at the other end of the sky, now practised for an audience of astronauts.
