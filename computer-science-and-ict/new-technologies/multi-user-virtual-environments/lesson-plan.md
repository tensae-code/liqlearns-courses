## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Connecting — entering a shared world** — A **multi-user virtual environment** is a simulated world **hosted once and entered by many users together**: each user **connects** (account, connection, their **avatar** — the figure that stands for them inside), and the environment keeps **one shared world-state**, showing every user the same world with all the others in it, updated as each moves and acts. Connection is therefore not downloading a place; it is **arriving** in one.
2. **Navigating together — movement, presence, meeting** — **Navigating** the shared world means **moving the avatar** through its spaces, **seeing and being seen** — presence is the environment's social fact: others appear as figures whose movements and words arrive as they happen, so the world supports **meeting, talking and working together** inside it, not merely sightseeing side by side. The chapter places these environments beside VR/AR systems as their social form: reality simulated once, society included.

1. **Do it first** — The virtual-reality lesson put one person inside a simulated world. The EPAL chapter's next step removes the loneliness: the same world, entered by many people at once, each present to the others. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of connecting — entering a shared world as the lesson describes it — the student studies the picture and names what each part shows.
   - ADD GIF: a GIF of navigating together — movement, presence, meeting in action — the student watches once, then says what changed and why.
