# Big data — and the privacy problem

## Do it first

Every like, photo and location on a social network becomes data. Multiply yours by millions of users, every day. Feel the size first. Then read what 'big' means — and what it costs privacy.

## Sub lesson 1 — Big data — volume, variety, velocity

**Big data** is data **too large to process by traditional methods**. It is characterised by three Vs: **volume** (the amount), **variety** (the kinds) and **velocity** (the speed it arrives). The scale example: **Facebook generating 4 petabytes — a million gigabytes — a day in 2020**.

## Sub lesson 2 — The privacy problem — and its two protections

Data-science uses such as social networking rest on **collecting masses of user data**, so **privacy becomes contentious**, especially under **strong privacy regulation**. The suggested protections are **data anonymisation** — **removing personally identifiable information** — and **data generalisation** — **grouping data into broad categories such as age groups and areas**. But both protections **limit the insight** that can be drawn — so the tension remains: protect people, and the data says less; use everything, and privacy is at risk.
