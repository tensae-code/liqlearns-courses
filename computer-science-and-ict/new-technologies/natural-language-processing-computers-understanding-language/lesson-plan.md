# Lesson plan — Natural Language Processing — computers understanding language

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** New Technologies · **Lesson:** Natural Language Processing — computers understanding language
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Emerging Technologies (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Natural Language Processing (NLP) is the branch of AI that lets computers understand written and spoken human language as people do.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Grade 11 Emerging Technologies unit around it. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or in steps — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the other lessons of this unit. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define NLP: computers understanding human language as people do.
2. Name the six applications, with Alexa/Siri and Google Translate as examples.
3. Say the three sentiments sentiment analysis sorts into.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Talk to a phone assistant, or type a sentence into a translator. The machine just handled your language — not numbers, not code. List two more times a computer handled your words. Then read. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the parts below, in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 16505]: a picture of spoken and written words entering a computer and coming out as translation, answers and summaries — the student names which NLP job each output shows.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading this lesson's key words aloud — the student listens, then says each word and what it means.
3. **Say it to your companion** — explain the lesson back in your own words (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the lesson retold in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-or-false, fill-in-the-blank, short-answer — served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the doing block at the end of lesson/lesson.md.

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the plan is the build sheet, so nothing the lesson needs gets forgotten.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks NLP is about programming languages → 'natural' language is human language; code is the opposite case. Mixes translation with summarisation → translation changes the language; summarisation shortens in (usually) the same language.
