No book error touches this lesson.

North Macedonia's own way (Year 2): a strikingly current first theme (cybersecurity, cryptography, blockchain, robotics, AI/ML and large language models) precedes deep C++ (data representation, functions, one-dimensional arrays), a full databases theme ending in SQL, multimedia with generative AI for graphics, and HTML web authoring.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Grade 10): Grade 10 jumps from using computers to building with them — simple electronic circuits, programming that controls an external device, robotics and artificial intelligence sit inside "Computer Architecture", while the software unit pairs operating-system installation with machine learning; typing rises to 30 wpm. (teacher guide — evidence, PARTIAL)

Egypt's own way (Grade 8, Term 1 student book): Two units — "Artificial Intelligence and Protecting Our Digital Data" and "Website Design and Creation" — ending with pupils creating their own school website with AI help.

Egypt's own way (Grade 9, Term 1 student book (Arabic)): Preparatory 3 goes deep on Python — computational thinking, AI algorithms, strings/lists, functions with parameters, input-error handling — then a second unit on AI and cybersecurity ending with deepfakes.

Egypt's own way (Grade 8, Term 2 student book (Arabic)): Term 2 teaches AI from the neuron up — artificial neuron, its role in AI — then Python for data analysis and even encryption with XOR, a depth most countries save for secondary school.

Kuwait's own way (Grade 4 Digital Explorer Term 2, printed title — records name it 'Our Digital World'): the Digital World unit mixes identity (pride in Arabic language and identity online), cloud storage on the national platform (OneDrive inside Teams), and a three-lesson AI arc (data → training a machine → AI decisions) before the tool unit; PowerPoint is taught as a five-step 'Exciting Presentation' journey ending in presentations about Kuwait itself.

Kuwait's own way (Grade 10 Information Technology Term 2): the Grade 10 year is a Python-to-AI arc — Term 2 moves from Python data structures and functions straight into AI concepts and a hands-on OpenCV face-recognition project, with the ministry's Hamad AI assistant threaded through the front matter.

Greece's own way (Grade 9, Pliroforiki C' Gymnasiou, 2024): Grade 9 is project-led — recursion first, then Python (EduBlocks and Thonny, rock-paper-scissors with randomness), physical computing (code for the International Space Station, micro:bit, Arduino with breadboard and Tinkercad), AI with App Inventor image recognition, collaboration in Greece's own +γραφίς (+grafis) document service, AI ethics/privacy/cookies/big and open data, and scientific programming (solving equations, exponential spread modelling).

Switzerland's own way (Grades 1–2, Décodage 1re–2e): Vaud's Romandie digital-education curriculum is taught in three axes — Science informatique (SI), Médias (M), Usages (U) — almost entirely UNPLUGGED at this age: algorithms are learned with the body (robot game, crane game, hand-washing, getting dressed, a crêpe recipe), a physical sorting machine, and the Blue-Bot floor robot; the class also builds a collective digital “charter book” of good digital habits across the whole cycle.

Switzerland's own way (Grades 3–4, Décodage 3e–4e): the 3e–4e volume repeats the 1re–2e unplugged activities at a higher level (loops and debugging join the robot and crane games and the sorting machine) and adds three new strands: Thymio robotics, a full ScratchJr scenario, and media “parcours” — Parcours des bestioles (how images influence emotions), Parcours du partage (image rights, digital traces, thinking before sharing) and Parcours de la pub (recognising and creating advertising). Pixel paravent introduces binary coding as transmitting a black-and-white image across a screen with an agreed code; Deviner la carte teaches the conditional through a magic trick and its logigramme.

Switzerland's own way (Grades 5–6, Décodage 5e–6e): Cycle 2 moves from unplugged games to real tools while keeping the game frame: the imaginary city “Square CT” teaches classification, decision trees, conditionals, indentation and flowcharts (logigrammes); ScratchJr tells emotion and climate stories; the Swiss Thymio robot is programmed in VPL; data is taught through binary pixel coding and colour coding; and “Enquêtes” (inquiries) teach addition-as-algorithm, debugging, sorting, the Nim winning strategy, Caesar ciphers and sensor experiments. Médias/Usages scenarios cover cyberbullying, screen practices, news and media stereotypes.

Switzerland's own way (Grades 7–8, Décodage 7e–8e): at 7e–8e the Décodage course becomes a real computer-science course under the Plan d'études romand EN 22/23: Thymio VPL missions (including Morse communication and self-driving-car autonomy levels), a full Scratch discovery course (movement, shortening programs, sub-programs as own blocks, remixing), a Scratch video-game project run in “studios” with a cahier des charges, data work (Huffman-style compression via Zip la marmotte, binary/octal/hex/ASCII codings, file sizes), access security (passwords, file encryption, phishing, authentication), home networks at two levels (IP addresses; message routing, packets, TCP and encryption), and inquiries into sorting, sorting networks, Caesar decryption and parity-bit error detection. Médias/Usages covers social networks and digital identity/footprint, information sources, cyberbullying and the environmental cost of digital technology.

Bangladesh's own way (HSC ICT, Classes XI–XII and Alim): one compulsory ICT book for every HSC and Alim student in the country, saturated with Bangladesh itself: Bangabandhu Satellite-1 and its ground stations, bdjobs and chakri.com job portals, Daraz and Bikroy e-commerce, the Walton factory's automated production line, the National ID card used to teach primary keys and its database used to teach RDBMS at work, a full Bangla Unicode table and a worked example of putting Bangla text on a web page with meta charset, the national letter-grade scale (A+ from 80) as the else-if programme, "I Love Bangladesh" as the loop example text, an algorithm for reaching college by bicycle or rickshaw, and the Digital Bangladesh programme — National Data Centre, hi-tech parks, Startup Bangladesh and Bangladesh Bank's QR-based Cashless Bangladesh. C is the HSC programming language, taught with scanf/printf in Code::Blocks.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 11 G11T2 Student): Cloud services are taught through the two commercial providers students can actually open — Microsoft Azure and Google Cloud — before data security in the cloud; the AI unit goes from expert systems through machine learning and NLP to modelling and simulation.

Vietnam's own way (Grade 12 Tin học 12 Khoa học máy tính): Bài 1–21 are the same common core as the Applied orientation book (spot-checked page-identical, e.g. Bài 1); the CS-only lessons go deeper into network devices, transmission media and network design, then introduce machine learning and data science, and end with simulation in problem-solving and education.


Kazakhstan's own way (Kolyeva G10 Informatika, humanities track): Mektep's humanities-track Grade 10 book mirrors the Arman-PV one (information security, video content, design theory, web design) and adds a fifth part no other humanities book has: artificial intelligence and blockchain — machine-learning principles, how neural networks are organised and work, the fields where AI is used, and blockchain technology.
