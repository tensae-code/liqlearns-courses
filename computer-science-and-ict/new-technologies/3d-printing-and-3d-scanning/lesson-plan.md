## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **3D printing — a thing built in layers** — **3D printing** builds a physical object **layer by layer** from a **digital 3D model**: the model is **sliced** into thin layers by the preparing program, and the printer lays material — melted filament, in the common school printer — **one layer on the last**, until the object stands whole. Its virtues are the chapter's list: shapes no mould could make, one-off and repaired parts made on demand, prototypes in hours — paid for in printing time and in the model's quality deciding the object's.
2. **3D scanning — a thing read into a model** — **3D scanning** runs the road backwards: the scanner **measures a real object's surface** — sweeping light or lasers over it, or photographing it from many angles — and builds the **digital model** of its shape, which can then be **studied, corrected, shared — and printed**, closing the chapter's loop (scan the broken part, repair the model, print the replacement). Scanning's care is its surfaces: shiny, dark and hollow places read badly, and the model's honesty must be checked against the thing itself before the loop is trusted.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — The EPAL technology chapter pairs the two directions of the 3D road: a printer that turns a model into a thing on the desk, and a scanner that turns a thing on the desk into a model. Together they close the loop between the digital and the physical. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of 3d printing — a thing built in layers as the lesson describes it — the student studies the picture and names what each part shows.
