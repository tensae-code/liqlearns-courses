## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **A microcontroller — a small computer on one chip** — A **microcontroller** is a **small computer on a single chip**: it joins a **processor, memory, and inputs and outputs** on one piece, and it is built **into a device** to control it. It runs **one program** — the device's own work — again and again: in a **solar lamp**, a **washing machine** or a **microwave**, all Sri Lanka's own examples. It is not a small desktop computer: it has no screen of its own and does no other job — the chip, the program and the device are one thing.
2. **Sensors — and learning with micro:bit and Arduino** — A microcontroller learns about the world through **sensors**: parts that **detect a change in the environment** — light, heat, movement — and turn it into a **signal** the chip can read. The chip reads the signal and **acts** as its program says: switching, timing, warning. Pupils meet this control with real kits — the **BBC micro:bit** and **Arduino** — building circuits that sense and respond, so the lamp that lights itself stops being magic and becomes a program they wrote.
