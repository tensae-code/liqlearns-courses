# Microcontrollers and Sensors — micro:bit and Arduino — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What is a microcontroller?**
**Answer: A small computer on a single chip, built into a device to control it**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What parts does a microcontroller join on its chip?**
**Answer: A processor, memory, and inputs and outputs**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) Where does a microcontroller work?**
**Answer: Inside a device — in a solar lamp, a washing machine or a microwave**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What does the microcontroller run?**
**Answer: One program — the device's own work, again and again**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) How is a microcontroller different from a desktop computer?**
**Answer: It is one small chip fitted to one device's job, with no screen of its own**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What does a sensor do?**
**Answer: It detects a change in the environment — light, heat or movement — and turns it into a signal**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What does the microcontroller do with a sensor's signal?**
**Answer: It reads the signal and acts — switching, timing or warning as its program says**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) Name a learning kit for microcontrollers.**
**Answer: The BBC micro:bit (or an Arduino kit)**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) What do learners build with a micro:bit or Arduino kit?**
**Answer: Real circuits that sense and respond**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) Give one of Sri Lanka's local examples of a microcontroller at work.**
**Answer: A solar lamp that lights itself when darkness falls (any one of: a washing machine, a microwave)**
