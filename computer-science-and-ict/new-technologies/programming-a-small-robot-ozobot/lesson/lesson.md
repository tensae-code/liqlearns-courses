# Programming a Small Robot — Ozobot

## Do it first

Programming a small robot — Ozobot: a pocket robot that follows lines and obeys colour codes for direction and speed, cared for and calibrated before it is programmed

## Sub lesson 1 — Meet Ozobot — care and calibration first

**Ozobot** is a small robot — a dome of sensors and wheels that fits in a palm — and Indonesia's Grade 8 book introduces it the way real machines are introduced: **care** first (perawatan — handling, charging, keeping its sensors clean), then **calibration** (kalibrasi — teaching the robot the paper's white and the line's black, so its light sensor reads truly on this desk, in this light). An uncalibrated robot does not disobey; it misreads — and the book's order (care, calibrate, then program) is the standing order for every sensing machine.

## Sub lesson 2 — Colour codes — direction and speed on the line

Ozobot's first language is drawn: it follows a **line**, and **colour codes** — short sequences of coloured segments laid across the line — are its commands. One code tells it a **direction** at a crossing (turn, go straight, turn back); another sets its **speed** (faster, slower, and the show-off moves between). Programming, here, is drawing the right code at the right place on the track — the algorithm made visible on paper, debugged by watching exactly where the robot's reading and the draw-er's intention part.
