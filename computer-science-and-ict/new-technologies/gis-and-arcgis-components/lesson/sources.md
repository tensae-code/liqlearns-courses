Rwanda's own way (S1 ICT Student Book, KLB): competency-based units each opening with a Key Unit Competency and keywords; a full GIS/ArcGIS unit and a Scratch music-making unit sit inside the first secondary year, and ICT use is grounded in Rwandan daily life.

Rwanda's own way (S1 ICT Teacher Guide, KLB): the guide mirrors the student units with content maps, additional teacher content per unit, SEN/multi-ability support, sample lesson plans (Appendix 1) and a glossary (Appendix 2); assessment is framed by Rwanda's competence-based curriculum. (teacher guide — evidence)

Rwanda's own way (S2 ICT Teacher Guide, KLB): S2 moves to data protection, ICT in finance (including Rwandan mobile banking risks and biometrics), spreadsheets in depth, a second ArcGIS unit, and Scratch game design with sprite interaction tables and score/level rules. (teacher guide — evidence)

Rwanda's own way (S3 ICT Learner's Book, Fountain): the same ten units as the S3 guide taught as long screenshot-by-screenshot practicals rooted in Rwandan school life (a GS Bumbogo parents'-meeting mail merge, an administration map of Rwanda built in ArcGIS, an environmental-protection presentation, 'Rwanda Nziza' hyperlink decks), closing with a complete Scratch platform game, Hard Journey.

Rwanda's own way (S3 ICT Teacher Guide, Fountain): the S3 guide (a different publisher from the KLB S1/S2 set) organises the year into four study areas — application software, GIS/spreadsheets/presentations, graphics and multimedia, networks and programming — ending in platform game programming in Scratch. (teacher guide — evidence)

Rwanda's own way (S2 ICT Student Book, KLB): the student companion to the S2 guide — the same nine units, taught through Rwandan financial life (mobile banking, ATMs, stock exchange) and Scratch game projects (Helicopter Game, Moving Eyes Game).


Kazakhstan's own way (Ataeva & Vyshkova G8 Informatics, NIS): the NIS Grade 8 course is the most distinctive of the wave: vector graphics flows into geographic information systems (planning an excursion, making maps, visualising data in GIS), spreadsheets are used to solve logical problems, loops are taught through mathematics, physics, business and multimedia problems, and the year ends with a full project cycle — six stages, presentation, documentation, defence — plus a mini-project on artificial intelligence.

Kazakhstan's own way (Atayeva/Vyushkova/Zhurbenko G8 «Информатика» NIS): the NIS Grade 8 book is the widest of the set: a whole section on vector graphics and geographic information systems (planning an excursion, making maps, visualising data in GIS), octal and hexadecimal coding, loops applied separately to mathematics, physics, business and animation, strings and subroutines, and a nine-step project method ending in a mini-project on artificial intelligence.
