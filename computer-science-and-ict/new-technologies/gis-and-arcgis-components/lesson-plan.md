## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **GIS — maps that answer questions** — A **GIS (geographic information system)** is a computer system for **capturing, storing, checking and displaying data about places** — every fact it holds is tied to a **location** on the earth. Unlike a paper map, a GIS map is built of **layers** that can be shown, hidden and **queried**: ask which land parcels lie in one village, and the system picks them out. Rwanda's books return to GIS in S1, S2 and S3 — it is a strand, not a visit.
2. **The components of ArcGIS** — **ArcGIS** is the GIS software family Rwanda's books use. Its working parts: **ArcMap**, where maps are made and queried; **ArcCatalog**, the filing cabinet that organises the geographic data; and the **data itself** — layers of places with their **attribute tables** of facts. ArcMap draws and asks; ArcCatalog keeps and finds. Together with the data they are the components every later GIS lesson in this course uses.
