## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The levels ladder** — Autonomy is measured on a **ladder of levels**: at the bottom, the car **assists** — warns, or steadies one thing (lane, speed) while the human drives. In the middle, the car drives **under supervision** — steering and speed together, but the human must watch and take over at once when asked. At the top, the car drives **itself** in its domain — the human may stop watching — and at the final level, everywhere, with no human driver needed at all. The Thymio mission asks “and in real life?” because the robot's own small autonomies sit on this same ladder's first rungs.
2. **Responsibility climbs with the level** — Each level is also a **responsibility** answer: while the human must supervise, the human answers for the driving; when the car truly drives itself, responsibility shifts toward its makers and operators — the reading keys (delegation, responsibility) applied to the road. That is why the levels matter beyond engineering: a buyer, a law and an insurer all need to know which level a car really is — not what its advertisement calls it.
