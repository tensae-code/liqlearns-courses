**Acceptance count: taught in 7 countries (Ethiopia, China, Egypt, Greece, Jordan, Armenia, Indonesia).**

Egypt's own way (Grade 8, Term 1 student book): Two units — "Artificial Intelligence and Protecting Our Digital Data" and "Website Design and Creation" — ending with pupils creating their own school website with AI help.

Greece's own way (Grade 9, Pliroforiki C' Gymnasiou, 2024): Grade 9 is project-led — recursion first, then Python (EduBlocks and Thonny, rock-paper-scissors with randomness), physical computing (code for the International Space Station, micro:bit, Arduino with breadboard and Tinkercad), AI with App Inventor image recognition, collaboration in Greece's own +γραφίς (+grafis) document service, AI ethics/privacy/cookies/big and open data, and scientific programming (solving equations, exponential spread modelling).

Switzerland's own way (Grades 1–2, Décodage 1re–2e): Vaud's Romandie digital-education curriculum is taught in three axes — Science informatique (SI), Médias (M), Usages (U) — almost entirely UNPLUGGED at this age: algorithms are learned with the body (robot game, crane game, hand-washing, getting dressed, a crêpe recipe), a physical sorting machine, and the Blue-Bot floor robot; the class also builds a collective digital “charter book” of good digital habits across the whole cycle.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 10 G10T1 Student): Grade 10 Term 1 is a data year: data and binary representation first, then big data and visualisation, then a long practical Excel lesson (text functions, AVERAGEIF/SUMIF, VLOOKUP, Pivot Tables, FORECAST and the Analysis ToolPak); the second unit builds web pages in HTML topic by topic.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 11 G11 student): Grade 11 (Stepanyan et al., Zangak) is the most advanced book in the set: online payment systems, digital biography, software licences, inclusive technology, logic-circuit adders, network performance, big data in tables, 1D/2D arrays, sorting and searching, recursion, OOP classes and inheritance, error types and algorithm speed — most of it beyond the current ICT course's 584 lessons, hence the high NEW share.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 12 G12 Informatika student): Grade 12 (original Kurikulum Merdeka edition) is built around making things: single-board computers/controllers and a full Arduino C course (IDE, UnoArduSim simulator, sensors, S.O.S. LED and melody projects), then networks with cyber security and data governance, social impact with the UU ITE law, and a cross-field group project (smart trash bin / waste-bank apps) with journals, reports and peer assessment.
