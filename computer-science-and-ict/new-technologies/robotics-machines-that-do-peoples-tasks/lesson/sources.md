**Acceptance count: taught in 13 countries (Ethiopia, Nepal, North Macedonia, Egypt, Mongolia, Kuwait, Laos, Greece, Bangladesh, Jordan, Vietnam, Pakistan, Kazakhstan).**

No book error touches this lesson.

North Macedonia's own way (Year 2): a strikingly current first theme (cybersecurity, cryptography, blockchain, robotics, AI/ML and large language models) precedes deep C++ (data representation, functions, one-dimensional arrays), a full databases theme ending in SQL, multimedia with generative AI for graphics, and HTML web authoring.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Grade 10): Grade 10 jumps from using computers to building with them — simple electronic circuits, programming that controls an external device, robotics and artificial intelligence sit inside "Computer Architecture", while the software unit pairs operating-system installation with machine learning; typing rises to 30 wpm. (teacher guide — evidence, PARTIAL)

Egypt's own way (Grade 7, Term 2 student book): Term 2 pivots from applications to computing itself — AI, sensors and robots, then a Scratch-to-Python bridge in the same term.

Mongolia's own way (Grade 12): the final book measures everything against Mongolia itself — the ITU index and Mongolia's slipping rank in it, 36,760 km of fibre to 299 soums, the Mongolsat-1 satellite, IoT chips on livestock, telemedicine at the State Central Hospital, the ХУР state data-exchange and ТҮЦ service machines, nano-products from horse bone and ger covers — and its chapter projects are real Mongolian systems: a school ICT-development survey, a study of the ebarimt VAT-receipt system, and a Small Basic "Bouncing Ball" game; programming examples draw the alkhan khee meander with a Turtle subprogram.

Kuwait's own way (Grade 5 Digital Explorer Term 2, printed title — records name it 'Our Digital World'): Grade 5 pairs a data-analysis lesson with a full Excel journey ('My Special Table', 1–4), then moves from screen coding to physical computing: robots in our lives, then micro:bit programming in MakeCode (Basic, Input and Logic block families) with an 'introduce yourself by programming' project.

Kuwait's own way (Grade 5 Digital Explorer Term 1): Grade 5 leaves ScratchJr for full Scratch (installed and online), but first comes a values-and-AI unit — citizenship, smart vs non-smart devices, what AI is, robots and their parts — and programming itself is taught as a four-stage craft (set the goal, the smart plan, the flowchart, then the code) before a single block is dragged.

Laos's own way (Grade 10 ICT student book): the upper-secondary course turns conceptual — a full information-systems syllabus (organisations, TPS/MIS/DSS/EIS/expert/office systems, the development life cycle), then the series' deepest security chapter (firewalls, cookies, spyware, spam, employee monitoring, privacy law), closing with artificial intelligence, with DOS and Unix taught alongside Windows in the senior years' background.

Greece's own way (Grade 7, Pliroforiki A' Gymnasiou, 2024): the new Gymnasium informatics course is built around algorithms from the first page (a Python binary example in Unit 1), then hardware/software, the internet with a full copyright/Creative Commons/AI-copyright sequence, cybersecurity with cryptography, word processing, algorithmics, Scratch storytelling/chatbot projects, and a long society unit (robots, drones, autonomous cars, e-government, AI in education/medicine).

Greece's own way (Grade 10, Efarmoges Pliroforikis A' Lykeiou): the older Lykeio applications book in four thematic units — hardware/software and applications, programming environments and creating applications (life cycle, modern IDEs, mobile-app and 3D object-oriented programming), communication and the internet (HTML5/CSS, learning online), and collaboration and safety online (cloud applications, telework, social networks, privacy).

Switzerland's own way (Grades 1–2, Décodage 1re–2e): Vaud's Romandie digital-education curriculum is taught in three axes — Science informatique (SI), Médias (M), Usages (U) — almost entirely UNPLUGGED at this age: algorithms are learned with the body (robot game, crane game, hand-washing, getting dressed, a crêpe recipe), a physical sorting machine, and the Blue-Bot floor robot; the class also builds a collective digital “charter book” of good digital habits across the whole cycle.

Switzerland's own way (Grades 3–4, Décodage 3e–4e): the 3e–4e volume repeats the 1re–2e unplugged activities at a higher level (loops and debugging join the robot and crane games and the sorting machine) and adds three new strands: Thymio robotics, a full ScratchJr scenario, and media “parcours” — Parcours des bestioles (how images influence emotions), Parcours du partage (image rights, digital traces, thinking before sharing) and Parcours de la pub (recognising and creating advertising). Pixel paravent introduces binary coding as transmitting a black-and-white image across a screen with an agreed code; Deviner la carte teaches the conditional through a magic trick and its logigramme.

Switzerland's own way (Grades 5–6, Décodage 5e–6e): Cycle 2 moves from unplugged games to real tools while keeping the game frame: the imaginary city “Square CT” teaches classification, decision trees, conditionals, indentation and flowcharts (logigrammes); ScratchJr tells emotion and climate stories; the Swiss Thymio robot is programmed in VPL; data is taught through binary pixel coding and colour coding; and “Enquêtes” (inquiries) teach addition-as-algorithm, debugging, sorting, the Nim winning strategy, Caesar ciphers and sensor experiments. Médias/Usages scenarios cover cyberbullying, screen practices, news and media stereotypes.

Switzerland's own way (Grades 7–8, Décodage 7e–8e): at 7e–8e the Décodage course becomes a real computer-science course under the Plan d'études romand EN 22/23: Thymio VPL missions (including Morse communication and self-driving-car autonomy levels), a full Scratch discovery course (movement, shortening programs, sub-programs as own blocks, remixing), a Scratch video-game project run in “studios” with a cahier des charges, data work (Huffman-style compression via Zip la marmotte, binary/octal/hex/ASCII codings, file sizes), access security (passwords, file encryption, phishing, authentication), home networks at two levels (IP addresses; message routing, packets, TCP and encryption), and inquiries into sorting, sorting networks, Caesar decryption and parity-bit error detection. Médias/Usages covers social networks and digital identity/footprint, information sources, cyberbullying and the environmental cost of digital technology.

Bangladesh's own way (Grade 7, Digital Technology): the Grade 7 book continues the Learning-Experience project format — intellectual property, virtual identity, cybercrime, robotics-through-pseudocode and e-services are each learned by doing a class project with the recurring robot character Nano. The NCTB Digital Technology line runs in parallel English and Bangla editions; placements are cited from the English Version with the Bangla Version parallel spot-checked.

Bangladesh's own way (HSC ICT, Classes XI–XII and Alim): one compulsory ICT book for every HSC and Alim student in the country, saturated with Bangladesh itself: Bangabandhu Satellite-1 and its ground stations, bdjobs and chakri.com job portals, Daraz and Bikroy e-commerce, the Walton factory's automated production line, the National ID card used to teach primary keys and its database used to teach RDBMS at work, a full Bangla Unicode table and a worked example of putting Bangla text on a web page with meta charset, the national letter-grade scale (A+ from 80) as the else-if programme, "I Love Bangladesh" as the loop example text, an algorithm for reaching college by bicycle or rickshaw, and the Digital Bangladesh programme — National Data Centre, hi-tech parks, Startup Bangladesh and Bangladesh Bank's QR-based Cashless Bangladesh. C is the HSC programming language, taught with scanf/printf in Code::Blocks.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 9 G9T2 Student): Cyber security is built from the CIA pillars outwards to encryption taught hands-on with the Caesar and Rail Fence ciphers; robotics is taught in a virtual simulator playground before any physical robot.

Vietnam's own way (Grade 10 Chuyên đề Tin học 10 Khoa học máy tính): the CS study-topics book for Grade 10 is not more Python — it is a full educational-robotics course (a STEM orientation, per its preface): build the robot, connect it to the computer, then program it to move, sense obstacles and follow a line on a test course (sa bàn).


Pakistan's own way (Grade 6 Computer Science 6): every unit opens with Students Learning Outcomes, is dotted with "Do you Know?", "Extra Bit!" and "Tips" boxes, and closes with Summary, MCQ/short/detailed exercises, project-based and activity-based questions, with an answers key at the back. Digital skills (Windows desktop, file management, Paint 3D, a typing tutor) are taught hands-on BEFORE programming; digital citizenship is framed through Pakistani citizenship first; the entrepreneurship unit teaches from Pakistani founders (Foodpanda/EatOye, Shopsy.pk, Zero Makeup, Investors Lounge).

Pakistan's own way (Grade 7 Computer Science 7): Grade 7 opens with emerging technologies and a deep ports-and-peripherals hardware treatment, then teaches email all the way to DKIM/SPF/DMARC authentication and POP3/IMAP/SMTP protocols; binary data representation (ASCII/Unicode, RGB colour, sound sampling) is taught inside the Programming unit, and entrepreneurship becomes business-plan craft — promotion, value proposition, quality assurance, SEO and platform business models, with Twitter and Snapchat origin stories.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kobdikova G1 Digital Literacy): every keyword is given in three languages (Kazakh–Russian–English); the same national curriculum frame as the Almatykitap Grade 1 book (information etiquette / programming / robots, themes "Journey" and "Traditions and folklore") but this Arman-PV team teaches robotics on LEGO Mindstorms Education EV3 instead of WeDo 2.0, and Scratch through Kazakh folklore heroes (Aldar Kose, Makta Kyz) and national instruments (dombyra, kobyz, zhetigen); each project lesson is tiered A/B/C by difficulty.

Kazakhstan's own way (Sagimbaeva G1 Digital Literacy): Grade 1 digital literacy is organised by cross-cutting themes ("Journey"; "Traditions and folklore" — a linear algorithm is taught through setting up a yurt); Scratch programming and Lego Education WeDo 2.0 robotics already start in Grade 1; lessons carry QR codes to videos.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G5 Informatika): Grade 5 Informatika by the Almatykitap team drops Scratch entirely: information theory first (transmission, encryption, binary, graphics in binary code), then raster work in Paint against vector work with curves, and half the year is EV3 robotics beyond simple movement — the gyroscopic sensor, line following and robo-sumo competitions.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kobdikova G5 Informatika): the Arman-PV Grade 5 book teaches the same five national sections as the other two publishers, but every second paragraph is a practicum or a "creating a project" lesson, and the safety section ends in cloud skills — placing, changing and downloading shared-access files — plus mini-projects on setting passwords on documents and preparing a document for print. The vector editor is Inkscape (named in the glossary), the raster editor Paint.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukhametzhanova G5 Informatika, 2020 edition): the Atamura team's 2020 Grade 5 book follows the same five national sections in numbered-paragraph form (1.1–5.5) with end-of-section final tasks; its graphics strand works in Paint for raster and Inkscape for vector (canvas size, SVG, Bezier pen), and the introduction also promises a first taste of 3D technologies (3D model, 3D editor, 3D printing) inside the graphics work.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G2 teacher guide «Цифровая грамотность. Методическое руководство» (Russian + Kazakh editions)): the guide teaches the same Grade 2 year as full scripted lesson plans (objectives referenced to the state programme codes, e.g. 2.1.3.1, differentiated tasks for all/most/some pupils, formative-assessment notes); its section names differ slightly from the student books («Текст и мультимедиа», «Роботы в нашей жизни»), and it adds lessons the student books fold together — robot wheel and movement blocks, the touch sensor, and exchanging data between programmes.

Kazakhstan's own way (Sagimbaeva G1 «Цифровая грамотность»): two cartoon guides (Arman and Saule, themselves first-graders) lead the year; lessons are told as a journey, QR codes open lesson videos, and from lesson 10 the class builds and programmes LEGO-style robots (windmill model, labyrinth runs).

Kazakhstan's own way (Kobdikova G1 «Цифрлық сауаттылық»): the Arman-PV Grade 1 book teaches programming through Scratch from §6 (creating a project, the «Қош келдің, Наурыз!» project, saving/opening a project, a «Музыкалық аспаптар» project) where the Almatykitap book uses an unnamed game environment, and its safety section adds a lesson on unwanted contacts online.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G5 «Информатика»): Grade 5 in the Almatykitap line is a robotics year: after information representation (encryption, binary, binary-coded graphics) and raster/vector graphics, two full sections run LEGO robots — gyro sensor, turns, line following and robo-sumo competitions — before a closing safety section that ends with shared folders and mini-projects.
