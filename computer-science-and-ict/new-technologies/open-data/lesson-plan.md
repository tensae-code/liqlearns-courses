## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What open data is** — **Open data** is data **published so that anyone may access, use and share it** — typically by governments and public bodies about the public world: transport, weather, maps, budgets, statistics. Its marks are three: it is **free to reach** (published on the web), **free to use** (a licence that permits reuse, including building services and businesses on it), and **machine-readable** (in formats programs can work with, not only pages people can read). Openness is a decision by the publisher, not a property of the data — the same table is open or closed by the terms it is published under.
2. **What open data is for — and its care** — Open data's uses are the unit's civic list: **citizens checking** what is done in their name (the budget read by anyone), **services built** on public ground (the journey planner standing on the timetable), **research and journalism** standing on evidence all may inspect. Its care is the mirror: what is opened must be opened **without persons inside** — personal data is not opened, only **aggregated and anonymised** where people contributed — so openness and the unit's privacy teaching stand as one lesson's two hands.
