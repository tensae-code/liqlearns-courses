# IoT applications — home, city, health, cars and farms

## Do it first

Count the IoT applications hiding inside one ordinary hour of your day: waking to a phone, travelling past watched junctions, a meter reading itself, a lock that knows your code. Then read the book's full set — home, city, supply chain, health, cars, outlets — and see what your hour missed.

## Sub lesson 1 — The application set

**Smart home** — **smoke detector, light bulbs, appliances, door and window locks, a pet monitoring camera, a smart thermostat**.

**Smart city** — **traffic management, waste management, water distribution**.

**Supply chain** — **real-time goods tracking and inventory information**.

**Connected health** — **patient care, calorie expenditure, heart rate, temperature, activity levels**.

**Connected cars** — **automatic billing, insurance and parking**.

**Smart outlets** — **switching a device on/off remotely and tracking its energy**.

[Picture 30121 — IoT applications everywhere]

The book's industry table widens the same picture: **home control, remote patient monitoring, fitness tracking, factory production-line control and asset tracking, automatic watering and soil monitoring in agriculture, connected cars and roads, and smart-city traffic and parking**.

## Sub lesson 2 — The connected home in detail (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide inventories the connected home pupils work from: **lighting control, gardening, safety and security, air-quality and water-quality monitoring, voice assistants, switches, locks, and energy and water meters** — and pupils must also **identify the devices and connectivity each use needs**.
