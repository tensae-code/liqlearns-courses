# Lesson plan — IoT applications — home, city, health, cars and farms

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** New Technologies · **Lesson:** IoT applications — home, city, health, cars and farms
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Emerging Technologies (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** IoT at work: smart homes, smart cities, tracked supply chains, connected health, connected cars and smart outlets — and the guide inventories the connected home in detail.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the IoT doorway, advantages and how-it-works lessons. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or in steps — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The application set** — smart home, smart city, supply chain, connected health, connected cars, smart outlets — and the industry table behind them.
2. **The connected home in detail (teacher guide — evidence)** — lighting control, gardening, safety and security, air and water quality, voice assistants, switches, locks, energy and water meters — each with its device and connectivity.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the other lessons of this unit. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the six application areas with examples.
2. Read the industry table's wider set.
3. Inventory the connected home from the guide, with devices and connectivity.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Count the IoT applications within one hour of your own day — waking, travelling, learning, eating, sleeping. Then read the book's set and see what you missed. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the parts below, in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 30121]: a picture of a map of application scenes — home, city junction, hospital, car, field — each with a sensor dot — the student points to each scene and names its application area and one item inside it.
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 30125]: a picture of a cutaway house with each named IoT use labelled in its room or garden (teacher guide — evidence) — the student walks room by room, naming each use and the device it needs.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading this lesson's key words aloud — the student listens, then says each word and what it means.
3. **Say it to your companion** — explain the lesson back in your own words (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the lesson retold in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-or-false, fill-in-the-blank, short-answer — served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the doing block at the end of lesson/lesson.md.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes smart home and smart city → home items live inside one household (locks, bulbs, thermostat); city items run a whole city (traffic, waste, water distribution). Thinks the guide's inventory replaces the book's → it details the same home from the teaching side; both are stocked, the guide's labelled as evidence.
