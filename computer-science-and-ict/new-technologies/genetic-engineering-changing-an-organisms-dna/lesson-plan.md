## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Recombinant DNA — cutting and joining the text** — **Genetic engineering** changes an organism's **DNA** deliberately. Its core craft is **recombinant DNA**: a chosen gene is cut from one organism's sequence and joined into another's, so the receiver gains a trait its own lineage never had — a bacterium making human insulin, a plant resisting an insect. The gene is found and checked by bioinformatics; enzymes do the cutting and joining in the laboratory; the changed organism then copies the new text into every cell as it grows.
2. **GMOs — and Bangladesh's genome work** — Organisms carrying such changes are **GMOs — genetically modified organisms** — and the book grounds them at home: the **Bangladesh Rice Research Institute (BRRI)** works on rice varieties for the country's real trials — flood, salt water, drought — and Bangladeshi scientists **decoded the jute genome**, reading the national fibre's whole text so its breeding can be designed instead of guessed. The lesson closes with the field's standing questions — safety testing, labelling, who owns a gene — which a citizen of a farming country will vote on, and should vote on understanding the craft first.
