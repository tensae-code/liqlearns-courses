# Lesson plan — IoT advantages — and what can go wrong

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** New Technologies · **Lesson:** IoT advantages — and what can go wrong
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Emerging Technologies (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** IoT brings efficiency, technical optimisation, convenience, conservation and personalisation — against limitations of security and privacy, dependence on connection and power, complexity, and higher cost.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the IoT doorway lesson before it. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or in steps — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the five advantages with examples.
2. Name the four limitations.
3. Explain the failure chain when connection or power fails.
4. Name the sensitive fields the security warning names.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — One phone in your hand runs your lights, your heat, your TV. Delightful — until the power cuts, or a stranger finds the door you left in your own house's systems. Weigh both before reading. (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes optimisation with efficiency → efficiency is DATA turned into saved resources (the voice-assistant example); optimisation is CONTROL gathered into one phone. Thinks cost means the devices are pricey only → the book's cost is the whole chain: many devices, bought, set up and professionally installed.
