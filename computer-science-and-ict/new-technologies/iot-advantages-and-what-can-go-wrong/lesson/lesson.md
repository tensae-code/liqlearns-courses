# IoT advantages — and what can go wrong

## Do it first

One phone in your hand runs your lights, your heat and your TV — you never walk to a switch again. Delightful. Until the power cuts and everything stops together. Or until a stranger finds a way into the systems of your house. Both sides are real; this lesson weighs them, five gains against four warnings.

## Sub lesson 1 — Advantages — five gains

**Efficiency** — **trustworthy real-time data turned into useful information, saving resources**: asking a **voice assistant such as Siri, Alexa, Google Home or Cortana** instead of opening a device.

**Technical optimisation** — **controlling TV volume, thermostats and lights from one smartphone** instead of operating many devices.

**Convenience and improved customer experience** — **understanding customers' pain points and giving quick, quality solutions**.

**Conservation** — **monitoring traffic, water and electricity use and air quality**, helping **smart cities**.

**Personalisation** — **devices learning your likes and dislikes and triggering services to match**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Limitations — four warnings

**Security and privacy** — sensitive uses in **healthcare and banking** carry real risk, and **privacy law now requires protection**.

**Connectivity and power dependence** — **when either fails, the device fails, and everything connected to it fails with it**.

**Complexity and integration** — **complicated design and deployment, unsettled protocols, and incompatible makers' hardware**.

**Higher cost** — **many devices to buy, set up and professionally install**.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the five advantages and the four limitations, one line each, and which limitation would hit your home hardest.
