# Lesson plan — Hosting a website — your own server or the cloud?

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** New Technologies · **Lesson:** Hosting a website — your own server or the cloud?
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Emerging Technologies (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** publishing a website the old way means buying, housing and running your own server; with cloud hosting the provider runs it, you press a button, and the site is public in minutes for much less.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the cloud doorway and both benefits lessons. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or in steps — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Walk the old way: buy, house, public IP, install it all yourself.
2. Walk the cloud way: borrow capacity, provider manages, button, live in minutes.
3. Say why the comparison shows every cloud benefit at once.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks 'hosting' means designing the site → hosting is where the finished site LIVES so the public can reach it; design happens before either road. Thinks the cloud way skips uploading → no: you still upload your content — by pressing a button, not by building a server room.
