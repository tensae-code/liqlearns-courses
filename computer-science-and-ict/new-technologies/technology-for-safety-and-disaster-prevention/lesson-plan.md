## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Warning before harm — sensing, forecasting, alerting** — Disaster technology works **before** the event wherever it can. **Sensors and monitoring** watch the things that become disasters — river levels, ground movement, weather, fire — and **forecasting** turns their readings into a prediction with a place and a time. The chain only saves anyone if the last link holds: the **alert** must reach the people in the path, in a form they understand, with time to act. Egypt's book teaches this as information technology, not geography, because every link is an information system: data collected, transmitted, processed, and delivered as a decision — move, shelter, close, evacuate.
2. **Limiting harm when the event comes — systems that keep working** — When prevention fails, technology's second job is **limiting harm**: emergency communication that survives the disaster itself, maps and records that tell responders where people and hazards are, and coordination systems that put help where it is needed first. The design rule running through all of it is **graceful failure**: a safety system must be built expecting the worst day, not the average one — backup power, backup links, and information copied somewhere the event cannot reach. The book pairs this with its safety-signs lesson deliberately: the sign on the wall and the national warning system are the same idea at two scales — information, placed where the decision is made, before the decision is urgent.
