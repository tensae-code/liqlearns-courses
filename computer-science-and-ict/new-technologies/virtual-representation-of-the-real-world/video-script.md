# Video script — Virtual Representation of the Real World

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

A **virtual representation** is a digital stand-in: the real thing is captured as **data** — a person's record, a map of a town, a model's shape, a sensor's readings — chosen to keep the features the task needs and drop the rest. A map represents a town by keeping roads and dropping trees; a game character represents a player by keeping position and score. Armenia's Grade 7 book opens with this idea (1.2 'Virtual representation of the real world') and returns to it when Python begins (6.2 'The real becomes virtual'): representation is the bridge every program crosses.
A representation is **good** when it is faithful **where it matters** for its task: a flight simulator must represent controls exactly and can simplify clouds; a weather model must represent pressure and wind and can ignore birds. Representations also **age**: the real thing changes, and an un-updated representation quietly becomes wrong — which is why systems refresh data, and why every user should ask of any screen: what does this stand for, what did it leave out, and when was it last true?
