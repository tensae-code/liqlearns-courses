# Lesson plan — Fog computing — processing at the edge, near the data

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** New Technologies · **Lesson:** Fog computing — processing at the edge, near the data
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Emerging Technologies (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** fog computing brings the cloud closer: nearby fog nodes decide what can be handled locally and what must travel on — quicker responses, lower latency — and the guide sharpens it as the intelligent gateway.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the cloud lessons before it — fog is the cloud, extended and brought closer. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or in steps — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Fog — the cloud brought closer** — billions of users congest the central cloud; fog nodes near the source process and store, deciding what goes up; quicker, lower latency, closer, wider, more mobile; video surveillance the worked example.
2. **Fog as the intelligent gateway (teacher guide — evidence)** — distributed against cloud's centralised; the gateway sorts send-up from handle-here, aggregates to save bandwidth, keeps sensitive data local for privacy and security, and fits time-sensitive work.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the other lessons of this unit. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say why fog exists: congestion at the central cloud.
2. Say what fog nodes decide, and where they sit.
3. Name fog's gains: speed, latency, proximity, reach, mobility.
4. State the guide's gateway picture: sorting, aggregating, privacy, and time-sensitive fits.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — A thousand cameras stream video to one far-away cloud, all day. The road jams. What if most of that video never needed to travel at all? Hold that question. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the parts below, in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 30117]: a picture of cameras and sensors sending to a nearby fog node, with only a thin stream continuing to a distant cloud — the student traces the thick local flow and the thin onward stream and says who decides the split.
   - ADD GIF [GIF 30128]: a picture of a video stream being checked locally at the fog node, with only an alert travelling to the cloud — the student watches ordinary frames stay local and says what finally travels, and when.
   - ADD GIF [GIF 30129]: a picture of data packets being stopped and handled at the fog gateway while only some travel on to the cloud (teacher guide — evidence) — the student counts handled-here packets against sent-up packets and states the gateway's rule.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading this lesson's key words aloud — the student listens, then says each word and what it means.
3. **Say it to your companion** — explain the lesson back in your own words (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the lesson retold in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-or-false, fill-in-the-blank, short-answer — served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the doing block at the end of lesson/lesson.md.
