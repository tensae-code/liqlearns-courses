## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Types and sources** — The types repeat the analysis sort at collection time: **quantitative** (numbers) and **qualitative** (descriptions) — the collector chooses instruments that capture the type the question needs. **Sources** divide into **primary** (data you collect yourself, for your question — fresh, fitted, expensive) and **secondary** (data collected by others — records, statistics, published tables: cheap and fast, but fitted to someone else's question and someone else's care).
2. **Collection methods** — The primary methods: **observation** (watching and recording — honest about behaviour, silent about reasons), **questionnaires and surveys** (many people, the same questions — breadth, at the mercy of honest answers), **interviews** (depth and reasons, few people), and **experiments / measurements** (instruments and controlled changes — the strongest for causes). Jordan's Grade 10 lesson weighs each method against the question, the time and the truth it can carry: a method is not good or bad, only fitted or unfitted — and the collection plan is written before the collecting, or the data arrives answering a question nobody asked.
