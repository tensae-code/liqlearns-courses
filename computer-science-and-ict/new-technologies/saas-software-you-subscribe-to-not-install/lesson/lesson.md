# SaaS — software you subscribe to, not install

## Do it first

Think of software you use that you never installed, never bought in a box, and do not own — it simply opens and works, on any device you pick up. Someone else hosts it, updates it and keeps it alive. That arrangement has a name, and it is the most common of the three service models.

## Sub lesson 1 — SaaS — hosted software by subscription

**Software as a Service (SaaS)** is a **distribution model where a vendor hosts applications and makes them available to customers over a network or the internet**.

You **subscribe monthly or yearly instead of buying upfront and installing**. It **works on any internet-enabled device**, and carries tasks such as **accounting, sales, invoicing and planning**.

[Picture 30114 — SaaS, software you subscribe to]

The book's examples: **Google Workspace, Dropbox, Microsoft Office 365, Slack, Cisco WebEx, Concur and GoToMeeting**.

## Sub lesson 2 — The three models compared by benefit (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide compares the three models by what each buys you. **SaaS is the most popular business option**: all you need is **a browser and an internet connection**, the **provider manages all technical issues**, with **easier administration, automatic updates and patch management, compatibility, easier collaboration and global accessibility**.

**PaaS's chief benefit is developer productivity** — faster development and more frequent releases, promoting business agility. **IaaS is the most flexible and dynamic model, cost-effective because it is pay-as-you-go**, and easy to use because **hardware deployment is automated**.

And the guide's reality check: the cloud services pupils already use include **mail services, social-media platforms, file-storage services, Google Classroom, Microsoft's education centre and virtual-meeting applications**.
