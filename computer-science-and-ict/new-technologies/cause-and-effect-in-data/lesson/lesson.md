# Cause and Effect in Data

## Do it first

Ice-cream sales and drowning rise together every summer. Neither causes the other — the sun causes both. This lesson is data analysis's most expensive distinction.

## Sub lesson 1 — Correlation is not cause

Two measures that move together are **correlated**; one **causes** the other only if changing it changes the other. Between correlation and cause stand the traps: a **third factor** driving both (the summer sun), **reverse cause** (the effect mistaken for the cause — do hospitals make people ill?), and **coincidence** in small or cherry-picked data. Jordan's lesson gives the pupil the standing question for every 'X causes Y' headline: what else could move both?

In your mind's eye: correlation is not cause.

## Sub lesson 2 — Establishing cause — the honest roads

Cause is earned, not spotted: by **experiment** (change only X, hold the rest, watch Y — the gold road, not always open), by **mechanism** (a believable chain from X to Y, link by link), by **order and dose** (the cause comes first; more cause, more effect), and by **ruling out** the third factors one by one. The lesson's closing discipline: in data work, say 'is linked to' until the roads are walked — the words 'causes' are spent only when earned, because decisions built on false causes waste the money the analysis was hired to save.

In your mind's eye: establishing cause — the honest roads.
