# Video script — Living Things, Robots and Machines

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

The **game of the living (jeu du vivant)** sorts the world by life's signs: a living thing **is born or germinates, feeds, grows, reproduces, and dies** — its cycle (le cycle de vie) runs from itself, powered from within. A plant at rest still runs the cycle slowly; a stone never starts it. The activities make young pupils argue the border cases — a seed, a flame, a river — because the border is where the definition earns its keep.
A **robot** can imitate life's outside — move, sense, respond, even “learn” — while running no life cycle at all: it does not feed itself, grow, or reproduce; switch it off and nothing dies. An **automaton/machine** is plainer still. The comparison's point is precision, not disappointment: calling a robot “alive” because it moves mistakes the imitation for the thing — and the activities train exactly that distinction, with the robot pupils love as the test case.
