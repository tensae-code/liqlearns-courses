# The Seven-Layer IoT Architecture — and the Five-Layer Model

## Do it first

Three floors were enough to stand the building up. Seven floors let specialists argue precisely about which floor failed. This lesson climbs the full stack.

## Sub lesson 1 — The seven layers — from things to business

The seven-layer model runs: **1 perception** (sensors and things), **2 network** (transport), **3 middleware / processing** (storage and analysis), **4 application** (services), **5 business** (the organisation's goals, plans and profits built on the services), with **security** and **management** standing as vertical concerns across all layers in the model's full telling — some tellings count them in the seven. Each added layer over the three-layer map names work the simple map left hidden.

## Sub lesson 2 — The five-layer model — the middle map

The **five-layer model** splits the three-layer skeleton once: perception, **transport**, **processing**, application, **business**. It is the map many textbooks and vendors actually use — fine enough to assign responsibilities, coarse enough to hold in the head. Jordan's lesson closes with the choice itself: the model is chosen to fit the audience and the argument, and fluency is moving between the three maps without losing the system.
