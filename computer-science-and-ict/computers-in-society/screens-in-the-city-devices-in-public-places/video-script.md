# Video script — Screens in the City — Devices in Public Places

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

A city runs on public screens: departure and arrival boards, ticket and parking machines, cash machines, advertising panels, information points, the display at the bus stop counting minutes. Unlike home screens, these belong to everyone and no one: they are **used standing up, quickly, among strangers**, and they must be readable at a glance. The Décodage scenario takes pupils out — in pictures and on walks — to read their own city.
Using a city device well is a small craft: read the whole screen before touching; choose deliberately (the mouse or finger selects — a choice made is an action done); shield private entries like codes from other eyes; take your ticket, card and change; and leave the device ready for the next person. The same care the course teaches for classroom machines applies doubled in public, where the machine serves a queue.
