## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Buying considerations — needs first, then the machine** — Buying well starts with the **user**: what work must this computer do, and for whom? Against those needs the buyer weighs the **prepared specification** — processor, hard disk, monitor and peripherals — and the **price**, together. Price alone never decides: a cheap computer that does not fit the user's needs is not a saving, it is the wrong computer bought cheaply. The specification is prepared for a real user with real work, and every buying consideration is checked against it.
2. **After-sales service — the help that comes after the sale** — **After-sales service** is the help the seller gives **after** the purchase: the **warranty or guarantee** that faults within a set time will be repaired or put right, **repairs** when the machine breaks, and **service** that keeps it working. It counts inside the buying decision, not after it: a computer that cannot be repaired nearby, or whose seller gives no service, costs its owner time and money for years — so two computers at the same price are not the same buy when only one of them stands behind its sale.
