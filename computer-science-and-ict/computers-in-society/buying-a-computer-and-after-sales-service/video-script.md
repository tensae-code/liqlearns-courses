# Video script — Buying a Computer and After-Sales Service

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Buying well starts with the **user**: what work must this computer do, and for whom? Against those needs the buyer weighs the **prepared specification** — processor, hard disk, monitor and peripherals — and the **price**, together. Price alone never decides: a cheap computer that does not fit the user's needs is not a saving, it is the wrong computer bought cheaply. The specification is prepared for a real user with real work, and every buying consideration is checked against it.
**After-sales service** is the help the seller gives **after** the purchase: the **warranty or guarantee** that faults within a set time will be repaired or put right, **repairs** when the machine breaks, and **service** that keeps it working. It counts inside the buying decision, not after it: a computer that cannot be repaired nearby, or whose seller gives no service, costs its owner time and money for years — so two computers at the same price are not the same buy when only one of them stands behind its sale.
