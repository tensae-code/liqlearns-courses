## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Choosing where to look — purpose before pages** — Wise browsing starts before the first click: name what you are looking for, then choose the kind of source that can actually answer it — an official site for a rule, a reference work for a fact, a maker's page for a product's own specifications. Indonesia's Grade 11 book teaches this as menjelajah informasi digital dengan bijak — exploring digital information wisely — because the search box is a doorway to every kind of page at once, and the browser who knows their purpose can tell a doorway that fits from one that merely shouts.
2. **Reading the page's intentions — and your own trail** — Every page wants something: your reading, your click, your money, your agreement. The wise browser asks who made the page and what they gain if it is believed or shared, checks the date and the evidence offered, and compares before settling. Browsing also leaves a trail and takes time — the two quiet costs — so wisdom includes closing what does not serve the purpose and keeping what does in a place it can be found again.
