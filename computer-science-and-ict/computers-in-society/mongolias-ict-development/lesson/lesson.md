# Mongolia's ICT Development

## Do it first

The index measured the world; this lesson measures home. Mongolia's Grade 12 book tells its own country's ICT story with the dates and distances left in — a national account its pupils are meant to know, argue with, and continue.

## Sub lesson 1 — The build — policy, fibre, satellite

Mongolia's development is steered by the **State ICT Policy 2017–2025**. Its ground facts are steppe-scale: internet service began on **17 January 1996** with about **500 users**; by 2017 they numbered **2.6 million**. The backbone is **36,760 km of fibre** reaching **299 soums across 21 aimags**, with **VSAT** satellite links and **triple-play IPTV** (television, internet and phone in one service) carrying what fibre cannot. In 2017 Mongolia's own satellite, **Mongolsat-1**, took the story to orbit — for a country of Mongolia's distances, space is not prestige but infrastructure.

## Sub lesson 2 — The divide — the lesson inside the account

The same account names its wound: the **digital divide** — the gap between the connected city and the far soum, between those the fibre reached and those it has not. Mongolia's falling place in the world's index (the previous lesson's table) is taught as the divide's shadow in statistics. The book's purpose is plain and is the course's last civic lesson in this strand: the account is given to the pupils because the account is unfinished — the fibre's next kilometres, the divide's closing, are quite literally the homework of the generation reading the book.
