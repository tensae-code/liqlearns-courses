# Lesson plan — Mongolia's ICT Development

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Computers in Society · **Lesson:** Mongolia's ICT Development
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Mongolia (2026-11-14) — in the title Computers in Society on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Mongolia's student books (Ministry of Education, Culture and Science(s) of Mongolia, Ulaanbaatar), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree and live lesson content were checked for the slug and for near matches before building.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Mongolia's ICT development as its own final book tells it — the State ICT Policy 2017–2025, internet service from 17 January 1996 (~500 users then, 2.6 million by 2017), 36,760 km of fibre reaching 299 soums in 21 aimags, VSAT and triple-play IPTV, the digital divide, and the Mongolsat-1 satellite (2017)
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The build — policy, fibre, satellite** — Mongolia's development is steered by the **State ICT Policy 2017–2025**. Its ground facts are steppe-scale: internet service began on **17 January 1996** with about **500 users**; by 2017 they numbered **2.6 million**. The backbone is **36,760 km of fibre** reaching **299 soums across 21 aimags**, with **VSAT** satellite links and **triple-play IPTV** (television, internet and phone in one service) carrying what fibre cannot. In 2017 Mongolia's own satellite, **Mongolsat-1**, took the story to orbit — for a country of Mongolia's distances, space is not prestige but infrastructure.
2. **The divide — the lesson inside the account** — The same account names its wound: the **digital divide** — the gap between the connected city and the far soum, between those the fibre reached and those it has not. Mongolia's falling place in the world's index (the previous lesson's table) is taught as the divide's shadow in statistics. The book's purpose is plain and is the course's last civic lesson in this strand: the account is given to the pupils because the account is unfinished — the fibre's next kilometres, the divide's closing, are quite literally the homework of the generation reading the book.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
