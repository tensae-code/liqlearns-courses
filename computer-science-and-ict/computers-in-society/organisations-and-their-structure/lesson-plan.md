## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What an organisation is** — An **organisation** is a group of people joined to reach **shared goals** they could not reach alone — a school, a ministry, a factory, a shop. It has people, work to divide, and resources to manage, and it survives only by coordinating all three. Laos opens its information-systems chapter here because a system is never built in the abstract: it is built for this organisation, with these goals, and it succeeds or fails by serving them.
2. **The structure — vertical levels and horizontal functions** — An organisation's **structure** arranges it two ways at once. **Vertically**, it stands in **levels**: a top that directs, a middle that manages, and a floor that does the daily work — authority and responsibility step down the levels, and reports step up. **Horizontally**, it divides into **functions or departments** side by side — production, sales, accounts, personnel — each doing one kind of work. The drawing of both together (who stands above whom, and which department does what) is the organisation's chart, and every later lesson in the chapter — levels of decision, types of system — is read off this chart.
