# Information Technology Laws and Regulations — the UU ITE

## Do it first

Information technology laws and regulations — Indonesia's UU ITE, the Electronic Information and Transactions Law: what its articles forbid online (Articles 27, 28, 29) and why a networked country writes such a law

## Sub lesson 1 — Why a law for electronic information — the UU ITE

When a country's life moves online — its letters become messages, its money transfers, its quarrels posts — the law must follow it there. Indonesia's answer is the **UU ITE** (Undang-Undang Informasi dan Transaksi Elektronik), the Electronic Information and Transactions Law: the statute that makes electronic information and transactions legally real, and sets what may not be done with them. Grades 11 and 12 both teach it — Grade 11 as the regulation chapter of digital literacy, Grade 12 inside social impact — because a citizen online needs to know the law of the place they are standing, and online they are standing in it.

## Sub lesson 2 — The forbidden acts — Articles 27, 28 and 29

Grade 12 names the articles pupils meet most. **Article 27** forbids distributing electronic content that violates decency, that gambles, that insults or defames, and that extorts or threatens in the taking. **Article 28** forbids spreading false news (berita bohong) that harms consumers, and content meant to raise hatred or hostility against groups. **Article 29** forbids sending threats of violence or menace aimed at a person. The pattern under the articles is the lesson: the law does not forbid the network; it forbids acts — the same acts the ethics lessons forbid — now with the state's penalties behind them.
