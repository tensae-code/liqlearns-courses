# Respecting Diversity in a Digital World

## Do it first

No two fingerprints in your class match. No two voices, no two families, no two home languages in the story of one classroom. Grade 6's final experience builds a tree of those differences — and this lesson asks what the tree teaches the digital world its growers are entering.

## Sub lesson 1 — The diversity tree — differences made visible

Each learner adds their **diversity card** to the class **tree**: the differences that make them themselves — the fingerprint and voice of the companion identity lesson, family, language, birthplace, foods, festivals, skills. Hung together, the cards show the class its true shape: not one kind of person repeated, but a tree of differences that share one root — the class itself. The lesson's first teaching is simple sight: diversity is not a problem the class has; it is what the class is made of.

## Sub lesson 2 — Respect online — the tree's rule carried out

The digital world widens the class to the world: every screen holds people more different from you than anyone in your street. The tree's rule travels: **difference is met with curiosity and respect, never mockery** — in comments, in group chats, in what we forward about other people's customs, languages and beliefs. The same technology that carries mockery fastest can carry respect just as far, and the Grade 6 grower of the tree is the citizen who chooses which one rides on their name.
