# Lesson plan — Computers Helping Learners with Disabilities

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Computers in Society · **Lesson:** Computers Helping Learners with Disabilities
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Fiji (2026-11-14) — in the title Computers in Society on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Fiji's student books (Fiji Ministry of Education / Technology and Employment Skills Training (TEST) section, Suva), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Lets a blind learner type and read by touch — the screen's words turned into touch.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Help for learners who cannot see or hear well** — For a learner who is **blind or sees little**, the computer speaks and touches back: a **Braille keyboard** and Braille display turn the screen's words into touch; **screen-reading and magnification** turn print into speech or into print large enough to read. For a learner who **hears little**, the computer shows what others hear — captions, written instructions, visual signals. Fiji names the Braille keyboard itself: in its schools this is not theory but the equipment list of an inclusive classroom.
2. **Help for learners who learn differently — tutorials and patience of the machine** — The book's second help is the **tutorial program**: lessons on the computer that a learner can take **at their own pace**, repeating without embarrassment, with the machine patient at the tenth try as at the first. For learners whose bodies make writing hard, the computer also takes dictation and works by switch or touch. Fiji's point for the whole class: these are not favours — they are how the same lesson reaches every learner, and the learners who use them are doing the same work as everyone else.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

1. Lets a blind learner type and read by touch — the screen's words turned into touch.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil, and a computer with the program this lesson uses, if available.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — A classroom is not fair when only some learners can read the page. Fiji's Years 7–8 book gives computers in society a page it is proud of: the ways computers help disabled students learn alongside everyone else — Braille keyboards, tutorial programs and more. This lesson is that page, grown to a lesson. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of help for learners who cannot see or hear well as the lesson describes it — the student studies the picture and names what each part shows.
   - ADD GIF: a GIF of help for learners who learn differently — tutorials and patience of the machine in action — the student watches once, then says what changed and why.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the lesson's key names and definitions aloud, in the order the lesson gives them — the student listens once through, then reads the same lines aloud along with the voice. (Audio is a described slot in this build; no audio file is made.)
3. **Say it to your companion** — the companion block in lesson/lesson.md.
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the lesson in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-false, matching, short-answer — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the doing block at the end of lesson/lesson.md.
