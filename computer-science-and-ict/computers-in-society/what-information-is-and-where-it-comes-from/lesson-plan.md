## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What information is** — **Information** is something that reduces your uncertainty about the world: a fact, an event, a measurement, stated so it can be checked. Not everything on a screen is information — decoration, opinion and noise travel the same wires. A piece earns the name when it tells you something about the world that you could, in principle, verify — and when it is new to you: the same fact told twice informs once.
2. **What a source is** — Every information has a **source**: the person, organisation, instrument or document it comes from. Naming the source is the beginning of judging: a measurement's source is the instrument and who read it; a report's source is the reporter and who sent them. “It says online” names no source at all. The fiche's rule is the media course in one line: no source, no trust — first find where it comes from, then decide what it is worth.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Someone says “I saw it online.” Saw what, exactly — and where did it come from before it reached the screen? If you cannot answer the second part, you do not yet hold information. This lesson starts there. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of what information is as the lesson describes it — the student studies the picture and names what each part shows.
   - ADD GIF: a GIF of what a source is in action — the student watches once, then says what changed and why.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the lesson's key names and definitions aloud, in the order the lesson gives them — the student listens once through, then reads the same lines aloud along with the voice. (Audio is a described slot in this build; no audio file is made.)
3. **Say it to your companion** — the companion block in lesson/lesson.md.
