# Video script — Lateral Reading — Checking Whether Content Is True

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

**Lateral reading** (membaca lateral) judges a page by stepping **sideways** from it: open other sources and ask what the rest of the web says about this claim and about the organisation behind the page — instead of reading the page itself more deeply ('vertically') and being persuaded by its own polish. Indonesia's Grade 11 book teaches it as the core move of evaluating content truth: a page's own 'about' text is advertising; what independent sources say about it is evidence. The reader's question changes from 'does this look right?' to 'who else says so?'
Lateral reading completes itself as **triangulation**: a claim is fixed the way a position is fixed — by bearings from **more than one independent point**. Find the claim in sources that do not copy each other; where independent reports agree, confidence is earned; where they split, the split itself is the finding, and the honest reader reports a contested claim as contested. Grade 11 pairs lateral reading with the credibility characteristics of text, image and video: first leave the page, then weigh what you find by the credibility marks.
