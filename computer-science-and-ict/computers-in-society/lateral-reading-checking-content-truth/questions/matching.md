# Lateral Reading — Checking Whether Content Is True — matching

Match each item on the left with the one it belongs to on the right.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What is lateral reading?** → **Judging a page by stepping sideways from it — seeing what other sources say about the claim and its maker**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What is vertical reading, its opposite?** → **Reading the page itself more deeply — and being persuaded by its own polish**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) Why is a page's own 'about' text weak evidence?** → **It is advertising; independent sources' accounts are evidence**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) How does the reader's question change?** → **From 'does this look right?' to 'who else says so?'**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) What is triangulation?** → **Fixing a claim by bearings from more than one independent point, as a position is fixed**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What must the sources in triangulation not do?** → **Copy each other — they must be independent**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What is earned where independent reports agree?** → **Confidence**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What is the finding where they split?** → **The split itself — the claim is reported as contested**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) What does Grade 11 pair lateral reading with?** → **The credibility characteristics of text, image and video sources**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) What is the Indonesian term?** → **Membaca lateral — lateral reading**
