## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Reading laterally — leave the page to judge the page** — **Lateral reading** (membaca lateral) judges a page by stepping **sideways** from it: open other sources and ask what the rest of the web says about this claim and about the organisation behind the page — instead of reading the page itself more deeply ('vertically') and being persuaded by its own polish. Indonesia's Grade 11 book teaches it as the core move of evaluating content truth: a page's own 'about' text is advertising; what independent sources say about it is evidence. The reader's question changes from 'does this look right?' to 'who else says so?'
2. **Triangulation — three bearings on one claim** — Lateral reading completes itself as **triangulation**: a claim is fixed the way a position is fixed — by bearings from **more than one independent point**. Find the claim in sources that do not copy each other; where independent reports agree, confidence is earned; where they split, the split itself is the finding, and the honest reader reports a contested claim as contested. Grade 11 pairs lateral reading with the credibility characteristics of text, image and video: first leave the page, then weigh what you find by the credibility marks.
