# Digital identity management — limit, check and correct your data

## Do it first

Somewhere, a record about you has a wrong birth date. Somewhere else, the same fact about you is stored three times. This lesson is the housekeeping of being a person online — four verbs, done regularly.

## Sub lesson 1 — The four management verbs

**Managing a digital identity means limiting the data a person gives out, avoiding excessive storage, checking what data is stored about you, correcting incorrect personal data and avoiding redundant data**.

None of the five is done once and finished — records change, copies multiply, errors creep in. Management is a habit, not an event.

## Sub lesson 2 — How identity is verified — and the guide's safety list (teacher guide — evidence)

Identity itself can be verified by **database checks, document and identity verification, and biometric verification** — after which **a user can reach services such as banking, health and travel**.

The guide (teacher guide — evidence) adds the practical safety list the student section does not itemise: **learn each platform's privacy options and check your settings regularly; be careful disclosing birthdate, age or location, which data-mining firms and identity thieves can use; use third-party programs with caution; if you accept a stranger's friend request, restrict what they can see; take extra care if you have experienced stalking, harassment or domestic abuse; and log out of accounts you no longer need**.

1. Run the checklist on one of your accounts today: limit one thing given out, check one stored fact, correct or note one error, find one duplicate.
2. Go through the guide's six tips against your own profiles and write done or to-do beside each.
