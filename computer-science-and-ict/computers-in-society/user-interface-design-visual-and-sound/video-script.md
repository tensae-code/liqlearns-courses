# Video script — User Interface Design — Visual and Sound

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

An **interface** is how the user and the program meet, and the chapter sorts its kinds — the **command line** (typed orders), the **menu-driven** meeting, and the **graphical** interface of windows and direct touching — before judging them: the right kind is the one its **users** can work without fear. **Visual design** then governs the graphical face: **colour** used with meaning and restraint (the same colour for the same kind of thing; alarm colours kept rare so they still alarm), **layout** that groups what belongs together, and **error messages written as help** — naming what happened, in the user's words, with the road out.
A program also **speaks in sound**, and the chapter designs that voice: **signals** that confirm (the soft click of a taken action), **warnings** that must cut through without wounding, **spoken and musical** elements where the design calls for them — each sound **earning its place**, because a program that sounds at everything teaches its user to hear nothing. Sound and picture obey one law between them: the interface's every signal, seen or heard, exists to tell the user **what is true now** — and the designer's whole craft is keeping that promise kindly.
