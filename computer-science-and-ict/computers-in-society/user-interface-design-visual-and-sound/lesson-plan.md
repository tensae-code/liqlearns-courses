## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Kinds of interface — and visual design** — An **interface** is how the user and the program meet, and the chapter sorts its kinds — the **command line** (typed orders), the **menu-driven** meeting, and the **graphical** interface of windows and direct touching — before judging them: the right kind is the one its **users** can work without fear. **Visual design** then governs the graphical face: **colour** used with meaning and restraint (the same colour for the same kind of thing; alarm colours kept rare so they still alarm), **layout** that groups what belongs together, and **error messages written as help** — naming what happened, in the user's words, with the road out.
2. **Sound design — the interface's second voice** — A program also **speaks in sound**, and the chapter designs that voice: **signals** that confirm (the soft click of a taken action), **warnings** that must cut through without wounding, **spoken and musical** elements where the design calls for them — each sound **earning its place**, because a program that sounds at everything teaches its user to hear nothing. Sound and picture obey one law between them: the interface's every signal, seen or heard, exists to tell the user **what is true now** — and the designer's whole craft is keeping that promise kindly.
