**Acceptance count: taught in 7 countries (Ethiopia, Cuba, Laos, Greece, Bangladesh, Indonesia, Vietnam).**

Nothing in this lesson claims a page the placements do not give; the one Tip box is LiqLearns' own, marked as such in the lesson and in the table above.

Cuba's own way (Grade 9): Grade 9 is the formal programming-foundations year — algorithms, data, variables, counters/accumulators, flowcharts and pseudocode, taught language-independently — paired with a full cybersecurity chapter (passwords/MFA, safe internet, safe social networks).

Laos's own way (Grade 12 ICT student book): the final year is a modern, applied course — problem-solving method, a long English-titled internet-safety sequence (Internet Safety, Email Tips for Scams and Spam, Smart Social Networking, Cyber harassment/Stalking/Addiction), data summaries and PivotTables for decision-making, copyright and Creative Commons licensing, a full PowerPoint course, and choosing technology for your future occupation.

Greece's own way (Grade 10, Efarmoges Pliroforikis A' Lykeiou): the older Lykeio applications book in four thematic units — hardware/software and applications, programming environments and creating applications (life cycle, modern IDEs, mobile-app and 3D object-oriented programming), communication and the internet (HTML5/CSS, learning online), and collaboration and safety online (cloud applications, telework, social networks, privacy).

Switzerland's own way (Grades 7–8, Décodage 7e–8e): at 7e–8e the Décodage course becomes a real computer-science course under the Plan d'études romand EN 22/23: Thymio VPL missions (including Morse communication and self-driving-car autonomy levels), a full Scratch discovery course (movement, shortening programs, sub-programs as own blocks, remixing), a Scratch video-game project run in “studios” with a cahier des charges, data work (Huffman-style compression via Zip la marmotte, binary/octal/hex/ASCII codings, file sizes), access security (passwords, file encryption, phishing, authentication), home networks at two levels (IP addresses; message routing, packets, TCP and encryption), and inquiries into sorting, sorting networks, Caesar decryption and parity-bit error detection. Médias/Usages covers social networks and digital identity/footprint, information sources, cyberbullying and the environmental cost of digital technology.

Bangladesh's own way (Grade 8, Digital Technology): Grade 8 deepens the same project pattern — a public information-verification campaign, a family phone-safety investigation, a planned civic/e-commerce service, Python with binary logic first, and an online fair for the Asia-Pacific experience. The NCTB Digital Technology line runs in parallel English and Bangla editions; placements are cited from the English Version with the Bangla Version parallel spot-checked.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 12 G12 Informatika student): Grade 12 (original Kurikulum Merdeka edition) is built around making things: single-board computers/controllers and a full Arduino C course (IDE, UnoArduSim simulator, sensors, S.O.S. LED and melody projects), then networks with cyber security and data governance, social impact with the UU ITE law, and a cross-field group project (smart trash bin / waste-bank apps) with journals, reports and peer assessment.

Vietnam's own way (Grade 11 Tin học 11 Khoa học máy tính): from Grade 11 the two orientations are separate books but share a common core (Bài 1–16 here: operating systems, inside the computer, and a full databases strand ending at SQL); the CS-only second half is algorithm engineering in Python — arrays, search and sort, time complexity, stepwise refinement, modular design and building code libraries.

Vietnam's own way (Grade 11 Tin học 11 Tin học ứng dụng): Bài 1–16 are the same common core as the Computer Science orientation book (spot-checked page-identical, e.g. Bài 1); the Applied-only second half is hands-on database administration in Microsoft Access (through backup), then photo editing in GIMP and film-making/animation.
