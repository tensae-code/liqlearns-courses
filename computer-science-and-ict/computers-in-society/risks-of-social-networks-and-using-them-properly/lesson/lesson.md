# Risks of social networks — and using them properly

## Do it first

A stranger online asks for your phone number and where you live. A message calls a classmate a hurtful name, again and again. A page that looks like news is inventing its story. Three dangers, met before any list is given. How many more can you name? The lesson's list has eight.

## The eight risks, named

**Used improperly**, the risks are these — learn them by name:

1. **Being exposed to inappropriate content** — mean, aggressive or sexual comments or images.
2. **Uploading inappropriate content** — photos or videos of yourself or others.
3. **Sharing personal information with strangers** — phone numbers, date of birth, location.
4. **Cyberbullying** — using digital technology to deliberately and repeatedly hurt someone.
5. **Online bullying or harassment.**
6. **Exposure to too much targeted advertising and marketing.**
7. **Personal information being stolen.**
8. **Untrustworthy, misleading content** being easy to find.

## The closing question — your own family's use

The set closes with a question to take home and discuss: **how much do you and your family use social networks — and for what purpose?** Purpose is the dividing line of this whole set: the benefits lesson and this one are the same networks, told by their use.

## Tip — from us, not from the books

**Tip — from us, not from the books:** if a message hurts you or will not stop, do not answer it and do not delete it — show it to an adult you trust, exactly as it arrived. The message itself is the evidence.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion all eight risks, and the closing question you will ask at home.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the eight risks from memory, then check the count.
2. For three risks, write the proper-use habit that answers each one.
3. Ask the closing question at home — how much does your family use social networks, and for what purpose — and write the answers.
