The lessons teach the topics, not the copy faults. Where the guide's keyed answers correct or extend the student text, they are filed as labelled teacher evidence and say so in the table above.

Egypt's own way (Grade 10, Term 1 Programming and Artificial Intelligence student book): A full Japanese-style "Information Study" course adopted for Egypt's new secondary track: 13 chapters running from information theory, ethics and security through number systems, data analysis (frequency, variance, correlation, regression in spreadsheets), simulation and queues, Python, and a final AI-programming project chapter building web pages, a quiz app and a block-breaker game with generative AI.

Fiji's own way (Year 12 Computer Studies student book): Written locally by the same TEST/USP panel for the trialled Year 12 syllabus; the Internet chapter is grounded in Fiji (fiji.gov.fj URL anatomy with the .fj country code, and Fiji ISPs Connect Fiji, Unwired Fiji, Vodafone, Digicel and Kidanet by Fintel), programming is a full Visual Basic course with hands-on examples, and data management is taught as a move to cloud storage (Google Drive, Dropbox, SpiderOak).

Libya's own way (Grade 12 Humanities, Information Technology student book): the Humanities final year pairs the social/legal side of ICT (data security, e-government, computer law and software licences) with a long multimedia-and-PowerPoint practical; the closing projects are Libyan — a deck about Libyan dishes and a deck about Libya's archaeological cities and landmarks.

Libya's own way (Grade 12 Science, Information Technology student book): the Science section shares the Humanities book's first three chapters (security, e-government, computer law) word-for-structure, then diverges into a full second Visual Basic course — the VB environment and form controls, arrays, subroutines and built-in functions, and the remaining toolbox controls. Visual Basic is the Grade 12 programming language in both sections.

Laos's own way (Grade 10 ICT student book): the upper-secondary course turns conceptual — a full information-systems syllabus (organisations, TPS/MIS/DSS/EIS/expert/office systems, the development life cycle), then the series' deepest security chapter (firewalls, cookies, spyware, spam, employee monitoring, privacy law), closing with artificial intelligence, with DOS and Unix taught alongside Windows in the senior years' background.

Greece's own way (Grade 9, Pliroforiki C' Gymnasiou, 2024): Grade 9 is project-led — recursion first, then Python (EduBlocks and Thonny, rock-paper-scissors with randomness), physical computing (code for the International Space Station, micro:bit, Arduino with breadboard and Tinkercad), AI with App Inventor image recognition, collaboration in Greece's own +γραφίς (+grafis) document service, AI ethics/privacy/cookies/big and open data, and scientific programming (solving equations, exponential spread modelling).

Greece's own way (Grade 10, Efarmoges Pliroforikis A' Lykeiou): the older Lykeio applications book in four thematic units — hardware/software and applications, programming environments and creating applications (life cycle, modern IDEs, mobile-app and 3D object-oriented programming), communication and the internet (HTML5/CSS, learning online), and collaboration and safety online (cloud applications, telework, social networks, privacy).

Switzerland's own way (Grades 5–6, Décodage 5e–6e): Cycle 2 moves from unplugged games to real tools while keeping the game frame: the imaginary city “Square CT” teaches classification, decision trees, conditionals, indentation and flowcharts (logigrammes); ScratchJr tells emotion and climate stories; the Swiss Thymio robot is programmed in VPL; data is taught through binary pixel coding and colour coding; and “Enquêtes” (inquiries) teach addition-as-algorithm, debugging, sorting, the Nim winning strategy, Caesar ciphers and sensor experiments. Médias/Usages scenarios cover cyberbullying, screen practices, news and media stereotypes.

Bangladesh's own way (Grade 6, Digital Technology): the Competency-based National Curriculum 2022 book is organised as nine project "Learning Experiences" done in groups through stories, games and fieldwork — no conventional chapters; even learners without a computer at home learn technology through experience around school and home. The NCTB Digital Technology line runs in parallel English and Bangla editions; placements are cited from the English Version with the Bangla Version parallel spot-checked.

Bangladesh's own way (Grade 8, Digital Technology): Grade 8 deepens the same project pattern — a public information-verification campaign, a family phone-safety investigation, a planned civic/e-commerce service, Python with binary logic first, and an online fair for the Asia-Pacific experience. The NCTB Digital Technology line runs in parallel English and Bangla editions; placements are cited from the English Version with the Bangla Version parallel spot-checked.

Bangladesh's own way (HSC ICT, Classes XI–XII and Alim): one compulsory ICT book for every HSC and Alim student in the country, saturated with Bangladesh itself: Bangabandhu Satellite-1 and its ground stations, bdjobs and chakri.com job portals, Daraz and Bikroy e-commerce, the Walton factory's automated production line, the National ID card used to teach primary keys and its database used to teach RDBMS at work, a full Bangla Unicode table and a worked example of putting Bangla text on a web page with meta charset, the national letter-grade scale (A+ from 80) as the else-if programme, "I Love Bangladesh" as the loop example text, an algorithm for reaching college by bicycle or rickshaw, and the Digital Bangladesh programme — National Data Centre, hi-tech parks, Startup Bangladesh and Bangladesh Bank's QR-based Cashless Bangladesh. C is the HSC programming language, taught with scanf/printf in Code::Blocks.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 7 G7T2 Student): Citizenship comes before code in Term 2 — digital citizenship, intellectual property with the actual patent-application procedures, data privacy law, and balanced use of communication tools — then a pure algorithms unit with no computer needed (writing conditions, tracking, representation, flowcharts, improving algorithms).

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 6 G6 student): Same author pair as Grade 5, one year on: the § sequence moves from society/data-rights into number systems and computing systems, then a long spreadsheet-and-diagrams block, and ends with algorithms in flowcharts and a block-based programming language.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 7 G7 Informatika student): Kurikulum Merdeka Informatika built on four elements (Berpikir Komputasional, Literasi Digital, Analisis Data, Algoritma dan Pemrograman), mixing plugged and unplugged activities with coded activity IDs (e.g. VII-BK, VII-LD) and QR-linked resources; Grade 7 covers only BK + Literasi Digital + data handling.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 9 G9 Informatika student): Grade 9 completes junior-high Informatika with tree/graph data structures, a full data-analysis workflow, modular programming and the block-to-text transition (Scratch/Blockly toward C and Python), productivity content design with AI, and a full digital-security chapter.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 7 G7 Informatika teacher guide): Panduan Umum sets out capaian pembelajaran, pedagogy principles and assessment for Grade 7; each chapter guide presents the essential material, pre-learning assessment, a step-by-step guide to the student book, enrichment/remedial, answer keys and parent interaction. Pakistan's own way (Grade 6 Computer Science 6): every unit opens with Students Learning Outcomes, is dotted with "Do you Know?", "Extra Bit!" and "Tips" boxes, and closes with Summary, MCQ/short/detailed exercises, project-based and activity-based questions, with an answers key at the back. Digital skills (Windows desktop, file management, Paint 3D, a typing tutor) are taught hands-on BEFORE programming; digital citizenship is framed through Pakistani citizenship first; the entrepreneurship unit teaches from Pakistani founders (Foodpanda/EatOye, Shopsy.pk, Zero Makeup, Investors Lounge).

Pakistan's own way (Grade 7 Computer Science 7): Grade 7 opens with emerging technologies and a deep ports-and-peripherals hardware treatment, then teaches email all the way to DKIM/SPF/DMARC authentication and POP3/IMAP/SMTP protocols; binary data representation (ASCII/Unicode, RGB colour, sound sampling) is taught inside the Programming unit, and entrepreneurship becomes business-plan craft — promotion, value proposition, quality assurance, SEO and platform business models, with Twitter and Snapchat origin stories.

Pakistan's own way (Grade 9 Computer Science 9): Grade 9 is a full problem-solving course before any coding — candid solutions, the 5 Ws of problem analysis, test data in five types, trace tables, verification vs validation — then number systems carried through to Boolean algebra with truth tables, networks taught through the postal-system analogy for TCP/IP layers, a data-and-privacy unit built on Code.org cipher widgets, NADRA and Pakistan's NR3C cybercrime centre, and a first website built page by page as "Pakistan.html".
