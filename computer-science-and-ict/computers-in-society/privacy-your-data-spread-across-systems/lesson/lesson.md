# Privacy — your data spread across systems

## Do it first

Count the systems that hold something personal about you: your mail, one social account, a bank or money account, your school record. Four doors, four copies of you. Now ask the lesson's question: who may open those doors — and who may not?

## Sub lesson 1 — Data everywhere, behind passwords

**Personal data is spread across digital systems** — it does not live in one place. It **should sit behind personal passwords**: the password is the lock on each door.

Yet the lock is not the whole story: **mail, social and bank accounts can be reached by unauthorised people**. Privacy has to be defended, not assumed.

## Sub lesson 2 — Looking is wrong — and can become legal

**Looking at someone's private information is wrong in itself** — nothing has to be stolen for the wrong to happen. The looking is the violation. And it **can become a legal matter**, not only a moral one.

The same limit binds the powerful: **even an employer checking staff computer use must do it without damaging individual privacy**. A job gives no one a free pass into a person's private information.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the systems that hold data about you, what should protect each one, and why even an employer may not look freely.

## Do It Yourself
