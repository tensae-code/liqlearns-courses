# ICT in Daily Life and at Work

## Do it first

The information society was named in the lesson before; this one walks it. Mongolia's Grade 9 book asks what the new society actually does all day — where ICT stands in daily life and at work — and what kind of citizen it needs.

## Sub lesson 1 — ICT in daily life and at work

The information society **performs its roles** through ICT placed inside life's standing institutions. **Banking**: money kept, moved and checked electronically. **Government**: services and records delivered through systems, not queues. **Industry**: production planned, monitored and machined by information systems. **Medicine**: records, images and consultations carried by networks. **Education**: lessons and materials reaching learners anywhere. The pupil's own day already touches all five — the lesson's work is to make the familiar visible as a system.

In your mind's eye: iCT in daily life and at work.

## Sub lesson 2 — The information-society citizen

A society built on information asks **qualities** of its citizens beyond using devices: to **find** information and judge its truth, to **guard** their own and others' data, to **communicate** within the ethics of the network, and to **keep learning** as the tools change under them. Mongolia's measure is civic, not technical: the device skills are taught across the whole book, but this lesson names the citizen those skills are for — the roles are performed by institutions, and answered by people.

In your mind's eye: the information-society citizen.
