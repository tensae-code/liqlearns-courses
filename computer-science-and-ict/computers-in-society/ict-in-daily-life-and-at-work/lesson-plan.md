# Lesson plan — ICT in Daily Life and at Work

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Computers in Society · **Lesson:** ICT in Daily Life and at Work
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Mongolia (2026-11-14) — in the title Computers in Society on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Mongolia's student books (Ministry of Education, Culture and Science(s) of Mongolia, Ulaanbaatar), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree and live lesson content were checked for the slug and for near matches before building.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The roles the information society performs — ICT at work in daily life and in the working world: banking, government, industry, medicine and education — and the qualities expected of an information-society citizen
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **ICT in daily life and at work** — The information society **performs its roles** through ICT placed inside life's standing institutions. **Banking**: money kept, moved and checked electronically. **Government**: services and records delivered through systems, not queues. **Industry**: production planned, monitored and machined by information systems. **Medicine**: records, images and consultations carried by networks. **Education**: lessons and materials reaching learners anywhere. The pupil's own day already touches all five — the lesson's work is to make the familiar visible as a system.
2. **The information-society citizen** — A society built on information asks **qualities** of its citizens beyond using devices: to **find** information and judge its truth, to **guard** their own and others' data, to **communicate** within the ethics of the network, and to **keep learning** as the tools change under them. Mongolia's measure is civic, not technical: the device skills are taught across the whole book, but this lesson names the citizen those skills are for — the roles are performed by institutions, and answered by people.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
