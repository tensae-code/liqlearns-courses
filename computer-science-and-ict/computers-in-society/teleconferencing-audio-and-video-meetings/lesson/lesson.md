# Teleconferencing — audio and video meetings

## Do it first

Three people, three towns, one meeting — and nobody travelled. If only voices travel, it is one kind of meeting; if faces travel too, it is another. This lesson tells the two apart.

A **teleconference** **joins two or more people in different places electronically**.

- **Audio conferencing** is **voice-only**, over **telephone lines** — the **conference call**.
- **Video conferencing** adds **live images**, so participants **see facial expressions and appearance** — **close to a face-to-face meeting**. Named software: **Zoom, Google Meet and Microsoft Teams**.

[Picture 16122 — the same meeting twice: phones only, then faces on screens]

**Benefits:** **saved time and travel**, **joining from home or office**, **lower hosting costs**, and **room for more participants**. Down at school level, the guide adds (teacher guide — evidence): teleconferencing lets **pupils finish group assignments together without meeting outside school hours**.

## Say it to your companion

## Do It Yourself

1. Hold a three-person phone call if you can, voices only. Afterwards list what the missing faces cost the meeting — and what they did not.
2. Plan a group assignment meeting by teleconference: who joins from where, audio or video, and why that choice? (teacher guide — evidence)
3. Write the invitation for a video meeting: time, software name, and one rule that keeps it close to face-to-face.
