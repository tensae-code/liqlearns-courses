# Lesson plan — Teleconferencing — audio and video meetings

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Computers in Society · **Lesson:** Teleconferencing — audio and video meetings
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 14 — Computers in Society of the Computer Science and ICT ladder (TITLES.md); Ethiopia Grade 11, Unit 1 — Information Systems and Its Applications. The ladder is the recommended order, never a forced one.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a teleconference joins people in different places electronically — audio by telephone line, video adding live faces — saving time, travel and hosting costs.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the telemedicine lesson (recommended first, never forced): meeting at a distance, after care at a distance.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds, looping.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define a teleconference and tell audio from video conferencing.
2. Name Zoom, Google Meet and Microsoft Teams.
3. List the benefits and the school-level use (teacher guide — evidence).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. A phone, if you have one, for the audio try-out.

## If the learner is stuck
Thinks any phone call is a video conference → video adds live images; voice-only over telephone lines is audio conferencing, the conference call. Thinks teleconferencing is only for companies → the guide brings it to school group work (teacher guide — evidence).
