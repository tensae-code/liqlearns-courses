# Lesson plan — Information Dissemination

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Computers in Society · **Lesson:** Information Dissemination
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Tanzania (2026-11-14) — in the title Computers in Society on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Tanzania's *Information and Computer Studies for Secondary Schools — Student's Book, Form One*, because no existing lesson teaches this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Information dissemination is spreading information out so that it reaches many people — and the modern ways of doing it (mobile phones, the internet and its websites and social media, email and messaging, alongside radio and television) carry it faster and further than the older ways alone ever could.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the learner's own daily world — the announcements, radio programmes, phone messages and posters they already meet — and on the idea of data and information; everything else is built inside the lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What information dissemination is** — Information kept in one place helps one person; dissemination is spreading it out — sending it from its source to many receivers — so that the people who need it actually get it.
2. **Modern ways of disseminating information** — The modern channels: mobile phones and messaging, the internet with its websites and social media, email — and broadcast by radio and television — and what each one is good for: speed, reach, and letting people answer back.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Data and From Data to Information in First Steps. Recommended, never forced — a learner may land on this lesson directly, and it stands alone.
- Tanzania's own way is kept: in the Form One book this lesson sits inside the chapter on data and information — dissemination is taught as part of what information *does* in society, a full chapter before any hardware is named.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what information dissemination means, in their own words.
2. Name the modern ways of disseminating information.
3. Match a message to a good channel for it, and say why that channel fits.
4. Say one thing modern channels do that the older ways alone could not: reach many people fast, far away, with an answer coming back.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — the announcements and messages of your own week are the examples.

## If the learner is stuck
Thinks dissemination is just "sending a message" → one message to one friend is sending; dissemination is spreading to *many* receivers who need it — ask how many people the message must reach before the word fits. Cannot choose a channel → judge by three things: how fast it must arrive, how far it must go, and whether the receivers must be able to answer back.
