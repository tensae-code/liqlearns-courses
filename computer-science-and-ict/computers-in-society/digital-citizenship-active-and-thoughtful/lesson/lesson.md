# Digital citizenship — active and thoughtful, not just careful

## Do it first

Most safety teaching is a list of don'ts. This lesson inverts it: a citizen is not someone who merely avoids harm online, but someone who acts — for their community, with respect, with a voice, with a checked source. Four do's. Learn them as actions.

## Sub lesson 1 — Citizenship — the do's

**Digital citizenship is the responsible use of technology by anyone using computers, the internet and digital devices to engage with society** — engaging **positively, critically and competently, respectfully of human rights and dignity**. Good citizenship builds **communication, empathy and lasting connections**; poor citizenship is **online bullying, careless social-media use and ignorance of internet safety**.

The book stresses the do's: **use technology to make your community better; engage respectfully with people of different beliefs; use technology to make your voice heard by public leaders and the global community; and determine the validity of online sources**.

## Sub lesson 2 — The citizen in action — campaign and Ambassador (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide (teacher guide — evidence) defines the good digital citizen positively: someone who **uses technology successfully to interact with others, participate in society, project a positive image and consume digital information safely and responsibly — creating a positive and useful digital footprint and seeing opportunities instead of threats**.

Its task: **assess a pressing issue in your own community** — the guide suggests **promoting cultural-heritage and tourist sites, discouraging unwanted cultural practices, or national agendas needing citizen engagement** — then **prepare a campaign on available digital tools and social media** to carry the message to the wider regional and global community. And it adds a motivational device of its own: a **"Digital Citizenship Challenge Ambassador" competition** among pupils.

[Picture 29744 — a pupil's campaign poster about a local heritage site being shared from a phone to a wide crowd (teacher guide — evidence)]

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the definition, the four do's, and then pitch your campaign: the issue, the message, the tools, the audience.

## Do It Yourself

1. Choose your pressing community issue and write the campaign's first post — positive, respectful, source-checked, as the definition requires.
2. Audit your own digital footprint for one week of posts and comments: positive and useful, or careless? Write the verdict honestly.
