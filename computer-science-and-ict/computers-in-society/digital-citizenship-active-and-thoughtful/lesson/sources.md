The lessons teach the topics, not the copy faults. Where the guide's keyed answers correct or extend the student text, they are filed as labelled teacher evidence and say so in the table above.

North Macedonia's own way (Year 1): five themes move from modern digital technology and online living into a full structured-programming course taught in C++ (with Scratch/C++/Java/Python/PHP/Lisp compared), then advanced Word work (styles, contents, templates) and spreadsheets up to pivot tables.

Mauritania's own way (Grade 7): Informatics is taught for the first time in 1AS as thirty 30-minute teacher fiches moving hardware → information systems (illustrated with Mauritanian systems Bankily, Masrvi, Houwiyeti and the Ministry's Sirage platform) → a long Microsoft Word sequence → a full unit on internet ethics before browsers and search are even introduced.

Mauritania's own way (Grade 8): The second year is organised as five applied units of 35 thirty-minute fiches — image processing first (Paint/GIMP/Photoshop, bitmap vs vector), then internet ethics, a full Excel data-processing unit ending in a practical project, web source-checking and responsible downloading, and finally email — each unit closed by evaluation and support fiches with full answer keys at the end.

Mauritania's own way (Grade 9): The final secondary year is five practical blocks in 30-minute fiches — a full PowerPoint sequence, internet ethics, a first formal algorithms unit (variables, conditions, loops, writing and testing algorithms, no named programming language), simple networks, and email with attachments — each block closed by evaluation fiches.

Egypt's own way (Grade 5, Term 2 student book (Arabic)): Primary ICT is organised as themed units — "The Digital Citizen in the Internet World" then "My Digital Project" — with every lesson phrased as a journey or a how-to question.

Fiji's own way (Years 7–8 Computer Education student book): A practical, exercise-driven primary book built around a class "students folder" of set files (work is saved as U<unit>-Ex<n>-<FirstName>); local Fijian names and schools throughout (e.g. Babasiga Secondary School), Internet searching is practised on the "Map of Fiji" with kids' search engines, and a full unit covers computers in Fijian society including Braille keyboards for blind learners. PARTIAL: the PDF as received has no cover, title page, contents or imprint — the PARTIAL label travels with every citation of this book.

Libya's own way (Grade 9, Computer student book): the whole year is networks and the internet — a full networks unit (types, cables, topology, components), then Internet Explorer and Google search taught as named skills, then email and a closing tour of social sites (Facebook, YouTube, Twitter, Instagram, Yahoo) with practical activities.

Mongolia's own way (Grade 10): Grade 10 is the "citizen" book — ICT ethics, law and intellectual property first, taught with Mongolian digital content as the examples (the Nogun comic, the Chinggis Khaan animation, GoGo Kids, Maamuu and YoLo; the CRC's black-list.mn), then a distinctive "package information" chapter — collecting, storing, merging and presenting information as one whole, worked through a 24-slide presentation on the sights of Bayankhongor aimag — multimedia production with free tools (Pencil2D, Pivot Animator, Unfreez, Audacity), and a full MS Access database chapter built around a Mongolian phone-book database and the state's unified registration system.

Kuwait's own way (Grade 4 Digital Explorer Term 1): every lesson runs استكشف–تعلم–طبق (explore–learn–apply) around a story with Hamad, an AI robot assistant mascot; digital citizenship is framed as 'with my ethics I am an ambassador of my country'; block coding starts with ScratchJr, and using the national MS Teams platform (moe.edu.kw accounts) is taught as core curriculum in the front 'Digital Classroom' section.

Kuwait's own way (Grade 5 Digital Explorer Term 1): Grade 5 leaves ScratchJr for full Scratch (installed and online), but first comes a values-and-AI unit — citizenship, smart vs non-smart devices, what AI is, robots and their parts — and programming itself is taught as a four-stage craft (set the goal, the smart plan, the flowchart, then the code) before a single block is dragged.
