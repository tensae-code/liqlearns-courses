Mauritania's own way (Grade 7): Informatics is taught for the first time in 1AS as thirty 30-minute teacher fiches moving hardware → information systems (illustrated with Mauritanian systems Bankily, Masrvi, Houwiyeti and the Ministry's Sirage platform) → a long Microsoft Word sequence → a full unit on internet ethics before browsers and search are even introduced.

Mauritania's own way (Grade 8): The second year is organised as five applied units of 35 thirty-minute fiches — image processing first (Paint/GIMP/Photoshop, bitmap vs vector), then internet ethics, a full Excel data-processing unit ending in a practical project, web source-checking and responsible downloading, and finally email — each unit closed by evaluation and support fiches with full answer keys at the end.

Mauritania's own way (Grade 9): The final secondary year is five practical blocks in 30-minute fiches — a full PowerPoint sequence, internet ethics, a first formal algorithms unit (variables, conditions, loops, writing and testing algorithms, no named programming language), simple networks, and email with attachments — each block closed by evaluation fiches.

Egypt's own way (Grade 5, Term 2 student book (Arabic)): Primary ICT is organised as themed units — "The Digital Citizen in the Internet World" then "My Digital Project" — with every lesson phrased as a journey or a how-to question.

Fiji's own way (Years 7–8 Computer Education student book): A practical, exercise-driven primary book built around a class "students folder" of set files (work is saved as U<unit>-Ex<n>-<FirstName>); local Fijian names and schools throughout (e.g. Babasiga Secondary School), Internet searching is practised on the "Map of Fiji" with kids' search engines, and a full unit covers computers in Fijian society including Braille keyboards for blind learners. Libya's own way (Grade 9, Computer student book): the whole year is networks and the internet — a full networks unit (types, cables, topology, components), then Internet Explorer and Google search taught as named skills, then email and a closing tour of social sites (Facebook, YouTube, Twitter, Instagram, Yahoo) with practical activities.

Mongolia's own way (Grade 10): Grade 10 is the "citizen" book — ICT ethics, law and intellectual property first, taught with Mongolian digital content as the examples (the Nogun comic, the Chinggis Khaan animation, GoGo Kids, Maamuu and YoLo; the CRC's black-list.mn), then a distinctive "package information" chapter — collecting, storing, merging and presenting information as one whole, worked through a 24-slide presentation on the sights of Bayankhongor aimag — multimedia production with free tools (Pencil2D, Pivot Animator, Unfreez, Audacity), and a full MS Access database chapter built around a Mongolian phone-book database and the state's unified registration system.

Kuwait's own way (Grade 4 Digital Explorer Term 1): every lesson runs استكشف–تعلم–طبق (explore–learn–apply) around a story with Hamad, an AI robot assistant mascot; digital citizenship is framed as 'with my ethics I am an ambassador of my country'; block coding starts with ScratchJr, and using the national MS Teams platform (moe.edu.kw accounts) is taught as core curriculum in the front 'Digital Classroom' section.

Kuwait's own way (Grade 5 Digital Explorer Term 1): Grade 5 leaves ScratchJr for full Scratch (installed and online), but first comes a values-and-AI unit — citizenship, smart vs non-smart devices, what AI is, robots and their parts — and programming itself is taught as a four-stage craft (set the goal, the smart plan, the flowchart, then the code) before a single block is dragged.

Laos's own way (Grade 12 ICT student book): the final year is a modern, applied course — problem-solving method, a long English-titled internet-safety sequence (Internet Safety, Email Tips for Scams and Spam, Smart Social Networking, Cyber harassment/Stalking/Addiction), data summaries and PivotTables for decision-making, copyright and Creative Commons licensing, a full PowerPoint course, and choosing technology for your future occupation.

Moldova's own way (Grade 7, Informatică): lower-secondary Informatica is a real textbook discipline, not computer-skills practice — number systems, text/image/sound encoding and data types in Pascal sit alongside GUI literacy, and two full chapters are given to behaviour, law and information culture in virtual spaces.

Switzerland's own way (Grades 1–2, Décodage 1re–2e): Vaud's Romandie digital-education curriculum is taught in three axes — Science informatique (SI), Médias (M), Usages (U) — almost entirely UNPLUGGED at this age: algorithms are learned with the body (robot game, crane game, hand-washing, getting dressed, a crêpe recipe), a physical sorting machine, and the Blue-Bot floor robot; the class also builds a collective digital “charter book” of good digital habits across the whole cycle.

Switzerland's own way (Grades 3–4, Décodage 3e–4e): the 3e–4e volume repeats the 1re–2e unplugged activities at a higher level (loops and debugging join the robot and crane games and the sorting machine) and adds three new strands: Thymio robotics, a full ScratchJr scenario, and media “parcours” — Parcours des bestioles (how images influence emotions), Parcours du partage (image rights, digital traces, thinking before sharing) and Parcours de la pub (recognising and creating advertising). Pixel paravent introduces binary coding as transmitting a black-and-white image across a screen with an agreed code; Deviner la carte teaches the conditional through a magic trick and its logigramme.

Switzerland's own way (Grades 5–6, Décodage 5e–6e): Cycle 2 moves from unplugged games to real tools while keeping the game frame: the imaginary city “Square CT” teaches classification, decision trees, conditionals, indentation and flowcharts (logigrammes); ScratchJr tells emotion and climate stories; the Swiss Thymio robot is programmed in VPL; data is taught through binary pixel coding and colour coding; and “Enquêtes” (inquiries) teach addition-as-algorithm, debugging, sorting, the Nim winning strategy, Caesar ciphers and sensor experiments. Médias/Usages scenarios cover cyberbullying, screen practices, news and media stereotypes.

Switzerland's own way (Grades 7–8, Décodage 7e–8e): at 7e–8e the Décodage course becomes a real computer-science course under the Plan d'études romand EN 22/23: Thymio VPL missions (including Morse communication and self-driving-car autonomy levels), a full Scratch discovery course (movement, shortening programs, sub-programs as own blocks, remixing), a Scratch video-game project run in “studios” with a cahier des charges, data work (Huffman-style compression via Zip la marmotte, binary/octal/hex/ASCII codings, file sizes), access security (passwords, file encryption, phishing, authentication), home networks at two levels (IP addresses; message routing, packets, TCP and encryption), and inquiries into sorting, sorting networks, Caesar decryption and parity-bit error detection. Médias/Usages covers social networks and digital identity/footprint, information sources, cyberbullying and the environmental cost of digital technology.

Bangladesh's own way (Grade 6, Digital Technology): the Competency-based National Curriculum 2022 book is organised as nine project "Learning Experiences" done in groups through stories, games and fieldwork — no conventional chapters; even learners without a computer at home learn technology through experience around school and home. The NCTB Digital Technology line runs in parallel English and Bangla editions; placements are cited from the English Version with the Bangla Version parallel spot-checked.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 7 G7T2 Student): Citizenship comes before code in Term 2 — digital citizenship, intellectual property with the actual patent-application procedures, data privacy law, and balanced use of communication tools — then a pure algorithms unit with no computer needed (writing conditions, tracking, representation, flowcharts, improving algorithms).

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 8 G8 Informatika student): Grade 8 deepens the four elements: spreadsheet data analysis (lookup, pivot), computational thinking through functions, sets and number systems, and programming that moves from Scratch to Blockly Games, procedural blocks and a physical Ozobot robot.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 9 G9 Informatika student): Grade 9 completes junior-high Informatika with tree/graph data structures, a full data-analysis workflow, modular programming and the block-to-text transition (Scratch/Blockly toward C and Python), productivity content design with AI, and a full digital-security chapter.

Vietnam's own way (Grade 7 Tin học 7): Grade 7 is the spreadsheet year (five Excel lessons in a row, in Windows/Excel/PowerPoint named openly) and its computer-science strand is pure algorithms with no code at all — sequential search, binary search and sorting acted out and flowcharted before any language appears.

Vietnam's own way (Grade 10 Tin học 10): the Grade 10 book is one shared book for both orientations (ICT-coloured and CS-coloured lesson title bands tell each track its own lessons, per its preface); its data strand is unusually deep for school level — binary integers, logic data, sound and image data — and its programming strand is a complete first Python course from variables to functions, scope, error recognition and testing.

Vietnam's own way (Grade 12 Tin học 12 Tin học ứng dụng): the final-year common core (Bài 1–21, shared with the CS book) opens with artificial intelligence and network protocols, then teaches real web design — HTML structure, lists, tables, links, forms, then CSS through selector priority — and closes with ICT careers (repair/maintenance services, IT administration). Vietnam's own way (Grade 12 Tin học 12 Khoa học máy tính): Bài 1–21 are the same common core as the Applied orientation book (spot-checked page-identical, e.g. Bài 1); the CS-only lessons go deeper into network devices, transmission media and network design, then introduce machine learning and data science, and end with simulation in problem-solving and education.
