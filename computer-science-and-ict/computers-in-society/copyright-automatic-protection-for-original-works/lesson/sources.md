**Acceptance count: taught in 10 countries (Ethiopia, Mozambique, Bulgaria, Bhutan, Mongolia, Laos, Greece, Bangladesh, Armenia, Indonesia).**

Book errors touching Unit 1, taught corrected and named here: the student book numbers "Brainstorming 1.7" twice (for §1.4, printed p. 18, and again for §1.5, printed p. 22); the teacher guide's §1.2 competency reads "Define Intellectual property (IR)" for IP, and the guide's Discussion strategy is copy-pasted as discussing "Digital Identity Management issues" in §§1.2, 1.4 and 1.5 whatever the section. The lessons teach the topics, not the copy faults. Where the guide's keyed answers correct or extend the student text, they are filed as labelled teacher evidence and say so in the table above.

Bhutan's own way (Class VIII, Literacy with ICT student book): The final primary/middle year is project-led — a full Audacity audio unit (record, edit, mix, share), information ethics taught through Copyright, Creative Commons and software piracy, and Scratch taught to game level with lists, custom blocks (functions) and logical operators.

Mongolia's own way (Grade 9): Grade 9 turns outward to society and real tools — the information society taught through Mongolia's own shift, open-source software through Soyombo Linux (Mongolia's own Linux) and Mongolian OpenOffice, copyright through Mongolia's 2006 copyright law; algorithms are drawn as flowcharts and run in Turtle Art; the long spreadsheet unit grades pupils with nested IFs, builds Fibonacci sequences, grows a 500,000-tögrög bank deposit, and charts the depth and area of Mongolia's great lakes (Khuvsgul, Uvs, Khar-Us); e-services are the real Mongolian ones — the ТҮЦ service kiosk, bookstore.mn and internet banking.

Laos's own way (Grade 12 ICT student book): the final year is a modern, applied course — problem-solving method, a long English-titled internet-safety sequence (Internet Safety, Email Tips for Scams and Spam, Smart Social Networking, Cyber harassment/Stalking/Addiction), data summaries and PivotTables for decision-making, copyright and Creative Commons licensing, a full PowerPoint course, and choosing technology for your future occupation.

Greece's own way (Grade 7, Pliroforiki A' Gymnasiou, 2024): the new Gymnasium informatics course is built around algorithms from the first page (a Python binary example in Unit 1), then hardware/software, the internet with a full copyright/Creative Commons/AI-copyright sequence, cybersecurity with cryptography, word processing, algorithmics, Scratch storytelling/chatbot projects, and a long society unit (robots, drones, autonomous cars, e-government, AI in education/medicine).

Bangladesh's own way (Grade 6, Digital Technology): the Competency-based National Curriculum 2022 book is organised as nine project "Learning Experiences" done in groups through stories, games and fieldwork — no conventional chapters; even learners without a computer at home learn technology through experience around school and home. The NCTB Digital Technology line runs in parallel English and Bangla editions; placements are cited from the English Version with the Bangla Version parallel spot-checked.

Bangladesh's own way (Grade 7, Digital Technology): the Grade 7 book continues the Learning-Experience project format — intellectual property, virtual identity, cybercrime, robotics-through-pseudocode and e-services are each learned by doing a class project with the recurring robot character Nano. The NCTB Digital Technology line runs in parallel English and Bangla editions; placements are cited from the English Version with the Bangla Version parallel spot-checked.

Bangladesh's own way (Grade 9, Digital Technology): Grade 9 turns practical — a full Python course (Thonny IDE) inside the book, website building with WordPress and Google services for a citizen-service site, and hands-on networking between two school computers. The NCTB Digital Technology line runs in parallel English and Bangla editions; placements are cited from the English Version with the Bangla Version parallel spot-checked.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 6 G6 student): Same author pair as Grade 5, one year on: the § sequence moves from society/data-rights into number systems and computing systems, then a long spreadsheet-and-diagrams block, and ends with algorithms in flowcharts and a block-based programming language.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 6 G6 teacher): The guide reorganises the student book's 24 §s into 13 standard themes (6-1 to 6-13), each with lesson topic, outcome codes, cross-cutting competencies and practical work; it closes with a thematic plan, a worked sample lesson plan (§3) and slide-presentation assessment rubrics.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 7 G7 teacher): SERIES MISMATCH NOTED — this guide (Velijanyan & Aslanyan) is the teacher guide for the OTHER Grade 7 series, whose student book (§1 Electronic security … §23 Computer hardware maintenance, Python-based) is NOT among the records; it does not match the Topchyan & Melkonyan Grade 7 student book filed as Book 12. The guide is complete in itself: 20 themes, a thematic plan, the student book's questions/tasks with answers for §1–§23, worked Python solutions, and assessment rubrics.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 10 G10 Informatika student): Senior-high Informatika opens with real data work — multi-variable search, data collection by Python web scraping in Google Colab (pandas/Plotly) — then algorithms chosen for real-life problems, office automation, IT careers/certifications, and information security with classical ciphers.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 10 G10 Informatika SMK Sem2): Semester 2 turns to algorithms in depth, the social side of informatics (history of IT, intellectual property, professions), and a full informatics project-management chapter — plan, monitor, evaluate.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 10 G10 Informatika SMK teacher guide): Per-meeting teaching guides with activity steps, rubrics and remedial/enrichment for every SMK Grade 10 topic, from logic propositions and number conversion to a full Ubuntu installation demonstration and flowchart activity sets.
