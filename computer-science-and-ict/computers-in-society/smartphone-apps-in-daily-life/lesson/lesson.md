# Smartphone Apps in Daily Life

## Do it first

Count the jobs your family's phone did today — calls, messages, money, directions, pictures. Each job was done by a program with a name. In this lesson you name them: the apps of daily life.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a smartphone app is

An **app** — an application — is a program made for one kind of job, carried on a **smartphone**: a phone that is also a computer, running programs far beyond calls. The phone's own jobs (calling, the camera, the clock) come as apps too, and more apps are added from an app store. Sudan's Grade 6 book closes its systems unit here because the smartphone is the computer its pupils already hold: the app is where they meet software every day.

## Sub lesson 2 — Apps in daily life

The book walks the day through apps: apps for **talking and messaging** family, for **learning** and reading, for **banking and paying**, for **maps and travel**, for photographs, music and play. Each app does one institution's or one tool's work in the pocket — the bank branch, the map, the library and the post office all shrunk into programs on one device. Naming the app behind each job of the day is the lesson's habit: behind every tap there is a program somebody wrote.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: An app — an application — is a program made for one kind of job, carried on a smartphone: a phone that is also a computer, running programs far beyond calls. The book walks the day through apps: apps for talking and messaging family, for learning and reading, for banking and paying, for maps and travel, for photographs, music and play. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
