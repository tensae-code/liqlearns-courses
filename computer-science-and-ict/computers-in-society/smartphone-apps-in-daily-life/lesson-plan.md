**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Computers in Society · **Lesson:** Smartphone Apps in Daily Life
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Sudan (2026-11-14) — in the title Computers in Society on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Sudan's Grade 6 ICT student book, because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.

The book walks the day through apps: apps for talking and messaging family, for learning and reading, for banking and paying, for maps and travel, for photographs, music and play.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What a smartphone app is** — An **app** — an application — is a program made for one kind of job, carried on a **smartphone**: a phone that is also a computer, running programs far beyond calls. The phone's own jobs (calling, the camera, the clock) come as apps too, and more apps are added from an app store. Sudan's Grade 6 book closes its systems unit here because the smartphone is the computer its pupils already hold: the app is where they meet software every day.
2. **Apps in daily life** — The book walks the day through apps: apps for **talking and messaging** family, for **learning** and reading, for **banking and paying**, for **maps and travel**, for photographs, music and play. Each app does one institution's or one tool's work in the pocket — the bank branch, the map, the library and the post office all shrunk into programs on one device. Naming the app behind each job of the day is the lesson's habit: behind every tap there is a program somebody wrote.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Count the jobs your family's phone did today — calls, messages, money, directions, pictures. Each job was done by a program with a name. In this lesson you name them: the apps of daily life. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of what a smartphone app is as the lesson describes it — the student studies the picture and names what each part shows.
   - ADD GIF: a GIF of apps in daily life in action — the student watches once, then says what changed and why.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the lesson's key names and definitions aloud, in the order the lesson gives them — the student listens once through, then reads the same lines aloud along with the voice. (Audio is a described slot in this build; no audio file is made.)
3. **Say it to your companion** — the companion block in lesson/lesson.md.
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the lesson in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-false, matching, short-answer — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the doing block at the end of lesson/lesson.md.
