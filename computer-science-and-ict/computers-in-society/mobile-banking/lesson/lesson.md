# Mobile Banking

## Do it first

A bank branch keeps hours and has a queue. A phone is in your pocket now. Rwanda's S2 book teaches mobile banking as its own topic inside ICT in finance — this lesson is that topic.

## Sub lesson 1 — What mobile banking is

**Mobile banking** is using a **mobile phone** to work with your **bank account**: the phone becomes the bank's counter. Through a bank's app or a dialled code menu, the customer reaches the account **anywhere the phone network reaches** — no branch, no queue, no paper form. Rwanda's S2 book places it between e-commerce and the ATM in its finance unit: three electronic doors into the same money.

## Sub lesson 2 — What you can do — and what to guard

The everyday jobs: **check a balance**, **send money** to another account or phone, **pay bills and fees**, and **receive a record** of every movement as a message. The same phone that carries the money carries the risk: guard the **PIN** as the account's key, because whoever holds the phone and knows the PIN holds the account. Rwanda's guide names the shadow side plainly — mobile banking's disadvantages and smishing (fraud by text message) are taught beside the services (teacher guide — evidence, stocked in the phishing lesson).
