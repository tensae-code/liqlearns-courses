## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Three levels — doing, managing, directing** — Management stands in **levels**. At the **operational (lower) level**, supervisors run the day's work — today's orders made, today's workers present. At the **tactical (middle) level**, managers run a department or a season — this quarter's production, this term's budget. At the **strategic (top) level**, directors decide the organisation's direction — new products, new buildings, new markets. Fiji's ladder is a ladder of *time and scope*: the higher the level, the longer the time and the wider the ground each decision covers.
2. **Each level, its own information** — The levels are taught because each **eats different information**. Operational decisions eat **detail, now** — every order, every absence. Tactical decisions eat **summaries over weeks and months** — totals, comparisons, trends. Strategic decisions eat **the widest summaries and the outside world** — years of results, competitors, the economy. This is why the senior book teaches levels before systems: an information system that feeds a director daily detail, or a supervisor yearly summaries, is failing both — the level decides the diet.
