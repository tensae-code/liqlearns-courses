# E-waste — what is inside a thrown-away device

## Do it first

An old phone goes into a pit behind the house. It does not disappear — it begins a slower journey, chemical by chemical, into the soil and the water. This lesson opens the device and reads what is inside.

## Sub lesson 1 — What is inside — and where it leaks

Devices **consume resources and power** in life — and in death they leave their materials behind. Most contain **non-biodegradable, heavy-metal and toxic materials such as cadmium, lead and mercury**.

Those materials **can leak into the ground and contaminate drinking water, food plants and animals**. The guide (teacher guide — evidence) adds the category: discarded computers and electronics are **hazardous waste**, and disposal technique must be kept in mind while use is maximised.

## Sub lesson 2 — E-waste management — and pupils as ambassadors (teacher guide — evidence)

**E-waste management** is the answer the book teaches: **gathering electronic waste systematically, recovering and recycling material safely, and disposing of the rest so environmental damage is minimised** — and it is **the user's responsibility too**, not only the manufacturer's.

The guide (teacher guide — evidence) turns pupils outward: groups **survey the green technologies actually present in their community — solar, wind and biogas are the named families** — and the teacher is to make pupils **"guardians and ambassadors" of their environment**, forming or joining a club or initiative for environmental protection and e-waste management.

[Picture 29719 — discarded electronics marked hazardous beside solar, wind and biogas panels (teacher guide — evidence)]

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what is inside a thrown-away phone, where it can end up, and the three things proper e-waste management does.

## Do It Yourself

1. Count the dead devices in your home — phones, batteries, chargers, boards. Write where each one is now, and where it should go instead.
2. Survey your street or village: which green technologies are actually present — solar, wind, biogas? List them with one example each, as the guide directs.
