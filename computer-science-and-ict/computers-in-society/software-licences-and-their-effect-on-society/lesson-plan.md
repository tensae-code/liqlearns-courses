## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The licence — permission that shapes use** — A **software licence** is the maker's permission, written in advance: who may use the software, copy it, change it, pass it on, and at what price. **Proprietary** licences keep the code closed and copying forbidden; **free and open-source** licences grant use, study, change and sharing, sometimes on the condition that sharing keeps the same freedoms (**copyleft**). Armenia's Grade 11 book teaches licences by their *societal* question (Lesson 3, Theme 3): not only 'what may I do?' but 'what does this do to us?'
2. **The effects — learning, building, dependence and price** — Licence terms ripple outward. **Learning**: open code is a library anyone may study; closed code teaches use without understanding. **Building**: open licences let small makers stand on giants' code; strict terms can lock a country's schools and offices into one vendor's prices and formats (**dependence**). **Price and access**: free licences put real tools in poor schools; per-seat pricing can fence them out. Armenia's Grade 11 guide even sets the question as a project: the impact of software licences on technological development — evidence that the choice of licence is a choice about a society's future, made one program at a time.
