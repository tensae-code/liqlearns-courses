# E-government — public services online

## Do it first

A birth certificate used to mean a queue, a trip, another queue. Now picture the same service arriving through a phone in your hand. What changed is not the certificate — it is the road to it. This lesson names that change.

## Sub lesson 1 — E-government and portals

**E-services** are **public services delivered through technology**. **E-government** uses **information systems** to serve citizens with more **efficiency, transparency and participation**.

A **portal** **gathers an organisation's services into one web interface** — the book's example is the **Ethiopian Ministry of Health portal**. Through such doors, municipal services can move online: **business registration, vehicle registry, tax payment, birth certificates and ID cards**.

[Picture 16115 — a citizen on a phone reaching many services through one portal door]

What online services bring: **trust, information-sharing between agencies, lower running costs**, and services **reachable by phone** — such as **paying an electricity bill**.

## Sub lesson 2 — E-government in Ethiopian daily life (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide grounds it in daily life (teacher guide — evidence). Its brainstorming answer is the **Ethiopian Electric Corporation's mobile bill-payment system**: on one side, **automatic data capture and instant management reports**; on the other, **no queueing** for the citizen.

[Picture 16116 — a phone paying an electricity bill beside the counter queue it replaces]

And the services a citizen can actually reach, in the guide's list: **paying water and electricity bills by phone, birth certificates, trade-licence renewal and government tax payment**. E-government is not a promise here — it is a bill already paid from a phone.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what a portal is, with the Ministry of Health example. Then work the electricity bill both ways: what the citizen gains, what the corporation gains (teacher guide — evidence).

## Do It Yourself

1. List five public services you or your family use. Mark which could move online through a portal, and what each move would save.
2. Draw one portal door with the book's five municipal services behind it. Label what the citizen does on the phone side.
3. Ask at home: has anyone paid a bill by phone? Write the two sides of that story — no queue for them; what data for the company? (teacher guide — evidence)
