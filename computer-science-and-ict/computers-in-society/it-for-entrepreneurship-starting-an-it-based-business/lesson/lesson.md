# IT for entrepreneurship — starting an IT-based business

## Do it first

One founder in this lesson started with an idea and a message service — no shop, no fleet, no fortune. Her company now moves a city. This closing lesson of the unit asks what she had that money cannot be the whole answer to.

## Sub lesson 1 — Entrepreneurship and the IT-enabled business

**Entrepreneurship** is **designing, launching and running a new business**. The **entrepreneur** **carries most of the risk and reward** and is **seen as an innovator**.

An **IT-enabled business** uses **IT as its platform**: **online training, data-entry services, internet cafés, online selling, app and game development**. It **needs little starting capital** — **an idea and a laptop can be enough**.

[Picture 16123 — a young founder with a laptop and four business icons growing from it]

Why it matters for **youth employment**: **more jobs need IT skills**; **IT is changing how young entrepreneurs trade**; and **online learning for job seekers is exploding**.

## Sub lesson 2 — RIDE — and starting where you stand (teacher guide — evidence)

The Ethiopian example is **RIDE**, founded by **Samrawit Fikru**, grown **from an SMS service into a widely used internet taxi-hailing service** that **created many jobs**.

The guide sets the start-up contrast plainly (teacher guide — evidence): a **traditional business** must **rent a shop and hire staff — heavy starting cost** — while an **internet business** is **flexible, cheap and worldwide from day one**, with **no shop rent and no salesperson** to pay first.

[Picture 16124 — a rented shuttered shop beside a phone product catalogue]

And the guide's two starter ideas a pupil could actually attempt (teacher guide — evidence): an **online tutoring service**, and a **product catalogue on Facebook for selling without premises**. The unit ends where it could begin for you: one idea, one laptop, one first customer.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what an entrepreneur carries and is seen as. Then tell the RIDE story, and set the guide's two starts side by side: the rented shop and the phone catalogue (teacher guide — evidence).

## Do It Yourself

1. Write one IT-enabled business idea of your own. List its platform, its first customer, and what little capital it truly needs first.
2. Price the guide's contrast for your town: one month of shop rent and one salesperson, against a phone, data and a Facebook catalogue page (teacher guide — evidence).
3. Retell RIDE's growth as three steps. Mark the step where an SMS service became a platform — what changed?
