## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The paperless office — files that travel** — In the **paperless office**, documents are born digital: written, signed, filed and found by search instead of by drawer. A file can be in two places at once, copied without fading, and sent across the world in seconds; a whole archive fits on one drive — with a backup, because one drive is also one point of failure. Office automation — software doing the filing, calculating, scheduling and reminding — removes the office's slowest, most repetitive work first.
2. **Remote work — and automation's cost** — **Remote work** frees the office from its room: a team can span **time zones**, handing work around the clock — one member's evening is another's morning. The same automation that enables it also **costs jobs**: tasks that were a clerk's whole work — copying, filing, totalling — are exactly the tasks software does best, so the office keeps fewer of those jobs and asks new skills of the people it keeps: judgement, communication, and care of the systems themselves.
