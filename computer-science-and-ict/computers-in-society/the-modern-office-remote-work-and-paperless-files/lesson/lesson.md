# The Modern Office — Remote Work and Paperless Files

## Do it first

The old office was a room full of paper: files in cabinets, letters in trays, a clerk who knew which drawer held what. The new office fits in a bag — and its workers may never share a room, or even a waking hour. This lesson walks into the modern office and looks at both its gifts and its bill.

## Sub lesson 1 — The paperless office — files that travel

In the **paperless office**, documents are born digital: written, signed, filed and found by search instead of by drawer. A file can be in two places at once, copied without fading, and sent across the world in seconds; a whole archive fits on one drive — with a backup, because one drive is also one point of failure. Office automation — software doing the filing, calculating, scheduling and reminding — removes the office's slowest, most repetitive work first.

In your mind's eye: the paperless office — files that travel.

## Sub lesson 2 — Remote work — and automation's cost

**Remote work** frees the office from its room: a team can span **time zones**, handing work around the clock — one member's evening is another's morning. The same automation that enables it also **costs jobs**: tasks that were a clerk's whole work — copying, filing, totalling — are exactly the tasks software does best, so the office keeps fewer of those jobs and asks new skills of the people it keeps: judgement, communication, and care of the systems themselves.

In your mind's eye: remote work — and automation's cost.
