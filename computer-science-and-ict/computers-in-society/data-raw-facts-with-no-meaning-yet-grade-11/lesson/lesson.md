# Data — raw facts with no meaning yet (Grade 11)

## Do it first

Look at these three things: **17** — **Selam** — a photograph of a market street. What does 17 count? Whose name is Selam, and what is she doing? What is happening in the photograph, and when? Each one leaves you asking questions. Keep those questions — this lesson names what you are holding.

## Sub lesson 1 — Data — numbers, text and images

**Data** is **raw facts with no meaning on their own**. A bare figure or a bare word means nothing until it is **related to other data** — 17 could be an age, a price, a house number or a mark, and nothing in the figure itself tells you which.

Data comes in three forms:

- **Numbers** — the form that suits a fact like **age**.
- **Text** — the form that suits a fact like a **name**.
- **Images** — pictures; **satellite imagery** has made image data common.

[Picture 16101 — the same fact as a number, a word and an image]

The **best form depends on the kind of data**: an age suits numbers, a name suits text, and the shape of land suits an image. Data is not weak because it means nothing alone — it is the raw material everything in this unit is built from. The next lesson shows what happens when data is processed.

## Sub lesson 2 — The road ahead — six units, and how this course is taught (teacher guide — evidence)

This course — Grade 11 Information Technology — runs in **six units**, in this order:

1. **Information Systems and Its Applications** — the unit you are in now.
2. **Emerging Technologies**
3. **Database Management**
4. **Web Development**
5. **Hardware Troubleshooting and Preventive Maintenance**
6. **Fundamentals of Programming** — ending with your first Python program.

[Picture 16100 — the six-unit road map, from information systems to a first Python program]

Every unit runs the same way: printed **Learning Outcomes**, a **Unit Overview**, a **Brainstorming** opener that starts from daily life, numbered sections and **Activities**, a **Unit Summary**, **Key Terms**, and a multi-part **Review**. Ethiopian examples run throughout — the Ministry of Health portal, Ethio-telecom, Ethiopian Airlines, the Commercial Bank of Ethiopia.

How the year is taught (teacher guide — evidence): the guide budgets **105 periods** for the year — Unit 1 takes **12** (Units 2: 12, 3: 20, 4: 25, 5: 11, 6: 25) — and teaches section by section in one rhythm: **interactive lecture, then demonstration, then group discussion**, assessed by portfolio, question-and-answer, exercises and observation. Two inclusion instructions repeat in nearly every section: **face the class while lecturing**, in case of pupils with hearing difficulties, and **use appropriate audio** to accommodate visually impaired pupils. Pupils who fall behind get **extra time and special support** arranged for them.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what data is and give one fact in each form — a number, a word, an image. Then name the six units of this course in order, and say how every unit opens.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write one bare fact — a number with nothing else. Give it to a partner and ask what it means. Then relate it to other data until it does mean something, and write down what changed.
2. Draw the six-unit road map on paper, in order, from memory. Check it against the picture and fix any unit out of place.
3. Find one example of each form of data at home: a number, a piece of text, an image. Say for each why that form suits that fact.
