# Lesson plan — Data — raw facts with no meaning yet (Grade 11)

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Computers in Society · **Lesson:** Data — raw facts with no meaning yet (Grade 11)
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 14 — Computers in Society of the Computer Science and ICT ladder (TITLES.md); Ethiopia Grade 11, Unit 1 — Information Systems and Its Applications. The ladder is the recommended order, never a forced one.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** data is raw facts with no meaning on their own, shown as numbers, text or images — and the best form depends on the kind of fact.
**This lesson's own level:** it opens Unit 1 of the Grade 11 course and this band: it stands on the learner's own everyday facts — ages, names, photographs — and on the Grade 7 data lesson only as a recommended first step, never a gate. It also carries the Grade 11 course road map (the book's own shape) and the guide's course-level teaching pattern as labelled teacher evidence.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds, looping.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Data — numbers, text and images** — what data is, why a bare fact means nothing alone, and which form suits which fact.
2. **The road ahead — six units, and how this course is taught (teacher guide — evidence)** — the six-unit Grade 11 road map from information systems to a first Python program, the 105-period budget and the lecture, demonstration, discussion rhythm with its two inclusion instructions.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what data is: raw facts with no meaning on their own.
2. Give a fact as a number, as text and as an image, and say which form suits age, a name, and land seen from a satellite.
3. Name the six Grade 11 units in order and say how each unit runs.
4. State the guide's 105-period budget, the lecture, demonstration, discussion rhythm and the two inclusion instructions (teacher guide — evidence).

## If the learner is stuck
Thinks data must already mean something → hold up the bare 17 again: until it is related to other data, nobody can say what it counts. That is what raw means. Mixes the forms up → ask what the fact IS: how old (number), who (text), what it looks like (image).
