# Lesson plan — Electronic Services — E-Government, E-Learning and E-Banking

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Computers in Society · **Lesson:** Electronic Services — E-Government, E-Learning and E-Banking
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Mongolia (2026-11-14) — in the title Computers in Society on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Mongolia's student books (Ministry of Education, Culture and Science(s) of Mongolia, Ulaanbaatar), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree and live lesson content were checked for the slug and for near matches before building.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Using electronic services as one whole — Mongolia's own way of teaching them together: the ТҮЦ service kiosk and e-government, e-learning and m-learning, e-commerce in its kinds (B2C, B2B, G2B, C2C), an online bookshop, and internet banking
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **E-government and the ТҮЦ kiosk — the state as a service** — An **electronic service** is a service delivered through ICT instead of a counter. Mongolia's emblem is the **ТҮЦ service kiosk** — the state's self-service machine standing in public places, giving citizens certificates and services without the office queue; behind it stands the Grade 12 book's **ХУР state information-exchange system**, joining the state's own databases so one office can serve what another holds. **E-government** is the whole pattern: the state met through systems — and e-services stand or fall on the citizen's trust that the system is the state, not a stranger wearing its name.
2. **E-learning, e-commerce and e-banking — the family's other houses** — **E-learning** delivers teaching through networks, and its pocket form, **m-learning**, through the phone. **E-commerce** buys and sells electronically, in kinds the book names by their parties: **B2C** (business to citizen — the online bookshop, bookstore.mn, is the book's own example), **B2B** (business to business), **G2B** (government to business) and **C2C** (citizen to citizen). **Internet banking** moves money through the bank's electronic doors. Grade 12 widens the same family to society itself: e-education, e-agriculture with IoT chips on livestock, e-health telemedicine, and GIS — one family, growing with the pupil, from the kiosk of Grade 9 to the systems of Grade 12.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
