# Video script — Media Literacy and Types of Media

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

The book sorts media by the job they do. **Expression media** are how a message is shaped — words, pictures, sound, video: the form the sender chooses. **Transmission media** are how it travels — the network, the broadcast, the printed copy carried hand to hand. **Recording media** are how it is kept — the file, the disc, the archive that lets a message outlive its moment. One message usually uses all three: a lesson is expressed as a video, transmitted over the internet, and recorded as a file. Sorting a message this way is not tidiness for its own sake — each family can fail, and can mislead, in its own way, and the literate reader checks the right one.
**Media literacy** is the skill of judging what the media bring: who made this, for what purpose, what was chosen and what was left out, and whether the claim can be checked somewhere independent. The literate reader treats a message as something **made** — every expression choice (the headline's words, the picture's crop, the clip's cut) was decided by someone — and asks what the same event would look like expressed differently. Egypt places this at the head of its information course, before ethics and security, because the later lessons all assume it: you cannot protect information, or use it honestly, if you cannot first judge the messages that carry it.
