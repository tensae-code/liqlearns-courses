# Video script — Stereotypes in the Media

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

A **stereotype** is a ready-made picture of a group: “all X are like this” — repeated so often it feels like knowledge. A **prejudice** is a judgement made before knowing: the stereotype turned into a verdict on a real person. The Décodage livret insists on the difference, because the fight differs: stereotypes are unpicked by counter-examples and closer looks; prejudices, by meeting the actual person or fact before judging.
Media repeat stereotypes because they are shortcuts: the advertisement's mother always cooking, the game's hero always a boy, the news expert always a man. **Gender stereotypes** in media quietly teach who may do what — until a viewer starts counting. The scenario's craft is counting: watch, tally who does what, and the pattern that felt like “just how things are” becomes a choice someone made — and choices can be made differently.
