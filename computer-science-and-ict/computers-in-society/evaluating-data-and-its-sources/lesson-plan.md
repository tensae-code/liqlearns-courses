## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Validation — is this data what it claims to be?** — **Evaluating data** (validasi data) tests the data itself before any analysis stands on it. The tests are plain: is it **accurate** (does it match the real thing it counts or describes), **complete** (are the missing rows the story), **current** (old data about a changed world misleads politely), and **consistent** (do its parts agree with each other and with what is known)? Data that fails validation is not argued with — it is repaired, replaced or refused, because every later step multiplies its faults.
2. **Evaluating the source — where data comes from is part of the data** — Data arrives from a **source**, and the source is part of its meaning: who collected it, by what method, for what purpose, and with what care. A figure from a careful official count and the same-looking figure from an interested party's leaflet are not the same data. Indonesia's Grade 10 chapter pairs the two evaluations — data and source — inside its data chapter, before scraping and searching begin: the habit of asking 'where did this come from, and how was it made?' is what keeps a data worker from building confidently on sand.
