# Trade secrets — valuable information kept confidential

## Do it first

A drink's recipe is worth a fortune — and it is written nowhere public. A company's customer list sits in one guarded file. This lesson is about the shield that works by silence: the secret itself.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a trade secret is

A **trade secret is intellectual property in confidential information that may be sold or licensed**. Its protection is the confidentiality itself — the information is valuable because others do not have it.

The book's examples: **customer lists, source code, food and drink recipes — Pepsi, Coca-Cola and Sprite are the named drinks — and software processes**.

## Sub lesson 2 — The violation

**Taking, using or disclosing** a trade secret **contrary to honest commercial practice is an unfair practice and a violation**.

Notice the standard: not secrecy at any cost, but **honest commercial practice**. Winning a customer fairly is commerce; stealing the list that names them is not.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what a trade secret is, give two examples from different families, and explain what makes taking one a violation.

## Do It Yourself

1. Invent a small business. Write its three trade secrets and the honest-practice line: what a rival may fairly do, and what would be a violation.
2. For one named example (a drink recipe), write what would count as taking, using, and disclosing — three separate acts.
