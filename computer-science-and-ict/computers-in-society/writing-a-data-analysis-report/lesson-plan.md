# Lesson plan — Writing a Data Analysis Report

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Computers in Society · **Lesson:** Writing a Data Analysis Report
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — China (2026-11-14) — in the title Computers in Society on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from China's *IT Compulsory 1 (Grade 11 record)*, because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A data analysis report is the written end of the data-processing journey: it states the question, describes the data and how it was collected and cleaned, presents the findings with charts, draws conclusions, and recommends what to do — so that people who never saw the data can trust the answer and act on it.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the whole data-processing chain — process, collection, cleaning, analysis, visualisation. It is the lesson where the chain's work is handed over to other people.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What a report contains** — A data analysis report has a steady skeleton. **The question**: what was asked, and why it matters. **The data**: where it came from, how it was collected, how it was cleaned — stated honestly, gaps included, because the reader's trust is built here. **The findings**: what the analysis showed, with the key charts doing the persuading (a chart in a report is evidence, not decoration). **The conclusions**: what the findings mean, stated as plainly as the evidence allows — and no further. **The recommendations**: what should be done, by whom, following from the conclusions. Each part exists because a reader deciding whether to act will ask exactly those questions, in that order.
2. **Writing it clearly — and applying it** — A report is written for its **reader**, not its author: plain words, the question answered in the first page, every chart titled so it can be understood alone, and numbers always tied to their meaning ('mathematics fell 12 points in Grade 9' — not 'some values decreased'). Honesty is a writing rule, not only a data rule: a finding the data only half-supports is written as a half-finding. And the report's life ends in **application**: the recommendations are discussed, decided on, and acted on — the report that changes nothing was a school exercise; the report that changes a decision completes the whole data-processing process this chapter teaches.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the parts of a data analysis report, in order, and say what question each part answers for the reader.
2. Explain why the data section must state gaps and cleaning honestly.
3. Write a finding as a clear sentence with its numbers tied to their meaning.
4. Say why application — acting on the recommendations — is part of the process, not an extra.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil; the findings and charts from the analysis lesson (or the teacher's set) to report on.

## If the learner is stuck
Writes the report as a diary of what the team did → the reader asks: what was the question, what did you find, what should we do? Reorder the writing to the reader's questions. Overstates a conclusion → match the sentence to the evidence: if the data covers one term, the conclusion speaks of one term.
