# Digital ownership — ask the creator first

## Do it first

Copy a paragraph from a page. Paste it into your own document. It took two seconds — and it felt like nothing. This lesson is about why that easy act has an owner, a right, and a correct way through: ask first.

## Sub lesson 1 — What digital ownership is

**Digital ownership is being able to access, use and protect data, information and knowledge about yourself or things you own.** Three verbs carry it: access it, use it, protect it.

Ownership is hard to hold online because **digital work copies and pastes so easily**. The ease of copying does not remove the owner — it makes the owner's right easier to break by accident.

## Sub lesson 2 — Permission — what asking does

**Using someone else's digital work without the creator's permission is wrong.** The correct road is short: ask, and credit.

Permission does three things at once: it **credits the source**, it **spreads knowledge properly**, and it **protects the user from the legal results of breaking ownership rights**. Asking is not politeness added on top — it is the mechanism that makes use lawful.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what digital ownership means, then walk them through what you would do before using a photo you found online in your school project.

## Do It Yourself

1. Find one digital work you used recently (a photo, a text, a song). Write: who created it, did you have permission, how would you ask?
2. Write a two-line permission request you could actually send to a creator.
