# Information Culture — Norms, Rules and Principles Online

## Do it first

Grade 7's informatics ends not with a program but with a culture: the whole final chapter is about how a person should carry themselves in the information world. This first section lays out the concept itself — the norms, rules and principles.

## Sub lesson 1 — What information culture is

**Information culture** is the part of a person's general culture that governs their life with information: the **knowledge** of what information is and where it lives, the **skills** of seeking, judging and using it, and the **habits and values** — accuracy, honesty, respect for others' work — that steer both. A person with information culture does not merely *use* the virtual space; they know its rules of the road and keep them even when nobody is watching. The book places it as the crown of the course for that reason.

In your mind's eye: what information culture is.

## Sub lesson 2 — Norms, rules and principles — the working list

The section's norms are practical. **Judge before you trust**: a source is checked, not believed at sight. **Credit before you take**: another's text, picture or program is used with its author named and its licence kept. **Share responsibly**: what you pass on becomes partly yours — falsehood forwarded is falsehood spread. **Behave as in real space**: the politeness and law of the physical world do not dissolve online. And **guard your own and others' private information** as you would a house key. These five are the chapter's principles in working form.
