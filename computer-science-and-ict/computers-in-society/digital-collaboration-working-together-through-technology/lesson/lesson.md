# Digital collaboration — working together through technology

## Do it first

Three pupils, three towns, one essay — due tomorrow, already half-written, and every change visible to all of them the second it happens. That ordinary miracle has a name and a structure. This lesson names it.

## Sub lesson 1 — What digital collaboration is

**Digital collaboration is working with other people or teams and getting things done using digital devices and technology — tools, apps, software, procedures and processes together**.

It **improves communication, document and content management and information flow across an organisation, raising productivity** — and **the internet lets people collaborate in real time across time zones and geography**, in organisations, groups, communities, schools and creative groups.

## Sub lesson 2 — A way of working, not a set of tools (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide (teacher guide — evidence) states the framing plainly: **introducing collaboration tools is not the point — digital collaboration is a way of working or learning that makes collaboration easier even when pupils and teachers are in different physical places**.

Used that way, it lets a teacher **give a more dynamic experience, modify classes to meet each pupil's needs, and let pupils work at their own speed, pursue their interests and engage with classmates at the same time**. The tools serve the working; they are not the working.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the definition, the three improvements, and the guide's framing — then give one example where the tool mattered less than the way of working.
