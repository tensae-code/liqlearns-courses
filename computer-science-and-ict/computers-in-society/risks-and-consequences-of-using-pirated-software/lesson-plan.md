## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The risks inside the copy — malware and broken software** — A pirate source has no reputation to protect: its copies are a favourite carrier of **malware** — viruses and spyware packed with the program, installed by the user's own hand. Pirated software also comes **broken and alone**: it cannot be **updated**, so its holes stay open; it may fail or lose work; and no maker's help, manual or support stands behind it. The 'free' program is paid for in risk.
2. **The consequences — law and loss** — Using pirated software breaks **copyright law**, and the consequences reach the user: **fines and legal action** where the law is enforced, removal of the software, and — in a school or office — blame that falls on the whole institution. The lesson's sum is the unit's sum: piracy steals from the maker, endangers the user, and breaks the law — three costs for one copy that was never honestly free.
