# Risks and Consequences of Using Pirated Software

## Do it first

Pirated software is not only wrong — it is dangerous to its own user. Class VIII's next lesson counts the cost: what a pirate copy can carry into a machine, what it can cost in law, and what its user gives up without knowing.

## Sub lesson 1 — The risks inside the copy — malware and broken software

A pirate source has no reputation to protect: its copies are a favourite carrier of **malware** — viruses and spyware packed with the program, installed by the user's own hand. Pirated software also comes **broken and alone**: it cannot be **updated**, so its holes stay open; it may fail or lose work; and no maker's help, manual or support stands behind it. The 'free' program is paid for in risk.

## Sub lesson 2 — The consequences — law and loss

Using pirated software breaks **copyright law**, and the consequences reach the user: **fines and legal action** where the law is enforced, removal of the software, and — in a school or office — blame that falls on the whole institution. The lesson's sum is the unit's sum: piracy steals from the maker, endangers the user, and breaks the law — three costs for one copy that was never honestly free.
