# Lesson plan — ICT and Economic Development — Jobs, Investment, Productivity

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Computers in Society · **Lesson:** ICT and Economic Development — Jobs, Investment, Productivity
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Bangladesh (2026-11-14) — in the title Computers in Society on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Bangladesh's NCTB student books (Bangladesh HSC ICT (Classes XI–XII and Alim)), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree and live lesson content were checked for the slug and for near matches before building.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** ICT's role in economic development: how information technology grows an economy — new jobs, attracted investment, raised productivity, faster communication — and its environmental side (Bangladesh HSC ICT, Chapter 1, §1.6.1).
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Jobs and investment — work appears, capital follows** — ICT grows economies first through **jobs**: whole occupations exist only because the technology does — programmers, network engineers, support workers, the outsourcing and freelancing workforce of the companion lesson — and every connected business employs people to run its systems. Then **investment** follows the connections: companies build where communication is fast and reliable, so a country that wires itself becomes a country capital chooses. Bangladesh's hi-tech parks and Startup Bangladesh, in the Digital Bangladesh lesson, are this lever pulled deliberately.
2. **Productivity and communication — more from the same day** — **Productivity** is output per worker, and ICT raises it everywhere at once: a farmer with a price before selling, a clerk whose files search themselves, a factory whose machines report their own faults. Underneath all of it is **communication** — cheaper and faster every year — shrinking the cost of every agreement, order and delivery. The book adds the **environment** to the ledger: paperless work and travel replaced by meetings save real resources, while the village's devices and data centres spend energy that must be counted honestly on the other side.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — A country gets richer when its people can make more, sell wider, and waste less. This lesson watches one tool — ICT — push on every one of those levers at once, which is why governments that cannot afford everything still find money for connections. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of jobs and investment — work appears, capital follows as the lesson describes it — the student studies the picture and names what each part shows.
