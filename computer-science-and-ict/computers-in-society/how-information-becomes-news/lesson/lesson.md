# How Information Becomes News

## Do it first

Something happens in a village at dawn. By noon it is on screens in the capital; by evening, the world has an opinion about it. The event did not travel by itself — hands carried it, chose it, shaped it. This lesson walks the road from event to news, so you can see the hands.

## Sub lesson 1 — Gathering — agencies and reporters

News begins as **information**: an event someone witnessed. **Reporters** gather it — who, what, where, when, why — and **news agencies** collect reports from many places and sell or share them to many newsrooms at once, which is why the same event appears in a hundred papers in similar words. Not everything gathered becomes news: editors choose what matters to their readers — that choosing, called gatekeeping, is the first shaping every story undergoes.

## Sub lesson 2 — Publishing — print, broadcast and online journalism

The chosen report is written, checked against sources, and published — in print, on air, and now instantly by **online journalism**, where a story can be updated as facts arrive and readers can answer back. Speed is online news's gift and its danger: a story published before its **fact-checking** is done carries errors to millions before the correction wakes. The reader's defence is the course's own skill — verify before you share — applied hardest to the newest news.
