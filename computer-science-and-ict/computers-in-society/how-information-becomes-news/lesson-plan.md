## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Gathering — agencies and reporters** — News begins as **information**: an event someone witnessed. **Reporters** gather it — who, what, where, when, why — and **news agencies** collect reports from many places and sell or share them to many newsrooms at once, which is why the same event appears in a hundred papers in similar words. Not everything gathered becomes news: editors choose what matters to their readers — that choosing, called gatekeeping, is the first shaping every story undergoes.
2. **Publishing — print, broadcast and online journalism** — The chosen report is written, checked against sources, and published — in print, on air, and now instantly by **online journalism**, where a story can be updated as facts arrive and readers can answer back. Speed is online news's gift and its danger: a story published before its **fact-checking** is done carries errors to millions before the correction wakes. The reader's defence is the course's own skill — verify before you share — applied hardest to the newest news.
