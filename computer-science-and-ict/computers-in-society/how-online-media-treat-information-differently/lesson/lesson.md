# How Online Media Treat Information Differently

## Do it first

A printed newspaper cannot change after breakfast. An online front page changes while you read it — and shows your neighbour a different front page. Before reading on, name one more way online news is not like paper news.

## Sub lesson 1 — Speed, updating and the unfinished story

Online media publish **at once** and **correct in place**: a story appears as a first alert, grows through the day, and its early errors may be silently replaced. The reader therefore meets unfinished stories — speed bought with certainty. Links carry the reader outward to sources and sideways to related pieces, and multimedia — video, sound, live data — joins the text. The paper's fixed edition becomes a flowing stream.

In your mind's eye: speed, updating and the unfinished story.

## Sub lesson 2 — The reader steers — and is steered

Online, the reader chooses paths through links and search — but the medium also chooses for the reader: rankings, recommendations and feeds order what is seen first, shaped by what the reader clicked before. Comments and shares make readers into distributors. The scenario's lesson is double vision: read the story, and read the machinery that brought this story, in this order, to you.

In your mind's eye: the reader steers — and is steered.
