# Lesson plan — My Language and Identity in the Digital World

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Computers in Society · **Lesson:** My Language and Identity in the Digital World
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Kuwait (2026-11-14) — in the title Computers in Society on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Kuwait's student books (Ministry of Education, State of Kuwait), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree and live lesson content were checked for the slug and for near matches before building.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Pride in your own language and identity online — Arabic as a language of identity, and writing correct Arabic in digital communication
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. ## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Arabic — a language of identity** — **Identity** is who you are and where you belong; **language** is identity's loudest voice. Kuwait teaches that **Arabic is a language of identity** for its pupils: the language of their home, their country and their history, and it belongs in the digital world at full strength — in messages, posts and schoolwork — not apologised for and not abandoned for a more fashionable tongue.
2. **Writing correct Arabic in digital communication** — Pride is practised in the writing: **correct Arabic** in digital communication — real words written properly, sentences that say what they mean, the language's letters and forms used with care even in a quick message. The lesson's claim is that sloppy writing is not modern and careful writing is not old-fashioned: every message a pupil sends in Arabic carries the language a little further into the digital world, exactly as well — or as badly — as it is written.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — The digital world speaks many languages, and a child can feel theirs is small there. Kuwait's Grade 4 Term 2 book opens its Digital World unit against that feeling: Lesson 1 is pride — I am proud of my language and my identity — taught as a digital skill. This lesson is that pride, and the care for the language that carries it. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
