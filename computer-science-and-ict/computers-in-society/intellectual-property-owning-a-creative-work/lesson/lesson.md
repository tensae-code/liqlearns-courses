# Intellectual property — owning a creative work

## Do it first

You write a program. A friend invents a machine. A shop paints its sign. Three creations, three different legal shields — and mixing them up leaves a creator unprotected. This lesson lays out the family before the next four lessons take one member each.

## Sub lesson 1 — What intellectual property is — in Ethiopian law

**Intellectual property rights are intangible rights of ownership over an asset such as a software program or a designed system**, protected differently by type — you cannot hold them in your hand, but the law holds them for you.

The book quotes Ethiopia's **Intellectual Property Office Establishment Proclamation No. 320/2003**: intellectual property is **a legal right over a creative work of the human intellect**, including **patent, trademark, registration certificate and copyright**.

## Sub lesson 2 — The four types — and the guide's comparison table (teacher guide — evidence)

For software and systems the book takes **four types: trade secrets, patents, copyrights and trademarks** — the next four lessons give each its own.

The guide's review table (teacher guide — evidence) sets three of them side by side. **By what each protects:** copyright — **original works of authorship**; patent — **inventions such as processes, machines, manufactures and compositions of matter and their improvements**; trademark — **any word, phrase, symbol or design identifying the source of goods**. **By what protection requires:** copyright — the work be **original, creative and fixed in a tangible medium**; patent — **new, useful and non-obvious**; trademark — **distinctive enough to identify a source**. **By the right granted:** copyright — **control of reproduction, derivative works, distribution, public performance and display**; patent — **preventing others making, selling, using or importing the invention**; trademark — **using the mark and stopping confusingly similar marks**.
