# Lesson plan — Intellectual property — owning a creative work

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Computers in Society · **Lesson:** Intellectual property — owning a creative work
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 14 — Computers in Society of the Computer Science and ICT ladder (TITLES.md); Ethiopia Grade 12, Unit 1 — Information Systems and Their Applications. The ladder is the recommended order, never a forced one.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** intellectual property is a legal right over a creative work of the human intellect — Ethiopia's Proclamation No. 320/2003 — and for software and systems it takes four types: trade secrets, patents, copyrights and trademarks.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the digital-ownership lesson earlier in this unit (recommended first, never forced): ownership was the idea; this is its legal form.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What intellectual property is — in Ethiopian law** — intangible rights over assets like software and designed systems; the Proclamation No. 320/2003 definition quoted.
2. **The four types — and the guide's comparison table (teacher guide — evidence)** — trade secrets, patents, copyrights, trademarks; the guide's three-way table by what each protects, what it requires, what right it grants.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define intellectual property and cite Proclamation No. 320/2003.
2. Name the four types for software and systems.
3. Compare copyright, patent and trademark on the guide's three rows.
