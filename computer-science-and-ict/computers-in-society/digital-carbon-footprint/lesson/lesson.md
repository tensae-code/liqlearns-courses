# The Digital Carbon Footprint

## Do it first

Digital feels weightless. It is not: every message, stream and stored photo burns energy somewhere, and the burning has a measure. This lesson learns the measure.

## Sub lesson 1 — What the digital carbon footprint is

A **carbon footprint** is the total greenhouse-gas emissions a activity causes, counted in CO₂-equivalent. The **digital** footprint counts digital life's share: the **devices** (their making — the largest part for most gadgets — and their charging), the **networks** that carry, and the **data centres** that store and compute. A single message's share is tiny; the lesson's first honesty is scale: tiny × billions × always-on is a country's power station, and 'the cloud' is other people's very material buildings.

## Sub lesson 2 — Counting and shrinking it

Counting follows the life: **making** (mining, manufacture, shipping), **using** (electricity × the grid's dirtiness × hours), **storing forever** (the photo never deleted, copied in three buildings, forever warm), and **throwing away** (the e-waste lesson's ground). Shrinking mirrors the counting: keep devices **longer**, delete and unsubscribe, stream at the size the screen needs, choose services on renewable power, and let old kit be recycled. Jordan places the footprint inside green computing because a footprint is a principle with a number on it — and numbers can be improved on purpose.
