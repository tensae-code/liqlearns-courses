## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What the digital carbon footprint is** — A **carbon footprint** is the total greenhouse-gas emissions a activity causes, counted in CO₂-equivalent. The **digital** footprint counts digital life's share: the **devices** (their making — the largest part for most gadgets — and their charging), the **networks** that carry, and the **data centres** that store and compute. A single message's share is tiny; the lesson's first honesty is scale: tiny × billions × always-on is a country's power station, and 'the cloud' is other people's very material buildings.
2. **Counting and shrinking it** — Counting follows the life: **making** (mining, manufacture, shipping), **using** (electricity × the grid's dirtiness × hours), **storing forever** (the photo never deleted, copied in three buildings, forever warm), and **throwing away** (the e-waste lesson's ground). Shrinking mirrors the counting: keep devices **longer**, delete and unsubscribe, stream at the size the screen needs, choose services on renewable power, and let old kit be recycled. Jordan places the footprint inside green computing because a footprint is a principle with a number on it — and numbers can be improved on purpose.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Digital feels weightless. It is not: every message, stream and stored photo burns energy somewhere, and the burning has a measure. This lesson learns the measure. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of what the digital carbon footprint is as the lesson describes it — the student studies the picture and names what each part shows.
   - ADD GIF: a GIF of counting and shrinking it in action — the student watches once, then says what changed and why.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the lesson's key names and definitions aloud, in the order the lesson gives them — the student listens once through, then reads the same lines aloud along with the voice. (Audio is a described slot in this build; no audio file is made.)
3. **Say it to your companion** — the companion block in lesson/lesson.md.
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the lesson in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-false, matching, short-answer — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the doing block at the end of lesson/lesson.md.
