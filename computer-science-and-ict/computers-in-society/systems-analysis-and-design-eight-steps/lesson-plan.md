## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Building a computerised system in eight steps** — When an organisation replaces a hand system with a computerised one, the work follows an ordered road — Sudan's Grade 11 book counts **eight steps** from the first look at the present system to the new system running. The steps are climbed in order because each one answers the question the next one needs: what is wrong, what is needed, what the new system will be, and only then its building, testing and handing over. Skipping a step does not save the step — it moves its cost into the running system, where every mistake is dearer.
2. **Systems analysis, then systems design** — The road's first half is **systems analysis**: studying the present system — its data, its people, its faults — and fixing exactly what the new system must do. The second half begins with **systems design**: drawing the new system before it is built — its files, its inputs, its processing and its outputs — so the builders work from a plan, not from guesses. Analysis asks *what*; design answers *how*; the book's worked example after this lesson, a complete system for a spare-parts factory, is analysed and designed exactly this way.
