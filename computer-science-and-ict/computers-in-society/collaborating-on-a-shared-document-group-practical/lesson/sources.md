**Acceptance count: taught in 6 countries (Ethiopia, Cambodia, Cuba, Bhutan, Greece, Armenia).**

Book errors touching Unit 1, taught corrected and named here: the student book numbers "Brainstorming 1.7" twice (for §1.4, printed p. 18, and again for §1.5, printed p. 22); the teacher guide's §1.2 competency reads "Define Intellectual property (IR)" for IP, and the guide's Discussion strategy is copy-pasted as discussing "Digital Identity Management issues" in §§1.2, 1.4 and 1.5 whatever the section. The lessons teach the topics, not the copy faults. Where the guide's keyed answers correct or extend the student text, they are filed as labelled teacher evidence and say so in the table above.

Cuba's own way (Grade 6): Grade 6 adds the three productivity firsts in one year — digital presentations, Scratch animation with event/control blocks, and spreadsheets with formulas and charts — closing with a TIC chapter on searching and processing digital information; computer generations are taught inside Chapter 1.

Bhutan's own way (Class VI, Literacy with ICT student book): School computing runs on Google's G Suite for Education — email is Gmail on the school account, collaboration is Google Classroom, and the end-of-year food-chain project is agreed in Classroom, built in Word, password-protected, cited, filed in folders and reported to the teacher by email.

Greece's own way (Grade 9, Pliroforiki C' Gymnasiou, 2024): Grade 9 is project-led — recursion first, then Python (EduBlocks and Thonny, rock-paper-scissors with randomness), physical computing (code for the International Space Station, micro:bit, Arduino with breadboard and Tinkercad), AI with App Inventor image recognition, collaboration in Greece's own +γραφίς (+grafis) document service, AI ethics/privacy/cookies/big and open data, and scientific programming (solving equations, exponential spread modelling).

Switzerland's own way (Grades 1–2, Décodage 1re–2e): Vaud's Romandie digital-education curriculum is taught in three axes — Science informatique (SI), Médias (M), Usages (U) — almost entirely UNPLUGGED at this age: algorithms are learned with the body (robot game, crane game, hand-washing, getting dressed, a crêpe recipe), a physical sorting machine, and the Blue-Bot floor robot; the class also builds a collective digital “charter book” of good digital habits across the whole cycle.

Switzerland's own way (Grades 3–4, Décodage 3e–4e): the 3e–4e volume repeats the 1re–2e unplugged activities at a higher level (loops and debugging join the robot and crane games and the sorting machine) and adds three new strands: Thymio robotics, a full ScratchJr scenario, and media “parcours” — Parcours des bestioles (how images influence emotions), Parcours du partage (image rights, digital traces, thinking before sharing) and Parcours de la pub (recognising and creating advertising). Pixel paravent introduces binary coding as transmitting a black-and-white image across a screen with an agreed code; Deviner la carte teaches the conditional through a magic trick and its logigramme.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 4 G4 Teacher): Guide plans mirror the workbook blocks (PowerPoint, Excel, Word) with the same per-block learning products and assessment appendices as the other early-grades guides.

Jordan's own way (Grade 4 Digital Skills Teacher Guide — links-pass L2 sections): Digital Skills is not a standalone subject book at this level — the guide embeds digital skills inside the child's other subjects as six "learning environments" (Science and Maths units) plus staged learning projects, each with named digital tools (PowerPoint, Excel, Word, Padlet, PhET), learning outcomes, and a performance rubric.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 5 G5 student (Antares)): One of two different Grade 5 books in the set (this one by Velijanyan & Ayvazyan, Antares); it runs as numbered § lessons mixing information theory, careful judgement of fake vs true information, and a long practical spine — text editor, spreadsheets, algorithms and variables — ending in teamwork principles.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 5 G5 teacher): The guide adds a strand the student book does not print — touch-typing with ten fingers — plus per-theme outcome/source tables (including the KuMir programming environment for variables), sample lesson plans, and full assessment rubrics for projects and slide presentations. Page numbers below are the guide's PDF pagination, which matches its printed folios where the text layer shows them.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 8 G8 teacher): Matches Book 14 exactly: its mapping table ties the student book's 19 §s to 14 standard themes (8-1 to 8-14), each theme with outcomes, competencies and practical work; the guide adds a thematic plan, the book's questions/tasks with answers, and slide-presentation assessment criteria.
