# Collaborating on a shared document — group practical

## Do it first

No reading first this time — the lesson IS the doing. Form your group of five and name it. Everything after this line is the practical itself, in the book's order; the explanation comes inside the work, where it belongs.

## Sub lesson 1 — The practical, step by step

In the book's order: **form a group of five and name it. Write a half-page summary of the benefits of digital collaboration tools for online learning and group work. Create it in chosen cloud word-processing software as an online document named with the group name. Send the link to all members by email or social media.**

Then: **choose a screen-sharing meeting tool (Zoom, Google Meet, Teams or another); one member generates the meeting link and another joins; and the group debates and reviews the summary, changing the document by screen sharing from wherever they are.**

## Sub lesson 2 — Discuss, improve, share with the class

The practical does not end at the edited document. **Discuss how such tools improve learning and teamwork, and share the experience with the class** — the difficulties included, since the guide (teacher guide — evidence) asks pupils to present **both what they learned and the challenges**.

What to listen for in the discussion: a change the group could not have made on paper, a disagreement the screen-share settled, and a moment the technology itself got in the way. Those three are the honest report.

## Say it to your companion

As a group, tell another group your story: the summary, the meeting, the edit that changed it most, and the hardest moment.

## Do It Yourself

1. Re-run one step better: hold a second, shorter meeting and fix the one weakness your class report named.
2. Write your group's one-page manual for the next class: the steps, the tool choices, and the three warnings you earned.
