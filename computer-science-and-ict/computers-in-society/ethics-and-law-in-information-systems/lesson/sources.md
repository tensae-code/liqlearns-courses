The lessons teach the topics, not the copy faults. Where the guide's keyed answers correct or extend the student text, they are filed as labelled teacher evidence and say so in the table above.

Egypt's own way (Grade 10, Term 1 Programming and Artificial Intelligence student book): A full Japanese-style "Information Study" course adopted for Egypt's new secondary track: 13 chapters running from information theory, ethics and security through number systems, data analysis (frequency, variance, correlation, regression in spreadsheets), simulation and queues, Python, and a final AI-programming project chapter building web pages, a quiz app and a block-breaker game with generative AI.

Fiji's own way (Years 7–8 Computer Education student book): A practical, exercise-driven primary book built around a class "students folder" of set files (work is saved as U<unit>-Ex<n>-<FirstName>); local Fijian names and schools throughout (e.g. Babasiga Secondary School), Internet searching is practised on the "Map of Fiji" with kids' search engines, and a full unit covers computers in Fijian society including Braille keyboards for blind learners. PARTIAL: the PDF as received has no cover, title page, contents or imprint — the PARTIAL label travels with every citation of this book.

Libya's own way (Grade 12 Humanities, Information Technology student book): the Humanities final year pairs the social/legal side of ICT (data security, e-government, computer law and software licences) with a long multimedia-and-PowerPoint practical; the closing projects are Libyan — a deck about Libyan dishes and a deck about Libya's archaeological cities and landmarks.

Libya's own way (Grade 12 Science, Information Technology student book): the Science section shares the Humanities book's first three chapters (security, e-government, computer law) word-for-structure, then diverges into a full second Visual Basic course — the VB environment and form controls, arrays, subroutines and built-in functions, and the remaining toolbox controls. Visual Basic is the Grade 12 programming language in both sections.

Malaysia's own way (Grade 11, SK Tingkatan 5): the final-year elective is exam-level computer science — computer architecture (fetch-decode-execute, Von Neumann, pipelining) and logic gates with Boolean expressions, a 57-page advanced SQL chapter that starts from ERD and normalisation, and a full web-programming chapter pair: client-side JavaScript (including sorting/searching, standard library, arrays) and server-side PHP working with text files, validation, queries, sign-up/login, ending in an interactive website build.

Mongolia's own way (Grade 10): Grade 10 is the "citizen" book — ICT ethics, law and intellectual property first, taught with Mongolian digital content as the examples (the Nogun comic, the Chinggis Khaan animation, GoGo Kids, Maamuu and YoLo; the CRC's black-list.mn), then a distinctive "package information" chapter — collecting, storing, merging and presenting information as one whole, worked through a 24-slide presentation on the sights of Bayankhongor aimag — multimedia production with free tools (Pencil2D, Pivot Animator, Unfreez, Audacity), and a full MS Access database chapter built around a Mongolian phone-book database and the state's unified registration system.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 6 Reading Book): a story/dialogue-led "Reading Book" with a talking computer character; every chapter cross-refers to numbered Workbook activities; local sectors (school office, ATM, ICU, factories, agriculture) and the 3R e-waste method are taught from Grade 6.

Kuwait's own way (Grade 10 Information Technology Term 1): secondary school switches to the subject name Information Technology and a more academic register: a full cyber-security unit built on the CIA triad (with hands-on two-factor authentication in Google Authenticator and the Ministry app), then a complete first Python course in PyCharm — variables, strings, conditions, loops, debugging and exceptions — and a team-built digital product.

Laos's own way (Grade 6 ICT student book): a theory-only foundation year — no hands-on computer practicals at all; ICT is introduced through Lao daily-life examples (TV remote, household appliances) and every chapter opens with objectives, guiding questions and a discussion activity (ກິດຈະກໍາ) before the exposition. The RIES ICT line runs ມ.1–ມ.7 (Grades 6–12).

Moldova's own way (Grade 7, Informatică): lower-secondary Informatica is a real textbook discipline, not computer-skills practice — number systems, text/image/sound encoding and data types in Pascal sit alongside GUI literacy, and two full chapters are given to behaviour, law and information culture in virtual spaces.

Bangladesh's own way (Grade 9, Digital Technology): Grade 9 turns practical — a full Python course (Thonny IDE) inside the book, website building with WordPress and Google services for a citizen-service site, and hands-on networking between two school computers. The NCTB Digital Technology line runs in parallel English and Bangla editions; placements are cited from the English Version with the Bangla Version parallel spot-checked.

Bangladesh's own way (HSC ICT, Classes XI–XII and Alim): one compulsory ICT book for every HSC and Alim student in the country, saturated with Bangladesh itself: Bangabandhu Satellite-1 and its ground stations, bdjobs and chakri.com job portals, Daraz and Bikroy e-commerce, the Walton factory's automated production line, the National ID card used to teach primary keys and its database used to teach RDBMS at work, a full Bangla Unicode table and a worked example of putting Bangla text on a web page with meta charset, the national letter-grade scale (A+ from 80) as the else-if programme, "I Love Bangladesh" as the loop example text, an algorithm for reaching college by bicycle or rickshaw, and the Digital Bangladesh programme — National Data Centre, hi-tech parks, Startup Bangladesh and Bangladesh Bank's QR-based Cashless Bangladesh. C is the HSC programming language, taught with scanf/printf in Code::Blocks.

Vietnam's own way (Grade 8 Tin học 8): Grade 8 opens with a history of calculating tools (abacus, Pascal's Pascaline, computer generations) and its applied theme is a genuine fork — track a does advanced Word/PowerPoint, track b learns photo editing in GIMP; programming is Scratch again, but now with data representation and a named debugging lesson (Gỡ lỗi).

Vietnam's own way (Grade 9 Tin học 9): Grade 9 teaches simulation software (PhET, Geometer's Sketchpad) as a way to learn by experiment, and its applied fork is advanced Excel (data validation, COUNTIF/SUMIF/IF, a family-finance workbook project) versus a full video-production track in Video Editor from storyboard to export.

South Sudan's own way (Grade 10 S2 Student): Year 2 moves from single-computer skills to connected and analytical work — networks taught through intranet file-sharing and a full Microsoft Outlook unit (contacts, calendar, meetings, task lists), then a deep Excel unit that goes well beyond basics into lookup/financial/text functions, inventories, correlation and regression, what-if analysis with Solver, and workbook protection; social media is taught platform by platform with its ethical and legal issues.

South Sudan's own way (Grade 10 S2 Teacher): The guide mirrors the student book's three units with teaching notes, exercise answers and research tasks (e.g. learners research hybrid topologies themselves); it keeps the same emphasis on resource sharing in an intranet and on Excel as an analysis tool (what-if, Goal Seek, Solver). (teacher guide — evidence)

South Sudan's own way (Grade 11 S3 Student): Year 3 is creation and programming: learners build and run a real personal website/blog end-to-end in WordPress (hosting, domain, themes, posts, legal issues and comment moderation), study social networks platform by platform (LinkedIn, Google+ and the Students Circle Network, with South Sudanese business use), then learn programming through data types, arrays and structures in C/C++ and writing programs in Notepad, closing on ethical conduct and intellectual property.
