# Lesson plan — Ethics and law in information systems

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Computers in Society · **Lesson:** Ethics and law in information systems
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 14 — Computers in Society of the Computer Science and ICT ladder (TITLES.md); Ethiopia Grade 12, Unit 1 — Information Systems and Their Applications. The ladder is the recommended order, never a forced one.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** ethics is deciding right from wrong — and information systems change so fast that their ethical and legal rules are still being settled, so privacy, security, gathering and ownership must be learned directly.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Grade 11 information-systems lessons in this same title (recommended first, never forced) and needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds, looping.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Ethics, and why information systems raise questions** — ethics as deciding right from wrong; the missing settled standard for fast-changing systems.
2. **The guide's worked entry — online banking risk (teacher guide — evidence)** — the guide opens ethics through digital banking: public devices, recovered logins, users who are not vigilant.
3. **What breaking ethics costs (teacher guide — evidence)** — the guide's graded consequences: for a person, an organisation, and under the law.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the lesson named in this plan's level line, inside this same title. Recommended, never forced. A learner can land here directly; the lesson stands alone.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define ethics and say why information systems lack a settled ethical standard.
2. Name the four question areas: privacy, security, gathering, ownership.
3. Explain the public-computer banking risk and name what it costs to break ethics.

## If the learner is stuck
Mixes ethics and law → ethics is the right-from-wrong judgement; law is the written rule with a sanction. This lesson holds both: judge first, then check what the law adds.
