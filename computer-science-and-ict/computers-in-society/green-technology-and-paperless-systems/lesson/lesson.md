# Green technology and paperless systems

## Do it first

One school term of printed handouts for one class: picture the stack. Now picture the same term sent as files. This lesson is the wider version of that picture — the technologies whose whole point is a smaller footprint.

## Sub lesson 1 — Green technology — the named examples

The response the book teaches is **green or clean technology that monitors or reduces technology's environmental impact**.

Its examples are named: **solar panels, electric vehicles, smart meters, natural gas and wind turbines**. Each one earns its place by the same test — less damage for the same work.

[Picture 29720 — solar panel, electric vehicle and wind turbine beside a paper stack shrinking to an email]

## Sub lesson 2 — Paperless working and the virtual laboratory

**Electronic, paperless communication — email, portals, applications — cuts tree-felling for paper.** The document still travels; the tree stays standing.

And **worldwide virtual collaboration lets experts share research without the carbon emissions of travelling to meet** — the book's 'virtual laboratory': the meeting happens, the flight does not.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the green-technology test, three examples, and the two working patterns that shrink the footprint.

## Do It Yourself

1. Audit your schoolwork for one week: count printed pages against files sent. Write the two totals and the trees' side of the story in one line.
2. Find one green technology near you (a solar panel, a smart meter). Write what impact it monitors or reduces.
