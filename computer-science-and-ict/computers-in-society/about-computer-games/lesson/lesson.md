# About Computer Games

## Do it first

A computer can work, draw and teach — and it can also play. This lesson looks at games themselves: what kinds there are and what a player actually does in them.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a computer game is

A **computer game** is a program made for playing: it shows a world on the screen, takes the player's actions through the keyboard, mouse or joystick, and answers at once — the score changes, the figure moves, the level ends. Uzbekistan's Grade 5 book gives games a lesson of their own near the end of the year, after Paint: the pupil who has drawn and typed now meets the computer as a playmate, and learns to name what a game is before playing one.

## Sub lesson 2 — Kinds of games and playing with care

Games come in **kinds**: races and adventures, puzzles and logic games, builders and simulators, and games that teach. A good player keeps the same care as at any computer work — sits well, rests the eyes, and stops at the agreed time. The book's point is balance: the computer is a tool first and a toy second, and the pupil who can say what kind a game is, and what it trains — speed, memory, attention, planning — plays it as a lesson, not only as a pastime.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: A **computer game** is a program made for playing: it shows a world on the screen, takes the player's actions through the keyboard, mouse or joystick, and answers at once — the score changes, the figure moves, the level ends. Games come in **kinds**: races and adventures, puzzles and logic games, builders and simulators, and games that teach. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: A program made for playing. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: Through the keyboard, mouse or joystick. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: Races and adventures; puzzles and logic games (any two kinds). Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: Speed, memory, attention and planning. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
