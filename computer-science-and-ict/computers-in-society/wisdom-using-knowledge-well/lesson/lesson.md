# Wisdom — using knowledge well

## Do it first

Two drivers both know the road is wet — same knowledge. One slows down early; the other speeds on and skids. The knowledge was equal. Something else was not. This short lesson names that something.

**Wisdom** is **the ability to make wise decisions and sound judgements using one's knowledge**. It is the **highest level of human understanding**, linked to **good judgement** and to **living well**.

Knowledge tells you what is true and how things work. Wisdom is what you **do** with it when a real choice arrives: pausing, weighing, and choosing the course that holds up well — for yourself and for others.

Wisdom sits at the top of the climb this unit is making: data, information, knowledge — and now wisdom. The next lesson draws the whole climb as one pyramid.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what wisdom is, and give one choice from daily life where two people with the same knowledge could choose differently — one wisely, one not.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write down one decision you made this month. Mark what knowledge you used, and whether the judgement was sound. Say what wisdom would have changed, if anything.
2. Ask an elder at home about a decision they are glad they made slowly. Write the knowledge they used and the judgement they showed.
3. For one day, pause before three small choices and ask: what do I know, and what is the wise use of it? Note the three pauses.
