# Copyright and Artificial Intelligence

## Do it first

Grade 7's copyright sequence does something few school books anywhere do: it walks straight from Creative Commons into the question of the decade — when an AI makes the picture, whose picture is it?

## Sub lesson 1 — Who owns what an AI makes?

Copyright was built for **human creators**, so AI-made works strain its first question — **who is the author?** The section works the honest positions: the **user** who directed the making (chose, prompted, selected, arranged) holds the strongest human claim; the **maker of the AI** built the tool, as a camera-maker builds cameras without owning photographs; and a work with **no meaningful human hand** may have **no author at all** — falling outside protection, free for all, which is itself an answer with consequences. The book teaches the question as open because it is: laws and courts are deciding it now.

In your mind's eye: who owns what an AI makes?.

## Sub lesson 2 — Training on others' works — the input side

The second question faces the other way: AI systems are **trained on enormous collections of existing works** — the protected pictures and texts of millions of creators, taken without asking each. Is training **fair use** (learning, as a student learns by looking) or **copying at scale** (the works were taken, and their makers unconsented and unpaid)? The section closes the unit's arc with the citizen's equipment: meet an AI work and ask **both** questions — whose is the output, and what was taken for the input — because digital citizenship in an AI age is exactly the habit of asking them.

In your mind's eye: training on others' works — the input side.
