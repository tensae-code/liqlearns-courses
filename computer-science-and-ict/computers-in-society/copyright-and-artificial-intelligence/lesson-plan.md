# Lesson plan — Copyright and Artificial Intelligence

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Computers in Society · **Lesson:** Copyright and Artificial Intelligence
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Greece (2026-11-14) — in the title Computers in Society on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Greece's student books (ΙΤΥΕ «Διόφαντος» / ΙΕΠ, Ministry of Education), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree and live lesson content were checked for the slug and for near matches before building.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The newest copyright question: works made with and by AI — whose are they, and what may be taken to train the machine (Greece Grade 7, Pliroforiki A' Gymnasiou Unit 5)
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. ## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Who owns what an AI makes?** — Copyright was built for **human creators**, so AI-made works strain its first question — **who is the author?** The section works the honest positions: the **user** who directed the making (chose, prompted, selected, arranged) holds the strongest human claim; the **maker of the AI** built the tool, as a camera-maker builds cameras without owning photographs; and a work with **no meaningful human hand** may have **no author at all** — falling outside protection, free for all, which is itself an answer with consequences. The book teaches the question as open because it is: laws and courts are deciding it now.
2. **Training on others' works — the input side** — The second question faces the other way: AI systems are **trained on enormous collections of existing works** — the protected pictures and texts of millions of creators, taken without asking each. Is training **fair use** (learning, as a student learns by looking) or **copying at scale** (the works were taken, and their makers unconsented and unpaid)? The section closes the unit's arc with the citizen's equipment: meet an AI work and ask **both** questions — whose is the output, and what was taken for the input — because digital citizenship in an AI age is exactly the habit of asking them.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
