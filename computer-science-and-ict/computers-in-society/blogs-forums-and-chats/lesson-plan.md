# Lesson plan — Blogs, Forums and Chats

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Computers in Society · **Lesson:** Blogs, Forums and Chats
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Belarus (2026-11-14) — in the title Computers in Society on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Belarus's *Інфарматыка* Grade 10, because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Blogs, forums and chats are three written forms of Internet community, differing by time and shape: a blog is one author's dated journal that readers comment on, a forum is a lasting discussion sorted by topics, and a chat is live written talk that flows past.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Blogs and forums** — A **blog** is an author's own page of dated entries — newest first — where the author publishes and readers answer in **comments** under each entry; the author's voice leads and the talk hangs on the entries. A **forum** belongs to no single author: members open **topics** (threads), others reply under them, and the discussion stays, sorted and searchable, for later readers. The forum's strength is that a question asked once keeps its answers where the next asker can find them.
2. **Chats — live written talk** — A **chat** is written conversation in **real time**: participants are present together, messages appear as they are sent, and the talk flows past like speech — quick, informal, and soon gone or buried. The three forms divide the jobs of written community: publish and gather comments — a blog; ask, answer and keep — a forum; talk now — a chat. All three are public or semi-public writing: a message written in any of them can be copied, kept and read by people it was not written for, which is why the book teaches them together with the chapter's social networks, under one care rule — write as if the whole world may read it.

2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
