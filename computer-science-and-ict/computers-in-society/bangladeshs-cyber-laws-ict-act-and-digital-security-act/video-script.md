# Video script — Bangladesh's Cyber Laws — the ICT Act and the Digital Security Act

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Bangladesh's **ICT Act 2006** was the country's first law for the digital world: it gave legal recognition to electronic records and signatures — so digital documents and e-commerce could stand in law at all — and it made computer crimes punishable: hacking into systems, damaging or stealing data, and publishing false or harmful material through electronic media. Every later Bangladeshi cyber law builds on this foundation: the digital act, done on a computer, judged by a real court.
The **Digital Security Act 2018** widened the frame: digital fraud and forgery, identity theft, defamation and harmful content online, and attacks on the country's digital security — with a Digital Security Agency to match. Beside it, the **Pornography Control Act 2012** criminalises making, carrying and spreading pornographic material, including through phones and the internet. The lesson the book draws is the citizen's one: online is not outside the law — the same honesty, and sharper care, is owed there.
