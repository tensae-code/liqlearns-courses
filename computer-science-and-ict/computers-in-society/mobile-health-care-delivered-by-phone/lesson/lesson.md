# Mobile health — care delivered by phone

## Do it first

A nurse in a rural clinic photographs a record, messages a doctor in the city, and checks a patient's readings — all before breakfast, all on one phone. The teacher guide gives this a name and a full working picture. This lesson teaches it — and says honestly, here and in its sources, that it comes from the guide alone.

## Sub lesson 1 — What mobile health is (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide's answer (teacher guide — evidence) defines **mobile health as using technological tools to track health, collect health data and deliver care remotely**, so **health and wellness programmes can be run outside the clinic by nurse leaders, doctors and patients**.

The clinic stops being the only place care happens: the phone carries the record, the message and the consultation to where the patient actually is.

## Sub lesson 2 — What the medical apps do (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide lists what **the fast-growing stock of medical apps** now does for health professionals: **managing information and time, keeping and accessing health records, communicating with patients, consulting other professionals, gathering references, managing and monitoring patients, making clinical decisions, and learning and practising medicine**.

And it sends pupils out with a research question of its own: **assess which medical apps and services actually exist in Ethiopia**. This lesson keeps that question open — answering it is the hands-on work below.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the definition, five of the eight uses, and the guide's open question about Ethiopia — then your first step toward answering it.

## Do It Yourself

1. Begin the guide's assessment: list every medical app or phone-based health service you can verify exists in Ethiopia, with one line of evidence each.
2. Map one clinic visit you know onto the phone: which parts could the guide's tools have carried remotely, and which truly needed the room?
