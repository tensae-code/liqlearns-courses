# Lesson plan — Bibliographic Managers for Citations and References

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Computers in Society · **Lesson:** Bibliographic Managers for Citations and References
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Cuba (2026-11-14) — in the title Computers in Society on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Cuba's Mi Mundo Digital / Informática student books and teacher-side books (Ministerio de Educación / Editorial Pueblo y Educación), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A **bibliographic manager** (gestor bibliográfico) is a program that **stores your sources** — author, title, year, place — and then **inserts citations** in your text and **builds the reference list** at the end, all in the style the work requires.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Citations — giving every source its name** — Scientific work stands on other people's work, and honesty names it: a **citation** inside the text points to the source an idea or fact came from, and the **reference list** at the end gives every source in full — **author, title, year, publisher or journal**. Done by hand across a long work, citations drift: a year mistyped, a source used and never listed, a style that changes halfway. The need is not care — it is a tool that keeps the record once and reuses it exactly.
2. **Bibliographic managers — store once, cite everywhere** — A **bibliographic manager** keeps a **library of your sources**, each entered or captured once with all its parts. While you write, it **inserts the citation** at the point you choose, numbered or named as the required **style** demands; at the end it **builds the whole reference list** from the citations actually used — nothing cited missing, nothing listed unused. Change the style and the manager **re-forms every citation** to match. Cuba closes its school ICT with this tool because university work begins with it.
