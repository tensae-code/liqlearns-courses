# E-commerce — buying and selling online

## Do it first

You tap 'buy' on a phone. Days later a parcel knocks. Between the tap and the knock, your order visited people you never saw. This lesson follows it, step by step.

## Sub lesson 1 — How a transaction flows

**E-commerce** is **buying and selling goods and services over the internet**. Follow one order:

1. The **buyer orders on a website** by phone or computer.
2. The **merchant receives the order**.
3. **Payment is processed through the bank**.
4. The **goods are delivered**.

Four actors carry it: **merchants, buyers, banks and transporters**.

## Sub lesson 2 — E-commerce in Ethiopia — gains and limits

In **Ethiopia** such businesses are **growing** because **buyers can browse and compare prices** and **sellers need no physical shop** — though the book notes the **supporting infrastructure is still young**.

**Advantages:** **cutting out intermediaries**, **convenience**, **stronger buyer bargaining power** and **global reach** — held honestly beside its shadow, **global competition**: the same door that lets a seller reach the world lets the world reach the seller's customers.

## Say it to your companion

Walk one order for your companion through the four actors, in order. Then give Ethiopia's two growth reasons and two advantages.

## Do It Yourself

1. Follow a real or imagined purchase: write each step and name the actor at each step. Where could it fail? Name the actor who must fix it.
2. Compare one product's price in two online places and one physical shop. Write what browsing and comparing did to your bargaining power.
3. List what a seller saves by needing no physical shop — and what young infrastructure could still cost them (delays, payments, delivery).
