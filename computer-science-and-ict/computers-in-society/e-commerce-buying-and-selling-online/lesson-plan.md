# Lesson plan — E-commerce — buying and selling online

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Computers in Society · **Lesson:** E-commerce — buying and selling online
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 14 — Computers in Society of the Computer Science and ICT ladder (TITLES.md); Ethiopia Grade 11, Unit 1 — Information Systems and Its Applications. The ladder is the recommended order, never a forced one.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** e-commerce is buying and selling over the internet — an order flows buyer, merchant, bank, transporter, door — cutting out intermediaries and reaching the world.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the e-banking lesson (recommended first, never forced): the payment step inside this flow is e-banking at work.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds, looping.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **How a transaction flows** — the four actors and the order's journey from click to door.
2. **E-commerce in Ethiopia — gains and limits** — why such businesses grow in Ethiopia, and the advantages: no intermediaries, convenience, bargaining power, global reach.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the lesson named in this plan's level line, inside this same title. Recommended, never forced. A learner can land here directly; the lesson stands alone.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Trace an order through buyer, merchant, bank, transporter.
2. Give Ethiopia's two growth reasons and the young-infrastructure note.
3. Name the four advantages, with global competition beside global reach.
