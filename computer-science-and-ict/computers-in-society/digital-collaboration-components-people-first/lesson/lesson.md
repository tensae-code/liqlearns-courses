# Digital collaboration components — people first

## Do it first

A group buys the best tools, shares the folder, schedules the call — and still produces the same paragraph three times, written by three people who never spoke. The missing component was not software. This lesson starts where the book starts: people.

## Sub lesson 1 — People at the heart

**People are at the heart of collaboration**: the first component is not a tool but the team itself.

**Workflows and established practices keep them in touch** — especially when working remotely, **by schedule preference or under restrictions such as COVID-19**. The practice, agreed in advance, is what carries a team when the room disappears.

[Picture 29736 — a team linked by workflow lines, with a duplicate-task icon crossed out]

## Sub lesson 2 — Arrangement removes duplicated work

**Proper arrangement and communication on shared projects removes unnecessary and duplicated work** — the paragraph written three times is an arrangement failure, not an effort failure.

The people's component is therefore practical: who does what, said early, checked often. Collaboration fails socially long before it fails technically.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion why the book puts people first, what workflows do for a remote team, and the cost of poor arrangement.

## Do It Yourself

1. Take your last group task. List any duplicated or unnecessary work it contained and the arrangement that would have prevented each item.
2. Write the workflow for a two-person remote task: hand-offs, check-ins, and the moment both call it done.
