## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Transparency — the service in the open** — **Transparency** means the service's facts are public before anyone asks: what it gives, what it costs, how long it takes, what papers it needs, and who decides. Hidden facts are power over citizens; published facts are power for them — a citizen who knows the real fee and the real waiting time cannot be charged a private one. Digital technology serves transparency directly: the same facts on a website or noticeboard reach everyone at once and cannot be quietly changed for one visitor.
2. **Accountability — and the guidelines that bind them** — **Accountability** is transparency with a name attached: every decision has a decider, every complaint a road and a deadline, every taka a record. The Grade 9 session has teams **write the guidelines** for a real service: publish the charter of fees and times; record each application and its decider; give the citizen a receipt and a complaint number; review the records in the open. Guidelines are promises written before they are tested — the service binds itself in advance, which is the only binding a citizen can rely on.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — A service hides its fee, and the citizen pays whatever is asked. A service publishes its fee, and the asking ends. Grade 9's book turns that difference into a project: write the guidelines that keep a citizen service transparent — and its officials accountable — by design, not by luck. (lesson/lesson.md).
