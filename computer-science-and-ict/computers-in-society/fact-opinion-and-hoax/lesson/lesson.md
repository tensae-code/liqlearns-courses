# Fact, Opinion and Hoax

## Do it first

Fact, opinion and hoax — telling a checkable truth from a person's judgement, and both from a deliberate lie made to be believed and spread

## Sub lesson 1 — Fact and opinion — the checkable and the judged

A **fact** is a statement that can be **checked** against the world: it happened or it did not, it measures so or it does not. An **opinion** is a person's **judgement** — what they think, prefer or conclude — which can be wise or foolish but is not settled by checking alone. Indonesia's Grade 7 literacy chapter teaches the pair together because mixed writing hides one inside the other: a report earns trust by keeping its facts checkable and its opinions owned — 'I think' is the honest uniform of an opinion.

In your mind's eye: fact and opinion — the checkable and the judged.

## Sub lesson 2 — Hoax — the lie built to travel

A **hoax** (hoaks) is neither: a statement **made to deceive**, built to be believed and forwarded — dressed as fact, sweetened with emotion, and aimed at a crowd. Its marks are the opposite of a credible source's: no accountable maker, no inspectable evidence, and pressure to share before thinking. The defence is the course's whole literacy kit used at the moment of arrival: pause, name what kind of statement it is, check it against a source whose business is truth, and let a hoax die unforwarded — a chain broken by one careful reader.
