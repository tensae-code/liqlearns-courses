## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The licences — permission granted in advance** — A **Creative Commons (CC) licence** is a standard permission a creator attaches to a work — a picture, a song, a text — so anyone may use it **as the licence says**, without asking each time. The licence is built from conditions: **attribution** (credit the maker) always; and, as the maker chooses, no commercial use, no changed versions, or share-alike — copies and adaptations must carry the same licence.
2. **Using CC work — credit and obey the terms** — To use a CC work lawfully, the user **reads its licence, follows its conditions, and gives the credit it asks** — the maker's name and the licence named with the work. CC is how the internet's honest sharing works: Bhutan's pupils, who learn citation and copyright in the same unit, meet CC as the bridge — work shared on purpose, used with gratitude, never mistaken for ownerless.
