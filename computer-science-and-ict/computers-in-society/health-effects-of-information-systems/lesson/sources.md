Book errors touching Unit 1, taught corrected and named here: the student book numbers "Brainstorming 1.7" twice (for §1.4, printed p. 18, and again for §1.5, printed p. 22); the teacher guide's §1.2 competency reads "Define Intellectual property (IR)" for IP, and the guide's Discussion strategy is copy-pasted as discussing "Digital Identity Management issues" in §§1.2, 1.4 and 1.5 whatever the section. The lessons teach the topics, not the copy faults. Where the guide's keyed answers correct or extend the student text, they are filed as labelled teacher evidence and say so in the table above.

Iraq's own way (Grade 7): a four-unit spiral repeated at every grade — computer fundamentals, computer software (here: the operating system plus an unusually deep treatment of Windows right-click shortcut menus), algorithms and programming principles (ending in Scratch), and information technology and the internet — every lesson built on the Iraqi five-step learning cycle with opening activity, main idea, learning outcomes, vocabulary and lesson review.

Bhutan's own way (Class VI, Literacy with ICT student book): School computing runs on Google's G Suite for Education — email is Gmail on the school account, collaboration is Google Classroom, and the end-of-year food-chain project is agreed in Classroom, built in Word, password-protected, cited, filed in folders and reported to the teacher by email.

Greece's own way (Grade 7, Pliroforiki A' Gymnasiou, 2024): the new Gymnasium informatics course is built around algorithms from the first page (a Python binary example in Unit 1), then hardware/software, the internet with a full copyright/Creative Commons/AI-copyright sequence, cybersecurity with cryptography, word processing, algorithmics, Scratch storytelling/chatbot projects, and a long society unit (robots, drones, autonomous cars, e-government, AI in education/medicine).

Bangladesh's own way (HSC ICT, Classes XI–XII and Alim): one compulsory ICT book for every HSC and Alim student in the country, saturated with Bangladesh itself: Bangabandhu Satellite-1 and its ground stations, bdjobs and chakri.com job portals, Daraz and Bikroy e-commerce, the Walton factory's automated production line, the National ID card used to teach primary keys and its database used to teach RDBMS at work, a full Bangla Unicode table and a worked example of putting Bangla text on a web page with meta charset, the national letter-grade scale (A+ from 80) as the else-if programme, "I Love Bangladesh" as the loop example text, an algorithm for reaching college by bicycle or rickshaw, and the Digital Bangladesh programme — National Data Centre, hi-tech parks, Startup Bangladesh and Bangladesh Bank's QR-based Cashless Bangladesh. C is the HSC programming language, taught with scanf/printf in Code::Blocks.


Pakistan's own way (Grade 6 Computer Science 6): every unit opens with Students Learning Outcomes, is dotted with "Do you Know?", "Extra Bit!" and "Tips" boxes, and closes with Summary, MCQ/short/detailed exercises, project-based and activity-based questions, with an answers key at the back. Digital skills (Windows desktop, file management, Paint 3D, a typing tutor) are taught hands-on BEFORE programming; digital citizenship is framed through Pakistani citizenship first; the entrepreneurship unit teaches from Pakistani founders (Foodpanda/EatOye, Shopsy.pk, Zero Makeup, Investors Lounge).

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G8 Informatika, Lazarus edition): Grade 8 by the Almatykitap team measures information two ways (the probabilistic and alphabetic approaches, with Hartley-formula problems), works Excel hard (absolute/relative references, built-in functions, data analysis, applied problems), and teaches programming in the Lazarus IDE — case, for, nested, repeat-until and while operators, tracing, and procedures and functions — before a final part that runs the whole problem-solving cycle from statement to tested program.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G8 Informatika, Python edition): the newer, shorter Grade 8 edition from the same team keeps the measurement, health and Excel parts but replaces the Lazarus IDE with Python: for and while loops, nested loops, continue, break and loop-else, algorithm tracing, and a closing part on practical programming that ends with olympiad-style tasks.

Kazakhstan's own way (Salgaraeva G8 Informatika, 2018 edition): the Arman-PV Grade 8 book runs the shared national frame in 34 numbered paragraphs with a test after every part: two paragraphs split the processor from its characteristics, the Excel part goes furthest of the three publishers (data formats, text and logical functions, plotting and restyling function graphs), and the Lazarus part adds algorithm tracing and a practicum on typical programming errors.

Kazakhstan's own way (Shaniev G9 Informatics): the bilingual Grade 9 book (taught in English for the trilingual programme) opens with computer literacy including netiquette and pricing a computer configuration, teaches databases practically (design, build, sort/filter, pivot tables, charts, a "family shop" mini-project), then Python lists in depth — and finishes with a whole chapter of 2D game programming in PyGame.

Kazakhstan's own way (Salgaraeva G8 Informatika, updated edition): the updated-content revision of the Arman-PV Grade 8 book keeps the measurement, health and Excel parts of the 2018 edition (with a fuller glossary — digital reputation, worms, DoS/DDoS attacks, the Hartley formula) but replaces the Lazarus part with Python loops (while, for, continue, break, loop-else, tracing, typical errors) and ends with practical programming.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukhametzhanova G8 Informatika): the Atamura Grade 8 book is the leanest of the three publishers — five parts only, tasks ranked by Bloom's taxonomy, and a rich glossary that reaches from pivot tables, sparklines and data consolidation in Excel to botnets, phishing, cyberbullying and griefing in the safety part; Python loops include loop-else and trace tables.

Kazakhstan's own way (Adekenova & Pryanichnikova G9 Informatics, NIS): the NIS Grade 9 course is built around databases and object-oriented programming: computer architecture with logic circuits, coding raster images and sound, single-table then multi-table databases in Access (forms, queries, reports), five lessons introducing OOP, nine lessons developing applications in an OOP environment (C#), and a thirteen-lesson capstone — choosing a problem, designing the interface, programming, making a video instruction, testing, documenting and evaluating the project.

Kazakhstan's own way (Yerzhanov G9 Informatics, English-medium): the English-medium twin of the Mektep Grade 9 book — the same four chapters (computer literacy with netiquette and pricing a computer, database basics with pivot tables and a "family shop" mini-project, Python lists, PyGame 2D games ending in a Program Arcade Game) taught in English with trilingual terminology.

Kazakhstan's own way (Zhunusov G8 Informatics, bilingual edition): the bilingual Grade 8 book follows the updated national frame (measurement and coding, processor, networks and bandwidth; health; Excel statistics; Python while/for with break/continue/else and tracing) and ends its practical-programming part with a four-part worked game project, "Akkala".

Kazakhstan's own way (Zhunusov G8 Informatics, English-medium): the English-medium twin of the bilingual Grade 8 book (Book 40) — same five chapters; the practical-programming game project is the "Melting Snowman" game in four parts.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukametzhanova G8 «Информатика»): a «spiral» course (the preface says so): Grade 7's C++ integrated development environment is continued and deepened — classification of software, IDE components, switch, the three loop forms and algorithm tracing — alongside spreadsheet statistics and applied problem solving, and a full problem-solving cycle (define → algorithm → programme → test) as the closing section.

Kazakhstan's own way (Salgaraeva G9 «Информатика»): Grade 9 in the Arman-PV line is a maker's year: work with information (netiquette, shared documents), choosing and costing a real computer, databases built first in spreadsheets, then Python arrays, and a full PyGame 2D game — background, characters, sprite animation, keyboard control — built from a ready scenario.

Kazakhstan's own way (Salgaraeva G8 «Информатика»): the Arman-PV Grade 8 book follows the same five-section state programme as the Atamura book (Book 11) but stretches it: a separate unit on processor characteristics, a practicum on modern cyber-threats, text and logical spreadsheet functions, plotting mathematical functions in a spreadsheet and changing the chart's properties, and a practicum on typical programming errors inside the C++ IDE sections.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G8 «Информатика» 2019 edition): the 2019 Almatykitap Grade 8 book splits "measuring information" into two named approaches — the probability approach and the alphabet approach — and its IDE section is a Pascal/Lazarus visual-component environment, ending in subroutines; the 2023 re-issue (152 pages, Book 21) is a different, shorter book.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G8 «Информатика» 2023 edition): the 2023 re-issue keeps the 2019 book's first three sections but replaces the Pascal IDE programming with a Python loops course: for, nested loops, while, then a full unit each on continue, break and the loop else clause, closing with olympiad preparation tasks.

Kazakhstan's own way (Shaniyev team G9 «INFORMATICS»): the Astana-kitap Grade 9 English-medium book is where Kazakhstan teaches databases properly — design, build, sort/filter, pivot tables and charts, ending in a "family shop" mini project — and then spends two chapters on Python lists (one- and two-dimensional, with search/swap/sort/insert/delete) and a full PyGame 2D games chapter.

Kazakhstan's own way (Salgaraeva G8 «Информатика» 2021 edition): the 2021 Arman-PV revision of the Grade 8 book (Book 19 is the 2018 original) keeps sections 1–3 but replaces the C++ IDE section with Python loop control — while, for, continue, break and the loop else — plus tracing and a practicum on errors that arise in programming.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukametzhanova G8 «Информатика» 2021 edition): the 2021 Atamura revision of the Grade 8 book (Book 11 is the 2018 original) compresses the course — no separate software-classification or IDE units — and teaches Python loop control directly: while, for, break, continue, else and tracing, with an extra-tasks block closing each section.

Kazakhstan's own way (Atayeva/Vyushkova/Zhurbenko G8 «Информатика» NIS): the NIS Grade 8 book is the widest of the set: a whole section on vector graphics and geographic information systems (planning an excursion, making maps, visualising data in GIS), octal and hexadecimal coding, loops applied separately to mathematics, physics, business and animation, strings and subroutines, and a nine-step project method ending in a mini-project on artificial intelligence.

Kazakhstan's own way (Pryanichnikova team G9 «Информатика» NIS): the NIS Grade 9 book is a step up in level: logic expressions, truth tables and logic circuits inside "computer architecture"; sound encoding beside raster encoding; single-table then multi-table databases in Access with forms, queries and reports; and a full object-oriented programming course in C# — components of OOP, multimedia applications, and a software-solution project with a video instruction and a slide show.
