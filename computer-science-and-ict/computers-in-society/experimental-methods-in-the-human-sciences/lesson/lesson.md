# Experimental Methods in the Human Sciences

## Do it first

Grade 12's electives end the course looking outward: the same informatics, serving the sciences of people. The experiment — natural science's sharpest tool — changes its shape when its material can refuse, misunderstand and be harmed.

## Sub lesson 1 — The experiment on people — variables and control

A **human-sciences experiment** still follows the classic design: an **independent variable** is changed deliberately (a teaching method, a question's wording), a **dependent variable** is measured (marks, choices, times), and **other conditions are held equal** — above all by a **control group** that receives everything except the change, so the difference in results can be **credited to the variable** and not to the day, the teacher or chance. Questionnaires, observation and tests are its instruments; samples stand for populations by the same care statistics demands everywhere.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ethics and the computer — the human material

People are not pendulums: the elective's second half is **limits**. Participants must **consent** and may **withdraw**; deception and harm are bounded by ethics rules stricter than any in the physics lab; results about persons are **private data**, protected in storage and publication. The computer serves as the processor: coding the questionnaires, computing the tables and averages, drawing the comparisons — **the same database and list crafts of this course**, now under an ethical discipline that is the real syllabus: in the human sciences, method includes how people may be treated.
