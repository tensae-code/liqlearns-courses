# Ethical Dilemmas from Using AI

## Do it first

Rules say what is forbidden. Dilemmas are harder: the cases where two good things collide and any choice loses one of them. Grade 9's AI unit is built around those collisions.

## Sub lesson 1 — Help against honesty — the learner's dilemma

The unit's first dilemma is the school's own: an AI that **does the learner's work** gives **help and harm in one act** — the essay exists, the learning does not. Around it stand its cousins: the answer that **sounds certain and is wrong** (trust it or check it — and what does checking cost the time it saved?), and the work **presented as one's own** that a machine largely made. The book's method is not a verdict list: it gives the **questions a dilemma is judged by** — who gains, who is harmed, what is hidden, what would I defend if it were known?

## Sub lesson 2 — Safety against privacy — and who answers

The second family is society's: systems that **watch to protect** (the camera that finds the lost child also follows everyone), **decisions made by systems no one fully explains** (the loan refused by a score), and **bias learned from the world's data** (the machine repeating its teachers' unfairness at scale). Over them all stands the unit's closing question — **who answers** when an AI's act harms: the user, the maker, the owner, the machine? The book leaves the learner with the principle the AI-ethics lessons also teach: 'the AI did it' answers nothing — a person or an organisation always stands behind the deployment, and ethics is naming them before the harm, not after.
