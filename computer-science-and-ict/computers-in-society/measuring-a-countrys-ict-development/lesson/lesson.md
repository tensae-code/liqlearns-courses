# Measuring a Country's ICT Development

## Do it first

Grade 12 opens by measuring the world. Mongolia's final book teaches that 'ICT development' is not a feeling but an index — built by the ITU from named parts — and that a citizen should be able to read the measuring as critically as the measured.

## Sub lesson 1 — The IDI — access, use, skills

The **ICT Development Index (IDI)** is the **ITU's** measure of a country's information society, built from three baskets. **Access**: can people reach ICT — subscriptions, coverage, bandwidth, households with computers and internet. **Use**: do they actually use it — internet users, fixed and mobile broadband at work. **Skills**: can they use it well — schooling and literacy measures standing for capability. One number ranks the country, but the three baskets are the reading: two countries with the same index can be strong in opposite baskets, and the remedy for each is opposite too.

## Sub lesson 2 — The standings — and reading them like a citizen

The book's snapshot is **2017**: **Iceland, South Korea and Switzerland** stand on top of the index, and the world's internet users number **4.156 billion** — passed at last beyond half of humanity. Mongolia teaches the table as a citizen reads it: the leaders show what the baskets reward (small rich states and one giant of broadband discipline alike), the world's user count marks the revolution's true scale, and every rank is a year-stamped photograph, not a destiny — the next lesson turns the same instrument on Mongolia itself, whose slipping rank is the book's spur to its pupils.
