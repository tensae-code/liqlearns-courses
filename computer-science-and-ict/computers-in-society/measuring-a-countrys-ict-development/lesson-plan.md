## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The IDI — access, use, skills** — The **ICT Development Index (IDI)** is the **ITU's** measure of a country's information society, built from three baskets. **Access**: can people reach ICT — subscriptions, coverage, bandwidth, households with computers and internet. **Use**: do they actually use it — internet users, fixed and mobile broadband at work. **Skills**: can they use it well — schooling and literacy measures standing for capability. One number ranks the country, but the three baskets are the reading: two countries with the same index can be strong in opposite baskets, and the remedy for each is opposite too.
2. **The standings — and reading them like a citizen** — The book's snapshot is **2017**: **Iceland, South Korea and Switzerland** stand on top of the index, and the world's internet users number **4.156 billion** — passed at last beyond half of humanity. Mongolia teaches the table as a citizen reads it: the leaders show what the baskets reward (small rich states and one giant of broadband discipline alike), the world's user count marks the revolution's true scale, and every rank is a year-stamped photograph, not a destiny — the next lesson turns the same instrument on Mongolia itself, whose slipping rank is the book's spur to its pupils.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
