# Lesson plan — ICT Use in Rwanda

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Computers in Society · **Lesson:** ICT Use in Rwanda
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Rwanda (2026-11-14) — in the title Computers in Society on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Rwanda student books (Kenya Literature Bureau / Fountain Publishers Rwanda, approved by the Rwanda Education Board), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree and live lesson content were checked for the slug and for near matches before building.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** How ICT is actually used in Rwanda today — in schools, health, farming, money and government — as Rwanda's own S1 book reports it
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **ICT in Rwandan daily life** — Rwanda's book grounds ICT in the learner's own country first. **ICT in Rwanda** runs through **daily life**: phones carry **money and messages**, computers keep **school and office records**, and **radios, television and the internet** carry news and learning. The point of the unit is recognition — the learner lists the ICT already around them before any new machine is named. Rwanda's own way starts from home ground: the technology is not foreign equipment, it is the tools Rwandans already use every day.
2. **ICT in services — money, health, farming and government** — The book's survey widens to **services**: **mobile money and banking** move funds without a branch visit; **health services** keep patient records and link clinics; **farmers** get prices and weather by phone; and **government services** reach citizens through computer systems instead of paper queues. Each field shows the same pattern — information that used to travel by foot and paper now travels **by signal**, faster and recorded as it goes.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Before any definition: name one place in Rwandan daily life where a computer or a phone already does a job that paper used to do. Rwanda's S1 book opens its ICT course exactly there — with the current use of ICT in Rwanda itself. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of ict in rwandan daily life as the lesson describes it — the student studies the picture and names what each part shows.
   - ADD GIF: a GIF of ict in services — money, health, farming and government in action — the student watches once, then says what changed and why.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the lesson's key names and definitions aloud, in the order the lesson gives them — the student listens once through, then reads the same lines aloud along with the voice. (Audio is a described slot in this build; no audio file is made.)
3. **Say it to your companion** — the companion block in lesson/lesson.md.
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the lesson in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-false, matching, short-answer — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the doing block at the end of lesson/lesson.md.
