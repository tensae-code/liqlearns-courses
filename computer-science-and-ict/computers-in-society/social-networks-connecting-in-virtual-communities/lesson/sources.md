**Acceptance count: taught in 13 countries (Ethiopia, Cambodia, Belarus, Cuba, Fiji, Bhutan, Libya, Mongolia, Rwanda, Greece, Bangladesh, Indonesia, Vietnam).**

Belarus's own way (Grade 10): social networks are taught inside one chapter-section with blogs, forums and chats (§ 20) — the virtual-community forms are compared together, not taken one tool at a time.

Nothing in this lesson claims a page the placements do not give; the one Tip box is LiqLearns' own, marked as such in the lesson and in the table above.

Cuba's own way (Grade 12): The final pre-university year is networks in depth (types, topologies, protocols, Internet services, Web 2.0/Web 3.0 and social networks), then teleformación (distance learning) through the Moodle platform, and bibliographic managers for citations in scientific research — a university-preparation finish.

Fiji's own way (Year 12 Computer Studies student book): Written locally by the same TEST/USP panel for the trialled Year 12 syllabus; the Internet chapter is grounded in Fiji (fiji.gov.fj URL anatomy with the .fj country code, and Fiji ISPs Connect Fiji, Unwired Fiji, Vodafone, Digicel and Kidanet by Fintel), programming is a full Visual Basic course with hands-on examples, and data management is taught as a move to cloud storage (Google Drive, Dropbox, SpiderOak).

Bhutan's own way (Class VII, Literacy with ICT student book): Social media is taught hands-on — learners create a Facebook account, post with audience settings, and follow netiquette and an Acceptable Use Policy; backup is taught to Google Drive, and the end-of-year experience is shared on Facebook.

Libya's own way (Grade 9, Computer student book): the whole year is networks and the internet — a full networks unit (types, cables, topology, components), then Internet Explorer and Google search taught as named skills, then email and a closing tour of social sites (Facebook, YouTube, Twitter, Instagram, Yahoo) with practical activities.

Mongolia's own way (Grade 8): Grade 8 is built around two long projects — a folded leaflet on Ulaanbaatar's air pollution (Word templates, styles, WordArt, charts and SmartArt) that then becomes a PowerPoint presentation with its own master slide and hyperlink buttons, and a Paint.NET collage of the five-snout livestock (таван хошуу мал) in a steppe landscape. Models are taught on Mongolian material (the livestock, Rutherford's atom, a virus-growth formula); spreadsheets open with the chessboard-and-wheat legend and count Mongolia's real 2014 livestock by aimag; social networks are taught on Facebook and Twitter ("жиргээ") with Mongolian examples.

Rwanda's own way (S2 ICT Teacher Guide, KLB): S2 moves to data protection, ICT in finance (including Rwandan mobile banking risks and biometrics), spreadsheets in depth, a second ArcGIS unit, and Scratch game design with sprite interaction tables and score/level rules. (teacher guide — evidence)

Rwanda's own way (S2 ICT Student Book, KLB): the student companion to the S2 guide — the same nine units, taught through Rwandan financial life (mobile banking, ATMs, stock exchange) and Scratch game projects (Helicopter Game, Moving Eyes Game).

Greece's own way (Grade 7, Pliroforiki A' Gymnasiou, 2024): the new Gymnasium informatics course is built around algorithms from the first page (a Python binary example in Unit 1), then hardware/software, the internet with a full copyright/Creative Commons/AI-copyright sequence, cybersecurity with cryptography, word processing, algorithmics, Scratch storytelling/chatbot projects, and a long society unit (robots, drones, autonomous cars, e-government, AI in education/medicine).

Greece's own way (Grade 10, Efarmoges Pliroforikis A' Lykeiou): the older Lykeio applications book in four thematic units — hardware/software and applications, programming environments and creating applications (life cycle, modern IDEs, mobile-app and 3D object-oriented programming), communication and the internet (HTML5/CSS, learning online), and collaboration and safety online (cloud applications, telework, social networks, privacy).

Switzerland's own way (Grades 7–8, Décodage 7e–8e): at 7e–8e the Décodage course becomes a real computer-science course under the Plan d'études romand EN 22/23: Thymio VPL missions (including Morse communication and self-driving-car autonomy levels), a full Scratch discovery course (movement, shortening programs, sub-programs as own blocks, remixing), a Scratch video-game project run in “studios” with a cahier des charges, data work (Huffman-style compression via Zip la marmotte, binary/octal/hex/ASCII codings, file sizes), access security (passwords, file encryption, phishing, authentication), home networks at two levels (IP addresses; message routing, packets, TCP and encryption), and inquiries into sorting, sorting networks, Caesar decryption and parity-bit error detection. Médias/Usages covers social networks and digital identity/footprint, information sources, cyberbullying and the environmental cost of digital technology.

Bangladesh's own way (HSC ICT, Classes XI–XII and Alim): one compulsory ICT book for every HSC and Alim student in the country, saturated with Bangladesh itself: Bangabandhu Satellite-1 and its ground stations, bdjobs and chakri.com job portals, Daraz and Bikroy e-commerce, the Walton factory's automated production line, the National ID card used to teach primary keys and its database used to teach RDBMS at work, a full Bangla Unicode table and a worked example of putting Bangla text on a web page with meta charset, the national letter-grade scale (A+ from 80) as the else-if programme, "I Love Bangladesh" as the loop example text, an algorithm for reaching college by bicycle or rickshaw, and the Digital Bangladesh programme — National Data Centre, hi-tech parks, Startup Bangladesh and Bangladesh Bank's QR-based Cashless Bangladesh. C is the HSC programming language, taught with scanf/printf in Code::Blocks.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 7 G7 Informatika student): Kurikulum Merdeka Informatika built on four elements (Berpikir Komputasional, Literasi Digital, Analisis Data, Algoritma dan Pemrograman), mixing plugged and unplugged activities with coded activity IDs (e.g. VII-BK, VII-LD) and QR-linked resources; Grade 7 covers only BK + Literasi Digital + data handling.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 8 G8 Informatika student): Grade 8 deepens the four elements: spreadsheet data analysis (lookup, pivot), computational thinking through functions, sets and number systems, and programming that moves from Scratch to Blockly Games, procedural blocks and a physical Ozobot robot.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 12 G12 Informatika student): Grade 12 (original Kurikulum Merdeka edition) is built around making things: single-board computers/controllers and a full Arduino C course (IDE, UnoArduSim simulator, sensors, S.O.S. LED and melody projects), then networks with cyber security and data governance, social impact with the UU ITE law, and a cross-field group project (smart trash bin / waste-bank apps) with journals, reports and peer assessment.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 12 G12 Informatika teacher guide): Panduan Umum explains the SMA informatics curriculum, plugged and unplugged modes, and Fase F learning outcomes; each chapter guide gives strategies, lesson organisation, meaningful-learning design, enrichment/remedial, rubrics and parent interaction. (teacher guide — evidence)

Vietnam's own way (Grade 7 Tin học 7): Grade 7 is the spreadsheet year (five Excel lessons in a row, in Windows/Excel/PowerPoint named openly) and its computer-science strand is pure algorithms with no code at all — sequential search, binary search and sorting acted out and flowcharted before any language appears.
