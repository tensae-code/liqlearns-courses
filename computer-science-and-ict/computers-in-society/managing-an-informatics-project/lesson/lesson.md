# Managing an Informatics Project

## Do it first

Managing an informatics project — planning the work, the people, the time and the resources so a computing project finishes as intended

## Sub lesson 1 — The plan — what, who, when, with what

An informatics **project** is a bounded piece of computing work with a goal, and **project management** (manajemen proyek informatika) is what keeps the bound real. The SMK book's management chapter opens with the plan's four questions: **what** exactly will be made (the scope, written down, with what is NOT included), **who** does each part (roles named, not assumed), **when** each part lands (a schedule with milestones), and **with what** (the devices, software, money and access the work needs). A project without a written scope does not have a finish line — only a stopping place.

In your mind's eye: the plan — what, who, when, with what.

## Sub lesson 2 — Running the plan — risks, changes and the record

Managing is what happens after planning: tracking the work against the schedule while the project is young enough to fix, naming **risks** early (the missing device, the one person who knows a part, the data that has not arrived) with a response for each, and handling **change** honestly — a change to scope is a change to time or resources, recorded and agreed, never silently absorbed. The project's documents are its memory and its proof. The chapter closes the SMK course because it is the skill that turns all the course's other skills into delivered work.

In your mind's eye: running the plan — risks, changes and the record.
