# Using Technology Ethically

## Do it first

Knowing the wrongs is half the lesson; Fiji's Years 7–8 ethics unit closes with the other half — the positive rules for using technology ethically, the habits of a person others can trust with information and machines. This lesson is those rules.

## Sub lesson 1 — The rules for information — respect, honesty, permission

Ethical use of **information** runs on three habits. **Respect ownership**: what another person made or holds is theirs — look with permission, copy by the rules, and **credit** what you use. **Be honest**: pass on only what you believe is true, and correct what you shared when it proves false. **Keep confidences**: information given to you privately stays private. Fiji states these as the working rules of the classroom's own information life — essays, searches, shared files — not as distant law.

## Sub lesson 2 — The rules for machines and people — care, fairness, repair

Ethical use of the **technology** is manners with machines: **care** — leave shared computers, accounts and networks as you found them, or better; **fairness** — take your turn, do not lock others out of what the whole class shares, and do not use the machine to gain an unfair edge in work that must be your own; and **repair** — when your use harms someone, say so and put it right. Fiji's closing picture of the ethical user is a person whose information and machine habits others never have to guard against.
