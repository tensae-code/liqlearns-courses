## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The rules for information — respect, honesty, permission** — Ethical use of **information** runs on three habits. **Respect ownership**: what another person made or holds is theirs — look with permission, copy by the rules, and **credit** what you use. **Be honest**: pass on only what you believe is true, and correct what you shared when it proves false. **Keep confidences**: information given to you privately stays private. Fiji states these as the working rules of the classroom's own information life — essays, searches, shared files — not as distant law.
2. **The rules for machines and people — care, fairness, repair** — Ethical use of the **technology** is manners with machines: **care** — leave shared computers, accounts and networks as you found them, or better; **fairness** — take your turn, do not lock others out of what the whole class shares, and do not use the machine to gain an unfair edge in work that must be your own; and **repair** — when your use harms someone, say so and put it right. Fiji's closing picture of the ethical user is a person whose information and machine habits others never have to guard against.
