**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Armenia (2026-11-14) — in the title Computers in Society on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). **The one thing this lesson teaches:** the digital divide — the gap between those who have access to digital technology and the skills to use it, and those who do not (Armenia Grade 10).
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The divide — access first** — The **digital divide** is the gap between people who can use digital technology and people who cannot. Its first level is **access**: a device, a connection, electricity, and prices a household can pay. The divide runs between countries, between city and village, between rich and poor households — and inside one classroom, between the learner with a quiet connected home and the learner without. Armenia's senior-school book gives the divide its own lesson (Grade 10, Theme 6) inside its society sequence.
2. **The deeper levels — skills, use and outcomes; closing the gap** — Access alone does not close the divide. The second level is **skills and use**: two people with the same connection differ if one can search, judge and create while the other can only consume. The third level is **outcomes**: who turns access into education, work and income. Closing the gap works at all three levels — public access points and affordable connections, digital-skills teaching for all ages, and services designed for low-end devices and slow connections, so nobody is locked out by the shape of their phone.
