## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Why the class talks about digital life** — Digital life raises questions no exercise can settle alone: May I share this photo? Why did that video upset me? Who may see what I post? The Décodage guides give these questions a standing place — a **class dialogue** (discussion philosophique) where pupils bring real moments, listen to each other, and think together, with the teacher keeping the talk safe and fair. The point is not one right answer; it is learning to reason about digital life out loud, together.
2. **How a good dialogue runs** — A good dialogue has a shape: one question at a time; speak from your own experience; listen fully before answering; disagree with the idea, never attack the person; and let the question grow — a good talk ends with better questions than it started with. Image rights and shocking content return often in these talks, because they are the moments young pupils actually meet: a photo of someone else, a picture that frightens, a share that cannot be taken back.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Think of one digital moment that puzzled or bothered you — a strange picture, a message, a video you were not sure about. You are not alone with it. This lesson is the class sitting down to talk about exactly those moments, together. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of why the class talks about digital life as the lesson describes it — the student studies the picture and names what each part shows.
   - ADD GIF: a GIF of how a good dialogue runs in action — the student watches once, then says what changed and why.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the lesson's key names and definitions aloud, in the order the lesson gives them — the student listens once through, then reads the same lines aloud along with the voice. (Audio is a described slot in this build; no audio file is made.)
3. **Say it to your companion** — the companion block in lesson/lesson.md.
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the lesson in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-false, matching, short-answer — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the doing block at the end of lesson/lesson.md.
