# Video script — Information Processing and Decision Making

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

A **decision** is a choice between actions — admit this pupil or not, order more stock or wait, build the road here or there. Every real decision is only as good as the **information** under it: wrong numbers make wrong choices, late information makes late ones, and missing information makes guesses. Fiji's chain is the lesson: data is **processed** into information precisely so that a decision-maker receives it **in the form a decision needs** — summarised, compared, on time.
Processing serves the decision when it does three things. It **selects** — from all the data, the part this decision turns on. It **shapes** — totals, averages, comparisons and trends, the forms a decider can weigh at a glance. And it **arrives in time** — information after the decision is history, not help. Fiji's Year 11 reader meets this in offices they know: the principal deciding class sizes from enrolment summaries, the shopkeeper deciding orders from sales totals — processing exists because deciding does.
