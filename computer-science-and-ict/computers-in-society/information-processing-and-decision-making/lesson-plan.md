## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Decisions eat information** — A **decision** is a choice between actions — admit this pupil or not, order more stock or wait, build the road here or there. Every real decision is only as good as the **information** under it: wrong numbers make wrong choices, late information makes late ones, and missing information makes guesses. Fiji's chain is the lesson: data is **processed** into information precisely so that a decision-maker receives it **in the form a decision needs** — summarised, compared, on time.
2. **Processing shaped for the decider** — Processing serves the decision when it does three things. It **selects** — from all the data, the part this decision turns on. It **shapes** — totals, averages, comparisons and trends, the forms a decider can weigh at a glance. And it **arrives in time** — information after the decision is history, not help. Fiji's Year 11 reader meets this in offices they know: the principal deciding class sizes from enrolment summaries, the shopkeeper deciding orders from sales totals — processing exists because deciding does.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Why does an office process information at all? Fiji's Year 11 book answers with the decision: information is processed so that someone can decide — a manager, a principal, a minister — with facts instead of guesses. This lesson follows information from its processing to the decision it serves. (lesson/lesson.md).
