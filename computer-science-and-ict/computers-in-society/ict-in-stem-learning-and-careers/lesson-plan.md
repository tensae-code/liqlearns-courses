# Lesson plan — ICT in STEM Learning and Careers

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Computers in Society · **Lesson:** ICT in STEM Learning and Careers
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Mozambique (2026-11-14) — in the title Computers in Society on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Mozambique's *Módulo de Tecnologias de Informação e Comunicação (TIC) — Programa do Ensino Secundário à Distância, 1º Ciclo (PESD I)*, because no existing lesson teaches this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** STEM is Science, Technology, Engineering and Mathematics learning and working together — and ICT is the tool inside all four: it finds information, records and works on data, models and tests ideas safely on a screen, writes programs, and shares results — and those same tools are the daily tools of STEM careers.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the learner's own school subjects — science and mathematics are already on their timetable — and on the opening idea of what ICT is; the four STEM fields are named and built inside the lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **STEM — four fields that work together** — STEM is a short name for four fields that belong together.
2. **How ICT helps you learn STEM** — ICT is inside every part of STEM learning.
3. **STEM careers that use ICT** — The tools of STEM learning are also the tools of STEM work.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: What ICT Is in First Steps. Recommended, never forced — a learner may land on this lesson directly, and it stands alone.
- Mozambique's own way is kept: in the PESD I distance module this is Lição 4, placed early — right after the role of technology in contemporary society and before any hardware is named — so the learner meets the STEM context of ICT before the machine itself.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what STEM stands for, and name what each of its four fields does.
2. Name ways ICT helps learning in each STEM field — finding, recording, modelling, calculating and sharing.
3. Name STEM careers that use ICT, and say what the computer does in each.
4. Give one example from their own school work where ICT and a STEM subject meet.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil, and your own school timetable if you have one.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Look at your school timetable. Find Science and Mathematics. Now ask: where could a computer help in those lessons — finding a fact, doing a long calculation, drawing what an experiment would do? And what jobs, when you grow up, use a computer in that same way? Keep your answers — this lesson gives those four subjects one shared name, STEM, and shows ICT working inside all of them, in learning and in careers. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of four labelled panels — Science testing water, Technology as tools and a computer, Engineering designing a pump, Mathematics measuring and calculating — joined into one clean-water project — the student studies the picture and says, for each part of it, which part of the lesson it shows.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of a learner at a computer doing four STEM jobs — searching for a fact, a table of measurements turning into a chart, a simulation changing on screen, and work being sent to a teacher far away — the student studies the picture and says, for each part of it, which part of the lesson it shows.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of four STEM workers at their computers — a scientist with results, an engineer with a design, a programmer with code, and a health worker with patient information — the student studies the picture and says, for each part of it, which part of the lesson it shows.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the lesson's key names and definitions aloud, in the order the lesson gives them — the student listens once through, then reads the same lines aloud along with the voice. (Audio is a described slot in this build; no audio file is made.)

## If the learner is stuck
Thinks STEM is a fifth school subject → it is a name for four fields working together, not a new timetable lesson. Can name the fields but not the ICT inside them → take one field, say Science: first find a fact, then record a measurement, then chart it, then share it — four ICT jobs in one experiment. Thinks STEM careers are only for other people → start from their own timetable: the science and mathematics they already study, plus this ICT, are the road in.
