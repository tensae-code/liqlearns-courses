# Biometrics in Banking

## Do it first

A PIN can be told to another person; a card can be lent. A fingerprint cannot. Rwanda's S2 teacher guide adds biometrics to its banking unit — this lesson stands on that guide page.

## Sub lesson 1 — What biometrics is

**Biometrics** identifies a person by a **measurable part of their body or behaviour** — a **fingerprint**, the **face**, the **voice**, the pattern of the eye. The bank first **records** the customer's biometric once; at each later visit the live sample is **compared** with the record, and a match opens the account. What is checked is not something you carry or remember — it is **something you are**.

In your mind's eye: what biometrics is.

## Sub lesson 2 — Why banks add it

The guide places biometrics inside **financial transactions** because money needs certainty: a biometric is **hard to lend, steal or forget**, so it fights the frauds that cards and PINs invite — the lent card, the watched PIN, the forged signature. It also speeds the counter: one touch **proves the customer** without a document hunt. Rwanda's finance unit teaches it beside mobile banking's risks — the new lock taught next to the new door (teacher guide — evidence).

In your mind's eye: why banks add it.
