# Lesson plan — Problems of Bad Data Processing

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Computers in Society · **Lesson:** Problems of Bad Data Processing
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Indonesia (2026-11-14) — in the title Computers in Society on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Indonesia's Informatika books (Indonesia Grade 9 G9 Informatika student), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree and live lesson content were checked for the slug and for near matches before building.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** problems of bad data processing — what goes wrong when data is collected, kept or processed carelessly: wrong results, lost trust and real harm, and the care that prevents them (Indonesia Grade 9).
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **How processing goes wrong — faults at every stage** — Data processing fails at the stages it runs through. **Collection** faults: asking the wrong people, recording carelessly, losing part of the returns. **Keeping** faults: duplicates, contradictions, files nobody can find or read. **Processing** faults: the wrong tool for the data, a formula trusted without checking, results read as saying more than they do. Indonesia's Grade 9 chapter names these as the basic problems of data processing (permasalahan dasar pemrosesan data) because each stage's small fault becomes the next stage's false fact.
2. **What bad processing costs — and the care that prevents it** — The costs are real: decisions taken on wrong numbers, money and time spent twice, and — the slowest to heal — **trust** lost in every figure the office publishes afterwards. The prevention is the processing discipline the rest of the course teaches: validate at entry, keep one clean copy of the truth, check a result against a second road before publishing, and match the tool to the data's size and kind. Bad processing is rarely bad luck; it is skipped care, and its bill always arrives.
