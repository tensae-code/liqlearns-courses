# Assessing the Quality of Digital Artefacts

## Do it first

Anyone can make a digital thing. This lesson learns how to judge whether a made thing is good.

## Sub lesson 1 — What quality means for a digital artefact — purpose first

A **digital artefact** (computational work) is anything made with a computer: a document, a spreadsheet, a program, a presentation, a website. Its **quality** is judged first against its **purpose**: does it do the job it was made for, for the people it was made for? Armenia's Grade 6 book gives this judgement its own lesson (§8): a beautiful artefact that fails its purpose is low quality, and a plain one that serves its purpose well is high quality.

## Sub lesson 2 — The checks — correctness, clarity, craft and improvement

After purpose come the working checks: **correctness** (the content and calculations are right), **clarity** (a user can understand and use it without help), **craft** (layout, consistency, no rough edges that distract), and **fitness details** (file type, size, names in order). Assessing ends with **improvement**: name the artefact's two weakest points and the change that would raise each — an assessment that cannot say what to improve has not finished.
