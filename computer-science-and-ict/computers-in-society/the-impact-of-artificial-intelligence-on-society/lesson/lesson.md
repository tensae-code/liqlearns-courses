# The Impact of Artificial Intelligence on Society

## Do it first

A hospital uses AI to spot shadows on scans a tired eye might miss. The same year, a firm's new system answers the customer questions that twenty clerks used to answer. Both stories are true, and both are 'AI's impact'. This lesson refuses to tell only one of them.

## Sub lesson 1 — What AI is changing

AI applications now work across society: **industry and agriculture** (inspecting products, guiding machines, watching crops), **services** (customer question-answering, translation, recommendations), **medicine and education** (assisting diagnosis, tutoring and marking support), and **daily life** (voice assistants, face-unlock, the chatbot built in the last lesson). The pattern underneath the list: AI takes tasks that are **routine, pattern-shaped and data-rich** first — it reads the ten-thousandth scan with the same attention as the first — and it **assists** professionals where judgement stays human, doing the tireless part while people decide. Work changes shape: some tasks shrink away, new tasks — building, training, checking and working alongside AI — grow.

## Sub lesson 2 — Weighing the impact

The benefits are real: speed and tirelessness, wider access (a village clinic with expert assistance; a student with a patient tutor), and capability no unaided team could afford. The risks are equally real, and the book's closing section names the kind: **jobs displaced** faster than new ones appear for some workers; **fairness** — a system trained on biased data repeats the bias at scale; **privacy** — intelligent services feed on personal data; and **responsibility** — when an AI-assisted decision harms someone, the question 'who answers for this?' must have a human answer. The mature position the chapter builds toward is neither worship nor fear: use AI where it genuinely helps, keep humans deciding where humans must answer, and teach every citizen — not only programmers — to weigh both sides.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: AI is changing work and daily life by taking routine, pattern-shaped tasks and assisting professionals; its benefits — speed, access, capability — must be weighed against risks to jobs, fairness, privacy and responsibility, keeping humans answerable where humans must answer. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
