# Terms and Conditions for Using a Work

## Do it first

Every app you install asks you to agree to pages you never read. Those pages are terms and conditions — and in Grade 7, Bangladesh's learners do not just read them: they write their own, for a work of their own. Writing the sheet teaches what reading never does: every line is a decision about what you allow.

## Sub lesson 1 — What terms and conditions are

**Terms and conditions** are the rules a work's owner sets for its use: who may use it, for what, for how long, at what price or free, and what may never be done with it. They turn the owner's rights — the copyright of the companion lessons — into a usable agreement: the user who accepts the terms gains the use; the user who breaks them loses it, and the law stands behind the sheet. A work with no terms is not free for all — the owner's full rights apply by default.

## Sub lesson 2 — Making the sheet — the class project

The Grade 7 session has each team **make a terms and conditions sheet** for a work of theirs: name the work and its owner; state what use is **allowed** (see, copy for study, perform, build on); state what is **forbidden** (selling it, claiming it, changing it without credit); state the **price or freedom** and the **time** it lasts; and end with what happens when the terms are broken. Writing both columns — allowed and forbidden — is the whole craft: a sheet that only forbids teaches nothing about the use it exists to grant.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: The rules a work's owner sets for its use. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: Who may use it, for what, and at what price (also how long, and what is forbidden). Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: The use of the work. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: The use is lost, and the law stands behind the sheet. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
