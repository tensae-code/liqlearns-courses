**Acceptance count: taught in 5 countries (Ethiopia, Mozambique, Bulgaria, Afghanistan, Yemen).**

Yemen's own way (Grade 10): a skills ladder — every unit alternates short teaching topics with a named تدريب (guided training) and نشاط (activity), and Windows itself is taught as a school subject for a full unit before any application is opened.

Yemen's own way (Grade 11): the book pivots from operating the machine to understanding information itself — data kinds, number systems and coding get full units before the applications (presentations, Excel) and a networks/Internet finale; every unit closes with a formal تقويم (assessment).

Afghanistan's own way (Grade 10): a Windows XP-era office apprenticeship — one full chapter per application, taught menu by menu (File, Edit, Insert, Format), with the Dari keyboard language added as its own lesson; networks arrive only in the final chapter.

Afghanistan's own way (Grade 11): grade 11 is the health-and-PowerPoint year — a full chapter on computer health and strain-relief exercises, then Word finished off (Format, Insert, Edit) before a five-lesson PowerPoint block and a first step onto the Web.

Afghanistan's own way (Grade 12): the final year turns outward — computers in society (e-commerce, communications, even military systems), system software and virus protection, then Excel for the first time and a closing block on the Web, e-mail and browsers (taught through Internet Explorer).

Book errors touching Unit 1, taught corrected and named here: the student book numbers "Brainstorming 1.7" twice (for §1.4, printed p. 18, and again for §1.5, printed p. 22); the teacher guide's §1.2 competency reads "Define Intellectual property (IR)" for IP, and the guide's Discussion strategy is copy-pasted as discussing "Digital Identity Management issues" in §§1.2, 1.4 and 1.5 whatever the section.
