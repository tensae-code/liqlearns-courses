# How information systems change society — gains and problems

## Do it first

Name one way your family's life is better because of phones and the internet — and one way it is worse. Both answers came from the same advance. This lesson holds the two sides together, the way the rest of this unit will.

## Sub lesson 1 — The good side first

Wider access has brought **real social gains**: **access that narrows the digital divide**, **AI and augmented reality in public life**, and **digital platforms, social media and games for business and entertainment**.

These are not small things. A farmer checking prices, a pupil studying beyond her school's library, a business reaching customers it could never have met — the gains are the reason the technology spread.

## Sub lesson 2 — The problems — and why they grow (teacher guide — evidence)

**The same advances have directly caused social problems** — the lessons after this one take them one by one, **so their harm can be reduced while the benefits are kept**. That pairing is the unit's whole method.

The guide's answers (teacher guide — evidence) supply the reasoning: pupils who spend **more unmanaged time on social media and games do worse academically**, because that time **replaces reading and course resources**; **more hours online are associated with more cyberbullying**, because teenagers follow different social standards online, where **anonymity and freedom from penalty** make them more aggressive or judgemental; and addiction grows in the same soil. Schools can answer with **awareness campaigns or support groups**.
