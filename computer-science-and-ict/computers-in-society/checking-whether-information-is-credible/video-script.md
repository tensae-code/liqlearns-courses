# Video script — Checking Whether Information Is Credible

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

A **credible** source can be believed because it shows its grounds. Indonesia's books name the marks: the source is **trustworthy** (known, accountable, with a record to lose), the claims carry **evidence** that can be inspected, and the author has **qualifications** — real knowledge of the subject they write about. Grade 7 teaches the check as kredibilitas sumber informasi; Grade 11 deepens it into the characteristics of credible text, image and video. A claim with none of the three marks is not yet false — it is not yet believable, which is a different and safer verdict.
Each medium has its own tricks, so the check changes shape: for **text**, who wrote it and what do they cite; for an **image**, is it the scene it claims — or an old, cropped or edited picture wearing a new caption; for **video**, is the clip whole, in its real order and place. The last resort is the **fact-check**: a claim important enough to act on is looked up in a source whose business is checking. Credibility is a judgement with reasons, not a feeling about a page's polish.
