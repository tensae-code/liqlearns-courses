## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The marks of a credible source — trust, evidence, qualifications** — A **credible** source can be believed because it shows its grounds. Indonesia's books name the marks: the source is **trustworthy** (known, accountable, with a record to lose), the claims carry **evidence** that can be inspected, and the author has **qualifications** — real knowledge of the subject they write about. Grade 7 teaches the check as kredibilitas sumber informasi; Grade 11 deepens it into the characteristics of credible text, image and video. A claim with none of the three marks is not yet false — it is not yet believable, which is a different and safer verdict.
2. **Credibility by medium — text, image, video — and the fact-check** — Each medium has its own tricks, so the check changes shape: for **text**, who wrote it and what do they cite; for an **image**, is it the scene it claims — or an old, cropped or edited picture wearing a new caption; for **video**, is the clip whole, in its real order and place. The last resort is the **fact-check**: a claim important enough to act on is looked up in a source whose business is checking. Credibility is a judgement with reasons, not a feeling about a page's polish.
