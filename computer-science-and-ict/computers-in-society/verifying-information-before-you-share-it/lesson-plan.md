## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The checks — source, sides, agreement** — Verification begins with three questions. **Source**: who first said this, and would they know — a named authority, or an unnamed forward of a forward? **Impartiality**: does the message show more than one side, or does it argue like a lawyer for one side only — the Grade 9 book's test of differences between reality and the telling of it. **Agreement**: do independent sources say the same, in their own words? A claim that passes all three has earned forwarding; a claim that fails one gets held, however exciting it is.
2. **Technology that verifies — and the share decision** — The Grade 8 book adds the tools: **reverse image search** shows whether a photo is old or from another place; checking the **original date and context** kills most recycled scares; official websites and fact-checking services answer directly. Then comes the decision the whole lesson serves: **share, hold, or correct**. Sharing a verified thing is a service; holding an unchecked thing is a kindness; and sending a correction back along the chain you received from is citizenship — the chain that carried the falsehood can carry its cure.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Your phone buzzes: a dramatic message, already forwarded many times, begging you to forward it further. Your thumb hovers. That hover — the two minutes between receiving and sharing — is where this lesson lives. What you do in those two minutes decides whether a falsehood dies with you or rides on your trust to fifty more people. (lesson/lesson.md).
