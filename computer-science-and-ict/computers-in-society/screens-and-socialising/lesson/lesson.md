# Screens and socialising — the family and café problem

## Do it first

Picture the book's scene: friends in a café, one table, every face down in a touchscreen, nobody talking. They are together and not together. This lesson names what is being lost — and the habit that protects it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The café problem

**Family and friend socialising holds a group together** — the talking, eating and being present is not spare time; it is the glue.

But **homes full of computers and portable devices for information and entertainment can hollow it out**. The book's picture is **friends in a café each staring at a touchscreen instead of talking** — present in body, absent in company.

[Picture 29710 — a café table where everyone looks at a phone and no one talks]

## Sub lesson 2 — Digital well-being — the remedy

The remedy the book teaches is **digital well-being**: **let technology improve life, not distract from it and from other people**.

Well-being is not switching everything off. It is the device returning to its place — a tool inside a life — instead of quietly becoming the life, one glance at a time.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the café picture from memory, then give them one rule of digital well-being you could actually keep at a family meal.

## Do It Yourself

1. Observe one meal or café table (your own or nearby). Count: people present, people on screens at the same moment. Write the two numbers without names.
2. Set one well-being rule for your home for a week — write it, keep it, and note on day seven whether the talking changed.
