# Digital Bangladesh — National Data Centre, IT Parks and Cashless Payments

## Do it first

Some lessons teach a technology; this one teaches a country's bet on it. Bangladesh decided its future would be digital — services, industry and money itself — and built the pieces this lesson names. You will use some of them this week without noticing; here they are, noticed.

## Sub lesson 1 — The foundations — data centre, parks, startups, laptops

A digital country needs somewhere for its data to live: the **National Data Centre** holds government data and services on home soil. It needs places for its technology industry to grow: **hi-tech parks** gather IT companies with the connections and power they need. It needs new companies: **Startup Bangladesh** backs young technology firms. And it needs machines of its own: the book names **Made-in-Bangladesh laptops** — a country that assembles its own computers employs its own people at every level of the ladder, from factory floor to software house.

In your mind's eye: the foundations — data centre, parks, startups, laptops.

## Sub lesson 2 — Cashless Bangladesh — money by QR code

The programme reaches the citizen's pocket as **Cashless Bangladesh**: Bangladesh Bank's **QR-based payments**, where a shopper scans a code and money moves account to account — no cash, no card machine, the same code working across banks and wallets. Cashless payment leaves a record (harder to steal, easier to account), costs less to handle than cash, and brings small sellers — the tea stall, the rickshaw — into the recorded economy. The book's closing picture is the programme whole: e-services, e-agriculture and e-governance riding on foundations the citizen rarely sees, into a wallet the citizen uses daily.
