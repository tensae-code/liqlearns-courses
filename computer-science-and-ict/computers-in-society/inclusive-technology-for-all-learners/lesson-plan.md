**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Armenia (2026-11-14) — in the title Computers in Society on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Armenia's Digital Literacy and Computer Science books (Armenia Grade 11 G11 student), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree and live lesson content were checked for the slug and for near matches before building.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** inclusion and technology — designing digital tools so that every learner, whatever their body, language or means, can take part (Armenia Grade 11).
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Inclusion — who is shut out, and by what** — **Inclusive technology** is technology designed so nobody is shut out by a barrier they did not choose: the learner who cannot see the screen, cannot hear the audio, cannot use a mouse, reads another language, or owns only a cheap phone on a slow connection. The barriers live in the **design**, not in the learner: text with no spoken form shuts out the blind learner; video with no captions shuts out the deaf learner. Armenia's Grade 11 book gives inclusion its own theme (Theme 4, 'Technologies ensuring inclusion').
2. **Ensuring inclusion — the maker's toolkit** — Inclusion is ensured by building the second path in from the start: **captions and transcripts** for audio and video, **text-to-speech and screen readers** welcomed by clean structure, **keyboard-only operation**, **scalable text and high-contrast** display, **plain-language and multi-language** versions, and **low-end modes** that work on cheap devices. The test of inclusion is not a checklist but a person: can *this* learner, with *their* body, language and phone, finish the lesson? Design that passes that test is simply design finished.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

Paper and a pencil, and a computer with the program this lesson uses, if available.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — A tool that only some can use is unfinished. This lesson learns what finishing it means. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of inclusion — who is shut out, and by what as the lesson describes it — the student studies the picture and names what each part shows.
   - ADD GIF: a GIF of ensuring inclusion — the maker's toolkit in action — the student watches once, then says what changed and why.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the lesson's key names and definitions aloud, in the order the lesson gives them — the student listens once through, then reads the same lines aloud along with the voice. (Audio is a described slot in this build; no audio file is made.)
3. **Say it to your companion** — the companion block in lesson/lesson.md.
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the lesson in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-false, matching, short-answer — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the doing block at the end of lesson/lesson.md.
