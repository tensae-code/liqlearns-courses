## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The women who were there from the start** — The first program ever written for a computer was written by a woman: **Ada Lovelace**, in the 1840s, for a machine that was never even built. When electronic computing began, programming was largely women's work — the first programmers of ENIAC were women, and **Grace Hopper** built the first compiler and pushed programming toward human-readable languages. The Décodage annexes tell this history on purpose: computing was not made by men alone, and its first chapter belongs to women.
2. **The question of gender today** — If women were there from the start, why are they a minority in computing classes and jobs today? The guides pose the question honestly as a question of **gender (le genre)**: images and stereotypes — “computing is for boys” — steer choices long before talent does, and the field loses minds it needs. The lesson the annexes teach is double: know the true history, and notice the stereotype when it speaks — in a textbook picture, a joke, or your own first thought.
