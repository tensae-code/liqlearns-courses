# Lesson plan — From an Idea to a Digital Project

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Computers in Society · **Lesson:** From an Idea to a Digital Project
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Egypt (2026-11-14) — in the title Computers in Society on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Egypt's student books (Egypt Ministry of Education and Technical Education / Ministry of Education), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** As an idea — something to show, teach, celebrate or solve.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The idea becomes a plan — what, for whom, in what parts** — Every digital project begins as an **idea** — something to show, teach, celebrate or solve. The idea becomes buildable when three questions are answered: **what** exactly will the finished thing be, **who** is it for, and **what parts** will it be made of — the data collected, the slides, the page — in what order? Egypt's Grade 5 unit has supplied each part in its own lesson, so the plan is a gathering, not an invention: the project's parts are the unit's skills, chosen and ordered for this idea. A plan written down, even in five lines, is what separates a project from a pile of beginnings.
2. **Parts become a project — build, join, show** — Building follows the plan part by part, and the project's craft is in the **joining**: the collected data appears in the presentation; the presentation's best page becomes the website's front; names, colours and words agree across the parts so the audience meets **one thing**, not three exercises. The road ends in **showing**: the project is presented to its real audience, whose questions reveal what the maker could not see. Egypt ends primary ICT's project unit here — idea, plan, parts, joining, showing — because that five-step road is the one every bigger project in this course, up to the secondary programming projects, will walk again at greater length.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
