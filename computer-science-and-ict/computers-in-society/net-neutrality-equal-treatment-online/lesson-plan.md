# Lesson plan — Net Neutrality — Equal Treatment Online

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Computers in Society · **Lesson:** Net Neutrality — Equal Treatment Online
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT leftovers — France (2026-11-22) — in the title Computers in Society on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from France's *SNT 2de — Manuel Libre de Sciences numériques et technologie, Seconde* (Les Manuels Libres), because no existing lesson covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree was checked for the slug and for near matches before building.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Net neutrality is the rule that an internet provider must carry all traffic the same way — it may not slow down, block or charge extra for some sites or services while favouring others — so that a small site travels the network on the same terms as a giant one.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on knowing what an internet provider is and that data travels in packets through the provider's network. No technical background beyond that is needed.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What net neutrality says** — The principle itself: providers must treat all data equally — no blocking of lawful sites, no deliberate slowing (throttling) of some services, no paid fast lanes — whatever the sender, the receiver or the content.
2. **Why it is argued about** — The reasons the rule exists — a small creator reaches the public on the same terms as a giant, and users, not providers, choose what succeeds — and the providers' counter-argument about paying for the network; what is at stake for ordinary users either way.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State what net neutrality requires of an internet provider.
2. Name the three practices the rule forbids: blocking, throttling and paid priority.
3. Give the main argument for the rule — equal terms for small creators and free choice for users.
4. Give the main argument providers make against it, fairly, in your own words.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else — this is a lesson about a rule and the reasons for it.

## If the learner is stuck
Thinks neutrality means the internet is free of charge → it is about equal treatment of traffic, not about price to the user; you still pay for your connection. Thinks throttling is the same as a slow server → throttling is the provider deliberately slowing a service; a slow site may simply have weak servers of its own. Can only argue one side → the pass mark needs both: the open-road argument and the providers' who-pays argument.
