# Lesson plan — ICT in Agriculture

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Computers in Society · **Lesson:** ICT in Agriculture
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Mozambique (2026-11-14) — in the title Computers in Society on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Mozambique's *Módulo de Tecnologias de Informação e Comunicação (TIC) — Programa do Ensino Secundário à Distância, 1º Ciclo (PESD I)*, because no existing lesson teaches this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** ICT helps a farmer at every step of the farming year: telephones, radio, computers and the internet bring weather forecasts, planting and pest advice and expert help from far away, keep farm records straight, and connect the farmer to prices, buyers and supplies — so decisions are made on information, not on guessing.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the learner's own home ground — the fields, gardens and animals they see every day — and on the opening idea of what ICT is; every tool is built inside the lesson from the farming year itself.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The farmer's ICT tools** — A farmer's ICT starts with tools many homes already have.
2. **ICT for growing — crops, animals and the weather** — Growing is a chain of decisions, and ICT strengthens each link.
3. **ICT for selling, buying and keeping records** — A good harvest sold badly is still a poor year, so ICT works on the money side too.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: What ICT Is in First Steps. Recommended, never forced — a learner may land on this lesson directly, and it stands alone.
- Mozambique's own way is kept: in the PESD I distance module this is Lição 27, in the closing run of applied lessons — teaching and learning, then agriculture, then health — so ICT is shown at work in the learner's own community before the module ends.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the ICT tools a farmer can use, and say what each one brings.
2. Say how ICT helps with growing crops and raising animals — weather, planting, pests, disease and expert advice.
3. Say how ICT helps with the money side of farming — prices, buyers, supplies and records.
4. Walk through one farming decision and show where ICT improves it, step by step.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil, and knowledge of one real farm, garden or animal-keeping home near you.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Think of a farmer you know. How does that farmer decide when to plant? How do they know what price their crop will fetch in town, or what to do when insects attack the leaves? In the past, those answers came by walking, asking and guessing. Keep those questions in mind — this lesson shows ICT bringing the answers to the farm. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of a farmer in a field holding a mobile phone, with a radio at the home and a computer with farming information — the three ICT tools of the farm — the student studies the picture and says, for each part of it, which part of the lesson it shows.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of a farming year in three panels — a weather forecast on a phone before planting, a photographed sick leaf being sent for advice, and a healthy crop at harvest — the student studies the picture and says, for each part of it, which part of the lesson it shows.

## If the learner is stuck
Thinks ICT in agriculture means machines doing the farming → the tools here bring information and connection; the farmer still farms. Can name tools but not uses → walk the farming year: forecast before planting, advice while growing, price before selling. Thinks prices do not matter if the harvest is good → a good harvest sold at the first low price offered is still a poor year; information protects the selling too.
