# ICT in Agriculture — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What does ICT mainly bring to a farm?**
a) Rain  b) Information and connection  c) Seeds that plant themselves
**Answer: b) Information and connection — Forecasts, advice, prices and contact, arriving in time to matter.**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) Which tool carries farming programmes and weather news to many farmers at once, even where the internet is weak?**
a) A spreadsheet  b) A printer  c) Radio
**Answer: c) Radio — It reaches many farmers at once in the languages they speak.**

**3. (Sub lesson 2) A weather forecast received by phone helps a farmer most with —**
a) Choosing when to plant  b) Selling the harvest  c) Keeping records
**Answer: a) Choosing when to plant — Planting into the right rain starts with the forecast.**

**4. (Sub lesson 2) A farmer photographs a sick leaf and sends the picture for advice. What is ICT doing here?**
a) Watering the crop  b) Carrying the question to an expert and bringing the answer back  c) Scaring insects
**Answer: b) Carrying the question to an expert and bringing the answer back — Expert help at a distance.**

**5. (Sub lesson 2) For animals, ICT helps a farmer to —**
a) Make animals grow without food  b) Hide sick animals  c) Reach animal-health advice and a veterinary worker quickly
**Answer: c) Reach animal-health advice and a veterinary worker quickly — The same information pattern as for crops.**

**6. (Sub lesson 3) Why does a farmer check the market price in town before selling?**
a) To decide where and when to sell instead of accepting the first price offered  b) To make the crop grow faster  c) To avoid harvesting
**Answer: a) To decide where and when to sell instead of accepting the first price offered — Information protects the selling.**

**7. (Sub lesson 3) Farm records kept on a phone or computer show the farmer —**
a) Tomorrow's weather only  b) Which crop truly paid and which one lost money  c) Other farmers' secrets
**Answer: b) Which crop truly paid and which one lost money — Planted, spent, harvested and sold, kept straight.**
