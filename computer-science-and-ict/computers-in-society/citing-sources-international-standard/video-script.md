# Video script — Citing Sources — the International Standard

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

A **citation** is a promise: this idea, figure or sentence came from a work the reader can find. Citing does three jobs at once — it gives the maker their credit, it lets the reader check the claim at its source, and it separates your words from borrowed ones, which is the line plagiarism crosses. The international standard is simply the agreed shape of the promise: author, title, where it was published, when — the same order of facts everywhere, so any reader in any country can follow it home.
Word processors carry the standard inside them: Google Docs' **citation menu** (in the Tools menu) lets the writer add a source once — choosing its kind (book, website, journal) and filling its facts — and then insert the citation wherever the work is used, with the bibliography built in the chosen style. Indonesia's Grade 11 enrichment teaches exactly this menu. The tool keeps the form; the writer's duties stay human: cite everything borrowed, cite it where it is used, and never list a work that was not opened.
