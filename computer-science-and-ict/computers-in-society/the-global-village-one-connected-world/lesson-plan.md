## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The village idea — distance collapses** — Marshall McLuhan called the connected world a **global village**: when communication is instant and worldwide, the planet behaves like a village — news travels to everyone at once, strangers become neighbours, and what happens far away is felt at home the same day. The village is not a metaphor for the cables; it is what the cables *do* to distance. Its components are the connections themselves — communication networks, shared information, and the services that ride on them — and each of the village's rooms is a part of ordinary life remade: talking, working, learning, healing, buying, and governing.
2. **Life inside the village** — In the village, **communication** is instant and cheap; **employment** crosses borders without the worker moving; **education** arrives through screens; **medicine** consults at a distance; **business** sells to the world from one shop; and **news and culture** flow everywhere at once. The village also has a village's problems: rumours outrun corrections, a failure far away can close a service at home, and cultures meet so fast that each must decide what of itself to keep. Bangladesh's book teaches the village first because every later chapter — networks, databases, programming — is one of its streets.
