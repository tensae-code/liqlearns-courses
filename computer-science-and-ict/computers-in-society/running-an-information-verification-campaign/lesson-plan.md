# Lesson plan — Running an Information Verification Campaign

**The one thing this lesson teaches:** running an information verification campaign: taking verification public — a class or community campaign that teaches checking together, verifies claims as a team, and reports back after the campaign (Bangladesh Grade 8 Digital Technology, Learning Experience 1, Sessions 6–8).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Verifying together — the campaign's work** — A **campaign** is organised persuasion for a good habit: the Grade 8 teams choose the claims their community actually forwards, verify them **together** — dividing the checks of the companion lesson among members so each claim gets source, impartiality and agreement examined by different eyes — and prepare the verdicts for the public: what was claimed, what the checks found, what is true. Working together is the method and the message: verification is shown as something ordinary people do in the open, not a scolding delivered from above.
2. **After the campaign — what changed** — The book closes the experience by looking back: **after the campaign**, the teams report what they did, which claims they settled, what the community answered, and what they would run differently. A campaign that is never reviewed teaches half its lesson. The review turns one week's project into a standing habit — a class that has campaigned once keeps a checker on duty in every group chat it belongs to, which is the campaign's real product.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
