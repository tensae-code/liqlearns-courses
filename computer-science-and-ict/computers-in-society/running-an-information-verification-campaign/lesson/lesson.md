# Running an Information Verification Campaign

## Do it first

One checker stops one falsehood. A school of checkers changes what a whole neighbourhood forwards. Grade 8's book does not stop at teaching verification — it sends its learners out to campaign for it. This lesson is how such a campaign is run: planned, public, and honestly reviewed afterwards.

## Sub lesson 1 — Verifying together — the campaign's work

A **campaign** is organised persuasion for a good habit: the Grade 8 teams choose the claims their community actually forwards, verify them **together** — dividing the checks of the companion lesson among members so each claim gets source, impartiality and agreement examined by different eyes — and prepare the verdicts for the public: what was claimed, what the checks found, what is true. Working together is the method and the message: verification is shown as something ordinary people do in the open, not a scolding delivered from above.

In your mind's eye: verifying together — the campaign's work.

## Sub lesson 2 — After the campaign — what changed

The book closes the experience by looking back: **after the campaign**, the teams report what they did, which claims they settled, what the community answered, and what they would run differently. A campaign that is never reviewed teaches half its lesson. The review turns one week's project into a standing habit — a class that has campaigned once keeps a checker on duty in every group chat it belongs to, which is the campaign's real product.

In your mind's eye: after the campaign — what changed.
