# Balanced Use of Digital Communication Tools

## Do it first

The same tool carries a grandmother's face and a 3 a.m. argument. The tool did not choose — the use did. This lesson learns balance: the positives kept, the negatives governed.

## Sub lesson 1 — Positives and negatives — the honest list

Digital communication tools' **positives**: distance abolished (family, teachers, help at a message's distance), speed and cheapness, groups and communities of interest, a record of what was said. Their **negatives**: time consumed past intention, misunderstanding without faces and voices, exposure (the screenshot outlives the chat), pressure of permanent availability, and contact with strangers and harms. Jordan's Grade 7 list refuses the lazy verdicts — neither 'tools are bad' nor 'more is better' — because balance is a judgement remade per tool, per person, per hour.

## Sub lesson 2 — Irresponsible use — risks and the balanced craft

**Irresponsible use** risks, for the individual and society: words sent in anger that cannot be unsent, private things shared into public keeping, rumours forwarded into fires, hours that eat sleep, study and family. The **balanced craft** answers with habits: **purpose before opening** (know the errand), **limits kept** (times and places without tools), **the pause before sending** (the angry message sleeps a night), and **presence** (the person in the room outranks the person on the screen). Balance is not less life; it is the tools kept in their place — as servants of the life, not its landlords.
