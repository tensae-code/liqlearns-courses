## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Positives and negatives — the honest list** — Digital communication tools' **positives**: distance abolished (family, teachers, help at a message's distance), speed and cheapness, groups and communities of interest, a record of what was said. Their **negatives**: time consumed past intention, misunderstanding without faces and voices, exposure (the screenshot outlives the chat), pressure of permanent availability, and contact with strangers and harms. Jordan's Grade 7 list refuses the lazy verdicts — neither 'tools are bad' nor 'more is better' — because balance is a judgement remade per tool, per person, per hour.
2. **Irresponsible use — risks and the balanced craft** — **Irresponsible use** risks, for the individual and society: words sent in anger that cannot be unsent, private things shared into public keeping, rumours forwarded into fires, hours that eat sleep, study and family. The **balanced craft** answers with habits: **purpose before opening** (know the errand), **limits kept** (times and places without tools), **the pause before sending** (the angry message sleeps a night), and **presence** (the person in the room outranks the person on the screen). Balance is not less life; it is the tools kept in their place — as servants of the life, not its landlords.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — The same tool carries a grandmother's face and a 3 a.m. argument. The tool did not choose — the use did. This lesson learns balance: the positives kept, the negatives governed. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of positives and negatives — the honest list as the lesson describes it — the student studies the picture and names what each part shows.
   - ADD GIF: a GIF of irresponsible use — risks and the balanced craft in action — the student watches once, then says what changed and why.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the lesson's key names and definitions aloud, in the order the lesson gives them — the student listens once through, then reads the same lines aloud along with the voice. (Audio is a described slot in this build; no audio file is made.)
