## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The varieties of wrong information** — Wrong information wears several coats. An honest **mistake** is wrong but meant no harm. A **rumour** is an unchecked story passed on because it might be true. **Propaganda** is information shaped — true parts chosen, false parts added — to push a side. **Fake news** is invented whole and dressed as reporting. Bangladesh's Grade 8 book opens its verification campaign with these varieties because the cure differs by kind: a mistake needs a correction, propaganda needs its shaping exposed, and a fabrication needs to be named as invented.
2. **How falsehood spreads — and why it outruns truth** — Digital media give falsehood three engines: **speed** (a forward costs nothing and reaches hundreds), **trust** (we believe what arrives from our own people), and **feeling** (anger and fear are shared faster than calm facts). The truth travels slower because it must be checked first — which is why the book's answer is not censorship but a population of checkers: every receiver who pauses, verifies, and refuses to forward is a wall the falsehood cannot pass. The companion lessons teach the checking itself and the campaign that spreads it.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — A fake job offer cost a family its savings. A rumour emptied a market in an afternoon. Neither was printed by a newspaper or spoken by an official — both travelled phone to phone, friend to friend, believed because they arrived from someone trusted. This lesson studies the travelling falsehood itself: its kinds, and its speed. (lesson/lesson.md).
