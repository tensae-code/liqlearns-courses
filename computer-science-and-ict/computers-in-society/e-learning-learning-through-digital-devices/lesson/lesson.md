# E-learning — learning through digital devices

## Do it first

A lesson is travelling tonight — not in a bag, not on paper — from a teacher in one town to a learner in another, who will open it after supper, at her own speed. You may be holding such a lesson now. This one names it.

## Sub lesson 1 — E-learning and its components

**E-learning** is **learning delivered through digital devices** — also called **online, virtual, distributed or computer-based learning**, and **online learning** when it runs **over the internet**.

Its **components** are: the **internet**, **computers or phones**, **digital learning resources**, **content developers** such as **teachers and graphic editors**, and **learners**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Benefits, drawbacks — and commitment (teacher guide — evidence)

**Benefits:** learn **at any time**, learn **at your own pace**, reach **resources from many schools**, **rich multimedia**, and **no travel or accommodation costs**. The guide's fuller list adds **reaching remote pupils** and **better materials from other institutions** (teacher guide — evidence).

**Drawbacks:** learners are **physically isolated**, teachers can **monitor progress only weakly**, and **technology costs shut out low-income learners**.

And the guide's warning, plainly (teacher guide — evidence): in e-learning **nobody forces you — it runs on personal commitment**. The freedom and the weakness are the same door, seen from two sides.
