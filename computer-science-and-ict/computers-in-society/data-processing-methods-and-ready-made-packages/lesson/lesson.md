# Data Processing Methods and Ready-Made Packages

## Do it first

A school office adds up five hundred exam marks. It could gather them all month and process them together, or process each paper the hour it is marked. And the program doing it — was it bought ready, or written for this school alone? This lesson names both choices.

## Sub lesson 1 — The computer as a data-processing machine — and the methods of processing

Sudan's Grade 11 book opens by naming the computer a **data-processing machine**: it takes data in, works on it by set rules, and gives out information. The **methods** of processing differ in when the work is done: data can be gathered first and processed together in a **batch**, or processed **at once as it arrives**, transaction by transaction. Older ways stand behind both: processing by hand, and processing by machines that are not computers — slower, and bound to the people working them. The method is chosen by the job: a monthly payroll suits a batch; a bank balance does not wait for one.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ready-made packages

Not every program is written for one user. A **ready-made package** is software written once and sold ready to many users — the word processors, spreadsheets and accounts programs any office can buy and use as they come. Against them stand **specially written programs**, built for one organisation's exact needs, like the spare-parts factory system later in this book. The package is cheaper and instant but fits its buyer only as well as it fits everyone; the written program fits exactly and costs exactly that. Naming which of the two a job should use is the lesson's judgement.
