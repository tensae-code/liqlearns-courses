## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The computer as a data-processing machine — and the methods of processing** — Sudan's Grade 11 book opens by naming the computer a **data-processing machine**: it takes data in, works on it by set rules, and gives out information. The **methods** of processing differ in when the work is done: data can be gathered first and processed together in a **batch**, or processed **at once as it arrives**, transaction by transaction. Older ways stand behind both: processing by hand, and processing by machines that are not computers — slower, and bound to the people working them. The method is chosen by the job: a monthly payroll suits a batch; a bank balance does not wait for one.
2. **Ready-made packages** — Not every program is written for one user. A **ready-made package** is software written once and sold ready to many users — the word processors, spreadsheets and accounts programs any office can buy and use as they come. Against them stand **specially written programs**, built for one organisation's exact needs, like the spare-parts factory system later in this book. The package is cheaper and instant but fits its buyer only as well as it fits everyone; the written program fits exactly and costs exactly that. Naming which of the two a job should use is the lesson's judgement.
