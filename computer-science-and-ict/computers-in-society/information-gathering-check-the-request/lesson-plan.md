# Lesson plan — Information gathering — check the request before you answer it

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Computers in Society · **Lesson:** Information gathering — check the request before you answer it
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 14 — Computers in Society of the Computer Science and ICT ladder (TITLES.md); Ethiopia Grade 12, Unit 1 — Information Systems and Their Applications. The ladder is the recommended order, never a forced one.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** systems collect user data and credentials as part of working; what may be gathered is disputed, consent is the protection, and the user must examine the intention behind any request before handing anything over.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the privacy and ownership lessons just before it (recommended first, never forced) and needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds, looping.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Gathering is part of how systems work — and it is disputed** — systems collect data and credentials as part of working; what may be gathered, and why, is disputed.
2. **Consent — examine the intention first** — consent protects against information being stolen; examine the intention behind any request, especially for personal information or credentials.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the lesson named in this plan's level line, inside this same title. Recommended, never forced. A learner can land here directly; the lesson stands alone.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what systems collect as part of working.
2. Explain why gathering is disputed.
3. State the pause rule: examine the intention, especially for credentials.
