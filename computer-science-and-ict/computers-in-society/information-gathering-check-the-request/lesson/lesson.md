# Information gathering — check the request before you answer it

## Do it first

A new app asks for your name, your contacts and your password to another service — before it has done anything for you. Stop. This lesson teaches the pause: what is being gathered, why, and whether you consent.

## Sub lesson 1 — Gathering is part of how systems work — and it is disputed

**Many systems collect user data and credentials as part of working** — gathering is built into how they function, not an accident added later.

But **what may be gathered, and why, is disputed**. There is no single settled answer, which is exactly why the user's own judgement matters at every request.

[Picture 29705 — an app asking for credentials with a magnifying glass over its reason]

## Sub lesson 2 — Consent — examine the intention first

**Consent is the protection against information being stolen.** Information you hand over without examining the request has left your control.

So the rule is a pause: **examine the intention behind any request** — especially one asking for **personal information or credentials** — before handing anything over. A request that cannot explain itself has answered your question.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion about a request for information you have seen, and judge it aloud: what was gathered, why, and would you consent?

## Do It Yourself

1. List three requests for your information you met this month. For each write: what was asked, the stated reason, your decision.
2. Write the three questions you will ask any app or form before giving credentials.
