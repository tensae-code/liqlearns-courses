# Computers in Military Systems

## Do it first

Armies ran on paper maps and radio voices; now they run on computers. In this lesson you learn where computers work in military systems, as Afghanistan's Grade 12 book teaches it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Computers in command, communication and sensing

A modern military system is an information system under pressure, and computers carry its information. **Communications** networks link commanders and units so orders and reports move in seconds, protected by encryption. **Navigation** systems fix position exactly, from satellites, in any weather. **Radar and surveillance** systems are computer senses: they watch the sky and ground continuously, sort real threats from noise, and show commanders a picture no single human lookout could build. In each case the computer does what computers do — sense, communicate, calculate — but the cost of slowness or error is measured differently here, which is why these systems are built to keep working when parts of them fail.

## Sub lesson 2 — Training, logistics and guided equipment

Behind the front line, computers run the army's ordinary miracles: **logistics** — tracking supplies, vehicles, fuel and spare parts across a whole country, the same database work a supermarket does, at national scale and under deadline. **Training simulators** let pilots and crews practise dangerous work safely, failing on a screen instead of in the air — interactivity in uniform. And modern equipment itself is guided by computers: aircraft systems, air defence, and precision-guided weapons all calculate continuously to hit what they are aimed at. The book teaches the topic plainly, as knowledge of the world its students will live in — and its lesson for every field is the same: wherever information must move fast and be right, computers will be found, and someone must understand them.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Computers serve in military systems — in communications, navigation, radar and surveillance, training simulators, logistics and the guidance of modern equipment: Afghanistan's final-year book teaches this openly as one of the fields computers have changed. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
