# What an information system is

## Do it first

Your school holds your quiz marks, assignment marks and exam marks. At term's end a report appears with averages and rankings — nobody re-added every mark by hand that morning. Something collected, processed, stored and shared. This lesson names that something.

An **information system** is **a set of interrelated components working together to collect, process, store and share information with its users**.

It **produces the reports employees use to do their work**:

- **Managers pricing a product** work from the system's reports.
- A **school system** turns **quiz, assignment and exam marks** into **averages and rankings**.

[Picture 16111 — a school system collecting marks and producing a ranked report]

Four verbs carry the whole definition — **collect, process, store, share**. Wherever those four happen together, through connected parts working as one, an information system is at work. The next lesson takes one apart into its five components.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the definition of an information system and its four working verbs. Then name the two report examples: the manager's and the school's.

## Do It Yourself

1. Find one information system in your school or town. Write its four verbs: what it collects, how it processes, where it stores, who it shares with.
2. Follow one mark of yours from a quiz paper to the term report. List every hand or machine it passed through.
3. Name a decision at home or school that is made from a report. Say which system produced the report.
