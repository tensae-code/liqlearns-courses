# Video script — Using Information to Solve Problems

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Every problem, looked at honestly, is a **gap**: you know some things, you need others, and the answer sits on the far side. Vietnam's Grade 9 book teaches problem-solving as information work first: **state the problem exactly**, then ask **what information is missing** — what you must find out before any solving can start. A pupil who cannot name the missing information does not yet understand the problem; a pupil who can name it already holds half the solution, because the search now has a target.
With the gap named, the work runs in order: **find** the needed information (in books, on the Internet, by asking, by measuring), **judge** it (is it trustworthy, current, and really about this problem?), **use** it to close the gap, and only then **solve and check** — does the answer actually answer the problem that was stated? Vietnam teaches this inside its information theme because the computer is the solver's great information tool: it finds fast, but the judging and the using stay human work.
