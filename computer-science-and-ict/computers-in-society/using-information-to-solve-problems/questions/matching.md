# Using Information to Solve Problems — matching

Match each item on the left with the one it belongs to on the right.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What is a problem, in information terms?** → **A gap — some things are known, others are needed, and the answer sits across the gap**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What is the first step?** → **State the problem exactly, in your own words**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What question comes before solving?** → **What information is missing — what must be found out first?**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) Why does naming the missing information matter?** → **It gives the search a target — it is half the solution**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) What does a solver who cannot name the missing information show?** → **That they do not yet understand the problem**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What are the four steps, in order?** → **Find the information, judge it, use it, then solve and check**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) Where can needed information be found?** → **In books, on the Internet, by asking people, and by measuring**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What three judgements are made on found information?** → **Is it trustworthy, is it current, and is it really about this problem?**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) What is checked at the end?** → **That the answer really answers the problem that was stated**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) Which parts stay human work when a computer helps?** → **The judging of the information and the using of it**
