# Lesson plan — Unethical Use of Information and Technology

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Computers in Society · **Lesson:** Unethical Use of Information and Technology
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Fiji (2026-11-14) — in the title Computers in Society on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Fiji's student books (Fiji Ministry of Education / Technology and Employment Skills Training (TEST) section, Suva), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The unethical uses of information and computer technology.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. ## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Misuse of information — taking, copying, changing** — Unethical use begins with **information itself**: opening, reading or changing information that is **not yours to touch**; **copying** another's work, words or programs and passing them off as your own; **spreading information you know is false**; and using information gathered for one purpose for **another purpose its owner never agreed to**. Fiji's order matters — these are wrongs done with data, before any machine is damaged: the harm is to the person the information belongs to.
2. **Misuse of the technology — breaking in and breaking things** — The second half of the catalogue is misuse of the **technology**: getting into accounts, files or systems **without permission**; planting or passing on **harmful programs**; using a computer to **steal, cheat or harass**; and using machines and networks in ways that **waste or damage** what a whole class or office shares. Fiji teaches the catalogue beside the ethics definition for a reason: a learner who can *name* a misuse when they see it — in a story, in a friend's plan, in their own temptation — has the first defence against doing it.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — The same tool, turned the wrong way. After defining computer ethics, Fiji's Years 7–8 book names the unethical uses themselves — the catalogue of ways information and computer technology are misused. This lesson learns the catalogue so it can be recognised on sight. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of misuse of information — taking, copying, changing as the lesson describes it — the student studies the picture and names what each part shows.
   - ADD GIF: a GIF of misuse of the technology — breaking in and breaking things in action — the student watches once, then says what changed and why.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the lesson's key names and definitions aloud, in the order the lesson gives them — the student listens once through, then reads the same lines aloud along with the voice. (Audio is a described slot in this build; no audio file is made.)

4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-false, matching, short-answer — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
