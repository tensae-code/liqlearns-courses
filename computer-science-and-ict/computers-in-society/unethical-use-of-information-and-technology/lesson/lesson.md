# Unethical Use of Information and Technology

## Do it first

The same tool, turned the wrong way. After defining computer ethics, Fiji's Years 7–8 book names the unethical uses themselves — the catalogue of ways information and computer technology are misused. This lesson learns the catalogue so it can be recognised on sight.

## Sub lesson 1 — Misuse of information — taking, copying, changing

Unethical use begins with **information itself**: opening, reading or changing information that is **not yours to touch**; **copying** another's work, words or programs and passing them off as your own; **spreading information you know is false**; and using information gathered for one purpose for **another purpose its owner never agreed to**. Fiji's order matters — these are wrongs done with data, before any machine is damaged: the harm is to the person the information belongs to.

In your mind's eye: misuse of information — taking, copying, changing.

## Sub lesson 2 — Misuse of the technology — breaking in and breaking things

The second half of the catalogue is misuse of the **technology**: getting into accounts, files or systems **without permission**; planting or passing on **harmful programs**; using a computer to **steal, cheat or harass**; and using machines and networks in ways that **waste or damage** what a whole class or office shares. Fiji teaches the catalogue beside the ethics definition for a reason: a learner who can *name* a misuse when they see it — in a story, in a friend's plan, in their own temptation — has the first defence against doing it.

In your mind's eye: misuse of the technology — breaking in and breaking things.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: The unethical uses of information and computer technology. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: Opening, reading or changing information that is not yours to touch. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: Getting into accounts, files or systems without permission. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: Programs planted or passed on to damage or spy — a misuse of technology. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
