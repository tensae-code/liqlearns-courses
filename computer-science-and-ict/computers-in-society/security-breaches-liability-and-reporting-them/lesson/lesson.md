# Security breaches, liability and reporting them

## Do it first

A shop you used announces that customer details were taken. Your name and card details may be in strangers' hands. Who is answerable? What can be done with your details? And who must be told, how fast? This lesson works through all three.

## Sub lesson 1 — Breaches, liability and identity theft

As **personal and financial information moves and is stored online**, a **security breach exposes an organisation to liability** — it becomes answerable for what was lost. Accepted procedures exist precisely because the stakes are this concrete.

**Stolen personal details can be harvested for identity theft** — someone else living behind your name and accounts. And the liability spreads wider than databases: **blogs and instant messaging add further liability** of their own.

## Sub lesson 2 — Report promptly — and the INSA answer (teacher guide — evidence)

The duty is promptness: **any breach on your own systems must be reported promptly to the organisation's concerned unit or the government body responsible for protecting the country's information and infrastructure**.

The guide (teacher guide — evidence) names that body: the **Information Network Security Administration (INSA)**, whose job is to **protect Ethiopia's information and information infrastructure**; pupils are sent to its website to learn **how to report an attempt to harm national information**. The same guide lesson debates the other side of state power: classes split **for and against government agencies monitoring citizens' digital data for national security** — the guide adds that law-enforcement and security bodies **do monitor social media** for unlawful activity and in investigations, and that **how far each platform cooperates is not always clear from its privacy policy**.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what happens after a breach — liability, identity theft, reporting — and then give them the strongest argument for monitoring and the strongest against.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the report you would send if your school account were breached: what happened, what data, when you noticed, who you are telling.
2. Hold the debate at home: two minutes for monitoring for national security, two minutes against. Write the sentence that decided it for you.
