## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The means — what 'modern technological means' covers** — Jordan's phrase covers the **digital means of modern life**: communication tools (phones, messaging, platforms), information tools (search, media, learning), work and money tools (offices in pockets, payments without cash), and the smart devices spreading through homes and cities. The lesson's first act is an inventory with a rule: a means is named by **what it lets a person do** that could not be done, or not so easily, before — the definition that keeps the list honest when the gadgets change.
2. **Effects — on the individual and on society** — The effects divide by their stage. On the **individual**: time and attention reshaped, skills gained and let rust, health of body and mind helped and harmed, privacy thinned. On **society**: work and shopping moved, communities formed and fractured, information fast and false together, the gap between the connected and the unconnected become a gap in everything else. The book's weighing is deliberately two-handed — the next lesson takes the risks alone — because a pupil who can only praise or only blame has not yet learned to weigh.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — The phone, the platform, the smart everything: modern technology is not arriving, it has arrived and moved in. This lesson names the means and weighs their effects — on one person, and on everyone together. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of the means — what 'modern technological means' covers as the lesson describes it — the student studies the picture and names what each part shows.
   - ADD GIF: a GIF of effects — on the individual and on society in action — the student watches once, then says what changed and why.
