# Lesson plan — Planning a New Civic or E-Commerce Service

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Computers in Society · **Lesson:** Planning a New Civic or E-Commerce Service
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Bangladesh (2026-11-14) — in the title Computers in Society on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). **The one thing this lesson teaches:** planning a new civic or e-commerce service: the full planning craft — prerequisites, a service-delivery plan, a proposal, and the planning poster — for a service that does not exist yet (Bangladesh Grade 8 Digital Technology, Learning Experience 3, Sessions 3–8).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Prerequisites and the delivery plan** — A new service stands on **prerequisites**: a real need it answers, the people it serves, the offices, devices and connections it requires, the staff and their skills, and the money to run after the launch day. The **service-delivery plan** then walks one citizen through it: where they apply, what happens to the application at each desk or screen, what they receive, and how long each step may take. A plan that cannot walk a citizen from door to done is a wish, not a plan.
2. **The proposal and the planning poster** — The plan must then persuade: the **proposal** argues the service's case — the need, the plan, the cost, the benefit — to the people who can approve and fund it. And the **planning poster** compresses the whole design onto one public sheet: the service, its users, its steps, its needs. The poster is the plan's honesty test — what cannot be drawn clearly has not been thought clearly — and the class presents and defends it, question by question, as a real service team must.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. A real need, its people and its required offices/devices (also staff skills, running money)
2. One citizen's whole journey, door to done
3. What the application meets at each desk or screen, and how long it may take
4. A wish, not a plan

## What you need
Paper and a pencil, and a computer with the program this lesson uses, if available.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Anyone can wish for a service. Grade 8's learners must plan one: what it needs before it starts, how each delivery step will run, why it deserves to exist — argued in a proposal — and the whole plan on one poster a stranger can judge. This lesson is that planning, whole. (lesson/lesson.md).
