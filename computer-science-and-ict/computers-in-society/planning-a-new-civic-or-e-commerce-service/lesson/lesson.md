# Planning a New Civic or E-Commerce Service

## Do it first

Anyone can wish for a service. Grade 8's learners must plan one: what it needs before it starts, how each delivery step will run, why it deserves to exist — argued in a proposal — and the whole plan on one poster a stranger can judge. This lesson is that planning, whole.

## Sub lesson 1 — Prerequisites and the delivery plan

A new service stands on **prerequisites**: a real need it answers, the people it serves, the offices, devices and connections it requires, the staff and their skills, and the money to run after the launch day. The **service-delivery plan** then walks one citizen through it: where they apply, what happens to the application at each desk or screen, what they receive, and how long each step may take. A plan that cannot walk a citizen from door to done is a wish, not a plan.

## Sub lesson 2 — The proposal and the planning poster

The plan must then persuade: the **proposal** argues the service's case — the need, the plan, the cost, the benefit — to the people who can approve and fund it. And the **planning poster** compresses the whole design onto one public sheet: the service, its users, its steps, its needs. The poster is the plan's honesty test — what cannot be drawn clearly has not been thought clearly — and the class presents and defends it, question by question, as a real service team must.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: A real need, its people and its required offices/devices (also staff skills, running money). Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: One citizen's whole journey, door to done. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: What the application meets at each desk or screen, and how long it may take. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: A wish, not a plan. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
