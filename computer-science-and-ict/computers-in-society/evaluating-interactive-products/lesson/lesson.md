# Evaluating Interactive Products

## Do it first

Before Malaysia's learners design interactions, they judge them: existing products, examined as users. In this lesson you learn how an interactive product is evaluated — by its users' success, not its maker's hopes.

## Sub lesson 1 — Evaluation — judging the meeting

To **evaluate** an interactive product is to judge **how well its meeting with users works**: can the intended users actually **do their tasks** with it? Evaluation is done **with users or as users** — walking the product's tasks in its users' shoes — not by admiring its features list. Its evidence is what happens at the meeting: tasks finished or abandoned, errors made, time taken, and the user's own reported experience.

## Sub lesson 2 — The three measures

Malaysia's evaluation weighs three measures. **Effectiveness**: can users complete the task, correctly? **Efficiency**: what does completing it cost — time, steps, effort? **Satisfaction**: how does using it feel — confidence or frustration, willingness to return? A product can pass one and fail the others: correct but exhausting (effectiveness without efficiency), pleasant but error-strewn (satisfaction without effectiveness). The evaluation's product is a **judgement with evidence**, naming the measure each finding belongs to — the form the chapter's own quantitative evaluation later numbers.
