# Evaluating Interactive Products — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What is evaluation of an interactive product?**
a) Judging how well its meeting with users works — whether intended users can do their tasks  b) With users, or as users — walking the tasks in the users' shoes  c) What happens at the meeting — tasks finished or abandoned, errors, time, reported experience  d) Effectiveness, efficiency, satisfaction
**Answer: a) Judging how well its meeting with users works — whether intended users can do their tasks**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) How is evaluation done?**
a) What happens at the meeting — tasks finished or abandoned, errors, time, reported experience  b) With users, or as users — walking the tasks in the users' shoes  c) Effectiveness, efficiency, satisfaction  d) Can users complete the task, correctly?
**Answer: b) With users, or as users — walking the tasks in the users' shoes**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What is its evidence?**
a) Effectiveness, efficiency, satisfaction  b) Can users complete the task, correctly?  c) What happens at the meeting — tasks finished or abandoned, errors, time, reported experience  d) What completing it costs — time, steps, effort
**Answer: c) What happens at the meeting — tasks finished or abandoned, errors, time, reported experience**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) Name the three measures.**
a) Can users complete the task, correctly?  b) What completing it costs — time, steps, effort  c) How using it feels — confidence or frustration, willingness to return  d) Effectiveness, efficiency, satisfaction
**Answer: d) Effectiveness, efficiency, satisfaction**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) What does effectiveness ask?**
a) Can users complete the task, correctly?  b) What completing it costs — time, steps, effort  c) How using it feels — confidence or frustration, willingness to return  d) Efficiency — it has effectiveness without efficiency
**Answer: a) Can users complete the task, correctly?**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What does efficiency ask?**
a) How using it feels — confidence or frustration, willingness to return  b) What completing it costs — time, steps, effort  c) Efficiency — it has effectiveness without efficiency  d) Effectiveness
**Answer: b) What completing it costs — time, steps, effort**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What does satisfaction ask?**
a) Efficiency — it has effectiveness without efficiency  b) Effectiveness  c) How using it feels — confidence or frustration, willingness to return  d) A judgement with evidence, each finding named by its measure
**Answer: c) How using it feels — confidence or frustration, willingness to return**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) A product correct but exhausting fails which measure?**
a) Effectiveness  b) A judgement with evidence, each finding named by its measure  c) Judging how well its meeting with users works — whether intended users can do their tasks  d) Efficiency — it has effectiveness without efficiency
**Answer: d) Efficiency — it has effectiveness without efficiency**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) A pleasant but error-strewn product fails which?**
a) Effectiveness  b) A judgement with evidence, each finding named by its measure  c) Judging how well its meeting with users works — whether intended users can do their tasks  d) With users, or as users — walking the tasks in the users' shoes
**Answer: a) Effectiveness**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) What is the evaluation's product?**
a) Judging how well its meeting with users works — whether intended users can do their tasks  b) A judgement with evidence, each finding named by its measure  c) With users, or as users — walking the tasks in the users' shoes  d) What happens at the meeting — tasks finished or abandoned, errors, time, reported experience
**Answer: b) A judgement with evidence, each finding named by its measure**
