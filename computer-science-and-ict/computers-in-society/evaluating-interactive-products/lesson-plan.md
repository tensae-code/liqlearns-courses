## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Evaluation — judging the meeting** — To **evaluate** an interactive product is to judge **how well its meeting with users works**: can the intended users actually **do their tasks** with it? Evaluation is done **with users or as users** — walking the product's tasks in its users' shoes — not by admiring its features list. Its evidence is what happens at the meeting: tasks finished or abandoned, errors made, time taken, and the user's own reported experience.
2. **The three measures** — Malaysia's evaluation weighs three measures. **Effectiveness**: can users complete the task, correctly? **Efficiency**: what does completing it cost — time, steps, effort? **Satisfaction**: how does using it feel — confidence or frustration, willingness to return? A product can pass one and fail the others: correct but exhausting (effectiveness without efficiency), pleasant but error-strewn (satisfaction without effectiveness). The evaluation's product is a **judgement with evidence**, naming the measure each finding belongs to — the form the chapter's own quantitative evaluation later numbers.
