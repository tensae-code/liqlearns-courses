# Organising an Online Fair

## Do it first

A fair gathers makers and visitors in one place to show, judge and celebrate work. Move the fair online and the place becomes a set of pages and meetings — but every fair problem survives the move, wearing new clothes. Grade 8's final project is to organise one, guidelines first.

## Sub lesson 1 — Guidelines for the online fair

The teams first **create the guidelines**: what the fair is for, who may exhibit, what each exhibit must contain, how visitors reach and move among exhibits, how work is judged, and the conduct expected of exhibitors and visitors — the fair's constitution, written before the first stall is built. Online, the guidelines must also carry the digital rules: working links, honest authorship (the plagiarism lessons stand behind this), safe behaviour in comments and meetings, and a plan for the visitor whose connection is weak.

## Sub lesson 2 — Running the fair

Then the **fair itself**: exhibits published, visitors welcomed and guided, presentations given at set times, questions answered, judging done by the published rules and results announced. The organisers keep the fair's records — who exhibited, who visited, what was judged best — and close it the way the campaign lesson closes: with a review. An online fair's gift is reach — families, other schools, the region beyond the gate can walk its stalls — and its review asks the organiser's question: what did the reach give that a hall could not, and what did the hall still do better?
