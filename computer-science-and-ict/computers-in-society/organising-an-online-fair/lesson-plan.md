# Lesson plan — Organising an Online Fair

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Computers in Society · **Lesson:** Organising an Online Fair
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Bangladesh (2026-11-14) — in the title Computers in Society on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Bangladesh's NCTB student books (Bangladesh Grade 8 Digital Technology), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree and live lesson content were checked for the slug and for near matches before building.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** organising an online fair: planning and running a fair that happens on screens — its guidelines, its stalls, its visitors — as the closing project of Bangladesh's Grade 8 Asia-Pacific experience (Grade 8 Digital Technology, Learning Experience 6, Sessions 4–6).
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Guidelines for the online fair** — The teams first **create the guidelines**: what the fair is for, who may exhibit, what each exhibit must contain, how visitors reach and move among exhibits, how work is judged, and the conduct expected of exhibitors and visitors — the fair's constitution, written before the first stall is built. Online, the guidelines must also carry the digital rules: working links, honest authorship (the plagiarism lessons stand behind this), safe behaviour in comments and meetings, and a plan for the visitor whose connection is weak.
2. **Running the fair** — Then the **fair itself**: exhibits published, visitors welcomed and guided, presentations given at set times, questions answered, judging done by the published rules and results announced. The organisers keep the fair's records — who exhibited, who visited, what was judged best — and close it the way the campaign lesson closes: with a review. An online fair's gift is reach — families, other schools, the region beyond the gate can walk its stalls — and its review asks the organiser's question: what did the reach give that a hall could not, and what did the hall still do better?

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
