# Collaborative tools — communication, files, projects, charts and notes

## Do it first

A team needs to talk, share files, track tasks, show numbers and keep notes. Five jobs — and the book sorts the tools that do them into five kinds, with names. This lesson is the map of the toolbox.

## Sub lesson 1 — Choosing a tool — the four tests

**Not every tool fits** — choosing is part of the work. **Features, price, policies and ease of use decide effectiveness**: a powerful tool nobody can operate loses to a simple one the whole team uses.

The book then sorts the toolbox into **five kinds**, one per job the team actually has.

[Picture 29737 — five tool trays, one per kind, each with its example names]

## Sub lesson 2 — The five kinds and their examples

**Communication tools** — integrated messaging, co-working, privacy and access settings, conference calling, video chat with screen sharing: **Teams, Skype, Google Meet, Zoom, Webex, Slack, Yammer**. **Documentation and file-sharing tools** — file types, comments and notes, track changes and versioning, editing permissions, folder sharing: **Google Drive, Google Docs, Dropbox, SharePoint, MediaFire**.

**Project management tools: Trello, Asana, ClickUp. Data visualisation tools: Google Charts, Tableau, Power BI, Grafana, Chartist, FusionCharts, Datawrapper, Infogram. Note-taking tools: Evernote, OneNote, Google Keep.** Five jobs, five trays — the team picks per job, by the four tests.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the four tests, then walk the five trays, naming two tools from each without looking.

## Do It Yourself

1. Take your group's real toolbox. Sort every tool you use into the five kinds. Which tray is empty? Which is overcrowded?
2. Judge one tool by the four tests — features, price, policies, ease of use — in four lines. Would your team choose it again?
