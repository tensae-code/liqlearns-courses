## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Computers in police institutions** — Sudan's Grade 6 book ends its tour of computers at work with the **police**. Police institutions keep records the whole country depends on — who a person is, what documents they hold — and computers hold those records where they can be **searched in seconds and shared between offices** instead of buried in paper files. A record that took a day to find on paper is found before the visitor leaves the counter.
2. **ID documents and traffic control** — The book names the work: issuing the **national number** that identifies every citizen, the **passport** for travel, and the **national card** a person carries — each printed from the computer's records, each checked against them. And on the roads, computers serve **traffic control**: vehicle and licence records, and the watching of traffic the book describes. The pattern is the unit's pattern — banks, schools, now police: the computer keeps the nation's records, and the document in your hand is its printout.
