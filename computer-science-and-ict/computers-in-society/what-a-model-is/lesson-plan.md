# Lesson plan — What a Model Is

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Computers in Society · **Lesson:** What a Model Is
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Turkmenistan (2026-11-14) — in the title Computers in Society on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Turkmenistan's *Basics of Informatics, Grade 1*, because no existing lesson teaches this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A model is a made thing that stands for a real thing and shows what it is like — smaller, simpler and safe to hold: a model car stands for a real car, and a model of Turkmen national clothing shows the dress without the wearer.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on nothing but the toys, pictures and made things a six-year-old already knows; what makes them models is built inside the lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **A model stands for a real thing** — A model copies the look of a real thing in a way you can hold, look at from all sides and learn from — it is not the real thing, and that is exactly why it is useful.
2. **Models around us — including clothes** — Models come in kinds: a small copy you can hold, a picture or drawing that shows the thing flat, and a made copy of a real object's dress and shape — the book's own examples include models of Turkmen clothing.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: nothing is needed before this lesson. Recommended, never forced — a learner may land on this lesson directly, and it stands alone.
- Turkmenistan's own way is kept: in Turkmenistan's Grade 1 book modelling comes as its own chapter (Chapter IV, Modelling), and its examples of models include models of Turkmen clothing — a model is first met in the child's own culture and play, not in a computer.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a model is: a made thing that stands for a real thing and shows what it is like.
2. Say why a model is useful: it is smaller, simpler and safe to hold and turn over.
3. Name kinds of models, including a model of Turkmen clothing.

## What you need
A toy or model in the room if there is one — a toy car, a doll, a drawing — plus paper and a pencil.

1. **Do it first** — Hold a toy car, or look at a picture of one. It looks like a car — but can it drive you home? No. And yet it shows you a car's shape better than a real car rushing past. This lesson is about things like that: models. (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
Thinks a model must be the real thing, only small → ask the test: can it do the real thing's job? A model car cannot carry you; it shows, it does not do. Thinks a drawing cannot be a model → a flat picture that shows a thing's shape is one kind of model; it stands for the thing, which is the model's job.
