# What a Model Is

## Do it first

Hold a toy car, or look at a picture of one. It looks like a car — but can it drive you home? No. And yet it shows you a car's shape better than a real car rushing past. This lesson is about things like that: models.

## Sub lesson 1 — A model stands for a real thing

A **model** is a made thing that stands for a real thing.

Look at a model car. It has wheels, windows and the shape of a real car — but you can hold it in one hand, and nobody can drive it. It shows you what a real car is like, smaller and simpler. That is a model's job: to show the real thing's shape and parts, in a size your eyes and hands can learn from.

[Picture — a small model car beside a real car, the same shape in two sizes]

A model is not the real thing. It leaves out what you do not need, and keeps what you are learning. Because it is small and safe, you can turn it over, look underneath, and make a new one if it breaks — things you cannot do with the real thing.

## Sub lesson 2 — Models around us — including clothes

Once you know what a model is, you start seeing them everywhere.

Some models are small copies you can hold: a model car, a model house, a doll.

Some models are flat: a picture or a drawing that shows what a thing looks like. A drawing of a boat cannot float — but it shows the boat's shape, and you can plan with it.

And some models copy how a real thing is made and dressed. The book's own examples include models of Turkmen clothing: a small made copy of the national dress shows its shape, its colours and its patterns, so a child can learn them closely without the real dress being worn.

[Picture — a small model of Turkmen national dress, showing its shape, colours and patterns]

Hold or flat, every model does the same job: it stands for a real thing and shows what that thing is like.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: a model is a made thing that stands for a real thing and shows what it is like; it is smaller, simpler and safe to hold; models can be held or flat, like a drawing; and a model of Turkmen clothing shows the dress's shape, colours and patterns. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: find a model in the room or in a picture, name the real thing it stands for, and say three things it kept and one thing it left out.
2. Show it, don't just say it: draw a flat model of something in your home, and tell a friend what real thing it shows.
3. Show it, don't just say it: look at a model of clothing (or a picture of one) and point out the shape, colours and patterns it shows.
