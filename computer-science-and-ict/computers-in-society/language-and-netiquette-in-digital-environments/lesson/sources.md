Placement choice: this lesson was built new for the ICT expansion (Mozambique, 2026-11-14) because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree was checked first: Computers in Society holds digital citizenship and digital identity lessons, but none teaches language in digital environments together with netiquette as its own lesson. The placements file named this slug directly (NEW LESSON NEEDED — computers-in-society/language-and-netiquette-in-digital-environments). It sits in the Computers in Society title (computers-in-society/). It has two sub lessons: language in digital environments first (clear words that stay and travel, right for the place), then netiquette, the manners that govern that language — two distinct sequential parts, each opening by proving the one before.

Mozambique's own way: a self-study distance-learning module (each lesson states objectives and a minimum study time, with a preparation test and answer key at the end) that runs from ICT concepts and device care through networks, digital citizenship and cybersecurity to ICT applied in teaching, agriculture and health. This lesson keeps that shape — it is a self-study lesson from a distance module, written to be learned alone with stated objectives, and it sits in the module's order as the placements record gives it.

Bhutan's own way (Class VII, Literacy with ICT student book): Social media is taught hands-on — learners create a Facebook account, post with audience settings, and follow netiquette and an Acceptable Use Policy; backup is taught to Google Drive, and the end-of-year experience is shared on Facebook.
