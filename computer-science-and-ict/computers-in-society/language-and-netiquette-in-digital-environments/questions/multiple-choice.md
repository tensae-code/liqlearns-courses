# Language and Netiquette in Digital Environments — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) Why must online language be checked before you send it?**
a) Because the phone changes the words  b) Because written words stay and can be copied and forwarded  c) Because messages delete themselves
**Answer: b) Because written words stay and can be copied and forwarded — The reader may not be the only reader.**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) A message to a teacher should be written —**
a) In the loose, short way used with a close friend  b) With as many abbreviations as possible  c) In full, careful and respectful language
**Answer: c) In full, careful and respectful language — The place decides the language.**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) The reader of your message cannot see your face or hear your voice, so —**
a) The words themselves must carry the meaning and the tone  b) Tone does not matter  c) You should never write
**Answer: a) The words themselves must carry the meaning and the tone — Nothing else carries it for you.**

**4. (Sub lesson 2) Netiquette means —**
a) A program that blocks messages  b) The good manners of the internet  c) The rules for buying a phone
**Answer: b) The good manners of the internet — Net plus etiquette, good manners.**

**5. (Sub lesson 2) Writing a whole message in ALL CAPITAL LETTERS reads as —**
a) Whispering  b) A formal letter  c) Shouting
**Answer: c) Shouting — Capital-letter messages are the shouting of digital places.**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) Which of these keeps netiquette?**
a) Asking permission before sharing another person's photo  b) Posting a friend's private photo without asking  c) Forwarding a private message to a group
**Answer: a) Asking permission before sharing another person's photo — Privacy is respected, not broken.**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) You have written an angry message. Netiquette says —**
a) Send it at once  b) Wait, reread it, and only then decide whether to send  c) Send it twice so it is clear
**Answer: b) Wait, reread it, and only then decide whether to send — Once sent, a message cannot truly be taken back.**
