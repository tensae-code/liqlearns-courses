# Lesson plan — Building Models from Geometric Shapes

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Computers in Society · **Lesson:** Building Models from Geometric Shapes
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Turkmenistan (2026-11-14) — in the title Computers in Society on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Turkmenistan's *Basics of Informatics, Grade 1*, because no existing lesson teaches this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A model can be built by joining flat geometric shapes — circles, squares, triangles and rectangles cut out and laid or glued together as appliqué: the shapes become the parts of the thing, as a class builds a boat model from shapes together.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on What a Model Is and on knowing the flat shapes by sight; building with them is taught inside the lesson.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The shapes are the parts** — Every part of the thing you want to build matches a flat shape: a circle for a wheel or the sun, a triangle for a sail or a roof, a square or rectangle for a body or a wall — first look at the thing, then choose its shapes.
2. **Joining the shapes — appliqué** — Cut or take the shapes in the colours you chose, lay them where the parts go, look once before you glue, then stick them down as appliqué — and in the book's group work, a whole class joins its shapes into one boat model.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the four shapes used as building pieces: circle, square, triangle, rectangle.
2. Look at a thing and say which shape each of its parts is.
3. Lay out shapes into a model, check before gluing, and stick them down as appliqué.

## What you need
Coloured paper, scissors and glue with the teacher's help — or ready-cut paper shapes — plus a picture of a boat to build from.

## If the learner is stuck
Cannot see shapes in a thing → trace the thing's outline with a finger, part by part, and ask of each part only: round, straight, or pointy? Round is a circle; straight sides are a square or rectangle; a point is a triangle. Glues before looking → make layout-first a rule with a name: lay, look, then stick.
