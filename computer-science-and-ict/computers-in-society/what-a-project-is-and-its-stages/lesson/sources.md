Placement choice: this lesson was built new for the ICT expansion (Kazakhstan, 2026-11-14) because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree was checked for the slug and the live lesson content was checked for near matches before building. It sits in the Computers in Society title (computers-in-society/), following the book's own order. Provenance scope note: the placements stocked here are from Kazakhstan's okulyk.kz (OKULYK) textbooks, recommended by the Ministry of Education and Science of the Republic of Kazakhstan — the Digital Literacy and Informatika lines of the Almatykitap, Arman-PV and Atamura teams and the Nazarbayev Intellectual Schools (NIS) books, all student books except the two Kadirkulov teacher guides named in the entries below, whose placements are stocked exactly as filed. Many books exist in parallel language editions (Kazakh / Russian / Uzbek / Uyghur / English) of the same textbook: each distinct book is taught once, and every distinct edition is named and cited in the book entry. Kazakhstan's own ways are kept: one textbook line in up to five parallel language editions, the okulyk.kz digital library as the books' home, Scratch from Grade 1 and robotics strands (LEGO WeDo 2.0 and Mindstorms EV3) from Grade 1–2, and every keyword given in Kazakh–Russian–English in the early grades. Media slots in lesson/lesson.md are described slots only ([Picture — …]); no picture numbers are invented.


Kazakhstan's own way (Kobdikova G1 Digital Literacy): every keyword is given in three languages (Kazakh–Russian–English); the same national curriculum frame as the Almatykitap Grade 1 book (information etiquette / programming / robots, themes "Journey" and "Traditions and folklore") but this Arman-PV team teaches robotics on LEGO Mindstorms Education EV3 instead of WeDo 2.0, and Scratch through Kazakh folklore heroes (Aldar Kose, Makta Kyz) and national instruments (dombyra, kobyz, zhetigen); each project lesson is tiered A/B/C by difficulty.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G2 Digital Literacy): one Grade 2 year mixes Scratch, a word processor, the WavePad sound editor and Lego Mindstorms EV3 robotics, and ends with a built-and-defended robotics project ("Dancing Robot"); every section closes with research and creative task pages (printed pp. 18, 34, 58).

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G3 Digital Literacy): Grade 3 is built around making a whole Scratch game (scenario, scenes, characters, costumes) and an EV3 robotics project, with Word and PowerPoint taught as the project's tools; "Check yourself! Creative tasks" lessons close each section (printed pp. 29, 59, 95, 126 — assessment pages, not placed).

Kazakhstan's own way (Kobdikova G2 Digital Literacy): the same six-part national Grade 2 frame as the Almatykitap book, but the tools differ: the "word by word" part builds a spoken-text dialogue between two Scratch characters, sound is recorded and trimmed in the Windows Voice Recorder app (not WavePad), media players AIMP and Windows Media Player are named, the keyboard trainer is the online typingstudy.com Kazakh course, the text editor is WordPad, and the Dancing Robot dances to the Kazakh folk melody "Kamajai" loaded through the EV3 Sound Editor.

Kazakhstan's own way (Isabaeva G2 Digital Literacy): a third Grade 2 course on the same six-part national frame, from the Kokzhiek/Atamura side: the keyboard trainer is RapidTyping 5 with full finger-placement rules, sound is edited in Wave Editor (wave-editor.com) by selecting waveform segments, voice recording also uses a smartphone dictaphone, and the final part has no text-editor lesson — the project algorithm itself is the writing task. Unnumbered end-of-part project and summary pages (printed pp. 27, 50, 84, 99, 114, 136–137) are not placed separately.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G5 Informatika): Grade 5 Informatika by the Almatykitap team drops Scratch entirely: information theory first (transmission, encryption, binary, graphics in binary code), then raster work in Paint against vector work with curves, and half the year is EV3 robotics beyond simple movement — the gyroscopic sensor, line following and robo-sumo competitions.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kobdikova G4 ICT): Grade 4 ICT deepens every primary strand at once: Scratch grows variables, logical and comparison operators on the way to "My game"; EV3 robotics is taught through its sensors (colour, ultrasonic) towards labyrinth and kegeling; video is a real subject in the NCH VideoPad editor; and the final part pairs e-mail file exchange with password reliability and "computers of the future". Augmented-reality markers (the AR+ app from arman-pv.kz) animate pictures through a phone.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G3 ICT): the Almatykitap team's Grade 3 ICT runs the same eight cross-cutting themes as the Arman-PV book (Living nature; What is good, what is bad; Time; Architecture; Art; Outstanding personalities; Water — source of life; Culture of rest. Holidays) but as 34 short lessons with named mini-project defences ("My idol"); the glossary names Tux Paint as the children's raster editor and WordPad as the text editor. The mixed review lesson 21 ("Creative tasks: searching, typing, drawing", printed p. 53) is not placed.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukhametzhanova G5 Informatika, 2020 edition): the Atamura team's 2020 Grade 5 book follows the same five national sections in numbered-paragraph form (1.1–5.5) with end-of-section final tasks; its graphics strand works in Paint for raster and Inkscape for vector (canvas size, SVG, Bezier pen), and the introduction also promises a first taste of 3D technologies (3D model, 3D editor, 3D printing) inside the graphics work.

Kazakhstan's own way (Adekenova & Pryanichnikova G9 Informatics, NIS): the NIS Grade 9 course is built around databases and object-oriented programming: computer architecture with logic circuits, coding raster images and sound, single-table then multi-table databases in Access (forms, queries, reports), five lessons introducing OOP, nine lessons developing applications in an OOP environment (C#), and a thirteen-lesson capstone — choosing a problem, designing the interface, programming, making a video instruction, testing, documenting and evaluating the project.

Kazakhstan's own way (Ataeva & Vyshkova G8 Informatics, NIS): the NIS Grade 8 course is the most distinctive of the wave: vector graphics flows into geographic information systems (planning an excursion, making maps, visualising data in GIS), spreadsheets are used to solve logical problems, loops are taught through mathematics, physics, business and multimedia problems, and the year ends with a full project cycle — six stages, presentation, documentation, defence — plus a mini-project on artificial intelligence.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kobdikova G1 «Цифрлық сауаттылық»): the Arman-PV Grade 1 book teaches programming through Scratch from §6 (creating a project, the «Қош келдің, Наурыз!» project, saving/opening a project, a «Музыкалық аспаптар» project) where the Almatykitap book uses an unnamed game environment, and its safety section adds a lesson on unwanted contacts online.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukametzhanova G5 «Информатика» 2020 edition): the 2020 Atamura revision of the Grade 5 book is rebuilt on the renewed programme — the same five sections as the Almatykitap book (Book 18): presenting information with encryption and binary, raster and vector graphics with curves, robotics with the gyroscope sensor and turns, robot competitions (line following and robo-sumo), and computer safety moved to the end.
