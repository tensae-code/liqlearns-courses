# Patents — an exclusive right for an invention

## Do it first

An inventor spends five years on a machine. Without protection, the first factory to see it can copy it in a week. The patent is the bargain this lesson explains: exclusive rights for the inventor, published knowledge for everyone.

## Sub lesson 1 — The patent bargain

A **patent is an exclusive right granted for an invention**: **the owner decides how, or whether, others may use it** — and **in exchange the technical information is published in the patent document**, so knowledge grows even while use is controlled.

While it lasts, **others may not make, use or offer the product for sale**. The examples run wide: **software-related inventions (Airbnb, Amazon one-click, Google's PageRank)** to the **light bulb, telephone and Bluetooth** — but **not all software qualifies; it must meet the responsible authority's standards**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Applying in Ethiopia — and the guide's distinction (teacher guide — evidence)

In Ethiopia the authority is the **Ethiopian Intellectual Property Office**; **applications follow set procedures and can be processed online through its portal, eipo.gov.et**.

The guide (teacher guide — evidence) names the **Ethiopian Intellectual Property Authority** as the body handling all intellectual-property rights in Ethiopia, and keys the distinction pupils must reach: **a patent stops others making or selling an invention, while a trademark protects the words, phrases, symbols, logos or other devices that identify where goods or services come from**. Its group work sends pupils to the authority's site for the **requirements for getting a patent**, and to judge a set case — whether a named pupil's software can have patent right or not.
