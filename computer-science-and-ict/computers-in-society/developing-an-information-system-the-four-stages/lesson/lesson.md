# Developing an Information System — the Four Stages

## Do it first

Imagine your school decides to replace its paper attendance registers with a computer system. Nobody starts by typing code. First someone asks: do we really need this, and can we afford it? Then: what exactly must it record? Then: how will the screens and records look? Only then is it built. Those four questions, in that order, are this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Stage one and two — planning and analysis

**Stage 1: Planning.** The work starts by investigating the present situation: what problem is hurting, what the organisation hopes a system will fix, and whether a new system is **feasible** — possible with the money, people, time and technology actually available. Planning ends with a decision: go ahead, or not yet.

[Picture — two workers with clipboards — one asking a head teacher 'can we do this at all?', the other following a paper register through an office and drawing its path]

**Stage 2: Analysis.** The team studies the current way of working in detail — what data flows where, what goes wrong, what the users truly need — and writes down the **requirements**: exactly what the new system must do. Analysis answers the question *what*, never yet *how*. A requirement is a promise the finished system will be judged against.

## Sub lesson 2 — Stage three and four — design and implementation

**Stage 3: Design.** Now the *how* is drawn, as a blueprint: what data is stored and in what structure, what processes turn inputs into outputs, what the screens and printed reports look like, and how the parts connect. Nothing is built yet — design mistakes are cheap on paper and expensive in code.

[Picture — a blueprint drawing of a school attendance system slowly filling in with colour and becoming the real screens of the working system]

**Stage 4: Implementation.** The design is built — programs written or configured, hardware installed — then **tested** against the requirements from analysis, and the organisation **changes over** to the new system: data is moved across, people are trained, and the old way is retired. After changeover the system is operated and maintained, and what is learned feeds the next round of development.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: an information system is developed in four stages — planning asks if it is needed and feasible, analysis fixes the requirements (what it must do), design draws the blueprint (how), and implementation builds, tests and changes over to it. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
