## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **CAD — designing on the computer** — **CAD — computer-aided design** — is **drawing and designing a product on a computer** instead of on paper. The designer draws the part once, then **turns it, zooms it and changes it** on screen; a mistake costs an edit, not a new model. The finished design is exact numbers, ready to hand to the machines that will make the thing. Rwanda's guide files CAD under the application areas of computers (teacher guide — evidence).
2. **CIM — the computer runs the factory** — **CIM — computer-integrated manufacturing** — goes one step further: the **whole factory is linked by computer**, from the CAD design to the **machines that cut, weld and assemble**, to the **stock counts** of parts and finished goods. The design file itself can drive the machine, so what is made matches what was drawn. CAD designs the thing; CIM organises **making it, start to finish**.
