# Lesson plan — Computers in Shops and Supermarkets

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Computers in Society · **Lesson:** Computers in Shops and Supermarkets
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Afghanistan (2026-11-14) — in the title Computers in Society on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Afghanistan's *Computer* Grade 10, because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Shops and supermarkets use computers at the till and behind it: the till records each sale and prints the receipt, and the shop's database of products, prices and stock is updated by every sale.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Computers at the till — the point of sale** — In a shop the computer sits at the **point of sale (POS)** — the place where goods are paid for. The cashier scans the item's **barcode** or keys its code, and the computer looks the item up in its **database**, finds the price, and adds it to the bill. When everything is scanned, the computer totals the bill, works out the change, takes cash or card payment, and prints a **receipt**. The sale is recorded as it happens, so the money in the till can always be checked against the computer's record.
2. **The database behind the counter** — Every sale also changes the shop's **stock**: the database count of that item goes down by the number sold. Because the database holds every product, its price and how many are left, the shopkeeper can ask it questions — what is running low, what sold today, what to **reorder**. In a supermarket the same system runs on many tills at once, all sharing one database, which is why a price changed once in the office is right at every till. The computer has joined the till, the shelves and the storeroom into one information system.

2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
