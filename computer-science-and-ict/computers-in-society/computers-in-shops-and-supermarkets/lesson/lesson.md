# Computers in Shops and Supermarkets

## Do it first

Watch a cashier for one minute: every beep is a computer finding a price, adding a bill and remembering what was sold. In this lesson you learn how computers run a shop — at the till, and in the stock behind it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Computers at the till — the point of sale

In a shop the computer sits at the **point of sale (POS)** — the place where goods are paid for. The cashier scans the item's **barcode** or keys its code, and the computer looks the item up in its **database**, finds the price, and adds it to the bill. When everything is scanned, the computer totals the bill, works out the change, takes cash or card payment, and prints a **receipt**. The sale is recorded as it happens, so the money in the till can always be checked against the computer's record.

[Picture — a shop till with a barcode scanner, a screen showing prices and total, and a printed receipt]

## Sub lesson 2 — The database behind the counter

Every sale also changes the shop's **stock**: the database count of that item goes down by the number sold. Because the database holds every product, its price and how many are left, the shopkeeper can ask it questions — what is running low, what sold today, what to **reorder**. In a supermarket the same system runs on many tills at once, all sharing one database, which is why a price changed once in the office is right at every till. The computer has joined the till, the shelves and the storeroom into one information system.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Shops and supermarkets use computers at the till and behind it: the till records each sale and prints the receipt, and the shop's database of products, prices and stock is updated by every sale. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
