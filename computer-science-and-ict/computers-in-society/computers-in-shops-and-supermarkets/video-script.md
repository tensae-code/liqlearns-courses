# Video script — Computers in Shops and Supermarkets

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

In a shop the computer sits at the **point of sale (POS)** — the place where goods are paid for.
The cashier scans the item's **barcode** or keys its code, and the computer looks the item up in its **database**, finds the price, and adds it to the bill.
When everything is scanned, the computer totals the bill, works out the change, takes cash or card payment, and prints a **receipt**.
The sale is recorded as it happens, so the money in the till can always be checked against the computer's record.
Every sale also changes the shop's **stock**: the database count of that item goes down by the number sold.
Because the database holds every product, its price and how many are left, the shopkeeper can ask it questions — what is running low, what sold today, what to **reorder**.
In a supermarket the same system runs on many tills at once, all sharing one database, which is why a price changed once in the office is right at every till.
The computer has joined the till, the shelves and the storeroom into one information system.
