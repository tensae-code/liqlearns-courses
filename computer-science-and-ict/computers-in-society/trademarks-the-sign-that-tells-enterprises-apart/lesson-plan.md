# Lesson plan — Trademarks — the sign that tells enterprises apart

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Computers in Society · **Lesson:** Trademarks — the sign that tells enterprises apart
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 14 — Computers in Society of the Computer Science and ICT ladder (TITLES.md); Ethiopia Grade 12, Unit 1 — Information Systems and Their Applications. The ladder is the recommended order, never a forced one.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a trademark is a sign that distinguishes one enterprise's goods or services from another's — Meta, RIDE and Microsoft are the book's examples; its rights come from use, can last indefinitely, and registration adds advantages.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the intellectual-property lesson (recommended first, never forced) — the last of the four types, in the book's order.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What a trademark is** — a sign distinguishing one enterprise's goods or services from another's; Meta, RIDE, Microsoft; Ethiopian companies such as Ride hold registered trademarks.
2. **Rights from use — and registration's advantages** — unlike patents and copyrights, trademark rights come from use and can be held indefinitely; registration not required, but advantageous.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define a trademark and name its examples.
2. Say where trademark rights come from and how long they can last.
3. State registration's position: not required, advantageous.
