# Trademarks — the sign that tells enterprises apart

## Do it first

Two drinks, same colour bottle. One word on the label decides which one you trust. That word — that sign — is doing legal work. This lesson is about the sign itself: what it is, where its rights come from, and how long they can last.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a trademark is

A **trademark is a sign that distinguishes one enterprise's goods or services from another's** — the mark answers the buyer's question: whose is this?

**Meta, RIDE and Microsoft are the book's recognisable examples, and Ethiopian companies such as Ride also hold registered trademarks** — the institution is at work at home, not only abroad.

## Sub lesson 2 — Rights from use — and registration's advantages

**Unlike patents and copyrights, trademark rights come from use and can be held indefinitely** — a mark in honest use does not expire the way a patent's term does.

And **although registration is not required, it gives extra advantages** — the pattern of this whole family: the right can exist without the register, and the register still strengthens it.
