## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Seven kinds — from whole-copy to patchwork** — The book counts **seven kinds of plagiarism**. At the brazen end: submitting another's whole work as yours, and copying large parts without credit. In the middle: **patchwork** — stitching sentences from several sources into a 'new' text; changing a copied text's words just enough to disguise it; and reusing your own old work as if new. At the quiet end: quoting without quotation marks, and naming a source in the list while the borrowed sentences themselves carry no credit. The kinds differ in daring; the wrong is the same — the reader is told a lie about whose work this is.
2. **Detecting it — software, problems, prevention** — **Detection software** compares a text against vast libraries of books, journals and web pages and reports matching passages — the copied paragraph glows. The **problems** plagiarism brings reach beyond punishment: it steals the original maker's credit, it hollows the plagiarist's own learning (the work was the learning), and it poisons trust in every honest work beside it. **Prevention** is the writer's craft from the companion lessons: take notes in your own words, mark quotations as quotations, credit every source where it is used — and the detector becomes a checker you welcome, not a hunter you fear.
