## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Outsourcing — work sent out** — **Outsourcing** is work a company gives to people outside itself — often in another country — instead of hiring staff to do it in-house: design, programming, data entry, customer support. Companies outsource to find skills and lower costs; workers gain access to the whole world's employers. The meeting place is the **online job portal** — Bangladesh's book names its own, bdjobs and chakri.com, beside the international freelance marketplaces where clients post jobs and workers bid for them with profiles and past work as their shop window.
2. **Freelancing — and its downsides** — A **freelancer** works job by job for many clients, with no single employer: free to choose work and hours, and free to go unpaid between jobs. The book is honest about the downsides: income is **irregular**, there is **no job security** or paid leave, payment can arrive late or not at all, and the worker competes with the whole world on price. The skills that protect a freelancer are the village's skills — a strong profile, kept promises, clear communication, and work good enough that clients return.
