# Cybercrime — and defending your country's digital resources

## Do it first

A stolen password used to empty an account. A copied program sold as someone's own. A ministry website attacked from abroad. One word covers them all — this lesson defines it, then turns to the defence, including a career the book names.

## Sub lesson 1 — What cybercrime is

**Cybercrime is using a computer to further illegal ends** — ends such as **fraud, stealing identities, or violating privacy and intellectual property**.

It **has grown as computers became central to education, commerce, entertainment and government**: the more of life runs through computers, the more crime follows it there.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ethiopia's attacks — and the cyber warrior

The book reports, from Ethiopia's **Information Network Security Agency (INSA)**, that **attacks on Ethiopia are increasing** and need a **collaborative effort**. Pupils are to prepare as **mindful users protecting personal, organisational and national digital resources**.

And there is a path for the interested and skilled: becoming a **"cyber warrior" — a trained information-security professional who manages networks and systems**. The guide (teacher guide — evidence) adds the research route: **national cyber-attack reports from the designated offices** are the source for the common attack types and the defences against them.
