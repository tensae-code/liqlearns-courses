# The five components of an information system

## Do it first

Take the school system from the last lesson. Pull it apart in your mind: the machines, the programs, the marks themselves, the people, and the rules about who may change a mark. Five kinds of part. This lesson names all five — then walks your own school through them.

## Sub lesson 1 — The five components

Every information system has **five components**:

1. **Hardware** — the **digital devices** that **store, process and communicate data**, linked by **wired and wireless networks**.
2. **Software** — the **operating system, DBMS, applications, antivirus** and the rest of the programs.
3. **Data/information** — the organisation's **critical asset**, **captured from customers and suppliers** and **stored in a database** for reports.
4. **People** — the **trained specialists** who **run the technology** and **decide what new data to capture**.
5. **Procedure** — the **step-by-step policies** that say **what data is captured, who may see it and who may change it**.

[Picture 16112 — five components around a central database]

Remove any one and the system limps: machines with no procedure share secrets; people with no data decide blind.

## Sub lesson 2 — Our school as the example (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide works the pupils' **own school** end to end (teacher guide — evidence):

- **Data captured:** pupil and teacher **names, age, sex, grade/qualification, address, assessment and exam marks, attendance, facilities**.
- **Information generated:** **teachers by qualification**, **pupil-to-teacher and pupil-to-classroom ratios**, **absenteeism summaries**.
- **Decisions driven:** **build more classrooms** when the ratio beats the Ministry standard; **act on pupils with heavy absence**.

[Picture 16113 — a school: data in at the gate, report cards out]

Trace one line through it: an attendance register (data) becomes an absenteeism summary (information) becomes a decision to visit a pupil's home. That is a system earning its keep.
