## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Each country, its own Acts** — A **cyber law** is a country's law for acts done with computers and networks — computer misuse (breaking into systems), data protection, electronic transactions, copyright online, harmful content. Each country **makes its own**: Malaysia's chapter names its own Acts (such as the Computer Crimes Act and the Personal Data Protection Act) beside other countries' equivalents, with different scopes, penalties and definitions. There is no single world cyber law to learn — there are laws, plural, each stopping at its own border.
2. **The same act, different judgements** — Because the laws differ, the **same online act** can be **lawful in one country and an offence in another** — and a network act often touches several countries at once (the writer in one, the server in a second, the reader in a third). Malaysia's lesson for the user is practical caution: find out **whose law** you stand under before acting, obey the **stricter** where laws conflict, and remember that 'everyone does it' and 'it was only online' are defences in no country's Act. The chapter closes the ethics strand of the course: law is ethics with a border and a penalty.
