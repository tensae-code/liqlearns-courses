## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What employee monitoring is** — **Employee monitoring** is an organisation **watching and recording its workers' use of its systems** — which sites are visited, what mail is sent, sometimes which keys are pressed or which calls are made. Employers do it for real reasons: to protect the network from careless or malicious use, to keep work time for work, and to answer for what leaves the organisation in its name. The tools are the security chapter's own tools turned inward: logs, filters and reports on the organisation's own machines.
2. **The line — notice, purpose and proportion** — Monitoring has a **privacy line**. The worker is a person, not only a resource: monitoring done **in secret, without a stated purpose, or beyond what the purpose needs** treats the person as a suspect by default and breaks the trust work runs on. The book's balance, taught beside privacy law, is the standard one: the organisation should **tell workers what is monitored and why**, monitor **only what its stated purpose requires**, and use what it gathers **only for that purpose**. Security that forgets the person it protects has failed at the other side of its job.
