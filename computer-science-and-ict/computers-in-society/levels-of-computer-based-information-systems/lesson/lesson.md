# Levels of Computer-Based Information Systems

## Do it first

One organisation, several systems — each built for one level of deciding. Fiji's Year 13 book names the levels of computer-based information systems: the systems that serve the floor, the managers, the deciders and the directors. This lesson is the set: TPS, MIS, DSS and ESS.

## Sub lesson 1 — The working systems — TPS and MIS

The **Transaction Processing System (TPS)** serves the **operational level**: it records the day's transactions — sales, wages, orders — one by one, completely and accurately; the organisation's memory of what actually happened. The **Management Information System (MIS)** serves the **tactical level**: it takes the TPS's records and produces the **summaries and reports** managers run departments by — totals, comparisons, exceptions that stand out. Fiji's order is a food chain: the MIS can only summarise what the TPS faithfully kept.

In your mind's eye: the working systems — TPS and MIS.

## Sub lesson 2 — The deciding systems — DSS and ESS

The **Decision Support System (DSS)** serves managers facing **choices**: it lets them try *what if* — what if prices rise, what if we make chairs instead of tables — by working on the data with models, before the real decision is spent. The **Executive Support System (ESS/EIS)** serves the **top level**: the widest view, gathered from inside and outside the organisation, presented for directors who steer by it. Four levels of system for the book's three levels of management and its floor — the senior course's complete map of who is served by what.
