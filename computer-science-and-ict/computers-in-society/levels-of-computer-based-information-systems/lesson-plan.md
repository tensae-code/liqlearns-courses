## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The working systems — TPS and MIS** — The **Transaction Processing System (TPS)** serves the **operational level**: it records the day's transactions — sales, wages, orders — one by one, completely and accurately; the organisation's memory of what actually happened. The **Management Information System (MIS)** serves the **tactical level**: it takes the TPS's records and produces the **summaries and reports** managers run departments by — totals, comparisons, exceptions that stand out. Fiji's order is a food chain: the MIS can only summarise what the TPS faithfully kept.
2. **The deciding systems — DSS and ESS** — The **Decision Support System (DSS)** serves managers facing **choices**: it lets them try *what if* — what if prices rise, what if we make chairs instead of tables — by working on the data with models, before the real decision is spent. The **Executive Support System (ESS/EIS)** serves the **top level**: the widest view, gathered from inside and outside the organisation, presented for directors who steer by it. Four levels of system for the book's three levels of management and its floor — the senior course's complete map of who is served by what.
