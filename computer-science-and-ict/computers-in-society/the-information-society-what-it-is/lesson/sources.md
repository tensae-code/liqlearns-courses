**Acceptance count: taught in 2 countries (China, Mongolia).**

Placement choice: this lesson was built new for the ICT expansion (China, 2026-11-14) because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree was checked for the slug and for near matches before building. It sits in the Computers in Society title (computers-in-society/), following the book's own order.

China's own way: the Compulsory 2 book opens not with a machine but with society — Chapter 1 first surveys information technology and its applications, then steps back and asks what kind of society that technology has built, before any system is studied. The lesson keeps that order: origin, definition, features.

The two Teacher's Guides in China's set (to Compulsory 1 and to Compulsory 2) are record-only and are cited nowhere in this course: their library records are swapped against the student books (mismatched pairings) and both PDFs are partial (front portions only).

Mongolia's own way (Grade 9): Grade 9 turns outward to society and real tools — the information society taught through Mongolia's own shift, open-source software through Soyombo Linux (Mongolia's own Linux) and Mongolian OpenOffice, copyright through Mongolia's 2006 copyright law; algorithms are drawn as flowcharts and run in Turtle Art; the long spreadsheet unit grades pupils with nested IFs, builds Fibonacci sequences, grows a 500,000-tögrög bank deposit, and charts the depth and area of Mongolia's great lakes (Khuvsgul, Uvs, Khar-Us); e-services are the real Mongolian ones — the ТҮЦ service kiosk, bookstore.mn and internet banking.
