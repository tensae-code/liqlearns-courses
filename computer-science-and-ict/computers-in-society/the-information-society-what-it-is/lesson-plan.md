# Lesson plan — The Information Society — What It Is

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Computers in Society · **Lesson:** The Information Society — What It Is
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — China (2026-11-14) — in the title Computers in Society on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from China's *IT Compulsory 2*, because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** An information society is a society where making, processing and sharing information has become a main activity — information itself is a key resource, networks link people and organisations everywhere, and more and more of work, services and daily life runs through information systems.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the learner's own daily world — phones, networks and online services they already use — and on the First Steps lessons about what ICT is. No new machine skill is needed before it.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Where the idea came from** — Human societies have changed their centre before. In an **agricultural society**, most people worked the land and food was the key resource. In an **industrial society**, factories and machines took the centre, and energy and manufactured goods became the key resources. The book's story is that we have moved once more.
2. **What an information society is, and its basic features** — An **information society** is a society in which the creation, processing, storage and sharing of information is a central activity — information is treated as a key resource, the way land was in the agricultural age and energy in the industrial age.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say how the centre of society changed from the agricultural age to the industrial age to the information age.
2. Say what an information society is, in your own words.
3. Name the basic features of an information society: information as a key resource, information technology running through everything, growing information work, and information flowing fast and far.
4. Give one example from your own town of each feature.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Your own town, school and home are the examples.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Think of your grandparents' village or town when they were young. News travelled by letter and by word of mouth; a farmer sold what he grew at the market he could walk to; the bank was a building you visited. Now think of the same place today: prices checked on a phone, money sent in seconds, a lesson watched from another country. Something big changed — this lesson names that change. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.

## If the learner is stuck
Thinks an information society just means 'a place with computers' → computers are the tools, not the point: the point is that information itself became a key resource and a main kind of work. Confuses industrial and information ages → ask what most people in each age spent their day making: goods, or information?
