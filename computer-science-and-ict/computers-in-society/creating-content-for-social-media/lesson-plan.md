# Lesson plan — Creating Content for Social Media

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Computers in Society · **Lesson:** Creating Content for Social Media
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Indonesia (2026-11-14) — in the title Computers in Society on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Indonesia's Informatika books (Indonesia Grade 12 G12 Informatika student), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree and live lesson content were checked for the slug and for near matches before building.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** creating content for social media — making video, photo and written posts (blog and microblogging, with copywriting) that are made on purpose for the platform's people (Indonesia Grade 12).
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The maker's forms — video, photo, writing** — Social media content is made, not spilled. Indonesia's Grade 12 book teaches creative use of social media through its forms: **video** (short, made for sound-off first seconds and a phone screen), **photo** (one clear subject, chosen and framed on purpose), and **writing** — the **blog** post that develops an idea and the **microblog** post that must land in a few lines. Each form has its own craft, and the maker's first skill is choosing the form that fits the message instead of forcing every message into one.
2. **Copywriting — words that earn their place** — **Copywriting** is the writing that makes a post work: a first line that stops the thumb honestly, a body that gives the value it promised, and a close that tells the reader what to do next. Creative use, in the book's sense, is the opposite of noise: content made for a known audience, true to what it shows, and posted where its form belongs. The maker also owns the duties of the platform — credit for others' work, care with other people's images, and honesty about what is advertisement and what is not.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Choosing the form that fits the message — video, photo, blog or microblog
2. A phone screen — and first seconds that work even with the sound off
3. One clear subject, chosen and framed on purpose
4. The blog develops an idea; the microblog must land in a few lines

Paper and a pencil, and a computer with the program this lesson uses, if available.

2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
