**Acceptance count: taught in 9 countries (Ethiopia, China, Yemen, Laos, Greece, Bangladesh, Armenia, Indonesia, Kazakhstan).**

Yemen's own way (Grade 11): the book pivots from operating the machine to understanding information itself — data kinds, number systems and coding get full units before the applications (presentations, Excel) and a networks/Internet finale; every unit closes with a formal تقويم (assessment).

Book errors touching Unit 1, taught corrected and named here: the student book's figure numbering jumps from Figure 1.10 (e-banking) to Figure 1.13 (e-learning) — Figures 1.11 and 1.12 never appear; printed p. 19 prints \u201cFigureure 1.17\u201d for Figure 1.17; and \u201cstatment\u201d is misspelled in review headings throughout the book. The lessons teach from the pictured content and the correct spellings, and name the errors here rather than copying them.

Laos's own way (Grade 6 ICT student book): a theory-only foundation year — no hands-on computer practicals at all; ICT is introduced through Lao daily-life examples (TV remote, household appliances) and every chapter opens with objectives, guiding questions and a discussion activity (ກິດຈະກໍາ) before the exposition. The RIES ICT line runs ມ.1–ມ.7 (Grades 6–12).

Laos's own way (Grade 10 ICT student book): the upper-secondary course turns conceptual — a full information-systems syllabus (organisations, TPS/MIS/DSS/EIS/expert/office systems, the development life cycle), then the series' deepest security chapter (firewalls, cookies, spyware, spam, employee monitoring, privacy law), closing with artificial intelligence, with DOS and Unix taught alongside Windows in the senior years' background.

Greece's own way (Grade 7, Pliroforiki A' Gymnasiou, 2024): the new Gymnasium informatics course is built around algorithms from the first page (a Python binary example in Unit 1), then hardware/software, the internet with a full copyright/Creative Commons/AI-copyright sequence, cybersecurity with cryptography, word processing, algorithmics, Scratch storytelling/chatbot projects, and a long society unit (robots, drones, autonomous cars, e-government, AI in education/medicine).

Bangladesh's own way (Grade 6, Digital Technology): the Competency-based National Curriculum 2022 book is organised as nine project "Learning Experiences" done in groups through stories, games and fieldwork — no conventional chapters; even learners without a computer at home learn technology through experience around school and home. The NCTB Digital Technology line runs in parallel English and Bangla editions; placements are cited from the English Version with the Bangla Version parallel spot-checked.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 5 G5 student (Antares)): One of two different Grade 5 books in the set (this one by Velijanyan & Ayvazyan, Antares); it runs as numbered § lessons mixing information theory, careful judgement of fake vs true information, and a long practical spine — text editor, spreadsheets, algorithms and variables — ending in teamwork principles.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 7 G7 Informatika student): Kurikulum Merdeka Informatika built on four elements (Berpikir Komputasional, Literasi Digital, Analisis Data, Algoritma dan Pemrograman), mixing plugged and unplugged activities with coded activity IDs (e.g. VII-BK, VII-LD) and QR-linked resources; Grade 7 covers only BK + Literasi Digital + data handling.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 8 G8 Informatika student): Grade 8 deepens the four elements: spreadsheet data analysis (lookup, pivot), computational thinking through functions, sets and number systems, and programming that moves from Scratch to Blockly Games, procedural blocks and a physical Ozobot robot.


Kazakhstan's own way (Salgaraeva G9 «Информатика»): Grade 9 in the Arman-PV line is a maker's year: work with information (netiquette, shared documents), choosing and costing a real computer, databases built first in spreadsheets, then Python arrays, and a full PyGame 2D game — background, characters, sprite animation, keyboard control — built from a ready scenario.
