# From data to information — processed data with meaning — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Information is —
a) raw facts alone  b) data processed into a meaningful form for its users  c) a guess  d) a computer
**Answer: b) data processed into a meaningful form for its users** — processing is what adds the meaning.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** Daily sales figures grouped by week show —
a) nothing new  b) whether sales are rising or falling  c) the shopkeeper's name  d) next week's weather
**Answer: b) whether sales are rising or falling** — the book's own example.

**3. (Sub lesson 1)** Which questions turn data into information?
a) why and how much  b) who, what, when and where  c) which and whose  d) is it true
**Answer: b) who, what, when and where** — the four questions named in the book.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** The bottom of the processing ladder holds — (teacher guide — evidence)
a) complex models  b) sorting, summarising and averaging  c) guessing  d) printing
**Answer: b) sorting, summarising and averaging** — the ladder climbs from these to models.

**5. (Sub lesson 2)** The point of processing, in the guide's answer, is — (teacher guide — evidence)
a) more paper  b) a readable format — graphs, charts, documents  c) hiding the data  d) slower work
**Answer: b) a readable format — graphs, charts, documents** — readable formats are the point of the exercise.
