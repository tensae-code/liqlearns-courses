# Lesson plan — Marketing Your Talents — an Introduction to Marketing

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Computers in Society · **Lesson:** Marketing Your Talents — an Introduction to Marketing
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Kuwait (2026-11-14) — in the title Computers in Society on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Kuwait's student books (Ministry of Education, State of Kuwait), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree and live lesson content were checked for the slug and for near matches before building.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Marketing introduced through your own talent — making a product from what you do well, and advertising it so the right people hear
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Market your talents — from skill to product** — **Marketing** begins before any advertisement: it begins with a **talent** — a thing you do well that others value — shaped into a **product**: a defined offer a person can choose, with a name, a form and a price or exchange in view. Kuwait's order is the discipline: the Grade 7 pupil markets the designs the year has taught them to make, so the product is real before the advertising begins; marketing a thing that does not exist is only noise.
2. **Advertising — telling the right people** — The second half is the **telling**: an advertisement carries the product to the **people it is for** — saying what it is, what it does for them, and why this maker's version — in the places those people actually look. The lesson keeps the telling honest as a matter of craft: an advertisement promises what the product keeps. The year's design skills close the loop — the poster and publication lessons are how this product gets its face.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
