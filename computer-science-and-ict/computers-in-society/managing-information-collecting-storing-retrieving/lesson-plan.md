## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Collecting and storing — getting it and keeping it** — **Managing information** begins with **collecting**: gathering the data an organisation or a person needs, from its sources, completely and accurately — what is not collected cannot be used later. Then **storing**: keeping what was collected in an ordered place — files, records, a database — where it stays safe and findable. Collecting without storing scatters; storing without order buries. The book's pair is deliberate: get it, then keep it where a hand can reach it again.
2. **Processing, presenting and retrieving — making it usable and finding it again** — Stored data becomes useful by **processing** — sorting, calculating, summarising — and by **presenting**: showing the result in the form its user needs. And the test of the whole cycle is **retrieving**: asking for a piece of stored information and getting it back, quickly and exactly. A store that cannot be searched has failed at its job even if nothing was ever lost from it. Laos teaches managing as one cycle because each act earns its meaning from the next: we collect in order to retrieve.
