# Scientific Methods of Inquiry — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What are the method's working parts, in order?**
**Answer: Observation and question, hypothesis, test, and honestly recorded evidence**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What makes a hypothesis scientific?**
**Answer: It is stated so it could prove wrong — a test can refute it**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What must the test's result be able to do?**
**Answer: Support or refute the hypothesis — either outcome counts**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What does honest evidence include?**
**Answer: The results you did not hope for, recorded as carefully as the hoped-for ones**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) Where does Armenia place scientific methods?**
**Answer: Grade 10, Lesson 34 (Theme 24) — the closing lesson of the book**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What makes a test fair?**
**Answer: It changes one variable at a time and holds the rest still, as controls**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) Why must a test be repeatable?**
**Answer: So others can run it and get the same result — one unrepeatable result convinces nobody**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What is peer review?**
**Answer: Organised doubt — others review, repeat and challenge published methods and data**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) How is a bug report a scientific object?**
**Answer: It is a hypothesis — 'it fails when…' — waiting for its test**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) Name two computing homes of the same method.**
**Answer: A/B tests and benchmarks (and debugging)**
