# Human-Computer Interaction

## Do it first

A powerful program that its users cannot work is a failed program. Malaysia's third Tingkatan 4 chapter is devoted to the meeting point itself: human-computer interaction. In this lesson you learn what HCI is — and why a whole chapter stands on it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The meeting point

**Human-computer interaction (HCI)** is the study and design of **how people and computers meet**: the screens, controls and responses through which a person tells a computer what to do and understands what it has done. Every program has this meeting point whether it was designed or not — the question HCI asks is whether the meeting was **designed for the person** or merely happened. HCI draws on knowing people (how they see, remember, err) as much as knowing machines.

## Sub lesson 2 — Why HCI is needed

HCI is **needed** because the meeting decides whether the computer's power reaches the person: a system whose interaction is confusing is **unused or misused** — its correct workings locked behind a door its users cannot open, and its errors blamed on the users who were never given a fair chance. Malaysia's need is stated practically: systems are built **for** people — pupils, clerks, teachers — whose time, training and patience are the real limits. Designing the interaction is not decoration added to finished work; it is half the work, the half the user actually meets.
