# Human-Computer Interaction — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What is HCI?**
a) The study and design of how people and computers meet  b) Screens, controls and responses  c) Yes — designed or not  d) People — how they see, remember, and err
**Answer: a) The study and design of how people and computers meet**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) Through what does the meeting happen?**
a) Yes — designed or not  b) Screens, controls and responses  c) People — how they see, remember, and err  d) Whether it was designed for the person, or merely happened
**Answer: b) Screens, controls and responses**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) Does every program have a meeting point?**
a) People — how they see, remember, and err  b) Whether it was designed for the person, or merely happened  c) Yes — designed or not  d) The meeting decides whether the computer's power reaches the person
**Answer: c) Yes — designed or not**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) HCI draws on knowing what, besides machines?**
a) Whether it was designed for the person, or merely happened  b) The meeting decides whether the computer's power reaches the person  c) It is unused or misused — its workings locked behind a door users cannot open  d) People — how they see, remember, and err
**Answer: d) People — how they see, remember, and err**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) What is the question HCI asks of any meeting point?**
a) Whether it was designed for the person, or merely happened  b) The meeting decides whether the computer's power reaches the person  c) It is unused or misused — its workings locked behind a door users cannot open  d) The users' — their time, training and patience
**Answer: a) Whether it was designed for the person, or merely happened**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) Why is HCI needed, in one line?**
a) It is unused or misused — its workings locked behind a door users cannot open  b) The meeting decides whether the computer's power reaches the person  c) The users' — their time, training and patience  d) No — it is half the work, the half the user actually meets
**Answer: b) The meeting decides whether the computer's power reaches the person**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What happens to a confusing system?**
a) The users' — their time, training and patience  b) No — it is half the work, the half the user actually meets  c) It is unused or misused — its workings locked behind a door users cannot open  d) The users, who were never given a fair chance
**Answer: c) It is unused or misused — its workings locked behind a door users cannot open**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) Whose limits are the real limits?**
a) No — it is half the work, the half the user actually meets  b) The users, who were never given a fair chance  c) The study and design of how people and computers meet  d) The users' — their time, training and patience
**Answer: d) The users' — their time, training and patience**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) Is designing interaction decoration?**
a) No — it is half the work, the half the user actually meets  b) The users, who were never given a fair chance  c) The study and design of how people and computers meet  d) Screens, controls and responses
**Answer: a) No — it is half the work, the half the user actually meets**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) Who is blamed for a badly designed system's errors, wrongly?**
a) The study and design of how people and computers meet  b) The users, who were never given a fair chance  c) Screens, controls and responses  d) Yes — designed or not
