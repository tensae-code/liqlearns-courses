# Human-Computer Interaction — true-false

Say whether each statement is true or false.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "What is HCI?" is: The study and design of how people and computers meet**
**Answer: True**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "Through what does the meeting happen?" is: Yes — designed or not**
**Answer: False** — Screens, controls and responses

**3. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "Does every program have a meeting point?" is: Yes — designed or not**
**Answer: True**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "HCI draws on knowing what, besides machines?" is: Whether it was designed for the person, or merely happened**
**Answer: False** — People — how they see, remember, and err

**5. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "What is the question HCI asks of any meeting point?" is: Whether it was designed for the person, or merely happened**
**Answer: True**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "Why is HCI needed, in one line?" is: It is unused or misused — its workings locked behind a door users cannot open**
**Answer: False** — The meeting decides whether the computer's power reaches the person

**7. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "What happens to a confusing system?" is: It is unused or misused — its workings locked behind a door users cannot open**
**Answer: True**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "Whose limits are the real limits?" is: No — it is half the work, the half the user actually meets**
**Answer: False** — The users' — their time, training and patience

**9. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "Is designing interaction decoration?" is: No — it is half the work, the half the user actually meets**
**Answer: True**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "Who is blamed for a badly designed system's errors, wrongly?" is: The study and design of how people and computers meet**
**Answer: False** — The users, who were never given a fair chance
