## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What makes a thing news** — A **news item (une information d'actualité)** reports something that happened, recently, to someone, somewhere — and it can say who reports it, when, and where from. Around it swims everything else: opinion, advertising, entertainment, rumour, old news dressed as new. Spotting news is the first media skill: before believing or sharing, name what kind of thing you are looking at.
2. **The first checks** — A spotted news item gets first checks, fast: Is the source a real newsroom or a copy of one? Is there a date — and is it today's story or an old one recirculating? Do other sources report the same event, in their own words? Does the headline promise more than the text delivers? An item that passes is worth reading closely; one that fails has told you what it is.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Scroll in your mind through the last ten things you saw online. How many were news — something that happened, reported by someone whose job is reporting? Most people cannot say. This lesson teaches the spotting. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of what makes a thing news as the lesson describes it — the student studies the picture and names what each part shows.
   - ADD GIF: a GIF of the first checks in action — the student watches once, then says what changed and why.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the lesson's key names and definitions aloud, in the order the lesson gives them — the student listens once through, then reads the same lines aloud along with the voice. (Audio is a described slot in this build; no audio file is made.)
3. **Say it to your companion** — the companion block in lesson/lesson.md.
