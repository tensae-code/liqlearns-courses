# Lesson plan — Knowledge — information put into action

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Computers in Society · **Lesson:** Knowledge — information put into action
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 14 — Computers in Society of the Computer Science and ICT ladder (TITLES.md); Ethiopia Grade 11, Unit 1 — Information Systems and Its Applications. The ladder is the recommended order, never a forced one.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** knowledge is understanding gained by joining up information — it includes skill, and it splits into explicit knowledge you can share and tacit knowledge that lives in the knower.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Information lesson just before it (recommended first, never forced).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 2 seconds, looping.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Knowledge — understanding that can act** — the bicycle example: being told how is information, riding is knowledge; how knowledge is built.
2. **Explicit and tacit knowledge (teacher guide — evidence)** — the guide's split the student book never makes: shareable knowledge in words and formulas, and the larger unshareable kind in the knower's hands.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what knowledge is: understanding from joined-up information, including skill.
2. Work the bicycle example: told how (information) versus riding (knowledge).
3. Name how knowledge is built: reading, doing activities, discussing, observing.
4. Tell explicit from tacit knowledge and give the driving example (teacher guide — evidence).

## If the learner is stuck
Thinks knowledge is just stored information → return to the bicycle: the explanation was complete information, and the learner still could not ride. Knowledge is what lets a person act. Thinks tacit knowledge can be emailed → it lives in the knower and passes by working together; only the explicit kind travels in documents.
