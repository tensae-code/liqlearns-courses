# Knowledge — information put into action

## Do it first

Someone explains, very clearly, how to ride a bicycle: sit, pedal, balance, steer. You understand every word. Now sit on the bicycle. Something is still missing — and that missing thing is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Knowledge — understanding that can act

**Knowledge** is **understanding gained by processing and joining up different sets of information** — and it **includes skill**. Being **told how to ride a bicycle is information**; **actually riding it is knowledge**.

Knowledge is built from **reading, doing activities, discussing and observing**. It is the **personal capability to think and act to solve a problem or finish a task** — information you can stand on and use.

## Sub lesson 2 — Explicit and tacit knowledge (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide splits knowledge into two kinds the student book does not separate (teacher guide — evidence):

- **Explicit knowledge** — the kind that **can be put into words, diagrams and formulas** and **shared through documents and websites**.
- **Tacit knowledge** — the larger, **unshareable** kind that **lives in the knower**, is **tied to skill**, is **gained by applying information in action**, and can only be **passed on by working together**.

[Picture 16106 — two knowledge boxes: a written formula, and a driver's hands on a wheel]

The guide's worked example of the tacit kind is **driving a car well**: no document holds it; it lives in the driver's hands, and a learner gains it by driving alongside, not by reading alone.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what knowledge is, with the bicycle example. Then sort two things — a written formula and driving well — into explicit and tacit, and say why (teacher guide — evidence).

## Do It Yourself

1. Choose a skill you have — cooking, a game, a craft. Write what part of it is explicit (you could write it down) and what part is tacit (it lives in your hands).
2. Teach someone one small piece of information. Then watch: what do they still need before it becomes knowledge? Name it.
3. List the last thing you learned under its building way: reading, doing, discussing or observing.
