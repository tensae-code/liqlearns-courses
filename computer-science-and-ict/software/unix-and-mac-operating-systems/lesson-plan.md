## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Unix — the multi-user ancestor** — **Unix** is the **older, powerful operating system** built for **many users and many tasks at once** on one machine — the opposite pole from DOS's one user, one program. It works largely by the **command line**, is famous for stability on servers and large machines, and is the **ancestor** of much that followed: Linux was made in its image (the guide names GNU/Linux beside it), and its ideas — files in a tree, small tools joined together, permissions for each user — run through modern systems everywhere.
2. **The Mac system — Apple's own way** — The **Macintosh (Mac)** system is **Apple's operating system for its own computers**: a graphical system of windows and mouse from its early days, made to work only with Apple's machines, and known for its design and its use in publishing, picture and sound work. The guide places Mac beside Unix deliberately — the modern Mac system is itself built on a Unix heart, so the lesson's pair is also a family: the ancestor, and the polished descendant that hides the ancestor's command line under glass.
