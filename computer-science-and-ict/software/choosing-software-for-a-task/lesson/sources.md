Provenance scope note: the placements stocked here are from Kazakhstan's okulyk.kz (OKULYK) textbooks, recommended by the Ministry of Education and Science of the Republic of Kazakhstan — the Digital Literacy and Informatika lines of the Almatykitap, Arman-PV and Atamura teams and the Nazarbayev Intellectual Schools (NIS) books, all student books except the two Kadirkulov teacher guides named in the entries below, whose placements are stocked exactly as filed. Many books exist in parallel language editions (Kazakh / Russian / Uzbek / Uyghur / English) of the same textbook: each distinct book is taught once, and every distinct edition is named and cited in the book entry. Kazakhstan's own ways are kept: one textbook line in up to five parallel language editions, the okulyk.kz digital library as the books' home, Scratch from Grade 1 and robotics strands (LEGO WeDo 2.0 and Mindstorms EV3) from Grade 1–2, and every keyword given in Kazakh–Russian–English in the early grades. Media slots in lesson/lesson.md are described slots only ([Picture — …]); no picture numbers are invented.


Kazakhstan's own way (Kolyeva G9 Informatika): the Mektep Grade 9 book is the Google-cloud book: joint work on documents and presentations in Google services with shared-access settings and network etiquette, a practical chapter on choosing and pricing a computer and its software, databases built inside spreadsheets, then Python lists (search, replace, sort, insert/delete, two-dimensional) and 2D games with PyGame.

Kazakhstan's own way (Yerzhanov G9 Informatics, English-medium): the English-medium twin of the Mektep Grade 9 book — the same four chapters (computer literacy with netiquette and pricing a computer, database basics with pivot tables and a "family shop" mini-project, Python lists, PyGame 2D games ending in a Program Arcade Game) taught in English with trilingual terminology.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukhametzhanova G9 Informatika): the Atamura Grade 9 book covers the same ground as the Mektep book (information properties and joint document work, choosing a computer, databases, Python lists, PyGame) but in longer, deeper lessons — each topic runs seven to ten pages instead of two to four.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G9 Informatika): Almatykitap's Grade 9 book splits sorting and filtering into separate lessons plus an advanced-filter lesson, gives data input/output in one-dimensional arrays its own lesson, and has the fullest PyGame part of the wave — seven lessons ending in a complete "Footballer" game with sprite-collision detection.

Kazakhstan's own way (Shaniyev team G9 «INFORMATICS»): the Astana-kitap Grade 9 English-medium book is where Kazakhstan teaches databases properly — design, build, sort/filter, pivot tables and charts, ending in a "family shop" mini project — and then spends two chapters on Python lists (one- and two-dimensional, with search/swap/sort/insert/delete) and a full PyGame 2D games chapter.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukametzhanova G9 «Информатика»): the Atamura Grade 9 book works with Google services for collaborative documents, builds the database inside a spreadsheet, and its database section separates searching methods from sorting and filtering before the Python arrays and PyGame chapters.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kolyeva G9 «Информатика»): the Mektep Grade 9 book is the compact version of the state programme — five chapters only, with the "choosing a computer" chapter separating configuration, software choice and the final choice itself, and the PyGame chapter compressed into three double units.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G9 «Информатика»): the Almatykitap Grade 9 book gives Google Drive collaborative editing its own unit, splits sorting from filtering and adds an advanced-filter unit in the database section, separates array input/output and the "main parameters" of a two-dimensional array into their own units, and ends the PyGame chapter with sprite collision detection and a football game.
