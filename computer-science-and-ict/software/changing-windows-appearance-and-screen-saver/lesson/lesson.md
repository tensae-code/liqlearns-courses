# Changing Windows Appearance and Screen Saver

## Do it first

Leave a computer alone and watch its screen. After a while the picture may start to move on its own. In this lesson you learn who ordered that — you did, in the appearance settings — and how the whole look of Windows is changed there.

## Sub lesson 1 — The general appearance of Windows

Windows does not have to look the way it arrived. Its **general appearance** — the picture behind the icons (the **background**), the colours of windows and bars, and the size of what is shown — is changed in the appearance settings, reached through the Control Panel. Changing the appearance changes nothing about how programs work: it dresses the same Windows in a look the person at the computer chose, which is why Sudan's book teaches it early, before the tools are used for real work.

## Sub lesson 2 — The screen saver

A **screen saver** is a moving picture or pattern that covers the screen when the computer is left alone for a set number of minutes. It was born to protect old screens from a still picture burning into them, and it still guards the screen — and hides your work from passing eyes. You **set** the screen saver in the same appearance settings: choose the saver, choose how many minutes of idleness start it. Touching the mouse or a key wakes the screen and brings your work back exactly as you left it.
