**Acceptance count: taught in 5 countries (Saudi Arabia, Cuba, Bhutan, Rwanda, Kazakhstan).**

Placement choice: this lesson was built new for the ICT expansion (Saudi Arabia, 2026-11-14) because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree was checked for the slug and for near matches before building. The placements file named this slug directly (NEW LESSON NEEDED — software/the-desktop-taskbar-and-start-menu). It sits in the Software title (software/), following the book's own order. The Grade 4 guide's placement for the same topic was filed as software/the-desktop-icons-taskbar-and-start-menu (NEW LESSON NEEDED); it is stocked here, in the lesson built under the student book's slug, with its original slug recorded in its provenance row, as guide evidence. The Grade 4 guide citation is PARTIAL — Term 1 only.

Saudi Arabia's own way (Grade 3, continuing education): a workbook-style book for the continuing-education (adult/lifelong learning) track — every one of the 16 lessons opens with "I want to learn" objectives and closes with written exercises, and everything is taught on an older Windows desktop (Start menu, "My Computer", Recycle Bin) with Notepad as the only application, worked through step-by-step practicals in the school lab.

Saudi Arabia's own way (Grade 4, Digital Skills): the primary Digital Skills course (Binary Logic) is built as three units per term with the same guide pattern as the intermediate books (outcomes, important points, steps, answers, unit project, "Test Yourself"), files on the Ain platform, and a strong Arabic/English bilingual-typing strand — pupils switch the keyboard between Arabic and English with Alt+Shift and learn text direction, hamza and Arabic punctuation on the keyboard alongside Word skills; Scratch is introduced in Term 1 through algorithms from daily life. This citation is PARTIAL — Term 1 only. (teacher guide — evidence)

Cuba's own way (Grade 2): Play-based (lúdico) introduction through the graficador (Paint-type drawing program) and educational software; correct workstation posture/ergonomics is taught explicitly in Grade 2, and files/folders are introduced intuitively through saving and opening drawings in a class-group folder. (teacher guide — evidence)

Cuba's own way (Grade 5): The Grade 5 programme is built around extracting information from Cuban educational software, encyclopedias and EcuRed into word-processor documents, then Scratch — drawing/animation first (Unit 3), control/movement/appearance blocks in Unit 4. (teacher programme — evidence)

Cuba's own way (Grade 5): Same Grade 5 content as the Programa, recast as per-unit teaching tables with hour allocations; Unit 4 is renamed “Aprendiendo y creando” and taught purely as Scratch control, movement and appearance blocks. (teacher guide — evidence)

Cuba's own way (Grade 5): Student text mirrors the programme in four chapters; word-processing is taught as a research workflow (synonyms, symbols, searching, copying texts and images from sources), and Scratch is introduced through its paint editor before movement/appearance/control blocks.

Cuba's own way (Grade 6): Grade 6 adds the three productivity firsts in one year — digital presentations, Scratch animation with event/control blocks, and spreadsheets with formulas and charts — closing with a TIC chapter on searching and processing digital information; computer generations are taught inside Chapter 1.

Bhutan's own way (Class IV, Literacy with ICT student book): Competency-based "Literacy with ICT" (not procedure drill): every chapter opens with learning objectives, mixes off-machine "Try This" with "Try This On Machine", and closes with Now You Know / Check Your Progress / Explore Further; content is localised (Bhutanese names, kuenselonline, education.gov.bt, Bhutan's 1999 Internet start) and one class per week across the year.

Rwanda's own way (S1 ICT Student Book, KLB): competency-based units each opening with a Key Unit Competency and keywords; a full GIS/ArcGIS unit and a Scratch music-making unit sit inside the first secondary year, and ICT use is grounded in Rwandan daily life.

Rwanda's own way (S1 ICT Teacher Guide, KLB): the guide mirrors the student units with content maps, additional teacher content per unit, SEN/multi-ability support, sample lesson plans (Appendix 1) and a glossary (Appendix 2); assessment is framed by Rwanda's competence-based curriculum. (teacher guide — evidence)


Kazakhstan's own way (Salgaraeva & Ilyasova G6 Informatika): the Arman-PV Grade 6 book runs the full 2018 programme in numbered paragraphs with a test after every part: systems and networks, presenting information (transmission, encryption, binary coding of text), vector graphics with a "Zhailau" drawing practicum, then the two-part game build in the Scratch environment, and a final Word part that adds references, copyright and plagiarism, a referat mini-project and the game document.

Kazakhstan's own way (Salgaraeva G6 «Информатика»): the Arman-PV Grade 6 book is a game-making year wrapped in document skills: ergonomics and wireless networks first, then binary coding and encryption, vector graphics (drawing a «Жайлау» summer pasture), a Scratch game designed, built and given a sound track and title screen — and the year ends with footnotes, hyperlinks, copyright and plagiarism, a table of contents and the written documentation for the game.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G6 «Информатика»): the Almatykitap Grade 6 book follows the same state programme as the Arman-PV book (Book 24) — ergonomics, wireless networks, footnotes and hyperlinks, copyright, contents, report — but swaps vector graphics for a whole 3D-printing section in a SketchUp-style editor (cone, cylinder and sphere models, print setup) and swaps the Scratch game for a first Python course: IDE, alphabet and syntax, data types, arithmetic expressions, number input and output, linear algorithms.
