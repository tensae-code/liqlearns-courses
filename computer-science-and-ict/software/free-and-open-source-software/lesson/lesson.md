# Free and Open-Source Software

## Do it first

The licence lesson names licence types; this lesson meets the movement behind one of them. Mongolia's Grade 9 book gives open-source software its own section — taught proudly, because Mongolia has its own distribution in the story.

## Sub lesson 1 — Free software and the GPL — freedom with a guard

**Free software** is software the user may run, study, share and improve — the freedom is in the rights, not the price. Its guard is the **GPL** (General Public License): a licence that grants those freedoms and defends them, requiring that shared and improved versions keep the same freedoms — freedom that cannot be quietly taken away downstream. **Open-source** names the same world by its visible side: the source code stands open for any programmer to read and better.

In your mind's eye: free software and the GPL — freedom with a guard.

## Sub lesson 2 — The free programs — and Soyombo, Mongolia's own

The world the licence built is standing software, not theory: **Linux**, the free operating system; **OpenOffice**, the free office suite — in Mongolia, **Mongolian OpenOffice**; **GIMP** for images, **Inkscape** for drawing, **Blender** for 3D. Mongolia's own flag in this world is **Soyombo Linux** — a Mongolian Linux distribution, named for the Soyombo symbol, made so the free system speaks Mongolia's language and serves Mongolia's users. The lesson's pride is the point: open source is not only received by Mongolia; Mongolia builds in it.

In your mind's eye: the free programs — and Soyombo, Mongolia's own.
