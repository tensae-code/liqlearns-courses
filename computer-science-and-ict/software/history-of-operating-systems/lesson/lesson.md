# History of Operating Systems

## Do it first

The operating system was invented — in stages, each solving the previous stage's pain. This lesson walks the stages.

## Sub lesson 1 — From hand-loading to Unix — the first generations

The first computers had **no operating system**: operators loaded programs by hand, one at a time, and the machine idled while humans worked. The first systems were **batch monitors** (1950s–60s) that queued jobs automatically. **Time-sharing** systems (1960s–70s) let many users interact at once, and **Unix** (1969) set the lasting pattern — small tools, files for everything, portability across machines — from which Linux and, indirectly, today's mobile systems descend. Armenia's Grade 9 book closes its operating-system block with this history (§14).

## Sub lesson 2 — The graphical and mobile age — systems for everyone

Personal computers brought systems for one person: **MS-DOS** (1981, typed commands), then the **graphical** systems — Macintosh (1984) and Windows — where windows, icons and a mouse replaced command lines, and the OS became something a family could use. **Linux** (1991) proved a system could be built openly by a world of volunteers. Phones then shrank the system again: **iOS (2007) and Android (2008)** rebuilt it around touch, apps and sensors. Each generation's system answers its hardware's question: scarce machine time, then one person's desk, then everyone's pocket.
