**Acceptance count: taught in 7 countries (Ethiopia, Egypt, Fiji, Bhutan, Sri Lanka, Indonesia, Pakistan).**

Two book faults touch this lesson and are taught corrected. The student book's printed p. 43 carries one of its stray character runs — typesetting faults that appear throughout the book; they carry no meaning and are taught as nothing. The teacher guide spells this section's word as “Deriver” throughout its Unit 3 (contents, unit table, §3.3 heading and competency); the lesson teaches the correct word, **driver**, as the student book prints it.

Egypt's own way (Grade 7, Term 1 student book): One chapter ("Digital Programs and Applications") of 10 self-assessed lessons, each opening with "I can…" objectives and closing with Learn-by-doing; national platforms are the examples throughout — Digital Egypt platform, InstaPay, IDSC — and a separate Arabic activity booklet mirrors every lesson.

Fiji's own way (Year 11 Computer Education student book): The first locally written text of its kind (the book's Preface): written by a panel of Computer Studies officers and University of the South Pacific lecturers for the new Year 11 Computer Education syllabus trialled in 2014; each chapter uses "Discovery Learning" prompts, and the database chapter is a full Access 2007 practical building a Student database in a "Lab Chapter 3" folder.

Bhutan's own way (Class VII, Literacy with ICT student book): Social media is taught hands-on — learners create a Facebook account, post with audience settings, and follow netiquette and an Acceptable Use Policy; backup is taught to Google Drive, and the end-of-year experience is shared on Facebook.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 12 Science for Technology, Tamil): the A/L technology-stream ICT volume teaches every office skill twice — once in Microsoft Office 2010 and once in LibreOffice (Writer/Calc/Impress 4.1) — and closes with creating a real Gmail account, mailbox folders, attachments and password safety; hardware goes down to cache levels L1/L2/L3, ROM types and file systems (FAT/NTFS/ext4).

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 12 G12 Informatika student): Grade 12 (original Kurikulum Merdeka edition) is built around making things: single-board computers/controllers and a full Arduino C course (IDE, UnoArduSim simulator, sensors, S.O.S. LED and melody projects), then networks with cyber security and data governance, social impact with the UU ITE law, and a cross-field group project (smart trash bin / waste-bank apps) with journals, reports and peer assessment.


Pakistan's own way (Grade 6 Computer Science 6): every unit opens with Students Learning Outcomes, is dotted with "Do you Know?", "Extra Bit!" and "Tips" boxes, and closes with Summary, MCQ/short/detailed exercises, project-based and activity-based questions, with an answers key at the back. Digital skills (Windows desktop, file management, Paint 3D, a typing tutor) are taught hands-on BEFORE programming; digital citizenship is framed through Pakistani citizenship first; the entrepreneurship unit teaches from Pakistani founders (Foodpanda/EatOye, Shopsy.pk, Zero Makeup, Investors Lounge).
