# Lesson plan — The Control Panel and Its Settings

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Software · **Lesson:** The Control Panel and Its Settings
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Afghanistan (2026-11-14) — in the title Software on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Afghanistan's *Computer* Grade 12, because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The Control Panel is Windows' settings workshop: from its one window you reach the settings for the display, the mouse, the keyboard, sound, dates, user accounts, and adding or removing programs.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the Afghanistan book's own order — it follows the book's sections in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. ## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The Control Panel — one door to all settings** — The **Control Panel** is the place Windows gathers **settings** — the choices that decide how the computer itself behaves and looks. Open it from the Start menu, and its window shows a set of icons (or categories), one per area of the machine: display, mouse, keyboard, sound, date and time, printers, user accounts, and more. Afghanistan's Grade 12 book teaches the Control Panel as its own lesson, late in the course, because by now the learner has met many of its rooms one by one — a language added here, a program removed there — and is ready to see the whole workshop at once: nothing about the computer's own setup lives anywhere else.
2. **What its settings cover** — Walk the workshop: **Display** sets the wallpaper, screen saver and resolution; **Mouse** and **Keyboard** set those tools' behaviour; **Date and Time** keeps the clock true; **Sounds** chooses what the machine says and how loudly at the system level; **User Accounts** makes and guards the people's accounts; **Add or Remove Programs** installs and removes software — the correct way to remove a program, never by deleting its folder. The workshop's one rule is respect: a setting is a lever on the whole machine, so change one thing at a time, remember what it was, and put it back if the change was wrong. A learner who knows the Control Panel does not fear a strange computer: every unfamiliar machine is adjusted from the same room.
