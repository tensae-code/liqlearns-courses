# Working with the Start Menu

## Do it first

One small button in the corner opens every program on the computer — and also turns the computer off properly. In this lesson you learn the Start menu, part by part.

## Sub lesson 1 — Opening the Start menu and its programs

Click the **Start button** at the left end of the taskbar (or press the Windows key) and the **Start menu** opens. Its left side lists programs: **All Programs** opens the full list of everything installed — Word, Paint, games and the rest — some of them grouped in folders that open as you point. Above the list, Windows keeps the programs you use most, so your common work is one click away. Clicking a program's name opens it; the menu closes itself behind it.

[Picture — the Windows XP Start menu open — programs on the left, places and settings on the right]

## Sub lesson 2 — The rest of the Start menu — and shutting down

The menu's right side reaches the rest of your computer: **My Documents** and **My Computer** for your files and drives, **Control Panel** for settings, **Help and Support**, and **Search** for finding things. At the bottom sit the two leaving buttons: **Log Off**, which ends your session but leaves the computer on for the next person, and **Turn Off Computer**, which shuts Windows down safely. Always leave by Turn Off Computer — never by pulling the power — so Windows can close every program and save its own work first.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: The Start menu is Windows XP's front door: from the Start button you reach every program, your documents and settings, help, search — and the safe way to shut the computer down. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
