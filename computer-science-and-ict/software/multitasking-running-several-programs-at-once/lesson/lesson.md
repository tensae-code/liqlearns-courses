# Multitasking — Running Several Programs at Once

## Do it first

A pupil's real work never lives in one program. Mongolia's Grade 8 book names the environment modern work happens in — several programs alive at once under a multitasking system — and teaches its traffic rules.

## Sub lesson 1 — Multitasking — many programs, one machine

A **multitasking operating system** runs **several programs at once**: each keeps its window, its work and its place, and the system shares the machine's time among them so swiftly that all seem alive together. Of the open windows, one is **active** — it takes the keyboard and stands in front; the rest are **inactive** — present, holding their work, waiting. The environment has a discipline the book makes explicit: inactive is not closed, and a program's work survives untouched while the user works elsewhere.

## Sub lesson 2 — Switching and carrying — Alt+Tab, and Paint into Word

The traffic between programs has its instruments. **Alt+Tab** is the switch: hold, tap, choose — the windows cycle until the wanted one takes the front. Work itself travels by **copying between programs**: the book's own journey is a **picture drawn in Paint, copied, and pasted into a Word document** — one program's product becoming another's citizen, the clipboard the ferry. Mongolia's closing rule for the multi-program worker: name your active window before you type, and know which program owns the work in your hands — most multi-program accidents are one program receiving another's commands.
