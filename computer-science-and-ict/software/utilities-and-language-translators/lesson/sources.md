Book error taught corrected: in the student book's §3.2 (printed p. 42) all three named utility families are lettered "A." instead of A/B/C. The lesson teaches the families by their names, in the book's order, without the book's letters — the families themselves are exactly as the book lists them. Sub lesson 4's explanations are teacher-guide evidence: the student book lists the utilities by name, and the guide's keyed answer to Activity 3.1 explains each one; the explanations are filed here as the guide gives them.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Grade 9): one TIA teacher guide carries two ICT subjects side by side — Communication Technology (networks, ergonomics, safety, societal impact) and Computer Technology (architecture, software) — organised by content standards and numbered benchmarks (9.4.x / 9.5.x), with the design brief used as the problem-solving method for both a product and a simple network, and typing assessed at 20 wpm with 80% accuracy. (teacher guide — evidence, PARTIAL)

Fiji's own way (Year 11 Computer Education student book): The first locally written text of its kind (the book's Preface): written by a panel of Computer Studies officers and University of the South Pacific lecturers for the new Year 11 Computer Education syllabus trialled in 2014; each chapter uses "Discovery Learning" prompts, and the database chapter is a full Access 2007 practical building a Student database in a "Lab Chapter 3" folder.

Fiji's own way (Year 13 Computer Education student book): The senior book is built around a Fijian case organisation (Viti Group of Companies, a Fiji furniture manufacturer) for information systems; it teaches the full systems life cycle phase by phase, web design with real HTML and CSS, and text programming in C++ with worked programs (pay calculation, grade from mark, triangle and circle areas) using Fijian student names.

Bhutan's own way (Class VII, Literacy with ICT student book): Social media is taught hands-on — learners create a Facebook account, post with audience settings, and follow netiquette and an Acceptable Use Policy; backup is taught to Google Drive, and the end-of-year experience is shared on Facebook.

Mongolia's own way (Grade 11): a deep, engineering-style systems book (CPU registers, disk access-time calculations) that turns practical fast — pupils price up a computer from Mongolian web shops (pc-mall.mn, bsb.mn, itzone.mn); the web chapter builds a real "Our School" site and an "Animals of the Mongolian Red Book" page; programming is Microsoft Small Basic, and its loops-and-graphics work draws Mongolian ornament — the alkhan khee meander, the khaan buguivch knot and the ulzii endless knot — with code; the book closes by comparing every Small Basic construct with Python and C side by side.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 10 G10 student): The senior-school series (Stepanyan, Torosyan, Martirosyan, Amirkhanyan, Matinyan) is built on four ideas around digital tools and collaboration; Grades 10–12 switch the programming language to C++ and go deep: Boolean logic, translators, types, code quality, debugging, procedures, recursion, divide-and-conquer, testing, development stages, AI and data.


Pakistan's own way (Grade 6 Computer Science 6): every unit opens with Students Learning Outcomes, is dotted with "Do you Know?", "Extra Bit!" and "Tips" boxes, and closes with Summary, MCQ/short/detailed exercises, project-based and activity-based questions, with an answers key at the back. Digital skills (Windows desktop, file management, Paint 3D, a typing tutor) are taught hands-on BEFORE programming; digital citizenship is framed through Pakistani citizenship first; the entrepreneurship unit teaches from Pakistani founders (Foodpanda/EatOye, Shopsy.pk, Zero Makeup, Investors Lounge).
