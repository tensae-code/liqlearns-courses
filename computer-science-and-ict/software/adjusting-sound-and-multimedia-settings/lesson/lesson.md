# Adjusting Sound and Multimedia Settings

## Do it first

The video is playing but nothing is heard — is it broken, or just set wrong? In this lesson you learn the computer's sound settings and how to adjust them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Sound — output, input, and volume

A computer's sound has two directions: **output** — what you hear through speakers or headphones — and **input** — what a microphone records. Between them sits the **volume** control, and most sound troubles live there, not in broken hardware: the volume is down, the sound is **muted**, the wrong device is chosen, or a plug sits in the wrong socket. Afghanistan's Grade 11 book teaches this in the operating-system chapter for a reason: adjusting the machine's media settings is a system skill, the same family as adding a language or a program — the learner is expected to run the machine, not only use its programs.

## Sub lesson 2 — Adjusting the settings

The everyday adjustment is the **Volume Control**: open it from the taskbar's speaker icon or the Control Panel, and its sliders set the master volume and the balance and level of each source — the wave sound, the microphone, the CD or line in. Raise the master first, check **Mute** is off, then set each source so nothing drowns the rest. In the fuller sound settings you also choose the **playback and recording devices** when a machine has more than one, and assign the sounds Windows itself makes. The troubleshooting order the lesson teaches: plug, power, mute, volume, device — from the wall socket to the settings, in that order, and most silent computers speak before you reach the end.
