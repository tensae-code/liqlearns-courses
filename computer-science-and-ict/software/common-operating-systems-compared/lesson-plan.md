**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Software · **Lesson:** Common Operating Systems Compared
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Libya (2026-11-14) — in the title Software on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). **The one thing this lesson teaches:** The common operating systems a computer may run, named and compared by what each one is and where it is used
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. ## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The common operating systems — the named set** — The **common operating systems** are the ones a learner will actually meet: **Microsoft Windows**, the system of most school and office personal computers; **Linux**, the free, open system found on servers, school machines and programmers' computers; and the other widely used systems the book names alongside them. Each does the same manager's jobs — starting the machine, running programs, managing files and devices — so the comparison is never 'which works' but 'which fits'.
2. **Comparing them — same jobs, different fits** — Operating systems are compared on the things a user feels: **what machines they run on**, **what they cost** (Windows is sold; Linux is free), **who may change them** (open-source code may be studied and altered; closed code may not), and **what programs and support** surround them. Libya's point in comparing, not just listing, is judgement: the right system for a school lab, a server and a home computer may be three different answers, each right for its place.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
