**Acceptance count: taught in 4 countries (Ethiopia, Tanzania, Yemen, Iraq).**

Yemen's own way (Grade 10): a skills ladder — every unit alternates short teaching topics with a named تدريب (guided training) and نشاط (activity), and Windows itself is taught as a school subject for a full unit before any application is opened.

Sub lesson 4 is teacher-guide evidence — the keyed answer to the student book's group activity asking whether traffic lights and ATMs need software. Its statement that the vast majority of ATMs run Windows XP (Professional or Embedded) is the guide's own 2022-era wording, taught as printed, as the course's record of what the book teaches — not as a current claim about today's ATMs. The student book's printed p. 43 also carries one of its stray character runs; they are typesetting faults and are taught as nothing.

The Grade 9 teacher guide's closing summary for this part (printed pp. 39-40) introduces a description of system software with the words "while application software is..." — the two labels are swapped in that sentence. The lesson teaches the frame corrected: application software is what the user works with directly; system software keeps the hardware working and provides the interface.

Iraq's own way (Grade 11): the final preparatory year is the most forward-looking of the series — smart devices and the Internet of Things (with a lesson on choosing a computer or smartphone by its specifications), a full Excel unit ending in AutoSum, one- and two-dimensional arrays with nested loops (the series' first data structures), and electronic services: e-government, email and cloud computing with its applications.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Grade 12): the final year is design-and-build — simulation models, a seven-method technological problem-solving process, biotechnology with a DNA-technology case study, CAD, the software development process, IT career pathways, and GUI work with client-side scripting and interactive menus. (teacher guide — evidence)

Morocco's own way (Grade 1): ICT from age 6 built entirely on handling real hardware first — plug in, switch on, draw with the mouse, type in Word — with every unit taught in the same five-step rhythm (mobilisation, découverte, application, évaluation, réemploi) in pairs/groups, and files taught through paper folders and plastic sleeves before the screen. (teacher guide — evidence)
