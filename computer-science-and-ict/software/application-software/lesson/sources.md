**Acceptance count: taught in 14 countries (Ethiopia, Tanzania, Yemen, Iraq, Sudan, Fiji, Libya, Mongolia, Sri Lanka, Laos, Greece, Armenia, Indonesia, South Sudan).**

Yemen's own way (Grade 10): a skills ladder — every unit alternates short teaching topics with a named تدريب (guided training) and نشاط (activity), and Windows itself is taught as a school subject for a full unit before any application is opened.

Sub lesson 4 is teacher-guide evidence — the keyed answer to the student book's group activity asking whether traffic lights and ATMs need software. Its statement that the vast majority of ATMs run Windows XP (Professional or Embedded) is the guide's own 2022-era wording, taught as printed, as the course's record of what the book teaches — not as a current claim about today's ATMs. The student book's printed p. 43 also carries one of its stray character runs; they are typesetting faults and are taught as nothing.

The Grade 9 teacher guide's closing summary for this part (printed pp. 39-40) introduces a description of system software with the words "while application software is..." — the two labels are swapped in that sentence. The lesson teaches the frame corrected: application software is what the user works with directly; system software keeps the hardware working and provides the interface.

Iraq's own way (Grade 11): the final preparatory year is the most forward-looking of the series — smart devices and the Internet of Things (with a lesson on choosing a computer or smartphone by its specifications), a full Excel unit ending in AutoSum, one- and two-dimensional arrays with nested loops (the series' first data structures), and electronic services: e-government, email and cloud computing with its applications.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Grade 12): the final year is design-and-build — simulation models, a seven-method technological problem-solving process, biotechnology with a DNA-technology case study, CAD, the software development process, IT career pathways, and GUI work with client-side scripting and interactive menus. (teacher guide — evidence)

Morocco's own way (Grade 1): ICT from age 6 built entirely on handling real hardware first — plug in, switch on, draw with the mouse, type in Word — with every unit taught in the same five-step rhythm (mobilisation, découverte, application, évaluation, réemploi) in pairs/groups, and files taught through paper folders and plastic sleeves before the screen. (teacher guide — evidence)

Sudan's own way (Grade 6): the final primary book is built around real Sudanese systems — networks and cloud first, then a full security unit taught through named children's everyday internet dilemmas, and a worked example of the ipSchools school-management system (with a real Bakht Al-Ruda school results table) before closing with computers in banks and police work.

Cuba's own way (Grade 2): Play-based (lúdico) introduction through the graficador (Paint-type drawing program) and educational software; correct workstation posture/ergonomics is taught explicitly in Grade 2, and files/folders are introduced intuitively through saving and opening drawings in a class-group folder. (teacher guide — evidence)

Fiji's own way (Year 11 Computer Education student book): The first locally written text of its kind (the book's Preface): written by a panel of Computer Studies officers and University of the South Pacific lecturers for the new Year 11 Computer Education syllabus trialled in 2014; each chapter uses "Discovery Learning" prompts, and the database chapter is a full Access 2007 practical building a Student database in a "Lab Chapter 3" folder.

Libya's own way (Grade 11 Term 1, Information Technology student book): a programming year taught in old-school order — problem solving and algorithms, flowcharts, memory/variables, then Visual Basic statement by statement (LET, PRINT, INPUTBOX, IF, SELECT CASE, FOR…NEXT, DO…WHILE, DO…UNTIL), every chapter ending in graded exercises. The book is printed for BOTH the Science and Humanities sections (للقسمين العلمي والأدبي), although the records label it Humanities only — cited as printed, and taught to Science and Humanities together.

Mongolia's own way (Grade 6): ICT is built on Mongolian examples throughout — information and objects taught through the Mazaalai (Gobi bear), the ger (with a paper origami model), Mongolian pattern (khee) motifs and the national flag; touch typing is taught on the Mongolian Cyrillic keyboard layout with local tutor programs (MonTT, Fast type); web work uses Mongolian sites (surguuli.edu.mn, kids.gogo.mn, tsag-agaar.mn).

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 6 Reading Book): a story/dialogue-led "Reading Book" with a talking computer character; every chapter cross-refers to numbered Workbook activities; local sectors (school office, ATM, ICU, factories, agriculture) and the 3R e-waste method are taught from Grade 6.

Laos's own way (Grade 7 ICT student book): the hardware year is taught through the von Neumann model (the book pictures John von Neumann beside an early machine) and the software chapter ends with a full comparison table of real compilers (Visual Basic, Turbo Pascal, C++, Fortran…) by platform and licence.

Greece's own way (Grade 7, Pliroforiki A' Gymnasiou, 2024): the new Gymnasium informatics course is built around algorithms from the first page (a Python binary example in Unit 1), then hardware/software, the internet with a full copyright/Creative Commons/AI-copyright sequence, cybersecurity with cryptography, word processing, algorithmics, Scratch storytelling/chatbot projects, and a long society unit (robots, drones, autonomous cars, e-government, AI in education/medicine).

Greece's own way (Grade 10, Efarmoges Pliroforikis A' Lykeiou): the older Lykeio applications book in four thematic units — hardware/software and applications, programming environments and creating applications (life cycle, modern IDEs, mobile-app and 3D object-oriented programming), communication and the internet (HTML5/CSS, learning online), and collaboration and safety online (cloud applications, telework, social networks, privacy).

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 2 G2 student): A story-led primary book built around two Grade 2 characters (Hayk and Arpi); every lesson uses fixed activity "rooms" (key knowledge, key words, skills/tasks) and QR codes link to audio/video material; safety and health come before any machine skills.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 8 G8 Informatika student): Grade 8 deepens the four elements: spreadsheet data analysis (lookup, pivot), computational thinking through functions, sets and number systems, and programming that moves from Scratch to Blockly Games, procedural blocks and a physical Ozobot robot.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 9 G9 Informatika student): Grade 9 completes junior-high Informatika with tree/graph data structures, a full data-analysis workflow, modular programming and the block-to-text transition (Scratch/Blockly toward C and Python), productivity content design with AI, and a full digital-security chapter.
