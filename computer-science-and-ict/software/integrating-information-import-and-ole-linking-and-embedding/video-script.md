# Video script — Integrating Information — Import and OLE Linking and Embedding

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Merging information into a work is done three ways. **Importing** brings the other work's content in, converted, as the receiving program's own material — the tie to the source is cut at the door. **OLE linking** places the object so it **stays tied to its source file**: the work shows the object, and when the source changes, the work's copy follows — one truth, kept in one place. **OLE embedding** moves the object **into the work for good**: it travels with the document, editable there by its own program's tools, but cut off from the original — the source may change and the embedded copy will not know.
The book's worked integration is a production: a **24-slide presentation on the sights of Bayankhongor aimag**, built by merging — texts, pictures, tables and media imported, linked and embedded from the programs that made them, into one presentable whole; the chapter's team project, **'My homeland tour'**, repeats the production for each team's own ground. Mongolia's judgement closes the topic: integration is chosen per part — link what must stay true, embed what must travel, import what must become native — and the worker who cannot name the three will merge by accident and maintain by sorrow.
