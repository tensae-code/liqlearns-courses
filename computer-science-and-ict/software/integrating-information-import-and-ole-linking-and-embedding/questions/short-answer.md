# Integrating Information — Import and OLE Linking and Embedding — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) Name the three ways of merging information.**
**Answer: Importing, OLE linking and OLE embedding**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What happens to the source tie in importing?**
**Answer: It is cut — the content becomes the receiving program's own material**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What does a linked object stay tied to?**
**Answer: Its source file — when the source changes, the work follows**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What does an embedded object do?**
**Answer: Moves into the work for good, travelling with it, cut off from the original**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) Can an embedded object be edited?**
**Answer: Yes — in place, by its own program's tools**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What is the book's worked example?**
**Answer: A 24-slide presentation on the sights of Bayankhongor aimag**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What is the team project?**
**Answer: "My homeland tour" — the same production for the team's own ground**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) State the choosing rule.**
**Answer: Link what must stay true; embed what must travel; import what must become native**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) What happens to the worker who cannot name the three?**
**Answer: They merge by accident and maintain by sorrow**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) Whose own topic is OLE integration?**
**Answer: Mongolia's — taught by name in its Grade 10 book**
