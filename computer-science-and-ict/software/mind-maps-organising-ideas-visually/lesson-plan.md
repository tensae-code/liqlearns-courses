## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Centre, branches, twigs — the shape of a mind map** — A **mind map** (sơ đồ tư duy) organises ideas **visually**: the main topic sits in the **centre**, the main ideas grow out of it as **branches**, and details hang from the branches as **twigs**. One or two **keywords** ride each branch — the map is a picture of thinking, not sentences in disguise. Vietnam's Grade 6 book teaches it with **MindMaple Lite** named openly: draw the centre, add branches, and let the map grow as the ideas come — reordering branches is cheaper than rewriting notes.
2. **Using a mind map — planning, learning, presenting together** — The map earns its place three ways: **planning** (a project's parts seen whole before work starts), **learning** (a lesson's structure held in one picture, easier to remember than a page of lines), and **presenting together** — Grade 9's practical uses visual tools, mind maps among them, to present information in **exchange and collaboration**, where the map on the screen gives a group one shared picture to point at, question and improve.
