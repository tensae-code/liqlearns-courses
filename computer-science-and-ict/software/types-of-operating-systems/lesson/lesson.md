# Types of Operating Systems

## Do it first

Not every operating system works the same way. This lesson sorts them by their manner of working.

## Sub lesson 1 — By how jobs are handled — batch, time-sharing, real-time

Operating systems are typed first by how they handle jobs. **Batch** systems collect jobs and run them in groups without interaction (the payroll at night). **Time-sharing** systems slice the processor's time among many interactive users, so each feels alone on the machine. **Real-time** systems guarantee an answer within a strict deadline — the control systems of aircraft, medical devices and factory robots, where a late answer is a wrong answer. Armenia's Grade 9 book follows its operating-systems lesson with their types (§13) and their history (§14).

In your mind's eye: by how jobs are handled — batch, time-sharing, real-time.

## Sub lesson 2 — By users and by home — single/multi-user, desktop, mobile, embedded

A second sorting is by **users**: **single-user** systems serve one person at a time (a home PC's system); **multi-user** systems serve many at once (a server's system). A third sorting is by **home**: **desktop** systems for personal computers, **mobile** systems for phones (touch, sensors, battery care), and **embedded** systems hidden inside appliances and vehicles, running one dedicated job. One real system can sit in several types at once — a phone's system is single-user, mobile, embedded-home and time-sharing all together.

In your mind's eye: by users and by home — single/multi-user, desktop, mobile, embedded.
