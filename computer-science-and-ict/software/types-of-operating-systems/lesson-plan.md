## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **By how jobs are handled — batch, time-sharing, real-time** — Operating systems are typed first by how they handle jobs. **Batch** systems collect jobs and run them in groups without interaction (the payroll at night). **Time-sharing** systems slice the processor's time among many interactive users, so each feels alone on the machine. **Real-time** systems guarantee an answer within a strict deadline — the control systems of aircraft, medical devices and factory robots, where a late answer is a wrong answer. Armenia's Grade 9 book follows its operating-systems lesson with their types (§13) and their history (§14).
2. **By users and by home — single/multi-user, desktop, mobile, embedded** — A second sorting is by **users**: **single-user** systems serve one person at a time (a home PC's system); **multi-user** systems serve many at once (a server's system). A third sorting is by **home**: **desktop** systems for personal computers, **mobile** systems for phones (touch, sensors, battery care), and **embedded** systems hidden inside appliances and vehicles, running one dedicated job. One real system can sit in several types at once — a phone's system is single-user, mobile, embedded-home and time-sharing all together.
