# Desktop Publishing Programs

## Do it first

A word processor writes pages; a desktop publishing program *designs* them. Fiji's Year 11 book opens its advanced applications with desktop publishing — FrameMaker, PageMaker, Ventura, QuarkXPress — the programs behind newspapers, magazines and books. This lesson is what they do that a word processor does not.

## Sub lesson 1 — Publishing — the page as a design

**Desktop publishing (DTP)** is producing **designed pages** on a computer — pages where text, pictures, headlines and columns are placed exactly, the way a newspaper or magazine page is built. The DTP program works with **frames and pages**, not a flowing document: each block of text or picture sits in its own frame, positioned by the designer to the millimetre. Fiji names the profession's programs of its time — **FrameMaker, PageMaker, Ventura Publisher and QuarkXPress** — the tools of publishing houses.

## Sub lesson 2 — DTP or word processor — the deciding question

The two programs divide by the page's job. If the document is **read in order** — a letter, an essay, a report — the word processor is the tool. If the page is **looked at as a whole** — a poster, a newsletter, a magazine — and its look is part of its message, that is DTP. Fiji teaches the pair together because learners meet both in one office: the same school produces letters in one program and its magazine in the other, and choosing wrong makes both jobs harder.
