# Lesson plan — Adding a Keyboard Language

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What a keyboard language is** — The letters a key produces are not fixed by the plastic: they are set by the **keyboard language** (input language) Windows is using. With English active, a key gives its English letter; switch the language and the same keys give that language's letters, in its own layout. A computer can hold several keyboard languages at once. Afghanistan's Grade 10 book teaches this as its own lesson because it is the step that makes the computer usable in the country's own language: adding **Dari** lets the learner type Dari text in any program.
2. **Adding Dari — and switching to it** — The language is added through the language settings (in the book's Windows XP, through Regional and Language Options in the Control Panel): open the settings, choose **Add**, pick the language — Dari — and its keyboard layout, and confirm. From then on the **language bar** on the taskbar shows the active language, and you switch between your languages by clicking it or with its key combination (in the book's Windows, **Alt+Shift**). The rule to remember: look at the language bar before you type — the program follows the language that is active, not the letters printed on the keys.
