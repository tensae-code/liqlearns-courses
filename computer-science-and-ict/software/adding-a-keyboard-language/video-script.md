# Video script — Adding a Keyboard Language

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

The letters a key produces are not fixed by the plastic: they are set by the **keyboard language** (input language) Windows is using.
With English active, a key gives its English letter; switch the language and the same keys give that language's letters, in its own layout.
A computer can hold several keyboard languages at once.
Afghanistan's Grade 10 book teaches this as its own lesson because it is the step that makes the computer usable in the country's own language: adding **Dari** lets the learner type Dari text in any program.
The language is added through the language settings (in the book's Windows XP, through Regional and Language Options in the Control Panel): open the settings, choose **Add**, pick the language — Dari — and its keyboard layout, and confirm.
From then on the **language bar** on the taskbar shows the active language, and you switch between your languages by clicking it or with its key combination (in the book's Windows, **Alt+Shift**).
The rule to remember: look at the language bar before you type — the program follows the language that is active, not the letters printed on the keys.
