# The Parts of a Window

## Do it first

Open any program. Before you touch its work, learn the box it lives in. In this lesson you name every part of a window — so any program, old or new, feels familiar at once.

## Sub lesson 1 — Frame, title bar and its buttons

A window is held in a **frame** — its border. Across the top runs the **title bar**, which shows the window's name. At the end of the title bar sit three buttons: **close** shuts the window, **minimise** hides it down to the taskbar, and **maximise** fills the whole screen — after maximising, that button becomes **restore**, which brings the window back to its former size.

## Sub lesson 2 — Menu bar, scroll bars and work area

Under the title bar sits the **menu bar** — the row of menus (such as File and Edit) that hold the program's commands. At the right edge and the bottom edge sit the **scroll bars**: drag them, or click their arrows, to move through work too big for the window. Everything else — the large middle — is the **work area**, where the typing, drawing or reading actually happens.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Every program opens inside a window, and every window is built the same way: a frame around it, a title bar on top carrying the close, minimise and maximise or restore buttons, a menu bar, scroll bars at the edges, and the work area in the middle where the work is done. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
