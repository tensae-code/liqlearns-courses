# Lesson plan — Right-Click Shortcut Menus

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Software · **Lesson:** Right-Click Shortcut Menus
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Iraq (2026-11-14) — in the title Software on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Iraq's Grade 7 Computer student book, because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A right-click opens a shortcut menu: a short list of orders that belong to the thing you clicked. Right-clicking This PC opens the menu for the computer itself and its drives.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. ## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The desktop menu and the file/folder menu** — A **right-click** opens a **shortcut menu**: a short list of orders that belong to the thing you clicked. Right-clicking the **desktop** gives the desktop menu, for arranging the desktop itself. Right-clicking a **file or folder** gives its own menu, with the orders that act on that file or folder — the menu changes because the thing clicked changed.
2. **The This PC menu, the Recycle Bin and the taskbar menu** — Right-clicking **This PC** opens the menu for the computer itself and its drives. The **Recycle Bin** sits in this part of the book too: deleted files wait there until they are restored or removed for good. Right-clicking the **taskbar** opens the taskbar menu, with orders for the bar and the windows open on it. One habit serves everywhere: right-click a thing to see what you can do with it.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
