# The Calculator Program

## Do it first

What is 7 + 5? Work it out in your head — then let the computer do it: open Calculator, press 7, +, 5, =. The answer, 12, appears. This lesson teaches calculating in the Calculator program.

## The Calculator Program

The **Calculator** is a program that works out calculations. Its screen shows the numbers you press, and its buttons carry the digits and the signs: **+** for adding, **−** for subtracting, **×** for multiplying, **÷** for dividing, and **=** for the answer.

To calculate: open the Calculator program (it opens in its own window on the desktop), press the first number, press the sign, press the second number, and press **=**. The answer appears on the Calculator's screen. For a new calculation, clear the old one first, so old numbers do not mix into the new working.

The Calculator does exactly what you press — like the executor in the algorithm lessons. If you press 7 + 5 but meant 7 + 6, it will still answer 12, faithfully. So the careful habits are: say the calculation aloud before pressing, check each number on the screen as you press it, and ask whether the answer makes sense when it appears.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: The Calculator is a program for calculating: type the numbers and the sign, press equals, and the program works out the answer — adding, subtracting, multiplying and dividing. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: say what the Calculator program is for, and name the signs +, −, ×, ÷ and =.
2. Show it, don't just say it: work out a short calculation in Calculator: number, sign, number, =.
3. Show it, don't just say it: clear a finished calculation before starting a new one, and check whether an answer makes sense.
