# Step 1 — identify the network problem

## Do it first

Half the school has no network this morning. Before touching a single cable, write what a troubleshooter must find out first. Then work step 1 of the network method.

## Sub lesson 1 — Inside or outside your control — the first decision

First decide **whether the failing part is inside your control — your local network, which you can solve yourself — or outside it — beyond your network, where whoever runs that network must help**.

> **Teacher evidence (teacher guide — evidence, printed p. 110):** the guide draws the same line in its own words — a **local network** is infrastructure **designed and managed by ourselves** (the **school computer-laboratory network** is its example); an **outside network** is **designed and managed by an outsider, a third party or a service provider**, over which we have **no control** (a **telecom network** is the example), and reaching it needs the **provider's permission and a subscription** to its service packages. ## Sub lesson 2 — Identifying the problem itself — six moves

Identifying the problem means:

1. **Gather information** on the network's current state with the available tools.
2. **Duplicate the problem** on test hardware/software if possible.
3. **Ask users** about the errors they have met.
4. **Identify the symptoms** — complete loss? slowness? network-wide or one user?
5. Determine whether **anything changed** before the problems appeared — new hardware, new users, a software update.
6. **Define each individual problem clearly** when there are several.
