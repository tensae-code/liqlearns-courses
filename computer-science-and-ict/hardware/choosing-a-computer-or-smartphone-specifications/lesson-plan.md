# Lesson plan — Choosing a Computer or Smartphone — Specifications

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Hardware · **Lesson:** Choosing a Computer or Smartphone — Specifications
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Iraq (2026-11-14) — in the title Hardware on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Iraq's Grade 11 Computer student book, because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Choosing well means comparing criteria — specifications — against the work the device must do: the processor's speed, the amount of memory and storage, the screen, and the battery for a carried device. The book compares the smart device and the computer directly: what they share (processor, memory, storage, operating system) and where they differ (size, battery, how they are carried and used).
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Criteria for choosing** — Choosing well means comparing **criteria — specifications** — against the work the device must do: the **processor's speed**, the amount of **memory** and **storage**, the screen, and the battery for a carried device. Grade 11's lesson sets the criteria beside each other for the **computer and the smartphone**, because the same questions serve both.
2. **Comparing the smart device and the computer** — The book **compares the smart device and the computer** directly: what they share (processor, memory, storage, operating system) and where they differ (size, battery, how they are carried and used). The buyer who can read both specification lists with the same criteria can choose either device well — and say why.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Two phones cost the same. One feels fast for two years, the other slows within months — the difference was on the specifications list all along. Iraq's Grade 11 book teaches choosing by those lists, for computers and smartphones together. This lesson follows it. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of criteria for choosing as the lesson describes it — the student studies the picture and names what each part shows.
   - ADD GIF: a GIF of comparing the smart device and the computer in action — the student watches once, then says what changed and why.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the lesson's key names and definitions aloud, in the order the lesson gives them — the student listens once through, then reads the same lines aloud along with the voice. (Audio is a described slot in this build; no audio file is made.)
