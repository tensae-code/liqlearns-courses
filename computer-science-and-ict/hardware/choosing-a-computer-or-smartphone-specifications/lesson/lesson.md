# Choosing a Computer or Smartphone — Specifications

## Do it first

Two phones cost the same. One feels fast for two years, the other slows within months — the difference was on the specifications list all along. Iraq's Grade 11 book teaches choosing by those lists, for computers and smartphones together. This lesson follows it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Criteria for choosing

Choosing well means comparing **criteria — specifications** — against the work the device must do: the **processor's speed**, the amount of **memory** and **storage**, the screen, and the battery for a carried device. Grade 11's lesson sets the criteria beside each other for the **computer and the smartphone**, because the same questions serve both.

## Sub lesson 2 — Comparing the smart device and the computer

The book **compares the smart device and the computer** directly: what they share (processor, memory, storage, operating system) and where they differ (size, battery, how they are carried and used). The buyer who can read both specification lists with the same criteria can choose either device well — and say why.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Choosing well means comparing criteria — specifications — against the work the device must do: the processor's speed, the amount of memory and storage, the screen, and the battery for a carried device. The book compares the smart device and the computer directly: what they share (processor, memory, storage, operating system) and where they differ (size, battery, how they are carried and used). Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
