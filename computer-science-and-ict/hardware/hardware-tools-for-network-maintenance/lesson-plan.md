# Lesson plan — Hardware tools for network maintenance — crimper to multimeter

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Hardware · **Lesson:** Hardware tools for network maintenance — crimper to multimeter
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Maintenance and Troubleshooting (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Five hardware tools make and prove network cabling: the crimper attaches connectors, the cable tester verifies signals, the punch-down tool seats wires in panels, the tone generator traces a wire pair, and the multimeter measures voltage, current and resistance.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the whole network section: the software tools are known; these are the tools of the hand. This lesson rests on the student book alone — the teacher guide gives no teaching section for this part of the unit (see sources).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or in steps — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Making and proving cables — crimper and cable tester** — the crimper makes and modifies cables; the tester verifies a cable's signal; the guide's review definitions as labelled evidence.
2. **Seating, tracing, measuring — punch-down tool, tone generator, multimeter** — punch-down seating in panels; the tone through one UTP pair; the multimeter's three measures and two forms.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Command-line tools — tracert, nslookup and netstat, in this same title. Recommended, never forced — a learner may land on this lesson directly, and it stands alone.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Attach connectors with a crimper to make or modify cables.
2. Verify a cable's signal with a cable tester.
3. Seat wires with a punch-down tool; trace a pair with a tone generator.
4. Measure voltage, current and resistance with a multimeter.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. The real tools to look at if the school has them — no cabling work needed.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes crimper and tester → the crimper MAKES the cable end; the tester PROVES the finished cable.
- Reaches for the multimeter to find a cable → tracing a pair is the tone generator's job; the multimeter measures electrical quantities.
