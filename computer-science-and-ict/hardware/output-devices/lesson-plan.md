# Lesson plan — Output devices — getting results out

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Hardware · **Lesson:** Output devices — getting results out
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Computer Hardware — in the title Hardware on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** An output device lets the computer communicate processing results to the outside world in a form humans understand: the monitor displays, the printer makes a hard copy on paper, the plotter draws highly graphical pictures and posters, and the speaker outputs sound.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on Inside the CPU: data has been processed; now it must come out where humans can use it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what an output device does: communicates processing results in a form humans understand.
2. Name the four devices and each one's output; name the monitor's three pictured types: CRT, LCD, LED.
3. Say what a hard copy is; and name the guide's two added output examples: robot and headphone.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil.
