**Acceptance count: taught in 11 countries (Ethiopia, Tanzania, Yemen, Iraq, Fiji, Bhutan, Sri Lanka, Laos, Greece, Armenia, Vietnam).**

Yemen's own way (Grade 10): a skills ladder — every unit alternates short teaching topics with a named تدريب (guided training) and نشاط (activity), and Windows itself is taught as a school subject for a full unit before any application is opened.

No book error touches this lesson's content.

Saudi Arabia's own way (Grade 4, Digital Skills): the primary Digital Skills course (Binary Logic) is built as three units per term with the same guide pattern as the intermediate books (outcomes, important points, steps, answers, unit project, "Test Yourself"), files on the Ain platform, and a strong Arabic/English bilingual-typing strand — pupils switch the keyboard between Arabic and English with Alt+Shift and learn text direction, hamza and Arabic punctuation on the keyboard alongside Word skills; Scratch is introduced in Term 1 through algorithms from daily life. This citation is PARTIAL — Term 1 only. (teacher guide — evidence)

Iraq's own way (Grade 7): a four-unit spiral repeated at every grade — computer fundamentals, computer software (here: the operating system plus an unusually deep treatment of Windows right-click shortcut menus), algorithms and programming principles (ending in Scratch), and information technology and the internet — every lesson built on the Iraqi five-step learning cycle with opening activity, main idea, learning outcomes, vocabulary and lesson review.

Morocco's own way (Grade 1): ICT from age 6 built entirely on handling real hardware first — plug in, switch on, draw with the mouse, type in Word — with every unit taught in the same five-step rhythm (mobilisation, découverte, application, évaluation, réemploi) in pairs/groups, and files taught through paper folders and plastic sleeves before the screen. (teacher guide — evidence)

Fiji's own way (Year 11 Computer Education student book): The first locally written text of its kind (the book's Preface): written by a panel of Computer Studies officers and University of the South Pacific lecturers for the new Year 11 Computer Education syllabus trialled in 2014; each chapter uses "Discovery Learning" prompts, and the database chapter is a full Access 2007 practical building a Student database in a "Lab Chapter 3" folder.

Bhutan's own way (Class V, Literacy with ICT student book): Word is taught together with typing in Dzongkha, the national language (Dzongkha keyboard software and fonts); communication is taught on the school LAN with Qualia LAN Messenger (chat, file sharing and broadcast with no Internet connection), and the end-of-year folder is sent to the teacher as a LAN-messenger attachment.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 6 Reading Book): a story/dialogue-led "Reading Book" with a talking computer character; every chapter cross-refers to numbered Workbook activities; local sectors (school office, ATM, ICU, factories, agriculture) and the 3R e-waste method are taught from Grade 6.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 9 Reading Book): it opens with preparing a computer specification for a real buyer (user needs, peripherals, processor/HDD/monitor/VGA specs, after-sales service); microcontrollers are taught with BBC micro:bit and Arduino and local sensor examples (solar lamp, washing machine, microwave).

Laos's own way (Grade 7 ICT student book): the hardware year is taught through the von Neumann model (the book pictures John von Neumann beside an early machine) and the software chapter ends with a full comparison table of real compilers (Visual Basic, Turbo Pascal, C++, Fortran…) by platform and licence.

Greece's own way (Grade 7, Pliroforiki A' Gymnasiou, 2024): the new Gymnasium informatics course is built around algorithms from the first page (a Python binary example in Unit 1), then hardware/software, the internet with a full copyright/Creative Commons/AI-copyright sequence, cybersecurity with cryptography, word processing, algorithmics, Scratch storytelling/chatbot projects, and a long society unit (robots, drones, autonomous cars, e-government, AI in education/medicine).

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 2 G2 student): A story-led primary book built around two Grade 2 characters (Hayk and Arpi); every lesson uses fixed activity "rooms" (key knowledge, key words, skills/tasks) and QR codes link to audio/video material; safety and health come before any machine skills.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 3 G3 student): Grade 3 (Stepanyan, Amirkhanyan, Matinyan) keeps the Hayk-and-Arpi story frame and QR-linked media: device safety, technology around us, picture and text editors, e-mail with attachments, input/output devices, keyboard keys, data collection with programs, then a Scratch strand — environment, first program, pictures, repeated actions, and game scores as a first step into variables.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 5 G5 student (Antares)): One of two different Grade 5 books in the set (this one by Velijanyan & Ayvazyan, Antares); it runs as numbered § lessons mixing information theory, careful judgement of fake vs true information, and a long practical spine — text editor, spreadsheets, algorithms and variables — ending in teamwork principles.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 8 G8 student): The Velijanyan & Aslanyan series continues: after e-security and information sources it turns practical — multimedia slides, spreadsheets, blogging and online content — then computer architecture (von Neumann, memory, I/O), networks, and a Python block (logic, loops, lists, functions, errors, comments, debugging).

Vietnam's own way (Grade 7 Tin học 7): Grade 7 is the spreadsheet year (five Excel lessons in a row, in Windows/Excel/PowerPoint named openly) and its computer-science strand is pure algorithms with no code at all — sequential search, binary search and sorting acted out and flowcharted before any language appears.
