# Output devices — getting results out

## Do it first

Your sum is finished inside the machine. Useless — until you can see it, print it, or hear it. Name every way a result has ever reached you from a computer. This lesson names the four output devices the book teaches.

## Output devices — getting results out

An output device lets the computer communicate processing results to the outside world, in a form humans understand. 

[Picture 10131 — the four devices each showing its output: screen, paper page, poster, sound]



**The monitor** displays information as pictures or text. The book pictures three types: CRT, LCD and LED. **The printer** prints data on paper, making a **hard copy** — the paper version you met in the scanner lesson, coming out this time instead of going in. **The plotter** is a printer-like device for highly graphical pictures and posters — the big, careful drawings. **The speaker** outputs computer sound.

One output moment deserves watching closely. 

[GIF 10132 — a printer feeding out a printed page]

 The page slides out and the result that existed only inside the machine is now paper in your hand: a hard copy.

The teacher guide's discussion key adds two more output examples to the book's four: the **robot** and the **headphone** (teacher guide — evidence).

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: An output device lets the computer communicate processing results to the outside world in a form humans understand: the monitor displays, the printer makes a hard copy on paper, the plotter draws highly graphical pictures and posters, and the speaker outputs sound. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
