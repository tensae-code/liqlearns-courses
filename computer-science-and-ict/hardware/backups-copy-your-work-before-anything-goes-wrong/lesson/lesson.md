# Backups — copy your work before anything goes wrong

## Do it first

Tomorrow you will troubleshoot a teacher's computer — one that holds a whole term of marks. Write the ONE thing you must make sure of before you touch a single setting. Then learn the rule and the Windows steps that honour it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Why back up — and the rule before troubleshooting

A **backup copies data or files to external storage** — a **cloud server** or an **external hard drive** — so work can be **recovered after a failure**. Keep **at least one method**, and **update it as often as needed**.

[Picture 17714 — files flowing from a computer to an external drive and a cloud, with a 'before troubleshooting' flag on top]

The book's standing rule for this whole unit: **a backup is the first step before any troubleshooting. If none exists, do not start.** First check with the user:

- the **date of the last backup**,
- **what it contains**,
- its **integrity** (is it sound?),
- and that **all backup media needed for a restore are available**.

> **Teacher evidence, in plain consequence terms:** a backup is the copy kept on an external drive or cloud server that **lets work continue after a failure** — the guide argues it through a bank whose virus-damaged system cannot serve a single withdrawal until its data is restored (teacher guide, printed pp. 95–98).

## Sub lesson 2 — The Windows backup steps (doing)

The book's Windows route, in order:

1. Open **Control Panel**.
2. Choose **Backup and Restore**.
3. Choose **Set up backup**.
4. Choose **Create a system image**.
5. **Select the destination drive** — a hard disk, a DVD, or a network place.
6. Click **Next**, then **Start backup**.

The second door to the same room: **search 'backup'** and choose **Backup settings**.
