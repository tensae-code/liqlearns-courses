## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Stand-offs and alignment — the board must not touch the case** — The board never screws directly to the case wall. **Stand-offs** — small metal spacers screwed into the case — hold it raised, so its underside circuits **touch nothing conductive**, and the board's **screw holes are matched to the stand-offs meant for them** (a spare stand-off left under a part of the board with no hole is a short circuit waiting). The board is laid in with its **rear ports aligned through the case's back opening**, the CPU and memory already fitted or fitted by the book's order, and every hole confirmed over its stand-off before a screw is lifted.
2. **Screwing down — firm, even, not forced** — Screws go in **a few turns each, working across the board**, before any one is tightened — the board settles flat instead of being pulled crooked by the first corner. Tightening is **firm, never forced**: an over-driven screw cracks the board or strips its stand-off. Power leads from the power supply are then connected to the board's sockets. Laos's check closes the section: the board sits flat, every screw present and firm, ports showing through the back, nothing trapped underneath — the foundation the rest of the build can trust.
