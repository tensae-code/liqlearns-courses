# The keyboard — ten groups of keys

## Do it first

Look at a real keyboard, or picture one. Your fingers know it is not one flat field — the keys cluster in families. Before reading on, point to where the letters live, where the numbers cluster like a calculator, and the single key at the very top-left.

## Sub lesson 1 — The ten groups

The keyboard enters data through keys grouped by function: Escape, which cancels or closes an operation, at the top-left; Backspace, which deletes the character before the cursor; the function keys, which do specific tasks — F1 is mostly Help; the navigation and editing keys — arrows, Home, End, Page Up, Page Down, Delete, Insert; the typing or alphanumeric keys — letters, numbers, punctuation and symbols; and the four directional arrow keys, which also appear among the navigation keys.

## Sub lesson 2 — Keys that work with partners — CTRL, ALT, the Windows key

CTRL is used with other keys — bold, italic, underline, save, copy, cut and paste are done with CTRL held down. ALT gives alternate operations in combination with other keys. The Windows key opens and closes the Start menu — watch the GIF: one tap opens it, a second tap closes it. With D it shows the desktop, with E it opens Computer, and with L it locks the computer or switches users.

[GIF 10928 — the Windows key opening Start]

## Sub lesson 3 — The numeric keypad

At the right of most full keyboards sits the numeric keypad: numbers grouped together like a calculator, for quick entry of figures. If you type many numbers — prices, marks, measurements — the keypad is the fast road in.

## Say it to your companion

Walk your companion across the keyboard: name the ten groups in any order, then demonstrate (or mime) three: Escape, Backspace, and the Windows key with D, E and L.
