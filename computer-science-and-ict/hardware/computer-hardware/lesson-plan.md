# Lesson plan — Computer hardware — the part you can touch

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Hardware · **Lesson:** Computer hardware — the part you can touch
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Computer Hardware — in the title Hardware on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Hardware is the physical part of the computer you can see and touch, and by function it falls into four families: input devices, the central processing unit, output devices and storage devices.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on Hardware and software — the two parts: this is the first Unit 2 lesson, opening the hardware half of the frame.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. ## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what hardware is: the physical part you can see and touch.
2. Name the four families in the book's order: input devices, CPU, output devices, storage devices.
3. Match a real part — keyboard, screen, disk — to its family.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil; a real computer to look at if one is near.

## If the learner is stuck
Sorts by shape or size → the families are by function, by job: in, process, out, keep. Confuses the CPU with the whole box → the CPU is the processing family itself, taught in its own lessons next.
