# Computer hardware — the part you can touch

## Do it first

Run your hand — in your mind — over a desktop computer: screen, keyboard, the box, the mouse, the printer beside it. Every part your hand touched belongs to this lesson's one word. But the parts do different jobs. This lesson sorts them into their four families.

## Sub lesson 1 — Hardware — the part you can touch

Hardware is the physical part of the computer — the part you can see and touch. If your hand can rest on it, it is hardware. The keyboard is hardware. The screen is hardware. The box and everything solid inside it is hardware.

## Sub lesson 2 — The four families

Hardware is not one heap. By function — by the job each part does — it falls into four families. 

[Picture 10121 — a desktop computer with its parts grouped and colour-coded into the four families]



1. **Input devices** — they put data in (Unit 2's next lessons).
2. **The central processing unit (CPU)** — it does the processing.
3. **Output devices** — they bring results out.
4. **Storage devices** — they keep data.

Notice the echo: input, process, output and store are the four functions from Unit 1 — now met as families of real, touchable parts. The rest of this unit takes the families in turn.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Hardware is the physical part of the computer you can see and touch, and by function it falls into four families: input devices, the central processing unit, output devices and storage devices. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
