# How Real Numbers Are Represented Inside the Computer — matching

Match each item on the left with the one it belongs to on the right.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What are a stored real's three fields?** → **Sign, exponent and mantissa**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What formula gives the value?** → **sign × mantissa × 2^exponent**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What does normalisation fix?** → **The mantissa's first digit is 1**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What do more exponent bits buy, and more mantissa bits?** → **Wider range; finer precision**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) Why is the total a budget?** → **The bit count is fixed — each field's share is taken from the others'**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What are the representation's two edges?** → **Range (overflow and underflow) and precision (values sharing one stored neighbour)**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) Why is 0.1 stored approximately?** → **Its binary mantissa does not terminate — the stored value is a close neighbour**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) Where do long computations' errors come from?** → **Accumulated rounding at the precision limit**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) Which later lessons grow from this?** → **Grade 12's lessons on calculation errors**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) Is floating point a failure?** → **No — a chosen compromise: range and honesty about precision in one row of bits**
