# Lesson plan — Assembling a Desktop Computer

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Hardware · **Lesson:** Assembling a Desktop Computer
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Uganda (2026-11-14) — in the title Hardware on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Uganda's *Information Communication Technology Textbook — Senior One* (NCDC Prototype, Lower Secondary Curriculum) (student book, with the teacher's guide stocked as evidence), because no existing lesson teaches this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a desktop computer is assembled by putting its separate parts — system unit, monitor, keyboard, mouse and cables — together in the right places, gently and with the power off, then checking every connection before switching on.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on knowing the parts of a computer and their names; the order of assembly, the matching of cables to ports and the checking before start-up are built inside the lesson from the activity itself.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The parts you will assemble** — The system unit, monitor, keyboard, mouse and cables — each named, each with its job and its place on the desk.
2. **Putting the parts together** — Joining the parts to the system unit one at a time, power off, matching cables to ports and never forcing, with the power cable last.
3. **Checking and starting the assembled computer** — Checking every connection, switching on, and working through the joins in order if the screen stays dark.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the parts of a desktop computer — system unit, monitor, keyboard, mouse and cables — and say what each one does.
2. Assemble a desktop computer: join each part to the system unit with the power off, matching cables to ports without forcing, and connect the power last.
3. Check an assembled computer before and at start-up, and work through its connections in order when the screen stays dark.

## What you need
A real desktop computer in its parts — system unit, monitor, keyboard, mouse and their cables — a table or desk, and a power socket. The power stays off and unplugged while parts are connected.

## If the learner is stuck
A cable will not go in → it is the wrong port or the wrong way round; stop, turn it, match its shape to the port — never force it. Screen dark at first switch-on → check connections in order (monitor cable, monitor power, wall power) before deciding anything is broken. Parts joined in a tangle → unplug the power, lay the parts where they will sit, and join them one at a time.
