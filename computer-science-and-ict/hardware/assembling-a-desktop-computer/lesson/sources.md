Placement choice: this lesson was built new for the ICT expansion (Uganda, 2026-11-14) because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree was checked first: Hardware holds lessons naming a computer's parts and categories, but none teaches assembling a desktop computer from its parts as its own lesson. The placements file named this slug directly (NEW LESSON NEEDED — hardware/assembling-a-desktop-computer). It sits in the Hardware title (hardware/). It has three sub lessons: the parts first, then putting them together, then checking and starting — three distinct sequential parts of one assembly, each opening by proving the one before.

Uganda's own way: a slim activity-led prototype — every topic is an "Activity" learners do (assembling a real desktop, managing the Recycle Bin, word-processing a letter as the integration task), under Uganda's revised Lower Secondary Curriculum; the teacher's guide mirrors the learner's book chapter by chapter with teacher preparation, instructions and possible responses for every activity, plus chapter summaries — the facilitator model of the revised curriculum. This lesson keeps that shape: the learner does the thing with a real tool or document in front of them, and the guide's rows are stocked as labelled evidence only.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Grade 11): the senior guide turns learners into junior system administrators and builders — computer administration, datasheets ("data books") and timing technology, documenting server and printer configurations, data science, pseudocode, Green IT, and building static web pages with tools like Canva and Figma; typing rises to 40 wpm at 85–100% accuracy.
