# Using Digital Devices with a Computer

## Do it first

Take any photograph on a phone or camera near you. The photograph is not finished work yet — it is trapped on a small screen, in a long list, with no name. In this lesson you move it to the computer and turn it into a kept, named, usable file.

## Sub lesson 1 — Devices that work with the computer

A **digital device** captures information in digital form: a **digital camera** captures photographs, a **mobile phone** captures photographs, sound and video and carries its own small computer, and a **webcam** captures moving pictures of whatever is in front of the computer. None of these works alone for long — the picture or recording is made on the device, but it is kept, sorted, edited and shared from the computer. That is why the book's chapter is called digital devices *for processing information*: capturing is only the first step of the processing.

[Picture — a digital camera, a phone and a webcam standing in a row beside a computer, each with an arrow carrying a photograph into the computer screen]

## Sub lesson 2 — Connecting and using them together

To use a device with the computer, the two are **connected** — most often by a cable to the computer, or without wires — and the computer must be able to speak to the device, through the driver and program that came for it. Then the joint work follows one order: capture on the device, transfer to the computer, check what arrived, process it (crop the photograph, trim the recording), and save the result as a file with a name you will find again. A webcam works the other way round in time: it sends its pictures to the computer *while* you use it, for example in a video call, instead of storing them first.

[Picture — the joint-work order as five boxes in a row — capture, transfer, check, process, save — with a photograph moving along them]

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Digital devices — cameras, phones and webcams — do their real work together with the computer: the device captures, the computer receives, stores, processes and shows the result. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: photographs in digital form. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: because the captured material must still be transferred, checked, processed and saved on the computer. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: capture on the device, transfer to the computer, check, process, then save as a named file. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: working on it on the computer, for example cropping it. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
