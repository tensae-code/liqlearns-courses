# Lesson plan — Using Digital Devices with a Computer

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Hardware · **Lesson:** Using Digital Devices with a Computer
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Belarus (2026-11-14) — in the title Hardware on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Belarus's *Інфарматыка* Grade 10, because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Digital devices — cameras, phones and webcams — do their real work together with the computer: the device captures, the computer receives, stores, processes and shows the result.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the Belarus book's own order — it follows the book's sections in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Devices that work with the computer** — A **digital device** captures information in digital form: a **digital camera** captures photographs, a **mobile phone** captures photographs, sound and video and carries its own small computer, and a **webcam** captures moving pictures of whatever is in front of the computer. None of these works alone for long — the picture or recording is made on the device, but it is kept, sorted, edited and shared from the computer. That is why the book's chapter is called digital devices *for processing information*: capturing is only the first step of the processing.
2. **Connecting and using them together** — To use a device with the computer, the two are **connected** — most often by a cable to the computer, or without wires — and the computer must be able to speak to the device, through the driver and program that came for it. Then the joint work follows one order: capture on the device, transfer to the computer, check what arrived, process it (crop the photograph, trim the recording), and save the result as a file with a name you will find again. A webcam works the other way round in time: it sends its pictures to the computer *while* you use it, for example in a video call, instead of storing them first.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Photographs in digital form.
2. Because the captured material must still be transferred, checked, processed and saved on the computer.
3. Capture on the device, transfer to the computer, check, process, then save as a named file.
4. Working on it on the computer, for example cropping it.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil, and a computer with the program this lesson uses, if available.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Take any photograph on a phone or camera near you. The photograph is not finished work yet — it is trapped on a small screen, in a long list, with no name. In this lesson you move it to the computer and turn it into a kept, named, usable file. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of a digital camera, a phone and a webcam standing in a row beside a computer, each with an arrow carrying a photograph into the computer screen — the student studies the picture and names what each part shows.
   - ADD GIF: a GIF of the joint-work order as five boxes in a row — capture, transfer, check, process, save — with a photograph moving along them — the student watches once, then says what changed and why.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the lesson's key names and definitions aloud, in the order the lesson gives them — the student listens once through, then reads the same lines aloud along with the voice. (Audio is a described slot in this build; no audio file is made.)
3. **Say it to your companion** — the companion block in lesson/lesson.md.
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the lesson in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-false, matching, short-answer — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the doing block at the end of lesson/lesson.md.
