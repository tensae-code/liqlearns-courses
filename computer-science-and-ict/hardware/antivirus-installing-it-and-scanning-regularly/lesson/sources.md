**Acceptance count: taught in 5 countries (Ethiopia, Mozambique, Rwanda, Pakistan, Kazakhstan).**

The no-antivirus consequence stands on teacher-guide evidence (teacher guide, printed pp. 95–98), labelled as such; the virus and antivirus teaching is pupil-taught from the student book. The student book's review headings misspell "statment" throughout; statement is taught.

Rwanda's own way (S3 ICT Learner's Book, Fountain): the same ten units as the S3 guide taught as long screenshot-by-screenshot practicals rooted in Rwandan school life (a GS Bumbogo parents'-meeting mail merge, an administration map of Rwanda built in ArcGIS, an environmental-protection presentation, 'Rwanda Nziza' hyperlink decks), closing with a complete Scratch platform game, Hard Journey.

South Sudan's own way (Grade 9 S1 Teacher): The guide teaches the same four units as the student book through co-operative group investigation, with answer keys and assessment guidelines; distinctive content includes using the internet to promote South Sudanese culture and creating user accounts from the Windows command line (net user). (teacher guide — evidence)


Pakistan's own way (Grade 8 Computer Education 8): the Grade 8 book is the older National-Curriculum-2006 Punjab book (not SNC): it teaches networks through physical media and cutting-edge wireless (cellular, satellite, GPS, Bluetooth), a whole unit of hands-on antivirus management (definitions, updates, an AVG whole-computer scan), Excel 2007 taught through three full worked reports (monthly expenses, salaries, school results), and programming in GW-BASIC with line numbers — the pre-SNC Punjab way into code.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kopeeva G5 Informatika): Grade 5 Informatika (the older, pre-"Digital Literacy" subject name) is built around Scratch as a "game programming environment": a whole chapter runs from a first program through blocks, animation graphics, sound effects and dialogue to linear/branching/cyclic commands; earlier chapters pair Internet safety with data protection, and the year ends with print-ready project work — a Word newspaper page with WordArt and columns, presented as slides.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G5 Informatika): Grade 5 Informatika by the Almatykitap team drops Scratch entirely: information theory first (transmission, encryption, binary, graphics in binary code), then raster work in Paint against vector work with curves, and half the year is EV3 robotics beyond simple movement — the gyroscopic sensor, line following and robo-sumo competitions.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kobdikova G5 Informatika): the Arman-PV Grade 5 book teaches the same five national sections as the other two publishers, but every second paragraph is a practicum or a "creating a project" lesson, and the safety section ends in cloud skills — placing, changing and downloading shared-access files — plus mini-projects on setting passwords on documents and preparing a document for print. The vector editor is Inkscape (named in the glossary), the raster editor Paint.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukhametzhanova G5 Informatika, 2020 edition): the Atamura team's 2020 Grade 5 book follows the same five national sections in numbered-paragraph form (1.1–5.5) with end-of-section final tasks; its graphics strand works in Paint for raster and Inkscape for vector (canvas size, SVG, Bezier pen), and the introduction also promises a first taste of 3D technologies (3D model, 3D editor, 3D printing) inside the graphics work.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukhametzhanova G5 Informatika, 2017 edition): the 2017 Atamura Grade 5 book follows the older national programme shared with the Arman-PV book of the same year: computer safety, information and raster graphics, algorithms through labyrinths (real and virtual), a first Scratch program and "living graphics", and project work ending in a print-ready document; a full Scratch block dictionary is printed as an appendix (printed pp. 119–127). The book was written with Microsoft "Partnership in Education" material.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G7 Informatika): Grade 7 by the Almatykitap team is measurement and memory first (units, file formats and file sizes), then networks with antivirus security and the user interface, a long spreadsheet part that ends in modelling processes in Excel, and Python taught through files and branching — nested and compound conditions included. The creative-tasks lesson 2.4 (printed p. 40) is not placed.

Kazakhstan's own way (Vyshkova & Zhurbenko G7 Informatics, NIS): the Nazarbayev Intellectual Schools course is a different programme from the national one: business documents, databases inside the information-processing part, logo design and GIF animation in raster graphics, a whole chapter on modelling the world (including forecasting social problems from data), client–server networks, utilities, and linear-structure programming ending in building your own calculator. Lessons run two pages each with research-style tasks.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukhametzhanova G7 Informatika, 2017 edition): the 2017 Atamura Grade 7 book adds two things its rivals lack — file archiving (creating archives and extracting them) in the first part, and a fifth part on 3D modelling of objects and events; its programming part works with programming languages and systems in general (the glossary's syntax examples are C++: main, cout, cin) and includes designing the project's interface.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukhametzhanova G7 Informatika, updated edition): the updated-content revision of the Atamura Grade 7 book keeps the 2017 skeleton (units, archives, networks, Excel, practical programming) but replaces the general programming part with Python: working with files, branching, nested and compound conditions and organising a choice; an appendix teaches building electronic learning games with a game constructor (Editor and Class versions).

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G7 Informatika, 2019 edition): Almatykitap's other Grade 7 book (2019, printed in Turkey) follows the same five-part frame as the Atamura 2017 book — units and memory, networks and antivirus, spreadsheets with conditional formatting and modelling, programming solutions with interface design (its bibliography is Lazarus/Delphi-based), and 3D modelling of objects and events in SketchUp.

Kazakhstan's own way (Zhunusov G7 Informatics, English-medium): the English-medium Grade 7 book teaches the updated national programme in English with trilingual terminology boxes — spreadsheets are Google Sheets (a "My to-do list" first spreadsheet), and practical programming is three worked games: a Quiz game, Rock Paper Scissors, a Guessing game and a Camel game, each split into algorithm-development and programming lessons.
