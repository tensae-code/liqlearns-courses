# Antivirus — installing it and scanning regularly

## Do it first

A friend plugs their flash disk into the school computer, and a week later the machine misbehaves. Write where the trouble most likely rode in, and the name of the guard that should have been standing at the gate. Then learn how that guard is installed.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a virus is — and how infection arrives

A **virus** is a program that, when run, **copies itself by modifying other programs and inserting its own code** into them.

Infection arrives two named ways: **over the internet**, or **on someone else's flash disk**. And it is a quiet guest: infection **usually shows itself only once it causes a problem**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Antivirus — installing it and scanning regularly (doing)

**Antivirus (anti-malware) software prevents, detects and removes malware.** It comes in **free and commercial** forms; the free examples the book names are **AVAST, AVG, AVIRA and Bitdefender**.

Installing follows the **ordinary route**, in order:

1. **Search** for the antivirus.
2. **Open the maker's result**.
3. **Install directly** — or **save the setup file to the hard disk and run it** like any other application.

The maintenance rule: **scan regularly**. Some malicious programs **embed silently and wait for a trigger** — a single scan on install day does not guard next month.

> **Teacher evidence:** the guide keys a machine without antivirus in one plain consequence — it is simply open to **infection** (teacher guide, printed pp. 95–98).
