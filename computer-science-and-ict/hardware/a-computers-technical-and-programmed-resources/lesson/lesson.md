# A Computer's Technical and Programmed Resources

## Do it first

Grade 11's architecture chapter steps back from circuits and instructions to inventory the whole machine. Everything a computer *has* divides into two estates — what it is made of, and what it is loaded with.

## Sub lesson 1 — Technical resources — the hardware estate

A computer's **technical resources** are its **hardware**, counted and measured: the **processor** (its clock frequency, word length, instruction set), the **memory** (RAM's capacity and speed, storage's volume), and the **devices** attached (input, output, communication). These resources are **finite and priced**: a job a machine cannot do is usually a resource it lacks — memory too small, processor too slow. Naming a machine honestly means listing this estate with its numbers, not its advertisements.

In your mind's eye: technical resources — the hardware estate.

## Sub lesson 2 — Programmed resources — the software estate

The **programmed resources** are the **software the machine holds**: the **system software** (the operating system and its utilities — the programs that make the hardware usable) and the **application software** (the programs for users' work). The estates multiply each other: hardware without software is inert; software without hardware is a text. A computer's real capability for any job is judged **by both together** — the architecture chapter's closing balance, and the buyer's and builder's first question ever after: what does it have, and what does it run?

In your mind's eye: programmed resources — the software estate.
