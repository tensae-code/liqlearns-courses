## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Expansion interfaces — connections inside the box** — Inside the system unit, the motherboard offers **expansion interfaces** — slots where cards are added. **PCI** is the general expansion bus for cards such as network and sound cards; **AGP** was the dedicated graphics slot; **SCSI** is an interface for chains of devices, above all disks; a **sound card** is the classic expansion example, adding audio the board lacks. An interface is a bargain between two sides: a physical connector plus the rules of exchange, and both sides must hold the same bargain or nothing connects.
2. **Ports and storage interfaces — connections at the edge** — At the case's edge stand the **ports**: **USB**, the universal port for nearly everything; the older **serial** and **parallel** ports; **FireWire** and **Thunderbolt**, the fast ports for video and drives; and **HDMI**, carrying picture and sound to a screen in one cable. Inside, the disks connect by their own interfaces: **PATA**, the older parallel ribbon, replaced by **SATA**, the serial interface of modern drives. Mongolia's engineering point: every generation of connection is a trade of speed, width and simplicity — read a machine's ports and you read its age and its possibilities.
