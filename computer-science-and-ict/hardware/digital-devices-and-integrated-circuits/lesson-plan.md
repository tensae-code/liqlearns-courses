## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Digital devices — the family** — A **digital device** works with discrete values — the 0s and 1s of this course — rather than the smooth slide of analogue quantities: computers, phones, digital watches and cameras, calculators, and the controllers inside washing machines and cars. Their shared inside is logic: gates deciding, flip-flops remembering, counters counting — the whole Chapter 3 family at work. Naming a device digital names its arithmetic: everything it does is the binary lessons, electrified.
2. **Integrated circuits — the circuit becomes one thing** — An **integrated circuit (IC)** builds a whole circuit — many gates and their connections — into a **single chip** of semiconductor, printed photographically layer by layer. Integration is why digital devices shrank and cheapened: one chip replaces a board of separate parts, uses less power, fails less, and can be made by the million identically. The chip's pins are its doors — power in, signals in and out — and inside, invisible, the gates of the earlier lessons stand in their millions, which is where the chapter's road has been leading all along.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
