# Digital Devices and Integrated Circuits

## Do it first

Every gate you built in the logic lessons was drawn on paper, one gate at a time. The phone in your pocket holds billions of them — not wired by hand, but printed into pieces of silicon smaller than your thumbnail. This lesson crosses from the drawn gate to the manufactured device.

## Sub lesson 1 — Digital devices — the family

A **digital device** works with discrete values — the 0s and 1s of this course — rather than the smooth slide of analogue quantities: computers, phones, digital watches and cameras, calculators, and the controllers inside washing machines and cars. Their shared inside is logic: gates deciding, flip-flops remembering, counters counting — the whole Chapter 3 family at work. Naming a device digital names its arithmetic: everything it does is the binary lessons, electrified.

## Sub lesson 2 — Integrated circuits — the circuit becomes one thing

An **integrated circuit (IC)** builds a whole circuit — many gates and their connections — into a **single chip** of semiconductor, printed photographically layer by layer. Integration is why digital devices shrank and cheapened: one chip replaces a board of separate parts, uses less power, fails less, and can be made by the million identically. The chip's pins are its doors — power in, signals in and out — and inside, invisible, the gates of the earlier lessons stand in their millions, which is where the chapter's road has been leading all along.
