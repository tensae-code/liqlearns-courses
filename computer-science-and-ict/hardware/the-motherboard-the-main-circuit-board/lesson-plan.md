## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The main board — everything connects here** — The **motherboard** — also called the **system board** or main board — is the computer's **main circuit board**: the large board inside the system unit to which **every other component connects**. The CPU sits in its socket, memory in its slots, storage and cards on its connectors and buses. Fiji's sentence carries the lesson: the motherboard is what makes separate parts one computer — remove it, and the parts are a box of unrelated electronics.
2. **What lives on it — socket, slots, buses** — Three kinds of fitting live on the board. The **CPU socket** holds the microprocessor. **Slots** hold memory modules and expansion cards, so the machine can be grown. **Buses and connectors** — the board's printed pathways and ports — carry data, instructions and power between everything plugged in. When a technician asks whether a part 'fits this machine', they are asking about this board: its socket, its slots and its connectors decide what the computer can become.
