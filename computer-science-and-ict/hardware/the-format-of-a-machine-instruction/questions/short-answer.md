# The Format of a Machine Instruction — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What are a machine instruction's fields?**
**Answer: An operation code field and address field(s)**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What does the opcode name?**
**Answer: What to do — add, load, store, jump — from the processor's instruction set**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What do address fields name?**
**Answer: Where the operands are — registers or memory addresses**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) In a 16-bit instruction with a 4-bit opcode, how many operations and addresses fit?**
**Answer: 16 operations; a 12-bit address reaches 4096 cells**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) What is the format, in the lesson's phrase?**
**Answer: A budget of bits — spent between variety of operations and reach of addresses**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What classes has the format by address count?**
**Answer: Three-address, two-address, one-address and zero-address**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What does a one-address instruction work with?**
**Answer: An implied accumulator**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What does zero-address work on?**
**Answer: A stack — the operands are understood**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) What do fewer addresses buy, and cost?**
**Answer: Shorter instructions and a simpler processor; more instructions per job**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) What is the format choice called in the lesson?**
**Answer: The architecture's founding compromise**
