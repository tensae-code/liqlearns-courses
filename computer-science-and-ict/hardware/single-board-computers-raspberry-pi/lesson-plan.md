## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **One board, whole computer** — A **single-board computer (SBC)** carries a complete computer — processor, memory, storage, connections — on **one printed circuit board**. It runs a real operating system and ordinary programs, like a PC, but at a board's size and price. Indonesia's Grade 12 chapter opens the systems unit with it because the SBC is where the course's two worlds meet: it is a computer you use, and a board you wire. Its named example is the **Raspberry Pi** (the book works with the Pi 3), the credit-card computer built for learning.
2. **GPIO — the computer reaches out — and the Pico** — What makes an SBC more than a small PC is its **GPIO** — general-purpose input/output pins along the board's edge, through which the computer itself senses and drives the world: lights, buttons, sensors, motors. The chapter sets the family in order: the **PC** (a computer of many boards, no GPIO), the **smartphone** (a whole computer, sealed), the **SBC** (whole computer, open pins), the single-board **controller** (next lesson — a microcontroller, no full operating system), and the **Raspberry Pi Pico**, which despite its family name is a controller board, not a computer.
