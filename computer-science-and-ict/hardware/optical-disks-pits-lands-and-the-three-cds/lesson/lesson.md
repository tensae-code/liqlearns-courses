# Optical disks — pits, lands and the three CDs

## Do it first

A laser pointer's dot lands on a wall. Imagine that dot, made tiny and fierce, burning marks into a spinning disk — each mark a bit of data. Before reading on, guess what the burned mark and the untouched surface are each called.

## Sub lesson 1 — Pits and lands

An optical disk is read and written by laser light. To store data, the laser burns the disk's surface: the burned part is the pit, the unburned part is the land. Land represents 0 and pit represents 1. Watch the GIF: the laser spot travels the surface and a pit appears where it burns — writing a 1 into the disk.

## Sub lesson 2 — The CD and its three types

A CD stores 700 MB on one side. It comes in three types. CD-ROM is read only: data can only be read, never written or erased. CD-R is recordable: write once, then read many times — also called WORM. CD-RW is read-write, the erasable optical disc: write and erase as many times as you want.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion which is 0 and which is 1 — land or pit — then line up the three CDs by how often each can be written: never, once, many times.
