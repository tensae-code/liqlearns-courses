# Video script — Steps 2–6 — test a scenario, fix, verify, document

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. This is the FULL network-troubleshooting walkthrough: all the steps, on one example fault — the laboratory network that dies whenever the old router warms up.

---

The fault, from Step 1: the lab loses its network every afternoon. The problem is identified — local, ours to fix, and it follows the old router warming up.

Step 2 — develop a scenario. A working explanation: the old router fails when it gets hot. A scenario can be as simple as a tripped wire; this one is a tiring router.

Step 3 — test the scenario. Swap in another router, and see. The afternoon passes — the network holds. The scenario survives its test. And remember: if the swap had changed nothing, that would not be failure. A disproved scenario sends you back to build a new one.

Step 4 — make a plan of action. This plan is short: replace the old router with the tested one, permanently, this evening when the lab is empty. A plan can also be long — ordering a part, or rolling software back on many machines.

Step 5 — implement the solution. The swap is made, cables seated, the lab reconnected — alone, or with other experts and users helping.

Step 6a — verify system functionality. Every machine in the lab connects; printing works; and we watch for new issues the change may have caused. None appear.

Step 6b — document the issue. Symptoms: network lost every afternoon. Scenario: the old router failing hot. Test strategy: the router swap. Solution: permanent replacement. Written down, this shortens the next technician's downtime — maybe yours.

Identify. Scenario. Test. Plan. Implement. Verify. Document. That is the whole method, on one real fault.
