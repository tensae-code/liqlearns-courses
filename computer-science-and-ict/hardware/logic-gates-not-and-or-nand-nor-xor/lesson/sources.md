Placement choice: this lesson was built new for the ICT expansion (Montenegro, 2026-11-14) because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree was checked for the slug and for near matches before building. It sits in the Hardware title (hardware/), alongside the course's other hardware-and-logic lessons, following the book's own order: Montenegro's book teaches this material as one climb from number systems to converters, and the expansion keeps that climb.

Montenegro's own way: computing is reached from the electronics side — a computer-systems-and-networks technician first masters the binary logic a computer is built from, with a full laboratory practicum and circuit-simulation software (TINA, Circuit Wizard) alongside the theory. This lesson keeps that order: the theory is taught whole, and the hands-on work points the learner at building and simulating the same circuits the book's practicum builds on the bench.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Grade 9): one TIA teacher guide carries two ICT subjects side by side — Communication Technology (networks, ergonomics, safety, societal impact) and Computer Technology (architecture, software) — organised by content standards and numbered benchmarks (9.4.x / 9.5.x), with the design brief used as the problem-solving method for both a product and a simple network, and typing assessed at 20 wpm with 80% accuracy. (teacher guide — evidence, PARTIAL)

Egypt's own way (Grade 10, Term 1 Programming and Artificial Intelligence student book): A full Japanese-style "Information Study" course adopted for Egypt's new secondary track: 13 chapters running from information theory, ethics and security through number systems, data analysis (frequency, variance, correlation, regression in spreadsheets), simulation and queues, Python, and a final AI-programming project chapter building web pages, a quiz app and a block-breaker game with generative AI.

Egypt's own way (Grade 12 Programming Projects student book, Unit Two (English file) — PARTIAL): Each logic gate is taught three times — the gate and its truth table, a simulation project in VB.NET, and the same project in PHP (PHP versions marked enrichment) — closing by using gates to model life decisions. Books 10 and 11 are the two halves of ONE Grade 12 Programming Projects book (Book 10 = Unit Two only, PARTIAL alone; Book 11 = Unit One only, PARTIAL alone / COMPLETE as a pair); the records listed them as parallel editions and say Term 1, but Book 10's inner title page says Second Term.
