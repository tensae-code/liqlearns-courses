# Lesson plan — Keyboard and mouse

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Hardware · **Lesson:** Keyboard and mouse
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — Computer Hardware — in the title Hardware on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The keyboard is the most common input device for entering data, and the mouse is a pointing device for pointing at things on screen, clicking them and moving them around.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on Input devices — putting data in: the first two devices of the eight, taken closely.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The keyboard** — The most common input device for entering data — typed words, numbers and commands.
2. **The mouse** — A pointing device: pointing at things on screen, clicking them, and moving them around — pointer-and-drag shown moving.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the lessons before this one in Hardware on the ladder. Recommended, never forced — a learner may land on this lesson directly, and it stands alone.
- The book's own rhythm is kept: this lesson opens with a brainstorm — the learner first says what they already know — before any definition is given.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say the keyboard's standing: the most common input device for entering data.
2. Name the mouse's three jobs: point, click, move things around.
3. Describe a drag: press, hold, move, release.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil; a real keyboard and mouse to handle if one is near.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Put your hands in the air as if at a desk. Your fingers fall on invisible keys; your other hand rests on an invisible mouse. Two different jobs are happening at once. This lesson separates them: what the keyboard does, and what the mouse does. (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
Mixes the two devices' jobs → type goes with the keyboard; point-click-drag goes with the mouse. Cannot describe dragging → replay the GIF's stages: travel, click-hold, move, let go.
