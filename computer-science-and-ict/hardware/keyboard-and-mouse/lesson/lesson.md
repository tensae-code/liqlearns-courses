# Keyboard and mouse

## Do it first

Put your hands in the air as if at a desk. Your fingers fall on invisible keys; your other hand rests on an invisible mouse. Two different jobs are happening at once. This lesson separates them: what the keyboard does, and what the mouse does.

## Sub lesson 1 — The keyboard

The keyboard is the most common input device for entering data. Letter by letter, number by number, what you type enters the computer. Whenever the data is text — a name, an essay, a password — the keyboard is usually the door it enters by. 

[Picture 10123 — a keyboard and mouse with "type" and "point and click" captions]



## Sub lesson 2 — The mouse

The mouse is a pointing device. It does three things: it **points** at things on the screen, it **clicks** them to choose them, and it **moves them around** — press, hold and drag.

Watch the three jobs happen. 

[GIF 10124 — a pointer moving, clicking and dragging an icon]

 The pointer travels to the icon, clicks to take hold of it, drags it across the screen, and lets it go in its new place. Point, click, drag: the mouse's whole trade.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: The keyboard is the most common input device for entering data, and the mouse is a pointing device for pointing at things on screen, clicking them and moving them around. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: say the keyboard's standing: the most common input device for entering data. Do this with a real example from your own home, school or street, and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: name the mouse's three jobs: point, click, move things around. Do this with a real example from your own home, school or street, and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: describe a drag: press, hold, move, release. Do this with a real example from your own home, school or street, and write or say how you know it is right.
