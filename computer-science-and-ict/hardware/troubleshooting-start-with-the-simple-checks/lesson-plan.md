# Lesson plan — Troubleshooting — start with the simple checks

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Hardware · **Lesson:** Troubleshooting — start with the simple checks
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Hardware Troubleshooting and Preventive Maintenance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Hardware troubleshooting is a systematic way of finding the cause of a fault — from general issues to specific ones — and many faults give way to four simple checks first: power, switches, the monitor, and the plugged-in devices.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the hardware lessons of this title — the learner can name the parts of a computer. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or in steps — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What troubleshooting is** — A systematic approach: review, diagnose and identify the problem, moving from general to specific. Hardware troubleshooting works on devices; software troubleshooting works on errors and bugs in software.
2. **The first simple checks** — Plugged into a working outlet; everything switched on; for a blank screen, the monitor's power and its connection to the system unit; keyboard, mouse, monitor and speakers properly plugged in.
3. **Port or device? Swap to find out** — Try a different port, or swap the device for one that works, to separate a port fault from a device fault. Safety: switch the computer off before removing or replacing parts.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. A mouse has stopped working. Write the simple checks you run, in order, before you swap anything.
2. Swap the mouse into a different port: it works. Write which part was faulty — port or device — and how you know.
3. Write the safety rule from memory, word for word in meaning: what must happen before any part is removed or replaced?

## What you need
A Windows school computer if one is available — paper works too, screen by screen. Paper and a pencil.

## If the learner is stuck
- Jumps to opening the case → go back: most faults found in class are the simple checks. Power, switches, monitor, plugs — in that order, first.
- Cannot tell port from device → one swap answers it: a known-good device in the suspect port.
