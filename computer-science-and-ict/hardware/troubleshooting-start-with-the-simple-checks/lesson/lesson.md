# Troubleshooting — start with the simple checks

## Do it first

A computer on the desk will not start, and the screen is blank. Before opening anything or calling anyone, write down the first four things you would check, in order. Then work through this lesson and mark what you missed. Doing-lessons keep the book's order exactly.

## Sub lesson 1 — What troubleshooting is

Troubleshooting is a **systematic approach to finding the cause of a fault**. You move **from general issues to specific ones**: first the wide, easy checks, then the narrow, exact ones.

To troubleshoot is to **review, diagnose and identify** operational or technical problems in a device. That is **hardware troubleshooting**. The same work on errors and bugs inside software is **software troubleshooting** — a different lesson's ground; this unit stays with hardware.

## Sub lesson 2 — The first simple checks

Many problems give way to simple checks first. Work them in this order:

1. Is the computer **plugged into a working outlet**?
2. Is **everything switched on**?
3. For a blank screen: check the **monitor's power** and its **connection to the system unit**.
4. Are the **keyboard, mouse, monitor and speakers properly plugged in**?

## Sub lesson 3 — Port or device? Swap to find out

A device that does not answer may be faulty itself — or the **port** it is plugged into may be faulty. Separate the two: **try a different port**, or **swap the device** for one you know works. If the known-good device also fails in that port, the port is the fault. If the first device fails in every port, the device is the fault.

**Safety, always:** switch the computer **off** before removing or replacing any part.

> **Teacher evidence — how this is demonstrated in class:** the teacher runs the start-up self-check live, opens the error log and the device list on a real machine, and sets a real fault for pupils to find and fix. BIOS settings are shown only as error messages — never taught — in the lesson on BIOS.

## Say it to your companion

Explain troubleshooting to your companion: what the word means, the four simple checks in order, and how a swap separates a port fault from a device fault. End with the safety rule.

## Do It Yourself

1. A mouse has stopped working. Write the simple checks you run, in order, before you swap anything.
2. Swap the mouse into a different port: it works. Write which part was faulty — port or device — and how you know.
3. Write the safety rule from memory, word for word in meaning: what must happen before any part is removed or replaced?
