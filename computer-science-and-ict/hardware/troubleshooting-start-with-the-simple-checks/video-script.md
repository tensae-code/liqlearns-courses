# Video script — Troubleshooting — start with the simple checks

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then. FULL script, as the placement names a video need for this lesson.

---

This is how you start when a computer will not work.

Do not open the case. Do not guess.
Troubleshooting means: find the cause, step by step.
Wide checks first. Narrow checks after.

CHECK ONE. Is it plugged in?
A working outlet. Not a dead one.

CHECK TWO. Is everything switched on?
The computer. The monitor. The wall switch.

CHECK THREE. Blank screen?
Check the monitor's power.
Check its cable to the system unit.

CHECK FOUR. Are the parts plugged in?
Keyboard. Mouse. Monitor. Speakers.

Still dead? Swap.
Try a different port.
Try a device you know works.
If the good device fails in that port — the port is the fault.
If the first device fails in every port — the device is the fault.

And the rule that never changes:
Switch the computer OFF
before you remove or replace any part.

In a real class, your teacher shows this live:
the computer checks itself at start-up,
the error log opens, the device list opens —
and then YOU get a machine with a real fault,
and you fix it.

That is troubleshooting. Simple checks first.
