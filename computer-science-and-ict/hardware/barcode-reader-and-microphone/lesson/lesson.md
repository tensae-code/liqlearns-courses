# Barcode reader and microphone

## Do it first

Picture a shop till. The assistant does not type the price of every item — a beep, and the price is in. Then picture speaking to a computer instead of typing. Two last doors into the computer. This lesson opens both.

## Sub lesson 1 — Barcode reader

A **barcode** is a pattern of lines of different thickness. It looks like a tiny fence, and it carries data: each pattern of thick and thin lines stands for a particular item.

The **barcode reader** reads that pattern. 

[GIF 10128 — a scan beam sweeping across a barcode]

 The beam sweeps across the lines, and the data enters the computer quickly and without error — no typing, and none of typing's mistakes. That is the barcode's whole purpose: speed with accuracy. 

[Picture 10127 — a reader scanning a striped barcode, and a voice wave entering a microphone]



## Sub lesson 2 — Microphone

A **microphone** inputs the spoken word — as data, or as commands. What the keyboard does for the written word, the microphone does for the voice: your speech crosses into the computer.

With these two, the book's eight input devices are complete: keyboard, mouse, scanner, touch screen, light pen, barcode reader, microphone, camera. Eight doors — and every one of them exists because data cannot walk in by itself.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: A barcode is a pattern of lines of different thickness that lets data enter quickly and without error, read by a barcode reader; a microphone inputs the spoken word as data or commands. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
