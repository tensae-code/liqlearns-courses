**Acceptance count: taught in 11 countries (Ethiopia, Yemen, Saudi Arabia, Iraq, Sudan, Uzbekistan, Fiji, Bhutan, Sri Lanka, Laos, Pakistan).**

Yemen's own way (Grade 10): a skills ladder — every unit alternates short teaching topics with a named تدريب (guided training) and نشاط (activity), and Windows itself is taught as a school subject for a full unit before any application is opened.

No book error touches this lesson's content.

Saudi Arabia's own way (Grade 3, continuing education): a workbook-style book for the continuing-education (adult/lifelong learning) track — every one of the 16 lessons opens with "I want to learn" objectives and closes with written exercises, and everything is taught on an older Windows desktop (Start menu, "My Computer", Recycle Bin) with Notepad as the only application, worked through step-by-step practicals in the school lab.

Iraq's own way (Grade 8): the same four-unit spiral one year on — the hardware unit becomes “the computer platform” and opens with choosing a computer's specifications before buying; Word is taught tab by tab (Home, Insert, Design, Layout); the algorithms unit advances to selection statements and logic truth tables, still landing in Scratch; the internet unit ends with writing a report from web research.

Sudan's own way (Grade 5): a short, highly practical Arabic primary series that revises the previous grade first, then works entirely inside Windows and its built-in apps (Calculator, Paint) before reaching Word — every lesson ends with hands-on activities and each unit with an assessment.


Uzbekistan's own way (Grade 8): grade 8 is theory-first — number systems, coding and Boolean logic before the machine's own story — then a full operating-system chapter and a five-lesson Word course; safety rules return as a closing appendix.

Fiji's own way (Year 11 Computer Education student book): The first locally written text of its kind (the book's Preface): written by a panel of Computer Studies officers and University of the South Pacific lecturers for the new Year 11 Computer Education syllabus trialled in 2014; each chapter uses "Discovery Learning" prompts, and the database chapter is a full Access 2007 practical building a Student database in a "Lab Chapter 3" folder.

Bhutan's own way (Class VIII, Literacy with ICT student book): The final primary/middle year is project-led — a full Audacity audio unit (record, edit, mix, share), information ethics taught through Copyright, Creative Commons and software piracy, and Scratch taught to game level with lists, custom blocks (functions) and logical operators.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 9 Reading Book): it opens with preparing a computer specification for a real buyer (user needs, peripherals, processor/HDD/monitor/VGA specs, after-sales service); microcontrollers are taught with BBC micro:bit and Arduino and local sensor examples (solar lamp, washing machine, microwave).

Laos's own way (Grade 7 ICT student book): the hardware year is taught through the von Neumann model (the book pictures John von Neumann beside an early machine) and the software chapter ends with a full comparison table of real compilers (Visual Basic, Turbo Pascal, C++, Fortran…) by platform and licence.


Pakistan's own way (Grade 6 Computer Science 6): every unit opens with Students Learning Outcomes, is dotted with "Do you Know?", "Extra Bit!" and "Tips" boxes, and closes with Summary, MCQ/short/detailed exercises, project-based and activity-based questions, with an answers key at the back. Digital skills (Windows desktop, file management, Paint 3D, a typing tutor) are taught hands-on BEFORE programming; digital citizenship is framed through Pakistani citizenship first; the entrepreneurship unit teaches from Pakistani founders (Foodpanda/EatOye, Shopsy.pk, Zero Makeup, Investors Lounge).
