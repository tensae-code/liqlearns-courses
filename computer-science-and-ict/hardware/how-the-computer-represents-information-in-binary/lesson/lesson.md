# How the Computer Represents Information in Binary

## Do it first

Grade 6 met forms of information; Grade 7 opens the machine to see what becomes of them inside. Mongolia's answer is one word — binary — and this lesson is the inside view: memory, codes, and the scheme every computer obeys.

## Sub lesson 1 — Binary in memory, codes for characters

Inside the computer, information is represented in **binary form**: patterns of 0s and 1s held in the machine's **memory**. Numbers are stored as their binary values; text is stored by **character codes** — each character matched to a number, and the number stored in binary — so a page of Mongolian text is, inside, a long row of coded numbers. Pictures and sounds meet the same fate in their own codings. The rule has no exceptions: whatever the computer holds, it holds as binary.

## Sub lesson 2 — Data and program — the working scheme

Memory holds two kinds of binary: **data**, the information being worked on, and the **program**, the instructions for working on it — stored in the same memory, in the same form, fetched piece by piece. The book draws the whole machine as one scheme: **input** brings information in, the **processor** works on it in step with memory, and **output** carries results out. Processor, memory, input, output — the scheme every later hardware lesson hangs its parts on, taught here before the parts themselves multiply.
