# Beep codes — what the start-up sounds mean

## Do it first

Listen in your mind to a computer starting: one short beep. Now imagine: beep… beep… beep…, on and on. The sounds are a code. Write down what you think one short beep means, then learn the patterns.

## Sub lesson 1 — One short beep — and silence

Each BIOS maker has its own beep patterns for hardware failures, but some readings are widely shared. The happiest sound is **one short beep: a normal boot** — nothing is wrong.

Silence tells its own story:

- **No beep, but the machine runs** — the **beeper itself may be dead**; the machine cannot speak, though it works.
- **No beep at all** — the **power supply is unplugged, off, or dead**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Power-supply patterns

Two repeating patterns both point at the power supply:

- **Steady short beeps** — the power supply is **bad, or its voltages are wrong**; replacement is usually needed.
- **Steady long beeps** — the power supply has **gone bad**.

A beeping pattern that never stops is the machine asking for a new power supply, not for repeated restarts.

## Sub lesson 3 — RAM and video patterns

- **One long, continuous beep** — a **RAM stick has failed**. With two sticks, **remove one at a time** to find the bad one. With one stick, **replace it or upgrade it**.
- **One long and two short beeps** — **video-card failure**. Fix order: **reseat the card first** (take it out and fit it firmly back), **then replace it** if the fault stays.

The standing method for every pattern: **note the exact sequence, then search it online** — your machine's BIOS maker reads its own code best.

Remember the safety rule from the first lesson of this unit: switch the computer **off** before removing or replacing any part — RAM sticks and video cards included.

## Say it to your companion

Read your companion three patterns — steady short beeps; one long continuous beep; one long and two short. They name the fault and the first fix for each. Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. A machine gives one short beep and starts normally. Write what the beep told you.
2. A machine gives one long, continuous beep and has two RAM sticks. Write your steps, in order.
3. Note a pattern you hear on a real machine (or one your teacher plays). Search the exact sequence and write what your BIOS maker says it means.
