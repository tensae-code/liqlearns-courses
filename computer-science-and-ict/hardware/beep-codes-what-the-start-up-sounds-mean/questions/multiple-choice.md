# Beep codes — what the start-up sounds mean — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** One short beep means —
a) RAM failure  b) a normal boot  c) video failure  d) power failure
**Answer: b) a normal boot** — the healthy sound.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** No beep, but the machine runs, points at —
a) the RAM  b) a dead beeper  c) the video card  d) the disk
**Answer: b) a dead beeper** — the machine works but cannot speak.

**3. (Sub lesson 1)** No beep at all points at —
a) the keyboard  b) the power supply — unplugged, off or dead  c) the monitor cable  d) a virus
**Answer: b) the power supply — unplugged, off or dead** — no power, no beep.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** Steady short beeps mean —
a) normal boot  b) power supply bad or voltages wrong  c) RAM failed  d) video failed
**Answer: b) power supply bad or voltages wrong** — a power-supply pattern.

**5. (Sub lesson 2)** For steady power-supply beeps, the book's outcome is usually —
a) reseat the card  b) replacement of the power supply  c) restart the machine  d) ignore them
**Answer: b) replacement of the power supply** — replacement is usually needed.

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** One long, continuous beep means —
a) a RAM stick has failed  b) the video card failed  c) a normal boot  d) the beeper is dead
**Answer: a) a RAM stick has failed** — the RAM pattern.

**7. (Sub lesson 3)** With two RAM sticks and a continuous beep, you —
a) replace both at once  b) remove one at a time to find the bad one  c) remove the video card  d) search for a new beeper
**Answer: b) remove one at a time to find the bad one** — one at a time finds the bad stick.

**8. (Sub lesson 3)** One long and two short beeps: the first fix is —
a) replace the card  b) reseat the video card  c) replace the RAM  d) replace the power supply
**Answer: b) reseat the video card** — reseat first, replace second.
