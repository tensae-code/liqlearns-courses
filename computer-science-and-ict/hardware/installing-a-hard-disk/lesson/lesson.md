# Installing a Hard Disk

## Do it first

The hard disk is where the machine's world will live — its system, programs and files. Laos's Grade 8 chapter mounts it as its own install, with two connections that must both be right. This lesson fits it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Mounting in the bay

The disk is fitted into a **drive bay** of its size inside the case and **screwed firm on its sides** — a hard disk spins at thousands of turns a minute, and a loose disk vibrates, suffers and fails early. It is handled **by its body, never dropped or knocked**: of all the parts in the chapter, the disk is the one a single fall can kill outright, taking every file it held with it.

In your mind's eye: mounting in the bay.

## Sub lesson 2 — Power and data — the two leads

A mounted disk is still nothing until it is joined twice. The **power lead** comes from the power supply; the **data lead** joins the disk to the board, carrying everything read and written. Each lead fits its own socket, one way, without force. Laos's check: both leads seated, disk firm, and at the install of the system later in the chapter the disk must **appear to the machine** — an unseen disk is a lead or a seat to check, not a program to reinstall.

In your mind's eye: power and data — the two leads.
