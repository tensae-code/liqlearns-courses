## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What the display adapter does** — The **display adapter** (graphics card) is the part that **produces the image the monitor shows**. It takes the picture the programs describe and renders it — every letter, window, photograph and frame of a game — into the signal sent to the screen, at the screen's size and rate. It may live as a **card in an expansion slot** or be **built into** the board or processor; either way, the screen shows nothing without this work being done somewhere.
2. **The GPU — a processor for pictures** — The adapter's engine is the **GPU (graphics processing unit)** — a processor specialised for picture work. Where the main CPU handles tasks one deep chain at a time, picture work is thousands of small, similar calculations at once — the colour of every dot on the screen — and the GPU is built to do **many of them in parallel**. The adapter carries its **own memory** for the images it is building. Laos names the GPU inside the hardware year because choosing a machine for drawing, video or games is choosing this part: for picture work, the GPU, not the CPU, is the limit.
