# The Display Adapter and the GPU

## Do it first

The processor can calculate a picture, but something must turn those calculations into the signal a screen can show, fast enough that a moving image does not stutter. Laos's Grade 8 hardware chapter gives that worker its own section: the display adapter. This lesson meets it.

## Sub lesson 1 — What the display adapter does

The **display adapter** (graphics card) is the part that **produces the image the monitor shows**. It takes the picture the programs describe and renders it — every letter, window, photograph and frame of a game — into the signal sent to the screen, at the screen's size and rate. It may live as a **card in an expansion slot** or be **built into** the board or processor; either way, the screen shows nothing without this work being done somewhere.

In your mind's eye: what the display adapter does.

## Sub lesson 2 — The GPU — a processor for pictures

The adapter's engine is the **GPU (graphics processing unit)** — a processor specialised for picture work. Where the main CPU handles tasks one deep chain at a time, picture work is thousands of small, similar calculations at once — the colour of every dot on the screen — and the GPU is built to do **many of them in parallel**. The adapter carries its **own memory** for the images it is building. Laos names the GPU inside the hardware year because choosing a machine for drawing, video or games is choosing this part: for picture work, the GPU, not the CPU, is the limit.

In your mind's eye: the GPU — a processor for pictures.
