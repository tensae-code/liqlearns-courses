# Naming the Digital Devices Around Us

## Do it first

Point at the things in this room you could name in one word each. The digital ones hide among the furniture. Before reading on, list every digital device you can see or remember in this room — names only.

## Sub lesson 1 — The names of the devices

Before using devices well, the youngest pupils learn to **name** them: the **tablet (tablette)**, the **computer (ordinateur)**, the **screen (écran)**, the **mouse (souris)**, the keyboard, the phone, the camera. The scenario's fiche (Les différents appareils) is a naming hunt: picture, name, and the thing itself matched together — because a class cannot talk about its tools, share them, or take care of them until everyone calls them by the same names.

In your mind's eye: the names of the devices.

## Sub lesson 2 — One name, one thing

Naming is also sorting: a tablet is not a computer-without-keyboard in the child's first map, a screen is not the whole computer, and the mouse is a device of its own, not a toy animal. Each name pins one thing, and the pinning is the beginning of all the course's later precision — instructions, programs and care routines all fail when names slide between things.

In your mind's eye: one name, one thing.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: Tablette, ordinateur, écran, souris (tablet, computer, screen, mouse). Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: Picture, name, and the thing itself. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: A class cannot talk about, share or care for tools it cannot name together. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: Les différents appareils — the different devices. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
