# Storage devices — keeping data

## Do it first

Two kinds of keeping happen in your own desk: the exercise book open in front of you right now, and the cupboard where last year's books sleep. A computer keeps things both ways too. This lesson draws the line between them.

## Storage devices — keeping data

A storage device stores data — temporarily, or permanently. That 'or' is the whole lesson, because storage comes in two kinds. 

[Picture 10133 — a "now" shelf (primary) beside a "keep for good" cabinet (secondary)]



**Primary storage** — main memory — holds current data, the programs and the instructions the processor needs **now**. It is the open book on the desk: close to hand, in use this moment.

**Secondary storage** keeps data **permanently**. It is the cupboard: things put away safely, still there tomorrow and next year, whether the processor is using them this moment or not.

Now shelf and keep-for-good cabinet. Every storage device you meet in the next lessons belongs on one side of that line.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: A storage device stores data temporarily or permanently: primary storage (main memory) holds current data, programs and instructions the processor needs now, while secondary storage keeps data permanently. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
