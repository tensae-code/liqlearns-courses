# Lesson plan — Installing apps on mobile devices — use the proper store

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Hardware · **Lesson:** Installing apps on mobile devices — use the proper store
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Maintenance and Troubleshooting (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Phones and smart devices get their apps from their own system's store — the store is the safe door, and unknown downloads are the dangerous one.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on Installing software on a PC: trusted sources there become the proper store here.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or in steps — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **The proper store for each device** — apps come from the operating system's own store; the five named stores; free and paid apps; finding the right source first.
2. **Why unknown sources damage phones** — the damage unknown downloads can do; the phone's security settings that scan, alert and delete.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Installing software on a PC, in this same title. Recommended, never forced — a learner may land on this lesson directly, and it stands alone.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the five app stores and match a device to its store.
2. Explain why downloads from unknown sources are dangerous.
3. Say what the phone's security settings do about harmful apps.

## What you need
A phone if one is available to look at (no installing needed) — paper works too. Paper and a pencil.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks free means safe → the store also has free apps; the danger is the unknown source, not the price.
- Cannot find the store → the store belongs to the operating system; look for its own icon first.
