# Frequently Used Combinational Circuits — matching

Match each item on the left with the one it belongs to on the right.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What does an encoder do?** → **It turns one active input line (of 2ⁿ) into its n-bit binary code**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What does a decoder do?** → **It activates exactly one of 2ⁿ output lines, chosen by its n-bit code**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) Give an encoder at work.** → **Inside a keyboard — naming the pressed key as a code**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) Give a decoder at work.** → **Choosing which memory bank or display digit a code addresses**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) What is the pair's relation?** → **Mirror images around a code — compress lines to bits; expand bits to one line**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What does a multiplexer do?** → **It chooses one of several inputs to send to its single output, steered by control bits**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What does a demultiplexer do?** → **It routes one input to one of several outputs**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What does a comparator report?** → **Whether its two numbers stand greater, equal or less — three outputs**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) Which family do all these belong to?** → **Combinational circuits — output a pure function of today's inputs, no memory**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) What is built from this toolkit?** → **Everything memoryless the CPU does — its present-tense electronics**
