# Frequently Used Combinational Circuits — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What does an encoder do?**
**Answer: It turns one active input line (of 2ⁿ) into its n-bit binary code**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What does a decoder do?**
**Answer: It activates exactly one of 2ⁿ output lines, chosen by its n-bit code**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) Give an encoder at work.**
**Answer: Inside a keyboard — naming the pressed key as a code**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) Give a decoder at work.**
**Answer: Choosing which memory bank or display digit a code addresses**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) What is the pair's relation?**
**Answer: Mirror images around a code — compress lines to bits; expand bits to one line**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What does a multiplexer do?**
**Answer: It chooses one of several inputs to send to its single output, steered by control bits**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What does a demultiplexer do?**
**Answer: It routes one input to one of several outputs**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What does a comparator report?**
**Answer: Whether its two numbers stand greater, equal or less — three outputs**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) Which family do all these belong to?**
**Answer: Combinational circuits — output a pure function of today's inputs, no memory**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) What is built from this toolkit?**
**Answer: Everything memoryless the CPU does — its present-tense electronics**
