# Computer hardware — four categories

## Do it first

Touch everything on a computer desk, one by one: keys, mouse, box, screen, printer. Before reading on, sort them into four piles by what they do to data: put it in, work on it, keep it, or show it.

## Hardware — what you can touch

Hardware is the physical part of a computer that can be seen and touched. Divide it by the information-processing flow — the journey data takes, which you already know as the four functions — and hardware falls into four categories: input devices, processing devices, storage devices and output devices.

## Input devices — data enters, changed into 0 and 1

Input devices enter data into the computer, converting it into the 0-and-1 form the computer understands. The unit's list is: keyboard, mouse, scanner, optical recognition systems, speech recognition, joystick, light pen and touch screen. Each gets its own lesson in this title.

## How this unit is taught (teacher guide — evidence)

The teacher guide fixes how this unit is evidenced: every family — input, processing, storage, output — is taught in the laboratory or with the real devices brought into class, in groups, with a representative reporting back; and a pupil must be able to explain, for input, the mouse, the keyboard, optical mark recognition and optical character recognition, and for output, the monitor, printer, headphones, speakers, projector, braille reader and plotter (teacher guide — evidence). It restates the two conversions as the teaching core: input devices turn entered data into the electronic 0-and-1 form, and output devices turn the computer's form back into a human-understandable soft or hard copy.
