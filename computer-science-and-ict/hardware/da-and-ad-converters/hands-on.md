# Hands-on — DA and AD Converters

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Staircase by hand
An 8-bit DAC has Vref = 5 V. Compute its output for the codes 00000000, 01000000, 10000000, 11000000 and 11111111, and draw the five points as the first steps of the staircase. Then say how many steps the full staircase has and the height of one step in volts.

## Convert a voltage
A 4-bit ADC covers 0–8 V. (a) How wide is each level? (b) The input is 5.2 V: which level number does it fall in, and what 4-bit code comes out? (c) State the largest error the quantisation could be hiding, in volts, and connect it to the digitising lesson's name for it.

## In the simulator
In TINA or Circuit Wizard, place a DAC block (or the R–2R ladder if offered) and step its input through 0000, 0010, 0100, 1000 and 1111, recording the output voltage each time; check the outputs double and halve with the place values. Then drive an ADC with a slowly rising voltage and watch its output code climb — name the three digitising steps as they happen inside.
