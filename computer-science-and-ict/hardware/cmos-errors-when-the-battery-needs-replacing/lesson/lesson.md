# CMOS errors — when the battery needs replacing

## Do it first

A school computer has started showing the wrong date every morning, and an alert about CMOS appears at start-up. Write what you think has died inside the machine, and what the fix is. Then read the doing steps in order.

## Sub lesson 1 — What CMOS holds

The **CMOS chip** stores **everything from the time and date to the system hardware settings** — and it **holds the BIOS configuration**. A small battery keeps that memory alive while the machine is off.

When the battery runs down, the machine begins to forget: the clock slips, settings are lost, and an alert appears.

## Sub lesson 2 — Replacing the battery — the doing

A **CMOS alert on screen means its battery needs replacing**. The doing, in order:

1. Switch the computer **off** (the unit's safety rule, always).
2. **Remove the old battery carefully.**
3. **Fit a new battery exactly the same** as the old one.

[Picture 17705 — a small round battery being lifted from a motherboard, its identical replacement beside it]

Two cautions travel with the job:

- **Laptops are harder than desktops** — the battery sits deeper and the case opens less kindly.
- **Anyone without enough troubleshooting experience should leave the job to a professional technician.** Knowing when NOT to do a job is part of troubleshooting.
