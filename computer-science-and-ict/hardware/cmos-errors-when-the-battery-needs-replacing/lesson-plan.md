# Lesson plan — CMOS errors — when the battery needs replacing

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Hardware · **Lesson:** CMOS errors — when the battery needs replacing
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Hardware Troubleshooting and Preventive Maintenance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The CMOS chip holds the time, date and hardware settings; when its battery dies, a CMOS alert appears — and the fix is a new battery, exactly the same, fitted carefully.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on BIOS — the program that starts the computer: CMOS holds the BIOS configuration.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What CMOS holds** — The CMOS chip stores everything from the time and date to the system hardware settings — it holds the BIOS configuration.
2. **Replacing the battery — the doing** — A CMOS alert on screen means the battery needs replacing: remove the old battery carefully and fit a new one exactly the same. Cautions: laptops are harder than desktops; without enough experience, leave the job to a professional technician.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. A machine shows a CMOS alert. Write the fix in three numbered steps.
2. Write why the new battery must be exactly the same as the old one — in one sentence of your own, from what the lesson teaches about what CMOS holds.
3. Your friend's laptop shows a CMOS alert and she has never opened a computer. Write your advice, using the book's caution.

## What you need
