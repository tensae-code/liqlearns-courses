# Troubleshooting — find the fault, find its cause

## Do it first

The school computer shows a black screen and nothing else. Three pupils shout three different fixes at once. Write the calmer first move a troubleshooter makes — then work the lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — A systematic approach — hardware and software

**Troubleshooting is a systematic approach to locating a fault and identifying its cause** in a computer system. It covers **hardware problems** — mice, monitors, printers and modems malfunctioning — and **software problems** — the operating system and application software, including **protecting systems from malicious damage such as viruses and worms**.

## Sub lesson 2 — A skill that grows — and what to do when stuck

**No two troubleshooting processes are identical.** Users **refine their skill from knowledge and experience**, and can **fix common issues without a professional**. When stuck: **search the internet first** — others have met the same problem and shared solutions — and **ask a knowledgeable friend or family member without being afraid**. The black-screen case in Do it first is set as an investigation of exactly this kind: work it systematically, symptom by symptom.
