**Acceptance count: taught in 9 countries (Ethiopia, Iraq, Fiji, Sri Lanka, Laos, Jordan, Armenia, Vietnam, South Sudan).**

The student book sets the black-screen investigation as an activity with no printed solution (printed p. 143); the lesson teaches the systematic approach for it and invents no book answer. The book uses the number "Activity 5.2" for this troubleshooting activity as well as for the earlier uninstalling activity; the lesson teaches the skill, not the activity number.

Iraq's own way (Grade 10): the preparatory stage turns practical and examinable — a whole unit on computer maintenance (preventive vs corrective, hardware and software, hard-disk management with defragmentation, ScanDisk, partitions and FAT32 vs NTFS), then PowerPoint and Microsoft Publisher taught tab by tab, looping control structures in Scratch, and a final unit pairing HTML web-page building with information security.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Grade 9): one TIA teacher guide carries two ICT subjects side by side — Communication Technology (networks, ergonomics, safety, societal impact) and Computer Technology (architecture, software) — organised by content standards and numbered benchmarks (9.4.x / 9.5.x), with the design brief used as the problem-solving method for both a product and a simple network, and typing assessed at 20 wpm with 80% accuracy. (teacher guide — evidence, PARTIAL)

Papua New Guinea's own way (Grade 11): the senior guide turns learners into junior system administrators and builders — computer administration, datasheets ("data books") and timing technology, documenting server and printer configurations, data science, pseudocode, Green IT, and building static web pages with tools like Canva and Figma; typing rises to 40 wpm at 85–100% accuracy. (teacher guide — evidence)

Fiji's own way (Year 13 Computer Education student book): The senior book is built around a Fijian case organisation (Viti Group of Companies, a Fiji furniture manufacturer) for information systems; it teaches the full systems life cycle phase by phase, web design with real HTML and CSS, and text programming in C++ with worked programs (pay calculation, grade from mark, triangle and circle areas) using Fijian student names.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 8 Reading Book): number systems and binary conversion open Grade 8; "physical computing" is logic gates taught through water-valve and street-lamp analogies; Scratch selection structures and mobile/smart-device apps sit in the Programming chapter.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 8 Workbook): a companion workbook mirroring the Reading Book (Activities 1.1–6.3), with worked conversion drills for number systems.

Laos's own way (Grade 8 ICT student book): a full PC-technician year in full colour — learners take a computer apart and rebuild it (CPU, RAM, board, cards, drives), install Windows XP and drivers, fix install faults by BIOS beep codes, then finish with networks and the internet.

Switzerland's own way (Grades 7–8, Décodage 7e–8e): at 7e–8e the Décodage course becomes a real computer-science course under the Plan d'études romand EN 22/23: Thymio VPL missions (including Morse communication and self-driving-car autonomy levels), a full Scratch discovery course (movement, shortening programs, sub-programs as own blocks, remixing), a Scratch video-game project run in “studios” with a cahier des charges, data work (Huffman-style compression via Zip la marmotte, binary/octal/hex/ASCII codings, file sizes), access security (passwords, file encryption, phishing, authentication), home networks at two levels (IP addresses; message routing, packets, TCP and encryption), and inquiries into sorting, sorting networks, Caesar decryption and parity-bit error detection. Médias/Usages covers social networks and digital identity/footprint, information sources, cyberbullying and the environmental cost of digital technology.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 8 G8T1 Student): Grade 8 Term 1 is a maintenance year — a full unit on troubleshooting hardware and software faults with Windows 10 tools — paired with a from-zero Scratch unit (screen, project, design elements, blocks, variables, control).

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 4 G4 student): Grade 4 (Stepanyan, Amirkhanyan, Matinyan) opens with e-security and health (recognising device malfunctions) and what to tell about yourself online, then purposeful documents (paragraph formats, inserted pictures, tables), slide presentations, e-mail, files, solving technical problems, ways of picturing data, and a Scratch strand of story animation, conditions, loops and variables.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 4 G4 teacher): Companion to Book 5 in the Zangak guide template: exemplary programme and thematic planning (Theme 1 covers electrical safety and health, with the device-malfunctions lesson and its safety outcomes spelled out), textbook and task structure notes, formative-assessment checklists, sample daily lesson plans (Theme 8-1, solving technical problems, starting from power and network checks), and explanations of selected textbook tasks.

Vietnam's own way (Grade 10 Chuyên đề Tin học 10 Khoa học máy tính): the CS study-topics book for Grade 10 is not more Python — it is a full educational-robotics course (a STEM orientation, per its preface): build the robot, connect it to the computer, then program it to move, sense obstacles and follow a line on a test course (sa bàn).

South Sudan's own way (Grade 9 S1 Student): Opens ICT at secondary level entirely through doing — the whole first year is Microsoft Office application skills (Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Publisher, Paint) taught as step-by-step keyboard/mouse procedures with local data (South Sudan state populations, local disease statistics for charts), then internet use, hands-on troubleshooting, and Windows access control/security settings.

South Sudan's own way (Grade 9 S1 Teacher): The guide teaches the same four units as the student book through co-operative group investigation, with answer keys and assessment guidelines; distinctive content includes using the internet to promote South Sudanese culture and creating user accounts from the Windows command line (net user). (teacher guide — evidence)
