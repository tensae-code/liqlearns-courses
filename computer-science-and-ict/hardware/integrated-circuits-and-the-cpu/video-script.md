# Video script — Integrated Circuits and the CPU

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

An **integrated circuit (IC)** is a whole circuit — transistors, gates, their connections — **built as one piece on a single sliver (chip) of semiconductor**. Its **characteristics** are the chapter's measures: how **many** components it gathers (the integration scale, climbing from small-scale handfuls to the billions of today's processors), its **speed**, its **power** hunger, and its **pin** connections to the world outside. Integration is why the computer shrank: the room of wired panels became a thumbnail, cheaper and faster at every gathering.
The **central processing unit** is the machine's most gathered IC: its **control unit** directing, its **arithmetic-logic unit** calculating, its **registers** holding the moment's work, all on chips whose component counts the chapter traces into the **modern trends** — more cores working in parallel, smaller transistors at the edge of the physical, speed bought by cleverness of organisation as much as by size. The ladder closes where it aimed: gates became circuits, circuits became chips, chips became the brain the whole course has been programming.
