# Lesson plan — Project — An Illustrated Mini-Dictionary of Computer Hardware

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Hardware · **Lesson:** Project — An Illustrated Mini-Dictionary of Computer Hardware
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Morocco (2026-11-14) — in the title Hardware on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Morocco's Grade 1 “J'excelle en informatique” teacher guide, because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson. This lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence only (Morocco), named in lesson/sources.md, counted nowhere.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The project's words are the hardware of the year: the computer and its peripherals — the mouse, the keyboard, the screen, the speakers and headphones, the camera, the microphone and the printer. Each entry joins a drawing or picture of the part to its name and one line saying what it does: the mouse — it moves the pointer and clicks; the printer — it puts documents and images on paper.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — You have spent the year meeting the computer piece by piece — mouse, keyboard, printer, camera. Grade 1's closing project turns that knowledge into a book of your own: a mini-dictionary where every hardware word gets its picture. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of choosing the words — the hardware you have met as the lesson describes it — the student studies the picture and names what each part shows.
   - ADD GIF: a GIF of drawing and writing each entry in action — the student watches once, then says what changed and why.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the lesson's key names and definitions aloud, in the order the lesson gives them — the student listens once through, then reads the same lines aloud along with the voice. (Audio is a described slot in this build; no audio file is made.)
3. **Say it to your companion** — the companion block in lesson/lesson.md.
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the lesson in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-false, matching, short-answer — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the doing block at the end of lesson/lesson.md.

## If the learner is stuck
Returns to the one thing this lesson teaches and works it with a smaller example of their own, then tries the stuck part again. Mixes this lesson's tools or terms with a neighbouring lesson's → place the two side by side and name the difference in one sentence each.
