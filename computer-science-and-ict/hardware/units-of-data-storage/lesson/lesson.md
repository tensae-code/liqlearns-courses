# Units of data storage — bit, byte, KB, MB, GB, TB

## Do it first

You have already met two sizes in this unit: a CD's 700 MB and a DVD's 8.4 GB. But what is an MB? And how many of them make a GB? Climb the staircase in this lesson and those numbers will never puzzle you again.

## Units of data storage — bit, byte, KB, MB, GB, TB

Storage size is measured in units, and the units climb a staircase. 

[Picture 10137 — a staircase of units, each step 1,024 of the one below]



At the very bottom stands the **bit**: a single binary digit — a 1 or a 0. Nothing smaller carries data.

Eight bits make one **byte**: 1 byte = 8 bits.

From the byte upward, every step is **1,024** of the step below:

- **Kilobyte (KB)** = 1,024 bytes
- **Megabyte (MB)** = 1,024 KB
- **Gigabyte (GB)** = 1,024 MB
- **Terabyte (TB)** = 1,024 GB

Now the sizes from the last lesson stand on the staircase: a CD's 700 MB and a DVD's 8.4 GB are both measured in the middle steps — and a terabyte stands four great steps above a single byte, 1,024 at a time.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Storage size is measured from the bit (a single binary digit, 1 or 0) through the byte (8 bits), kilobyte (1,024 bytes), megabyte (1,024 KB), gigabyte (1,024 MB) and terabyte (1,024 GB). Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
