**Acceptance count: taught in 14 countries (Ethiopia, Uganda, Uzbekistan, Cuba, Egypt, Bhutan, Malaysia, Mongolia, Sri Lanka, Laos, Moldova, Indonesia, Vietnam, Pakistan).**

No book error touches this lesson's content.


Uzbekistan's own way (Grade 8): grade 8 is theory-first — number systems, coding and Boolean logic before the machine's own story — then a full operating-system chapter and a five-lesson Word course; safety rules return as a closing appendix.

Cuba's own way (Grade 7): Secondary Informática opens by contrasting proprietary Windows with Cuba's free operating system Nova; programming continues the primary Scratch line, here centred on the Scratch pen tool for drawing geometric figures and the linear function; word-processor work ends by exporting documents to HTML for the web.

Egypt's own way (Grade 10, Term 1 Programming and Artificial Intelligence student book): A full Japanese-style "Information Study" course adopted for Egypt's new secondary track: 13 chapters running from information theory, ethics and security through number systems, data analysis (frequency, variance, correlation, regression in spreadsheets), simulation and queues, Python, and a final AI-programming project chapter building web pages, a quiz app and a block-breaker game with generative AI.

Bhutan's own way (Class VIII, Literacy with ICT student book): The final primary/middle year is project-led — a full Audacity audio unit (record, edit, mix, share), information ethics taught through Copyright, Creative Commons and software piracy, and Scratch taught to game level with lists, custom blocks (functions) and logical operators.

Malaysia's own way (Grade 7, ASK Tingkatan 1): the KSSM Asas Sains Komputer (ASK — Foundations of Computer Science) course is built on the four computational-thinking techniques (decomposition, pattern recognition, abstraction, generalisation) introduced through a local food example (making popia basah); programming is done in Scratch 2.0, then learners build a real interactive website in HTML in the same year; every chapter ends with a mind map, self-check list and self-practice.

Mongolia's own way (Grade 7): the whole applications half is one long project — research and produce a printed booklet "Усаа хайрлая" (Cherish the Water) in Word, a Paint.NET layered picture, and a class PowerPoint; image work uses the free Paint.NET (downloaded from getpaint.net) rather than MS Paint; the e-communication chapter teaches Yahoo mail and Yahoo Messenger, Mongolian history of the örtöö relay post and the @ sign, and makes correct Latin transliteration of Mongolian Cyrillic (standard MNS 5217:2003) an ethics topic in its own right.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 8 Reading Book): number systems and binary conversion open Grade 8; "physical computing" is logic gates taught through water-valve and street-lamp analogies; Scratch selection structures and mobile/smart-device apps sit in the Programming chapter.

Laos's own way (Grade 11 ICT student book): a single-topic year — databases from first principles (the data hierarchy is taught as a Lao glossary table: Bit, Byte/Character, Field, Record, Files, Database) into a complete MS Access 2007 build: tables, relationships, forms, queries, reports.

Moldova's own way (Grade 2, Educație Digitală): Grade 2 builds the idea of information quantity (bit, byte, binary drawing) before any office software, teaches the keyboard including touch-typing posture, and keeps programming unplugged-but-precise: loops, jump (pen-up) instructions and multi-loop programs for the mouse, the painter and the hedgehog. The Digital Education module sits inside Technological Education.

Moldova's own way (Grade 7, Informatică): lower-secondary Informatica is a real textbook discipline, not computer-skills practice — number systems, text/image/sound encoding and data types in Pascal sit alongside GUI literacy, and two full chapters are given to behaviour, law and information culture in virtual spaces.

Moldova's own way (Grade 11, Informatică): Grade 11 is the theory year — how numbers, logic and circuits actually work inside the machine (Boolean algebra, adders, flip-flops, instruction formats, assembly language) — with structured programming (arrays, records, sets, files) carried over from Grade 10's languages; some sections are profile-specific (Real/Umanist).

Switzerland's own way (Grades 7–8, Décodage 7e–8e): at 7e–8e the Décodage course becomes a real computer-science course under the Plan d'études romand EN 22/23: Thymio VPL missions (including Morse communication and self-driving-car autonomy levels), a full Scratch discovery course (movement, shortening programs, sub-programs as own blocks, remixing), a Scratch video-game project run in “studios” with a cahier des charges, data work (Huffman-style compression via Zip la marmotte, binary/octal/hex/ASCII codings, file sizes), access security (passwords, file encryption, phishing, authentication), home networks at two levels (IP addresses; message routing, packets, TCP and encryption), and inquiries into sorting, sorting networks, Caesar decryption and parity-bit error detection. Médias/Usages covers social networks and digital identity/footprint, information sources, cyberbullying and the environmental cost of digital technology.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 7 G7 Informatika student): Kurikulum Merdeka Informatika built on four elements (Berpikir Komputasional, Literasi Digital, Analisis Data, Algoritma dan Pemrograman), mixing plugged and unplugged activities with coded activity IDs (e.g. VII-BK, VII-LD) and QR-linked resources; Grade 7 covers only BK + Literasi Digital + data handling.

Vietnam's own way (Grade 6 Tin học 6): the first lower-secondary book names its software openly (Google Chrome, MindMaple Lite, Microsoft Word in the how-to-use page) and its three characters each stand for one strand of the subject — digital literacy, ICT, and computer science; information safety gets a full lesson before any applied software work.


Pakistan's own way (Grade 6 Computer Science 6): every unit opens with Students Learning Outcomes, is dotted with "Do you Know?", "Extra Bit!" and "Tips" boxes, and closes with Summary, MCQ/short/detailed exercises, project-based and activity-based questions, with an answers key at the back. Digital skills (Windows desktop, file management, Paint 3D, a typing tutor) are taught hands-on BEFORE programming; digital citizenship is framed through Pakistani citizenship first; the entrepreneurship unit teaches from Pakistani founders (Foodpanda/EatOye, Shopsy.pk, Zero Makeup, Investors Lounge).

Pakistan's own way (Grade 7 Computer Science 7): Grade 7 opens with emerging technologies and a deep ports-and-peripherals hardware treatment, then teaches email all the way to DKIM/SPF/DMARC authentication and POP3/IMAP/SMTP protocols; binary data representation (ASCII/Unicode, RGB colour, sound sampling) is taught inside the Programming unit, and entrepreneurship becomes business-plan craft — promotion, value proposition, quality assurance, SEO and platform business models, with Twitter and Snapchat origin stories.

Pakistan's own way (Grade 9 Computer Science 9): Grade 9 is a full problem-solving course before any coding — candid solutions, the 5 Ws of problem analysis, test data in five types, trace tables, verification vs validation — then number systems carried through to Boolean algebra with truth tables, networks taught through the postal-system analogy for TCP/IP layers, a data-and-privacy unit built on Code.org cipher widgets, NADRA and Pakistan's NR3C cybercrime centre, and a first website built page by page as "Pakistan.html".
