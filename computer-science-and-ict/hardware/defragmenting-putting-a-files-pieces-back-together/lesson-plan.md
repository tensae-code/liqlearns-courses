# Lesson plan — Defragmenting — putting a file's pieces back together

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Hardware · **Lesson:** Defragmenting — putting a file's pieces back together
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Hardware Troubleshooting and Preventive Maintenance (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Files deleted and updated leave their pieces scattered across the disk — fragmentation — and reading scattered files is slow; a defragmenter gathers the pieces into adjoining regions and speeds the machine up.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on Backups — copy your work before anything goes wrong: disk care comes after data safety.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or in steps — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Fragmentation — what it is and why it slows the machine** — As files are deleted and updated, a file's pieces get scattered into non-adjacent spaces; reading scattered files takes the system longer. (The guide's plain-consequence definition — teacher evidence — says the same.)
2. **Defragmenting — the book's steps (doing)** — Search Windows Administrative Tools and open it; double-click Defragment and Optimize Drives; select the drive; click Optimize. A drive at 0% fragmented needs no defragmentation.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks defragging deletes files → it reorganises pieces of the SAME files into adjoining regions; nothing is deleted.
- Runs Optimize on a 0% drive → the book's note: 0% fragmented needs no defragmentation.
