# Input devices — putting data in

## Do it first

Think of everything that entered a computer the last time you saw one used: words typed, a photo taken, a voice recorded, a price scanned. Each thing entered through a different door. This lesson names all the doors at once.

## Input devices — putting data in

Input devices insert data into the computer system. Data cannot walk in by itself; every piece of it crosses through an input device.

The book's list has eight: 

[Picture 10122 — the eight named devices in a labelled grid]



1. **Keyboard** — typed words and numbers.
2. **Mouse** — pointing, clicking, moving things.
3. **Scanner** — paper documents and photographs, turned into softcopy.
4. **Touch screen** — worked by touching.
5. **Light pen** — a pen-shaped pointer for the screen.
6. **Barcode reader** — the striped codes on goods.
7. **Microphone** — the spoken word.
8. **Camera** — photographs.

Eight doors into the computer. The lessons after this one walk through them in small groups, device by device.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Input devices insert data into the computer system — the book's list is keyboard, mouse, scanner, touch screen, light pen, barcode reader, microphone and camera. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: say what input devices do: insert data into the computer system. Do this with a real example from your own home, school or street, and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: name the book's eight input devices. Do this with a real example from your own home, school or street, and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: give, for any named device, the kind of data it puts in. Do this with a real example from your own home, school or street, and write or say how you know it is right.
