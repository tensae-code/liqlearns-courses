# Hands-on — Boolean Laws and De Morgan's Theorems

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Prove a law
Choose the second distributive law, A + B·C = (A + B)·(A + C). Build the truth table of both sides — eight rows — and mark the two final columns. Only when they match may you write '=' between them. This law has no twin in school algebra, which is why it must be proved, not believed.

## Simplify and verify
Simplify Y = A·B + A·B̄, step by step, naming each law. (Factor out A with distribution, use the complement law, finish with identity.) Then simplify the bar over (Ā + B) using De Morgan. Verify both answers with truth tables.

## In the simulator
In TINA or Circuit Wizard, build the gate circuit for A + A·B and for A alone (the gates lesson shows the gate symbols if you need them). Run all four input combinations on both and record the lamp: two circuits, one behaviour — absorption saving real gates.
