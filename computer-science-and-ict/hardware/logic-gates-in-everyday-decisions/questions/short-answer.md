# Logic Gates in Everyday Decisions — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What are many everyday decisions, in this lesson's phrase?**
**Answer: Logic in disguise — AND, OR and NOT wearing words**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) 'Card AND teacher present' is which gate?**
**Answer: AND — both inputs true, or no entry**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) 'Smoke OR heat' is which gate, and when does it fire?**
**Answer: OR — either input fires the alarm**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What does writing a rule as gates expose?**
**Answer: Which conditions are truly required, which are alternatives, and which combinations nobody considered**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) What is a truth table for a life decision?**
**Answer: Every combination of inputs with the output the rule gives — a pre-mortem for the decision**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What must be true of the inputs you list?**
**Answer: Stated honestly — a sometimes-true condition is an input, not a certainty**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) How big should the decision model be?**
**Answer: Small enough to check whole**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What is the model's limit?**
**Answer: Real life adds inputs the table never listed — the table guides judgement, it does not replace it**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) In which two languages did the class simulate the gates before this lesson?**
**Answer: VB.NET and PHP**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) What does Egypt say about a pupil who can model decisions with gates?**
**Answer: They own the gates — one who can only draw them, rents them**
