# More input devices — scanner, touch screen, light pen, camera

## Do it first

Hold four things in your mind: a paper photograph you wish was on a computer; a phone screen you touch instead of typing; a pen that writes on a screen itself; a camera full of pictures. Four problems, four devices. This lesson names them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Scanner and touch screen

A **scanner** converts a hardcopy document or photograph into softcopy. Hardcopy is the paper version you can hold; softcopy is the version inside the computer. The scanner is the bridge between them: paper goes in, a computer version comes out.

A **touch screen** is a touch-sensitive pointing device, worked by touching. No mouse, no keyboard between you and it — your finger is the pointer. Watch a tap land. 

[GIF 10126 — a finger tapping a touch screen and the screen responding]

 The finger touches, and the screen answers at exactly that spot.

## Sub lesson 2 — Light pen and camera — and the joystick (teacher guide — evidence)

A **light pen** is a pen-shaped pointing device. You use it on the screen itself, for choosing menu items or drawing on the screen — the pen the screen can feel.

A **camera** captures photographs and saves them in its memory. The photograph is data the moment it is captured, ready to cross into a computer. 

[Picture 10125 — four panels, one device each, each showing what goes in]



One more device belongs in this lesson as teacher-guide evidence: the **joystick**, which the guide's Activity 2.1 key adds to the input list — a device for moving within a screen environment, widely used in games.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: A scanner converts a hardcopy document or photograph into softcopy; a touch screen is a touch-sensitive pointing device worked by touching; a light pen is a pen-shaped pointing device for choosing menu items or drawing on the screen; and a camera captures photographs and saves them in its memory. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: say what a scanner does: converts hardcopy into softcopy; and say what hardcopy and softcopy mean. Do this with a real example from your own home, school or street, and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: describe a touch screen and a light pen as pointing devices, and how each is worked. Do this with a real example from your own home, school or street, and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: say what a camera does as an input device; and name the joystick as the guide's added input device, used in games. Do this with a real example from your own home, school or street, and write or say how you know it is right.
