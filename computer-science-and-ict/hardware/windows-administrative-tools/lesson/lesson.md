# Windows Administrative Tools

## Do it first

Beyond the diagnostic kit stands the manager's shelf: tools that configure, schedule, partition and guard the whole system. This lesson learns the administrative tools of Windows 10.

## Sub lesson 1 — The management shelf

Windows 10's **Administrative Tools** gather the system's management consoles: **Computer Management** (the umbrella console — users, disks, services, events in one frame), **Disk Management** (partitions and drives shaped), **Services** (the background workers started, stopped and set), **Task Scheduler** (jobs run at times and triggers — the maintenance calendar automated), **Windows Defender Firewall with Advanced Security** (the gate's detailed rules), and **Performance Monitor** (the system's loads measured over time).

## Sub lesson 2 — Administration — power with procedure

Administrative tools differ from ordinary ones by **reach**: they change the system for **every user**, persist beyond a session, and can break as grandly as they fix. So administration is taught with its **procedure**: work under an administrator account only when administering, **record** what was changed (the change log is the next troubleshooter's diary), **test** on one machine before the lab, and keep a **way back** (restore points, backups). Jordan closes the maintenance unit here: the pupil who began by dusting a case ends entrusted with the system's levers — and the lesson's last word is that the levers are lent, not owned.
