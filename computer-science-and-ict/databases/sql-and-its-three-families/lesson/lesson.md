# SQL — DDL, DML and DQL

## Do it first

Three jobs, three different tools in a workshop. Job one: build a new shelf. Job two: put books on the shelf and move them around. Job three: find one book and read its title. SQL has exactly these three kinds of job — and each kind has its own family name. Guess which family builds, which changes, and which asks, before you read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — SQL — one standard language

**SQL — Structured Query Language** — is the **standard special-purpose language for accessing and manipulating relational databases** such as Access. It is not one program's private dialect: it is the common language the relational world shares.

Every SQL command belongs to one of three families, grouped by purpose. The rest of this title teaches them in turn — first the family that builds, then the families that fill and ask.


## Sub lesson 2 — DDL — Data Definition Language

**Data Definition Language (DDL)** commands **create or modify tables and establish relationships**. Its three commands in this title: **CREATE TABLE**, **ALTER TABLE**, **DROP TABLE**.

DDL works on the *structure* — the shelves themselves, before anything is put on them.


## Sub lesson 3 — DML and DQL — changing data, asking for data

**Data Manipulation Language (DML)** commands **insert, update, delete and navigate data**: **INSERT**, **UPDATE**, **DELETE**.

**Data Query Language (DQL)** **queries — selects — all or a subset of the data**: its command is **SELECT**.

The three families are **common to all DBMSs** — learn them in Access and you know their shape everywhere.


## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the three families, one command from each, and what job each family does.
