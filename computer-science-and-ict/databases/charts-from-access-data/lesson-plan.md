**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Why chart data where it lives** — A **chart from Access data** draws a table's or query's records **without leaving the database**: totals by group — members per class, sales per month — become **columns, bars or a pie** that a reader takes in at a glance. The data already lives in Access (summarised by the queries of the chapter's first sections); the chart is the database's own way of **presenting** what its summaries found, for the reports and presentations the chapter ends with.
2. **Making the chart — source, type, reading** — The making follows the chapter's order: first the **source** — a table, or better a **query that has already summarised** the records into the groups to compare; then the **chart type fitted to the question** — columns to compare groups, a line for change over time, a pie for parts of one whole; then the **labels and title** that make the picture read honestly. The discipline is the course's data discipline in picture form: a chart of unsummarised raw records is noise, so the summary comes first and the drawing second — Access holds the data, the query shapes it, the chart shows it.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
