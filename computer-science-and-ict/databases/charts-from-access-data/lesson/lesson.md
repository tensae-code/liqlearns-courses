# Charts from Access Data

## Do it first

The Laos Grade 12 course summarises data in Access, then in Excel — and closes the movement by drawing it. Its final database section makes charts from Access data itself. This lesson draws them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Why chart data where it lives

A **chart from Access data** draws a table's or query's records **without leaving the database**: totals by group — members per class, sales per month — become **columns, bars or a pie** that a reader takes in at a glance. The data already lives in Access (summarised by the queries of the chapter's first sections); the chart is the database's own way of **presenting** what its summaries found, for the reports and presentations the chapter ends with.

## Sub lesson 2 — Making the chart — source, type, reading

The making follows the chapter's order: first the **source** — a table, or better a **query that has already summarised** the records into the groups to compare; then the **chart type fitted to the question** — columns to compare groups, a line for change over time, a pie for parts of one whole; then the **labels and title** that make the picture read honestly. The discipline is the course's data discipline in picture form: a chart of unsummarised raw records is noise, so the summary comes first and the drawing second — Access holds the data, the query shapes it, the chart shows it.
