# Data Entry Forms in Access — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What is a form in Access?**
**Answer: A layout showing one record at a time in labelled fields, for entering and reading data**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What is wrong with entering straight into a table?**
**Answer: The bare grid makes it easy to type into the wrong row or miss a field**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) Does a form hold its own data?**
**Answer: No — everything typed into it lands in the underlying table**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What does creating a form involve?**
**Answer: Choosing the table and arranging its fields — which appear, in what order, with what labels**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) What does filling a form involve?**
**Answer: Moving to a new blank record and typing each field**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) How do you view earlier records in a form?**
**Answer: With the navigation controls, moving record by record**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) Who is a form made for?**
**Answer: People who use the database without needing to see a table**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What does a well-made form do?**
**Answer: Makes correct entry the easy path**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) Where does this section stand in the Access course?**
**Answer: After linking tables — before queries**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) Can a form correct an existing record?**
**Answer: Yes — navigate to it and edit its fields**
