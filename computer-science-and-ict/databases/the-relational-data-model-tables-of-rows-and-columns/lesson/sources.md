Belarus's own way (Grade 11): grade 11 is a web-and-data year — hand-written HTML in Notepad, then the same site rebuilt in FrontPage, a long PascalABC graphics practicum borrowed from other school subjects, and a full Access database course (tables, links, forms, queries, sorting, reports).

Honesty: Sub lesson 3 stands on teacher-guide evidence only (the guide's worked Activity 3.5 answer) and is labelled as such; Sub lessons 1–2 are pupil-taught, so the lesson counts as taught in 1 country. The teacher placement summarises the nine-row joined table and its populations without reproducing the figures row by row, so this lesson teaches the join — the linking attribute, the field count, the Kenyan cities, the nine cities across the four named countries — and does not quote population figures it was not given. No student-book error touches these pages.

Grade 12 growth (2026-11-12, Wave F3): sub lesson 4's frame is pupil-taught from the Grade 12 student book; its example sets are teacher-guide evidence, labelled as such in the table above. The lesson's acceptance count stays taught in 1 country (Ethiopia) — the growth is the same country's Grade 12 book, not a second country.

North Macedonia's own way (Year 1): five themes move from modern digital technology and online living into a full structured-programming course taught in C++ (with Scratch/C++/Java/Python/PHP/Lisp compared), then advanced Word work (styles, contents, templates) and spreadsheets up to pivot tables.

Sudan's own way (Grade 7): the middle-stage book spends half its length on one database built step by step in Microsoft Access (tables, relationships, forms, queries, reports on a pupils-and-subjects database), then teaches the internet through Sudanese examples — the DNS lesson resolves the Khartoum Ministry of Education site (moekh.gov.sd).

Egypt's own way (Grade 10, Term 1 Programming and Artificial Intelligence student book): A full Japanese-style "Information Study" course adopted for Egypt's new secondary track: 13 chapters running from information theory, ethics and security through number systems, data analysis (frequency, variance, correlation, regression in spreadsheets), simulation and queues, Python, and a final AI-programming project chapter building web pages, a quiz app and a block-breaker game with generative AI.

Fiji's own way (Year 13 Computer Education student book): The senior book is built around a Fijian case organisation (Viti Group of Companies, a Fiji furniture manufacturer) for information systems; it teaches the full systems life cycle phase by phase, web design with real HTML and CSS, and text programming in C++ with worked programs (pay calculation, grade from mark, triangle and circle areas) using Fijian student names.

Libya's own way (Grade 10, Information Technology student book): the entire Grade 10 year is databases — theory first (concepts, ER modelling, the relational model, mapping ER to tables), then a long Microsoft Access 2007 practical (environment, tables, data entry, relationships, queries, forms, reports) and three set projects at the end. The secondary line is تقنية المعلومات (Information Technology).

Libya's own way (Grade 10, Information Technology teacher guide): the guide walks the same 12 database chapters as the student book, each as topics / objectives / teacher activities / student activities / key points / answers to selected exercises — the Access practical is taught through set classroom activities. (teacher guide — evidence)

Kuwait's own way (Grade 11 Information Technology Term 1): Grade 11 does databases the programmer's way — not Access, but SQLite driven from Python (sqlite3), with every operation practised on one running example, a Kuwait_landmarks.db database of Kuwait's landmarks; the term ends by integrating AI with the database in a face-recognition attendance project (OpenCV Haar cascades + SQLite logging).

Moldova's own way (Grade 12, Informatică): the final year goes deep in three directions at once — real programming technique (complexity, greedy, sifting, recursion vs iteration), numerical modelling (errors, bisection, chord and rectangle methods), and a full Access databases course — the same Pascal/C++ pair carrying the programming chapters, and a closing Extensii chapter that points to CTICE downloads for five advanced topics.

Bangladesh's own way (HSC ICT, Classes XI–XII and Alim): one compulsory ICT book for every HSC and Alim student in the country, saturated with Bangladesh itself: Bangabandhu Satellite-1 and its ground stations, bdjobs and chakri.com job portals, Daraz and Bikroy e-commerce, the Walton factory's automated production line, the National ID card used to teach primary keys and its database used to teach RDBMS at work, a full Bangla Unicode table and a worked example of putting Bangla text on a web page with meta charset, the national letter-grade scale (A+ from 80) as the else-if programme, "I Love Bangladesh" as the loop example text, an algorithm for reaching college by bicycle or rickshaw, and the Digital Bangladesh programme — National Data Centre, hi-tech parks, Startup Bangladesh and Bangladesh Bank's QR-based Cashless Bangladesh. C is the HSC programming language, taught with scanf/printf in Code::Blocks.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 5 G5 student (Antares)): One of two different Grade 5 books in the set (this one by Velijanyan & Ayvazyan, Antares); it runs as numbered § lessons mixing information theory, careful judgement of fake vs true information, and a long practical spine — text editor, spreadsheets, algorithms and variables — ending in teamwork principles.

Vietnam's own way (Grade 11 Tin học 11 Khoa học máy tính): from Grade 11 the two orientations are separate books but share a common core (Bài 1–16 here: operating systems, inside the computer, and a full databases strand ending at SQL); the CS-only second half is algorithm engineering in Python — arrays, search and sort, time complexity, stepwise refinement, modular design and building code libraries.

Vietnam's own way (Grade 11 Tin học 11 Tin học ứng dụng): Bài 1–16 are the same common core as the Computer Science orientation book (spot-checked page-identical, e.g. Bài 1); the Applied-only second half is hands-on database administration in Microsoft Access (through backup), then photo editing in GIMP and film-making/animation.

South Sudan's own way (Grade 12 S4 Student): The final year is specialisation: a full databases unit in Microsoft Access with real SQL, forms and reports, normalisation and joins, ending in integrated library management systems; an advanced-formatting unit that goes from Word tables of contents, styles, track changes, templates and mail merge through PowerPoint multimedia to VBA macros in Excel; and a website-design unit built around HTML, CSS and Adobe Dreamweaver with FTP site management.

South Sudan's own way (Grade 12 S4 Teacher): The guide mirrors the student book's three units with teaching sequences, observation/conversation assessment points and worked projects — designing a database for a local medical practice, asking how books are managed in a school library, and a capstone project to design the school's own website, judged on navigation and usability. (teacher guide — evidence)


Pakistan's own way (Grade 12 Computer Science 12): the older-curriculum Grade 12 book is two courses in one binding: seven chapters of database theory and Microsoft Access practice (normalization worked to 3NF with Pakistani college examples, NADRA and CRICKINFO as the distributed-database examples) followed by seven chapters of C in the Turbo C++ IDE, ending in file handling and strings — the contacts-file programs (names and phone numbers separated by "!") carry the whole second half's examples.

Kazakhstan's own way (Shaniev G9 Informatics): the bilingual Grade 9 book (taught in English for the trilingual programme) opens with computer literacy including netiquette and pricing a computer configuration, teaches databases practically (design, build, sort/filter, pivot tables, charts, a "family shop" mini-project), then Python lists in depth — and finishes with a whole chapter of 2D game programming in PyGame.

Kazakhstan's own way (Vyshkova & Zhurbenko G7 Informatics, NIS): the Nazarbayev Intellectual Schools course is a different programme from the national one: business documents, databases inside the information-processing part, logo design and GIF animation in raster graphics, a whole chapter on modelling the world (including forecasting social problems from data), client–server networks, utilities, and linear-structure programming ending in building your own calculator.
