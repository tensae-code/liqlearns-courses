# Organising an Emergency Services Information Centre

## Do it first

Fire, injury, flood — in an emergency nobody can hunt for a number. The information must already be gathered, checked and waiting where a frightened person can find it in seconds. Grade 6's project builds exactly that: an information centre for emergency services, made by the class for its school.

## Sub lesson 1 — The emergency services — and the steps to reach them

First the knowledge itself: the **emergency services** — fire, police, ambulance and medical help, disaster and helpline numbers — what each is for, and the **steps to get them**: the number to call, what to say (who you are, where you are, what has happened), and what to do while help travels. Wrong-service calls waste the minutes the right service needed, so the centre teaches choosing as well as dialling: match the trouble to the service before the call, not during it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Building the centre — information ordered for panic

Then the building: the class collects the services' real names, numbers and addresses, **verifies** them (the verification habit applied to the numbers lives depend on), and arranges them so a stranger in a hurry succeeds — services grouped by trouble, numbers large and plain, copies where people actually are: classroom, office, gate. An information centre is a database before the word is taught: facts, checked, structured, and retrievable at the speed of need. The centre is finished only when it has been tested — a timed drill, a stranger, a found number.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: Fire, police and ambulance (also medical help, disaster lines). Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: Who you are, where you are, and what has happened. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: Wrong-service calls waste the right service's minutes. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: Choosing the service and dialling it. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
