# Video script — Forms of Data Representation

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Data is represented — given a form people and programs can work with — as **text** (words and characters), **numbers** (amounts that can be calculated with), **tables** (rows and columns that organise), **diagrams and charts** (shapes that show patterns and comparisons), **images**, and **sound**. Armenia's Grade 5 guide maps the student book's §23 as 'Forms of representing data' inside the standard's themes: the student book presents data by tables; the guide names the whole family of forms. (This lesson stands on that guide evidence — see sources.)
Representation is a choice, and the choice changes what the data says. A term's marks as a **table** invite lookup; as a **bar chart** they invite comparison; as one **average number** they invite a verdict — and hide every individual. The rules: match the form to the question (find one value → table; compare → chart; tell a story → text or picture), keep the form honest (axes that start at zero, labels that say what the numbers are), and remember every form drops something — name what your chosen form drops.
