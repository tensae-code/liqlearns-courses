## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **A real problem, real data — the forests of Indonesia** — The Grade 11 data chapter ends in a **case study** (studi kasus): the loss of Indonesia's forests — **deforestation** — studied as data. The project opens with **preparation**: fixing the question the data must answer (where is forest being lost, how fast, and what changes with it), finding data that is open, trusted and legal, and judging it fit before a single chart is drawn. A case study is the analysis stages walked on ground that matters: the numbers are about the learners' own country, and a careless reading has a real cost.
2. **Observation and visualisation — making the problem visible** — The project's working stages are **observation** — exploring the prepared data, comparing regions and years, noting what the data cannot say — and **visualisation of the problem**: charts and maps chosen so the pattern can be seen at a glance and argued about honestly. A deforestation chart can accuse or excuse depending on its scale and years; the project's discipline is to choose the view that shows the data, not the view that wins the argument, and to publish the limits beside the picture.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
