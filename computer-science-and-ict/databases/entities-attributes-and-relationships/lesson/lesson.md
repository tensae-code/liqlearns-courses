# Entities, attributes and relationships

## Do it first

Think of your school library. Pick **one book**. Now answer three questions about it. *What is the thing itself?* — the book. *What can you say about it?* — its title, its author, the year it was printed. *What does it connect to?* — the pupil who borrowed it. You have just done this whole lesson: the thing, its properties, its connection. The lesson gives each one its proper name.

## Sub lesson 1 — Entities — the things data is kept about

A **data model** is the **blueprint of a database** — it shows **what data to store**, before anything is built.

An **entity** is a **person, place or event that data is kept about**. The book's examples:

- a **bank** keeps data about **customers** and **accounts**;
- a **library** keeps data about **books**, **loans** and **borrowers**.

Remember this for later lessons: when the model is built, **an entity becomes a table**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Attributes — an entity's properties, and instances

An **attribute** is a **property of an entity** — one of the facts that **tells one entity from another**. A customer's attributes are its **ID, name, sex and address**: two customers may share a name, but the ID tells them apart.

One single occurrence of an entity — **one named city**, for example — is called an **instance**. The entity is the kind of thing; the instance is one real one of them.

## Sub lesson 3 — Relationships — the verb between entities

A **relationship** is an **association between entities** — a connection that the data must keep.

You read a relationship from its **verb**: a customer **has** an account. *Has* is the relationship, joining the customer entity to the account entity.

Entity, attribute, relationship — those three are the whole vocabulary of a data model. The next lesson counts how many instances each side of a relationship can have.
