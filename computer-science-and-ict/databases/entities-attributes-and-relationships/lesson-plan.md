# Lesson plan — Entities, attributes and relationships

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Databases · **Lesson:** Entities, attributes and relationships
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Database Management (Ethiopia Grade 11 Information Technology), in the Databases title of the Computer Science and ICT course. The ladder is the recommended order, never a forced one.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a data model is the blueprint of a database: entities are the people, places or events data is kept about, attributes are an entity's properties, and a relationship is an association between entities, shown by the verb.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the database approach (the lesson before it): before data can live in the one shared collection, someone must draw its blueprint.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Entities — the things data is kept about** — a data model is the blueprint of a database; entities are the people, places or events data is kept about, and an entity becomes a table when built
2. **Attributes — an entity's properties, and instances** — attributes are the properties that tell one entity from another — a customer's ID, name, sex and address; one single occurrence of an entity is an instance
3. **Relationships — the verb between entities** — a relationship is an association between entities, and it is shown by the verb — a customer *has* an account

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The database approach and the DBMS. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a data model is: the blueprint of a database, showing what data to store.
2. Name entities in the book's examples: a bank's customers and accounts; a library's books, loans and borrowers.
3. Say what an attribute is and list a customer's: ID, name, sex, address.
4. Read a relationship from its verb: a customer *has* an account.
5. Say what an instance is: a single occurrence of an entity.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes attribute and instance → an attribute is a property kind (name, address); an instance is one real occurrence (one named city).
- Cannot find the relationship → look for the verb between the two entity names: has, borrows, teaches.
