**Acceptance count: taught in 5 countries (Ethiopia, Nepal, Belarus, North Macedonia, Sudan).**

Belarus's own way (Grade 11): grade 11 is a web-and-data year — hand-written HTML in Notepad, then the same site rebuilt in FrontPage, a long PascalABC graphics practicum borrowed from other school subjects, and a full Access database course (tables, links, forms, queries, sorting, reports).

Honesty: nothing in this lesson rests on teacher-guide evidence; all three sub lessons are pupil-taught from the student book. No book error touches this lesson's pages directly; the unit-wide errors (the stray Emerging Technologies bullet in the Unit Summary, printed p.71, and the “statment” misspelling in review headings) are named in the sources of the lessons they touch.

Grade 12 growth (2026-11-12, Wave F3): sub lesson 4 stands on teacher-guide evidence only, labelled as such. Guide error corrected and flagged: the guide lists MongoDB among RDBMS examples; MongoDB is a NoSQL system, taught as NoSQL here. The lesson's acceptance count stays taught in 1 country (Ethiopia) on its pupil-taught sub lessons; this evidence sub lesson adds no country.

North Macedonia's own way (Year 2): a strikingly current first theme (cybersecurity, cryptography, blockchain, robotics, AI/ML and large language models) precedes deep C++ (data representation, functions, one-dimensional arrays), a full databases theme ending in SQL, multimedia with generative AI for graphics, and HTML web authoring.

Sudan's own way (Grade 6): the final primary book is built around real Sudanese systems — networks and cloud first, then a full security unit taught through named children's everyday internet dilemmas, and a worked example of the ipSchools school-management system (with a real Bakht Al-Ruda school results table) before closing with computers in banks and police work.

Sudan's own way (Grade 7): the middle-stage book spends half its length on one database built step by step in Microsoft Access (tables, relationships, forms, queries, reports on a pupils-and-subjects database), then teaches the internet through Sudanese examples — the DNS lesson resolves the Khartoum Ministry of Education site (moekh.gov.sd).
