# Databases at Work — Government, Corporate and E-Commerce

## Do it first

You have built tables in a classroom. Outside, the same tables run a country: your birth is in one, your exam result in another, your last online purchase in a third. This lesson visits the three workplaces where the HSC book finds its databases grown up.

## Sub lesson 1 — Government databases — the state remembers

A modern state is a database with a flag: Bangladesh's book names its own — the **National ID database** identifying every citizen (the primary-key example of the companion lesson), **passport** records, and the **education boards'** results that decide futures every year. Government databases must be exact (a wrong record is a wronged citizen), shared between offices under permission (the security lesson), and kept for lifetimes. Their scale is the classroom model stretched: same tables, same keys — a hundred million rows deep.

In your mind's eye: government databases — the state remembers.

## Sub lesson 2 — Corporate ERP and e-commerce — business remembers

A company integrates its memory in an **ERP** system — one database under purchasing, stock, payroll and sales, so the left hand knows the stock the right hand sold. **E-commerce** runs on the same pattern facing outward: the catalogue is a table, the cart a temporary table, the order a record that must never be lost, and the customer's history the shop's memory of them. Government, corporate, commerce — the workplaces differ in master, not in kind: rows, keys, queries, and the discipline of keeping them true.
