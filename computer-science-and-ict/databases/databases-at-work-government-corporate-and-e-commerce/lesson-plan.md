**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Databases · **Lesson:** Databases at Work — Government, Corporate and E-Commerce
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Bangladesh (2026-11-14) — in the title Databases on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). **The one thing this lesson teaches:** databases at work: the relational database in its three great workplaces — government (National ID, passports, education boards), corporate enterprise systems (ERP), and e-commerce (Bangladesh HSC ICT, Chapter 6, §§6.4.3–6.4.5).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Government databases — the state remembers** — A modern state is a database with a flag: Bangladesh's book names its own — the **National ID database** identifying every citizen (the primary-key example of the companion lesson), **passport** records, and the **education boards'** results that decide futures every year. Government databases must be exact (a wrong record is a wronged citizen), shared between offices under permission (the security lesson), and kept for lifetimes. Their scale is the classroom model stretched: same tables, same keys — a hundred million rows deep.
2. **Corporate ERP and e-commerce — business remembers** — A company integrates its memory in an **ERP** system — one database under purchasing, stock, payroll and sales, so the left hand knows the stock the right hand sold. **E-commerce** runs on the same pattern facing outward: the catalogue is a table, the cart a temporary table, the order a record that must never be lost, and the customer's history the shop's memory of them. Government, corporate, commerce — the workplaces differ in master, not in kind: rows, keys, queries, and the discipline of keeping them true.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
