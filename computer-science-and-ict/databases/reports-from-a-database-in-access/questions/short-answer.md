# Reports from a Database in Access — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What is a report?**
**Answer: A formatted document arranging database records for reading or printing**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) Where do a report's records come from?**
**Answer: From a table, or from a query that already chose them**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What is grouping in a report?**
**Answer: Records of one kind together — one class, one month — under their own heading**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What do totals do in a report?**
**Answer: Sum amounts within a group and overall**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) Is a report a copy of the data?**
**Answer: No — it is a view; printing it again shows the current data in the same arrangement**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) Who is a report made for?**
**Answer: A reader who will never open the database**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What does creating a report involve?**
**Answer: Choosing source records, fields, grouping and order, and layout**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) Can perfect data still make a failed report?**
**Answer: Yes — if the arrangement does not match how the reader reads**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) What are the Access course's parts, in order?**
**Answer: Tables, linking, forms, queries, sorting, then reports**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) Does the reader edit data in a report?**
**Answer: No — a report is made to be read, not edited**
