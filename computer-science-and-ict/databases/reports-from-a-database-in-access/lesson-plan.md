# Lesson plan — Reports from a Database in Access

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Databases · **Lesson:** Reports from a Database in Access
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Belarus (2026-11-14) — in the title Databases on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Belarus's *Інфарматыка* Grade 11, because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A report is the database's printed answer: it arranges records from tables or queries into a formatted document — grouped, ordered, with headings and totals — made to be read on paper or as a finished page, not to be edited.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the Belarus book's own order — it follows the book's sections in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What a report is for** — Tables hold data, queries find it, forms enter it — a **report** presents it. A report takes records (from a table, or better, from a **query** that already chose them) and lays them out as a **document**: a title, headings, the records in a chosen order, often **grouped** — all the members of one class together, all the sales of one month together — with group and final **totals** where amounts are involved. The report is made for a reader who will never open the database.
2. **Creating a report** — **Creating** one means choosing the source records, the fields to show, the grouping and order, and the layout — usually starting from the program's report-building help and then adjusting. A report is a **view, not a copy**: it holds no data of its own, so printing it again tomorrow shows tomorrow's data in the same arrangement. If the arrangement is wrong — ungrouped when the reader thinks in groups, unordered when the reader scans by name — the report fails even with perfect data, because its whole job is to be read.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. A formatted document arranging database records for reading or printing.
2. Records of one kind together — one class, one month — under their own heading.
3. A reader who will never open the database.
4. Tables, linking, forms, queries, sorting, then reports.

## What you need
