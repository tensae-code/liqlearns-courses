# Forms for Entering and Viewing Data

## Do it first

A raw table is a wall of cells. A form is the same data wearing a face people can use — this lesson builds that face.

## Sub lesson 1 — One record, laid out kindly

A **form** presents **one record at a time**: each field gets a **label** and a box, arranged in the order a person thinks, not the table's column order. Navigation buttons move record to record; the last position opens a **blank record** for new data. Nothing about the table changes — the form is a **view and doorway** onto it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Entering safely through a form

Forms protect data quality: fields can carry the table's **types and rules**, lists can offer **choices instead of free typing**, and required fields can refuse to be skipped. That is why real databases are filled in through forms, not grids — the book places forms between keys and queries in its database theme for exactly that reason.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: A database form is a designed screen laid over a table — one record shown at a time with labelled fields in a friendly order — so people can enter and view data without facing the raw table grid; the form writes to the same table, it stores nothing of its own. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
