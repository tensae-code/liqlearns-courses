**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Databases · **Lesson:** Advanced Processing of Database Information in List Form
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Moldova (2026-11-14) — in the title Databases on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). **The one thing this lesson teaches:** The Grade 12 elective on advanced list processing: treating database-extracted lists with the spreadsheet's heavy tools — multi-level sorting, filtering, subtotals and pivot-style summaries — and knowing when the work belongs in the database and when in the list (Moldova Grade 12, Informatică, §5.1).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The list — a table outside the database** — A **list** here is data **extracted into rows and columns** — the query's answer become a worksheet table: one row per record, one column per field, a header row naming them. In list form the data can travel (printed, mailed, archived) and submits to the spreadsheet's crafts, but it is **cut from its source**: relationships, rules and live updating stay behind in the database. The elective's first skill is recognising the moment: analysis and presentation live well in lists; **truth lives in the database**, and lists are its dated photographs.
2. **Advanced list crafts — sort, filter, subtotal, pivot** — The advanced crafts chain. **Multi-level sorting** orders by one column, ties by another (town, then name). **Filtering** shows chosen slices without deleting (this year, this class). **Subtotals** insert group sums into the sorted list at each change of the grouping column — the list echoing the totals query. **Pivot-style summaries** cross-tabulate: rows by one field, columns by another, values aggregated in the cells (marks: classes × subjects). Each craft's honesty rule is the elective's last word: **work on a copy, keep the source list untouched, and label every summary with the extraction it came from**.
