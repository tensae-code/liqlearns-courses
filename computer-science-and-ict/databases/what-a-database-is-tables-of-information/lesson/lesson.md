# What a Database Is — Tables of Information

## Do it first

Kuwait's Grade 12 database book opens with a question the earlier Access and SQLite years assumed: what is this thing programs keep connecting to? This lesson is the answer — the database as tables of information, and why a program wants one.

## Sub lesson 1 — A database — information with a shape

A **database** is information kept in an organised shape a computer can work on: not a pile of notes but **tables** — rows of records, columns of fields — holding one kind of thing each, under rules that keep it consistent. The shape is the point: because records share a form, the program can **search** the whole store in a blink, **sort** it any way, and trust that every record answers the same questions.

In your mind's eye: a database — information with a shape.

## Sub lesson 2 — Why a program connects to one

A program **connects** to a database when the information must **outlive the program's run** and be **shared**: the records stay on disk when the program closes, and many programs — or many users — work on the same single store instead of private copies that drift apart. Kuwait opens its final database book here because the VB year that follows is exactly this: forms and grids and reports, all of them windows onto tables that live apart from any one program.

In your mind's eye: why a program connects to one.
