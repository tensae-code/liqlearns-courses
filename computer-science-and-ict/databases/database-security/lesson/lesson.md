# Database Security

## Do it first

A database concentrates an organisation's memory in one place — which makes it the place most worth protecting. Fiji's Year 11 book closes its database chapter's teaching with database security: who may get in, what they may do, and what guards the data when people fail. This lesson is that protection.

## Sub lesson 1 — Who may do what — users, passwords, permissions

Database security begins with **access control**: every user has their **own account and password**, so the system knows who is asking; and each account carries **permissions** — what that user may **see, add, change or delete**. The school clerk reads and enters marks; the clerk does not delete pupils. Fiji's rule is need: a user gets the powers their job needs and no more, because every extra power is an extra risk carried by the whole school's data.

In your mind's eye: who may do what — users, passwords, permissions.

## Sub lesson 2 — Guarding the data itself — backup and honesty of records

The second half guards against loss and wrongness. **Backups** — copies of the database kept apart — mean a fire, a theft or a failed disk cannot take the only copy. **Accuracy** is security too: wrong data entered carelessly harms like wrong data entered in malice, so entries are checked and changes are **recorded** — who changed what, and when — so a wrong record can be traced to its moment. Fiji's picture is complete: locks on the door, copies in another house, and a written trail inside.

In your mind's eye: guarding the data itself — backup and honesty of records.
