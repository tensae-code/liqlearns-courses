**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The databases you already use** — The **school office** keeps its pupils in a database — names, classes, fees, marks — searched every time a list is made. The **library** keeps its books and borrowers in one: the catalogue that answers 'do we have this book, and who has it?' The **shop and the bank** keep theirs: stock and prices at the till; accounts and balances at the bank. Fiji's point is recognition — each of these answers questions from stored records, which is the database's job, whether or not anyone says the word.
2. **One shape under all of them** — Different lives, one shape: each everyday database holds **records** of one kind of thing — pupils, books, goods, accounts — each record in **fields**, and each is **searched** to answer a real question in seconds: Is this pupil in Year 8? Is this book out? What does this account hold? Seeing the shape under the examples is the lesson: when a learner meets a new system — an airline booking, a hospital file, a phone contact list — they already know what kind of thing it is.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
