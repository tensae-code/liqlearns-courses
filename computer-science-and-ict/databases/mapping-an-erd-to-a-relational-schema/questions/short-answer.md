# Mapping an ERD to a Relational Schema — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What does every entity become?**
**Answer: A table**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What does every attribute become?**
**Answer: A column of its entity's table**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What does the key attribute become?**
**Answer: The table's primary key**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) How is a multivalued attribute mapped?**
**Answer: As a table of its own — the key plus one value per record**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) After the straight mapping, what must every part of the ERD have?**
**Answer: A home in the schema, or a stated reason why not**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) How is a one-to-many relationship carried?**
**Answer: By a foreign key — the 'one' side's key placed in the 'many' side's table**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) How is a many-to-many relationship mapped?**
**Answer: As a new table holding both sides' keys as its composite key**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What else can the new table hold?**
**Answer: The relationship's own attributes — such as a mark**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) Give Malaysia's closing check.**
**Answer: Every relationship in the ERD must be traceable in the schema — as a foreign key or as a table**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) What is an ERD, against a schema?**
**Answer: A picture; the schema is the buildable design**
