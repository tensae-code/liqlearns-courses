# Mapping an ERD to a Relational Schema — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What does every entity become?**
a) A table  b) A column of its entity's table  c) The table's primary key  d) As a table of its own — the key plus one value per record
**Answer: a) A table**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What does every attribute become?**
a) The table's primary key  b) A column of its entity's table  c) As a table of its own — the key plus one value per record  d) A home in the schema, or a stated reason why not
**Answer: b) A column of its entity's table**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What does the key attribute become?**
a) As a table of its own — the key plus one value per record  b) A home in the schema, or a stated reason why not  c) The table's primary key  d) By a foreign key — the 'one' side's key placed in the 'many' side's table
**Answer: c) The table's primary key**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) How is a multivalued attribute mapped?**
a) A home in the schema, or a stated reason why not  b) By a foreign key — the 'one' side's key placed in the 'many' side's table  c) As a new table holding both sides' keys as its composite key  d) As a table of its own — the key plus one value per record
**Answer: d) As a table of its own — the key plus one value per record**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) After the straight mapping, what must every part of the ERD have?**
a) A home in the schema, or a stated reason why not  b) By a foreign key — the 'one' side's key placed in the 'many' side's table  c) As a new table holding both sides' keys as its composite key  d) The relationship's own attributes — such as a mark
**Answer: a) A home in the schema, or a stated reason why not**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) How is a one-to-many relationship carried?**
a) As a new table holding both sides' keys as its composite key  b) By a foreign key — the 'one' side's key placed in the 'many' side's table  c) The relationship's own attributes — such as a mark  d) Every relationship in the ERD must be traceable in the schema — as a foreign key or as a table
**Answer: b) By a foreign key — the 'one' side's key placed in the 'many' side's table**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) How is a many-to-many relationship mapped?**
a) The relationship's own attributes — such as a mark  b) Every relationship in the ERD must be traceable in the schema — as a foreign key or as a table  c) As a new table holding both sides' keys as its composite key  d) A picture; the schema is the buildable design
**Answer: c) As a new table holding both sides' keys as its composite key**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What else can the new table hold?**
a) Every relationship in the ERD must be traceable in the schema — as a foreign key or as a table  b) A picture; the schema is the buildable design  c) A table  d) The relationship's own attributes — such as a mark
**Answer: d) The relationship's own attributes — such as a mark**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) Give Malaysia's closing check.**
a) Every relationship in the ERD must be traceable in the schema — as a foreign key or as a table  b) A picture; the schema is the buildable design  c) A table  d) A column of its entity's table
**Answer: a) Every relationship in the ERD must be traceable in the schema — as a foreign key or as a table**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) What is an ERD, against a schema?**
a) A table  b) A picture; the schema is the buildable design  c) A column of its entity's table  d) The table's primary key
**Answer: b) A picture; the schema is the buildable design**
