# Hands-on — One-to-one, one-to-many, many-to-many

The lesson with your hands, not only your eyes.

## Count both ways
For each pair, write the two counts and name the type: (a) a pupil and their report card for one term; (b) a teacher and the classes they teach this year; (c) pupils and the sports they play. Defend each “many” out loud.

## Draw the three
Draw the three types as instance diagrams — dots for instances, lines for links — using the book's three examples. Under each, write its name and its two counts.

## Grade 12 — drag the link
With DEPARTMENT and TEACHER standing (DeptNo as TEACHER's foreign-key column), open the Relationships window by the book's route, add both tables, and drag D_Number onto DeptNo. Read the finished line: 1 at DEPARTMENT, ∞ at TEACHER. Screenshot or draw the diagram into your notes.
