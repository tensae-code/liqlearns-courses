# One-to-one, one-to-many, many-to-many — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Give the book's one-to-one example and its two counts.
**Answer:** A country and its capital city — one country to one capital, one capital to one country.

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** Give the book's one-to-many example.
**Answer:** One department and its many students.

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** Why are employees and projects many-to-many, not one-to-many?
**Answer:** Because the count is many in both directions: an employee works on many projects, and a project has many employees.
