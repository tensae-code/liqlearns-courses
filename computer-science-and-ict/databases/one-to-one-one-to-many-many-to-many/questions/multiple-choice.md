# One-to-one, one-to-many, many-to-many — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** A country and its capital city form a —
a) one-to-many relationship  b) one-to-one relationship  c) many-to-many relationship
**Answer: b)** — one country, one capital; one capital, one country.

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** One department with many students is —
a) one-to-many  b) one-to-one  c) many-to-many
**Answer: a)** — one on one side, many on the other.

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** An employee works on many projects, and a project has many employees. This is —
a) one-to-one  b) one-to-many  c) many-to-many
**Answer: c)** — many on both sides.

**4. (Sub lesson 3)** To name a relationship's type, you must count —
a) only from the left side  b) in both directions  c) the attributes
**Answer: b)** — both counts together name the type.

**5. (Sub lesson 4)** The Relationships window opens from —
a) Create tab → Query Design  b) Database Tools tab → Relationships group → Relationships  c) the Search box  d) Datasheet View
**Answer: b) Database Tools tab → Relationships group → Relationships** — the book's route.

**6. (Sub lesson 4)** In the window, a relationship is made by —
a) typing REFERENCES  b) dragging a field (typically the primary key) onto the common field  c) running SELECT  d) dropping a table
**Answer: b) dragging a field (typically the primary key) onto the common field** — dragging is the graphical method.

**7. (Sub lesson 4)** The many side of a diagram is marked —
a) 1  b) ∞  c) *  d) PK
**Answer: b) ∞** — 1 and ∞ are the end marks.

**8. (Sub lesson 4)** Department to Teacher is —
a) one-to-one  b) many-to-many  c) one-to-many  d) unrelated
**Answer: c) one-to-many** — one department, many teachers.
