# Database Uses and Issues

## Do it first

A senior course does not stop at how databases work — it asks what they are used for, and what goes wrong around them. Fiji's Year 13 database chapter closes with uses and issues together. This lesson takes both sides.

## Sub lesson 1 — The uses — what organisations run on databases

By Year 13 the uses are the organisation itself: databases run **stock and sales** at Viti, **pupils and results** at school, **accounts** at the bank, **patients** at the hospital, **bookings** at the airline. Fiji's pattern in every use is the database's three gifts working together: the record is kept **once**, found **fast**, and **shared** by everyone whose job needs it. A modern organisation does not *have* a database the way it has a cupboard — its daily work *is* the database being used.

## Sub lesson 2 — The issues — privacy, accuracy, dependence

The issues grow from the same gifts. **Privacy**: one shared store means one breach reaches everything, and records about people demand rules for who may look. **Accuracy**: a wrong record, shared fast, misleads everyone at once — the database makes errors efficient too. **Dependence**: when the system stops, work that forgot how to proceed without it stops as well. Fiji's closing balance is the mature one: use databases fully, and govern them as seriously as they deserve — backup, access rules, and checked data are not extras; they are the price of the gifts.
