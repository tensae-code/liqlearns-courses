## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The uses — what organisations run on databases** — By Year 13 the uses are the organisation itself: databases run **stock and sales** at Viti, **pupils and results** at school, **accounts** at the bank, **patients** at the hospital, **bookings** at the airline. Fiji's pattern in every use is the database's three gifts working together: the record is kept **once**, found **fast**, and **shared** by everyone whose job needs it. A modern organisation does not *have* a database the way it has a cupboard — its daily work *is* the database being used.
2. **The issues — privacy, accuracy, dependence** — The issues grow from the same gifts. **Privacy**: one shared store means one breach reaches everything, and records about people demand rules for who may look. **Accuracy**: a wrong record, shared fast, misleads everyone at once — the database makes errors efficient too. **Dependence**: when the system stops, work that forgot how to proceed without it stops as well. Fiji's closing balance is the mature one: use databases fully, and govern them as seriously as they deserve — backup, access rules, and checked data are not extras; they are the price of the gifts.
