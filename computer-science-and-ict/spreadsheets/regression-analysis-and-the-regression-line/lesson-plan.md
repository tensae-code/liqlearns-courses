## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The regression line — the best summary of the climb** — **Regression** fits a straight line through a scatter of points so the line summarises the relationship: as x grows by one, y grows by about the line's **slope**. The book's method is **least squares** — among all possible lines, choose the one whose **squared distances** from the points to the line are smallest in total, the same squaring trick variance used, so no point can pull the line cheaply and big misses count heavily. In the spreadsheet the line is drawn through the scatter chart and its equation — slope and intercept — is displayed and used.
2. **Using the line — estimation inside, caution outside** — With the equation in hand, the line **estimates**: put in an x the table never measured and read off the y the relationship predicts — a mark for six hours of revision from a line fitted on two to five. Estimates **inside** the measured range rest on evidence all around them; estimates **beyond** it — extrapolation — assume the climb continues unchanged, which real relationships rarely promise. Egypt closes its analysis chapters here because regression gathers the whole run: organised data, centre, spread, relationship, and now a model — with the standing honesty that a model summarises the past it was fitted on, and the future is allowed to differ.
