**Acceptance count: taught in 22 countries (Ethiopia, Eritrea, Belarus, Afghanistan, Yemen, Iraq, Uzbekistan, Cuba, Egypt, Fiji, Libya, Mongolia, Sri Lanka, Rwanda, Kuwait, Laos, Greece, Jordan, Armenia, Indonesia, Vietnam, South Sudan).**

Yemen's own way (Grade 11): the book pivots from operating the machine to understanding information itself — data kinds, number systems and coding get full units before the applications (presentations, Excel) and a networks/Internet finale; every unit closes with a formal تقويم (assessment).

Afghanistan's own way (Grade 12): the final year turns outward — computers in society (e-commerce, communications, even military systems), system software and virus protection, then Excel for the first time and a closing block on the Web, e-mail and browsers (taught through Internet Explorer).

Belarus's own way (Grade 10): the Zabaroŭski–Pupcaŭ course treats informatics as applied mathematics with office tools — a full-year arc from data archiving through Pascal strings and information modelling to Excel and 2010-era Internet communication (NetMeeting, Skype, blogs).

Mauritania's own way (Grade 8): The second year is organised as five applied units of 35 thirty-minute fiches — image processing first (Paint/GIMP/Photoshop, bitmap vs vector), then internet ethics, a full Excel data-processing unit ending in a practical project, web source-checking and responsible downloading, and finally email — each unit closed by evaluation and support fiches with full answer keys at the end.

Iraq's own way (Grade 11): the final preparatory year is the most forward-looking of the series — smart devices and the Internet of Things (with a lesson on choosing a computer or smartphone by its specifications), a full Excel unit ending in AutoSum, one- and two-dimensional arrays with nested loops (the series' first data structures), and electronic services: e-government, email and cloud computing with its applications.


Uzbekistan's own way (Grade 9): grade 9 is the capstone — computer graphics, Access, Excel, then a nine-lesson algorithms block and a 28-lesson Turbo Pascal 7.0 course that runs from the alphabet of the language to files, procedures and graphics mode, closing with the Internet. Every chapter ends with a dedicated repetition lesson, placed here to that chapter's main lesson.

Cuba's own way (Grade 6): Grade 6 adds the three productivity firsts in one year — digital presentations, Scratch animation with event/control blocks, and spreadsheets with formulas and charts — closing with a TIC chapter on searching and processing digital information; computer generations are taught inside Chapter 1.

Cuba's own way (Grade 8): Taught entirely in free software — LibreOffice Impress for presentations and LibreOffice Calc for spreadsheets; web work means designing and editing HTML pages with a plain text editor, and Scratch continues with arithmetic and logic operator blocks.

Egypt's own way (Grade 5, Term 2 student book (Arabic)): Primary ICT is organised as themed units — "The Digital Citizen in the Internet World" then "My Digital Project" — with every lesson phrased as a journey or a how-to question.

Fiji's own way (Years 7–8 Computer Education student book): A practical, exercise-driven primary book built around a class "students folder" of set files (work is saved as U<unit>-Ex<n>-<FirstName>); local Fijian names and schools throughout (e.g. Babasiga Secondary School), Internet searching is practised on the "Map of Fiji" with kids' search engines, and a full unit covers computers in Fijian society including Braille keyboards for blind learners. PARTIAL: the PDF as received has no cover, title page, contents or imprint — the PARTIAL label travels with every citation of this book.

Fiji's own way (Year 11 Computer Education student book): The first locally written text of its kind (the book's Preface): written by a panel of Computer Studies officers and University of the South Pacific lecturers for the new Year 11 Computer Education syllabus trialled in 2014; each chapter uses "Discovery Learning" prompts, and the database chapter is a full Access 2007 practical building a Student database in a "Lab Chapter 3" folder.

Libya's own way (Grade 8, Computer student book): a three-unit arc — computer history/generations and number systems first, then a whole practical unit built around the scanner (scan, save, copy, delete, scan via Paint), then Microsoft Excel 2007 taught step by step with screenshots. The basic-education line is الحاسوب (Computer) in Grades 8–9.

Mongolia's own way (Grade 9): Grade 9 turns outward to society and real tools — the information society taught through Mongolia's own shift, open-source software through Soyombo Linux (Mongolia's own Linux) and Mongolian OpenOffice, copyright through Mongolia's 2006 copyright law; algorithms are drawn as flowcharts and run in Turtle Art; the long spreadsheet unit grades pupils with nested IFs, builds Fibonacci sequences, grows a 500,000-tögrög bank deposit, and charts the depth and area of Mongolia's great lakes (Khuvsgul, Uvs, Khar-Us); e-services are the real Mongolian ones — the ТҮЦ service kiosk, bookstore.mn and internet banking.

Mongolia's own way (Grade 8): Grade 8 is built around two long projects — a folded leaflet on Ulaanbaatar's air pollution (Word templates, styles, WordArt, charts and SmartArt) that then becomes a PowerPoint presentation with its own master slide and hyperlink buttons, and a Paint.NET collage of the five-snout livestock (таван хошуу мал) in a steppe landscape. Models are taught on Mongolian material (the livestock, Rutherford's atom, a virus-growth formula); spreadsheets open with the chessboard-and-wheat legend and count Mongolia's real 2014 livestock by aimag; social networks are taught on Facebook and Twitter ("жиргээ") with Mongolian examples.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 9 Reading Book): it opens with preparing a computer specification for a real buyer (user needs, peripherals, processor/HDD/monitor/VGA specs, after-sales service); microcontrollers are taught with BBC micro:bit and Arduino and local sensor examples (solar lamp, washing machine, microwave).

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 9 Workbook): a companion workbook mirroring the Reading Book chapter-for-chapter.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 9 Teachers' Guide): a competency-based NIE guide (6 competencies, 30 periods) that unpacks the student book with CPU internal detail (input unit, ALU, memory unit, control unit, output unit), spreadsheet charts and sorting, and a named careers list (Software QA Engineer, Database Administrator, Software Architect, System Analyst, Web Application Developer, Graphic Designer). Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 12 Science for Technology, Tamil): the A/L technology-stream ICT volume teaches every office skill twice — once in Microsoft Office 2010 and once in LibreOffice (Writer/Calc/Impress 4.1) — and closes with creating a real Gmail account, mailbox folders, attachments and password safety; hardware goes down to cache levels L1/L2/L3, ROM types and file systems (FAT/NTFS/ext4).

Rwanda's own way (S2 ICT Teacher Guide, KLB): S2 moves to data protection, ICT in finance (including Rwandan mobile banking risks and biometrics), spreadsheets in depth, a second ArcGIS unit, and Scratch game design with sprite interaction tables and score/level rules. (teacher guide — evidence)

Kuwait's own way (Grade 5 Digital Explorer Term 2, printed title — records name it 'Our Digital World'): Grade 5 pairs a data-analysis lesson with a full Excel journey ('My Special Table', 1–4), then moves from screen coding to physical computing: robots in our lives, then micro:bit programming in MakeCode (Basic, Input and Logic block families) with an 'introduce yourself by programming' project.

Laos's own way (Grade 7 ICT teacher guide): uniquely, this guide — not the student book — carries the real software syllabus: separate activities for DOS, Windows/Linux, Unix/Mac, translators, BASIC and C, Logo, Java, and one activity per application family — a teacher-only expanded software syllabus. Windows XP-era office teaching runs through the middle years.

Greece's own way (Grade 8, Pliroforiki B' Gymnasiou, 2024): Grade 8 moves from bits/bytes and data rates into networks, then data analysis in spreadsheets and presentations, then modular Scratch programming (own blocks with parameters, realistic character motion), list data structures, a full AI unit training a neural network, and ICT's changes to the economy (e-commerce, Industry 4.0, new jobs, sustainability).

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 3 G3 Workbook): The Office ladder continues — Word carries the early blocks, PowerPoint the middle blocks (e.g. multiplication facts), and Excel appears in the geometry-and-measurement block, with Save As taught as an explicit step.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 3 G3 Teacher): Guide plans mirror the workbook's Office ladder — Word cards and writing first (Block 1 product: an ID card for a living thing with a drawing of its life cycle), then PowerPoint, then Excel.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 5 G5 Workbook): Grade 5 mixes all four tools (Paint, Word, PowerPoint, Excel) and adds an explicit password lesson — choosing a password you can remember without writing it down.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 5 G5 student (Antares)): One of two different Grade 5 books in the set (this one by Velijanyan & Ayvazyan, Antares); it runs as numbered § lessons mixing information theory, careful judgement of fake vs true information, and a long practical spine — text editor, spreadsheets, algorithms and variables — ending in teamwork principles.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 6 G6 student): Same author pair as Grade 5, one year on: the § sequence moves from society/data-rights into number systems and computing systems, then a long spreadsheet-and-diagrams block, and ends with algorithms in flowcharts and a block-based programming language.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 7 G7 Informatika student): Kurikulum Merdeka Informatika built on four elements (Berpikir Komputasional, Literasi Digital, Analisis Data, Algoritma dan Pemrograman), mixing plugged and unplugged activities with coded activity IDs (e.g. VII-BK, VII-LD) and QR-linked resources; Grade 7 covers only BK + Literasi Digital + data handling.

Vietnam's own way (Grade 7 Tin học 7): Grade 7 is the spreadsheet year (five Excel lessons in a row, in Windows/Excel/PowerPoint named openly) and its computer-science strand is pure algorithms with no code at all — sequential search, binary search and sorting acted out and flowcharted before any language appears.

South Sudan's own way (Grade 9 S1 Student): Opens ICT at secondary level entirely through doing — the whole first year is Microsoft Office application skills (Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Publisher, Paint) taught as step-by-step keyboard/mouse procedures with local data (South Sudan state populations, local disease statistics for charts), then internet use, hands-on troubleshooting, and Windows access control/security settings.
