# Probability and Random Sampling in a Spreadsheet — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) How is an event's probability computed?**
**Answer: Favourable outcomes divided by all outcomes**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What do frequencies become by division?**
**Answer: Probabilities**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What must a complete set of probabilities add to?**
**Answer: 1**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) Why teach probability inside the spreadsheet?**
**Answer: The counting it stands on is the spreadsheet's home ground**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) What is a frequency?**
**Answer: How often a value occurs in the data**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What makes a sample random?**
**Answer: Every member of the population has an equal chance of being chosen**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What does RAND() give?**
**Answer: A random fraction between 0 and 1, renewed at each recalculation**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What does RANDBETWEEN(1, 30) give?**
**Answer: A random whole number from 1 to 30**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) What is the sampling method?**
**Answer: Number the population, generate random numbers, take the members they point to**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) Why is 'the first ten' not a sample?**
**Answer: It is chosen by convenience, not by equal chance — conclusions from it lean**
