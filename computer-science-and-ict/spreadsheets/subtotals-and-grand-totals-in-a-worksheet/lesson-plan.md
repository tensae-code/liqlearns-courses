# Lesson plan — Subtotals and Grand Totals in a Worksheet

**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A subtotal is a total for one group of rows inside a list — sales for one class, one shop, one month — and the grand total is the total of all the subtotals together; a spreadsheet can insert them for you, but only after the list is sorted by the grouping column, and it writes them with the SUBTOTAL function so the outline can collapse and expand without breaking the sums.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What subtotals and grand totals are** — Grouping a list: the grouping column (class, shop, month), a subtotal row after each group, and the grand total at the very end. The Subtotal tool inserts the rows for you and writes each one with the SUBTOTAL function, which ignores other subtotal rows inside its range — so the grand total never counts a row twice.
2. **Building subtotals — sort first, then outline and remove** — The golden rule: sort the list by the grouping column first, or Excel starts a new group every time the value changes and the subtotals are nonsense. After inserting, the outline buttons (1, 2, 3) collapse the list to grand total only, to subtotals, or to full detail. Removing subtotals (Subtotal → Remove All) takes the inserted rows out and restores the plain list.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain the difference between a subtotal and a grand total, with an example.
2. Sort a list by its grouping column and insert subtotals with the spreadsheet's Subtotal tool.
3. Use the outline levels to show only the subtotals, or the subtotals and the grand total.
4. Remove subtotals cleanly and say why the list must be sorted first.

## What you need
A computer with a spreadsheet program (Microsoft Excel or similar), or paper ruled as a worksheet grid.
