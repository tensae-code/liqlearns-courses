## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The reusable template — quantities in, answer out** — A spreadsheet **model** separates the sheet's three citizens: the **input cells**, where the quantities of the case are written; the **formula cells**, which work the case's law and never change; and the **answer cells**, where results are read. Build it once with the law written truly, and every new case is answered by changing only the inputs — the template is **reusable** because the law and the data have stopped sharing cells. Mongolia's test of a model is replacement: pour in a second case, a third — if the answers stay true, it is a model; if not, it was a calculation wearing a model's coat.
2. **Heron's formula — the book's worked model** — The worked model is geometry's gift to modelling: **Heron's formula**, which gives a triangle's **area from its three sides alone** — first the half-perimeter, then the area from it — two formula cells chained, the second drinking from the first. Any three sides poured in return their area, for triangles no one has drawn. The unit's arc completes itself here: the spreadsheet began as rows and formulas, grew through functions, sorting and charts, and ends as an instrument — a model, in the same sense the course's first lessons gave the word: built for a purpose, judged by its work.
