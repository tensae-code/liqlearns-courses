# Lesson plan — Workbook Sheets and the Sheet Tab Bar

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Spreadsheets · **Lesson:** Workbook Sheets and the Sheet Tab Bar
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Afghanistan (2026-11-14) — in the title Spreadsheets on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Afghanistan's *Computer* Grade 12, because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** An Excel workbook holds several worksheets, and the sheet tab bar at the bottom is how you move between them, add them, rename them and keep a big workbook organised.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the Afghanistan book's own order — it follows the book's sections in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The workbook and its sheets** — An Excel file is a **workbook**, and a workbook is a book of **worksheets**: it opens (in the book's Excel) with **three sheets**, and can hold many more. Each sheet is a full grid of rows and columns with its own work on it — one sheet for January, one for February, one for the year's summary, all inside one file. This is how Excel expects real work to be organised: related tables together in one workbook, each on its own sheet, instead of scattered through separate files. Afghanistan's Grade 12 book gives the sheet selection bar its own lesson because the learner who cannot move between sheets is locked out of most of every workbook they open.
2. **The sheet tab bar — moving and managing** — At the bottom of the window sits the **sheet tab bar**: a **tab** for each sheet, labelled with its name (Sheet1, Sheet2, Sheet3 until renamed), with arrow buttons to scroll the tabs when they overflow. Clicking a tab **selects** that sheet and brings it to the front; **double-clicking** a tab lets you **rename** it — and names should say what the sheet holds, because 'January' guides and 'Sheet1' does not. A new sheet is added with Insert → Worksheet, unwanted sheets can be deleted (carefully — a deleted sheet's work is gone), and tabs can be dragged to reorder them. The bar is small and the lesson is large: every workbook you will ever open is navigated here.
