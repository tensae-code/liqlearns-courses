# Video script — Workbook Sheets and the Sheet Tab Bar

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

An Excel file is a **workbook**, and a workbook is a book of **worksheets**: it opens (in the book's Excel) with **three sheets**, and can hold many more.
Each sheet is a full grid of rows and columns with its own work on it — one sheet for January, one for February, one for the year's summary, all inside one file.
This is how Excel expects real work to be organised: related tables together in one workbook, each on its own sheet, instead of scattered through separate files.
Afghanistan's Grade 12 book gives the sheet selection bar its own lesson because the learner who cannot move between sheets is locked out of most of every workbook they open.
At the bottom of the window sits the **sheet tab bar**: a **tab** for each sheet, labelled with its name (Sheet1, Sheet2, Sheet3 until renamed), with arrow buttons to scroll the tabs when they overflow.
Clicking a tab **selects** that sheet and brings it to the front; **double-clicking** a tab lets you **rename** it — and names should say what the sheet holds, because 'January' guides and 'Sheet1' does not.
A new sheet is added with Insert → Worksheet, unwanted sheets can be deleted (carefully — a deleted sheet's work is gone), and tabs can be dragged to reorder them.
The bar is small and the lesson is large: every workbook you will ever open is navigated here.
