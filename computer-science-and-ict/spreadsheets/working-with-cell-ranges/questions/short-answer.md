# Working with Cell Ranges — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What is a range?**
**Answer: A rectangular block of cells**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) How is a range named?**
**Answer: By the addresses of its opposite corners, joined with a colon — for example B2:D5**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) Can a range name be used in a formula?**
**Answer: Yes — wherever a single cell address can be used**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) Is a single cell a range?**
**Answer: Yes — a range of one**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) How is a range selected?**
**Answer: By dragging from one corner to the opposite one**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) Name three actions that apply to a whole range at once.**
**Answer: Copying or moving it, filling a series across it, clearing or formatting it**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What is the care point before acting on a range?**
**Answer: Checking the selection — a range one row too tall carries the extra row into everything**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) Does a range name describe scattered cells?**
**Answer: No — it always describes the whole rectangle**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) What forms can a range take?**
**Answer: A stretch of one row, one column, or a block of several rows and columns**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) Why work with ranges at all?**
**Answer: To do once what would otherwise be done cell by cell**
