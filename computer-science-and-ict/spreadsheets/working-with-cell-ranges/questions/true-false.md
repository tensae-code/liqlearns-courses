# Working with Cell Ranges — true-false

Say whether each statement is true or false.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "What is a range?" is: A rectangular block of cells.**
**Answer: True**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "How is a range named?" is: Copying or moving it, filling a series across it, clearing or formatting it.**
**Answer: False** — By the addresses of its opposite corners, joined with a colon — for example B2:D5.

**3. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "Can a range name be used in a formula?" is: Yes — wherever a single cell address can be used.**
**Answer: True**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "Is a single cell a range?" is: No — it always describes the whole rectangle.**
**Answer: False** — Yes — a range of one.

**5. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "How is a range selected?" is: By dragging from one corner to the opposite one.**
**Answer: True**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "Name three actions that apply to a whole range at once." is: To do once what would otherwise be done cell by cell.**
**Answer: False** — Copying or moving it, filling a series across it, clearing or formatting it.

**7. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "What is the care point before acting on a range?" is: Checking the selection — a range one row too tall carries the extra row into everything.**
**Answer: True**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "Does a range name describe scattered cells?" is: By the addresses of its opposite corners, joined with a colon — for example B2:D5.**
**Answer: False** — No — it always describes the whole rectangle.

**9. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "What forms can a range take?" is: A stretch of one row, one column, or a block of several rows and columns.**
**Answer: True**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "Why work with ranges at all?" is: Yes — a range of one.**
**Answer: False** — To do once what would otherwise be done cell by cell.
