## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What a range is** — A **range** (дыяпазон) is a rectangle of cells: a stretch of one row, one column, or a block of several rows and columns. Its name joins the addresses of its **opposite corners** with a colon — the block from B2 to D5 is written B2:D5 — and that name can be used wherever a single cell address can, for example inside a formula that totals the whole block. A single cell is a range of one; the name always describes the whole rectangle, never a scattered pick of cells.
2. **Acting on a whole range** — Working with ranges means doing once what would otherwise be done cell by cell. **Select** the block by dragging from one corner to the opposite one; then the action applies to all of it: copy or move the block to a new place, fill a series across it, clear its contents, or format every cell in it at once. The care point is checking the selection before acting — a range one row too tall will carry an unwanted row into every copy, fill and total that follows.
