# Working with Cell Ranges

## Do it first

Formatting forty cells one by one takes forty actions — and forty chances to miss one. In this lesson you select the whole block once as a range, name it by its corners, and let one action do the forty.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a range is

A **range** (дыяпазон) is a rectangle of cells: a stretch of one row, one column, or a block of several rows and columns. Its name joins the addresses of its **opposite corners** with a colon — the block from B2 to D5 is written B2:D5 — and that name can be used wherever a single cell address can, for example inside a formula that totals the whole block. A single cell is a range of one; the name always describes the whole rectangle, never a scattered pick of cells.

## Sub lesson 2 — Acting on a whole range

Working with ranges means doing once what would otherwise be done cell by cell. **Select** the block by dragging from one corner to the opposite one; then the action applies to all of it: copy or move the block to a new place, fill a series across it, clear its contents, or format every cell in it at once. The care point is checking the selection before acting — a range one row too tall will carry an unwanted row into every copy, fill and total that follows.
