## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Several conditions at once — SUMIFS** — **SUMIFS** is the summarising function that obeys more than one master: **SUMIFS(sum_range, criteria_range1, criteria1, criteria_range2, criteria2, …)** adds the values in the sum range only for rows where **every** stated condition holds — the sales *in June, in the north shop, of rice*. Indonesia's Grade 8 data chapter teaches it inside data summarising (peringkasan data), because real summaries are never one condition deep. Note the family's trap: in SUMIFS the **summed range comes first** — the opposite order from its single-condition cousin SUMIF.
2. **Counting with conditions — COUNTIFS** — **COUNTIFS** counts instead of adding: the number of rows meeting every condition — how many pupils are *in Grade 8, in the science club, and present today*. Together the pair answers the two shapes of every summarising question — *how much* and *how many* — under any number of conditions, and beside them in the chapter stands the pivot table, the same summarising done by dragging instead of writing. The discipline is the analyst's: state the conditions before the number, and report them with it — a conditional total quoted bare is a whole truth cut in half.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Adds only the values whose rows meet every stated condition
2. The summed range first, then pairs of criteria range and criteria
3. SUMIF puts its summed range last; SUMIFS puts it first
4. Counts the rows meeting every condition
