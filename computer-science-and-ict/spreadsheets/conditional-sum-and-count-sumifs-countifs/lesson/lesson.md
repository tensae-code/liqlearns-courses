# Conditional Sum and Count — SUMIFS and COUNTIFS

## Do it first

Conditional sum and count — SUMIFS adds, and COUNTIFS counts, only the rows that meet several conditions at once

## Sub lesson 1 — Several conditions at once — SUMIFS

**SUMIFS** is the summarising function that obeys more than one master: **SUMIFS(sum_range, criteria_range1, criteria1, criteria_range2, criteria2, …)** adds the values in the sum range only for rows where **every** stated condition holds — the sales *in June, in the north shop, of rice*. Indonesia's Grade 8 data chapter teaches it inside data summarising (peringkasan data), because real summaries are never one condition deep. Note the family's trap: in SUMIFS the **summed range comes first** — the opposite order from its single-condition cousin SUMIF.

In your mind's eye: several conditions at once — SUMIFS.

## Sub lesson 2 — Counting with conditions — COUNTIFS

**COUNTIFS** counts instead of adding: the number of rows meeting every condition — how many pupils are *in Grade 8, in the science club, and present today*. Together the pair answers the two shapes of every summarising question — *how much* and *how many* — under any number of conditions, and beside them in the chapter stands the pivot table, the same summarising done by dragging instead of writing. The discipline is the analyst's: state the conditions before the number, and report them with it — a conditional total quoted bare is a whole truth cut in half.

In your mind's eye: counting with conditions — COUNTIFS.
