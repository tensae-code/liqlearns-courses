# Preparing a Worksheet for Printing

## Do it first

Excel's grid has no edges, but paper does. This lesson fits the one to the other before a single sheet is printed.

## Sub lesson 1 — Setting the page for the worksheet

In **Page Setup** (on the Page Layout tab) you choose the paper size, the **orientation** — portrait for tall sheets, landscape for wide ones — and the **margins**, the white frame around the print. Wide worksheets need **Fit to Page**: scaling that shrinks the sheet so all its columns print on one page wide. You can also set the rows or columns that repeat on every page, so long lists keep their headings.

[Picture — setting the page for the worksheet]

## Sub lesson 2 — Print preview and the final check

The **print preview** shows the worksheet exactly as paper will receive it: count the pages, check that no column is cut off at an edge, that the title and headings appear, and that the scaling has not shrunk the text past reading. Only when the preview is right do you print — and you print the **print area** you set if only part of the sheet is wanted, not the whole grid.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: A worksheet that looks right on screen can spill badly onto paper, so preparing it for printing means setting the page — orientation, margins, fit — and checking the preview until every column lands where it should. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
