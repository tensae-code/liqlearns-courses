# Conditional Formatting — Colouring Cells by Their Values — true-false

Say whether each statement is true or false.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "What does conditional formatting do?" is: Colours cells by their values, by rule**
**Answer: True**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "What happens when a cell meets the rule?" is: Above average and below average**
**Answer: False** — It is highlighted automatically

**3. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "Name two ready-made value rules." is: Above average and below average**
**Answer: True**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "What two odd-one-out rules are named?" is: A new formatting rule — condition plus the formatting it triggers**
**Answer: False** — Unique values and duplicate values

**5. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "What do you build when no ready rule fits?" is: A new formatting rule — condition plus the formatting it triggers**
**Answer: True**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "Are the colours fixed once applied?" is: The values, not the colours**
**Answer: False** — No — they change by themselves when values change

**7. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "What does the rule watch?" is: The values, not the colours**
**Answer: True**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "What happens when a value crosses the rule after an edit?" is: In the cells of the chosen range**
**Answer: False** — Its highlighting appears or disappears by itself

**9. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "In what does the highlighting live?" is: In the cells of the chosen range**
**Answer: True**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "What two things does a new rule state?" is: Colours cells by their values, by rule**
**Answer: False** — The condition, and the formatting it triggers
