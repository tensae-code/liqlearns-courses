# Conditional Formatting — Colouring Cells by Their Values

## Do it first

In a sea of numbers, the important ones should shout. In this lesson you teach Excel to colour them for you — by rule. (teacher guide — evidence)

## Sub lesson 1 — Highlighting by rule

**Conditional formatting** means the **formatting follows a condition**: if a cell's value meets the **rule**, the cell is **highlighted** — filled or coloured — automatically. Ready-made rules highlight values **above average** or **below average**, and pick out **unique** values or **duplicate** values in a range.

## Sub lesson 2 — Making a new rule — and living colours

When no ready rule fits, you build a **new formatting rule**: state the condition and the formatting it triggers. The deep point of the lesson: the colours are **alive** — edit a value so it crosses the rule, and its highlighting appears or disappears by itself, because the rule watches the values, not the colours.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Conditional formatting colours cells by their values: you set a rule — above average, below average, unique, duplicate, or a new rule of your own — and Excel highlights every cell that meets it, changing the colours by itself when the values change. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
