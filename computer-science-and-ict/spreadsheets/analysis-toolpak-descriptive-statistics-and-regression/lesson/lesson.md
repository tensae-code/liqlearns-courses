# The Analysis ToolPak — Descriptive Statistics and Regression in Excel

## Do it first

The data lesson's last tool is an add-in that computes in one dialog what took a page of formulas: the whole descriptive summary, and regression with its full report. This lesson learns the ToolPak.

## Sub lesson 1 — The ToolPak and its descriptive statistics

The **Analysis ToolPak** is Excel's built-in analysis add-in (switched on once in the add-ins settings), offering a menu of analyses. Its **Descriptive Statistics** tool takes a data range and returns the full summary in one table: **mean, median, mode, standard deviation, variance, range, minimum, maximum, sum and count** (and more) — the descriptive lesson's family, computed at once, in a form fit to paste into a report. Jordan teaches the tool *after* the measures themselves: the ToolPak is a calculator for ideas the pupil already owns, and its output is read with those ideas, never worshipped without them.

## Sub lesson 2 — Regression in the ToolPak

The **Regression** tool fits the line of the forecasting lesson properly: given the x range (the explainer) and y range (the explained), it returns the line's equation and the report around it — above all **R-squared**, the share of y's variation the line accounts for, the fit's honesty number (near 1: the line carries the story; near 0: it doesn't). The tool computes; the analyst judges — whether a straight line is the right shape at all, whether the relation could be cause or companion (the cause-and-effect lesson stands behind this one), and what the forecast may responsibly claim.
