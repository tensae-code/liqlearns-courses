# Inserting Objects — Pictures and Shapes in Excel

## Do it first

A worksheet of pure numbers can be correct and still unreadable. Add the shop's picture and a shape pointing at the one figure that matters, and the sheet starts to speak. Iraq's Grade 11 Excel unit teaches exactly that adding. This lesson follows it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Inserting objects into a worksheet

**Inserting objects** means bringing things that are not cell data into the worksheet — above all **pictures** and **shapes**. An inserted object sits **on** the sheet, over the grid, and can be moved and sized where the worksheet needs it, while the cells and their values carry on underneath.

## Sub lesson 2 — Pictures and shapes at work

A **picture** shows the real thing the numbers are about; a **shape** — an arrow, a box, a callout — **points, groups or explains**. The book places the lesson inside the formatting part of the Excel unit: objects are part of how a worksheet is finished, after its data is entered and formatted, before it is laid out for printing.
