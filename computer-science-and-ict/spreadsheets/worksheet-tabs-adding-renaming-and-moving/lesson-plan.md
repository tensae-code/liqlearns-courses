# Lesson plan — Worksheet Tabs — Adding, Renaming and Moving

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Spreadsheets · **Lesson:** Worksheet Tabs — Adding, Renaming and Moving
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Eritrea (2026-11-14) — in the title Spreadsheets on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Eritrea's *Information and Communications Technology for Grade 8 — Textbook*, because no existing lesson teaches this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A workbook's worksheets live on the tabs along the bottom of the Excel window: click a tab to work on that sheet; add a new sheet from the Insert Worksheet button (or Shift+F11, or right-click a tab and Insert); rename a tab by double-clicking it and typing its new name; and move a sheet by dragging its tab along the row — or copy and move it precisely with right-click, Move or Copy.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on opening Excel and knowing what a worksheet's rows, columns and cells are; everything about the tabs themselves — adding, renaming, moving — is built inside the lesson, in Excel 2007, the version Eritrea's book teaches.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Worksheet tabs and adding a new worksheet** — The tabs along the bottom of the Excel window, one for each worksheet in the workbook — a new workbook in the book's Excel starts with three: Sheet1, Sheet2 and Sheet3; clicking a tab brings that sheet to the front. Adding: click the Insert Worksheet button beside the last tab, or press Shift+F11, or right-click a tab and choose Insert — the new sheet appears before the tab you clicked.
2. **Renaming a worksheet tab** — Double-click the tab, and its name turns editable; type the new name — one that says what the sheet holds, like Marks or Expenses — and press Enter. The same rename lives on the right-click menu: right-click the tab, choose Rename.
3. **Moving and copying a worksheet** — Drag the tab along the tab row: a small arrow marks where it will land, and letting go drops it there — the sheets' order changes, their contents do not. For an exact move, or a copy, right-click the tab and choose Move or Copy: pick the workbook and the sheet it should sit before, and tick Create a copy when a copy — not a move — is wanted.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Switch between worksheets by clicking their tabs, and say how many sheets a new workbook starts with in the book's Excel.
2. Add a new worksheet three ways: the Insert Worksheet button, Shift+F11, and right-click a tab → Insert.
3. Rename a worksheet tab by double-clicking it, typing the new name and pressing Enter.
4. Move a worksheet by dragging its tab, and move or copy it to an exact place with right-click → Move or Copy.

## What you need
A computer running Microsoft Excel 2007 for the hands-on work (the book's own version). On paper first: draw a workbook's tab row and practise the order of tabs with paper slips.

## If the learner is stuck
Renames the sheet by typing over a cell → renaming touches the tab, not the grid: double-click the tab itself. Drags a tab and thinks the contents moved to another file → a drag only reorders the sheets inside the same workbook; contents travel with their tab. Cannot find a sheet → count and read the tabs along the bottom first; a workbook can hold many sheets behind one window.
