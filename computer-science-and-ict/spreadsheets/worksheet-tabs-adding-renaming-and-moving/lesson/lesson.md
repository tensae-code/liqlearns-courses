# Worksheet Tabs — Adding, Renaming and Moving

## Do it first

Open a workbook — or picture one. Along the bottom of the Excel window runs a row of small labels: Sheet1, Sheet2, Sheet3. Those labels are doors. Each one opens a whole separate worksheet inside the same file — and the names tell you nothing about what is inside each sheet. This lesson works those doors: adding new ones, giving them real names, and putting them in the right order.

## Sub lesson 1 — Worksheet tabs and adding a new worksheet

A workbook is one file holding several **worksheets**. You see them through the **worksheet tabs** — the labelled strips along the bottom of the window. In the Excel of this book, a new workbook starts with **three** worksheets: **Sheet1**, **Sheet2** and **Sheet3**.

[Picture — the bottom of an Excel window, with the worksheet tab row and the Insert Worksheet button labelled]

Click a tab, and that worksheet comes to the front: its grid fills the window, ready to work on. The other sheets do not change; they wait behind their tabs. Only one sheet is in front at a time.

Three sheets are often not enough — a term has more subjects, a year more months. To **add a worksheet**:

1. Click the **Insert Worksheet button** — the small sheet icon beside the last tab. A new sheet appears after the others.
2. Or press **Shift+F11**. The new sheet is inserted before the sheet you are on.
3. Or **right-click any tab** and choose **Insert**, then confirm. The new sheet appears before the tab you right-clicked.

Whichever way you use, the new sheet gets the next free name — Sheet4, Sheet5, and so on — and it is empty and ready.

## Sub lesson 2 — Renaming a worksheet tab

Sheet1, Sheet2, Sheet3 say nothing. A tab's name should say what its sheet holds, so the right sheet can be found at a glance.

To **rename** a tab:

1. **Double-click the tab.** Its name becomes editable — highlighted, ready to type over.
2. Type the new name — *Marks*, *Expenses*, *Term 2*.
3. Press **Enter**. The tab now carries the new name.

The same job lives on the menu: **right-click the tab** and choose **Rename**, then type and press Enter. Renaming changes only the label on the door — nothing inside the sheet changes.

## Sub lesson 3 — Moving and copying a worksheet

Sheets have an order, left to right, and the order should help the reader: first term before second term, January before February. To **move** a sheet, **drag its tab** along the tab row. As you drag, a small arrow travels along the top of the tabs, marking the place where the sheet will land. Let go, and the sheet drops there. Its contents travel with it, unchanged; only its position among the sheets is new.

For an exact move — or for making a **copy** — use the menu:

1. **Right-click the tab** and choose **Move or Copy**.
2. In the box that opens, choose the workbook (this one, or another open one) and the sheet your sheet should sit **before**.
3. If you want a copy left in both places, tick **Create a copy**. Without the tick, it is a move: the sheet leaves its old position.
4. Click **OK**.

Dragging is quick for a short hop along the row; Move or Copy is exact for long jumps, copies, and moving a sheet into another workbook.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: worksheets live behind the tabs at the bottom of the window; a new workbook starts with three; you add a sheet with the Insert Worksheet button, Shift+F11, or right-click → Insert; you rename by double-clicking the tab and typing; and you move a sheet by dragging its tab, or exactly — with a copy if you tick the box — through right-click → Move or Copy. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: add a fourth worksheet to a workbook, using a different one of the three ways each time you practise, and say where the new sheet landed each time.
2. Show it, don't just say it: rename Sheet1, Sheet2 and Sheet3 to names that say what each sheet could hold for a school term, using the double-click way for one and the right-click way for another.
3. Show it, don't just say it: put three renamed sheets into the order a reader needs — earliest first — once by dragging, and once through Move or Copy, and say which way you would choose for moving a sheet into a different workbook, and why.
