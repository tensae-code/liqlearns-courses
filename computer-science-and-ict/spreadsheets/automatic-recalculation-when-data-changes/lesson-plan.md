## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The living sheet — results that follow their data** — **Automatic recalculation** means: when a **value in a cell changes**, every **formula that depends on it** — directly, or through other formulas in a chain — **works itself out again at once** and shows its new answer. The total was never a typed number; it was a **standing instruction** (sum those cells) whose answer the sheet keeps current. This is why the formula lessons come first in the unit: a sheet of typed totals is a photograph; a sheet of formulas is a living calculator that cannot go stale.
2. **Chains and care — what recalculation demands of the builder** — Recalculation chains run **as far as the formulas reach** — change an early price and a total, a tax on the total, and a grand total all move in the same instant — so the builder's care is the mirror of the gift: **build with formulas wherever a value is derived**, never type a derived value by hand, and **check the chain's sources**, because one wrong source value now poisons every result downstream, instantly and everywhere. Grade 8 teaches the gift and the care in one section because they are one fact seen from two sides.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — On paper, a corrected price means re-adding the whole column by hand. In the spreadsheet, you change the price and the total simply IS different — nobody re-added anything. Grade 8 stops the course to honour that small miracle, because it is the reason spreadsheets exist. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
