# Freeze panes and Split — keeping headers in view

## Do it first

Your grade sheet has two hundred rows. Scroll to row 150 — the headings have vanished and the numbers mean nothing. Freeze the headings in place, then scroll again.

## Sub lesson 1 — Freeze Panes — what stays, stays

To keep headers in sight while scrolling, **freeze**, in the book's order:

1. **Select the row below** — or the **column right of** — what must stay. What is above and left of the selection is what freezes.
2. **View tab → Freeze Panes → Freeze Panes**.
3. A **grey line** marks the frozen part.
4. Scroll — scrolling continues **beneath or beside** the frozen part, which stands still.
5. Undo it from the same menu: **Unfreeze Panes**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Split — two separately scrolling panes

Splitting instead divides the worksheet into **separately scrolling panes**, for **comparing distant sections without a new window**:

1. **Select the split cell** — the cell where the split should begin.
2. **View → Split**.
3. **Scroll each pane independently** and compare the two sections side by side.
4. Click **Split again** to remove the split.

Freeze when headings must stay; split when two far-apart parts must be read together.

Tell your companion which cell you select to freeze a header row, where the grey line appears, and how Split differs from Freeze.

## Do It Yourself

1. Freeze the header row of a long grade sheet and scroll to its end; unfreeze it.
2. Split a sheet at a middle cell, scroll the two panes to two distant sections, and compare them.
3. Remove the split by clicking Split again.
