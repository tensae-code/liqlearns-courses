# Lesson plan — Freeze panes and Split — keeping headers in view

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Spreadsheets · **Lesson:** Freeze panes and Split — keeping headers in view
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Application Software, Grade 10 depth, in the Spreadsheets title of the Computer Science and ICT ladder (TITLES.md). The ladder is the recommended order, never a forced one.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Freeze Panes holds the rows above and columns left of your selection still while the rest scrolls, and Split divides the sheet into separately scrolling panes for comparing distant sections.
**This lesson's own level:** it is a doing-lesson. It stands on the spreadsheet lessons; the sheet is long, and this lesson keeps its headings in sight.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or in steps — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Freeze Panes — what stays, stays** — pass mark: freeze panes by the book's selection rule, name the grey line, and unfreeze.
2. **Split — two separately scrolling panes** — pass mark: split a sheet, scroll the panes independently, and remove the split.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Excel functions — the big five (grown lesson in this title). Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Freeze rows and columns by selecting the row below or column right of them.
2. Say what the grey line marks, and unfreeze from the same menu.
3. Split a sheet at a chosen cell and scroll each pane independently.
4. Remove a split, and say when Split serves better than Freeze.

## What you need
A computer with Excel and a long worksheet.

## If the learner is stuck
- The wrong rows froze → unfreeze, then select the row BELOW the rows that must stay — the selection is the line.
- Panes will not scroll apart → you froze instead of splitting; unfreeze, select the split cell, View → Split.
