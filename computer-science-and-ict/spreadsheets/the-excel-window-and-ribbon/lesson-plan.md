# Lesson plan — The Excel Window and Ribbon

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Spreadsheets · **Lesson:** The Excel Window and Ribbon
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Iraq (2026-11-14) — in the title Spreadsheets on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Iraq's Grade 11 Computer student book, because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The main interface of Excel is the window you work in: the worksheet of rows and columns in the middle, where the data goes, held inside the program's window with its title and controls. Across the top runs the ribbon: the strip of tabs and tools for working on the worksheet — formatting, inserting and laying out the page all live there in later lessons.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The main interface of Microsoft Excel** — The **main interface** of Excel is the window you work in: the **worksheet** of rows and columns in the middle, where the data goes, held inside the program's window with its title and controls. The unit's first lesson filled in what a spreadsheet is; this one names the parts of the screen it happens on.
2. **The ribbon** — Across the top runs the **ribbon**: the strip of **tabs and tools** for working on the worksheet — formatting, inserting and laying out the page all live there in later lessons. Learning the ribbon's layout once means every later Excel lesson can simply say where a tool lives, and the pupil already knows the way.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
