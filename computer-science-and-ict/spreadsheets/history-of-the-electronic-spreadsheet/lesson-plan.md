## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **From paper ledgers to VisiCalc — the first electronic spreadsheet** — For centuries, tables of numbers were kept on **paper ledgers**: change one number, and every total depending on it had to be re-added by hand. The **electronic spreadsheet** changed the rule: put the numbers in cells, write formulas once, and the sheet **recalculates itself** when any number changes. The first successful one was **VisiCalc** (1979, Dan Bricklin and Bob Frankston, on the Apple II) — so powerful that businesses bought the computer just to run it. Armenia's Grade 6 book opens its spreadsheet block with this history (§10) before teaching the sheet itself.
2. **Lotus, Excel and the sheet everywhere — how the idea won** — **Lotus 1-2-3** (1983) made the spreadsheet a business standard on the IBM PC, adding charts and database features. **Microsoft Excel** (1985 for Macintosh, then Windows) won with the graphical interface — click cells instead of memorising commands — and became the world's default sheet. The idea then escaped the office: free and online sheets (Google Sheets, 2006) made recalculation available to anyone with a browser. The lesson of the history: the winning feature was never the grid — it was **automatic recalculation**.
