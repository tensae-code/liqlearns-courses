# History of the Electronic Spreadsheet

## Do it first

The spreadsheet feels eternal. It has a birthday — and inventors. This lesson tells its story.

## Sub lesson 1 — From paper ledgers to VisiCalc — the first electronic spreadsheet

For centuries, tables of numbers were kept on **paper ledgers**: change one number, and every total depending on it had to be re-added by hand. The **electronic spreadsheet** changed the rule: put the numbers in cells, write formulas once, and the sheet **recalculates itself** when any number changes. The first successful one was **VisiCalc** (1979, Dan Bricklin and Bob Frankston, on the Apple II) — so powerful that businesses bought the computer just to run it. Armenia's Grade 6 book opens its spreadsheet block with this history (§10) before teaching the sheet itself.

In your mind's eye: from paper ledgers to VisiCalc — the first electronic spreadsheet.

## Sub lesson 2 — Lotus, Excel and the sheet everywhere — how the idea won

**Lotus 1-2-3** (1983) made the spreadsheet a business standard on the IBM PC, adding charts and database features. **Microsoft Excel** (1985 for Macintosh, then Windows) won with the graphical interface — click cells instead of memorising commands — and became the world's default sheet. The idea then escaped the office: free and online sheets (Google Sheets, 2006) made recalculation available to anyone with a browser. The lesson of the history: the winning feature was never the grid — it was **automatic recalculation**.

In your mind's eye: lotus, Excel and the sheet everywhere — how the idea won.
