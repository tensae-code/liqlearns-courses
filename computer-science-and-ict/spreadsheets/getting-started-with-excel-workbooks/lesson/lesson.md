# Getting Started with Excel Workbooks

## Do it first

Kuwait's Grade 5 Excel journey, 'My Special Table', begins before any cell is touched: with the workbook itself as an object — opened, looked around, saved, closed, and found again. This lesson is that beginning.

## Sub lesson 1 — Launching Excel and exploring its window

**Microsoft Excel** is launched like the Office programs before it, and its window is **explored** before it is used: the ribbon of commands across the top, the **grid** of rows and columns that fills the middle, the sheet's name below. The book's order is deliberate — the pupil who can name the window's parts can follow every later instruction in the journey ('in the ribbon…', 'on the sheet…') without hunting.

In your mind's eye: launching Excel and exploring its window.

## Sub lesson 2 — Workbooks — saving, opening and closing

The file Excel works on is a **workbook**. **Saving** writes the workbook to a place and name the pupil chooses — the act that turns screen work into kept work; **closing** puts the workbook away (asking, if unsaved changes remain, whether to keep them); **opening** brings a saved workbook back, exactly as it was left. Kuwait teaches the three acts in the first lesson because the rest of the journey's tables exist only if they can be kept and found again.

In your mind's eye: workbooks — saving, opening and closing.
