# Merging Cells in Excel

## Do it first

An Excel sheet is a grid of equal cells — but a table's title belongs over the whole table, not trapped in its first cell. Laos's Grade 9 Excel course opens its formatting chapter with the join that solves it: Merge Cell. This lesson makes titles sit where they belong.

## Sub lesson 1 — What merging does in a sheet

**Merging** joins a **selected block of cells into one large cell**. The block's **top-left value survives** as the merged cell's content; the other cells' separate identities disappear into the join. In Laos's Excel (the XP/2003-era program of the screenshots) the command is **Format → Cells → Alignment → Merge cells**, with the toolbar's **Merge and Center** button doing the same join and centring in one act. A merged cell still has one address — its top-left corner's — which formulas and the Name Box use.

In your mind's eye: what merging does in a sheet.

## Sub lesson 2 — Merge and Center — the title over the table

The command's daily job is the **title**: select the empty row of cells **across the table's width**, merge them, type the title, and it sits **centred over the whole table** as one heading. The book teaches merging first in the formatting chapter because it is formatting, not data work: the numbers below are untouched; only their heading's home has widened. Its warnings travel with it: merged cells can tangle **sorting and selecting** (a column partly merged refuses tidy sorts), so the careful maker merges titles and headings — and keeps the data's own grid regular.

In your mind's eye: merge and Center — the title over the table.
