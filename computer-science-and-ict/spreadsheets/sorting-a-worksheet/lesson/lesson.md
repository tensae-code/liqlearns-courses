# Sorting a worksheet

## Do it first

The grade report lists pupils in the order they arrived. You need them by first name, A to Z. Do not retype a single name — sort the sheet.

## Sub lesson 1 — The scope decision — whole sheet or only a range

Sorting **reorganises** a worksheet — **alphabetically, numerically, by date, by a custom list, or even by cell colour, font colour or icon**.

The scope decision comes first:

- **Sort the whole sheet by one column** — **all rows move together**, so each pupil keeps their own marks.
- **Sort only a range** — neighbouring tables are **untouched**.

Choose wrongly here and rows can be torn away from the data that belongs to them — decide before you click.

## Sub lesson 2 — Sorting the whole sheet — in order

For a sheet sort, in the book's order:

1. **Select a cell in the column to sort by** — the book's example sorts a **grade report by first name**.
2. Open the **Data tab**.
3. Click **Ascending (A→Z)** or **Descending (Z→A)**.
4. **The whole sheet follows the chosen column** — every row moves with its name.

Check the result: pick one pupil and confirm their marks are still on their own row.

## Say it to your companion

## Do It Yourself

1. Sort a grade report by first name, A→Z, and check one pupil's marks stayed on their row.
2. Sort the same sheet Z→A and say what changed.
3. Sort only a small range and confirm the neighbouring table did not move.
