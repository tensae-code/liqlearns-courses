# Lesson plan — Absolute cell references — $B$9

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Spreadsheets · **Lesson:** Absolute cell references — $B$9
**Place in the curriculum:** Title — Spreadsheets in the Computer Science and ICT ladder (TITLES.md); stocked from Ethiopia's Grade 9 book, Unit 3 — Application Software. The ladder is the recommended order, never a forced one.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** $ before the column and row pins a reference so it never moves when copied — one shared value, like a bonus mark, serving every row.
**This lesson's own level:** it is a doing-lesson. It stands on The fill handle — copying a formula.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what an absolute reference is: it stays constant even when copied or moved.
2. Make one with $ before the column and row, or by pressing F4 after selecting the reference.
3. Build =B2+C2+D2+E2+$B$9 and check F3–F6: relative cells change, $B$9 never does.
4. Read the guide's keyed mixed formula ((A1 + A4) − B5) × $F$2 piece by piece.
## What you need
A computer with a spreadsheet program; the mark sheet with its totals.
