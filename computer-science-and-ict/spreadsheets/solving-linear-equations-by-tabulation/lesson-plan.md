## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Both sides tabulated — walking toward the meeting** — To solve by **tabulation**, the unknown takes a column of growing values; beside it, two columns work out the equation's **left side** and **right side** at each value. Row by row the sides approach, cross, or — in the **meeting row** — stand **equal**: the unknown's value in that row is the **solution**. The method needs no rule of algebra beyond substitution, and it shows what a solution IS — not a ritual's result, but the value where the two sides agree.
2. **Fractional equations and the step's size** — The same table solves **fractional equations** — equations whose sides carry fractions — where algebra's manipulations grow thornier: the sheet substitutes and compares exactly as before. The craftsman's choice is the **step**: walk by 1 and the meeting is passed in coarse strides; walk finer around the crossing and the meeting is cornered. Mongolia's pairing of the two spreadsheet lessons is deliberate: sequences taught the sheet to carry a rule forward; equations teach it to hunt — the same columns, aimed at a meeting instead of a growth.
