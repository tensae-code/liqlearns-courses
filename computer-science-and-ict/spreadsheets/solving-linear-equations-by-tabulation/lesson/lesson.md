# Solving Linear Equations by Tabulation

## Do it first

Algebra solves an equation by rules; a spreadsheet solves it by patience. Mongolia's Grade 9 book teaches the second way as a method with its own honesty: build both sides, walk the unknown, and watch for the meeting.

## Sub lesson 1 — Both sides tabulated — walking toward the meeting

To solve by **tabulation**, the unknown takes a column of growing values; beside it, two columns work out the equation's **left side** and **right side** at each value. Row by row the sides approach, cross, or — in the **meeting row** — stand **equal**: the unknown's value in that row is the **solution**. The method needs no rule of algebra beyond substitution, and it shows what a solution IS — not a ritual's result, but the value where the two sides agree.

## Sub lesson 2 — Fractional equations and the step's size

The same table solves **fractional equations** — equations whose sides carry fractions — where algebra's manipulations grow thornier: the sheet substitutes and compares exactly as before. The craftsman's choice is the **step**: walk by 1 and the meeting is passed in coarse strides; walk finer around the crossing and the meeting is cornered. Mongolia's pairing of the two spreadsheet lessons is deliberate: sequences taught the sheet to carry a rule forward; equations teach it to hunt — the same columns, aimed at a meeting instead of a growth.
