# Forecasting and Trendlines in Excel

## Do it first

The data lesson turns from the past to the future: given what sales did for twelve months, what will they do in the thirteenth? This lesson learns Excel's forecasting — with its humility attached.

## Sub lesson 1 — Scatter and trendline — seeing the road

Forecasting begins by **seeing**: the values are drawn as a **scatter plot** (time across, value up), and a **trendline** — the straight (or curved) line that best follows the points — is laid over them, with its **equation** displayable. The trendline is a claim in picture form: 'the data climbs about so much per month.' Its fit can be read: points hugging the line support it; a cloud mocks it — and a trendline drawn through a season's rhythm without noticing the rhythm forecasts a fiction.

## Sub lesson 2 — FORECAST.LINEAR — and the humility

**FORECAST.LINEAR(x, known values, known times)** computes the line's value at a future x — the thirteenth month's predicted sales. Jordan teaches the function inside **predictive analysis** with its limits in the same breath: a forecast **extends the past's pattern** and breaks when the world changes (a competitor opens, a law changes, a season turns), and it is worth less the **farther** it reaches. The analyst reports a forecast **with** its pattern and its doubts — 'on the last year's trend, about X, if nothing changes' — because a bare predicted number is the past wearing the future's clothes and hoping no one asks.
