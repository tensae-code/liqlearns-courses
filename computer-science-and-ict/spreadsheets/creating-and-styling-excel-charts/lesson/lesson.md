# Creating and styling Excel charts

## Do it first

Your student grades sit in a table of numbers nobody reads. Turn them into a pie chart the class understands at a glance — then title it properly.

## Sub lesson 1 — Creating a chart — in order

A **chart displays data visually** — **bar, column, pie, line, area, scatter, surface and radar** types are named.

In the book's order:

1. **Select the source cells, including column titles and row labels**.
2. **Insert tab → a Chart command**.
3. **Choose the chart type** — or let **Recommended Charts** suggest types fitted to the data.
4. The **chart appears in the worksheet**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Shaping the chart — Design tab

Afterwards the chart is shaped from the **Design tab**:

- **Add Chart Element** inserts **titles, legends and data labels** — an element is edited by **double-clicking its placeholder and typing**.
- **Quick Layout** applies a **ready-made arrangement** in one pick.
- **Chart Styles** restyle its look in one pick.

The practical builds a **pie or line chart of student grades**, changes its layout, and retitles it **Student Grade Record**.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the eight chart types named, the creation order — selection first, titles included — and the three Design-tab shaping tools.

## Do It Yourself

1. Select grade data with its titles and labels, and insert a pie chart.
2. Add a title with Add Chart Element and type Student Grade Record.
3. Apply a Quick Layout and a new Chart Style, and say what each changed.
