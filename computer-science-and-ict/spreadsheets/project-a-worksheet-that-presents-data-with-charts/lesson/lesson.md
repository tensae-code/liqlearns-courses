# Project — a Worksheet That Presents Data with Charts

## Do it first

Numbers alone do not present themselves. Your group's job: one topic, one honest worksheet, and charts that present its data to an audience. (teacher guide — evidence)

## Sub lesson 1 — Building the group's worksheet

The group **chooses a topic** and gathers its **real data** into a **worksheet**: tidy rows and columns, clear labels, values that can be checked. The worksheet is the project's foundation — every chart drawn from it inherits its honesty or its errors.

[Picture — building the group's worksheet]

## Sub lesson 2 — Presenting the data with charts

From the worksheet, the group builds the **charts that present the data**: the right chart kind for each question the topic raises, formatted so an audience reads it at a glance. Presenting means the group can walk an audience from the data to the chart to the conclusion — and answer for all three.

[Picture — presenting the data with charts]

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: The unit's project: in a group, choose a topic, build a worksheet of its real data, and present that data with charts — the sheet and its charts together telling the topic's story. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: A topic. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: Tidy rows and columns, clear labels, checkable values. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: The right kind for each question the topic raises. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: From the data to the chart to the conclusion. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
