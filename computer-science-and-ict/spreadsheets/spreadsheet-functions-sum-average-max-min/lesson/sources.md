**Acceptance count: taught in 7 countries (Ethiopia, Eritrea, Belarus, Yemen, North Macedonia, Iraq, Uzbekistan).**

Yemen's own way (Grade 11): the book pivots from operating the machine to understanding information itself — data kinds, number systems and coding get full units before the applications (presentations, Excel) and a networks/Internet finale; every unit closes with a formal تقويم (assessment).

Belarus's own way (Grade 10): the Zabaroŭski–Pupcaŭ course treats informatics as applied mathematics with office tools — a full-year arc from data archiving through Pascal strings and information modelling to Excel and 2010-era Internet communication (NetMeeting, Skype, blogs).

Teacher-guide fault noted for the Grade 10 growth: the guide's Unit 3 Practical Exercises 3.2 and 3.3 carry the same description twice, Exercise 3.6's tip points to page-number/header help, and Exercises 3.7–3.9 repeat one data-entry tip regardless of topic — the guide was used here only for its keyed answers and chained-practical pacing, never for those exercise descriptions. Three of Sub lesson 4's nine features — formula auditing, password protection and find and replace — appear nowhere in the student book; they stand on teacher-guide evidence alone (Grade 10 Teacher Guide, printed pp. 63–64), labelled in the sources table.

The Grade 8 teacher guide's Unit 3 answer key (printed pp. 32–33) was used as a verified-answer check for this lesson's questions; its two demonstration tasks are filed in this lesson — the sum-and-average demonstration in Sub lesson 2 and the Do It Yourself block.

North Macedonia's own way (Year 1): five themes move from modern digital technology and online living into a full structured-programming course taught in C++ (with Scratch/C++/Java/Python/PHP/Lisp compared), then advanced Word work (styles, contents, templates) and spreadsheets up to pivot tables.

Mauritania's own way (Grade 8): The second year is organised as five applied units of 35 thirty-minute fiches — image processing first (Paint/GIMP/Photoshop, bitmap vs vector), then internet ethics, a full Excel data-processing unit ending in a practical project, web source-checking and responsible downloading, and finally email — each unit closed by evaluation and support fiches with full answer keys at the end.

Iraq's own way (Grade 11): the final preparatory year is the most forward-looking of the series — smart devices and the Internet of Things (with a lesson on choosing a computer or smartphone by its specifications), a full Excel unit ending in AutoSum, one- and two-dimensional arrays with nested loops (the series' first data structures), and electronic services: e-government, email and cloud computing with its applications.


Uzbekistan's own way (Grade 9): grade 9 is the capstone — computer graphics, Access, Excel, then a nine-lesson algorithms block and a 28-lesson Turbo Pascal 7.0 course that runs from the alphabet of the language to files, procedures and graphics mode, closing with the Internet. Every chapter ends with a dedicated repetition lesson, placed here to that chapter's main lesson.
