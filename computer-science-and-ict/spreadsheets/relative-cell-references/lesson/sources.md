**Acceptance count: taught in 6 countries (Ethiopia, Belarus, Sri Lanka, Rwanda, South Sudan, Kazakhstan).**

Belarus's own way (Grade 10): the Zabaroŭski–Pupcaŭ course treats informatics as applied mathematics with office tools — a full-year arc from data archiving through Pascal strings and information modelling to Excel and 2010-era Internet communication (NetMeeting, Skype, blogs).

Book errors taught corrected: the spreadsheet section's figure references drift from their captions in this part of Unit 3; this lesson teaches from the pictured content and cites no figure number.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 12 Science for Technology, Tamil): the A/L technology-stream ICT volume teaches every office skill twice — once in Microsoft Office 2010 and once in LibreOffice (Writer/Calc/Impress 4.1) — and closes with creating a real Gmail account, mailbox folders, attachments and password safety; hardware goes down to cache levels L1/L2/L3, ROM types and file systems (FAT/NTFS/ext4).

Rwanda's own way (S2 ICT Teacher Guide, KLB): S2 moves to data protection, ICT in finance (including Rwandan mobile banking risks and biometrics), spreadsheets in depth, a second ArcGIS unit, and Scratch game design with sprite interaction tables and score/level rules. (teacher guide — evidence)

Rwanda's own way (S2 ICT Student Book, KLB): the student companion to the S2 guide — the same nine units, taught through Rwandan financial life (mobile banking, ATMs, stock exchange) and Scratch game projects (Helicopter Game, Moving Eyes Game).

South Sudan's own way (Grade 10 S2 Student): Year 2 moves from single-computer skills to connected and analytical work — networks taught through intranet file-sharing and a full Microsoft Outlook unit (contacts, calendar, meetings, task lists), then a deep Excel unit that goes well beyond basics into lookup/financial/text functions, inventories, correlation and regression, what-if analysis with Solver, and workbook protection; social media is taught platform by platform with its ethical and legal issues.


Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G8 Informatika, Lazarus edition): Grade 8 by the Almatykitap team measures information two ways (the probabilistic and alphabetic approaches, with Hartley-formula problems), works Excel hard (absolute/relative references, built-in functions, data analysis, applied problems), and teaches programming in the Lazarus IDE — case, for, nested, repeat-until and while operators, tracing, and procedures and functions — before a final part that runs the whole problem-solving cycle from statement to tested program.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G8 Informatika, Python edition): the newer, shorter Grade 8 edition from the same team keeps the measurement, health and Excel parts but replaces the Lazarus IDE with Python: for and while loops, nested loops, continue, break and loop-else, algorithm tracing, and a closing part on practical programming that ends with olympiad-style tasks.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G8 «Информатика» 2019 edition): the 2019 Almatykitap Grade 8 book splits "measuring information" into two named approaches — the probability approach and the alphabet approach — and its IDE section is a Pascal/Lazarus visual-component environment, ending in subroutines; the 2023 re-issue (152 pages, Book 21) is a different, shorter book.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G8 «Информатика» 2023 edition): the 2023 re-issue keeps the 2019 book's first three sections but replaces the Pascal IDE programming with a Python loops course: for, nested loops, while, then a full unit each on continue, break and the loop else clause, closing with olympiad preparation tasks.
