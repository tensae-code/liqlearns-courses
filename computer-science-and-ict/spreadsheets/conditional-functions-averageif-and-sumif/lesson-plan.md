## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **SUMIF — summing with a condition** — **SUMIF(range, criteria, sum_range)** adds only the values whose row **meets the criteria**: sum the sales **where** the city is Amman; sum the payments **where** the month is June. Its three parts name the pattern of every conditional function: **where to test, what to test for, what to gather**. The condition may be an equality, a comparison ('>100'), or a pattern — and the function's honesty is that excluded rows are not approximated or hidden; they simply do not enter the sum.
2. **AVERAGEIF — and the pair's discipline** — **AVERAGEIF** works identically for the average: the mean of the marks **of those who passed**, of the prices **in one category**. The pair's discipline is the analyst's: state the criteria **before** computing (a condition chosen after seeing results is a story, not a statistic), and report the condition **with** the number ('average, among sitters of the second exam: …') because a conditional number quoted bare is a whole truth cut in half. Jordan teaches the pair inside data analysis because most real questions are conditional — data is rarely asked about 'everything'.
