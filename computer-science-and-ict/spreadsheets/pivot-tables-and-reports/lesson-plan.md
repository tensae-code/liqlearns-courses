# Lesson plan — Pivot Tables and Reports

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Spreadsheets · **Lesson:** Pivot Tables and Reports
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — North Macedonia (2026-11-14) — in the title Spreadsheets on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from North Macedonia's *Informatics* (2026), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A pivot table summarises a long list of records into a small report — it groups rows by a chosen field, totals (or counts, or averages) a chosen value for each group, and lets you rearrange the grouping in seconds without touching or changing the original data.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Grouping and totalling a list** — Start from a tidy list with **headings** (one record per row). Insert a **pivot table**, then drag fields into its areas: a field into **Rows** becomes the grouping (for example, one row per product), a numeric field into **Values** is **summarised** for each group — **sum** by default, or count, average, max or min. The source list is not changed; the pivot is a view built on top of it.
2. **Reading and reshaping the report** — A second field into **Columns** (or a filter) splits each total further — sales by product **and** by month, say. Dragging fields around **repivots** the report instantly, which is the whole point: the same data answers many questions. When the source data changes, **refresh** the pivot so the report catches up. North Macedonia's book ends its spreadsheet theme here — pivot reports are its deepest spreadsheet skill.
