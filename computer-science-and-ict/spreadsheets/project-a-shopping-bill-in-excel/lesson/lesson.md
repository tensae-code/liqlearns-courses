# Project: a shopping bill in Excel

## Do it first

Ebsa is sent to the local gulit market with a list: potatoes, sugar, onion, banana, coffee beans and salt — quantities like 1 kilo, ½ kilo, ¼ kilo, and a price for each. Before Ebsa pays, the bill must be worked out: each item's cost, the tax on each, and the one grand total. Build the whole sheet now — every skill from the last five lessons in one place.

## The sheet

Set up the shopping list: the six items — **potatoes, sugar, onion, banana, coffee beans and salt** — with their **quantities** (1 kilo, ½ kilo, ¼ kilo…) and **prices**, item by item down the sheet.

[Picture 13709 — the finished shopping sheet — price, tax, net price, grand total]

## Cost per item — a relative formula copied down

1. In **E3**, build one formula **multiplying price by quantity** for the first item.
2. **Copy it down** for every item — the references are relative, so each row prices its own item.

## Tax — an absolute reference at work

A **15% sales tax** is held in **G1**. In **column F**, apply the tax to every item using the **absolute reference $G$1**, and copy the formula down — the tax rate stays pinned to G1 for every row, exactly as the bonus mark stayed pinned to $B$9.

## Net price and the grand total

1. In **G3**, build the **net-price formula — total + tax** — and copy it down.
2. In **G9**, **sum all the net prices**. That sum is the **grand total — the amount Ebsa will pay** at the gulit market.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: one real bill built whole: a price-times-quantity formula copied down, a 15% tax pinned by an absolute reference, net prices, and a grand total — the amount Ebsa will pay. Use the lesson's own words for the key names.
