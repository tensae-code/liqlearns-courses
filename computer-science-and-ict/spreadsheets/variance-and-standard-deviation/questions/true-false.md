# Variance and Standard Deviation — true-false

Say whether each statement is true or false.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "What question do variance and standard deviation answer?" is: How far values scatter around their centre**
**Answer: True**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "What is variance?" is: So negatives cannot cancel positives, and big distances count heavily**
**Answer: False** — The average of the squared distances of the values from the mean

**3. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "Why are the distances squared?" is: So negatives cannot cancel positives, and big distances count heavily**
**Answer: True**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "Which spreadsheet function does Egypt's book use for variance?" is: Every value equals the mean — no scatter at all**
**Answer: False** — VAR.P — over the whole group being described

**5. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "What does a variance of zero mean?" is: Every value equals the mean — no scatter at all**
**Answer: True**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "What is standard deviation?" is: To return the spread to the data's own units — marks, not marks squared**
**Answer: False** — The square root of the variance

**7. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "Why take the square root?" is: To return the spread to the data's own units — marks, not marks squared**
**Answer: True**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "How is standard deviation read?" is: Yes — one bunched, one split — which is why the average alone misleads**
**Answer: False** — As the typical distance of a value from the mean

**9. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "Can two groups share an average yet differ in spread?" is: Yes — one bunched, one split — which is why the average alone misleads**
**Answer: True**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "What fair comparison does the spread enable?" is: How far values scatter around their centre**
**Answer: False** — Judging whether a difference in averages matters, given how wide each group scatters
