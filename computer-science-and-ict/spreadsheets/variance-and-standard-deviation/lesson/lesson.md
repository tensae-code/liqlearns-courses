# Variance and Standard Deviation

## Do it first

Egypt's Grade 10 data-analysis run climbs from organising data and averages into spread: how far values scatter around their centre. Variance and standard deviation are the two measures that answer it, computed in the spreadsheet with VAR.P and its square root.

## Sub lesson 1 — Variance — the average squared distance from the mean

Two classes can share the same average mark and be nothing alike — one tightly bunched, one split between top and bottom. **Variance** measures that scatter: for each value, take its **distance from the mean**, **square** it (so negatives cannot cancel positives, and big distances count heavily), and **average** the squares. In the spreadsheet this is **VAR.P** over the range — the book's population form, used when the data in hand is the whole group being described. A variance of zero means every value equals the mean; the larger the variance, the wider the scatter.

## Sub lesson 2 — Standard deviation — spread in the data's own units

Variance's flaw is its unit: squared marks are marks², which nobody can picture. **Standard deviation** fixes it by taking the **square root of the variance**, returning the spread to the data's own units — marks, centimetres, seconds. Read it as the **typical distance** of a value from the mean: most values lie within about one standard deviation of the centre. Egypt's analysis sequence uses it to compare groups fairly — two classes, two years, two products — because a difference in averages means one thing when the spread is small and quite another when the spread is wide.
