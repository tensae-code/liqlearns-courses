# Types of Data — Text, Numbers, Formulas

## Do it first

Three cells all show '12'. One is a label, one is a typed number, and one is an answer the sheet calculated — only one of them will change when the data changes. In this lesson you learn the three types of data a spreadsheet holds, and how to tell them apart.

## Sub lesson 1 — Text and numbers

**Text** data is words and labels: names, headings, notes — anything the sheet describes rather than calculates with. **Numeric** data is a number the sheet can calculate with: a quantity, a price, a mark. The difference is behaviour, not looks: a number typed as text (for example inside a label like 'Room 12') just sits there, while a true number can be added, averaged and compared. Dates and times count as numbers underneath, which is why the sheet can subtract one date from another and answer in days.

[Picture — three cells in a row showing the same 12 — one labelled text, one number, one formula — with the formula bar showing a different content for each]

## Sub lesson 2 — Formulas — the calculating kind

A **formula** is an entry that tells the sheet to calculate: it begins with the equals sign and names cells and operations, and the cell shows the formula's **result**, not the formula itself. Change a number the formula uses and the result changes with it — that is the whole point of a spreadsheet. Reading a sheet means knowing which kind each cell holds: a result that looks like an ordinary number may be a formula's answer that will move when its inputs move, while a typed number stays until someone retypes it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: A spreadsheet cell holds one of three kinds of data — text, a number, or a formula — and the program treats each kind differently, so the kind is decided by how the entry begins and what the cell must do. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
