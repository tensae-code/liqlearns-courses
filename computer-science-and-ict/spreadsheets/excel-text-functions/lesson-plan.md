## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Finding and cutting text** — **LEFT(text, n)** takes the first n characters — the code off the front of a string; **FIND(what, text)** reports where a piece sits inside a text (its position, or an error if absent) — together they dissect strings by rule: find the separator, cut at it. **SUBSTITUTE(text, old, new)** swaps every occurrence of one piece for another — the misspelt district renamed in a thousand rows at a keystroke. These three are the dissectors: position, cut, replace.
2. **Cleaning and casing text** — **TRIM** removes a text's extra spaces — the leading, trailing and doubled spaces that make 'Amman' and 'Amman ' count as two cities. **UPPER** and **LOWER** force a text wholly into capital or small letters, so codes and names written by many hands compare equal. Jordan's Grade 10 set is a cleaner's kit, taught inside the data-analysis lesson because analysis dies of dirty text: the functions here are how the cleaning stage of the analysis process is actually done in Excel, cell by formula, auditable by anyone.
