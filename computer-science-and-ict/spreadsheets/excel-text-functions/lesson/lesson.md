# Excel Text Functions

## Do it first

Data arrives as messy text: names in mixed case, codes buried in strings, spaces where no space should be. This lesson learns the functions that clean text without retyping it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Finding and cutting text

**LEFT(text, n)** takes the first n characters — the code off the front of a string; **FIND(what, text)** reports where a piece sits inside a text (its position, or an error if absent) — together they dissect strings by rule: find the separator, cut at it. **SUBSTITUTE(text, old, new)** swaps every occurrence of one piece for another — the misspelt district renamed in a thousand rows at a keystroke. These three are the dissectors: position, cut, replace.

In your mind's eye: finding and cutting text.

## Sub lesson 2 — Cleaning and casing text

**TRIM** removes a text's extra spaces — the leading, trailing and doubled spaces that make 'Amman' and 'Amman ' count as two cities. **UPPER** and **LOWER** force a text wholly into capital or small letters, so codes and names written by many hands compare equal. Jordan's Grade 10 set is a cleaner's kit, taught inside the data-analysis lesson because analysis dies of dirty text: the functions here are how the cleaning stage of the analysis process is actually done in Excel, cell by formula, auditable by anyone.

In your mind's eye: cleaning and casing text.
