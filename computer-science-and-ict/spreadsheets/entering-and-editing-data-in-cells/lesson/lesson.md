# Entering and Editing Data in Cells

## Do it first

A sheet with two hundred entries has one wrong digit. Retyping the whole entry risks a new mistake; in this lesson you learn to enter data cleanly and to edit a cell in place — and to save the workbook so the work stays done.

## Sub lesson 1 — Entering data

To **enter** data: select the cell, type the text, number or formula, and confirm — with Enter, which moves the selection down, or with another movement key, which confirms and moves that way. While you type, the entry is not yet in the cell: it can be cancelled, and it shows in the **formula bar** as you work. Typing into an occupied cell and confirming **replaces** its old content completely — entry is always whole-cell, never an addition to what was there.

[Picture — a selected cell with typed numbers appearing in it and in the formula bar above the sheet]

## Sub lesson 2 — Editing what is already there

**Editing** changes an existing entry without retyping all of it: double-click the cell (or use the formula bar) and the entry opens for correction — move the cursor inside it, delete or insert characters, and confirm. Editing is the right tool for a long formula with one wrong reference, or a label with one wrong letter. The section closes with the workbook itself: a finished sheet is kept by **saving the workbook**, so the entered and edited data survives the program closing — unsaved work exists only while the program stays open.

[Picture — a long entry opened for editing with the cursor blinking in its middle, one letter being corrected]

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Entering data means selecting a cell, typing, and confirming the entry; editing means changing what a cell already holds — by retyping it, or by correcting it in place in the cell or the formula bar — without disturbing its neighbours. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
