# Moving Around a Workbook

## Do it first

A workbook is bigger than its window — thousands of rows below, columns running off to the right, and more sheets behind the tabs. Fiji's Years 7–8 book teaches moving around it as its own step, before any creating. This lesson is that map of movement.

## Sub lesson 1 — Moving within a sheet

The **active cell** — the one with the thick border — is where you are. The **arrow keys** move it one cell at a time; **Page Down and Page Up** move it a screen at a time; **Ctrl+Home** returns it to **A1**, the sheet's start; and the **Name Box**, beside the formula bar, moves you exactly: type a reference such as **M50** and Enter, and you are there. The **scroll bars** change what you *see* without changing where you *are* — scrolling looks, the Name Box and keys travel.

In your mind's eye: moving within a sheet.

## Sub lesson 2 — Moving between sheets

A workbook's sheets sit behind the **sheet tabs** at the bottom of the window. Clicking a tab brings that sheet to the front; the tab arrows scroll the row of tabs when there are too many to show. Moving around a workbook is the pair of skills together — tabs between sheets, keys and the Name Box inside a sheet — and Fiji teaches it before creating a spreadsheet because a learner who cannot travel cannot check, correct or find anything in a big workbook.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: The cell with the thick border — the cell you are working in. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: Move the active cell one cell at a time. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: Behind the sheet tabs at the bottom of the window. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: Click its sheet tab. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
