# Video script — Moving Around a Workbook

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

The **active cell** — the one with the thick border — is where you are. The **arrow keys** move it one cell at a time; **Page Down and Page Up** move it a screen at a time; **Ctrl+Home** returns it to **A1**, the sheet's start; and the **Name Box**, beside the formula bar, moves you exactly: type a reference such as **M50** and Enter, and you are there. The **scroll bars** change what you *see* without changing where you *are* — scrolling looks, the Name Box and keys travel.
A workbook's sheets sit behind the **sheet tabs** at the bottom of the window. Clicking a tab brings that sheet to the front; the tab arrows scroll the row of tabs when there are too many to show. Moving around a workbook is the pair of skills together — tabs between sheets, keys and the Name Box inside a sheet — and Fiji teaches it before creating a spreadsheet because a learner who cannot travel cannot check, correct or find anything in a big workbook.
