# Spreadsheet formulas — starting with =

## Do it first

Two cells hold numbers: B2 holds 9, B3 holds 4. You want a third cell to show what is left when 4 is taken from 9 — and to keep showing the right answer if the numbers change. Typing "5" would freeze it. A formula keeps it alive. Build one now, in the order given.

## What a formula is

A **formula** is an expression that **operates on values in a range of cells or a cell**. The first example to hold in mind: a formula can **add the values of cells B2 and B3**. Whatever the operator, every formula is built the same way.

## Building a formula — the six steps, in order

1. **Select a cell** — the cell where the formula will stand.
2. **Type the equal sign (=)** in the cell. Formulas always begin with =.
3. **Select a cell, or type its address**, in the selected cell — the first value's home (for this walk-through, B2).
4. **Enter the operator** — in this walk-through, minus (−).
5. **Select the next cell, or type its address** — the second value's home (B3).
6. **Press Enter** — and the **result** of the calculation appears in the cell with the formula.

With B2 holding 9 and B3 holding 4, the cell shows **5**. Change B2 to 10 and the same cell answers 6 — the formula, not the frozen number, is what stands there.

> Tip — from us, not from the books: after building a formula, change one of the cells it refers to and watch the answer change. That one experiment proves the cell holds a formula, not a typed result.

## Do It Yourself

1. Build the formula =B2−B3 in a real spreadsheet, with your own numbers in B2 and B3; check the result by hand.
2. Change B3 and say the new result before you look.
3. Write the six steps from memory, numbered.
