# About the Computer Science and ICT course

**The first page (what learners are told):** everything in this
course comes from government textbooks — the first part is
built from Ethiopia's own federal Information Technology
student textbooks and teacher guides for Grades 7–12, read in
full, page by page. The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a
tip in a box is from us, the people making LiqLearns. Tips are
ours; the lessons are the books'.

## Where the course comes from

Computer Science and ICT is one world course: the thing itself
— data, the machine, the network — is the same everywhere, and
each country's way of teaching it shows inside as examples. The
first way stocked is Ethiopia's: ideas first (what ICT, IT and
a computer are), then the machine itself, then one hands-on
skill at a time, and programming saved for when the learner can
drive the machine. Every Ethiopian unit opens with a
brainstorming activity that links to what pupils already know
before any definition is given, and closes with activities, a
summary and a multi-part review; that rhythm is kept as
teaching in this course.

## The books (Ethiopia, Information Technology)

Book entries are named in full in each lesson's
`lesson/sources.md`. The set in hand for this build — all
twelve books COMPLETE, read in full in the 2026-11-11 Ethiopia
Wave 3 read (placements at
`liqlearns-courses-work/ethiopia-2026-11-11/information-technology/`):

- Student textbooks Grades 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12 — complete.
- Teacher guides Grades 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12 — complete, and
  edition-matched companions to their student books (same
  author teams). Teacher-guide material is stocked as evidence,
  labelled in each lesson's sources, and never counted as
  pupil-taught.
- Two Grade 7 teacher-guide items are course-level rather than
  lesson-level, and are recorded here: the guide's 72-period
  budget for the Grade 7 year, and its external reference shelf
  for the course. They are named in the band A2 build log and
  stand as teacher-guide evidence for the course as a whole.

## Honesty notes

- The Grade 8 federal teacher guide is **not** an issue for
  this course (the guides here are the IT guides, all matched);
  the unmatched-guide warning of this wave belongs to General
  Science and is recorded there.
- The books' own wording is kept where it is Ethiopia's way —
  for example the books expand LOGO as "Logic Oriented Graphics
  Oriented", which is not the standard expansion; it is
  preserved and flagged in the programming lessons' sources.
- Book errors (typesetting faults, misnumbered figures, wrong
  worked examples) are taught corrected, and each is named in
  the honesty paragraph of the sources of the lesson it touches.
- IT dates faster than any other school subject. This course
  teaches exactly what the books teach — the software versions,
  capacities and services the books name — and adds nothing
  newer except inside marked Tip boxes.
