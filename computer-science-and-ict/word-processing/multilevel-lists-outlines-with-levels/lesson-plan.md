# Lesson plan — Multilevel lists — outlines with levels

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Word Processing · **Lesson:** Multilevel lists — outlines with levels
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 5 — Word Processing in the Computer Science and ICT ladder (TITLES.md); stocked from Ethiopia's Grade 9 book, Unit 3 — Application Software. The ladder is the recommended order, never a forced one.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** any bulleted or numbered list becomes multilevel with the Tab key — Tab pushes a line a level in, Shift+Tab pulls it back — so a flat list becomes an outline with levels inside levels.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on both list lessons — bullets and numbering are its ground; this is the deepest list skill in the title.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. ## If the learner is stuck
- Tab typed spaces instead of changing level → the cursor must be on a list line; in a list, Tab at the line's start changes its level.
- Line went too deep → Shift+Tab pulls it back one level per press.
- Lost count of levels → read the line's indent against its neighbours: deeper in means deeper level.
