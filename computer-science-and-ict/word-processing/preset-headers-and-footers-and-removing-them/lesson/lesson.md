# Preset headers and footers — and removing them

## Do it first

Go Insert → Header and stop before clicking anything. A gallery of ready-made designs is waiting. This lesson chooses one, types over its placeholders, and then — because every addition needs its opposite — removes a header cleanly.

## Sub lesson 1 — Choosing a preset

Word offers **ready-made designs**: **Insert → Header or Footer, choose a preset**. Many presets contain **Content Control placeholder fields** — **title, author, date, page number** — which are **clicked and typed over**: the design's shape stays, your words replace its standing words.

[Picture 13348 — the preset header gallery]

## Sub lesson 2 — Editing and removing

A closed header or footer is **locked but still visible** — **double-click it to unlock and edit**. Removal is just as direct: **Header command → Remove Header** — and likewise **Footer → Remove Footer**. Nothing is deleted by hunting through pages: the command takes the header off every page at once.

## Sub lesson 3 — The closing practical — the whole Word block at once

The Word block closes with one exercise that uses it all. The specification: **centre the titles**; give the lists **Roman numerals**; set **1.5 line spacing**; insert **a page break**; **justify** the body; set **Arial 12 throughout**; add an **Arabic page number**; a **title header**; and a footer reading **"Prepared by Grade 9 Students"** — the finished document saved into a **new Word Project folder in Documents**. Work it item by item against the list; every item is a skill this title has taught.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion how a preset is chosen and filled, how a header is edited and removed, and read the closing practical's specification aloud from memory as far as you can.

## Do It Yourself

1. Choose a preset header and type over every placeholder field it carries.
2. Remove that header with Remove Header, then put a preset footer in and remove it too.
3. Do the closing practical to its full specification and save it in a new Word Project folder.
