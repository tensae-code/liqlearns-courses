# Lesson plan — Page Setup — Margins and Orientation

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Word Processing · **Lesson:** Page Setup — Margins and Orientation
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Uganda (2026-11-14) — in the title Word Processing on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Uganda's *Information Communication Technology Textbook — Senior One* (NCDC Prototype, Lower Secondary Curriculum), because no existing lesson teaches this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** before words are judged, the page itself is set: its orientation — tall portrait or wide landscape — its margins, the blank borders where no text goes, and, when the work calls for it, its text laid in columns like a newspaper.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on opening a word processor and typing in a document; orientation, margins and columns are built inside the lesson from the page itself, one setting at a time.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Changing page orientation — portrait and landscape** — The page's two shapes, what each fits, and setting the shape early because the text reflows with the page.
2. **Setting margins — the blank borders of the page** — Top, bottom, left and right margins, what wider and narrower margins do to the text, and setting each one deliberately.
3. **Using columns in text** — Splitting text into newspaper-style columns, the down-then-across reading order, and when columns suit the work.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Word Processing in Word Processing. Recommended, never forced — a learner may land on this lesson directly, and it stands alone.
- Uganda's own way is kept: in Uganda's Senior One prototype every topic is an "Activity" learners do, under the revised Lower Secondary Curriculum — this lesson is taught as a doing activity, not as notes to copy.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Change a page's orientation between portrait and landscape, and say which shape fits a given piece of work.
2. Set a page's margins — top, bottom, left and right — and say what wider and narrower margins do to the text.
3. Put text into columns and read it in the right order — down one column, then the top of the next — and say when columns suit a document.

## What you need
A computer with a word processor and a document to practise on — ideally one holding a wide table.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Open a document. It sits on an invisible page — and that page was decided before you typed a word: tall or wide, with wide or narrow blank edges. A wide table refuses to fit; a letter swims in empty space. This lesson takes charge of the page itself. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of the same page shown twice in a word processor — tall portrait on the left and wide landscape on the right — with a wide table fitting only on the wide page — the student studies the picture and says, for each part of it, which part of the lesson it shows.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of a page with its four margins marked as a frame — top, bottom, left and right blank borders — and the text sitting inside the frame — the student studies the picture and says, for each part of it, which part of the lesson it shows.

## If the learner is stuck
A wide table will not fit → the page is portrait; turn it landscape instead of shrinking the table. Text crowded at the edge → margins are too narrow; widen them. Reading columns across the page → columns are read down first, then across to the next column's top.
