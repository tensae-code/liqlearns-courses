# Printing a Document and Booklets

## Do it first

The booklet project ends where it aimed from the first lesson: on paper, folded, in readers' hands. Mongolia's printing lesson has two halves — the settings of any print run, and the special imposition that turns loose sheets into a booklet. The existing Printing a Document lesson teaches ordinary printing; this lesson carries the booklet half that no other lesson teaches.

## Sub lesson 1 — Print settings — governing the run

Printing is ordered, not merely clicked: the **printer** is chosen, the **pages** (all, or the range the run needs), the **copies**, and the paper agreement — the document's page setup must match the sheets actually loaded, or the run is clipped before it begins. The booklet project prints on the settings its planning promised: nothing about the run is decided at the printer, because everything about it was decided at the plan.

## Sub lesson 2 — Booklet printing — pages imposed for the fold

A **booklet** is printed by **imposition**: the pages are not sent in reading order 1, 2, 3, 4, but arranged on the sheets so that when the printed sheets are **folded** together, the pages fall into reading order — the first and last pages sharing a sheet's face, and so on inward. The word processor's booklet setting does the arranging; the printer prints the sheets, often on both sides; hands fold and fix them. Mongolia's pupils hold the proof in their hands: a document planned as a book, printed as a book — the whole Grade 7 Word unit in one folded object.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: Printer, pages, copies and the paper agreement (any three). Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: The sheets actually loaded in the printer. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: At the plan, not at the printer. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: Arranging pages on sheets so the folded sheets read in order. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
