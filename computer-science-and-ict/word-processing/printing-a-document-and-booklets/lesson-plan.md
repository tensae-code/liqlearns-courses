## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Print settings — governing the run** — Printing is ordered, not merely clicked: the **printer** is chosen, the **pages** (all, or the range the run needs), the **copies**, and the paper agreement — the document's page setup must match the sheets actually loaded, or the run is clipped before it begins. The booklet project prints on the settings its planning promised: nothing about the run is decided at the printer, because everything about it was decided at the plan.
2. **Booklet printing — pages imposed for the fold** — A **booklet** is printed by **imposition**: the pages are not sent in reading order 1, 2, 3, 4, but arranged on the sheets so that when the printed sheets are **folded** together, the pages fall into reading order — the first and last pages sharing a sheet's face, and so on inward. The word processor's booklet setting does the arranging; the printer prints the sheets, often on both sides; hands fold and fix them. Mongolia's pupils hold the proof in their hands: a document planned as a book, printed as a book — the whole Grade 7 Word unit in one folded object.
