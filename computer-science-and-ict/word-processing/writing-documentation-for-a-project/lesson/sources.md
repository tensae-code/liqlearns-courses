Many books exist in parallel language editions (Kazakh / Russian / Uzbek / Uyghur / English) of the same textbook: each distinct book is taught once, and every distinct edition is named and cited in the book entry. Kazakhstan's own ways are kept: one textbook line in up to five parallel language editions, the okulyk.kz digital library as the books' home, Scratch from Grade 1 and robotics strands (LEGO WeDo 2.0 and Mindstorms EV3) from Grade 1–2, and every keyword given in Kazakh–Russian–English in the early grades. Kazakhstan's own way (Mukhametzhanova G6 Informatika, 2018 edition): the 2018 Atamura Grade 6 course is built around making a real computer game across two whole parts — idea, scenario, stage and characters, implementation, sound accompaniment, a start page and improvements — with vector graphics in Inkscape before it and full Word documentation after it: footnotes, hyperlinks, an automatic contents and a formal game design document that teaches copyright and citing authors.

Kazakhstan's own way (Salgaraeva & Ilyasova G6 Informatika): the Arman-PV Grade 6 book runs the full 2018 programme in numbered paragraphs with a test after every part: systems and networks, presenting information (transmission, encryption, binary coding of text), vector graphics with a "Zhailau" drawing practicum, then the two-part game build in the Scratch environment, and a final Word part that adds references, copyright and plagiarism, a referat mini-project and the game document.

Kazakhstan's own way (Adekenova & Pryanichnikova G9 Informatics, NIS): the NIS Grade 9 course is built around databases and object-oriented programming: computer architecture with logic circuits, coding raster images and sound, single-table then multi-table databases in Access (forms, queries, reports), five lessons introducing OOP, nine lessons developing applications in an OOP environment (C#), and a thirteen-lesson capstone — choosing a problem, designing the interface, programming, making a video instruction, testing, documenting and evaluating the project.

Kazakhstan's own way (Ataeva & Vyshkova G8 Informatics, NIS): the NIS Grade 8 course is the most distinctive of the wave: vector graphics flows into geographic information systems (planning an excursion, making maps, visualising data in GIS), spreadsheets are used to solve logical problems, loops are taught through mathematics, physics, business and multimedia problems, and the year ends with a full project cycle — six stages, presentation, documentation, defence — plus a mini-project on artificial intelligence.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukametzhanova G6 «Информатика»): the whole second half of Grade 6 is one long game-development arc — idea, scenario, drawing scenes and characters (vector editor), realising the scenario, sound, a title screen, improving the project — and then documenting the game properly with footnotes, hyperlinks and an automatically built table of contents.

Kazakhstan's own way (Salgaraeva G6 «Информатика»): the Arman-PV Grade 6 book is a game-making year wrapped in document skills: ergonomics and wireless networks first, then binary coding and encryption, vector graphics (drawing a «Жайлау» summer pasture), a Scratch game designed, built and given a sound track and title screen — and the year ends with footnotes, hyperlinks, copyright and plagiarism, a table of contents and the written documentation for the game.

Kazakhstan's own way (Atayeva/Vyushkova/Zhurbenko G8 «Информатика» NIS): the NIS Grade 8 book is the widest of the set: a whole section on vector graphics and geographic information systems (planning an excursion, making maps, visualising data in GIS), octal and hexadecimal coding, loops applied separately to mathematics, physics, business and animation, strings and subroutines, and a nine-step project method ending in a mini-project on artificial intelligence.

Kazakhstan's own way (Pryanichnikova team G9 «Информатика» NIS): the NIS Grade 9 book is a step up in level: logic expressions, truth tables and logic circuits inside "computer architecture"; sound encoding beside raster encoding; single-table then multi-table databases in Access with forms, queries and reports; and a full object-oriented programming course in C# — components of OOP, multimedia applications, and a software-solution project with a video instruction and a slide show.
