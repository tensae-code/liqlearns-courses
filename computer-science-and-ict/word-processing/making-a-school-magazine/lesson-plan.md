**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Word Processing · **Lesson:** Making a School Magazine

**The one thing this lesson teaches:** making a school magazine: the class as a publishing house — writing reports, crediting sources, and producing the magazine itself, with intellectual property respected in practice (Bangladesh Grade 6 Digital Technology, Learning Experience 3, Sessions 1–5).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Writing for the magazine — reports and credit** — The magazine begins as **writing**: each team **reports** — observes, interviews, drafts, revises — on the school's own life. And because the same experience teaches intellectual property (the magazine is where the IP sessions live), every borrowed thing is handled by the law just learned: photographs and texts of others used with **permission and credit**, the sources named where they are used. A school magazine that credits honestly teaches copyright better than a chapter of rules — it is copyright, practised with the whole school watching.
2. **Producing the magazine — the word processor at work** — Then **production**: the reports are typed and formatted in the word processor — headings, columns of text, pictures placed and captioned, the cover designed, pages ordered into a contents list — and the magazine is printed or shared digitally for its real readers. The roles are a publisher's: writers, editors who check facts and credit, designers who keep the pages consistent, and a final reader who hunts errors in the proof. The finished magazine on the school shelf is the experience's evidence: skills, law and teamwork, bound together.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
