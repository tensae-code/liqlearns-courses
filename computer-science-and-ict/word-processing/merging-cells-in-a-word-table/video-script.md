# Video script — Merging Cells in a Word Table

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

**Merging** joins **two or more selected cells into one cell**. The joined cell spans the space of all its parts: a heading cell merged across the top of a table holds the table's title in one wide cell; cells merged down a column hold one entry for several rows. The contents of the merged cells gather into the single new cell. Merging is the table's way of breaking its own grid exactly where the information breaks it — where one thing belongs to many rows or columns at once.
The order in Word of the book's era is: **select the cells** to join (they must sit together, in a block), then give the **Merge Cells** command (Table menu / right-click), and the block becomes one cell. The reverse exists and must be known with it: **Split Cells** divides a cell back into the rows and columns named. Laos teaches merge as a click-by-click procedure because its errors are visible and fixable — a wrong merge is undone by splitting and re-merging, never by deleting the table and starting the document again.
