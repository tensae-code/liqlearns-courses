# Lesson plan — Comments and Reviewing a Document

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Word Processing · **Lesson:** Comments and Reviewing a Document
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Cuba (2026-11-14) — in the title Word Processing on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Cuba's Mi Mundo Digital / Informática student books and teacher-side books (Ministerio de Educación / Editorial Pueblo y Educación), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A **comment** is a note attached to a place in a document — a question, a correction, a suggestion — written beside the text without changing the text itself, so a document can be **reviewed** before it is finished.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Comments — notes pinned to the text** — A **comment** is pinned to a **word, sentence or place** in the document and shown **beside the text** — in the margin or a note area — with its author's name. The text itself is **not changed** by a comment: the note asks, doubts or proposes, and the writer still decides. Comments are how a second reader — a classmate, a teacher — works on a document without overwriting its author.
2. **Reviewing — reading a document through comments** — **Reviewing** is the whole job comments serve: read the document as its reader will, **comment wherever** it is unclear, wrong or weak, and hand it back; the writer then **answers each comment** — changing the text where the note is right, replying where it is not — until no open comment remains. A reviewed document is a finished document: Cuba places commenting right before printing in the Grade 7 chapter, because review is the last work before the page leaves the screen.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. A note attached to a place in a document.
2. Beside the text, in the margin or a note area.
3. Reading a document as its reader will and commenting wherever it is unclear, wrong or weak.
4. Answers it — changing the text or replying.
