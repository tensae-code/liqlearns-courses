# Comments and Reviewing a Document

## Do it first

Cuba's Grade 7 chapter teaches commenting the text — *Comentando el texto* — as a word-processor skill of its own: the document is finished not when it is written, but when it has been read, questioned and improved.

## Sub lesson 1 — Comments — notes pinned to the text

A **comment** is pinned to a **word, sentence or place** in the document and shown **beside the text** — in the margin or a note area — with its author's name. The text itself is **not changed** by a comment: the note asks, doubts or proposes, and the writer still decides. Comments are how a second reader — a classmate, a teacher — works on a document without overwriting its author.

## Sub lesson 2 — Reviewing — reading a document through comments

**Reviewing** is the whole job comments serve: read the document as its reader will, **comment wherever** it is unclear, wrong or weak, and hand it back; the writer then **answers each comment** — changing the text where the note is right, replying where it is not — until no open comment remains. A reviewed document is a finished document: Cuba places commenting right before printing in the Grade 7 chapter, because review is the last work before the page leaves the screen.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: A note attached to a place in a document. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: Beside the text, in the margin or a note area. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: Reading a document as its reader will and commenting wherever it is unclear, wrong or weak. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: Answers it — changing the text or replying. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
