# Video script — Finding Text with Search in Word

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

**Find** (the book's **Search**) is Word's way of looking through the whole document for you.
Open it from the Edit menu (or with **Ctrl+F**), type the **search text** — the word or phrase you want — and Word jumps to the first place it appears, showing it **selected** in the text.
The point is speed with certainty: a person skimming can miss the one occurrence that matters; Word misses none, because it compares every character in the document against your search text.
One word may appear many times, so **Find Next** takes you to each further occurrence in turn, until Word reports it has finished searching the document — that report is how you know you have seen them all.
Finding is usually the first half of fixing: with **Replace**, Word can change each found occurrence (or all of them at once with Replace All) into corrected text — find the wrong spelling, replace it with the right one, everywhere, in one pass.
The care rule: check a few replacements with your own eyes before trusting Replace All, because Word replaces exactly what you typed, including anywhere you did not mean.
