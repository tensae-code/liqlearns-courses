# Finding Text with Search in Word

## Do it first

Somewhere in a twenty-page document one name is spelled wrong — and reading every page would take an hour. In this lesson you learn to make Word find text for you.

## Sub lesson 1 — Find — letting Word do the reading

**Find** (the book's **Search**) is Word's way of looking through the whole document for you. Open it from the Edit menu (or with **Ctrl+F**), type the **search text** — the word or phrase you want — and Word jumps to the first place it appears, showing it **selected** in the text. The point is speed with certainty: a person skimming can miss the one occurrence that matters; Word misses none, because it compares every character in the document against your search text.

[Picture — Word's Find dialog — the search text typed in, and the found word selected in the document behind it]

## Sub lesson 2 — Find Next, and finding to fix

One word may appear many times, so **Find Next** takes you to each further occurrence in turn, until Word reports it has finished searching the document — that report is how you know you have seen them all. Finding is usually the first half of fixing: with **Replace**, Word can change each found occurrence (or all of them at once with Replace All) into corrected text — find the wrong spelling, replace it with the right one, everywhere, in one pass. The care rule: check a few replacements with your own eyes before trusting Replace All, because Word replaces exactly what you typed, including anywhere you did not mean.

[Picture — the Find and Replace dialog — the wrong spelling being replaced by the right one]

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Word's Search (Find) finds any word or phrase in a document in seconds: you type what you are looking for, and Word takes you to each place it appears, however long the document is. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
