Provenance scope note: the placements stocked here are from Kazakhstan's okulyk.kz (OKULYK) textbooks, recommended by the Ministry of Education and Science of the Republic of Kazakhstan — the Digital Literacy and Informatika lines of the Almatykitap, Arman-PV and Atamura teams and the Nazarbayev Intellectual Schools (NIS) books, all student books except the two Kadirkulov teacher guides named in the entries below, whose placements are stocked exactly as filed. Many books exist in parallel language editions (Kazakh / Russian / Uzbek / Uyghur / English) of the same textbook: each distinct book is taught once, and every distinct edition is named and cited in the book entry. Kazakhstan's own ways are kept: one textbook line in up to five parallel language editions, the okulyk.kz digital library as the books' home, Scratch from Grade 1 and robotics strands (LEGO WeDo 2.0 and Mindstorms EV3) from Grade 1–2, and every keyword given in Kazakh–Russian–English in the early grades. Media slots in lesson/lesson.md are described slots only ([Picture — …]); no picture numbers are invented.


Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G6 Informatika): Grade 6 by the Almatykitap team drops the Scratch-game strand of the 2018 books: after systems/networks (including a lesson on Internet addiction) it teaches 3D modelling and 3D printing, a first Python course (IDE, syntax, data types, input/output, linear programs), and academic Word work — footnotes, hyperlinks, an automatic table of contents, copyright and plagiarism, and how to write a referat.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukhametzhanova G6 Informatika, 2018 edition): the 2018 Atamura Grade 6 course is built around making a real computer game across two whole parts — idea, scenario, stage and characters, implementation, sound accompaniment, a start page and improvements — with vector graphics in Inkscape before it and full Word documentation after it: footnotes, hyperlinks, an automatic contents and a formal game design document that teaches copyright and citing authors.

Kazakhstan's own way (Salgaraeva & Ilyasova G6 Informatika): the Arman-PV Grade 6 book runs the full 2018 programme in numbered paragraphs with a test after every part: systems and networks, presenting information (transmission, encryption, binary coding of text), vector graphics with a "Zhailau" drawing practicum, then the two-part game build in the Scratch environment, and a final Word part that adds references, copyright and plagiarism, a referat mini-project and the game document.

Kazakhstan's own way (Salgaraeva & Kopeeva G6 Informatika): the Arman-PV Grade 6 book follows the newer programme (systems and networks, 3D printing, Python, academic Word work) in 30 paragraphs with a test after each section; the 3D editor is SketchUp (the book's resource list points to gsketchup.ru and python.org), the history of computing is split into two paragraphs, and almost every topic is doubled with a "creating a project" paragraph.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukhametzhanova G6 Informatika, 2020 edition): the 2020 Atamura Grade 6 book drops the game project of its 2018 predecessor and follows the newer programme in four parts; its 3D strand is taught in Paint 3D (with Remix 3D models, the Mixed Reality Viewer and 3D-printer safety rules), and the Python part works in IDLE with type conversion (int/float) given its own space.

Kazakhstan's own way (Vyushkova/Zhurbenko/Orazbekov G7 «Информатика» (NIS student book, Russian edition)): the NIS Grade 7 book is organised around data: measuring and representing information (including the von Neumann architecture), business documents, spreadsheets that grow into databases (error checking, sorting/filtering, protecting data from change), raster graphics and logo design, a whole modelling section (modelling games, school life and social problems, forecasting from data), networks and information systems, and linear-structure programming that ends with building your own calculator.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukametzhanova G6 «Информатика»): the whole second half of Grade 6 is one long game-development arc — idea, scenario, drawing scenes and characters (vector editor), realising the scenario, sound, a title screen, improving the project — and then documenting the game properly with footnotes, hyperlinks and an automatically built table of contents.

Kazakhstan's own way (Salgaraeva G6 «Информатика»): the Arman-PV Grade 6 book is a game-making year wrapped in document skills: ergonomics and wireless networks first, then binary coding and encryption, vector graphics (drawing a «Жайлау» summer pasture), a Scratch game designed, built and given a sound track and title screen — and the year ends with footnotes, hyperlinks, copyright and plagiarism, a table of contents and the written documentation for the game.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G6 «Информатика»): the Almatykitap Grade 6 book follows the same state programme as the Arman-PV book (Book 24) — ergonomics, wireless networks, footnotes and hyperlinks, copyright, contents, report — but swaps vector graphics for a whole 3D-printing section in a SketchUp-style editor (cone, cylinder and sphere models, print setup) and swaps the Scratch game for a first Python course: IDE, alphabet and syntax, data types, arithmetic expressions, number input and output, linear algorithms.

Kazakhstan's own way (Salgaraeva/Kopeeva G6 «Информатика»): this Arman-PV Grade 6 book (a third distinct Grade 6 book in this half) opens with an explicit discussion of internet addiction, and its text-document section treats hyperlinks, copyright and plagiarism, the table of contents and the school report (реферат) as project work — the document-skills tail of the Grade 6 programme.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukametzhanova G6 2020 «Информатика»): the 2020 Atamura Grade 6 revision drops the spreadsheet tail of its 2018 edition and follows the new programme instead — ergonomics, computer history, wireless networks, 3D printing, Python and a text-document section (instructions and links, hyperlinks, table of contents, report) — in four compact sections.
