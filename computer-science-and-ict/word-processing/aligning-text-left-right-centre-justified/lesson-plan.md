# Lesson plan — Aligning text — left, right, centre, justified

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Word Processing · **Lesson:** Aligning text — left, right, centre, justified
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 5 — Word Processing in the Computer Science and ICT ladder (TITLES.md); stocked from Ethiopia's Grade 9 book, Unit 3 — Application Software. The ladder is the recommended order, never a forced one.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** alignment is the orientation of a paragraph's edges — left, right, centre or justified — changed by putting the cursor anywhere in the paragraph and clicking one button (or its shortcut).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Paragraph group map — alignment is the group's second skill, practised here on the dam document each way in turn.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Align a paragraph left, right, centre and justified, by button or shortcut.
2. Describe each kind's edges: left even with jagged right; right mirrored; centre equal both sides; justified spread to both margins.
3. Say which kind suits titles and headings.

## What you need

## If the learner is stuck
- Only the clicked line changed → alignment works on the paragraph the cursor stands in; the whole paragraph is the unit.
- Justified text looks stretched → the spaces between words are adjusted to reach both margins; that stretch is the justified look.
