# Aligning text — left, right, centre, justified

## Do it first

Open the dam document. Click anywhere inside one paragraph. Now press Ctrl+L, then Ctrl+E, then Ctrl+R, then Ctrl+J, watching the paragraph's edges each time. Four shapes from four presses — this lesson names what you just did.

## Left and right

**Alignment** is the orientation of a paragraph's edges. You change it by putting the cursor **anywhere in the paragraph** and clicking one button — the whole paragraph obeys. **Left alignment (Ctrl+L)** lines text evenly at the **left margin**, leaving a **jagged right edge** — the commonest kind. **Right alignment (Ctrl+R)** is its mirror: the **last character of each line** lines up at the right margin.

## Centre and justified

**Centre alignment (Ctrl+E)** makes **equal spaces to both margins** and suits **titles and headings** rather than long paragraphs. **Justified alignment (Ctrl+J)** spreads each line to **both margins** by adjusting the **spaces between words**, removing the jagged look. Practise on the dam document: re-align it each way in turn and watch the edges.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the four alignments, their shortcuts, and which edge each one lines up.

## Do It Yourself

1. Re-align the dam document all four ways, naming each as you press it.
2. Centre a title over a left-aligned paragraph and say why centre suits it.
3. Justify a paragraph and point to where the extra space went.
