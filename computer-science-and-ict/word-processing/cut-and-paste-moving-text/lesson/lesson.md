# Cut and paste — moving text

## Do it first

A paragraph about Addis Ababa stands at the top of your document — but it belongs at the bottom. You do not want two copies; you want it MOVED. Copy will not do that. Cut will.

## Sub lesson 1 — Cutting the text

In order:

1. **Select** the text to move.
2. **Right-click** and choose **Cut** — or, on the **Clipboard group of the Home tab**, click **Cut**.

The text vanishes from its place. It is not lost: it waits on the **clipboard**, and cut and paste can move the same portion of text through the document for as long as the text remains on the clipboard.

## Sub lesson 2 — Pasting it in the new place

In order:

1. **Place the insertion point** where the text is to be inserted.
2. On the **Home tab's Clipboard group**, click **Paste** — or **right-click** and choose **Paste**.

The paragraph now stands in its new place — and only there. That is the whole difference: **copy** leaves the original standing and adds a twin; **cut** takes the text away and puts it down somewhere else.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the cut steps and the paste steps in order, and the one difference between cut and copy.

## Do It Yourself

1. Type the paragraph about Addis Ababa. Cut it and paste it at the end of the document.
2. Cut one sentence from the middle and paste it at the start.
3. Write, from memory, the two ways to give the Cut command.
