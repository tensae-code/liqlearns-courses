# Lesson plan — Making a numbered list

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Word Processing · **Lesson:** Making a numbered list
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 5 — Word Processing in the Computer Science and ICT ladder (TITLES.md); stocked from Ethiopia's Grade 9 book, Unit 3 — Application Software. The ladder is the recommended order, never a forced one.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the same text becomes a numbered list by choosing a numbering style — numbers, letters or Roman numerals — from the Numbering command, and numbering can be restarted where a list must begin again.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the bulleted-list lesson — numbering is its twin: same steps, counted marks instead of dots.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or in steps — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Making a bulleted list.  Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Turn text into a numbered list with numbers, letters or Roman numerals.
2. Preview a numbering style live before choosing it.
3. Restart numbering by right-clicking the list — for numeric and alphabetical lists alike.

## What you need
A computer with Word and a list from the bulleted-list lesson.

## If the learner is stuck
- List kept its bullets → choose a style from the Numbering command, not the Bullets command; the style replaces the marks.
- Second list continues the first list's count → right-click it and choose the restart option.
