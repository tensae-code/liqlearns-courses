# Capital Letters — Shift and Caps Lock — and Typing the Hamza

## Do it first

One key, held or locked, changes what every letter key says. In this lesson Shift and Caps Lock do their two jobs — in English and in Arabic. (teacher guide — evidence; PARTIAL — Term 1 only)

## Sub lesson 1 — Capitals in English — Shift for one, Caps Lock for many

Hold **Shift** while pressing a letter and that one letter comes out **capital** — for the **first letter of a sentence**, and for **names and countries**, which always begin with a capital in English. Press **Caps Lock** and every letter is capital until you press it again — right for a whole word or line of capitals, wrong for ordinary sentences.

## Sub lesson 2 — Shift in Arabic — the hamza and punctuation

In Arabic typing, **Shift** reaches the forms printed above the keys — above all the **hamza** form **أ** (alif with hamza above), typed with Shift where the plain key gives the bare letter. Shift likewise types the **full stop and the comma** where they sit as upper forms — the punctuation that ends and divides Arabic sentences.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Shift makes one capital; Caps Lock makes them all: English needs capitals for names, countries and the first letter of a sentence — and in Arabic the same Shift key types the hamza (أ) and reaches the full stop and comma. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: Makes that one letter capital. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: Makes every letter capital until it is pressed again. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: The forms printed above the keys. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: The full stop and the comma. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
