# Selecting Text and Moving Through It with Arrow Keys

## Do it first

Before text can be copied, formatted or deleted, it must be chosen. In this lesson you learn to travel through text — and to select it. (teacher guide — evidence; PARTIAL — Term 1 only)

## Sub lesson 1 — Moving through text with the arrow keys

The **arrow keys** move the **caret** through the text: left and right by one letter, up and down by one line. For a **small text** this is the better way — every step is exact and nothing is skipped. The caret always shows where you are; the arrows are walking for short distances.

[Picture — moving through text with the arrow keys]

## Sub lesson 2 — Selecting text — mouse or keyboard

**Selecting** marks a piece of text so a command acts on the whole piece at once. Drag the **mouse** across the text, or hold Shift and extend with the **keyboard**. For a **large text** selecting is the better way: one marked sweep covers in a second what a hundred arrow steps would crawl through.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: To work on text you must first move through it and mark it: the arrow keys walk the caret through a small text step by step, while selecting text — with the mouse or the keyboard — marks the larger piece that a command will act on. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
