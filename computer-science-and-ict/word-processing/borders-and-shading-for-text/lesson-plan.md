## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Borders — a line around the text** — A **border** is a drawn line around the outside of a paragraph, a piece of text, or a page. In Word it is set from **Format → Borders and Shading**: you choose which sides carry a line — all four for a box, or one side only, like the line under a heading — the **style** of the line (solid, dotted, double), its **width** and its **colour**, and whether it applies to the selected text or the whole paragraph. The preview in the dialog shows the result before you commit to it, because a border is a frame: it should be seen whole before it is worn.
2. **Shading — colour behind the text** — **Shading** fills the space behind the text with a colour. A light shading with a border around it is the classic way to make a notice, a warning or a key sentence impossible to miss on a page of plain text. The pairing has one discipline: **restraint**. Shading dark enough to fight the words, or borders around half the paragraphs, destroy the effect — everything highlighted is nothing highlighted. One shaded notice on a page speaks loudly; the lesson's rule is to use borders and shading the way a speaker raises their voice: rarely, briefly, and only where it matters.
