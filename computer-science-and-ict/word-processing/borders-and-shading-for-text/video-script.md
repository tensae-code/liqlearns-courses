# Video script — Borders and Shading for Text

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

A **border** is a drawn line around the outside of a paragraph, a piece of text, or a page.
In Word it is set from **Format → Borders and Shading**: you choose which sides carry a line — all four for a box, or one side only, like the line under a heading — the **style** of the line (solid, dotted, double), its **width** and its **colour**, and whether it applies to the selected text or the whole paragraph.
The preview in the dialog shows the result before you commit to it, because a border is a frame: it should be seen whole before it is worn.
**Shading** fills the space behind the text with a colour.
A light shading with a border around it is the classic way to make a notice, a warning or a key sentence impossible to miss on a page of plain text.
The pairing has one discipline: **restraint**.
Shading dark enough to fight the words, or borders around half the paragraphs, destroy the effect — everything highlighted is nothing highlighted.
One shaded notice on a page speaks loudly; the lesson's rule is to use borders and shading the way a speaker raises their voice: rarely, briefly, and only where it matters.
