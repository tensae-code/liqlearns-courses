# Borders and Shading for Text

## Do it first

A plain paragraph and an important notice can hold the same words — the difference is a line around one and colour behind it. In this lesson you learn borders and shading in Word.

## Sub lesson 1 — Borders — a line around the text

A **border** is a drawn line around the outside of a paragraph, a piece of text, or a page. In Word it is set from **Format → Borders and Shading**: you choose which sides carry a line — all four for a box, or one side only, like the line under a heading — the **style** of the line (solid, dotted, double), its **width** and its **colour**, and whether it applies to the selected text or the whole paragraph. The preview in the dialog shows the result before you commit to it, because a border is a frame: it should be seen whole before it is worn.

## Sub lesson 2 — Shading — colour behind the text

**Shading** fills the space behind the text with a colour. A light shading with a border around it is the classic way to make a notice, a warning or a key sentence impossible to miss on a page of plain text. The pairing has one discipline: **restraint**. Shading dark enough to fight the words, or borders around half the paragraphs, destroy the effect — everything highlighted is nothing highlighted. One shaded notice on a page speaks loudly; the lesson's rule is to use borders and shading the way a speaker raises their voice: rarely, briefly, and only where it matters.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: A border is a line drawn around text or a paragraph, and shading is a colour filled in behind it: together they make headings, notices and important text stand out on the page. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: A line drawn around the outside of a paragraph, a piece of text, or a page. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: Which sides, the line style, its width and colour, and text or paragraph. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: A light shading with a border around it. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: Nothing is highlighted. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
