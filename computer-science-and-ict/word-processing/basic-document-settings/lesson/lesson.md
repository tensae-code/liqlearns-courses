# Basic Document Settings

## Do it first

Before the text, the page: two Uzbekistan books — Grade 6 and Grade 8 — teach the document's main parameters in the same place in their Word courses, right after the window and before the writing grows.

## Sub lesson 1 — The page — size, margins, orientation

A document's **basic settings** begin with the page: its **size**, its **margins** — the blank frame on all four sides — and its **orientation**, portrait (tall) or landscape (wide). These are set first because every later judgement — where a line ends, whether a table fits — depends on them. Both books place the parameters lesson at position four of the Word course, before creating and saving the document itself.

## Sub lesson 2 — Text settings — font, size, spacing

The other basic parameters belong to the text: the **font** and its **size**, **line spacing**, and paragraph **alignment**. Set as defaults at the start, they keep a whole document in one dress; changed mid-way, they make patches. The lesson's discipline is simple: decide the document's parameters once, at the beginning, and let the writing — not the settings — carry the rest of the work.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: A document's **basic settings** begin with the page: its **size**, its **margins** — the blank frame on all four sides — and its **orientation**, portrait (tall) or landscape (wide). The other basic parameters belong to the text: the **font** and its **size**, **line spacing**, and paragraph **alignment**. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: Size, margins and orientation. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: The blank frame on the page's four sides. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: Font, font size and line spacing (or alignment). Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: The whole document. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
