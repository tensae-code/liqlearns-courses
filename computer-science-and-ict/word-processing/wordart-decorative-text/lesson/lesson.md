# WordArt — Decorative Text

## Do it first

Some words in a document are meant to be looked at, not just read: titles, posters, covers. Grade 6 teaches WordArt — text bent, coloured and shadowed into decoration — as its own Word lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — What WordArt is

**WordArt** is Word's decorative text: a short piece of writing turned into an **object** with a chosen style — shaped, coloured, outlined, shadowed. It is inserted like a picture, moved and resized like a picture, and edited by reopening its text. The Grade 6 book gives WordArt a full lesson between tables and formulas: decoration is a real document skill, with its own tool.

## Sub lesson 2 — When decoration serves the document

WordArt serves **titles and posters**: one short line, large, saying the document's name — never whole paragraphs, which become unreadable in decoration. The lesson's taste rule: if a reader must work to read it, it has failed; decoration carries the title and leaves the text plain. Grade 6 pupils practise on the cover of their own document from the course's earlier lessons.

Tell your companion, in your own words: **WordArt** is Word's decorative text: a short piece of writing turned into an **object** with a chosen style — shaped, coloured, outlined, shadowed. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

1. Show it, don't just say it: Decorative text — writing turned into a styled object. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: Shape, colour and outline the text (or shadow it). Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: Titles and posters. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
