# Shading a Paragraph and Putting a Border Around It

## Do it first

One paragraph in your document matters more than the rest. In this lesson you make the reader's eye stop there. (teacher guide — evidence; PARTIAL — Term 1 only)

## Sub lesson 1 — Shading — colour behind the words

**Shading** fills the area **behind a paragraph or a piece of text** with a colour. The words stay the same; the ground under them changes, so the paragraph lifts off the page. Choose a light shade — dark shading swallows dark letters — and shade only what must stand out, or nothing does.

## Sub lesson 2 — A border — a frame around the paragraph

A **border** draws a **frame (lines) around a paragraph** — on all sides, or only where you choose. Border and shading combine: a framed, lightly shaded paragraph reads as a separate notice inside the document — the way warnings, rules and announcements are set apart.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: A paragraph can be dressed two ways: shading fills the space behind its text with colour, and a border draws a frame around it — together they make one paragraph stand out from the page, like a notice pinned on a board. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
