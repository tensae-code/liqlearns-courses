# Closing a word processor — and first writing practice

## Do it first

Open Word. Write your full name. Now stop: how do you finish properly — so the work is kept and the program is closed, not just abandoned? This lesson gives the two closing routes, then sets the book's full first practice.

## Sub lesson 1 — Two ways to close

Word closes in two ways. **Way 1:** click the **Close button** — the X at the **top-right of the title bar**. **Way 2:** open the **File menu** and choose **Exit**. Both end the program properly. Remember the order of a good finish: **save first, then close** — closing asks about unsaved work, but the habit of saving first is what protects you.

## Sub lesson 2 — First writing practice

Now the book's closing practical, whole. **First:** open Word, write your **full name**, save the document, and close Word. **Second:** open Word again and write a short text about yourself, with your **name, grade and section at the top**; save it **on the desktop under your first name**, and close. **Third, discuss:** pen-and-pencil writing against word processing — which is easier to correct, to read, to keep? The teacher guide is plain about how this practical is marked: the teacher watches and helps while you write, save and close — it is marked on the doing.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the two ways to close Word, and walk them through the practical you just did, stage by stage.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the full practical once more, alone, start to finish.
2. Save the about-yourself text on the desktop under your first name and open it back to check.
3. Write three sentences: word processing against pen and pencil, from your discussion.
