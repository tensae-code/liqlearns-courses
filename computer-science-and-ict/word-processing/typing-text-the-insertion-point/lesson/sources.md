**Acceptance count: taught in 9 countries (Ethiopia, Afghanistan, Uzbekistan, Cuba, Sri Lanka, Moldova, Jordan, Vietnam, Pakistan).**

Afghanistan's own way (Grade 10): a Windows XP-era office apprenticeship — one full chapter per application, taught menu by menu (File, Edit, Insert, Format), with the Dari keyboard language added as its own lesson; networks arrive only in the final chapter.

Saudi Arabia's own way (Grade 4, Digital Skills): the primary Digital Skills course (Binary Logic) is built as three units per term with the same guide pattern as the intermediate books (outcomes, important points, steps, answers, unit project, "Test Yourself"), files on the Ain platform, and a strong Arabic/English bilingual-typing strand — pupils switch the keyboard between Arabic and English with Alt+Shift and learn text direction, hamza and Arabic punctuation on the keyboard alongside Word skills; Scratch is introduced in Term 1 through algorithms from daily life. This citation is PARTIAL — Term 1 only. (teacher guide — evidence)


Uzbekistan's own way (Grade 6): the whole of grade 6 is one application taught to mastery — MS Word from its interface to formulas and print — on the principle that one tool learned deeply beats many tools skimmed.

Cuba's own way (Grade 2): Play-based (lúdico) introduction through the graficador (Paint-type drawing program) and educational software; correct workstation posture/ergonomics is taught explicitly in Grade 2, and files/folders are introduced intuitively through saving and opening drawings in a class-group folder. (teacher guide — evidence)

Cuba's own way (Grade 5): Student text mirrors the programme in four chapters; word-processing is taught as a research workflow (synonyms, symbols, searching, copying texts and images from sources), and Scratch is introduced through its paint editor before movement/appearance/control blocks.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 7 Reading Book): the CPU is taught through a hotel-kitchen/chef analogy; programme development (decomposition, flowcharts, variables, bugs) precedes presentation software; HTML page-building is already in Grade 7.

Moldova's own way (Grade 2, Educație Digitală): Grade 2 builds the idea of information quantity (bit, byte, binary drawing) before any office software, teaches the keyboard including touch-typing posture, and keeps programming unplugged-but-precise: loops, jump (pen-up) instructions and multi-loop programs for the mouse, the painter and the hedgehog. The Digital Education module sits inside Technological Education.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 3 G3 Workbook): The Office ladder continues — Word carries the early blocks, PowerPoint the middle blocks (e.g. multiplication facts), and Excel appears in the geometry-and-measurement block, with Save As taught as an explicit step.

Vietnam's own way (Grade 5 Tin học 5): Grade 5 splits its applied theme into two alternative tracks — track A makes digital products with graphics software, track B follows tutorial videos to make physical craft products — and its programming theme is taught in a visual block language as pure structures first (sequence, loop, branch), then variables and expressions, ending with turning a written script/storyboard into a program.


Pakistan's own way (Grade 6 Computer Science 6): every unit opens with Students Learning Outcomes, is dotted with "Do you Know?", "Extra Bit!" and "Tips" boxes, and closes with Summary, MCQ/short/detailed exercises, project-based and activity-based questions, with an answers key at the back. Digital skills (Windows desktop, file management, Paint 3D, a typing tutor) are taught hands-on BEFORE programming; digital citizenship is framed through Pakistani citizenship first; the entrepreneurship unit teaches from Pakistani founders (Foodpanda/EatOye, Shopsy.pk, Zero Makeup, Investors Lounge).
