# Lesson plan — Writing Personal and Official Letters

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Word Processing · **Lesson:** Writing Personal and Official Letters
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Cambodia (2026-11-14) — in the title Word Processing on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Cambodia's *Information and Communication Technology, Grade 10*, because no existing lesson teaches this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A letter's form follows who it is for: a personal letter to someone you know carries your address and the date, a friendly greeting, your news in your own warm voice, a friendly closing and your name; an official letter to an office or authority carries the sender's and receiver's addresses, the date, a subject line naming its business, a formal greeting, clear paragraphs that state the matter, a formal closing, and your signature with your name (and position, if you write in one).
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on typing text in a word processor (the book's course works in OpenOffice Writer); both letter forms — their parts, their order and their different voices — are built inside the lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Personal letters — writing to someone you know** — The personal letter's parts in order: your own address and the date; a friendly greeting to a person you know; the body — your news, questions and feelings in your own warm, ordinary voice, in paragraphs; a friendly closing; and your name. Its mark is the relationship: it sounds like you, speaking to them.
2. **Official letters — writing to an office or an authority** — The official letter's parts in order: the sender's address, the receiver's address and office, the date, a subject line that names the business in a few words, a formal greeting, body paragraphs that state the matter clearly and in order — what you write about, the facts, what you ask — a formal closing, and the signature with the writer's name (and position, where the writer has one). Its mark is the opposite of the personal letter's: exact, respectful and to the point, whoever reads it.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Word Processing and Typing Khmer Script in Word Processing. Recommended, never forced — a learner may land on this lesson directly, and it stands alone.
- Cambodia's own way is kept: in Cambodia's Grade 10 book these are the two opening sessions of Chapter 2 Lesson 1 — letter writing opens the office-work chapter, in Khmer, before the Writer program itself is opened in the following session.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the parts of a personal letter in order, and say how its voice differs from an official letter's.
2. Name the parts of an official letter in order, including the subject line, and say what each part is for.
3. Write a short personal letter with all its parts in place.
4. Write a short official letter — addresses, date, subject, formal greeting, clear matter paragraphs, formal closing, signature and name.
5. Given a situation, choose the right kind of letter, and say why.

## What you need
A computer with a text-editing program open (the book's course works in OpenOffice Writer), or paper and a pencil first. Two real reasons to write help: one person you know, and one office with a real matter.

Say, before any form is given, three ways they must differ. This lesson gives both forms, part by part. (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
Writes the official letter like a message to a friend → return to the reader: an office does not know you; the form must carry the respect and the facts by itself — addresses, subject, formal greeting and closing. Forgets the subject line → the subject is the letter's label: an office files and finds letters by it, so the business must be named before the greeting. Puts the receiver's address in the personal letter → a personal letter goes to a person you know at a place you know; its head carries your address and the date, not a receiver's block.
