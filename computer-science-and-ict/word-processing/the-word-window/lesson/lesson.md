# The Word window — naming its parts

## Do it first

Open Word and look at the whole window for one quiet minute. It is crowded: bars, buttons, a ruler, a big white space. Guess what each part might be for. Then this lesson names them all, one by one — by the end, you will point to any part and say its job.

## Sub lesson 1 — The top of the window

The **title bar** sits at the top centre: it carries the program's name and the document's title. Below it, the **menu bar / tabs** — Home, Insert, Page Layout and more — open the tool sets. At the top corner, the **Quick Access Toolbar** holds Save, Undo and Repeat by default: the three buttons you reach for most, always in reach.

## Sub lesson 2 — The working middle

The **Ribbon** is the wide band of buttons, grouped in tabs by task — each tab shows the buttons for one kind of job. The **rulers** measure the page: margins, tabs, indents and columns are set against them. The big white **document area** is where your text and pictures go. **Help** is there when you are stuck.

## Sub lesson 3 — The bottom and the File tab

At the bottom, the **status bar** shows document information and where the insertion point is. The **view buttons** switch how the page is shown: Print Layout, Full Screen Reading, Web Layout, Outline and Draft. The **zoom control** makes the page bigger or smaller on screen. Behind everything sits the **File tab** — the door to Save, Save As, Open, Close, Info, Recent, New and Print.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the parts of the Word window from top to bottom, with one job for each part.

## Do It Yourself

1. Point to each part on a real Word window and say its name without looking at the lesson.
2. Write the File tab's list from memory.
3. Say which part you would use to make the page look bigger, and which shows where your cursor is.
