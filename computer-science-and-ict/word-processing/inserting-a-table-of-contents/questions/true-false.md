# Inserting a Table of Contents — true-false

Say whether each statement is true or false.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "What does Word build a table of contents from?" is: Heading styles — Heading 1, Heading 2 and their levels**
**Answer: True**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "Does big, bold text count as a heading for the table?" is: Heading 1**
**Answer: False** — No — only text marked with a heading style

**3. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "Which style marks a chapter title?" is: Heading 1**
**Answer: True**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "Which style marks a section inside a chapter?" is: They make headings look even through the whole document**
**Answer: False** — Heading 2

**5. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "What second job do heading styles do?" is: They make headings look even through the whole document**
**Answer: True**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "Where does the table of contents usually stand?" is: The References tab**
**Answer: False** — On its own page, after the title page

**7. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "Which tab holds the Table of Contents command?" is: The References tab**
**Answer: True**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "What does Word collect into the table?" is: Put the cursor where the table should stand**
**Answer: False** — Every styled heading, with its page number

**9. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "What must you do before building the table?" is: Put the cursor where the table should stand**
**Answer: True**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "What do you do after headings or pages change?" is: Heading styles — Heading 1, Heading 2 and their levels**
**Answer: False** — Click Update Table — never retype the table by hand
