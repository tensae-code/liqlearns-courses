# Inserting a Table of Contents

## Do it first

Long documents need a map at the front. This lesson builds that map the way Word expects: styles first, then the table itself in a few clicks.

## Sub lesson 1 — Heading styles come first

Word builds a table of contents from **heading styles**, not from text that is merely big or bold. Mark each chapter title with **Heading 1**, each section title inside it with **Heading 2**, and so on down the levels. The styles do two jobs at once: they make the headings look even through the whole document, and they tell Word exactly which lines belong in the table and at which level.

## Sub lesson 2 — Building the table of contents

With the headings styled, put the cursor where the table should stand — usually on its own page after the title page — open the **References** tab and choose **Table of Contents**, then an automatic style. Word collects every styled heading with its page number and builds the list. If headings or pages change later, do not retype the table: click **Update Table** and Word rebuilds it from the styles.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: A table of contents is a list of a document's headings with their page numbers, and Word can build it for you automatically — but only if the headings were written with heading styles first. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
