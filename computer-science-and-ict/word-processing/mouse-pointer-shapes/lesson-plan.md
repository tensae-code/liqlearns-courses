# Lesson plan — Mouse pointer shapes and what they mean

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Word Processing · **Lesson:** Mouse pointer shapes and what they mean
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 5 — Word Processing in the Computer Science and ICT ladder (TITLES.md); stocked from Ethiopia's Grade 7 book, Unit 3 — Computer Software. The ladder is the recommended order, never a forced one.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the pointer changes its meaning with its shape — the ordinary pointer for clicking, the timer when the computer is busy, the flashing insertion point where text will go, and the I-beam over text.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Word-window lesson — the pointer moves over the parts that lesson named.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or in steps — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The Word window — naming its parts.  Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the four pointer shapes in the book.
2. Say what each shape means.
3. Say what to do when the timer shows: wait.
4. Say where text will appear: at the flashing insertion point.

## What you need
A computer with Word open.

## If the learner is stuck
- Confuses I-beam and insertion point → the I-beam is the mouse over text; the flashing line is the place in the text itself. Clicking moves the line to where the I-beam points.
