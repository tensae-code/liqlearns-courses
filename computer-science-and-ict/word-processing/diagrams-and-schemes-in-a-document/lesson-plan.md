# Lesson plan — Diagrams and Schemes in a Document

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Word Processing · **Lesson:** Diagrams and Schemes in a Document
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Cuba (2026-11-14) — in the title Word Processing on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Cuba's Mi Mundo Digital / Informática student books and teacher-side books (Ministerio de Educación / Editorial Pueblo y Educación), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A **scheme** (esquema) is a diagram that shows a thing's structure — **boxes for the parts, lines or arrows for how they join** — and a word processor can build one inside a document with its shapes.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Schemes — a structure drawn** — A **scheme** draws **how a thing is organised**: the whole at the top or the start, its **parts in boxes** under or after it, and **connecting lines or arrows** showing what belongs to what and what follows what. A scheme of a lesson shows its units and sub-units; a scheme of a story shows its parts in order. The scheme is not decoration — it is the structure itself, made visible, and it is read by following the connections, not line by line.
2. **Building a scheme in the word processor** — The word processor builds a scheme from its **drawing shapes**: choose a shape for each box, place the boxes in their levels, write the part's name inside each, and join them with **connector lines or arrows**. Order matters: draw the whole first, then its parts under it, then the connections — a box added before its place is known makes tangles. Cuba teaches schemes inside the word processor, not a drawing program, because the scheme belongs inside the document it explains.
