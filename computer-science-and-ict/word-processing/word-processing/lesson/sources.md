**Acceptance count: taught in 10 countries (Ethiopia, Cambodia, Azerbaijan, Uganda, Yemen, Uzbekistan, Fiji, Bhutan, Sri Lanka, Rwanda).**

Yemen's own way (Grade 10): a skills ladder — every unit alternates short teaching topics with a named تدريب (guided training) and نشاط (activity), and Windows itself is taught as a school subject for a full unit before any application is opened.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Grade 9): one TIA teacher guide carries two ICT subjects side by side — Communication Technology (networks, ergonomics, safety, societal impact) and Computer Technology (architecture, software) — organised by content standards and numbered benchmarks (9.4.x / 9.5.x), with the design brief used as the problem-solving method for both a product and a simple network, and typing assessed at 20 wpm with 80% accuracy. (teacher guide — evidence, PARTIAL)


Uzbekistan's own way (Grade 6): the whole of grade 6 is one application taught to mastery — MS Word from its interface to formulas and print — on the principle that one tool learned deeply beats many tools skimmed.

Uzbekistan's own way (Grade 8): grade 8 is theory-first — number systems, coding and Boolean logic before the machine's own story — then a full operating-system chapter and a five-lesson Word course; safety rules return as a closing appendix.

Fiji's own way (Year 11 Computer Education student book): The first locally written text of its kind (the book's Preface): written by a panel of Computer Studies officers and University of the South Pacific lecturers for the new Year 11 Computer Education syllabus trialled in 2014; each chapter uses "Discovery Learning" prompts, and the database chapter is a full Access 2007 practical building a Student database in a "Lab Chapter 3" folder.

Bhutan's own way (Class V, Literacy with ICT student book): Word is taught together with typing in Dzongkha, the national language (Dzongkha keyboard software and fonts); communication is taught on the school LAN with Qualia LAN Messenger (chat, file sharing and broadcast with no Internet connection), and the end-of-year folder is sent to the teacher as a LAN-messenger attachment.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 7 Reading Book): the CPU is taught through a hotel-kitchen/chef analogy; programme development (decomposition, flowcharts, variables, bugs) precedes presentation software; HTML page-building is already in Grade 7.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 7 Workbook): a companion workbook mirroring the Reading Book chapter-for-chapter (CPU, OS, security, word processing, program development, presentations, internet).

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 8 Reading Book): number systems and binary conversion open Grade 8; "physical computing" is logic gates taught through water-valve and street-lamp analogies; Scratch selection structures and mobile/smart-device apps sit in the Programming chapter.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 8 Workbook): a companion workbook mirroring the Reading Book (Activities 1.1–6.3), with worked conversion drills for number systems.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 12 Science for Technology, Tamil): the A/L technology-stream ICT volume teaches every office skill twice — once in Microsoft Office 2010 and once in LibreOffice (Writer/Calc/Impress 4.1) — and closes with creating a real Gmail account, mailbox folders, attachments and password safety; hardware goes down to cache levels L1/L2/L3, ROM types and file systems (FAT/NTFS/ext4).

Rwanda's own way (S1 ICT Student Book, KLB): competency-based units each opening with a Key Unit Competency and keywords; a full GIS/ArcGIS unit and a Scratch music-making unit sit inside the first secondary year, and ICT use is grounded in Rwandan daily life.

Rwanda's own way (S1 ICT Teacher Guide, KLB): the guide mirrors the student units with content maps, additional teacher content per unit, SEN/multi-ability support, sample lesson plans (Appendix 1) and a glossary (Appendix 2); assessment is framed by Rwanda's competence-based curriculum. (teacher guide — evidence)

Laos's own way (Grade 7 ICT teacher guide): uniquely, this guide — not the student book — carries the real software syllabus: separate activities for DOS, Windows/Linux, Unix/Mac, translators, BASIC and C, Logo, Java, and one activity per application family — a teacher-only expanded software syllabus. (teacher guide — evidence)
