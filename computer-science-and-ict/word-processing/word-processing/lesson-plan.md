# Lesson plan — Word processing — writing on a computer

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Word Processing · **Lesson:** Word processing — writing on a computer
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 5 — Word Processing in the Computer Science and ICT ladder (TITLES.md); stocked from Ethiopia's Grade 7 book, Unit 3 — Computer Software. The ladder is the recommended order, never a forced one.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a word processor lets you create, edit, format and save documents — and add images, tables and charts — with text that is cleaner and easier to correct than pen-and-paper writing.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the application-software lesson — word processing is the first kind taken up as a hands-on skill; the Word practical takes half of Ethiopia's Unit 3.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or in steps — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Before this lesson

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say the four things a word processor lets you do: create, edit, format, save.
2. Name three things you can add to a document: images, tables, charts.
3. Give two advantages over pen-and-paper writing.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil; a computer with a word processor if you can use one.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner writes one sentence by hand, makes a deliberate mistake in it, and feels what fixing it costs on paper (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks word processing is only typing → typing is creating; editing, formatting and saving are the rest of the skill, and they are what make it better than paper.
