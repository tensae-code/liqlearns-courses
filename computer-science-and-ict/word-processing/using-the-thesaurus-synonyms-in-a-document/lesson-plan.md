**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A **synonym** is a word that means the same — or nearly the same — as another, and the word processor's **thesaurus** finds them: select a word, look it up, and replace it with the synonym that fits the sentence.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Synonyms — the same meaning, another word** — Words that carry the **same or nearly the same meaning** are **synonyms**: big and large, begin and start. A text that repeats one word tires its reader, and a text copied word-for-word from a source is not the writer's own. Synonyms answer both: they let a sentence say the same thing freshly. The choice is not free, though — a synonym must **fit the sentence**: its meaning in that place, and the tone of the text around it.
2. **The thesaurus — finding the synonym in the program** — The word processor carries a **thesaurus** — a synonym finder built into the program. The working order is: **select the word** in your text, **open the thesaurus**, read the synonyms it offers, **choose** the one that fits, and let the program **replace** the word — or type the change yourself. Then read the sentence again whole: the thesaurus offers, but the writer decides, because only the writer knows what the sentence must say.
