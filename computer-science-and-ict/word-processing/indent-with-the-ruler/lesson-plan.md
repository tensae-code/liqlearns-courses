# Lesson plan — Indent with the Ruler

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Word Processing · **Lesson:** Indent with the Ruler
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 5 — Word Processing in the Computer Science and ICT ladder (TITLES.md); stocked from Ethiopia's Grade 9 book, Unit 3 — Application Software. The ladder is the recommended order, never a forced one.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the ruler carries three markers — first-line, hanging and left indent — and dragging the chosen marker indents the selected paragraphs the way that marker promises.
**This lesson's own level:** it is the second of the four indentation methods, and the most visual: you drag the indent instead of pressing for it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. ## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Indent with the Tab key.  Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Show the ruler: View tab, tick Ruler in the Show group.
2. Name the three markers and say what each one moves.
3. Select paragraphs (Ctrl+A for a whole document), drag a marker, and release to indent.

## What you need
A computer with Word and a document of several paragraphs.

## If the learner is stuck
- No ruler on screen → View tab, the Show group, tick Ruler.
- Wrong lines moved → you dragged the wrong marker: top triangle is first line only, bottom triangle is the rest, the small box is both together.
- Only one paragraph changed → select first; Ctrl+A takes the whole document.
