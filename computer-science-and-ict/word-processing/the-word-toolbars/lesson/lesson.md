# The Word Toolbars

## Do it first

Dozens of little pictures under Word's menus — each one is a command waiting for one click. In this lesson you learn Word's toolbars and how to read them.

## Sub lesson 1 — The toolbars — commands as buttons

A **toolbar** is a row of **buttons**, each button a picture of the job it does. Toolbars exist because the commonest commands deserve to be one click away, not buried in menus. In the book's Word the two toolbars that matter are the **Standard toolbar** and the **Formatting toolbar**, sitting under the menu bar. A toolbar can be shown or hidden from the View menu, and if you do not know a button, you rest the mouse pointer on it and a **tooltip** — a small label — appears with its name. Never guess a button: hover first.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Standard and Formatting toolbars

The **Standard toolbar** is the working toolbar: New, Open, Save, Print, Print Preview, Cut, Copy, Paste, Undo and the rest of the file-and-editing jobs — the same commands the File and Edit menus hold, as buttons. The **Formatting toolbar** is the appearance toolbar: font name and size, Bold, Italic, Underline, alignment, bullets and numbering, borders and colours — everything that changes how the text looks. The split to remember: Standard changes the document as a whole and your place in it; Formatting changes how the selected text looks.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Word's toolbars are rows of buttons that give one-click commands: the Standard toolbar carries the file and editing commands, the Formatting toolbar carries the look of the text — and a button is found by resting the pointer on it to read its name. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
