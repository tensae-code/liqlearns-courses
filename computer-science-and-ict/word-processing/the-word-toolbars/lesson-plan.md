# Lesson plan — The Word Toolbars

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Word Processing · **Lesson:** The Word Toolbars
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Afghanistan (2026-11-14) — in the title Word Processing on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Afghanistan's *Computer* Grade 10, because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Word's toolbars are rows of buttons that give one-click commands: the Standard toolbar carries the file and editing commands, the Formatting toolbar carries the look of the text — and a button is found by resting the pointer on it to read its name.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the Afghanistan book's own order — it follows the book's sections in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The toolbars — commands as buttons** — A **toolbar** is a row of **buttons**, each button a picture of the job it does. Toolbars exist because the commonest commands deserve to be one click away, not buried in menus. In the book's Word the two toolbars that matter are the **Standard toolbar** and the **Formatting toolbar**, sitting under the menu bar. A toolbar can be shown or hidden from the View menu, and if you do not know a button, you rest the mouse pointer on it and a **tooltip** — a small label — appears with its name. Never guess a button: hover first.
2. **The Standard and Formatting toolbars** — The **Standard toolbar** is the working toolbar: New, Open, Save, Print, Print Preview, Cut, Copy, Paste, Undo and the rest of the file-and-editing jobs — the same commands the File and Edit menus hold, as buttons. The **Formatting toolbar** is the appearance toolbar: font name and size, Bold, Italic, Underline, alignment, bullets and numbering, borders and colours — everything that changes how the text looks. The split to remember: Standard changes the document as a whole and your place in it; Formatting changes how the selected text looks.
