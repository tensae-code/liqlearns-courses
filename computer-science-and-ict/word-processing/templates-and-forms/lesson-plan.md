# Lesson plan — Templates and Forms

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Word Processing · **Lesson:** Templates and Forms
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — North Macedonia (2026-11-14) — in the title Word Processing on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from North Macedonia's *Informatics* (2026), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A template is a ready-made document pattern — layout, styles and placeholder text saved once so every new document made from it starts identical — and a form is a document built for filling in, with fixed text protected and spaces (fields, lists, checkboxes) left for the answers.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Templates — start from a pattern** — A **template** saves a document's **skeleton**: margins, styles, headings, logos, standard text. Creating a document **from** a template copies the skeleton; the new document is filled in and saved under its own name while the template stays untouched for next time. Word offers ready templates, and any document you design can be **saved as a template** of your own.
2. **Forms — documents that ask questions** — A **form** fixes the questions and frees only the answers: **content controls** give the filler-in text fields, drop-down lists and checkboxes, and **protection** can lock everything except those fields. Schools, offices and clinics run on such forms — the designer's job is that the form cannot be accidentally broken by the person completing it.
