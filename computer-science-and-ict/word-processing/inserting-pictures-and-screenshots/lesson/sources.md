**Acceptance count: taught in 12 countries (Ethiopia, Iraq, Uzbekistan, Cuba, Bhutan, Mongolia, Sri Lanka, Moldova, Jordan, Armenia, Vietnam, Pakistan).**

Mauritania's own way (Grade 7): Informatics is taught for the first time in 1AS as thirty 30-minute teacher fiches moving hardware → information systems (illustrated with Mauritanian systems Bankily, Masrvi, Houwiyeti and the Ministry's Sirage platform) → a long Microsoft Word sequence → a full unit on internet ethics before browsers and search are even introduced.

Iraq's own way (Grade 8): the same four-unit spiral one year on — the hardware unit becomes “the computer platform” and opens with choosing a computer's specifications before buying; Word is taught tab by tab (Home, Insert, Design, Layout); the algorithms unit advances to selection statements and logic truth tables, still landing in Scratch; the internet unit ends with writing a report from web research.


Uzbekistan's own way (Grade 6): the whole of grade 6 is one application taught to mastery — MS Word from its interface to formulas and print — on the principle that one tool learned deeply beats many tools skimmed.

Uzbekistan's own way (Grade 8): grade 8 is theory-first — number systems, coding and Boolean logic before the machine's own story — then a full operating-system chapter and a five-lesson Word course; safety rules return as a closing appendix.

Cuba's own way (Grade 5): Student text mirrors the programme in four chapters; word-processing is taught as a research workflow (synonyms, symbols, searching, copying texts and images from sources), and Scratch is introduced through its paint editor before movement/appearance/control blocks.

Bhutan's own way (Class VI, Literacy with ICT student book): School computing runs on Google's G Suite for Education — email is Gmail on the school account, collaboration is Google Classroom, and the end-of-year food-chain project is agreed in Classroom, built in Word, password-protected, cited, filed in folders and reported to the teacher by email.

Mongolia's own way (Grade 7): the whole applications half is one long project — research and produce a printed booklet "Усаа хайрлая" (Cherish the Water) in Word, a Paint.NET layered picture, and a class PowerPoint; image work uses the free Paint.NET (downloaded from getpaint.net) rather than MS Paint; the e-communication chapter teaches Yahoo mail and Yahoo Messenger, Mongolian history of the örtöö relay post and the @ sign, and makes correct Latin transliteration of Mongolian Cyrillic (standard MNS 5217:2003) an ethics topic in its own right.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 12 Science for Technology, Tamil): the A/L technology-stream ICT volume teaches every office skill twice — once in Microsoft Office 2010 and once in LibreOffice (Writer/Calc/Impress 4.1) — and closes with creating a real Gmail account, mailbox folders, attachments and password safety; hardware goes down to cache levels L1/L2/L3, ROM types and file systems (FAT/NTFS/ext4).

Moldova's own way (Grade 8, Informatică): Grade 8 pairs a full office-grade Word course (styles, templates, mail merge, formulas) with algorithmic thinking taught formally — executors, subalgorithms, counted/conditional loops, branching — then implemented instruction by instruction in Pascal, closing with image editing in Paint 3D and GIMP.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 4 G4 Teacher): Guide plans mirror the workbook blocks (PowerPoint, Excel, Word) with the same per-block learning products and assessment appendices as the other early-grades guides.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 6 G6 Workbook): Grade 6 adds real Windows skills inside the activity blocks — creating a blank Word document, capturing the active window with Alt+Print Screen into the clipboard and Paint, and pixel art built in Excel with controlled dimensions.

Jordan's own way (Grade 4 Digital Skills Teacher Guide — links-pass L2 sections): Digital Skills is not a standalone subject book at this level — the guide embeds digital skills inside the child's other subjects as six "learning environments" (Science and Maths units) plus staged learning projects, each with named digital tools (PowerPoint, Excel, Word, Padlet, PhET), learning outcomes, and a performance rubric.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 4 G4 student): Grade 4 (Stepanyan, Amirkhanyan, Matinyan) opens with e-security and health (recognising device malfunctions) and what to tell about yourself online, then purposeful documents (paragraph formats, inserted pictures, tables), slide presentations, e-mail, files, solving technical problems, ways of picturing data, and a Scratch strand of story animation, conditions, loops and variables.

Vietnam's own way (Grade 5 Tin học 5): Grade 5 splits its applied theme into two alternative tracks — track A makes digital products with graphics software, track B follows tutorial videos to make physical craft products — and its programming theme is taught in a visual block language as pure structures first (sequence, loop, branch), then variables and expressions, ending with turning a written script/storyboard into a program.

Vietnam's own way (Grade 10 Chuyên đề Tin học 10 Ứng dụng): the whole Applied study-topics book is one continuous project — each class group role-plays a travel company advertising Vietnamese destinations at a "Ba miền" tourism fair, producing in turn a Word travel handbook, an Excel quotation program, and a PowerPoint media product.

South Sudan's own way (Grade 12 S4 Teacher): The guide mirrors the student book's three units with teaching sequences, observation/conversation assessment points and worked projects — designing a database for a local medical practice, asking how books are managed in a school library, and a capstone project to design the school's own website, judged on navigation and usability. Pakistan's own way (Grade 7 Computer Science 7): Grade 7 opens with emerging technologies and a deep ports-and-peripherals hardware treatment, then teaches email all the way to DKIM/SPF/DMARC authentication and POP3/IMAP/SMTP protocols; binary data representation (ASCII/Unicode, RGB colour, sound sampling) is taught inside the Programming unit, and entrepreneurship becomes business-plan craft — promotion, value proposition, quality assurance, SEO and platform business models, with Twitter and Snapchat origin stories.
