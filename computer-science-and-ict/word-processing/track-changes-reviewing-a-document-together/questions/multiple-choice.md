# Track Changes — Reviewing a Document Together — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What does Track Changes do?**
a) It marks every edit on the document instead of silently replacing text  b) Insertions, deletions and formatting changes  c) In colour, with the changer's name — underlined, struck through or in the margin  d) Its own history of changes as it is reviewed
**Answer: a) It marks every edit on the document instead of silently replacing text**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What kinds of change are marked?**
a) In colour, with the changer's name — underlined, struck through or in the margin  b) Insertions, deletions and formatting changes  c) Its own history of changes as it is reviewed  d) A reviewer improves or questions the document without overwriting its author
**Answer: b) Insertions, deletions and formatting changes**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) How are changes shown?**
a) Its own history of changes as it is reviewed  b) A reviewer improves or questions the document without overwriting its author  c) In colour, with the changer's name — underlined, struck through or in the margin  d) The document's author
**Answer: c) In colour, with the changer's name — underlined, struck through or in the margin**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What does a tracked document carry with it?**
a) A reviewer improves or questions the document without overwriting its author  b) The document's author  c) The change becomes plain text in the document  d) Its own history of changes as it is reviewed
**Answer: d) Its own history of changes as it is reviewed**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) How is tracking like commenting?**
a) A reviewer improves or questions the document without overwriting its author  b) The document's author  c) The change becomes plain text in the document  d) The original words stand
**Answer: a) A reviewer improves or questions the document without overwriting its author**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) Who decides the fate of each tracked change?**
a) The change becomes plain text in the document  b) The document's author  c) The original words stand  d) All together at once, when the review is trusted
**Answer: b) The document's author**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What does accepting a change do?**
a) The original words stand  b) All together at once, when the review is trusted  c) The change becomes plain text in the document  d) Peers review the end-of-year presentation with tracked changes and comments sent by email
**Answer: c) The change becomes plain text in the document**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What does rejecting a change do?**
a) All together at once, when the review is trusted  b) Peers review the end-of-year presentation with tracked changes and comments sent by email  c) It marks every edit on the document instead of silently replacing text  d) The original words stand
**Answer: d) The original words stand**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) How else can changes be accepted?**
a) All together at once, when the review is trusted  b) Peers review the end-of-year presentation with tracked changes and comments sent by email  c) It marks every edit on the document instead of silently replacing text  d) Insertions, deletions and formatting changes
**Answer: a) All together at once, when the review is trusted**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) How does Bhutan's Class VII use it at year's end?**
a) It marks every edit on the document instead of silently replacing text  b) Peers review the end-of-year presentation with tracked changes and comments sent by email  c) Insertions, deletions and formatting changes  d) In colour, with the changer's name — underlined, struck through or in the margin
**Answer: b) Peers review the end-of-year presentation with tracked changes and comments sent by email**
