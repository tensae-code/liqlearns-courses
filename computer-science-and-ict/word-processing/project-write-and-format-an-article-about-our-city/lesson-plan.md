# Lesson plan — Project — Write and Format an Article About Our City

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Word Processing · **Lesson:** Project — Write and Format an Article About Our City
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Saudi Arabia (2026-11-14) — in the title Word Processing on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Saudi Arabia's Grade 4 teacher guide, because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson. This lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence only (Saudi Arabia), named in lesson/sources.md, counted nowhere.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The unit's project: in a team, write an article about your city in Word and apply everything the unit taught — a title, symbols and bullets to mark its parts, and formatting throughout — until the article reads and looks like a finished piece.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the Saudi book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Writing the article as a team** — The **team** writes an **article about our city** in **Word**: agree the parts first — what our city is, its places, its life — then draft each part with the unit's typing and editing skills, in Arabic or English with the language switch where needed. Every member writes; the team reads the whole aloud before formatting begins.
2. **Applying everything — title, marks, formatting** — Then the article is **finished as a document**: a **title** at its head, **symbols and bullets to mark its parts**, and the unit's **formatting** — capitals where English needs them, bold and colour where emphasis belongs, alignment that suits each part. The test of the project: a reader can see the article's structure before reading a word.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
