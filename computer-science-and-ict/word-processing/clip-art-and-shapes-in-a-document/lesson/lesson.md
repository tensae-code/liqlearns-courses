# Clip art and shapes in a document

## Do it first

A greeting card needs balloons and flowers you cannot photograph. And an important sentence needs a circle drawn round it. Find the first ready-made; draw the second by hand.

## Sub lesson 1 — Clip art — ready-made imagery

**Clip art** is ready-made imagery for colour and presentation — greeting-card **balloons and flowers** are the book's example.

1. **Insert → Clip Art**.
2. **Search a category** in the task pane.
3. **Double-click a result** to insert it.

The other route: **Insert Online Pictures**, with the results **filtered to clip art**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Shapes — drag-drawn, perfect with Shift

Shapes are inserted from **Insert → Shapes**:

1. **Choose the shape**.
2. **Click-and-drag** in the document to size it.
3. Hold **Shift** while dragging for a **perfect version** — a true circle, a true square.
4. For many of the same shape: **right-click the shape → Lock Drawing Mode** — the shape stays armed for repeated drawing until **Esc**, or a **right-click on blank space**, cancels it.

The closing practical formats a two-paragraph document on the **eagle**: a found picture cropped, resized, rotated and wrapped with text, plus shapes — saved as **Eagle**.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the two clip-art routes, how Shift changes a drawn shape, and how Lock Drawing Mode is switched on and off.

## Do It Yourself

1. Find balloons or flowers by Insert → Clip Art and double-click one into a greeting card.
2. Draw a circle round an important sentence; draw it again with Shift held and compare.
3. Turn on Lock Drawing Mode and draw the same shape three times; cancel it with Esc.
4. Build the Eagle practical: two paragraphs, a cropped, resized, rotated picture wrapped with text, plus shapes — save as Eagle.
