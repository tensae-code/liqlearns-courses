# Lesson plan — Spell Check and Proofing Tools

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Word Processing · **Lesson:** Spell Check and Proofing Tools
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Uganda (2026-11-14) — in the title Word Processing on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Uganda's *Information Communication Technology Textbook — Senior One* (NCDC Prototype, Lower Secondary Curriculum) (student book, with the teacher's guide stocked as evidence), because no existing lesson teaches this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a word processor helps you clean a document before it goes out: find locates any word or phrase in the text, replace swaps it everywhere at once, the spell and grammar checker flags words and sentences it doubts, and a final human proofread catches what no checker can.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on typing and saving a document in a word processor; find, replace, the checkers and proofreading are built inside the lesson from a document with real mistakes in it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Locating and replacing text** — Find jumps to any word or phrase, replace swaps it — once or everywhere — and replace-all is used only when every appearance should change.
2. **Spell check, grammar check and proofreading** — The checkers flag and suggest while the writer decides, and a final human proofread catches the correctly-spelled wrong words the checkers pass.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Locate specific text in a document with Find, and replace text — once, or everywhere with replace-all when every appearance should change.
2. Run the spell checker and the grammar checker, judge their suggestions instead of accepting them blindly, and proofread the document yourself for meaning before it goes out.

## What you need
A computer with a word processor and a typed document with some real mistakes in it to clean.

## If the learner is stuck
Replace-all changed a word that should have stayed → find first and look; replace step by step where unsure, replace-all only when every appearance should change. Checker flags a person's name → its dictionary may not know the name; a flag is a question, not a verdict. Checker passes everything but the letter is still wrong → it cannot see a correctly spelled wrong word; proofread for meaning.
