It has two sub lessons: locating and replacing text first, then the checkers and the final proofread — two distinct sequential parts of cleaning one document, each opening by proving the one before.

Uganda's own way: a slim activity-led prototype — every topic is an "Activity" learners do (assembling a real desktop, managing the Recycle Bin, word-processing a letter as the integration task), under Uganda's revised Lower Secondary Curriculum; the teacher's guide mirrors the learner's book chapter by chapter with teacher preparation, instructions and possible responses for every activity, plus chapter summaries — the facilitator model of the revised curriculum. This lesson keeps that shape: the learner does the thing with a real tool or document in front of them, and the guide's rows are stocked as labelled evidence only.

Fiji's own way (Years 7–8 Computer Education student book): A practical, exercise-driven primary book built around a class "students folder" of set files (work is saved as U<unit>-Ex<n>-<FirstName>); local Fijian names and schools throughout (e.g. Babasiga Secondary School), Internet searching is practised on the "Map of Fiji" with kids' search engines, and a full unit covers computers in Fijian society including Braille keyboards for blind learners.
