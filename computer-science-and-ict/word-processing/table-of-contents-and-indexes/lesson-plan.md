# Lesson plan — Table of Contents and Indexes

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Word Processing · **Lesson:** Table of Contents and Indexes
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — North Macedonia (2026-11-14) — in the title Word Processing on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from North Macedonia's *Informatics* (2026), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A table of contents is Word's automatically built list of a document's styled headings with their page numbers, and an index is its alphabetical list of chosen words with the pages they appear on — both are generated from marks already in the document and can be updated in one command when the document changes.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the North Macedonia book's own order — it follows the book's sections in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Contents — built from headings** — If headings carry **heading styles**, Word can **insert a table of contents** that lists them in order with **page numbers**, indented by level. It is a field, not typed text: after edits, **Update Table** rebuilds it — new headings appear, page numbers correct themselves. A hand-typed contents cannot do that, which is why the book teaches styles first.
2. **Indexes — built from marked words** — An **index** starts by **marking entries**: select a word, mark it (with its main entry wording), and Word plants a hidden mark. Repeat for the words a reader would look up, then **insert the index** where it should stand — usually at the end — and Word gathers every marked word, alphabetises it and lists its pages. Update it the same way after changes.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
