# Table of Contents and Indexes

## Do it first

Long documents need maps. This lesson builds both of Word's maps — the contents at the front and the index at the back — without typing either by hand.

## Sub lesson 1 — Contents — built from headings

If headings carry **heading styles**, Word can **insert a table of contents** that lists them in order with **page numbers**, indented by level. It is a field, not typed text: after edits, **Update Table** rebuilds it — new headings appear, page numbers correct themselves. A hand-typed contents cannot do that, which is why the book teaches styles first.

## Sub lesson 2 — Indexes — built from marked words

An **index** starts by **marking entries**: select a word, mark it (with its main entry wording), and Word plants a hidden mark. Repeat for the words a reader would look up, then **insert the index** where it should stand — usually at the end — and Word gathers every marked word, alphabetises it and lists its pages. Update it the same way after changes.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: A table of contents is Word's automatically built list of a document's styled headings with their page numbers, and an index is its alphabetical list of chosen words with the pages they appear on — both are generated from marks already in the document and can be updated in one command when the document changes. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
