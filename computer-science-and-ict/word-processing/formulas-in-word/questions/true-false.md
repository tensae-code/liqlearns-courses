# Formulas in Word — true-false

Say whether each statement is true or false.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "How is a formula in Word made?" is: Assembled — choose the pattern and fill its boxes**
**Answer: True**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "Name three formula patterns." is: No — it is built from parts**
**Answer: False** — Fraction, power and root (or sum)

**3. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "Is a formula typed like a sentence?" is: No — it is built from parts**
**Answer: True**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "How does a finished formula behave?" is: After tables, the last new tool before printing**
**Answer: False** — As one object — movable and resizable as a whole

**5. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "Where do formulas sit in the Grade 6 Word course?" is: After tables, the last new tool before printing**
**Answer: True**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "Where can a formula sit?" is: What its letters mean**
**Answer: False** — On its own line, or inside the sentence that names it

**7. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "What must the text around a formula say?" is: What its letters mean**
**Answer: True**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "What does the lesson practise on?" is: So they can be printed and handed in**
**Answer: False** — Real sums from mathematics and physics homework

**9. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "Why set homework formulas in Word?" is: So they can be printed and handed in**
**Answer: True**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "What two qualities make a formula read well?" is: Assembled — choose the pattern and fill its boxes**
**Answer: False** — It is placed well and explained
