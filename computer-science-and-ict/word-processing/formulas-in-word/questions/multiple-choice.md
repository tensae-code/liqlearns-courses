# Formulas in Word — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) How is a formula in Word made?**
a) Assembled — choose the pattern and fill its boxes  b) Fraction, power and root (or sum)  c) No — it is built from parts  d) As one object — movable and resizable as a whole
**Answer: a) Assembled — choose the pattern and fill its boxes**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) Name three formula patterns.**
a) No — it is built from parts  b) Fraction, power and root (or sum)  c) As one object — movable and resizable as a whole  d) After tables, the last new tool before printing
**Answer: b) Fraction, power and root (or sum)**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) Is a formula typed like a sentence?**
a) As one object — movable and resizable as a whole  b) After tables, the last new tool before printing  c) No — it is built from parts  d) On its own line, or inside the sentence that names it
**Answer: c) No — it is built from parts**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) How does a finished formula behave?**
a) After tables, the last new tool before printing  b) On its own line, or inside the sentence that names it  c) What its letters mean  d) As one object — movable and resizable as a whole
**Answer: d) As one object — movable and resizable as a whole**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) Where do formulas sit in the Grade 6 Word course?**
a) After tables, the last new tool before printing  b) On its own line, or inside the sentence that names it  c) What its letters mean  d) Real sums from mathematics and physics homework
**Answer: a) After tables, the last new tool before printing**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) Where can a formula sit?**
a) What its letters mean  b) On its own line, or inside the sentence that names it  c) Real sums from mathematics and physics homework  d) So they can be printed and handed in
**Answer: b) On its own line, or inside the sentence that names it**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What must the text around a formula say?**
a) Real sums from mathematics and physics homework  b) So they can be printed and handed in  c) What its letters mean  d) It is placed well and explained
**Answer: c) What its letters mean**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What does the lesson practise on?**
a) So they can be printed and handed in  b) It is placed well and explained  c) Assembled — choose the pattern and fill its boxes  d) Real sums from mathematics and physics homework
**Answer: d) Real sums from mathematics and physics homework**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) Why set homework formulas in Word?**
a) So they can be printed and handed in  b) It is placed well and explained  c) Assembled — choose the pattern and fill its boxes  d) Fraction, power and root (or sum)
**Answer: a) So they can be printed and handed in**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) What two qualities make a formula read well?**
a) Assembled — choose the pattern and fill its boxes  b) It is placed well and explained  c) Fraction, power and root (or sum)  d) No — it is built from parts
**Answer: b) It is placed well and explained**
