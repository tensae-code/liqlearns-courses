## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The formula editor** — Word's **formula** tools (the equation editor) build mathematics from parts: choose the pattern — fraction, power, root, sum — and fill its boxes. A formula is not typed letter by letter like a sentence; it is **assembled**, and it stays one object that can be moved and resized as a whole. The Grade 6 book teaches formulas after tables, as the Word course's last new tool before printing.
2. **Writing mathematics that reads well** — A good formula in a document is **placed and explained**: it sits on its own line or inside the sentence where the text names it, and the text around it says what its letters mean. The pupil's own school subjects are the source — the lesson practises on real sums from mathematics and physics homework, set in Word so they can be printed and handed in.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
