# Formulas in Word

## Do it first

Word is for text — but Grade 6's course ends its climb with formulas inside the document: mathematical writing set properly, with fractions, powers and roots standing as they should.

## Sub lesson 1 — The formula editor

Word's **formula** tools (the equation editor) build mathematics from parts: choose the pattern — fraction, power, root, sum — and fill its boxes. A formula is not typed letter by letter like a sentence; it is **assembled**, and it stays one object that can be moved and resized as a whole. The Grade 6 book teaches formulas after tables, as the Word course's last new tool before printing.

## Sub lesson 2 — Writing mathematics that reads well

A good formula in a document is **placed and explained**: it sits on its own line or inside the sentence where the text names it, and the text around it says what its letters mean. The pupil's own school subjects are the source — the lesson practises on real sums from mathematics and physics homework, set in Word so they can be printed and handed in.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: Assembled — choose the pattern and fill its boxes. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: Fraction, power and root (or sum). Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: On its own line, or inside the sentence that names it. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: What its letters mean. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
