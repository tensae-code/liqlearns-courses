# Making a bulleted list

## Do it first

Type the principles of business ethics as plain lines — honesty, fairness, respect, and the rest. As plain lines they sit flat. By the end of this lesson they will stand in a bulleted list — and, if you go the whole way, bulleted with a symbol of your own choosing, in colour.

## Sub lesson 1 — Making the list

Bullets **outline, arrange and emphasise** text. The worked list is the **"12 Principles of Business Ethics"**. In the book's order: **select the text**; go **Home** and click the **drop-down arrow beside Bullets**; **hover** over styles to see a **live preview** on your text; **click the chosen style** — the text becomes a bulleted list.

[GIF 13345 — hovering bullet styles, the document previewing each]

Bullets are **removed** by selecting the list and clicking the **Bulleted list command again**. Inside the list, **Enter** starts a new bulleted line; **Enter twice** at the end returns to normal formatting. The ruler's indent markers adjust the list's indentation and the gap between bullet and text.

## Sub lesson 2 — Custom bullets — symbols and colours

A bullet need not be the standard dot. Select the list, open the **Bullets drop-down** on Home and choose **Define New Bullet**. For a **symbol bullet**: click **Symbol**, pick a font — **Wingdings and Symbol** are recommended for their useful symbols — choose the symbol and confirm; it now bullets the list. For a **coloured bullet**: reopen Define New Bullet, click **Font**, choose a colour from the **Font Color** drop-down and confirm. A **picture** can likewise be uploaded as a bullet.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion how a bulleted list is made and removed, what Enter does inside one, and how a symbol becomes a bullet in a colour.

## Do It Yourself

1. Make the bulleted list from your own typed principles, then remove the bullets and make it again.
2. Give the list a symbol bullet from Wingdings and set the bullets to a colour.
3. Say what Enter twice does at the end of a list.
