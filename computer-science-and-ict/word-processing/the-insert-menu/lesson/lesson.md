# The Insert Menu

## Do it first

Some things in a document are not typed — they are inserted, whole and ready-made. In this lesson you learn Word's Insert menu, taught by Afghanistan in both Grade 10 and Grade 11.

## Sub lesson 1 — Insert — putting ready-made things in

Everything on the **Insert menu** enters the document at the **insertion point**, whole: you choose the thing, and Word places it for you, correctly made. **Page Numbers** numbers the pages — Word keeps the numbers right even as the document grows. **Date and Time** inserts today's date, typed perfectly by the computer. **Symbol** gives the characters the keyboard does not have. **Break** ends the page where you are and starts a new one, so a chapter can begin at a page top without a row of empty Enter presses that will shift as you edit.

## Sub lesson 2 — Pictures and objects from Insert

**Picture** inserts an image — from the Clip Art collection or from a file — where the insertion point stands; the picture then lives in the document, moving with the text around it. The menu also inserts things like a **text box** and whole **files** — another document's text brought into this one. The thinking behind the menu is one idea: typing is for words you compose; inserting is for things that already exist, made correctly elsewhere — numbers, dates, symbols, pictures. Both Afghanistan books teach this menu as its own lesson, in Grade 10 and again in Grade 11, because a document that only holds typed text is only half a document.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Word's Insert menu puts ready-made things into a document at the insertion point: page numbers, the date, symbols, pictures, and breaks that start a new page. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: At the insertion point. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: Today's date, typed perfectly by the computer. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: Empty Enters shift as you edit; a break holds the new page's start. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: A text box, and another document's text as a file. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
