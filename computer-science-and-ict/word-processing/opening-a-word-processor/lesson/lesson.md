# Opening a word processor

## Do it first

Sit at a computer. Your job: open Microsoft Word. Try any way you can think of. However you did it — or if you got stuck — this lesson now gives you the book's three routes, in the book's order, so you always have a way in.

## Sub lesson 1 — Route 1 — through the Start menu

In the book's order: click the **Start menu**; click **All Programs**; open the **Microsoft Office folder**; click **Microsoft Word 2016**. Follow the GIF: the highlight moves Start → All Programs → Microsoft Office → Word, one click at a time. This route always works, even when the desktop is crowded and the search box is hidden.

## Sub lesson 2 — Route 2 — through Search

Click **Search**. Type the word **word**. From the results, choose **Microsoft Word 2016**. Searching is the fast route when you know the program's name but not where it lives.

## Sub lesson 3 — Route 3 — the desktop shortcut

If a Word shortcut sits on the desktop, **double-click** it — two quick clicks on the icon. Whichever route you took, the same thing waits at the end: **a blank document opens, ready for text.**

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the three routes to open Word, in the book's order, and what opens at the end of each.

## Do It Yourself

1. Open Word three times, once by each route.
2. Write the Start-menu route from memory, all four clicks.
3. Say which route you would use on a computer with a crowded desktop, and why.
