# The Paragraph group — a map of its buttons

## Do it first

Open Word and find the Paragraph group on the Home tab. It is a crowd of small buttons. Guess what each one does — some you can read from their pictures. This lesson names them all, letter by letter, so the crowd becomes a map you can use.

## The list commands

Three buttons make lists. **Bullets** put a dot or mark before each line. **Numbering** counts the lines. **Multilevel List** gives a list levels — lines inside lines. The indent buttons belong here too: **Decrease Indent** and **Increase Indent** move a paragraph out or in, and **Sort** arranges selected text in ascending or descending order.

## The alignment four

Four buttons set how a paragraph's edges line up: **Left** (Ctrl+L), **Centre** (Ctrl+E), **Right** (Ctrl+R) and **Justify** (Ctrl+J). One paragraph, one click — the alignment lessons ahead teach each one by doing it.

## Seeing, spacing, colouring, framing

**Show/Hide** (Ctrl+*) shows or hides the paragraph marks, so you can see the hidden structure of the text. **Line and Paragraph Spacing** sets the gaps between lines and between paragraphs. **Shading** colours a line's background, margin to margin. **Border** draws a frame around text. Every one of these buttons gets its own doing-lesson later in this title — this map is where they all live.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the Paragraph group's commands in families: the list three, the alignment four, and the seeing-spacing-colouring-framing four — with one job for each.

## Do It Yourself

1. Name the four alignment buttons and their shortcuts from memory.
2. Point to the button you would press to see the paragraph marks, and say its shortcut.
3. Say which button colours a line's background from margin to margin.
