# Footnotes and Endnotes

## Do it first

A text sometimes needs to whisper an aside or swear to a source without breaking its own sentence. Word's answer is the note system — and Mongolia's Grade 9 strand teaches its two houses and the machine that numbers them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Footnote against endnote — two houses for notes

A **footnote** stands at the **foot of the page** where its mark appears: the reader glances down, reads, and returns — suited to quick explanations and sources that serve the moment. An **endnote** is **gathered at the document's end** (or a section's): the reader travels to it when they choose — suited to the collected references of a whole work. The text itself carries only the small raised **mark**; the note's words live in their house. The writer's choice is a courtesy decision: how far should the reader travel for this aside?

## Sub lesson 2 — Automatic numbering — the machine keeps the count

Notes are **numbered automatically**: insert a note and Word gives it the next number, plants its mark in the text and its space in the house. Delete a note and the rest **renumber**; move a paragraph and its note travels and takes its new number. No hand-kept list survives editing — that is the point. Mongolia's booklet-and-report pupils use the system for the duty the ethics lessons taught: sources named where they are used, numbered by the machine, true after every change.
