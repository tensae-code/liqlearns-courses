# Lesson plan — End-of-Year Project — Food Chain Document

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Word Processing · **Lesson:** End-of-Year Project — Food Chain Document
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Bhutan (2026-11-14) — in the title Word Processing on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Bhutan's student books ("Literacy with ICT", Royal Education Council, Bhutan, Paro), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A food chain — who eats whom
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Building the food-chain document** — The **food-chain document** presents a food chain — who eats whom — in Word: the chain shown in a **table**, **pictures** inserted for the living things, a **header and footer** carrying the title and page numbers, and the online sources **cited** at the end. Every part is a chapter of the year used once, for real: tables, images, headers, protection and citation in a single finished document.
2. **Agreeing, filing and reporting it** — The project is **agreed in Google Classroom** first — the group settles its chain and its parts where the class collaborates. The finished document is **password-protected**, saved into the group's **folders**, and **reported to the teacher by email** — the report saying what was made and where it is filed. The project is the year's proof: Word, files, internet and collaboration in one hand-in.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
