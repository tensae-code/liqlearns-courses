# Lesson plan — Project — Making a Small Book in Word

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Word Processing · **Lesson:** Project — Making a Small Book in Word
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Mongolia (2026-11-14) — in the title Word Processing on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Mongolia's student books (Ministry of Education, Culture and Science(s) of Mongolia, Ulaanbaatar), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree and live lesson content were checked for the slug and for near matches before building.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Planning a small book as a Word project — fixing its purpose and audience, planning its structure, and gathering the information it will hold, before a page is typed: the opening of Mongolia's 'Cherish the Water' booklet project
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Purpose, audience, structure — the plan before the pages** — A **book project** begins with three decisions. **Purpose**: what the booklet is for — Mongolia's class booklet exists to teach its readers to value water. **Audience**: who will read it — pupils, parents, the school — whose reading level and interests shape every page. **Structure**: the booklet's parts in order — cover, contents, sections — sketched before any typing, so each gathered fact already has its home. A book planned this way is written once; a book begun by typing is rewritten all the way through.
2. **Gathering the information** — With the plan fixed, the class **gathers the information**: facts, texts and pictures for each section, collected from books, the internet and the pupils' own knowledge — and **checked** as it is gathered, because a printed book cannot be corrected after the run. Each gathered piece is marked with the section it belongs to. Gathering is the last of the planning lessons and the first of the making ones: from here the unit proceeds — type it into Word, set its pages, dress it with pictures and tables, and print the booklet — each later lesson a stage of this one project.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
