# Video script — Text as Characters, Words, Sentences and Paragraphs

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

The smallest piece of text is the **character**: one letter, digit, sign or space — everything that one key on the keyboard, or one code, stands for. Characters join into **words**: the runs of characters between spaces, each carrying a piece of meaning. This is how the computer 'understands' text — not as a picture of a page, but as a counted line of characters it can copy, search, count and change, one character at a time.
Words join into **sentences**: complete statements closed by their ending marks. Sentences join into **paragraphs**: the blocks of text, separated by new lines, that each carry one stretch of thought. The four levels — **character, word, sentence, paragraph** — are the map every later word-processing lesson uses: formatting acts on characters, alignment on paragraphs, and the learner who knows which level a tool works on knows what it will change before touching it.
