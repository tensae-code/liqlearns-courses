# Writing Good Visual Basic Applications — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) Who are a good application's two judges?**
a) Its user, and the next programmer who reads its code  b) Every control labelled so its job is plain  c) Buttons do what their labels promise, in the places users expect  d) It asks only for what it needs, warns before anything is lost, and reports in the user's words
**Answer: a) Its user, and the next programmer who reads its code**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What does clear mean at the surface?**
a) Buttons do what their labels promise, in the places users expect  b) Every control labelled so its job is plain  c) It asks only for what it needs, warns before anything is lost, and reports in the user's words  d) The user's — not the programmer's pride
**Answer: b) Every control labelled so its job is plain**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What does predictable mean?**
a) It asks only for what it needs, warns before anything is lost, and reports in the user's words  b) The user's — not the programmer's pride  c) Buttons do what their labels promise, in the places users expect  d) Meaningful names for controls and variables
**Answer: c) Buttons do what their labels promise, in the places users expect**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What does kind mean?**
a) The user's — not the programmer's pride  b) Meaningful names for controls and variables  c) Where a choice needs explaining  d) It asks only for what it needs, warns before anything is lost, and reports in the user's words
