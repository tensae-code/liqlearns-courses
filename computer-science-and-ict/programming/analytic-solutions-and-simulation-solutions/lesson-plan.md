## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The analytic solution — exact, when it exists** — An **analytic solution** derives the model's answer **exactly, by mathematics**: a formula which, given the data, gives the result — the quadratic formula for the equation, the compound-interest formula for the loan. Its virtues are total: **exact** for every input, **instant** to compute, and **insightful** — the formula itself shows how the answer depends on each datum. Its limit is existence: most real models (weather, epidemics, traffic) have **no formula** — the mathematics closes, and the analytic road ends.
2. **The simulation solution — the model enacted** — A **simulation solution** makes the computer **play the model**: start from the data, advance step by step by the model's rules — day by day of an epidemic, car by car through a junction — and read the answer from the play. It answers where formulas cannot, at the price of being **one run's story**: change the starting values slightly and the story may change; randomness in the model means runs must be **repeated and averaged**. The chapter's judgement: analytic where a formula lives; simulation where behaviour must be watched — and each road checks the other where both exist.
