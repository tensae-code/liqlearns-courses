# Sets and Venn Diagrams in Computing

## Do it first

Sets and Venn diagrams in computing — collections of distinct things, drawn as overlapping circles: members, unions, intersections, and why a thinking machine needs them

## Sub lesson 1 — The set — a collection with no duplicates

A **set** (himpunan) is a collection of distinct things, called its **members** or elements, where order does not matter and nothing appears twice: the set of pupils in the chess club, the set of files in a folder. Indonesia's Grade 8 computational-thinking chapter teaches sets because programs think in them constantly — a list with duplicates removed is a set, a search result is a set, and 'which of these are also in that' is the question half of data work asks. A set is named by its rule or by listing its members inside braces.

In your mind's eye: the set — a collection with no duplicates.

## Sub lesson 2 — Venn diagrams — sets you can see

A **Venn diagram** draws sets as **overlapping circles**, so their relations are visible at a glance: the overlap of two circles is the **intersection** (members in both), the whole coloured region is the **union** (members in either), and the part of one circle outside the other is the **difference**. With three circles the diagram sorts a class into eight regions without a word of algebra. The diagram is the lesson's gift to every later topic: conditions combined with AND and OR are intersections and unions wearing program clothes, and the pupil who has drawn them has already understood both.
