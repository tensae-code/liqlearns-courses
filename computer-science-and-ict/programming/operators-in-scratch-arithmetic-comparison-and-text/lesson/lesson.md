# Operators in Scratch — Arithmetic, Comparison and Text

## Do it first

A mean is not guessed: add every value, divide by how many there are. Palestine’s operator lessons give Scratch its arithmetic — and its comparisons, its text-joining, and operations nested inside a say block so the sprite speaks the answer.

## Sub lesson 1 — Arithmetic, comparison and joining text

Grade 8’s operations lesson lists the kit: **arithmetic + − × ÷**, **remainder**, **rounding** and **square root**; **comparisons** that answer true or false; and **joining text** into one string. Operations **nest inside say blocks**, so a sprite can speak a computed answer, not just a typed one.

## Sub lesson 2 — The arithmetic mean — a sum that accumulates

Grade 9 builds the **arithmetic mean**: a **sum variable accumulates each new value**, a count keeps how many values came, and **mean = sum ÷ count**. The program says the result **joined with words**, and the lesson extends it with a **STOP sprite** that ends the entering of values.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Operator blocks compute: arithmetic (+ − × ÷, remainder, rounding, square root), comparisons, and joining text — operations can nest inside other blocks, and a sum variable accumulating each new value gives the arithmetic mean as sum ÷ count. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
