**Acceptance count: taught in 4 countries (Palestine, Cuba, Bhutan, Kuwait).**

Placement choice: this lesson was built new for the ICT expansion (Palestine, 2026-11-14) because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree was checked for the slug and for near matches before building. The placements file named this slug directly (NEW LESSON NEEDED — programming/operators-in-scratch-arithmetic-comparison-and-text). It sits in the Programming title (programming/), following the book's own order.

Palestine’s own way (Grade 8, Programming): Grade 8 Scratch is organised around making things — Unit 1 programs drawings and ornaments (the unit opens with the ornaments of Jerusalem’s churches and the Dome of the Rock), Unit 2 weaves interactive stories with sound and dialogue (including a dialogue project on love of the homeland), Unit 3 designs real games with variables, operators and sensing.

Palestine’s own way (Grade 9, Programming): Grade 9 is a full applications course in Scratch — after a basics unit it builds real things: a photo editor, a video player, a phone PIN lock, a bank account, a library sign-up/login, an alarm clock, an elevator, a task list, a wall clock that can ring at prayer times. Its own workarounds are content too: Scratch 2 cannot type Arabic in its paint editor, so Arabic text is written in MS Paint, saved as a picture and uploaded; the news-ticker lesson’s breaking-news example is the Ministry’s own decision to teach programming in Grades 5–9; the letter-counting lesson’s example sentence says the Dome of the Rock stands in Jerusalem.

Cuba's own way (Grade 8): Taught entirely in free software — LibreOffice Impress for presentations and LibreOffice Calc for spreadsheets; web work means designing and editing HTML pages with a plain text editor, and Scratch continues with arithmetic and logic operator blocks.

Bhutan's own way (Class VI, Literacy with ICT student book): School computing runs on Google's G Suite for Education — email is Gmail on the school account, collaboration is Google Classroom, and the end-of-year food-chain project is agreed in Classroom, built in Word, password-protected, cited, filed in folders and reported to the teacher by email.

Bhutan's own way (Class VII, Literacy with ICT student book): Social media is taught hands-on — learners create a Facebook account, post with audience settings, and follow netiquette and an Acceptable Use Policy; backup is taught to Google Drive, and the end-of-year experience is shared on Facebook.

Kuwait's own way (Grade 6 Digital Intelligence Term 1): the first intermediate year is entirely creative computing — a full advanced-Scratch ladder (sensing, variables, broadcasting, lists, cloning, custom blocks, AI extensions) framed by Kuwaiti themes (volunteer work, famous mosques, the governorates), followed by a complete virtual-robotics unit in Open Roberta Lab that ends in a Champions Challenge.
