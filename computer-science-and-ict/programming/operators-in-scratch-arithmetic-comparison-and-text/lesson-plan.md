# Lesson plan — Operators in Scratch — Arithmetic, Comparison and Text

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Programming · **Lesson:** Operators in Scratch — Arithmetic, Comparison and Text
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Palestine (2026-11-14) — in the title Programming on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Palestine's Grade 8 and Grade 9 Programming student books, because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Operator blocks compute: arithmetic (+ − × ÷, remainder, rounding, square root), comparisons, and joining text — operations can nest inside other blocks, and a sum variable accumulating each new value gives the arithmetic mean as sum ÷ count.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Arithmetic, comparison and joining text** — Grade 8’s operations lesson lists the kit: **arithmetic + − × ÷**, **remainder**, **rounding** and **square root**; **comparisons** that answer true or false; and **joining text** into one string. Operations **nest inside say blocks**, so a sprite can speak a computed answer, not just a typed one.
2. **The arithmetic mean — a sum that accumulates** — Grade 9 builds the **arithmetic mean**: a **sum variable accumulates each new value**, a count keeps how many values came, and **mean = sum ÷ count**. The program says the result **joined with words**, and the lesson extends it with a **STOP sprite** that ends the entering of values.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — A mean is not guessed: add every value, divide by how many there are. Palestine’s operator lessons give Scratch its arithmetic — and its comparisons, its text-joining, and operations nested inside a say block so the sprite speaks the answer. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of arithmetic, comparison and joining text as the lesson describes it — the student studies the picture and names what each part shows.
   - ADD GIF: a GIF of the arithmetic mean — a sum that accumulates in action — the student watches once, then says what changed and why.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the lesson's key names and definitions aloud, in the order the lesson gives them — the student listens once through, then reads the same lines aloud along with the voice. (Audio is a described slot in this build; no audio file is made.)
