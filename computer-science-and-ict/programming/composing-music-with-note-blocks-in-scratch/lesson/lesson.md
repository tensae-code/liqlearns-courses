# Composing Music with Note Blocks in Scratch

## Do it first

A sprite can sing its own tune — if someone writes it the notes. Palestine’s Grade 8 sound lesson goes past playing clips: choose an instrument, play notes for beats, rest, and loop a tune of your own.

## Sub lesson 1 — Set instrument, play note, rest

The composing blocks work in threes: **set instrument** chooses the voice, **play note for beats** sounds a chosen note for a chosen length, and **rest** holds silence for its beats. A melody is these blocks in order — notes and rests together make the rhythm.

[Picture — set instrument, play note, rest]

## Sub lesson 2 — Looping a tune

A short composed sequence becomes a tune when it is **looped**: the same notes return, the listener recognises the pattern, and small changes between repeats can grow the tune. Composing is programming with the ears — every block is heard the moment the program runs.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Music is composed in Scratch with note blocks: set the instrument, play a note for a number of beats, rest for beats in silence — and loop the sequence to make a tune that repeats. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
