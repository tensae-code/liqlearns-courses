# Lesson plan — Descriptive Naming of Sprites, Costumes and Backdrops

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Programming · **Lesson:** Descriptive Naming of Sprites, Costumes and Backdrops
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Finland (2026-11-14) — in the title Programming on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Finland's *Ensiaskeleet ohjelmointiin* (teacher guide, Opettajan opas, Versio 2.0 — teacher notes only), because no existing lesson teaches this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Good programmers name things so the names describe them: sprites, costumes and backdrops carry names that say what they are — and level backdrops are numbered (Maze 1, Maze 2) — so scripts read clearly to anyone, including yourself next month. This lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence only.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on having made sprites, costumes and backdrops in Scratch; naming as a practice is built inside the lesson from the teacher guide's notes.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Adding and Drawing Your Own Scratch Sprite in Programming. Recommended, never forced — a learner may land on this lesson directly, and it stands alone.
- Finland's own way is kept: this placement sits in Finland's two-part Scratch path — Part 1 moves towards the interactive-story project and Part 2 towards the self-built maze game, in the Luetaan (Let's read) then Ohjelmoidaan (Let's program) rhythm, with pair work, reflection and peer showing built in.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Rename a sprite, a costume and a backdrop with descriptive names.
2. Number a series, like level backdrops, in its names.
3. Say what goes wrong in a project whose names describe nothing.

## What you need
A computer with Scratch.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Open an old project. Its sprites are called Sprite1, Sprite4 and Sprite7; its backdrops are backdrop1 and backdrop2; a costume is called costume3. Which one is the enemy? Which backdrop is level two? Even the maker cannot tell any more — and neither can their code. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of a sprite list renamed properly — Player, Chaser, Goal — beside backdrop thumbnails named Maze 1, Maze 2, Maze 3, with a script block switching to Maze 2 reading like a sentence — the student studies the picture and says, for each part of it, which part of the lesson it shows.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the lesson's key names and definitions aloud, in the order the lesson gives them — the student listens once through, then reads the same lines aloud along with the voice. (Audio is a described slot in this build; no audio file is made.)

## If the learner is stuck
Cannot tell sprites apart → rename by job, not by order of creation. Numbered names out of sequence → rename the set consistently before the project grows; a gap in numbers invites wrong switches.
