# What Programming Is — Giving Instructions to a Computer

## Do it first

Tell a friend how to make a sandwich, step by step, while they do ONLY what you say — no more, no less. If a step is missing or unclear, the sandwich goes wrong. Keep that feeling — a computer is a listener like that, only stricter.

## Sub lesson 1 — Programming is giving instructions — the baker story

Programming, also called coding, is giving instructions to a computer. The book's baker story makes the point: a baker follows a recipe exactly. If the recipe says to put the cake in the oven, the baker does that; the recipe must say everything, in the right order, because the baker in the story does not guess the missing steps. A computer is like that baker. It follows instructions **exactly** — it does not know what you meant, only what you told it. That is why programming is careful work: a missing step, a wrong order or a wrong number gives a wrong result, and the fault is in the instructions, not in the computer.

## Sub lesson 2 — The words: program, code, command, script, sprite

Five words carry the whole of Scratch programming. A **program** is the whole set of instructions that makes something happen. **Code** is those instructions written in the computer's form. A **command**, in Scratch a **block**, is one instruction — move, say, turn. A **script** is blocks joined together in order, so the computer runs them one after another. A **sprite** is the character or object on the stage that the script tells what to do — the cat is the first sprite everyone meets. When you build blocks into a script for a sprite, you have written a program, in code.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: programming is giving instructions to a computer, the computer follows them exactly like the baker in the story follows the recipe, and a script of blocks is how you give a sprite its instructions. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
