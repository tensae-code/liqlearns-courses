# Reading an Algorithm and Saying What It Does — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What do you note first in an unfamiliar algorithm?**
**Answer: Its inputs — what it asks for at the start**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What is a trace?**
**Answer: Writing down each variable's value as it changes, step by step**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) Which values should a first trace use?**
**Answer: Small, easy values**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What two structures must you note as you read?**
**Answer: Every decision and its branch condition, and every loop and what stops it**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) Reading without a trace is…**
**Answer: Guessing**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) How do you find the task an algorithm performs?**
**Answer: Compare what went in with what came out, across several traces**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) How should the task be said?**
**Answer: In one plain sentence naming the inputs and the result**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) How do you check your sentence about the task?**
**Answer: Run one more value and see whether the algorithm does exactly what it promises**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) Why trace more than one input?**
**Answer: One trace shows one case; the pattern across traces shows the task**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) What must your sentence not claim?**
**Answer: More — or less — than the algorithm actually does**
