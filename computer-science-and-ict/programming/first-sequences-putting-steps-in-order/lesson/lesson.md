# First Sequences — Putting Steps in Order

## Do it first

Act out three steps in order — stand up, turn around, sit down. Now swap the last two steps and act them again. The steps are the same, but the result changed: the order is part of the instruction.

## First Sequences — Putting Steps in Order

A **sequence** is a row of actions done one after another, in order. Bulgaria's Grade 3 book starts programming here, away from the screen, with code.org Course 1 puzzles: a character must reach a goal, and you build the path out of single steps — forward, turn, forward — placed in the right order. Each step is done exactly once, in its turn; nothing is skipped and nothing jumps ahead. If the order is wrong, the character ends in the wrong place, even though every step in the row was a good step. So the first programming skill is not typing or blocks: it is looking at a job, breaking it into steps, and putting the steps in the order that works.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: A sequence is actions in an order: do the steps one after another, in the order they are written, and the order itself decides what happens. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: build a sequence of steps that moves a character to a goal. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: say why the order of the steps matters as much as the steps themselves. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: fix a sequence that ends in the wrong place by reordering its steps. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
