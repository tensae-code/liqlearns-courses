# Lesson plan — Scratch Looks Blocks — Show, Hide and Say

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Programming · **Lesson:** Scratch Looks Blocks — Show, Hide and Say
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Cuba (2026-11-14) — in the title Programming on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Cuba's Mi Mundo Digital / Informática student books and teacher-side books (Ministerio de Educación / Editorial Pueblo y Educación), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Scratch's **looks blocks** govern how the sprite appears and speaks: **say** and **think** put words in bubbles, **show** and **hide** put the sprite on the stage or take it off, and costume, backdrop and size blocks change its look.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Say and think — the sprite speaks** — The **say** block puts the words written in it into a **speech bubble** over the sprite, for a set time or until it is changed — the sprite is heard by everyone on stage. The **think** block puts words in a **thought bubble**: the audience reads the sprite's mind, but the other characters do not hear it. Choosing between them is choosing who knows what: say it aloud to the scene, or think it silently for the audience. Cuba's Grade 5 unit names decir and pensar — say and think — as the first looks blocks a pupil owns.
2. **Show, hide and change the look** — **Show** puts the sprite on the stage; **hide** takes it off — still in the project, with its scripts, only unseen, waiting for its entrance. The look itself changes too: **switch costume** gives the sprite another of its looks, **switch backdrop** changes the stage behind it, and **change size** grows or shrinks it. Cuba's Grade 5 guide lists the full family in one line — decir, pensar, mostrar, esconder, disfraz, fondo, tamaño: say, think, show, hide, costume, backdrop, size.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
