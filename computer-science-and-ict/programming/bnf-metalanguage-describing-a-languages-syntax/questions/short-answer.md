# BNF Metalanguage — Describing a Language's Syntax — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What is a metalanguage?**
**Answer: A language used to describe another language**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What does BNF stand for, and what does it describe?**
**Answer: Backus–Naur Form — it describes a language's syntax**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What does ::= mean?**
**Answer: 'Is defined as' — it joins a construction's name to its definition**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What are nonterminals?**
**Answer: Constructions in angle brackets that unfold further, like <identifier>**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) What are terminals?**
**Answer: The language's own symbols, standing as themselves**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What does | separate in a production?**
**Answer: Alternatives — the choices for one construction**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) How is BNF read?**
**Answer: By unfolding — replacing nonterminals by alternatives until only terminals remain**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What does the recursive <identifier> production allow?**
**Answer: Names of any length — a finite grammar describing infinitely many texts**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) Give the digit production.**
**Answer: <digit> ::= 0 | 1 | … | 9**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) What is a production?**
**Answer: One definition — a name, ::=, and its definition**
