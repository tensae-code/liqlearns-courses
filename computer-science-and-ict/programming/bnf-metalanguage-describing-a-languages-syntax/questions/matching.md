# BNF Metalanguage — Describing a Language's Syntax — matching

Match each item on the left with the one it belongs to on the right.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What is a metalanguage?** → **A language used to describe another language**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What does BNF stand for, and what does it describe?** → **Backus–Naur Form — it describes a language's syntax**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What does ::= mean?** → **'Is defined as' — it joins a construction's name to its definition**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What are nonterminals?** → **Constructions in angle brackets that unfold further, like <identifier>**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) What are terminals?** → **The language's own symbols, standing as themselves**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What does | separate in a production?** → **Alternatives — the choices for one construction**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) How is BNF read?** → **By unfolding — replacing nonterminals by alternatives until only terminals remain**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What does the recursive <identifier> production allow?** → **Names of any length — a finite grammar describing infinitely many texts**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) Give the digit production.** → **<digit> ::= 0 | 1 | … | 9**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) What is a production?** → **One definition — a name, ::=, and its definition**
