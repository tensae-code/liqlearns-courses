# A Branching Algorithm — Making Choices

## Do it first

Decide how to travel today: if it rains, take the bus; if not, walk. You just ran a branching algorithm — a question, and two different ways onward.

## A Branching Algorithm — Making Choices

Grade 4's Scratch work began with the branching block; this lesson teaches the **branched algorithm (разклонен алгоритъм)** behind it, through the book's journey theme — **"Обичам пътешествията" (I love journeys)**. A straight algorithm does every step in order. A branching algorithm meets a **question with a yes/no answer**, and the road **splits**: follow the yes-way or the no-way, then rejoin or end. Writing one means choosing the question carefully — it must be answerable yes or no — and making sure **both ways** are written, sensible, and end somewhere. The learner plans branched journeys on paper first: a decision about weather, time or road, with two honest routes onward.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: A branching algorithm does not march straight: at a question it splits — one way if the answer is yes, the other way if no — like choosing a journey by the weather. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: say what makes an algorithm branching. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: write a branching algorithm whose question can be answered yes or no. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: check that both ways of a branch are written and end somewhere. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
