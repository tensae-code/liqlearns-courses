# Tracking an Algorithm

## Do it first

You cannot watch an algorithm run in your head all at once — but you can walk beside it, one step at a time, writing what changes. This lesson learns that walking: tracking.

## Sub lesson 1 — What tracking is

**Tracking** (tracing) an algorithm is working through it **step by step with chosen values**, recording the state after each step — usually in a **tracking table**: a row per step, a column per variable. The tracker plays the computer honestly: no skipping, no 'and then it works out' — each step's arithmetic is done and written. Tracking is how algorithms are **checked before a computer is touched**, and how a fault is cornered: the row where the table first goes wrong is the fault's neighbourhood.

In your mind's eye: what tracking is.

## Sub lesson 2 — Tracking loops and conditions

Loops are where tracking earns its keep: the table grows a row per **turn**, the counter and accumulator changing in writing, until the condition fails and the walk ends — the loop's whole story visible at a glance. Conditions are tracked by marking the **branch taken** at each meeting. Jordan's Grade 7 algorithms unit teaches tracking beside writing because the two are one craft seen from two sides: the writer composes steps the tracker can follow; the tracker reads steps the writer must answer for.

In your mind's eye: tracking loops and conditions.
