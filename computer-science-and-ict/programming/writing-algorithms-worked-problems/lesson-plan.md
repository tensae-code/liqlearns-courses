# Lesson plan — Writing algorithms — worked problems

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Programming · **Lesson:** Writing algorithms — worked problems
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 6 — Fundamentals of Programming (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** five problems solved in full (teacher guide — evidence): the area of a triangle two ways, the greatest of three numbers, a prime check that counts divisors, the roots of a quadratic equation by its discriminant, and RESULT = (U + V²) / W.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the whole unit — algorithms, flowcharts, loops and pseudocode. It is this unit's worked-problem practice. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or in steps — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Writing algorithms — add, decide, loop, and Loops in flowcharts. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Solve triangle area from base/height and from three sides via the semi-perimeter.
2. Find the greatest of three numbers by chained comparison into a largest variable.
3. Run the prime check: count exact divisors from 2 to N; zero means prime.
4. Branch a quadratic solution on the discriminant b² − 4ac.
5. Compute RESULT = (U + V²) / W in the right order.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Counts 1 and N as divisors in the prime check → the key's loop runs from 2 to N and counts what it finds there; a count of zero inside that loop is the prime signal — follow the key exactly.
- Branches the quadratic before computing D → D comes first; the branching is ON D. Formula after the branch is chosen.
