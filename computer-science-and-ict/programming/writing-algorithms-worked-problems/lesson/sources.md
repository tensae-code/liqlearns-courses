**Acceptance count: taught in 8 countries (Ethiopia, Palestine, Sudan, Bhutan, Malaysia, Greece, South Sudan, Pakistan).** The worked solutions stand on teacher-guide evidence (the guide's §6.8 key); the problems themselves are the student book's own review set.

Book error taught corrected: the teacher guide's §6.8 key defines an algorithm as a sequence of “ambiguous” step-by-step instructions (printed p. 116) — the opposite of both books' wording everywhere else. The lessons teach **unambiguous**, and the prime check and quadratic solutions above follow the key's worked steps with that word corrected.

Mauritania's own way (Grade 9): The final secondary year is five practical blocks in 30-minute fiches — a full PowerPoint sequence, internet ethics, a first formal algorithms unit (variables, conditions, loops, writing and testing algorithms, no named programming language), simple networks, and email with attachments — each block closed by evaluation fiches.

Saudi Arabia's own way (Grade 4, Digital Skills): the primary Digital Skills course (Binary Logic) is built as three units per term with the same guide pattern as the intermediate books (outcomes, important points, steps, answers, unit project, "Test Yourself"), files on the Ain platform, and a strong Arabic/English bilingual-typing strand — pupils switch the keyboard between Arabic and English with Alt+Shift and learn text direction, hamza and Arabic punctuation on the keyboard alongside Word skills; Scratch is introduced in Term 1 through algorithms from daily life. This citation is PARTIAL — Term 1 only. (teacher guide — evidence)

Palestine’s own way (Grade 5, Programming): programming is taught entirely through the offline Hour of Code Minecraft adventures (code.org) — block “bricks” snapped together to guide Steve/Alex; every puzzle is first planned on squared paper, run, checked against the success screen, and shortened; the word “algorithm” is introduced through al-Khwarizmi.

Sudan's own way (Grade 11): a university-style secondary text (single named author, Dr Awad Haj Ali of the University of Gezira) that teaches systems work before code — file types and file security in a floppy-disk/UPS world, a complete spare-parts-factory system as the running example, the algorithm named for al-Khwarizmi, desk-checking with real sales data, and then a full BASIC course (numbered lines, READ/DATA, GOSUB, DEF FN) with payroll and mean/variance programs — data processing taught as a school subject in its own right.

Sudan's own way (Grade 9): the whole Grade 9 book is programming — concepts and Pascal-style examples first, then algorithms and flowcharts, then a full Scratch course ending in a designed project. The Bakht Al-Ruda ICT series runs in the same pattern from Grade 4 to Grade 9.

Bhutan's own way (Class VI, Literacy with ICT student book): School computing runs on Google's G Suite for Education — email is Gmail on the school account, collaboration is Google Classroom, and the end-of-year food-chain project is agreed in Classroom, built in Word, password-protected, cited, filed in folders and reported to the teacher by email.

Malaysia's own way (Grade 10, SK Tingkatan 4): the upper-secondary Sains Komputer (SK — Computer Science) elective switches language to Java (NetBeans) and is organised as three big project-style chapters — Programming, Relational Databases (Access, ERD, normalisation to 3NF, macros/switchboard, a full SDLC database project), and Human-Computer Interaction with screen-design prototyping and quantitative evaluation (prototypes built in Java NetBeans).

Greece's own way (Grade 12, Anaptyxi Efarmogon Student Workbook — Tetradio Mathiti): the workbook is the practical half of AEPP — every main-book chapter 1-10 is reworked as graded examples and activities (medium/advanced marked) drawn from daily life and other subjects, expressed in ΓΛΩΣΣΑ and also in real lab languages (QuickBasic, Turbo Pascal, Visual Basic, Delphi), closing with self-assessment tests and printed answers.

South Sudan's own way (Grade 11 S3 Student): Year 3 is creation and programming: learners build and run a real personal website/blog end-to-end in WordPress (hosting, domain, themes, posts, legal issues and comment moderation), study social networks platform by platform (LinkedIn, Google+ and the Students Circle Network, with South Sudanese business use), then learn programming through data types, arrays and structures in C/C++ and writing programs in Notepad, closing on ethical conduct and intellectual property.


Pakistan's own way (Grade 6 Computer Science 6): every unit opens with Students Learning Outcomes, is dotted with "Do you Know?", "Extra Bit!" and "Tips" boxes, and closes with Summary, MCQ/short/detailed exercises, project-based and activity-based questions, with an answers key at the back. Digital skills (Windows desktop, file management, Paint 3D, a typing tutor) are taught hands-on BEFORE programming; digital citizenship is framed through Pakistani citizenship first; the entrepreneurship unit teaches from Pakistani founders (Foodpanda/EatOye, Shopsy.pk, Zero Makeup, Investors Lounge).

Pakistan's own way (Grade 7 Computer Science 7): Grade 7 opens with emerging technologies and a deep ports-and-peripherals hardware treatment, then teaches email all the way to DKIM/SPF/DMARC authentication and POP3/IMAP/SMTP protocols; binary data representation (ASCII/Unicode, RGB colour, sound sampling) is taught inside the Programming unit, and entrepreneurship becomes business-plan craft — promotion, value proposition, quality assurance, SEO and platform business models, with Twitter and Snapchat origin stories.

Pakistan's own way (Grade 9 Computer Science 9): Grade 9 is a full problem-solving course before any coding — candid solutions, the 5 Ws of problem analysis, test data in five types, trace tables, verification vs validation — then number systems carried through to Boolean algebra with truth tables, networks taught through the postal-system analogy for TCP/IP layers, a data-and-privacy unit built on Code.org cipher widgets, NADRA and Pakistan's NR3C cybercrime centre, and a first website built page by page as "Pakistan.html".

Pakistan's own way (Grade 10 Computer Science 10): Grade 10 is a complete first course in C, taught in Code::Blocks: environment and program structure first, then user interaction (printf/scanf), conditional logic, data and repetition (arrays and for-loops), and functions — every idea carried by short English example programs with Urdu explanation, and each unit closing with Programming Time practice (salary, electricity-bill and discount programs from Pakistani everyday life).

Pakistan's own way (Grade 12 Computer Science 12): the older-curriculum Grade 12 book is two courses in one binding: seven chapters of database theory and Microsoft Access practice (normalization worked to 3NF with Pakistani college examples, NADRA and CRICKINFO as the distributed-database examples) followed by seven chapters of C in the Turbo C++ IDE, ending in file handling and strings — the contacts-file programs (names and phone numbers separated by "!") carry the whole second half's examples.
