# An Algorithm Is a Clear Plan

## Do it first

A recipe is a plan for cooking: step 1, step 2, step 3 — follow them and the dish comes out right. A plan like that has a special name. This lesson teaches the algorithm: a clear plan of steps in order.

## An Algorithm Is a Clear Plan

An **algorithm** is a **clear plan** for finishing a task: steps written **in order**, each step telling exactly one thing to do.

A recipe is an algorithm for a dish. Directions to a friend's house are an algorithm for a walk. The hand-washing sequence from the last lesson becomes an algorithm when it is written as a clear plan that anyone can follow.

An algorithm must be **clear**: no step may be a guess. 'Add some water' is not clear; 'add one cup of water' is clear. It must be **complete**: every needed step is there. And it must **end**: after the last step, the task is finished. Whoever follows a good algorithm carefully gets the same result every time — that is the point of the plan. Later, computers will follow algorithms too — but the idea belongs to everyday life first.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: An algorithm is a clear plan: a set of steps in order that tells exactly what to do to finish a task — so clear that anyone following it gets the same result. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: say what an algorithm is, in your own words.
2. Show it, don't just say it: tell a clear step from an unclear step, and fix the unclear one.
3. Show it, don't just say it: write a short algorithm of 3–5 clear steps for an everyday task.
