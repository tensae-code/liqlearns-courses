# Instructions — Step-by-Step Orders

## Do it first

Before the word 'program' is allowed, Moldova's Grade 1 book teaches the word underneath it. An instruction is an order — and this lesson is about giving orders so clear that whoever follows them cannot go wrong.

## Sub lesson 1 — An instruction is one clear order

An **instruction** is an order that tells an executor **exactly one thing to do**: 'take the red pencil', 'open the door', 'take two steps forward'. A good instruction is **clear** (the executor knows what to do), **exact** (how much, where, which one) and **doable** (the executor really can do it). 'Bring me something nice' fails all three tests; 'bring me the blue book from the table' passes them. Instructions are the bricks of every algorithm and every program in this course.

In your mind's eye: an instruction is one clear order.

## Sub lesson 2 — Instructions are not only pictures

The book's second point surprises its six-year-olds: instructions do not live only in **pictures**. A picture sequence can show the steps of making a sandwich, but instructions also come as **words spoken**, **words written**, **signs and arrows**, and **sounds** — a whistle in a game is an instruction to stop. Whatever its form, an instruction keeps its nature: one order, for one executor, to be carried out exactly. Later lessons will write instructions a computer can follow; the form changes, the nature does not.

In your mind's eye: instructions are not only pictures.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: An order that tells an executor exactly one thing to do. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: It must be clear, exact and doable. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: It is not clear or exact — the executor cannot know what is wanted. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: 'Bring me the blue book from the table'. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
