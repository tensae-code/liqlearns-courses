# Building a Number Converter in PHP — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) Where do the PHP converter's parts live?**
**Answer: The form in the visitor's browser; the conversion on the server**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What happens on submit?**
**Answer: The values travel to the server; PHP reads, validates, converts, and generates the answer page**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What validation travels from the desktop project?**
**Answer: Refusing invalid entries — a binary field holding a 2 is refused**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What is the visitor's experience of the round trip?**
**Answer: Type, send, read**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) Which conversion rules change in the PHP telling?**
**Answer: None — repeated division, place values, and groups of four bits, unchanged**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What is the durable possession, in the project's argument?**
**Answer: The algorithm — the two languages are temporary houses for it**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) Against what is the PHP converter tested?**
**Answer: The same hand-worked conversions as its VB.NET twin**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What is a difference between the twin converters?**
**Answer: A fault in a house — never in the mathematics**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) Which Grade 11 teaching does this project spend?**
**Answer: Dynamic pages — request, processing, generated answer**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) How does Unit One close?**
**Answer: Learn the thing, build it twice, and say what stayed the same**
