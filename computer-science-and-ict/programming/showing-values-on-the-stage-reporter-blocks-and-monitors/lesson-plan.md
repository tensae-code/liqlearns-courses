# Lesson plan — Showing Values on the Stage — Reporter Blocks and Monitors

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Programming · **Lesson:** Showing Values on the Stage — Reporter Blocks and Monitors
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Palestine (2026-11-14) — in the title Programming on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Palestine's Grade 9 Programming student book, because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Reporter blocks carry values — position, direction, costume number, background name, size, volume, tempo, answer, timer, loudness, video motion — and ticking a reporter shows it on the stage as a monitor, some shown as sliders.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Reporter blocks — blocks that carry values** — **Reporter blocks** are the oval blocks that **carry a value** the program can use or show: **position, direction, costume number, background name, size, volume, tempo, answer, timer, loudness, video motion**. Where a round-edged block gives an order, a reporter gives a fact.
2. **Monitors — ticked to show on the stage** — A reporter is **ticked to show or hide as a stage monitor** — a small window on the stage that keeps showing the value as it changes, like the timer counting or the answer appearing. **Some monitors are shown as sliders**, joining this lesson to the photo editor’s: a value you can watch, and sometimes pull.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
