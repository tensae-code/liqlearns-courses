# Showing Values on the Stage — Reporter Blocks and Monitors

## Do it first

Where is the sprite? What is the answer? How loud is it? Scratch knows all of it — and can show it on the stage itself, in little windows called monitors. Grade 9’s lesson opens those windows.

## Sub lesson 1 — Reporter blocks — blocks that carry values

**Reporter blocks** are the oval blocks that **carry a value** the program can use or show: **position, direction, costume number, background name, size, volume, tempo, answer, timer, loudness, video motion**. Where a round-edged block gives an order, a reporter gives a fact.

## Sub lesson 2 — Monitors — ticked to show on the stage

A reporter is **ticked to show or hide as a stage monitor** — a small window on the stage that keeps showing the value as it changes, like the timer counting or the answer appearing. **Some monitors are shown as sliders**, joining this lesson to the photo editor’s: a value you can watch, and sometimes pull.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Reporter blocks carry values — position, direction, costume number, background name, size, volume, tempo, answer, timer, loudness, video motion — and ticking a reporter shows it on the stage as a monitor, some shown as sliders. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
