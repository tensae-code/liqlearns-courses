# Abstraction — Choosing the Important Aspects — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What problem does abstraction answer?**
**Answer: A situation carries more detail than any decision needs**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What is abstraction?**
**Answer: Deliberately choosing the important aspects for the purpose at hand and setting the rest aside**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) In the filling decision, which aspects matter?**
**Answer: Eater numbers and spoon size — not the colour of the stall cloth**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) Is the choice of aspects the same for every decision?**
**Answer: No — it is purpose-shaped; a different decision chooses differently**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) What makes clutter dangerous?**
**Answer: Holding all details at once makes the problem unsolvable**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) On what is the decision then made?**
**Answer: On the chosen aspects alone**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) When is an aspect kept?**
**Answer: Only if changing it would change the decision**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What is done with the dropped aspects?**
**Answer: They are named as dropped — the choice stays on record**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) Why keep the choice on record?**
**Answer: So a wrong outcome can be traced to a wrong choice of aspects and repaired**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) Is abstraction ignoring the world?**
**Answer: No — it is choosing which parts of the world this decision will stand on**
