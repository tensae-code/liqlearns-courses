# Codes — BCD and ASCII — true or false

Say whether each statement is true or false.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) In BCD, 47 and its plain binary form are the same pattern of bits.**
**Answer: False** — 47 in BCD is 0100 0111 (digit by digit); the whole number 47 in plain binary is 101111.

**2. (Sub lesson 1) If the pattern 1101 appears as a BCD group, something has gone wrong.**
**Answer: True** — 1101 is one of the six unused patterns (1010–1111); a decimal digit never produces it.

**3. (Sub lesson 2) The ASCII codes of the small letters come right after the codes of the capitals with no gap at all.**
**Answer: False** — Capitals end at Z = 90 and small letters start at a = 97 — there are other characters between the two runs.

**4. (Sub lesson 2) ASCII alone can write every letter of every language, including č, ć, š, ž and đ.**
**Answer: False** — 128 characters are not enough for all languages; wider codes were built later on the same idea.
