## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Lists — a chain that can grow** — A **list (λίστα)** is values joined in a chain, each value carrying the way to the next, so the chain can **grow and shrink** while the program runs — insert between two links, remove a link, and the rest of the chain stands. Where the array's strength is reaching any place at once by index, the list's strength is changing shape cheaply; the book teaches the learner to name which strength a job needs before choosing.
2. **Trees and graphs — branching and connecting** — A **tree (δένδρο)** is a chain that branches: one **root**, each value holding branches to children below it — the shape of folders inside folders and of a family's descent, with no loops back. A **graph (γράφημα)** is the freest shape: **nodes** joined by **edges** in any pattern, loops allowed — the shape of road maps, networks and friendships. The supplementary material teaches all three at abstract level with diagrams, because the exam asks the learner to recognise the shape a problem's data already has.
