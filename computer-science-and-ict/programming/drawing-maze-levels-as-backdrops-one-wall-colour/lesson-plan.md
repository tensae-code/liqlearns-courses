# Lesson plan — Drawing Maze Levels as Backdrops — One Wall Colour

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Programming · **Lesson:** Drawing Maze Levels as Backdrops — One Wall Colour
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Finland (2026-11-14) — in the title Programming on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Finland's *Ensiaskeleet ohjelmointiin* (student book, Oppilaan kirja, Versio 2.0), because no existing lesson teaches this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A maze level is a backdrop, drawn with one rule that makes the whole game work: the walls are painted in one single colour, and only that colour — because the game will test 'touching the wall colour' to know when the player has hit a wall. Each level's backdrop is named for the level it is.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on designing the maze game and on drawing backdrops; the one-wall-colour rule and naming levels are built inside the lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. ## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Draw a maze as a backdrop for level 1 and level 2.
2. Keep every wall in one single colour, and say why.
3. Name each level's backdrop so the game can tell the levels apart.

## What you need
A computer with Scratch.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Draw a maze however you like — walls in five pretty colours. Now the game must answer one question a hundred times a minute: 'is the player touching a wall?' With five wall colours, that is five questions and five chances to miss one. One colour makes it one question, always right. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.

## If the learner is stuck
Player walks through one wall → that stretch is painted in a different colour, or has a gap; repaint it in the wall colour and close the gap. Levels confused → the backdrops are unnamed or alike; name them Maze 1, Maze 2 in order.
