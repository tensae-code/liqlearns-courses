# Lesson plan — Computational problems — decision, search, counting, optimisation

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Programming · **Lesson:** Computational problems — decision, search, counting, optimisation
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 6 — Fundamentals of Programming (CURRICULUM.md) (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a computational problem can be solved step by step with a computer, and its four types are decision, search, counting and optimisation.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course — the learner knows what a computer, a network and the Internet are. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or in steps — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of this course. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define a computational problem.
2. Name the four types with the book's example of each.
3. Classify new questions into the four types.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Where the lesson is a doing-lesson, a school computer if one is available — paper works too.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner sorts four questions by the kind of answer each wants, before the four computational problem types are named (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the parts below, in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 20924]: a picture of computational problems — four types, four panels — the student studies each part of the picture and names what it shows.

3. **Say it to your companion** — explain the lesson back in your own words (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the lesson retold in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-or-false, fill-in-the-blank — served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the doing block at the end of lesson/lesson.md.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes up the key words → go back to the picture and point at each part while you say its name: a computational problem, a decision problem, a counting problem, an optimisation problem.
- Cannot say why it matters → use the lesson's own example again, step by step, before trying a new one.
