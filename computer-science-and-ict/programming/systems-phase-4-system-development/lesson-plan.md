**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Build it — programs and files from the design** — **System development** carries out the design: the **files or database are created** as designed, and the **programs are written** — in the languages and tools the design chose — one part at a time, each part doing exactly its designed job. Fiji keeps building separate from designing for a reason its earlier phases taught: the builder's questions are *how do I make this piece work*, and the design must already have answered *what piece and why* — a builder who must also decide is a project paying for design twice.
2. **Test it — pieces, then the whole** — Building includes its own proving. Each part is **tested as it is made** — fed real and awkward data, checked against its design — and when the parts stand, the **whole system is tested together**: data flowing in at the real inputs and out at the real reports. Fiji's testing rule is unglamorous and absolute: a system is not developed when it is *written*, but when it has been *shown* to do what its requirements said — the evidence, not the effort, ends the phase.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
