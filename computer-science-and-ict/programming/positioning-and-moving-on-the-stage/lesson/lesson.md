# Positioning and Moving on the Stage

## Do it first

Stand in the middle of the room. Now stand exactly at the door, then exactly at the window. You did not wander — you went to named places. A sprite on the stage can be placed the same exact way.

## Positioning and Moving on the Stage

A sprite that only moves forward from wherever it stands is hard to direct. This lesson teaches **positioning**: the stage is a place with places on it, and a sprite can be **put** at a chosen spot — with the go-to idea and the stage's left-right and up-down positions — and then **moved** from that spot in a planned way. Starting a scene becomes exact: each sprite begins where the story needs it, every time the green flag is clicked, not where it happened to stop last time. The learner practises placing, checking the place against the plan, and moving between two named places without extra wandering in between.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Every place on the Scratch stage has a position: a sprite can be put at an exact place, and moved from place to place on purpose — not just wandered forward. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: put a sprite at an exact chosen place on the stage. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: move a sprite between two named places in a planned way. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: say why a scene should place its sprites again at the start. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
