# Testing an Algorithm — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What does testing an algorithm mean?**
a) Running it step by step on chosen values and comparing with the known right result  b) The result you already know is right  c) Pencil, paper and patience — a step-by-step trace  d) Input, expected result, the algorithm's result, and the verdict
**Answer: a) Running it step by step on chosen values and comparing with the known right result**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What is an algorithm tested against?**
a) To the step where the trace leaves the right path  b) The result you already know is right  c) An ordinary value, the edges of the range, and a special or refused value  d) The algorithm may still break at the edges
**Answer: b) The result you already know is right**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) Where does a failed test point?**
a) Before any computer sees the algorithm  b) The smallest and the largest values allowed  c) To the step where the trace leaves the right path  d) At the edges of the range
**Answer: c) To the step where the trace leaves the right path**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) When is testing done?**
a) Pencil, paper and patience — a step-by-step trace  b) Input, expected result, the algorithm's result, and the verdict  c) Running it step by step on chosen values and comparing with the known right result  d) Before any computer sees the algorithm
**Answer: d) Before any computer sees the algorithm**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) What do you test with?**
a) Pencil, paper and patience — a step-by-step trace  b) An ordinary value, the edges of the range, and a special or refused value  c) The algorithm may still break at the edges  d) The result you already know is right
**Answer: a) Pencil, paper and patience — a step-by-step trace**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What three kinds of test values should be chosen?**
a) The smallest and the largest values allowed  b) An ordinary value, the edges of the range, and a special or refused value  c) At the edges of the range  d) To the step where the trace leaves the right path

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What are the edges of a range?**
a) Input, expected result, the algorithm's result, and the verdict  b) Running it step by step on chosen values and comparing with the known right result  c) The smallest and the largest values allowed  d) Before any computer sees the algorithm
**Answer: c) The smallest and the largest values allowed**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What does each written test record?**
a) The algorithm may still break at the edges  b) The result you already know is right  c) Pencil, paper and patience — a step-by-step trace  d) Input, expected result, the algorithm's result, and the verdict

**9. (Sub lesson 2) Why are ordinary values alone not enough?**
a) The algorithm may still break at the edges  b) At the edges of the range  c) To the step where the trace leaves the right path  d) An ordinary value, the edges of the range, and a special or refused value
