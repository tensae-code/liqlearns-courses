# Testing an Algorithm

## Do it first

Mauritania's Grade 9 algorithms unit gives testing its own fiche, right after writing. This lesson does the same: an algorithm is not finished when it is written, but when it has been tested.

## Sub lesson 1 — What testing an algorithm means

To **test** an algorithm is to run it — on paper, step by step — with chosen input values, and compare the result it produces with the result you already know is right. If the two match on every test, the algorithm earns trust; if they differ, the step where the trace leaves the right path is where the fault lives. Testing is done before any computer sees the algorithm, with pencil, paper and patience.

## Sub lesson 2 — Choosing test values and checking results

Good tests are chosen, not random: an **ordinary** value from the middle of the range, the **edges** — the smallest and largest allowed — and a value that should be refused or handled specially, if the problem has one. Write each test as a small table: input, expected result, result the algorithm gives, verdict. An algorithm that passes only ordinary values may still break at the edges, which is exactly where faults like to hide.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Writing an algorithm is not the end of the work: testing runs it on chosen values, follows it step by step by hand, and compares what it does with what it should do — until the results come out right. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: Running it step by step on chosen values and comparing with the known right result. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: To the step where the trace leaves the right path. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: An ordinary value, the edges of the range, and a special or refused value. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: Input, expected result, the algorithm's result, and the verdict. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
