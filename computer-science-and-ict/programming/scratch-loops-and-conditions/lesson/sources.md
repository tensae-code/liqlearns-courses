Palestine’s own way (Grade 6, Programming): Grade 6 programming is a full Scratch course (MIT, named in the book) in four units — basics, movement and costumes, sprites communicating, projects. Lessons after Unit 1 are deliberately non-cumulative: pupils imagine, program, then share their product in exhibitions and competitions. A local touch is kept: the “say” lesson notes Arabic text appears reversed because Scratch writes left-to-right.

Palestine’s own way (Grade 7, Programming): the Scratch course deepens in three units — programming graphics, sprites and backgrounds, sprite stories and dialogues. Local grounding is explicit: the pen unit opens by asking whether Scratch can draw ornaments like the decorations of Al-Aqsa Mosque; the stage is explained as a theatre whose actors (sprites) follow the director exactly and in order.

Palestine’s own way (Grade 8, Programming): Grade 8 Scratch is organised around making things — Unit 1 programs drawings and ornaments (the unit opens with the ornaments of Jerusalem’s churches and the Dome of the Rock), Unit 2 weaves interactive stories with sound and dialogue (including a dialogue project on love of the homeland), Unit 3 designs real games with variables, operators and sensing.

Iraq's own way (Grade 8): the same four-unit spiral one year on — the hardware unit becomes “the computer platform” and opens with choosing a computer's specifications before buying; Word is taught tab by tab (Home, Insert, Design, Layout); the algorithms unit advances to selection statements and logic truth tables, still landing in Scratch; the internet unit ends with writing a report from web research.

Iraq's own way (Grade 10): the preparatory stage turns practical and examinable — a whole unit on computer maintenance (preventive vs corrective, hardware and software, hard-disk management with defragmentation, ScanDisk, partitions and FAT32 vs NTFS), then PowerPoint and Microsoft Publisher taught tab by tab, looping control structures in Scratch, and a final unit pairing HTML web-page building with information security.

Morocco's own way (Grade 5): the Scratch spiral completes in Grade 5 with real program logic — wait/repeat-until, if–then–else, clones and sensors (answer, mouse position, touching colour, date) — and the year is half projects: a space-voyage story, a don't-touch-the-barriers ball game, and an aquarium game. (teacher guide — evidence)

Sudan's own way (Grade 9): the whole Grade 9 book is programming — concepts and Pascal-style examples first, then algorithms and flowcharts, then a full Scratch course ending in a designed project. The Bakht Al-Ruda ICT series runs in the same pattern from Grade 4 to Grade 9.

Cuba's own way (Grade 5): The Grade 5 programme is built around extracting information from Cuban educational software, encyclopedias and EcuRed into word-processor documents, then Scratch — drawing/animation first (Unit 3), control/movement/appearance blocks in Unit 4. (teacher programme — evidence)

Cuba's own way (Grade 5): Same Grade 5 content as the Programa, recast as per-unit teaching tables with hour allocations; Unit 4 is renamed “Aprendiendo y creando” and taught purely as Scratch control, movement and appearance blocks. (teacher guide — evidence)

Cuba's own way (Grade 5): Student text mirrors the programme in four chapters; word-processing is taught as a research workflow (synonyms, symbols, searching, copying texts and images from sources), and Scratch is introduced through its paint editor before movement/appearance/control blocks.

Cuba's own way (Grade 6): Grade 6 adds the three productivity firsts in one year — digital presentations, Scratch animation with event/control blocks, and spreadsheets with formulas and charts — closing with a TIC chapter on searching and processing digital information; computer generations are taught inside Chapter 1.

Cuba's own way (Grade 8): Taught entirely in free software — LibreOffice Impress for presentations and LibreOffice Calc for spreadsheets; web work means designing and editing HTML pages with a plain text editor, and Scratch continues with arithmetic and logic operator blocks.
