Vietnam's own way (Grade 5 Tin học 5): Grade 5 splits its applied theme into two alternative tracks — track A makes digital products with graphics software, track B follows tutorial videos to make physical craft products — and its programming theme is taught in a visual block language as pure structures first (sequence, loop, branch), then variables and expressions, ending with turning a written script/storyboard into a program.

Vietnam's own way (Grade 8 Tin học 8): Grade 8 opens with a history of calculating tools (abacus, Pascal's Pascaline, computer generations) and its applied theme is a genuine fork — track a does advanced Word/PowerPoint, track b learns photo editing in GIMP; programming is Scratch again, but now with data representation and a named debugging lesson (Gỡ lỗi).


Pakistan's own way (Grade 6 Computer Science 6): every unit opens with Students Learning Outcomes, is dotted with "Do you Know?", "Extra Bit!" and "Tips" boxes, and closes with Summary, MCQ/short/detailed exercises, project-based and activity-based questions, with an answers key at the back. Digital skills (Windows desktop, file management, Paint 3D, a typing tutor) are taught hands-on BEFORE programming; digital citizenship is framed through Pakistani citizenship first; the entrepreneurship unit teaches from Pakistani founders (Foodpanda/EatOye, Shopsy.pk, Zero Makeup, Investors Lounge).

Pakistan's own way (Grade 7 Computer Science 7): Grade 7 opens with emerging technologies and a deep ports-and-peripherals hardware treatment, then teaches email all the way to DKIM/SPF/DMARC authentication and POP3/IMAP/SMTP protocols; binary data representation (ASCII/Unicode, RGB colour, sound sampling) is taught inside the Programming unit, and entrepreneurship becomes business-plan craft — promotion, value proposition, quality assurance, SEO and platform business models, with Twitter and Snapchat origin stories.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G2 Digital Literacy): one Grade 2 year mixes Scratch, a word processor, the WavePad sound editor and Lego Mindstorms EV3 robotics, and ends with a built-and-defended robotics project ("Dancing Robot"); every section closes with research and creative task pages (printed pp. 18, 34, 58).

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G3 Digital Literacy): Grade 3 is built around making a whole Scratch game (scenario, scenes, characters, costumes) and an EV3 robotics project, with Word and PowerPoint taught as the project's tools; "Check yourself! Creative tasks" lessons close each section (printed pp. 29, 59, 95, 126 — assessment pages, not placed).

Kazakhstan's own way (Kobdikova G2 Digital Literacy): the same six-part national Grade 2 frame as the Almatykitap book, but the tools differ: the "word by word" part builds a spoken-text dialogue between two Scratch characters, sound is recorded and trimmed in the Windows Voice Recorder app (not WavePad), media players AIMP and Windows Media Player are named, the keyboard trainer is the online typingstudy.com Kazakh course, the text editor is WordPad, and the Dancing Robot dances to the Kazakh folk melody "Kamajai" loaded through the EV3 Sound Editor.

Kazakhstan's own way (Isabaeva G2 Digital Literacy): a third Grade 2 course on the same six-part national frame, from the Kokzhiek/Atamura side: the keyboard trainer is RapidTyping 5 with full finger-placement rules, sound is edited in Wave Editor (wave-editor.com) by selecting waveform segments, voice recording also uses a smartphone dictaphone, and the final part has no text-editor lesson — the project algorithm itself is the writing task. Unnumbered end-of-part project and summary pages (printed pp. 27, 50, 84, 99, 114, 136–137) are not placed separately.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kopeeva G5 Informatika): Grade 5 Informatika (the older, pre-"Digital Literacy" subject name) is built around Scratch as a "game programming environment": a whole chapter runs from a first program through blocks, animation graphics, sound effects and dialogue to linear/branching/cyclic commands; earlier chapters pair Internet safety with data protection, and the year ends with print-ready project work — a Word newspaper page with WordArt and columns, presented as slides.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukhametzhanova G5 Informatika, 2017 edition): the 2017 Atamura Grade 5 book follows the older national programme shared with the Arman-PV book of the same year: computer safety, information and raster graphics, algorithms through labyrinths (real and virtual), a first Scratch program and "living graphics", and project work ending in a print-ready document; a full Scratch block dictionary is printed as an appendix (printed pp. 119–127). The book was written with Microsoft "Partnership in Education" material.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kopeeva G5 «Информатика»): the year runs safety → internet safety → information and its processing → a long Scratch chapter, and it closes not with a test but with two production projects: a printed newspaper page made in a word processor (page setup, columns, WordArt, page numbers) and a defended Scratch project published to the Scratch community site.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G3 «Цифровая грамотность»): the Almatykitap Grade 3 digital literacy book is built as projects: a Scratch game built scene by scene, then a robotics project that oddly starts in Word (the project document is written and illustrated first) before the robot arm moves, and a cleaner robot is built; presentations and a photo collection close the year.
