# Simple Data and Structured Data

## Do it first

Grade 11 opens by redrawing the data map. Everything met so far was a single value in a single box; the year's first chapter is about the other kind — data with parts, many values travelling under one name.

## Sub lesson 1 — Simple data — one value, one name

**Simple (scalar) data** holds **one value under one name**: an integer variable holds one whole number, a char one character, a boolean one truth. The simple types met in Grades 7 and 10 — integer, real, char, boolean, the enumerations and subranges — are all simple in this sense, however subtle their internals. A simple value is used **whole**: assigned, compared, computed in one act. Its opposite is not 'easy' but 'assembled' — the division is of structure, not of difficulty.

In your mind's eye: simple data — one value, one name.

## Sub lesson 2 — Structured data — many values, one name, inner order

**Structured data** gathers **many values under one name**, organised by an inner order the type fixes: an **array** by index, a **string** by position, a **record** by named fields, a **set** by membership, a **file** by sequence. A structured variable is therefore worked **whole or by parts** — the whole array assigned, or its third element singled out. Grade 11's first chapter is the tour of these structures; this section is their map: whenever one name must hold a classful, a text or a table, a structure, not a simple type, is the tool.

In your mind's eye: structured data — many values, one name, inner order.
