# Loops with the Robot Artist

## Do it first

Draw the letter H by saying its strokes aloud: down, down, across. Now draw it again — you repeated the same strokes. A loop is the instruction that does that repeating for you.

## Loops with the Robot Artist

Now the noticing becomes a block. A **loop** wraps a group of actions and a number: do these actions, this many times. The book's hero for the loop is the **robot artist (Робот художник)** — a drawing robot in code.org Course 1 that draws letters with straight strokes. A letter is a perfect loop job: the same stroke pattern repeats, and the loop draws it cleanly every time, where writing every stroke out would be long and easy to spoil. The lesson's reading skill matters as much as the writing one: given a loop, say how many times the inside actions will run, and what picture will come out, before anything is run.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: A loop says "do these actions this many times" in one block — and the robot artist uses a loop to draw letters, repeating its strokes without writing each stroke out. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: say what a loop does, in your own words. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: wrap a repeating group of actions in a loop with the right count. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: read a loop and say how many times its inside actions run. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
