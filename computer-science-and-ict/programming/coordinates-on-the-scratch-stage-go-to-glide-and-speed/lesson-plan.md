# Lesson plan — Coordinates on the Scratch Stage — Go To, Glide and Speed

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Programming · **Lesson:** Coordinates on the Scratch Stage — Go To, Glide and Speed
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Palestine (2026-11-14) — in the title Programming on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Palestine's Grade 8 Programming student book, because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The stage is a grid: x runs from −240 to 240, y from −180 to 180, with the origin at the centre. Go-to places a sprite at exact coordinates; glide moves it there over seconds — a longer glide is a slower move; sensing blocks report the sprite’s x and y.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The stage as a grid — x, y and the origin** — The stage is a **grid**: **x from −240 to 240** left-to-right, **y from −180 to 180** bottom-to-top, and the **origin** — (0, 0) — at the centre. Every place on the stage has a pair of numbers, and shapes can be drawn from their **corner coordinates**.
2. **Go to, glide — and sensing x and y** — **Go to** puts the sprite at the given position at once; **glide** travels there **in seconds** — and the book’s rule of speed is plain: a **longer glide is a slower** move. The **x/y sensing blocks** report where the sprite is, so a program can ask the stage where its sprite stands.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
