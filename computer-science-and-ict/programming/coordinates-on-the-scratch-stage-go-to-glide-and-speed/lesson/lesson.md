# Coordinates on the Scratch Stage — Go To, Glide and Speed

## Do it first

“Stand over there” is not an instruction a sprite can obey. “Go to x 100, y 50” is. Palestine’s Grade 8 book turns the stage into a grid with numbers — and movement into exact positions and timed glides.

## Sub lesson 1 — The stage as a grid — x, y and the origin

The stage is a **grid**: **x from −240 to 240** left-to-right, **y from −180 to 180** bottom-to-top, and the **origin** — (0, 0) — at the centre. Every place on the stage has a pair of numbers, and shapes can be drawn from their **corner coordinates**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Go to, glide — and sensing x and y

**Go to** puts the sprite at the given position at once; **glide** travels there **in seconds** — and the book’s rule of speed is plain: a **longer glide is a slower** move. The **x/y sensing blocks** report where the sprite is, so a program can ask the stage where its sprite stands.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: The stage is a grid: x runs from −240 to 240, y from −180 to 180, with the origin at the centre. Go-to places a sprite at exact coordinates; glide moves it there over seconds — a longer glide is a slower move; sensing blocks report the sprite’s x and y. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
