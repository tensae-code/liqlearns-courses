# Systems Phase 6 — System Maintenance

## Do it first

The system is live — and the world immediately starts changing around it. Fiji's Phase 6 is system maintenance: the long last phase that keeps a running system true, useful and alive. This lesson is the keeping.

## Sub lesson 1 — Three kinds of keeping — fix, adapt, improve

**System maintenance** is all the work done on a system **after** implementation, and Fiji sorts it by cause. **Corrective** maintenance **fixes faults** the running system reveals — the report that totals wrong, the crash on the last day of the month. **Adaptive** maintenance **adjusts the system to a changed world** — a new tax rate, a new form the ministry demands. **Perfective** maintenance **improves** what works — a faster search, a clearer report — because users, living with the system, now know what it should have been.

In your mind's eye: three kinds of keeping — fix, adapt, improve.

## Sub lesson 2 — The longest phase — and the wheel turning

Maintenance is **the longest phase** of the cycle: a system may be investigated for weeks and maintained for **years**, and most of a system's whole cost is spent here, not in its building. It is also where the cycle earns its name: maintenance keeps a log of what it cannot fix and what the organisation newly needs, until the day that log becomes the complaint that opens a **new preliminary investigation** — and the wheel turns on the system's replacement. Fiji's maintenance lesson is the senior course's humility: building is an event; keeping is the life.

In your mind's eye: the longest phase — and the wheel turning.
