# Merge Sort — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What are merge sort's two phases?**
a) Split the list down to single items, then merge the pieces back in order  b) When every piece holds one item  c) A single item has nothing out of order  d) None — splitting is pure division
**Answer: a**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) When does splitting stop?**
a) When every piece holds one item  b) A single item has nothing out of order  c) None — splitting is pure division  d) Divide and conquer
**Answer: a**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) Why is a one-item list already sorted?**
a) A single item has nothing out of order  b) None — splitting is pure division  c) Divide and conquer  d) Repeatedly take the smaller of the two pieces' front items
**Answer: a**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) How much comparing happens during splitting?**
a) None — splitting is pure division  b) Divide and conquer  c) Repeatedly take the smaller of the two pieces' front items  d) The other piece's remaining items follow in order
**Answer: a**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) Which technique is merge sort the showpiece of?**
a) Divide and conquer  b) Repeatedly take the smaller of the two pieces' front items  c) The other piece's remaining items follow in order  d) Each half was sorted, and the merge never takes a larger front while a smaller one waits
**Answer: a**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) How much work does merge sort do?**
a) About n log n — log n levels of splitting, everything merged once per level  b) Each already sorted  c) Split the list down to single items, then merge the pieces back in order  d) When every piece holds one item
