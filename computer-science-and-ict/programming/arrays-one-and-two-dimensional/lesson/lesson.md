# Arrays in Visual Basic — One- and Two-Dimensional

## Do it first

Thirty marks need thirty variables — or one array. Libya's Grade 12 Science book gives arrays (matrices) their own chapter, taught in Visual Basic. This lesson is that chapter: the declaration, the two shapes, and the applications.

## Sub lesson 1 — The one-dimensional array — declaration and working with it

An **array** holds **many values under one name**, each reached by its **position (index)**. The **one-dimensional array** is a single row of values. In Visual Basic it is brought into being by its **declaration statement** — the statement that names the array, says **how many elements** it holds and **what type** they are — before any value is stored. Working with it means storing by index, reading by index, and walking the whole row with a **FOR..NEXT loop**, the pattern every array program in the chapter stands on.

In your mind's eye: the one-dimensional array — declaration and working with it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Two-dimensional arrays and the applications

A **two-dimensional array** holds values in **rows and columns**, like a table or the **matrix** the book also calls it; a value is reached by **two indexes** — row, then column — and walking the whole table takes a **loop inside a loop**. The chapter closes with **two sets of applications** on arrays: full worked programs that declare arrays, fill them, compute with them and print results — the declaration and the loops of the earlier sections earning their keep on real tasks.

In your mind's eye: two-dimensional arrays and the applications.
