# Drawing Your Own Sprite in the Scratch Paint Editor

## Do it first

The library has no traffic light. So Grade 8 draws one — then copies it three times and colours the copies red, orange and green, and the crossing works. This lesson is the paint editor: drawing a sprite yourself, and editing the looks it wears.

## Sub lesson 1 — Drawing a new sprite — the traffic light

In the **paint editor** a **new sprite is drawn** with the paint tools, shape by shape. The book’s worked drawing is a **traffic-light sprite**: drawn once, then **duplicated into three costumes** and **coloured red, orange and green** — one drawing, three working lights.

[Picture — drawing a new sprite — the traffic light]

## Sub lesson 2 — Editing, flipping, and sprites that talk

Grade 9’s painter lesson completes the skill: **flipping** a drawing to face the other way, **editing a library costume** instead of starting from nothing, and **adding a new costume** to a sprite that already has some. The drawn sprites join the program like library sprites — the traffic light’s sprites **communicate by message**.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: The paint editor draws a new sprite with its tools (and flips and edits library costumes): draw the sprite, duplicate its costume and recolour the copies — Grade 8’s traffic light is one drawing in three costumes, and sprites drawn this way can communicate by message like any other. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
