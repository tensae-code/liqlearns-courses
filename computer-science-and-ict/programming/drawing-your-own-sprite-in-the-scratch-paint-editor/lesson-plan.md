# Lesson plan — Drawing Your Own Sprite in the Scratch Paint Editor

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Programming · **Lesson:** Drawing Your Own Sprite in the Scratch Paint Editor
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Palestine (2026-11-14) — in the title Programming on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Palestine's Grade 8 and Grade 9 Programming student books, because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The paint editor draws a new sprite with its tools (and flips and edits library costumes): draw the sprite, duplicate its costume and recolour the copies — Grade 8’s traffic light is one drawing in three costumes, and sprites drawn this way can communicate by message like any other.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Drawing a new sprite — the traffic light** — In the **paint editor** a **new sprite is drawn** with the paint tools, shape by shape. The book’s worked drawing is a **traffic-light sprite**: drawn once, then **duplicated into three costumes** and **coloured red, orange and green** — one drawing, three working lights.
2. **Editing, flipping, and sprites that talk** — Grade 9’s painter lesson completes the skill: **flipping** a drawing to face the other way, **editing a library costume** instead of starting from nothing, and **adding a new costume** to a sprite that already has some. The drawn sprites join the program like library sprites — the traffic light’s sprites **communicate by message**.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
