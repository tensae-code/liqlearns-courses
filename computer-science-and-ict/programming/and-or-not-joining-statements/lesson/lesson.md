# AND, OR, NOT — Joining Statements

## Do it first

Listen to two sentences. 'I am wearing shoes AND I am wearing a hat.' Look at yourself: if one part is wrong, the whole sentence falls. Now: 'I will play OR I will read.' One part is enough there. This lesson is about those little joining words — AND, OR — and their flipping friend, NOT.

## Sub lesson 1 — AND and OR — joining two statements

Two statements can be joined into one big statement.

Join them with **AND**, and both parts must be true. *It is raining AND the wind is blowing.* Look outside: is it raining? Is the wind blowing? Only if **both** are true is the whole statement true. If even one part is false, the whole AND statement is false.

Join them with **OR**, and at least one part must be true. *I will drink tea OR water.* One of them is enough — tea will do, water will do, both will do. The OR statement is false only when **both** parts are false: no tea and no water.

[Picture — a rainy, windy day with the words AND between two small pictures, and a cup of tea and a glass of water with OR between them]

Say it short: AND is strict — both, or nothing. OR is kind — one is enough.

## Sub lesson 2 — NOT — the opposite statement

One small word can turn a statement right around: **NOT**.

Put NOT in a statement, and its truth flips. *The door is open* — true? Then *the door is NOT open* is false. *It is raining* — false today? Then *it is NOT raining* is true.

[Picture — an open door and the same door shut, with the word NOT between the two pictures]

NOT does not join two statements — it works on one, and changes it into its opposite. Whenever you meet NOT, do the flip in your head first: true becomes false, false becomes true.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: AND joins two statements and needs both true; OR joins two and needs at least one true; NOT works on one statement and flips it into its opposite. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: say one AND statement about today, check both of its parts out loud, and give the whole statement its verdict: true or false.
2. Show it, don't just say it: say one OR statement where one part is true and one is false, and explain why the whole statement is still true.
3. Show it, don't just say it: take the statement 'The window is open', check it, then say it with NOT and give the new verdict — showing the flip.
