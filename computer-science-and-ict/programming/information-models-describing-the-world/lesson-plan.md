## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What an information model is** — A **model** is a stand-in for a real thing, made to be studied instead of it. An **information model** is a stand-in made of information: it keeps the features of the original that matter for the maker's **purpose** and leaves the rest out. A timetable models a school's day, a map models a country's land, a formula models how a quantity depends on others. The same original can have many information models, because different purposes keep different features — a bus timetable and a road map both model the same town and share almost nothing.
2. **The forms a model takes** — The book names the forms by how the information is carried. A **verbal model** describes in words. A **formula model** describes with mathematical formulas, so values can be calculated. A **video (pictorial) model** shows with images or film, keeping how the thing looks. A **scheme model** draws the structure — boxes, lines and arrows that show parts and connections while dropping appearance. Choosing the form follows the purpose: calculate — take a formula; explain to a beginner — take words and a picture; show how parts connect — take a scheme.
