# The Systems Life Cycle

## Do it first

A system is not built; it is *grown through stages*, and Fiji's Year 13 book teaches the growing as a named cycle with six phases — then gives every phase its own section. This lesson is the map of the whole cycle; the six lessons after it walk the phases one by one, the way Fiji teaches them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Six phases, one journey

The **systems life cycle** is the ordered life of an information system from first idea to old age: **Phase 1 — preliminary investigation** (is there a problem, and is a new system worth it?), **Phase 2 — systems analysis** (exactly what must it do?), **Phase 3 — system design** (how will it do it?), **Phase 4 — system development** (build it), **Phase 5 — system implementation** (put it to work), **Phase 6 — system maintenance** (keep it true as the world changes). Fiji's Viti case carries the whole journey: one furniture company's system, followed from its first complaint to its keeping.

In your mind's eye: six phases, one journey.

## Sub lesson 2 — A cycle, not a line — and the alternatives at its side

It is called a **cycle** because maintenance does not end the story: the changes it brings, and the problems it cannot fix, start a **new investigation**, and the wheel turns again on a replacement system. Fiji also keeps the honest company of alternatives at the cycle's side — **prototyping and rapid application development**, taught in their own section (the lesson after Phase 6): quicker, looser ways to grow a system, chosen when the full cycle's care would cost more than its safety is worth. The map lesson ends with the choice every real project faces: which road, and why.

In your mind's eye: a cycle, not a line — and the alternatives at its side.
