# Lesson plan — The Systems Life Cycle

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Programming · **Lesson:** The Systems Life Cycle
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Fiji (2026-11-14) — in the title Programming on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Fiji's student books (Fiji Ministry of Education / Technology and Employment Skills Training (TEST) section, Suva), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The ordered life of an information system from first idea to old age, in six phases.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Six phases, one journey** — The **systems life cycle** is the ordered life of an information system from first idea to old age: **Phase 1 — preliminary investigation** (is there a problem, and is a new system worth it?), **Phase 2 — systems analysis** (exactly what must it do?), **Phase 3 — system design** (how will it do it?), **Phase 4 — system development** (build it), **Phase 5 — system implementation** (put it to work), **Phase 6 — system maintenance** (keep it true as the world changes). Fiji's Viti case carries the whole journey: one furniture company's system, followed from its first complaint to its keeping.
2. **A cycle, not a line — and the alternatives at its side** — It is called a **cycle** because maintenance does not end the story: the changes it brings, and the problems it cannot fix, start a **new investigation**, and the wheel turns again on a replacement system. Fiji also keeps the honest company of alternatives at the cycle's side — **prototyping and rapid application development**, taught in their own section (the lesson after Phase 6): quicker, looser ways to grow a system, chosen when the full cycle's care would cost more than its safety is worth. The map lesson ends with the choice every real project faces: which road, and why.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — A system is not built; it is *grown through stages*, and Fiji's Year 13 book teaches the growing as a named cycle with six phases — then gives every phase its own section. This lesson is the map of the whole cycle; the six lessons after it walk the phases one by one, the way Fiji teaches them. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of six phases, one journey as the lesson describes it — the student studies the picture and names what each part shows.
   - ADD GIF: a GIF of a cycle, not a line — and the alternatives at its side in action — the student watches once, then says what changed and why.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the lesson's key names and definitions aloud, in the order the lesson gives them — the student listens once through, then reads the same lines aloud along with the voice. (Audio is a described slot in this build; no audio file is made.)

4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-false, matching, short-answer — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
