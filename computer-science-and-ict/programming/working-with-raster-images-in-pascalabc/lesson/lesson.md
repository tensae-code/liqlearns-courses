# Working with Raster Images in PascalABC

## Do it first

Your drawn figures can move — but so can a whole photograph. In this lesson a PascalABC program loads a raster picture and walks it across the window, step by step, until still pictures become animation.

## Sub lesson 1 — Raster images in a program

A **raster image** is a picture made of a grid of coloured points (**pixels**) — the kind of picture a photograph or a saved drawing is. Unlike a figure the program draws from commands, a raster image is **loaded from a picture file** and placed on the graphics window at chosen coordinates, whole, as one object. The program can draw it again elsewhere, cover it, or layer figures over it. Its size in pixels is its size on the window: a big picture file covers a big area, whatever it shows.

[Picture — a photograph as a grid of pixels being lifted whole onto a graphics window]

## Sub lesson 2 — Moving and animating

**Animation** here is movement built from still steps: draw the image, wait a blink, clear or cover its old place, and draw it at the next coordinates — repeated quickly, the eye joins the steps into **motion**. Direction and speed are just the step pattern: how far the coordinates change each step, and how long the pause is. The craft problems are the visible ones — a trail left behind when the old position is not cleaned, jerky steps when the jumps are too big — and each is fixed in the step pattern, not in the picture.

[Picture — the same picture drawn at five positions across a window with arrows between them, joining into motion]

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: A raster image — a picture stored as a grid of pixels, loaded from a file — can be placed, moved and animated by a PascalABC program: the program redraws it at new coordinates step by step, and the steps become motion. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
