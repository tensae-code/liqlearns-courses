# The General Process of Data Processing

## Do it first

A school wants to know why Grade 9 results fell this year. The marks exist — in registers, exercise books, a clerk's drawer. Before anyone can answer the question, the marks must be gathered, tidied, worked on, and shown. That journey, from scattered records to an answer a head teacher can act on, follows the same stages everywhere. This lesson maps them.

## Sub lesson 1 — From scattered records to ready data — collect and organise

The process opens with **purpose**: a question someone needs answered decides what data is worth having. Then **collection** gathers the data from its sources — records, sensors, questionnaires, the web — as the next lesson studies in detail. Collection alone leaves a heap: different formats, missing entries, duplicates, mistakes. So the second stage **organises** the data: putting it into one structure (typically a table with agreed columns), and cleaning it — the 'dirty data' work of the lesson after next. Only data that has passed through both stages is *ready*: complete enough, consistent, and in a shape tools can work on.

[Picture — a funnel: scattered papers, forms and sensor readings pouring into the top, and a neat table of rows and columns flowing out of the bottom]

## Sub lesson 2 — From ready data to an answer — analyse, present, apply

With ready data, **analysis** asks the questions: totals, averages, groupings, comparisons, patterns over time — drawn out with tools, as the analysis lesson shows. Analysis kept as bare numbers persuades nobody, so the process **presents** the findings — charts and visual displays that let a reader see the pattern at a glance. Finally the results are **applied and reported**: a written report states the question, the data, the findings and what should be done, and the decision-makers act. The process is a chain of dependencies: dirty data poisons analysis, analysis without presentation persuades no one, and a report that nobody applies was effort wasted.

[Picture — the neat table from the first sub lesson travelling onward — into a magnifying glass (analysis), out as a rising bar chart (presentation), and landing as a signed decision on a head teacher's desk (application)]

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: data processing runs collect, organise and clean, analyse, present, apply and report — always in that order, because each stage depends on the one before: dirty data poisons analysis, and an unapplied report wastes the whole journey. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
