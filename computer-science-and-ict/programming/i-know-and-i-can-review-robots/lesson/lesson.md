# I Know and I Can — Review: Robots

## Do it first

Plan one route and one shape from memory, in robot steps. If the plan comes easily, the mat will prove the rest; if it does not, the review has found its first gap before the robot even moves.

## I Know and I Can — Review: Robots

After the screen is left behind, the book checks the new ground with a **"Знам и мога" (I know and I can)**: prove the **programmable device** idea, the **Blue-Bot** commands — forward, back, turns, clear, go — **route planning in robot steps**, the control environment, and **driving shapes** from the robot-and-shapes lesson. A robot review is proved where the robot works: on the mat. A route is proved when the bee arrives where the program said; a shape is proved when it closes. Paper plans are checked too, but no tick is given for a plan the robot cannot drive. Anything not proved is named — a forgotten clear, a stride-counted route — practised, and proved again on the mat.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: This "Знам и мога" mastery check proves the robot work: what a programmable device is, Blue-Bot commands and routes, and driving shapes — all proved on the mat, not on paper alone. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: prove a Blue-Bot route on the mat: planned, cleared, entered, arrived. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: prove a driven shape that closes. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: name the robot skill not yet proved — forgotten clear, wrong counting — and practise it. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
