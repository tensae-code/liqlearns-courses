# Polya's four steps

## Do it first

A problem lands in front of you. Most pupils grab a pen and start calculating. Polya — whose book How to Solve It this method comes from — says the calculating is only step three of four, and step three is allowed to fail and send you back. This lesson walks all four steps in order, as sub lessons: prove each before the next opens.

## Sub lesson 1 — Understand the problem

Step 1: understand the problem. What is asked? What is known, and what is not known? What type of answer is required? Is it like a problem seen before? Look up important terms. Nothing is calculated yet — a problem misunderstood is solved wrongly, however neat the working.

## Sub lesson 2 — Make a plan

Step 2: make a plan. The strategies include: making a list, drawing a picture, eliminating possibilities, using a formula, guessing and checking, and solving a simpler related problem. Choosing the strategy is the thinking; the step after this is only the doing.

## Sub lesson 3 — Execute the plan

Step 3: execute the plan. Use the chosen strategy, and use resources such as the textbook if stuck. And if the strategy itself fails, return to step 2 and choose another. In practice the steps are moved back and forth through, not walked once — going back is part of the method, not a defeat.

## Sub lesson 4 — Review and extend

Step 4: review and extend. Check the answer, preferably by a different method. And evaluate the method itself: was it effective? Could it be better? Where else could it be used? A solved problem that teaches nothing for the next one is only half harvested.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Polya's method: understand the problem, make a plan, execute the plan, then review and extend — moving back and forth between the steps, not walking them once. Then name the key words of this lesson — understanding the problem, making a plan, executing the plan, review and extend — and say what each one means.

## Do It Yourself

1. Solve a small problem and label which step you are in at each moment.
2. Write what 'check by a different method' means for one answer.
3. Say which step pupils most often skip, and what it costs.
