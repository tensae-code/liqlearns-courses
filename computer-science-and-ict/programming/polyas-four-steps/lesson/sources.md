**Acceptance count: taught in 15 countries (Ethiopia, China, Uzbekistan, Egypt, Libya, Malaysia, Mongolia, Sri Lanka, Laos, Bangladesh, Jordan, Indonesia, Vietnam, Pakistan, Kazakhstan).**

Book error, taught corrected: the student book's Polya table prints the typo "2tep 2" for Step 2; the lesson teaches Step 2 and flags the misprint here.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Grade 12): the final year is design-and-build — simulation models, a seven-method technological problem-solving process, biotechnology with a DNA-technology case study, CAD, the software development process, IT career pathways, and GUI work with client-side scripting and interactive menus. (teacher guide — evidence)


Uzbekistan's own way (Grade 9): grade 9 is the capstone — computer graphics, Access, Excel, then a nine-lesson algorithms block and a 28-lesson Turbo Pascal 7.0 course that runs from the alphabet of the language to files, procedures and graphics mode, closing with the Internet. Every chapter ends with a dedicated repetition lesson, placed here to that chapter's main lesson.

Egypt's own way (Grade 9, Term 1 student book (Arabic)): Preparatory 3 goes deep on Python — computational thinking, AI algorithms, strings/lists, functions with parameters, input-error handling — then a second unit on AI and cybersecurity ending with deepfakes.

Libya's own way (Grade 11 Term 1, Information Technology student book): a programming year taught in old-school order — problem solving and algorithms, flowcharts, memory/variables, then Visual Basic statement by statement (LET, PRINT, INPUTBOX, IF, SELECT CASE, FOR…NEXT, DO…WHILE, DO…UNTIL), every chapter ending in graded exercises. The book is printed for BOTH the Science and Humanities sections (للقسمين العلمي والأدبي), although the records label it Humanities only — cited as printed, and taught to Science and Humanities together.

Libya's own way (Grade 12 Science, Information Technology student book): the Science section shares the Humanities book's first three chapters (security, e-government, computer law) word-for-structure, then diverges into a full second Visual Basic course — the VB environment and form controls, arrays, subroutines and built-in functions, and the remaining toolbox controls. Visual Basic is the Grade 12 programming language in both sections.

Malaysia's own way (Grade 10, SK Tingkatan 4): the upper-secondary Sains Komputer (SK — Computer Science) elective switches language to Java (NetBeans) and is organised as three big project-style chapters — Programming, Relational Databases (Access, ERD, normalisation to 3NF, macros/switchboard, a full SDLC database project), and Human-Computer Interaction with screen-design prototyping and quantitative evaluation (prototypes built in Java NetBeans).

Mongolia's own way (Grade 12): the final book measures everything against Mongolia itself — the ITU index and Mongolia's slipping rank in it, 36,760 km of fibre to 299 soums, the Mongolsat-1 satellite, IoT chips on livestock, telemedicine at the State Central Hospital, the ХУР state data-exchange and ТҮЦ service machines, nano-products from horse bone and ger covers — and its chapter projects are real Mongolian systems: a school ICT-development survey, a study of the ebarimt VAT-receipt system, and a Small Basic "Bouncing Ball" game; programming examples draw the alkhan khee meander with a Turtle subprogram.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 11, Tamil): a full Visual Basic 6.0 programming course inside the O/L book (IDE, controls, data types, Select Case, arrays), an SDLC chapter, Photoshop-based multimedia, hand-coded HTML with Sri Lankan examples (the Sinharaja rainforest page, ucsc.cmb.ac.lk addresses, sltnet.lk domains), and sector chapters (agriculture, industry, government, commerce) before malware and health.

Laos's own way (Grade 12 ICT student book): the final year is a modern, applied course — problem-solving method, a long English-titled internet-safety sequence (Internet Safety, Email Tips for Scams and Spam, Smart Social Networking, Cyber harassment/Stalking/Addiction), data summaries and PivotTables for decision-making, copyright and Creative Commons licensing, a full PowerPoint course, and choosing technology for your future occupation.

Bangladesh's own way (Grade 6, Digital Technology): the Competency-based National Curriculum 2022 book is organised as nine project "Learning Experiences" done in groups through stories, games and fieldwork — no conventional chapters; even learners without a computer at home learn technology through experience around school and home. The NCTB Digital Technology line runs in parallel English and Bangla editions; placements are cited from the English Version with the Bangla Version parallel spot-checked.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 12 G12T1 Student): Same series design as Term 2; Unit 1 builds programming theory (SDLC, algorithms, Python data structures, recursion, packages) before Unit 2 applies Python to data analysis with charts and hypotheses.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 10 G10 Informatika SMK Sem1): The SMK Semester 1 book teaches the same Informatika elements with a logic-first opening (propositions and inference), applied office integration, hands-on network-device configuration, and an OS-installation practical.

Vietnam's own way (Grade 9 Tin học 9): Grade 9 teaches simulation software (PhET, Geometer's Sketchpad) as a way to learn by experiment, and its applied fork is advanced Excel (data validation, COUNTIF/SUMIF/IF, a family-finance workbook project) versus a full video-production track in Video Editor from storyboard to export.


Pakistan's own way (Grade 6 Computer Science 6): every unit opens with Students Learning Outcomes, is dotted with "Do you Know?", "Extra Bit!" and "Tips" boxes, and closes with Summary, MCQ/short/detailed exercises, project-based and activity-based questions, with an answers key at the back. Digital skills (Windows desktop, file management, Paint 3D, a typing tutor) are taught hands-on BEFORE programming; digital citizenship is framed through Pakistani citizenship first; the entrepreneurship unit teaches from Pakistani founders (Foodpanda/EatOye, Shopsy.pk, Zero Makeup, Investors Lounge).

Pakistan's own way (Grade 6 Computer Science 6 (link pass)): the SNC middle-school course is unusually broad for Grade 6 — it runs from ICT fundamentals through Paint 3D and Scratch to digital citizenship and a full unit on entrepreneurship in the digital age with Pakistani startup examples (Foodpanda/EatOye, Shoppy.pk, Zero Makeup)

Kazakhstan's own way (Vyushkova/Zhurbenko/Orazbekov G7 «Информатика» (NIS student book, Russian edition)): the NIS Grade 7 book is organised around data: measuring and representing information (including the von Neumann architecture), business documents, spreadsheets that grow into databases (error checking, sorting/filtering, protecting data from change), raster graphics and logo design, a whole modelling section (modelling games, school life and social problems, forecasting from data), networks and information systems, and linear-structure programming that ends with building your own calculator.
