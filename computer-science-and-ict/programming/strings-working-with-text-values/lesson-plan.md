## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Cutting and joining — the string as a row** — A **string** is a row of characters, and the Text object works it as one. **GetSubText** cuts out a part by its start and length; **GetSubTextToEnd** takes everything from a position to the row's end. **Joining** is the plus sign's second life: `a + b` on strings **glues** them into one longer row — the operation by which greetings are built from names and reports from parts. Cut and join together are the string's scissors and glue: nearly every text program in the book is those two acts, repeated with judgement.
2. **Case, length and finding — knowing the row** — The toolbox's second shelf knows the string. **ConvertToUpperCase** and **ConvertToLowerCase** fold the whole row's case — the great levellers that make 'Mongolia' and 'MONGOLIA' comparable. **GetLength** measures the row in characters. **GetIndexOf** finds where a part stands inside the row; **IsSubText** asks only whether it stands there at all; **StartsWith** and **EndsWith** test the row's two doors. Mongolia's set is complete for its purpose: with cut, join, fold, measure and find, the pupil holds every act the book's text programs — palindromes among them — are built from.
