# List Operations

## Do it first

Grade 8's data-structures unit gives the array's flexible cousin its working lesson: the list — and the five things you may do to it, each with its cost and its care.

## Sub lesson 1 — Adding, inserting, removing — the changing list

A **list** keeps its values **in order, one after another**, and its length is **not fixed at birth**: **add** appends a value at the end; **insert** opens a place in the middle and shifts the rest along; **remove** takes a value out and closes the company up. Each operation is a small honesty test — after it, the list must hold exactly the values it should, in the order it should, with no ghost of the removed and no loss of the shifted.

## Sub lesson 2 — Reaching and walking — index and traversal

A value is **reached** by its **position (index)** — the first, the third, the last — while the list's whole company is **walked** by **traversal**: a loop with a counter visiting every element once, the standing pattern for printing, summing and searching a list. The unit sets list against array as it closes: the array reaches any place at once but cannot grow; the list grows freely but is walked step by step — the data-structures choice the Grade 12 course will examine, met first here in working form.
