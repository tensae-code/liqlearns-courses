# What an Algorithm and a Program Are

## Do it first

Moldova's Grade 1 pupils meet the two biggest words of computing through their shoes and a treasure map: tying shoelaces has an order that must not be shuffled, and the road to the treasure must be written down so another can follow it.

## Sub lesson 1 — An algorithm — the plan in the right order

An **algorithm** is a plan of instructions **in the right order** for finishing a task. The book's example is tying a shoe: cross the laces, make the loop, pass the second lace around, pull tight — do the steps out of order and the knot fails. An algorithm must be **complete** (no step missing), **in order** (each step in its place) and **finished** (it ends with the task done). Recipes, morning routines and the way home are all algorithms of daily life.

In your mind's eye: an algorithm — the plan in the right order.

## Sub lesson 2 — A program — the plan written for an executor

A **program** is an algorithm **written down for an executor** — in the book's lesson, the plan that guides a character to the treasure: forward, forward, turn, forward, drawn as arrows on the map. The writer of the program and its executor are different: the writer thinks and orders; the executor (a person, a character, later a computer) **follows exactly**. If the plan is wrong, the executor still follows it — which is why the next lesson exists: finding the wrong instruction.

In your mind's eye: a program — the plan written for an executor.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: A plan of instructions in the right order for finishing a task. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: Tying a shoe — cross, loop, pass around, pull tight. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: The knot fails — the task is not finished. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: It must be complete, in order, and finished. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
