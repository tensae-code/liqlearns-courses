# Building a Calculator Programme — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What three things does the calculator programme do?**
**Answer: Asks, computes and prints — input, process, output**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What inputs does it ask for?**
**Answer: Two numbers and an operation**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What two design dangers must be decided in advance?**
**Answer: An unknown operation, and division by zero**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) Why is a confidently wrong calculator worse than a refusing one?**
**Answer: It misleads with authority**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) What shape is the calculator's design?**
**Answer: The input-process-output shape, grown to a tool**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What structure chooses the operation?**
**Answer: An if-elif-else chain**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) Why print from one place?**
**Answer: So the output's form is written once**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What tests come first?**
**Answer: Known answers — 2 + 3 must be 5**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) Name two edge tests.**
**Answer: Division by zero and a mistyped operation (also enormous numbers)**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) When is the programme finished?**
**Answer: When the edges behave as the design promised**
