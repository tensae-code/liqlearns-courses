# Lesson plan — Drawing Geometric Figures in PascalABC

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The graphics window and coordinates** — GraphABC opens a **graphics window**: a drawing surface where every point has **coordinates** — its distance across and its distance down from the corner — and every drawing command names the points it needs by their coordinates. A **line** is drawn between two points; a **rectangle** by its opposite corners; a **circle** by its centre and radius. Nothing is dragged with a mouse: the program states the numbers, and the figure appears exactly where the numbers put it — wrong numbers, wrong place, visibly.
2. **Building figures from commands** — Figures are **built**: a house is a rectangle, a triangle roof and a circle window, drawn by a sequence of commands in an order the programmer chooses. Colour and fill commands dress a figure, and a drawing program can be re-run with changed numbers to move or resize the whole construction — the figure is the numbers, so changing the numbers rebuilds it. This section's tasks are geometric in the school sense — constructions and patterns — which is why the Grade 11 programming part opens here: the screen shows at once whether the program, and the geometry inside it, are right.
