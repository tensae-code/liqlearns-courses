**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Programming · **Lesson:** Project — Building a Python Digital Product as a Team
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Kuwait (2026-11-14) — in the title Programming on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Kuwait's student books (Ministry of Education, State of Kuwait), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree and live lesson content were checked for the slug and for near matches before building.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Kuwait's Grade 10 digital product — what a digital product is, forming the team of 4–5 and its roles, and the project steps: idea, PyCharm environment, flowchart, code, testing, sharing
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Digital products and the team — 4–5 members, named roles** — A **digital product** is a finished piece of software made **for users** — it solves a problem or serves a purpose beyond its maker's mark-sheet. The project is worked by a **team of 4–5 members with named roles**: the work is divided so each part — the plan, the code, the testing, the presenting — has an owner, and the team is itself part of the teaching: real products are not built alone, and Kuwait's first Python year refuses to pretend otherwise.
2. **The project steps — idea to sharing** — The build runs the course's own craft in order: the **idea** (the product's purpose, in a sentence a user would want), the **work environment** set up in **PyCharm**, the **flowchart** (the plan drawn before code — the year's drawing lessons spent), the **code** itself, **testing** (the debugger's discipline turned on the team's own work), and **sharing** — the product handed to its users. Six steps, and the sequence is the quality system: a product that skips the flowchart codes its confusions; one that skips testing ships them.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
