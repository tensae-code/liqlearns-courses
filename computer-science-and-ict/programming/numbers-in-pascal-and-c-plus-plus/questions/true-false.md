# Numbers in Pascal and C++ — true or false

Say true or false, and fix the false ones in your own words.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: Digits with an optional sign — 42, −7, +1000**
**Answer: True**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: Digits with an optional sign — 42, −7, +1000** — as the answer to: Why is 1 000 wrong as one constant?
**Answer: False — Spaces may not interrupt a constant — it reads as two**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: Octal and hexadecimal — 017 = 15, 0x1F = 31**
**Answer: True**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: Octal and hexadecimal — 017 = 15, 0x1F = 31** — as the answer to: Does Pascal write integers in other bases?
**Answer: False — No — decimal only, in this course's writing**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: Its reading — base and kind — before its value**
**Answer: True**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: Its reading — base and kind — before its value** — as the answer to: What makes a constant real?
**Answer: False — A fractional part (point) or an exponent (E form)**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: 2.5 times ten to the power 3 — 2500**
**Answer: True**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: 2.5 times ten to the power 3 — 2500** — as the answer to: Why is 3,14 an error?
**Answer: False — The decimal separator is a point in both languages, never a comma**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: Its writing — point or E makes it real; plain digits make it integer**
**Answer: True**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: Its writing — point or E makes it real; plain digits make it integer** — as the answer to: Write 0.0012 in exponent form.
**Answer: False — 1.2E−3 (also written 1.2E-3)**
