# Sorting by Comparing Weights With a Balance — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What does one balance comparison answer?**
**Answer: Which of two objects is heavier — or that they are equal**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What is kept as objects are weighed?**
**Answer: A growing record of comparisons — A > B**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) Why can't the objects be judged by eye?**
**Answer: On purpose — so comparisons are the only road to order**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What is sorting with a balance, in one phrase?**
**Answer: Comparison made physical**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) Where else does this exact craft run?**
**Answer: Inside every comparison sort a computer runs**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What do comparisons cost?**
**Answer: Time, effort, turns of the balance**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What does transitivity give free?**
**Answer: If A > B and B > C, then A > C — no weighing needed**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) Name two economy habits.**
**Answer: Weigh neighbours first; carry winners forward (also: reuse knowledge)**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) What is counted per method?**
**Answer: Comparisons — the cheapest correct method wins**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) What is this, before its name arrives?**
**Answer: Algorithm analysis with your hands**
