# Sorting by Comparing Weights With a Balance

## Do it first

Five parcels, all alike, all different weights, no scale numbers — only a balance that says “left heavier, right heavier, or equal”. Order them lightest to heaviest. Before reading on: how few comparisons can you swear to need?

## Sub lesson 1 — The balance answers one question

A **balance** comparison answers exactly one question about exactly two objects: **which is heavier** (or: equal). Sorting with it is comparison made physical: pick two, weigh, place the knowledge — A > B — in a growing record. The activity's objects cannot be judged by eye on purpose: the only road to order is comparisons, patiently spent, exactly as inside every comparison sort a computer runs.

In your mind's eye: the balance answers one question.

## Sub lesson 2 — Spend comparisons like money

Comparisons cost — time, effort, turns of the balance — so the activity quietly teaches **economy**: once A > B and B > C are known, weighing A against C buys nothing (transitivity already knows). Good sorters plan their comparisons: neighbours first, winners carried forward, knowledge reused. Counting comparisons per method — and crowning the cheapest correct one — is algorithm analysis with your hands, years before the word complexity arrives.
