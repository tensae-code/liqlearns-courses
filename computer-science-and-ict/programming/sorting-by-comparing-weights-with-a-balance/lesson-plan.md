## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The balance answers one question** — A **balance** comparison answers exactly one question about exactly two objects: **which is heavier** (or: equal). Sorting with it is comparison made physical: pick two, weigh, place the knowledge — A > B — in a growing record. The activity's objects cannot be judged by eye on purpose: the only road to order is comparisons, patiently spent, exactly as inside every comparison sort a computer runs.
2. **Spend comparisons like money** — Comparisons cost — time, effort, turns of the balance — so the activity quietly teaches **economy**: once A > B and B > C are known, weighing A against C buys nothing (transitivity already knows). Good sorters plan their comparisons: neighbours first, winners carried forward, knowledge reused. Counting comparisons per method — and crowning the cheapest correct one — is algorithm analysis with your hands, years before the word complexity arrives.
