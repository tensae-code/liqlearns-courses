# Doing My Own Work — With a Computer's Help — matching

Match each item on the left with the one it belongs to on the right.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What is the project's subject?** → **The pupil's own work — the jobs of their own day**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What is listed first?** → **The jobs — what I do, in what order**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) Which parts of a job are marked specially?** → **The parts that are hard, slow, or easy to get wrong**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) Why does the first book end with this project?** → **Because before a school subject, the computer is a helper beside a real child's real work**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) Name three jobs of a school day.** → **Learning lessons, doing homework, drawing (any real jobs of the pupil's day)**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What can a computer help with?** → **Holding and searching information, showing and playing, practising with you, keeping work safe**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What can it not do for you?** → **Understand for you, decide for you, or do the learning itself**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What does each pupil do in the project?** → **Chooses one job, works it with the computer's help, and shows before and after**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) What is the project's rule?** → **The work stays mine — the computer carries the parts it is good at**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) Which parts should the computer carry?** → **The hard, slow or error-prone parts it is good at — not the learning itself**
