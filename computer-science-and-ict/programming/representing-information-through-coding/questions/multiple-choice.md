# Representing Information Through Coding — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) A code is —**
a) A secret  b) An assignment rule pairing information with signs that stand for it  c) A computer program only  d) A kind of paper
**Answer: b) An assignment rule pairing information with signs that stand for it** — The rule is the code; encoding and decoding are its two directions.

**2. (Sub lesson 1) Decoding is —**
a) Inventing a rule  b) Reading information back from its signs, using the rule  c) Hiding a message  d) Sending a message
**Answer: b) Reading information back from its signs, using the rule** — Encoding writes with the rule; decoding reads back with it.

**3. (Sub lesson 1) Morse code fits a channel that —**
a) Prints colour  b) Can only beep, flash or tap  c) Carries photographs  d) Stores files
**Answer: b) Can only beep, flash or tap** — Codes are chosen for their channel — Morse's dots and dashes suit beeps and flashes.

**4. (Sub lesson 2) A stream that can be split back in two different ways shows the code fails at being —**
a) Fast  b) Pretty  c) Uniquely decodable  d) Old
**Answer: c) Uniquely decodable** — If the split is not unique, decoding becomes guessing.

**5. (Sub lesson 2) In Morse, E is a single dot mainly because —**
a) E is unimportant  b) E is the most frequent letter — frequent items get short signs  c) The inventor liked E  d) Dots are rare
**Answer: b) E is the most frequent letter — frequent items get short signs** — Short-for-frequent keeps messages quick and small.

**6. (Sub lesson 2) A barcode's last digit is a check digit. Its job is to —**
a) Raise the price  b) Let the reader notice a misread or damage  c) Name the shop  d) Decorate the packet
**Answer: b) Let the reader notice a misread or damage** — A check digit is a tripwire computed from the other digits.
