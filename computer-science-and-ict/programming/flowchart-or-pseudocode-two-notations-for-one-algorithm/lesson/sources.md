**Acceptance count: taught in 13 countries (Ethiopia, North Macedonia, Iraq, Uzbekistan, Cuba, Malaysia, Sri Lanka, Greece, Jordan, Armenia, Indonesia, South Sudan, Pakistan).**

North Macedonia's own way (Year 1): five themes move from modern digital technology and online living into a full structured-programming course taught in C++ (with Scratch/C++/Java/Python/PHP/Lisp compared), then advanced Word work (styles, contents, templates) and spreadsheets up to pivot tables.

Iraq's own way (Grade 7): a four-unit spiral repeated at every grade — computer fundamentals, computer software (here: the operating system plus an unusually deep treatment of Windows right-click shortcut menus), algorithms and programming principles (ending in Scratch), and information technology and the internet — every lesson built on the Iraqi five-step learning cycle with opening activity, main idea, learning outcomes, vocabulary and lesson review.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Grade 11): the senior guide turns learners into junior system administrators and builders — computer administration, datasheets ("data books") and timing technology, documenting server and printer configurations, data science, pseudocode, Green IT, and building static web pages with tools like Canva and Figma; typing rises to 40 wpm at 85–100% accuracy. (teacher guide — evidence)


Uzbekistan's own way (Grade 9): grade 9 is the capstone — computer graphics, Access, Excel, then a nine-lesson algorithms block and a 28-lesson Turbo Pascal 7.0 course that runs from the alphabet of the language to files, procedures and graphics mode, closing with the Internet. Every chapter ends with a dedicated repetition lesson, placed here to that chapter's main lesson.

Cuba's own way (Grade 9): Grade 9 is the formal programming-foundations year — algorithms, data, variables, counters/accumulators, flowcharts and pseudocode, taught language-independently — paired with a full cybersecurity chapter (passwords/MFA, safe internet, safe social networks).

Malaysia's own way (Grade 7, ASK Tingkatan 1): the KSSM Asas Sains Komputer (ASK — Foundations of Computer Science) course is built on the four computational-thinking techniques (decomposition, pattern recognition, abstraction, generalisation) introduced through a local food example (making popia basah); programming is done in Scratch 2.0, then learners build a real interactive website in HTML in the same year; every chapter ends with a mind map, self-check list and self-practice.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 11, Tamil): a full Visual Basic 6.0 programming course inside the O/L book (IDE, controls, data types, Select Case, arrays), an SDLC chapter, Photoshop-based multimedia, hand-coded HTML with Sri Lankan examples (the Sinharaja rainforest page, ucsc.cmb.ac.lk addresses, sltnet.lk domains), and sector chapters (agriculture, industry, government, commerce) before malware and health.

Greece's own way (Grade 7, Pliroforiki A' Gymnasiou, 2024): the new Gymnasium informatics course is built around algorithms from the first page (a Python binary example in Unit 1), then hardware/software, the internet with a full copyright/Creative Commons/AI-copyright sequence, cybersecurity with cryptography, word processing, algorithmics, Scratch storytelling/chatbot projects, and a long society unit (robots, drones, autonomous cars, e-government, AI in education/medicine).

Greece's own way (Grade 11, Eisagogi stis Arches tis Epistimis ton H/Y B' Lykeiou): a theory-first introduction to computer science in three units — basic concepts (theoretical vs applied CS), theoretical CS (problem, algorithms in depth including complexity/computability, data structures operations, debugging logic errors and documentation, programming paradigms and structured programming, software life cycle), and applied CS (operating systems in depth, information systems with databases and SQL/XML, networks, AI).

Greece's own way (Grade 12, Anaptyxi Efarmogon — AEPP): Greece's national-exam programming book — problem analysis, algorithms, data structures (arrays, stack, queue, lists, trees, graphs; search and sort; recursion), algorithm design methods (divide-and-conquer, dynamic programming, greedy), algorithm performance/complexity/correctness, then programming in Greece's own school pseudocode language ΓΛΩΣΣΑ (selection/repetition commands, one- and multi-dimensional arrays, subprograms with parameters and scope, recursion), object-oriented and event-driven programming, GUI elements, user-interface design, debugging and runtime errors, and program evaluation/documentation/software life cycle.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 7 G7T2 Student): Citizenship comes before code in Term 2 — digital citizenship, intellectual property with the actual patent-application procedures, data privacy law, and balanced use of communication tools — then a pure algorithms unit with no computer needed (writing conditions, tracking, representation, flowcharts, improving algorithms).

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 6 G6 student): Same author pair as Grade 5, one year on: the § sequence moves from society/data-rights into number systems and computing systems, then a long spreadsheet-and-diagrams block, and ends with algorithms in flowcharts and a block-based programming language.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 9 G9 Informatika student): Grade 9 completes junior-high Informatika with tree/graph data structures, a full data-analysis workflow, modular programming and the block-to-text transition (Scratch/Blockly toward C and Python), productivity content design with AI, and a full digital-security chapter.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 10 G10 Informatika SMK Sem1): The SMK Semester 1 book teaches the same Informatika elements with a logic-first opening (propositions and inference), applied office integration, hands-on network-device configuration, and an OS-installation practical.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 10 G10 Informatika SMK Sem2): Semester 2 turns to algorithms in depth, the social side of informatics (history of IT, intellectual property, professions), and a full informatics project-management chapter — plan, monitor, evaluate.

South Sudan's own way (Grade 11 S3 Student): Year 3 is creation and programming: learners build and run a real personal website/blog end-to-end in WordPress (hosting, domain, themes, posts, legal issues and comment moderation), study social networks platform by platform (LinkedIn, Google+ and the Students Circle Network, with South Sudanese business use), then learn programming through data types, arrays and structures in C/C++ and writing programs in Notepad, closing on ethical conduct and intellectual property.

South Sudan's own way (Grade 11 S3 Teacher): The guide teaches the same three units through plenaries, discussion and problem-based learning, and adds subject content the student book does not have: a full Blogger classroom-blog editor guide, WordPress.com vs WordPress.org, eight orientations for teaching programming, and a long C/C++ practical guide with worked examples and ten answered practical programs (pay, banknotes, passwords, arrays, quadrants). (teacher guide — evidence)


Pakistan's own way (Grade 9 Computer Science 9): Grade 9 is a full problem-solving course before any coding — candid solutions, the 5 Ws of problem analysis, test data in five types, trace tables, verification vs validation — then number systems carried through to Boolean algebra with truth tables, networks taught through the postal-system analogy for TCP/IP layers, a data-and-privacy unit built on Code.org cipher widgets, NADRA and Pakistan's NR3C cybercrime centre, and a first website built page by page as "Pakistan.html".
