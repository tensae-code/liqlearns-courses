Moldova's own way (Grade 10, Informatică): the manual teaches Pascal and C++ side by side — each school chooses one or both — and upper-secondary programming is language-formal: BNF metalanguage and syntax diagrams, ordinal types, type conversions, every concept given in Pascal for teaching and in C++ for a technology career.

Moldova's own way (Grade 11, Informatică): Grade 11 is the theory year — how numbers, logic and circuits actually work inside the machine (Boolean algebra, adders, flip-flops, instruction formats, assembly language) — with structured programming (arrays, records, sets, files) carried over from Grade 10's languages; some sections are profile-specific (Real/Umanist).


Kazakhstan's own way (Salgaraeva G10 Informatika, natural-science track): in Grade 10 the subject splits by track; this natural-science book is the deepest in the set — networks with IP, domains and VPN, number systems and logic with truth tables and the computer's logical elements, procedures/functions, strings, files, sorting methods and graph algorithms, a full HTML/CSS/scripts web part, and information systems from Big Data to linking web pages with a database. Every topic is doubled with a practicum paragraph.

Kazakhstan's own way (Zhunusov G10 Computer Science, English-medium): the English-medium Grade 10 book is the most technical of the wave: the CIA triad and three separate authentication lessons (passwords twice, biometrics), encoding standards, Python dictionaries, DFS/BFS graph traversal, and a full SQL chapter in MySQL (CREATE, INSERT, SELECT/WHERE, MIN/MAX, JOIN, GROUP BY, SELECT INTO) before a JavaScript chapter. (Its preface is the publisher's generic science template, left over from another book.)

Kazakhstan's own way (Ataeva & Vyshkova G8 Informatics, NIS): the NIS Grade 8 course is the most distinctive of the wave: vector graphics flows into geographic information systems (planning an excursion, making maps, visualising data in GIS), spreadsheets are used to solve logical problems, loops are taught through mathematics, physics, business and multimedia problems, and the year ends with a full project cycle — six stages, presentation, documentation, defence — plus a mini-project on artificial intelligence.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G8 «Информатика» 2019 edition): the 2019 Almatykitap Grade 8 book splits "measuring information" into two named approaches — the probability approach and the alphabet approach — and its IDE section is a Pascal/Lazarus visual-component environment, ending in subroutines; the 2023 re-issue (152 pages, Book 21) is a different, shorter book.

Kazakhstan's own way (Atayeva/Vyushkova/Zhurbenko G8 «Информатика» NIS): the NIS Grade 8 book is the widest of the set: a whole section on vector graphics and geographic information systems (planning an excursion, making maps, visualising data in GIS), octal and hexadecimal coding, loops applied separately to mathematics, physics, business and animation, strings and subroutines, and a nine-step project method ending in a mini-project on artificial intelligence.
