# Lesson plan — Stopping Scripts — and Moving a Sprite with X and Y Coordinates

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Programming · **Lesson:** Stopping Scripts — and Moving a Sprite with X and Y Coordinates
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Finland (2026-11-14) — in the title Programming on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Finland's *Ensiaskeleet ohjelmointiin* (student book, Oppilaan kirja, Versio 2.0), because no existing lesson teaches this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A project must be able to stop on purpose — stop all, stop this script, or stop other scripts in the sprite — and a sprite's exact place on the stage is its x and y coordinates, changed to move it left, right, up or down by precise steps.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on Scratch scripts and loops; the three stop choices and the coordinate way of moving are built inside the lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Stopping scripts: all, this one, or the others** — Choose stop all, stop this script or stop other scripts for a situation.
2. **Moving a sprite by x and y** — Move a sprite in four directions by changing x and y.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Scratch Loops and Conditions in Programming. Recommended, never forced — a learner may land on this lesson directly, and it stands alone.
- Finland's own way is kept: this placement sits in Finland's two-part Scratch path — Part 1 moves towards the interactive-story project and Part 2 towards the self-built maze game, in the Luetaan (Let's read) then Ohjelmoidaan (Let's program) rhythm, with pair work, reflection and peer showing built in.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say the difference between stop all, stop this script and stop other scripts.
2. Choose the right stop for a game-over moment.
3. Say what a sprite's x and y coordinates are.
4. Move a sprite left, right, up and down by changing x and y.

## What you need
A computer with Scratch.

## If the learner is stuck
Something still runs after 'game over' → a stop-this-script was used where stop all was needed. Sprite moves the wrong way → check which coordinate you changed: sideways is x, up/down is y, and up means a bigger y.
