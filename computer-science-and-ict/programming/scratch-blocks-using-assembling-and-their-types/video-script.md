# Video script — Scratch Blocks — Using, Assembling and Their Types

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Blocks are **dragged** from the palette to the script area and **assembled** by snapping: a block's shape declares where it can join — stack blocks pile vertically into a sequence, and a script runs **from its top (its event) downward**. Blocks that do not fit cannot be forced; the shapes are the syntax, worn visibly. A script is **tested by clicking it**, and taken apart by dragging its lower half away — assembling and disassembling are the programmer's daily hand movements, and fluency in them is the Grade 8 lesson's first aim.
Blocks sort by shape and job: **event (hat) blocks** start scripts; **command (stack) blocks** do acts; **reporter blocks** (rounded) hand back values and fit inside others; **boolean reporters** (pointed) answer true/false for conditions; **C blocks** wrap other blocks inside them (loops and conditions — the wrap is the meaning); and **cap blocks** end a script. Jordan's types lesson is the Scratch unit's grammar chapter: a pupil who can name a block's type can predict a strange script's reading before running it — the beginning of reading code, in any language.
