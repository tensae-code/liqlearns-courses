Sudan's own way (Grade 9): the whole Grade 9 book is programming — concepts and Pascal-style examples first, then algorithms and flowcharts, then a full Scratch course ending in a designed project. The Bakht Al-Ruda ICT series runs in the same pattern from Grade 4 to Grade 9.

Libya's own way (Grade 11 Term 1, Information Technology student book): a programming year taught in old-school order — problem solving and algorithms, flowcharts, memory/variables, then Visual Basic statement by statement (LET, PRINT, INPUTBOX, IF, SELECT CASE, FOR…NEXT, DO…WHILE, DO…UNTIL), every chapter ending in graded exercises. The book is printed for BOTH the Science and Humanities sections (للقسمين العلمي والأدبي), although the records label it Humanities only — cited as printed, and taught to Science and Humanities together.

Libya's own way (Grade 11 Term 1, Information Technology teacher guide): the guide restates each programming chapter as target learning outcomes plus fully worked exercise solutions — the Visual Basic course is assessed through these set exercises. The guide is printed for both the Science and Humanities sections. (teacher guide — evidence)

Malaysia's own way (Grade 10, SK Tingkatan 4): the upper-secondary Sains Komputer (SK — Computer Science) elective switches language to Java (NetBeans) and is organised as three big project-style chapters — Programming, Relational Databases (Access, ERD, normalisation to 3NF, macros/switchboard, a full SDLC database project), and Human-Computer Interaction with screen-design prototyping and quantitative evaluation (prototypes built in Java NetBeans).

Kuwait's own way (Grade 10 Information Technology Term 1): secondary school switches to the subject name Information Technology and a more academic register: a full cyber-security unit built on the CIA triad (with hands-on two-factor authentication in Google Authenticator and the Ministry app), then a complete first Python course in PyCharm — variables, strings, conditions, loops, debugging and exceptions — and a team-built digital product.

Greece's own way (Grade 12, Pliroforiki Supplementary Material): the official supplementary volume expands exactly five AEPP areas with worked implementations — stack and queue implemented on a one-dimensional array, lists/trees/graphs at abstract level with diagrams, divide-and-conquer through binary search and its variants, the ΕΠΙΛΕΞΕ command, a full object-oriented unit (methodology, class diagrams, abstraction/encapsulation, inheritance, polymorphism), and a debugging unit organised by where logic errors hide (selection, repetition, arrays, subprograms) plus black-box testing; its own mapping table ties every unit back to the main book's chapters.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 8 G8 student): The Velijanyan & Aslanyan series continues: after e-security and information sources it turns practical — multimedia slides, spreadsheets, blogging and online content — then computer architecture (von Neumann, memory, I/O), networks, and a Python block (logic, loops, lists, functions, errors, comments, debugging).

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 8 G8 teacher): Matches Book 14 exactly: its mapping table ties the student book's 19 §s to 14 standard themes (8-1 to 8-14), each theme with outcomes, competencies and practical work; the guide adds a thematic plan, the book's questions/tasks with answers, and slide-presentation assessment criteria.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 11 G11 student): Grade 11 (Stepanyan et al., Zangak) is the most advanced book in the set: online payment systems, digital biography, software licences, inclusive technology, logic-circuit adders, network performance, big data in tables, 1D/2D arrays, sorting and searching, recursion, OOP classes and inheritance, error types and algorithm speed — most of it beyond the current ICT course's 584 lessons, hence the high NEW share.

Vietnam's own way (Grade 10 Tin học 10): the Grade 10 book is one shared book for both orientations (ICT-coloured and CS-coloured lesson title bands tell each track its own lessons, per its preface); its data strand is unusually deep for school level — binary integers, logic data, sound and image data — and its programming strand is a complete first Python course from variables to functions, scope, error recognition and testing.


Pakistan's own way (Grade 6 Computer Science 6): every unit opens with Students Learning Outcomes, is dotted with "Do you Know?", "Extra Bit!" and "Tips" boxes, and closes with Summary, MCQ/short/detailed exercises, project-based and activity-based questions, with an answers key at the back. Digital skills (Windows desktop, file management, Paint 3D, a typing tutor) are taught hands-on BEFORE programming; digital citizenship is framed through Pakistani citizenship first; the entrepreneurship unit teaches from Pakistani founders (Foodpanda/EatOye, Shopsy.pk, Zero Makeup, Investors Lounge).

Pakistan's own way (Grade 8 Computer Education 8): the Grade 8 book is the older National-Curriculum-2006 Punjab book (not SNC): it teaches networks through physical media and cutting-edge wireless (cellular, satellite, GPS, Bluetooth), a whole unit of hands-on antivirus management (definitions, updates, an AVG whole-computer scan), Excel 2007 taught through three full worked reports (monthly expenses, salaries, school results), and programming in GW-BASIC with line numbers — the pre-SNC Punjab way into code.
