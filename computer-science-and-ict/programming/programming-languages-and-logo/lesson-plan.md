# Lesson plan — Programming languages — and why LOGO

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Programming · **Lesson:** Programming languages — and why LOGO
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 12 — Programming in the Computer Science and ICT ladder (TITLES.md); stocked from Ethiopia's Grade 7 book, Unit 6 — Logic Oriented Graphics Oriented (LOGO) Programming. The ladder is the recommended order, never a forced one.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a programming language is an artificial language for instructing a computer; LOGO was designed for ease of use and graphics, and its turtle graphics build complex pictures from minimal coding.
**This lesson's own level:** it opens the Programming title — it stands on everything before it only in spirit: the learner now stops using programs and starts writing them.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or in steps — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define a programming language.
2. Name the four languages the book names.
3. Say what LOGO was designed for, and what turtle graphics are.
4. Give the book's three reasons for learning LOGO.

## What you need
Paper, a pencil, and a partner for the opening game.
