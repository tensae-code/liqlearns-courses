**Acceptance count: taught in 9 countries (Ethiopia, Egypt, Malaysia, Sri Lanka, Bangladesh, Jordan, Armenia, Indonesia, Vietnam).**

As printed: model 3's loop check reads “less than 10” in the book (printed p. 210); the lesson keeps the book's printed check and names it as printed, rather than silently rewriting the limit.

Mauritania's own way (Grade 9): The final secondary year is five practical blocks in 30-minute fiches — a full PowerPoint sequence, internet ethics, a first formal algorithms unit (variables, conditions, loops, writing and testing algorithms, no named programming language), simple networks, and email with attachments — each block closed by evaluation fiches.

Egypt's own way (Grade 10, Term 1 Programming and Artificial Intelligence student book): A full Japanese-style "Information Study" course adopted for Egypt's new secondary track: 13 chapters running from information theory, ethics and security through number systems, data analysis (frequency, variance, correlation, regression in spreadsheets), simulation and queues, Python, and a final AI-programming project chapter building web pages, a quiz app and a block-breaker game with generative AI.

Malaysia's own way (Grade 7, ASK Tingkatan 1): the KSSM Asas Sains Komputer (ASK — Foundations of Computer Science) course is built on the four computational-thinking techniques (decomposition, pattern recognition, abstraction, generalisation) introduced through a local food example (making popia basah); programming is done in Scratch 2.0, then learners build a real interactive website in HTML in the same year; every chapter ends with a mind map, self-check list and self-practice.

Malaysia's own way (Grade 8, ASK Tingkatan 2): data representation goes well beyond binary — octal and hexadecimal are taught as full conversion systems tied to ASCII and machine language; code work moves from Scratch (Tingkatan 1) to text Python 3.5.2, with data types, constants and operators treated formally before control structures.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 6 Reading Book): a story/dialogue-led "Reading Book" with a talking computer character; every chapter cross-refers to numbered Workbook activities; local sectors (school office, ATM, ICU, factories, agriculture) and the 3R e-waste method are taught from Grade 6.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 6 Workbook): a companion workbook whose numbered activities (1.1–6.2) are referenced activity-by-activity from the Reading Book; fill-in, labelling and categorising exercises rather than separate theory.

Switzerland's own way (Grades 1–2, Décodage 1re–2e): Vaud's Romandie digital-education curriculum is taught in three axes — Science informatique (SI), Médias (M), Usages (U) — almost entirely UNPLUGGED at this age: algorithms are learned with the body (robot game, crane game, hand-washing, getting dressed, a crêpe recipe), a physical sorting machine, and the Blue-Bot floor robot; the class also builds a collective digital “charter book” of good digital habits across the whole cycle.

Switzerland's own way (Grades 3–4, Décodage 3e–4e): the 3e–4e volume repeats the 1re–2e unplugged activities at a higher level (loops and debugging join the robot and crane games and the sorting machine) and adds three new strands: Thymio robotics, a full ScratchJr scenario, and media “parcours” — Parcours des bestioles (how images influence emotions), Parcours du partage (image rights, digital traces, thinking before sharing) and Parcours de la pub (recognising and creating advertising). Pixel paravent introduces binary coding as transmitting a black-and-white image across a screen with an agreed code; Deviner la carte teaches the conditional through a magic trick and its logigramme.

Switzerland's own way (Grades 5–6, Décodage 5e–6e): Cycle 2 moves from unplugged games to real tools while keeping the game frame: the imaginary city “Square CT” teaches classification, decision trees, conditionals, indentation and flowcharts (logigrammes); ScratchJr tells emotion and climate stories; the Swiss Thymio robot is programmed in VPL; data is taught through binary pixel coding and colour coding; and “Enquêtes” (inquiries) teach addition-as-algorithm, debugging, sorting, the Nim winning strategy, Caesar ciphers and sensor experiments. Médias/Usages scenarios cover cyberbullying, screen practices, news and media stereotypes.

Switzerland's own way (Grades 7–8, Décodage 7e–8e): at 7e–8e the Décodage course becomes a real computer-science course under the Plan d'études romand EN 22/23: Thymio VPL missions (including Morse communication and self-driving-car autonomy levels), a full Scratch discovery course (movement, shortening programs, sub-programs as own blocks, remixing), a Scratch video-game project run in “studios” with a cahier des charges, data work (Huffman-style compression via Zip la marmotte, binary/octal/hex/ASCII codings, file sizes), access security (passwords, file encryption, phishing, authentication), home networks at two levels (IP addresses; message routing, packets, TCP and encryption), and inquiries into sorting, sorting networks, Caesar decryption and parity-bit error detection. Médias/Usages covers social networks and digital identity/footprint, information sources, cyberbullying and the environmental cost of digital technology.

Bangladesh's own way (Grade 7, Digital Technology): the Grade 7 book continues the Learning-Experience project format — intellectual property, virtual identity, cybercrime, robotics-through-pseudocode and e-services are each learned by doing a class project with the recurring robot character Nano. The NCTB Digital Technology line runs in parallel English and Bangla editions; placements are cited from the English Version with the Bangla Version parallel spot-checked.

Bangladesh's own way (HSC ICT, Classes XI–XII and Alim): one compulsory ICT book for every HSC and Alim student in the country, saturated with Bangladesh itself: Bangabandhu Satellite-1 and its ground stations, bdjobs and chakri.com job portals, Daraz and Bikroy e-commerce, the Walton factory's automated production line, the National ID card used to teach primary keys and its database used to teach RDBMS at work, a full Bangla Unicode table and a worked example of putting Bangla text on a web page with meta charset, the national letter-grade scale (A+ from 80) as the else-if programme, "I Love Bangladesh" as the loop example text, an algorithm for reaching college by bicycle or rickshaw, and the Digital Bangladesh programme — National Data Centre, hi-tech parks, Startup Bangladesh and Bangladesh Bank's QR-based Cashless Bangladesh. C is the HSC programming language, taught with scanf/printf in Code::Blocks.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 7 G7T2 Student): Citizenship comes before code in Term 2 — digital citizenship, intellectual property with the actual patent-application procedures, data privacy law, and balanced use of communication tools — then a pure algorithms unit with no computer needed (writing conditions, tracking, representation, flowcharts, improving algorithms).

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 2 G2 student): A story-led primary book built around two Grade 2 characters (Hayk and Arpi); every lesson uses fixed activity "rooms" (key knowledge, key words, skills/tasks) and QR codes link to audio/video material; safety and health come before any machine skills.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 2 G2 teacher): Companion to Book 1 in the Zangak guide template: Grade 2 standard outcomes, an exemplary programme, a 32-hour thematic plan covering the student book's twelve themes with practical works and two summary tests, sample lesson plans (the first teaches the "Important commands" lesson — select, cut, copy, paste, undo, redo), and worked explanations of the book's electronic tasks, opening with correct sitting posture at the computer.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 7 G7 Informatika student): Kurikulum Merdeka Informatika built on four elements (Berpikir Komputasional, Literasi Digital, Analisis Data, Algoritma dan Pemrograman), mixing plugged and unplugged activities with coded activity IDs (e.g. VII-BK, VII-LD) and QR-linked resources; Grade 7 covers only BK + Literasi Digital + data handling.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 10 G10 Informatika SMK Sem2): Semester 2 turns to algorithms in depth, the social side of informatics (history of IT, intellectual property, professions), and a full informatics project-management chapter — plan, monitor, evaluate.

Vietnam's own way (Grade 6 Tin học 6): the first lower-secondary book names its software openly (Google Chrome, MindMaple Lite, Microsoft Word in the how-to-use page) and its three characters each stand for one strand of the subject — digital literacy, ICT, and computer science; information safety gets a full lesson before any applied software work.
