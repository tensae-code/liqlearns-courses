# Moving a Robot on a Grid — Quadrillage

## Do it first

The mat is a chequerboard and the robot a knight that only walks straight lines. It must visit the flower, avoid the puddle, and come home. Before reading on, trace the route with your finger — counting squares, not centimetres.

## Sub lesson 1 — The grid is the robot's map

On a **grid (quadrillage)**, every place is a square, and movement is counted in **squares**: forward two squares, turn, forward one square. The séance teaches grid discipline: know where the robot **is**, which way it **faces**, and count its steps in its own unit — the square — because the robot knows no other geography. A route is correct on the grid before it is ever run on the mat.

In your mind's eye: the grid is the robot's map.

## Sub lesson 2 — Turns happen in place

On the grid, a **turn changes facing, not place**: the robot pivots on its square, and the next forward goes a new way. Beginners' classic bug lives here — planning turns as moves, or forgetting that after a turn, “forward” has changed meaning. The séance drills routes with several turns until facing is tracked as carefully as position: a grid program is two counts kept at once, squares and facings.
