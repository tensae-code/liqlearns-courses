# Procedures and Functions — Actual and Formal Parameters

## Do it first

Three Greece books teach this one handshake — the main book, its study guide side by side, and the workbook in activities — because the exam returns to it every year: what crosses between a caller and its subprogram, and under which names.

## Sub lesson 1 — Procedures do, functions answer

A **procedure (διαδικασία)** is a subprogram called to **do** a job — print the table, sort the array, draw the shape; it is called as a **command in its own line** (ΚΑΛΕΣΕ in ΓΛΩΣΣΑ) and hands back its results **through its parameters**. A **function (συνάρτηση)** is a subprogram called to **answer one value** — the maximum, the average, the root; it is called **inside an expression**, wherever a value could stand, and hands its answer back as that value. The study guide works the two side by side on the same jobs so the difference is practised, not merely stated.

In your mind's eye: procedures do, functions answer.

## Sub lesson 2 — Formal and actual parameters — the handshake

The subprogram's heading declares its **formal parameters (τυπικές παράμετροι)** — the names the piece uses for the values it will receive. The call supplies **actual parameters (πραγματικές παράμετροι)** — the caller's own variables or values, matched to the formals **in order**. The chapter's care points are the exam's traps: the counts must match, the **types** must match, and whether a changed formal reaches the caller's actual depends on the passing the language makes — so the trace of a call, formals bound to actuals one by one, is drawn by hand until it is habit.

In your mind's eye: formal and actual parameters — the handshake.
