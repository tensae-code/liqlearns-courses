# Lesson plan — Loops and Graphics — Patterns and Randomness

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Programming · **Lesson:** Loops and Graphics — Patterns and Randomness
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Mongolia (2026-11-14) — in the title Programming on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Mongolia's student books (Ministry of Education, Culture and Science(s) of Mongolia, Ulaanbaatar), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree and live lesson content were checked for the slug and for near matches before building.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Loops joined with graphics in Small Basic — nested squares drawn by repetition, random colours and numbers, and the Mongolian ornament patterns (alkhan khee meander, khaan buguivch knot) drawn by code
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Loops drawing — nested squares and repetition** — A **loop around drawing commands** turns repetition into design: draw a square, shift or grow, draw again — the book's **nested squares** appear, square inside square, from a few lines of code. The loop's counter becomes a coordinate or a size, so each pass draws the same shape changed by a rule. This is the graphics payoff of the loop lessons: what the hand could draw once, the loop draws in ordered families, exactly, tirelessly.
2. **Randomness and the Mongolian patterns** — The **Math object's random numbers** break the order on purpose: **random colours** and positions scatter drawings into fields no two runs repeat — controlled surprise, set inside limits the programmer chooses. Then order returns at its highest: the book draws Mongolia's own ornaments — the **alkhan khee**, the endless meander, and the **khaan buguivch** knot pattern — with loops and graphics together. The lesson's claim is cultural as much as technical: a repeating pattern IS a loop, and the traditional patterns of Mongolia are programs that were drawn by hand for centuries before they were written in code.
