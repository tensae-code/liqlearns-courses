# Labels in Pascal and C++ — matching

Match each item on the left with the one it belongs to on the right.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What is a label?** → **A mark attached to an instruction — an address in the program text**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) How is a label written?** → **Before its instruction, separated by a colon — 10: writeln(x);**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What form do Pascal labels take?** → **Unsigned integer constants, declared in a label declaration**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What form do C++ labels take?** → **Identifiers — no declaration needed**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) Does a label do any work?** → **No — it exists only to be jumped to (by goto)**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) Why are labels rarely used now?** → **Structured instructions give every honest loop and branch a readable form**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) Where does the labelled jump survive?** → **At the edges — leaving deep nests at once, machine-generated code**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What is the course's reason for teaching labels?** → **Recognition — and knowing why its programs almost never wear one**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) What instruction uses a label?** → **goto**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) Is a label a variable?** → **No — it has no value and holds nothing; it names a place**
