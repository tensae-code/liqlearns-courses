# The Goto Instruction — and Why It Is Avoided — true or false

Say true or false, and fix the false ones in your own words.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: It transfers control at once to the labelled instruction, skipping everything between**
**Answer: True**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: It transfers control at once to the labelled instruction, skipping everything between** — as the answer to: Can goto jump both ways?
**Answer: False — Yes — forward (a branch) or backward (a loop, with an if)**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: Any loop or branch**
**Answer: True**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: Any loop or branch** — as the answer to: What is goto's seduction?
**Answer: False — Total freedom — it asks no design and fits every situation**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: A label marking the instruction**
**Answer: True**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: A label marking the instruction** — as the answer to: What is spaghetti code?
**Answer: False — Code whose control flow tangles like spaghetti strands from free jumps — unreadable at a glance**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: Governed instructions — sequence, if, while, for, repeat, case — each with one entry and one exit**
**Answer: True**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: Governed instructions — sequence, if, while, for, repeat, case — each with one entry and one exit** — as the answer to: Where does goto survive legitimately?
**Answer: False — Rare edges — escaping several nested loops at once, languages without better tools**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: Know goto, recognise it, and write without it**
**Answer: True**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: Know goto, recognise it, and write without it** — as the answer to: Why is a structured instruction readable locally?
**Answer: False — Its one entry and one exit bound its whole story on the page**
