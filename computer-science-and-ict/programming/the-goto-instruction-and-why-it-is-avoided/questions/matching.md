# The Goto Instruction — and Why It Is Avoided — matching

Match each item on the left with the one it belongs to on the right.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What does goto do?** → **It transfers control at once to the labelled instruction, skipping everything between**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) Can goto jump both ways?** → **Yes — forward (a branch) or backward (a loop, with an if)**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What can be improvised with goto alone?** → **Any loop or branch**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What is goto's seduction?** → **Total freedom — it asks no design and fits every situation**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) What does a goto need in its target?** → **A label marking the instruction**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What is spaghetti code?** → **Code whose control flow tangles like spaghetti strands from free jumps — unreadable at a glance**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What did structured programming answer with?** → **Governed instructions — sequence, if, while, for, repeat, case — each with one entry and one exit**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) Where does goto survive legitimately?** → **Rare edges — escaping several nested loops at once, languages without better tools**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) What is the course's rule?** → **Know goto, recognise it, and write without it**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) Why is a structured instruction readable locally?** → **Its one entry and one exit bound its whole story on the page**
