# Lesson plan - High-level languages - compilers and interpreters

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Programming · **Lesson:** High-level languages - compilers and interpreters
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 6 - Fundamentals of Programming (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** high-level languages sit close to human language; a compiler translates a whole program at once, while an interpreter translates and executes one statement at a time.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on it stands on Machine language and assembly language - the low-level languages and their translation problem. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on - an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of ..." in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or in steps - a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order - the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **High-level languages - close to human language** - High-level languages sit closer to human language and let the programmer concentrate on the problem rather than the language; the named examples are C, C++, Java, C#, Python, Perl and Ruby.
2. **Compilers - the whole program at once** - A compiler translates the whole program at once and the translated form is then executed; C, C++, Java and C# work this way.
3. **Interpreters - one statement at a time** - An interpreter translates and executes one statement at a time; Python, Perl and Ruby work this way. The GIF shows the line-by-line rhythm.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson - no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what makes a language high-level, and name the book's examples.
2. Say what a compiler does, and name the languages that work that way.
3. Say what an interpreter does, and name the languages that work that way.
4. Tell the two translators apart in one sentence each.

## If the learner is stuck
- Swaps the two translators -> anchor on the timing word: a compiler is whole-program-AT-ONCE (all translation before any running); an interpreter is ONE-STATEMENT-at-a-time (translation and running take turns).
- Thinks high-level means no translation -> every language except machine language needs translation; high-level only changes how the translation is organised.
