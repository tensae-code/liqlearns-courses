# Converting Between Binary, Octal, Hexadecimal and Decimal — true or false

Say true or false, and fix the false ones in your own words.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: Place-value expansion into decimal, and repeated division out of it**
**Answer: True**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: Place-value expansion into decimal, and repeated division out of it** — as the answer to: What is the power-of-two shortcut?
**Answer: False — Grouping bits — threes for octal, fours for hexadecimal**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: Through binary — expand to bits, regroup**
**Answer: True**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: Through binary — expand to bits, regroup** — as the answer to: What is the proof habit?
**Answer: False — Convert back and land on the number you left**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: Every pair among the four systems**
**Answer: True**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: Every pair among the four systems** — as the answer to: How do fractions convert?
**Answer: False — Repeated multiplication by the new base, taking whole parts as digits**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: 0.101**
**Answer: True**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: 0.101** — as the answer to: What happens to 0.1 decimal in binary?
**Answer: False — It becomes an endless pattern**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: That it does not finish, and the precision kept**
**Answer: True**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: That it does not finish, and the precision kept** — as the answer to: In what order are fraction digits taken?
**Answer: False — In the order the whole parts appear — first is nearest the point**
