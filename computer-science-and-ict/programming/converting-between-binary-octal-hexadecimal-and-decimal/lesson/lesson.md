# Converting Between Binary, Octal, Hexadecimal and Decimal

## Do it first

Earlier lessons converted along single roads: into decimal, out of decimal, between the power-of-two cousins. This lesson completes the map — every road between the four systems of the HSC chapter, including the road most courses skip: the fractions, the digits after the point.

## Sub lesson 1 — Whole numbers — every pair of bases

The four systems convert by two master methods and one shortcut. **Into decimal**: multiply each digit by its place value and add. **Out of decimal**: divide repeatedly by the new base and read the remainders backwards. **Between binary, octal and hexadecimal**: group the bits — threes for octal, fours for hexadecimal — with no arithmetic at all. Every pair of bases is one of these, or two in sequence through decimal or binary. The proof habit of the earlier lessons stands: convert back, and land on the number you left.

## Sub lesson 2 — Fractions — the point travels too

A fraction converts by the mirror method: **multiply repeatedly by the new base**, taking the whole part that pops out as the next digit after the point, until the fraction is spent or the digits suffice. So 0.625 in decimal: ×2 = 1.25 → digit 1; ×2 = 0.5 → 0; ×2 = 1.0 → 1: **0.101 in binary**. Some fractions never finish in the new base — 0.1 in decimal is an endless binary pattern — and the honest converter says so and states the precision kept, rather than pretending the road ended.

1. Show it, don't just say it: Place-value expansion into decimal, and repeated division out of it. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
