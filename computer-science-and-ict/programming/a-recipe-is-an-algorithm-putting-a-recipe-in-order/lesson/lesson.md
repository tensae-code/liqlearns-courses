# A Recipe Is an Algorithm — Putting a Recipe in Order

## Do it first

A recipe card lies shuffled: “serve warm” sits above “crack the eggs”. Dinner is in danger. Before reading on, sort six recipe steps by feel — then ask what rule your hands just obeyed.

## Sub lesson 1 — From story sentences to instructions

A recipe as written is a **story** — “Grandmother mixes the flour while the pan warms…” An **algorithm** is the same content as **instructions in order**: one action per line, each line doable, in the only order that works. The activity's craft (Recette des crêpes; le gâteau de Mémé Denise) is the conversion: hunt the actions inside the sentences, number them, and test the order by cooking it mentally — a step out of place is a bug you can taste.

## Sub lesson 2 — The loop inside the recipe

Recipes hide **loops**: “stir until smooth”, “make twelve crêpes” — do it again, and again, until a condition ends it. The 3e–4e version introduces the loop (la boucle) right here, inside the cake: repeat the action, check the condition, stop. A recipe thus carries all three flow shapes on one card — sequence in its steps, choice in its “if it sticks…”, loop in its repetitions.
