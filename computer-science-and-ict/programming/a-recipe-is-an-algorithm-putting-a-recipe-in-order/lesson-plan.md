## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **From story sentences to instructions** — A recipe as written is a **story** — “Grandmother mixes the flour while the pan warms…” An **algorithm** is the same content as **instructions in order**: one action per line, each line doable, in the only order that works. The activity's craft (Recette des crêpes; le gâteau de Mémé Denise) is the conversion: hunt the actions inside the sentences, number them, and test the order by cooking it mentally — a step out of place is a bug you can taste.
2. **The loop inside the recipe** — Recipes hide **loops**: “stir until smooth”, “make twelve crêpes” — do it again, and again, until a condition ends it. The 3e–4e version introduces the loop (la boucle) right here, inside the cake: repeat the action, check the condition, stop. A recipe thus carries all three flow shapes on one card — sequence in its steps, choice in its “if it sticks…”, loop in its repetitions.
