# Lesson plan — Adding Sounds to a Scratch Project

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Programming · **Lesson:** Adding Sounds to a Scratch Project
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Palestine (2026-11-14) — in the title Programming on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Palestine's Grade 6, Grade 7 and Grade 8 Programming student books, because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Each sprite has its own sounds: play-sound starts a clip, play-sound-until-done waits for it to finish — and sounds come from the sound library (about 240 sounds in Grade 8’s count), from external recordings, or recorded in Scratch itself.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Play-sound and play-until-done — each sprite’s own sounds** — The **sound blocks** play a sprite’s clips: **play-sound** starts the clip and the script runs on, while **play-sound until done** holds the script until the clip finishes — the difference the Grade 6 book makes pupils compare directly. Sounds belong to sprites: **each sprite has its own sound**, and sounds can be **repeated with loops and waits** in Grade 7’s telling.
2. **Where sounds come from — library, recordings, your own voice** — Grade 8 names the **three sources**: the **sound library of about 240 sounds**, **external recordings** brought in from outside, and **recording your own voice**. Choosing the source is part of the making: a library clip for a quick effect, your own voice when only your voice says it right.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
