**Acceptance count: taught in 3 countries (Palestine, Bhutan, Kazakhstan).**

Placement choice: this lesson was built new for the ICT expansion (Palestine, 2026-11-14) because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree was checked for the slug and for near matches before building. The placements file named this slug directly (NEW LESSON NEEDED — programming/adding-sounds-to-a-scratch-project). It sits in the Programming title (programming/), following the book's own order.

Palestine’s own way (Grade 6, Programming): Grade 6 programming is a full Scratch course (MIT, named in the book) in four units — basics, movement and costumes, sprites communicating, projects. Lessons after Unit 1 are deliberately non-cumulative: pupils imagine, program, then share their product in exhibitions and competitions. A local touch is kept: the “say” lesson notes Arabic text appears reversed because Scratch writes left-to-right.

Palestine’s own way (Grade 7, Programming): the Scratch course deepens in three units — programming graphics, sprites and backgrounds, sprite stories and dialogues. Local grounding is explicit: the pen unit opens by asking whether Scratch can draw ornaments like the decorations of Al-Aqsa Mosque; the stage is explained as a theatre whose actors (sprites) follow the director exactly and in order.

Palestine’s own way (Grade 8, Programming): Grade 8 Scratch is organised around making things — Unit 1 programs drawings and ornaments (the unit opens with the ornaments of Jerusalem’s churches and the Dome of the Rock), Unit 2 weaves interactive stories with sound and dialogue (including a dialogue project on love of the homeland), Unit 3 designs real games with variables, operators and sensing.

Morocco's own way (Grade 3): a full primary year given to one tool — Scratch — sequenced interface → sprites → stage/backdrops → sounds, each strand ending in a familiar story retold as a Scratch project (the hare and the tortoise; Little Red Riding Hood). (teacher guide — evidence)

Morocco's own way (Grade 4): Grade 4 stays inside Scratch but moves from the interface to behaviour — motion, looks, sound control and event handling taught block-family by block-family, then combined in two animated scenes (a city tour; an aquarium). (teacher guide — evidence)

Bhutan's own way (Class IV, Literacy with ICT student book): Competency-based "Literacy with ICT" (not procedure drill): every chapter opens with learning objectives, mixes off-machine "Try This" with "Try This On Machine", and closes with Now You Know / Check Your Progress / Explore Further; content is localised (Bhutanese names, kuenselonline, education.gov.bt, Bhutan's 1999 Internet start) and one class per week across the year.

Bhutan's own way (Class V, Literacy with ICT student book): Word is taught together with typing in Dzongkha, the national language (Dzongkha keyboard software and fonts); communication is taught on the school LAN with Qualia LAN Messenger (chat, file sharing and broadcast with no Internet connection), and the end-of-year folder is sent to the teacher as a LAN-messenger attachment.


Kazakhstan's own way (Kopeeva G5 Informatika): Grade 5 Informatika (the older, pre-"Digital Literacy" subject name) is built around Scratch as a "game programming environment": a whole chapter runs from a first program through blocks, animation graphics, sound effects and dialogue to linear/branching/cyclic commands; earlier chapters pair Internet safety with data protection, and the year ends with print-ready project work — a Word newspaper page with WordArt and columns, presented as slides.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukhametzhanova G6 Informatika, 2018 edition): the 2018 Atamura Grade 6 course is built around making a real computer game across two whole parts — idea, scenario, stage and characters, implementation, sound accompaniment, a start page and improvements — with vector graphics in Inkscape before it and full Word documentation after it: footnotes, hyperlinks, an automatic contents and a formal game design document that teaches copyright and citing authors.

Kazakhstan's own way (Salgaraeva & Ilyasova G6 Informatika): the Arman-PV Grade 6 book runs the full 2018 programme in numbered paragraphs with a test after every part: systems and networks, presenting information (transmission, encryption, binary coding of text), vector graphics with a "Zhailau" drawing practicum, then the two-part game build in the Scratch environment, and a final Word part that adds references, copyright and plagiarism, a referat mini-project and the game document.

Kazakhstan's own way (Salgaraeva G6 «Информатика»): the Arman-PV Grade 6 book is a game-making year wrapped in document skills: ergonomics and wireless networks first, then binary coding and encryption, vector graphics (drawing a «Жайлау» summer pasture), a Scratch game designed, built and given a sound track and title screen — and the year ends with footnotes, hyperlinks, copyright and plagiarism, a table of contents and the written documentation for the game.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G3 «Ақпараттық-коммуникациялық технологиялар»): the older Almatykitap Grade 3 ICT book (2018, a different book from the 2021 Digital Literacy, Book 23) is organised by cross-curricular themes — Living Nature, What is Good and What is Bad, Time, Architecture, Art, Outstanding People, Water is the Source of Life, Culture of Rest — and teaches sound (playback, effects, recording, processing) and the graphics editor inside them, ending every theme with a defended mini-project.
