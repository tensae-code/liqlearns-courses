# Adding Sounds to a Scratch Project

## Do it first

A silent project is half a story. Palestine’s Scratch books give every sprite its own sounds — from a library of hundreds, from recordings, from your own voice — and this lesson is how they are added and played.

## Sub lesson 1 — Play-sound and play-until-done — each sprite’s own sounds

The **sound blocks** play a sprite’s clips: **play-sound** starts the clip and the script runs on, while **play-sound until done** holds the script until the clip finishes — the difference the Grade 6 book makes pupils compare directly. Sounds belong to sprites: **each sprite has its own sound**, and sounds can be **repeated with loops and waits** in Grade 7’s telling.

## Sub lesson 2 — Where sounds come from — library, recordings, your own voice

Grade 8 names the **three sources**: the **sound library of about 240 sounds**, **external recordings** brought in from outside, and **recording your own voice**. Choosing the source is part of the making: a library clip for a quick effect, your own voice when only your voice says it right.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Each sprite has its own sounds: play-sound starts a clip, play-sound-until-done waits for it to finish — and sounds come from the sound library (about 240 sounds in Grade 8’s count), from external recordings, or recorded in Scratch itself. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
