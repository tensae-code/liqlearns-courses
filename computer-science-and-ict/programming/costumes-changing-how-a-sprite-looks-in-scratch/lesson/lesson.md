# Costumes — Changing How a Sprite Looks in Scratch

## Do it first

A walking character whose legs never move looks like a sliding statue. Give the sprite a second look — a costume — and switch between them, and it walks. Palestine teaches costumes in Grades 6, 7 and 8.

## Sub lesson 1 — Costumes and next-costume — walking animation

A sprite holds **more than one costume** — the same character in different poses. The **next-costume** block steps to the following look, so a sprite that repeats its movement **and** changes its appearance appears to walk: Grade 7’s example is **Avery walking**, where next-costume is what makes the walk look real.

## Sub lesson 2 — Switching to a named costume — and timing the animation

Grade 8 adds the deliberate switch: **switch to a named costume** picks exactly the look the moment needs, and the animation’s life is its timing — a **repeat-forever** switching costumes with the right waits makes a character that walks, flaps or dances for as long as the program runs.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: A costume is one of a sprite’s looks; switching costumes — next costume, or switch to a named costume — animates the sprite, and a repeat-forever with the right timing makes walking look real. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
