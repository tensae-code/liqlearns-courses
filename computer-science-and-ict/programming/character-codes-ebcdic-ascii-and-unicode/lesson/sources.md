Placement choice: this lesson was built new for the ICT expansion (Bangladesh, 2026-11-14) because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree was checked for the slug and the live lesson content was checked for near matches before building. The placements file named this slug directly (NEW LESSON NEEDED — programming/character-codes-ebcdic-ascii-and-unicode). It sits in the Programming title (programming/), following the book's own order. Near matches checked by content: Codes — BCD and ASCII teaches ASCII deeply but not EBCDIC or Unicode's storage forms, and How Data Is Encoded — Text, Sound and Images mentions Unicode only in passing; the three-code comparison with the Bangla Unicode table is built new. Provenance scope note: every placement stocked here is from a Bangladesh student book (all five distinct books in this expansion are student books, National Curriculum and Textbook Board (NCTB), Bangladesh; no ISBN printed; each read in the English Version with the Bangla Version parallel spot-checked — the English edition is cited, both editions named in the book entries). Media slots in lesson/lesson.md are described slots only ([Picture — …]); no picture numbers are invented.

Bangladesh's own way (HSC ICT, Classes XI–XII and Alim): one compulsory ICT book for every HSC and Alim student in the country, saturated with Bangladesh itself: Bangabandhu Satellite-1 and its ground stations, bdjobs and chakri.com job portals, Daraz and Bikroy e-commerce, the Walton factory's automated production line, the National ID card used to teach primary keys and its database used to teach RDBMS at work, a full Bangla Unicode table and a worked example of putting Bangla text on a web page with meta charset, the national letter-grade scale (A+ from 80) as the else-if programme, "I Love Bangladesh" as the loop example text, an algorithm for reaching college by bicycle or rickshaw, and the Digital Bangladesh programme — National Data Centre, hi-tech parks, Startup Bangladesh and Bangladesh Bank's QR-based Cashless Bangladesh. C is the HSC programming language, taught with scanf/printf in Code::Blocks.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 7 G7 student): A new author team (Topchyan & Melkonyan, 2025) rebuilds the course around the "virtual world": data and encoding, networks, e-security and e-communication, then a full Python chapter — decisions, lists, loops, functions, objects and classes — taught as making the real world virtual.


Pakistan's own way (Grade 7 Computer Science 7): Grade 7 opens with emerging technologies and a deep ports-and-peripherals hardware treatment, then teaches email all the way to DKIM/SPF/DMARC authentication and POP3/IMAP/SMTP protocols; binary data representation (ASCII/Unicode, RGB colour, sound sampling) is taught inside the Programming unit, and entrepreneurship becomes business-plan craft — promotion, value proposition, quality assurance, SEO and platform business models, with Twitter and Snapchat origin stories.

Pakistan's own way (Grade 9 Computer Science 9): Grade 9 is a full problem-solving course before any coding — candid solutions, the 5 Ws of problem analysis, test data in five types, trace tables, verification vs validation — then number systems carried through to Boolean algebra with truth tables, networks taught through the postal-system analogy for TCP/IP layers, a data-and-privacy unit built on Code.org cipher widgets, NADRA and Pakistan's NR3C cybercrime centre, and a first website built page by page as "Pakistan.html".

Kazakhstan's own way (Zhunusov G10 Computer Science, English-medium): the English-medium Grade 10 book is the most technical of the wave: the CIA triad and three separate authentication lessons (passwords twice, biometrics), encoding standards, Python dictionaries, DFS/BFS graph traversal, and a full SQL chapter in MySQL (CREATE, INSERT, SELECT/WHERE, MIN/MAX, JOIN, GROUP BY, SELECT INTO) before a JavaScript chapter. (Its preface is the publisher's generic science template, left over from another book.)

Kazakhstan's own way (Ataeva & Vyshkova G8 Informatics, NIS): the NIS Grade 8 course is the most distinctive of the wave: vector graphics flows into geographic information systems (planning an excursion, making maps, visualising data in GIS), spreadsheets are used to solve logical problems, loops are taught through mathematics, physics, business and multimedia problems, and the year ends with a full project cycle — six stages, presentation, documentation, defence — plus a mini-project on artificial intelligence.

Kazakhstan's own way (Salgaraeva G6 «Информатика»): the Arman-PV Grade 6 book is a game-making year wrapped in document skills: ergonomics and wireless networks first, then binary coding and encryption, vector graphics (drawing a «Жайлау» summer pasture), a Scratch game designed, built and given a sound track and title screen — and the year ends with footnotes, hyperlinks, copyright and plagiarism, a table of contents and the written documentation for the game.

Kazakhstan's own way (Atayeva/Vyushkova/Zhurbenko G8 «Информатика» NIS): the NIS Grade 8 book is the widest of the set: a whole section on vector graphics and geographic information systems (planning an excursion, making maps, visualising data in GIS), octal and hexadecimal coding, loops applied separately to mathematics, physics, business and animation, strings and subroutines, and a nine-step project method ending in a mini-project on artificial intelligence.
