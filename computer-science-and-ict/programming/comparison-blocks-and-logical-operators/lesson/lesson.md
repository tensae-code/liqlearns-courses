# Comparison Blocks and Logical Operators

## Do it first

Hold two numbers in your head: 7 and 5. Is 7 bigger? Yes. Is 7 bigger AND smaller than 10? Also yes — two tests, joined. Programs ask exactly these joined questions before they branch.

## Comparison Blocks and Logical Operators

Branching questions grow up in this lesson. **Comparison blocks** compare two values — **greater than, less than, equal to** — and answer true or false, which is exactly the shape a branching block needs for its question. **Logical operators** join those answers: **and** (both tests true), **or** (at least one true), **not** (flips true to false and false to true). With them a program can ask real questions: is the score greater than 10 **and** the time not over? The lesson teaches the reading order carefully — work out each small test first, then join the answers — because a compound question is only as trustworthy as its parts.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Comparison blocks ask how two numbers stand — greater, less, equal — and logical operators join tests with and, or and not, so a branch can ask a compound question. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: build a comparison block and say whether it answers true or false. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: join two tests with and, or and not, and say the joined answer. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: work a compound question out part by part, in order. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
