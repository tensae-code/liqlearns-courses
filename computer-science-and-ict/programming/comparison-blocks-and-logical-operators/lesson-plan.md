# Lesson plan — Comparison Blocks and Logical Operators

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Programming · **Lesson:** Comparison Blocks and Logical Operators
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Bulgaria (2026-11-14) — in the title Programming on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Bulgaria's *Компютърно моделиране* (Computer Modelling), Grade 4, because no existing lesson teaches this thing as its own lesson at this level.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Comparison blocks ask how two numbers stand — greater, less, equal — and logical operators join tests with and, or and not, so a branch can ask a compound question.
**This lesson's own level:** Grade 4 simple — it stands on the child's own everyday world and on the lessons named below; every new word is built inside the lesson from things the child can do at once.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons in Programming on the ladder. Recommended, never forced — a learner may land on this lesson directly, and it stands alone.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Build a comparison block and say whether it answers true or false
2. Join two tests with and, or and not, and say the joined answer
3. Work a compound question out part by part, in order

## What you need
A computer with Scratch.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Hold two numbers in your head: 7 and 5. Is 7 bigger? Yes. Is 7 bigger AND smaller than 10? Also yes — two tests, joined. Programs ask exactly these joined questions before they branch. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of a picture of green comparison blocks — greater, less, equal — feeding an and block and an or block, with a small true/false table beside them showing each joined answer — the student names what the picture shows and ties each part of it to the lesson's one thing.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the lesson's key names and definitions aloud, in the order the lesson gives them — the student listens once through, then reads the same lines aloud along with the voice. (Audio is a described slot in this build; no audio file is made.)
3. **Say it to your companion** — the companion block in lesson/lesson.md.
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the lesson in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-or-false, fill-in-the-blank, short-answer — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the doing block at the end of lesson/lesson.md.

## If the learner is stuck
Compound answer surprises you → split it: write each small test's answer (true/false) on paper first, then join them; never guess the whole question at once.
