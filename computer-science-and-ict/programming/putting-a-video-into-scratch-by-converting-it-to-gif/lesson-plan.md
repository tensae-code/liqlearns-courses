# Lesson plan — Putting a Video into Scratch by Converting It to GIF

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Programming · **Lesson:** Putting a Video into Scratch by Converting It to GIF
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Palestine (2026-11-14) — in the title Programming on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Palestine's Grade 9 Programming student book, because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A short clip (20–30 seconds, downloaded respecting the owner’s rights) is converted to GIF with an online converter; the GIF has no sound, so the sound is recorded separately and added; the GIF’s costumes stepped through in a loop make the motion.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Download short, convert to GIF** — The route starts with respect and brevity: a **short clip of 20–30 seconds, downloaded respecting the owner’s rights**, then **converted to GIF with an online converter**. The GIF is a strip of still pictures — which is exactly what Scratch costumes are.
2. **No sound in a GIF — record it separately** — **GIF has no sound**, so the clip’s **sound is recorded separately and added** as the project’s own sound. Inside Scratch, the **GIF’s costumes stepped through in a loop make the motion** — the video plays because the costumes change fast enough to fool the eye, with the added sound beside them.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Scratch has no video block for your own clip. But it has costumes — hundreds of them, stepped through in a loop, are a film. Grade 9 converts a short downloaded clip to GIF, records its sound separately, and plays a video inside Scratch. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of download short, convert to GIF as the lesson describes it — the student studies the picture and names what each part shows.
   - ADD GIF: a GIF of no sound in a GIF — record it separately in action — the student watches once, then says what changed and why.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the lesson's key names and definitions aloud, in the order the lesson gives them — the student listens once through, then reads the same lines aloud along with the voice. (Audio is a described slot in this build; no audio file is made.)
3. **Say it to your companion** — the companion block in lesson/lesson.md.
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the lesson in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-false, matching, short-answer — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
