# Search and Sort Algorithms — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What two jobs does this chapter study?**
**Answer: Searching — finding where a value is; sorting — putting values in order**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What does a search take, and what does it answer?**
**Answer: A list and a target value; it answers the target's position, or that it is not there**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) Is 'not found' a complete answer?**
**Answer: Yes — a good search can always give it and never hunts forever**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) By what is a search characterised?**
**Answer: What it may assume (such as an ordered list) and how it moves**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) Name two ways a search can move.**
**Answer: One by one from the start; or by halving the field**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What does a sort do?**
**Answer: Rearranges a list into order — ascending or descending**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What must a sorted list still hold?**
**Answer: Exactly the values it started with — nothing lost, nothing added**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) By what is a sort characterised?**
**Answer: Its movement pattern — comparisons, swaps, what is set aside and reinserted**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) Why are search and sort taught together?**
**Answer: They are partners — the fastest searches require an ordered list**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) What can pay for many searches?**
**Answer: Sorting once**
