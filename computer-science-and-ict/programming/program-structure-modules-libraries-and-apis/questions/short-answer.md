# Program Structure — Modules, Libraries and APIs — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What is a module?**
**Answer: A named part of a program with one job, hiding its insides and showing a small surface**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What is a library?**
**Answer: A collection of ready modules written by others, imported instead of invented**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) Why divide a program into modules?**
**Answer: Each part can be built, tested and fixed on its own — and reused**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What does a module hide, and what does it show?**
**Answer: It hides its insides; it shows a small surface the rest may use**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) Where is this structure named in Armenia's course?**
**Answer: The Grade 11 programme outcomes in the teacher guide (p. 44)**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What is an API?**
**Answer: The written agreement at a part's surface — the operations offered, what each needs, and what each returns**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What do callers promise, and what do makers promise?**
**Answer: Callers ask only through the API; makers may change the insides while the API holds**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) Why does the API promise matter?**
**Answer: It lets parts be improved or rebuilt without breaking everyone who uses them**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) Give an everyday analogy for an API.**
**Answer: A counter in a shop: you ask at the counter by the menu; the kitchen behind it may reorganise freely**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) Name one API you have used without coding.**
**Answer: Any service's public interface — for example a weather service other programs query**
