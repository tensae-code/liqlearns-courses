## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Letters, digits and symbols** — Pascal's **alphabet** is the Latin letters A–Z and a–z, the digits 0–9, and the **special symbols**: `+ - * / = < > ( ) [ ] . , ; : ' ` and the assignment sign **:=**. A program may contain nothing outside the alphabet — a letter from another script or a sign like ≠ stops the compiler at once. Words of the language — program, begin, end — are built from these letters and kept whole.
2. **Identifiers — names built by rule** — Names the programmer invents — **identifiers** — are built from letters and digits, must **begin with a letter**, and may not be a service word: `sum1` is allowed, `1sum` and `begin` are not. Good names say what the thing is. The alphabet lesson stands first in the course because every later rule — variables, operators, procedures — is written in these letters and names.
