Saudi Arabia's own way (Grade 4, Digital Skills): the primary Digital Skills course (Binary Logic) is built as three units per term with the same guide pattern as the intermediate books (outcomes, important points, steps, answers, unit project, "Test Yourself"), files on the Ain platform, and a strong Arabic/English bilingual-typing strand — pupils switch the keyboard between Arabic and English with Alt+Shift and learn text direction, hamza and Arabic punctuation on the keyboard alongside Word skills; Scratch is introduced in Term 1 through algorithms from daily life. This citation is PARTIAL — Term 1 only. (teacher guide — evidence)

Palestine’s own way (Grade 6, Programming): Grade 6 programming is a full Scratch course (MIT, named in the book) in four units — basics, movement and costumes, sprites communicating, projects. Lessons after Unit 1 are deliberately non-cumulative: pupils imagine, program, then share their product in exhibitions and competitions. A local touch is kept: the “say” lesson notes Arabic text appears reversed because Scratch writes left-to-right.

Palestine’s own way (Grade 7, Programming): the Scratch course deepens in three units — programming graphics, sprites and backgrounds, sprite stories and dialogues. Local grounding is explicit: the pen unit opens by asking whether Scratch can draw ornaments like the decorations of Al-Aqsa Mosque; the stage is explained as a theatre whose actors (sprites) follow the director exactly and in order.

Palestine’s own way (Grade 8, Programming): Grade 8 Scratch is organised around making things — Unit 1 programs drawings and ornaments (the unit opens with the ornaments of Jerusalem’s churches and the Dome of the Rock), Unit 2 weaves interactive stories with sound and dialogue (including a dialogue project on love of the homeland), Unit 3 designs real games with variables, operators and sensing.

Palestine’s own way (Grade 9, Programming): Grade 9 is a full applications course in Scratch — after a basics unit it builds real things: a photo editor, a video player, a phone PIN lock, a bank account, a library sign-up/login, an alarm clock, an elevator, a task list, a wall clock that can ring at prayer times. Its own workarounds are content too: Scratch 2 cannot type Arabic in its paint editor, so Arabic text is written in MS Paint, saved as a picture and uploaded; the news-ticker lesson’s breaking-news example is the Ministry’s own decision to teach programming in Grades 5–9; the letter-counting lesson’s example sentence says the Dome of the Rock stands in Jerusalem.

Iraq's own way (Grade 7): a four-unit spiral repeated at every grade — computer fundamentals, computer software (here: the operating system plus an unusually deep treatment of Windows right-click shortcut menus), algorithms and programming principles (ending in Scratch), and information technology and the internet — every lesson built on the Iraqi five-step learning cycle with opening activity, main idea, learning outcomes, vocabulary and lesson review.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Grade 10): Grade 10 jumps from using computers to building with them — simple electronic circuits, programming that controls an external device, robotics and artificial intelligence sit inside "Computer Architecture", while the software unit pairs operating-system installation with machine learning; typing rises to 30 wpm. (teacher guide — evidence, PARTIAL)

Morocco's own way (Grade 1): ICT from age 6 built entirely on handling real hardware first — plug in, switch on, draw with the mouse, type in Word — with every unit taught in the same five-step rhythm (mobilisation, découverte, application, évaluation, réemploi) in pairs/groups, and files taught through paper folders and plastic sleeves before the screen. (teacher guide — evidence)

Morocco's own way (Grade 3): a full primary year given to one tool — Scratch — sequenced interface → sprites → stage/backdrops → sounds, each strand ending in a familiar story retold as a Scratch project (the hare and the tortoise; Little Red Riding Hood). (teacher guide — evidence)

Sudan's own way (Grade 9): the whole Grade 9 book is programming — concepts and Pascal-style examples first, then algorithms and flowcharts, then a full Scratch course ending in a designed project. The Bakht Al-Ruda ICT series runs in the same pattern from Grade 4 to Grade 9.

Cuba's own way (Grade 5): The Grade 5 programme is built around extracting information from Cuban educational software, encyclopedias and EcuRed into word-processor documents, then Scratch — drawing/animation first (Unit 3), control/movement/appearance blocks in Unit 4. (teacher programme — evidence)

Cuba's own way (Grade 5): Student text mirrors the programme in four chapters; word-processing is taught as a research workflow (synonyms, symbols, searching, copying texts and images from sources), and Scratch is introduced through its paint editor before movement/appearance/control blocks.

Cuba's own way (Grade 7): Secondary Informática opens by contrasting proprietary Windows with Cuba's free operating system Nova; programming continues the primary Scratch line, here centred on the Scratch pen tool for drawing geometric figures and the linear function; word-processor work ends by exporting documents to HTML for the web.

Egypt's own way (Grade 7, Term 2 student book): Term 2 pivots from applications to computing itself — AI, sensors and robots, then a Scratch-to-Python bridge in the same term.

Bhutan's own way (Class IV, Literacy with ICT student book): Competency-based "Literacy with ICT" (not procedure drill): every chapter opens with learning objectives, mixes off-machine "Try This" with "Try This On Machine", and closes with Now You Know / Check Your Progress / Explore Further; content is localised (Bhutanese names, kuenselonline, education.gov.bt, Bhutan's 1999 Internet start) and one class per week across the year.

Rwanda's own way (S1 ICT Student Book, KLB): competency-based units each opening with a Key Unit Competency and keywords; a full GIS/ArcGIS unit and a Scratch music-making unit sit inside the first secondary year, and ICT use is grounded in Rwandan daily life.

Rwanda's own way (S1 ICT Teacher Guide, KLB): the guide mirrors the student units with content maps, additional teacher content per unit, SEN/multi-ability support, sample lesson plans (Appendix 1) and a glossary (Appendix 2); assessment is framed by Rwanda's competence-based curriculum. (teacher guide — evidence)

Kuwait's own way (Grade 5 Digital Explorer Term 1): Grade 5 leaves ScratchJr for full Scratch (installed and online), but first comes a values-and-AI unit — citizenship, smart vs non-smart devices, what AI is, robots and their parts — and programming itself is taught as a four-stage craft (set the goal, the smart plan, the flowchart, then the code) before a single block is dragged.
