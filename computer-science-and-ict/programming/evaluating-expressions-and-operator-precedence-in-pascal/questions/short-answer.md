# Evaluating Expressions — and Operator Precedence in Pascal — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What is an expression built from?**
**Answer: Operands (values) joined by operators (operations)**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What does evaluating an expression mean?**
**Answer: Working it out to one value**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) Why must a fraction's parts be bracketed?**
**Answer: Pascal expressions are written flat in one line — the line has no floors**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What is a standard function call inside an expression?**
**Answer: An operand — evaluated to its value where it stands**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) Name four operators from the lesson.**
**Answer: +, −, *, / (also: div, mod)**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) State the precedence order.**
**Answer: Brackets first, then the multiplication family (*, /, div, mod), then the addition family (+, −), left to right within a level**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What is 2 + 3 * 4, and why?**
**Answer: 14 — multiplication binds before addition**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) How do you make it 20?**
**Answer: Bracket it: (2 + 3) * 4 — brackets outrank all**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) Where do relations like < bind?**
**Answer: Weakest of all — they compare finished values**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) What is the professional's rule?**
**Answer: When the reader could hesitate, bracket — clarity is for people**
