# The Rectangle Method for the Approximate Definite Integral — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What is a definite integral, geometrically?**
**Answer: The area under the curve over [a, b]**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What are the method's cuts?**
**Answer: n equal subintervals of width h = (b − a) / n**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What is the approximation?**
**Answer: The sum of the rectangles' areas — Σ f(xᵢ)·h**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What happens as n grows?**
**Answer: h shrinks, the staircase hugs the curve, the sum approaches the true area**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) Which error source is at work?**
**Answer: Method error — shrinking under refinement**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) Name the three forms.**
**Answer: Left, right and midpoint rectangles — by where the height is taken**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What do left rectangles do to a rising function?**
**Answer: Under-shoot it (right rectangles overshoot it)**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) Why is the midpoint form usually most accurate?**
**Answer: Its errors from the two sides of the curve partly cancel**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) For a monotone function, what do left and right sums give?**
**Answer: A bracket around the truth — and their difference bounds the error**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) Write the sum's shape for left rectangles.**
**Answer: h · (f(x₀) + f(x₁) + … + f(xₙ₋₁))**
