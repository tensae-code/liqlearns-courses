# The Rectangle Method for the Approximate Definite Integral — matching

Match each item on the left with the one it belongs to on the right.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What is a definite integral, geometrically?** → **The area under the curve over [a, b]**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What are the method's cuts?** → **n equal subintervals of width h = (b − a) / n**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What is the approximation?** → **The sum of the rectangles' areas — Σ f(xᵢ)·h**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What happens as n grows?** → **h shrinks, the staircase hugs the curve, the sum approaches the true area**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) Which error source is at work?** → **Method error — shrinking under refinement**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) Name the three forms.** → **Left, right and midpoint rectangles — by where the height is taken**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What do left rectangles do to a rising function?** → **Under-shoot it (right rectangles overshoot it)**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) Why is the midpoint form usually most accurate?** → **Its errors from the two sides of the curve partly cancel**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) For a monotone function, what do left and right sums give?** → **A bracket around the truth — and their difference bounds the error**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) Write the sum's shape for left rectangles.** → **h · (f(x₀) + f(x₁) + … + f(xₙ₋₁))**
