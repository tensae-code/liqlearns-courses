# Subprograms — Characteristics, Advantages and Parameters

## Do it first

A hundred-line program is a wall of text. Ch.10 cuts doors in the wall: subprograms — named pieces with jobs — and this lesson is their charter.

## Sub lesson 1 — Characteristics of subprograms

A **subprogram (υποπρόγραμμα)** is a **named, self-standing piece of a program** that does **one job** and is **called** from wherever that job is needed. Its characteristics, as the chapter lists them: it has its **own name** and its **own local names**; it is written **once** and called **many times**; control **goes to it** at the call and **returns** to the place after the call; and it meets the rest of the program only through its **parameters** and, for a function, its returned value — a sealed room with a serving hatch.

## Sub lesson 2 — Advantages — and parameters, the connection

The division pays, and the chapter counts the payment: **shorter reading** (the main program becomes a plan of named jobs), **separate testing** (each piece can be proved alone), **reuse** (a proved piece serves many programs), and **team work** (different hands, different pieces, one agreement — the parameters). **Parameters** are that agreement written down: the list of values the subprogram needs from its caller, declared in its heading, so caller and piece can be written — and changed — apart, meeting only at the call.
