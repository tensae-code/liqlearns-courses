# Turning and Drawing with the Pen

## Do it first

Walk a square in the room: four steps, turn a quarter-turn, four steps, turn — four times. Your feet drew the square. A Scratch sprite with its pen down draws the same way, on the stage.

## Turning and Drawing with the Pen

Scratch begins, and its first drawing skill joins two ideas. **Turning**: a sprite does not only move forward — it turns, by a number of degrees, and a quarter-turn (90 degrees) is the turn that makes corners of squares and rectangles. **Drawing with the pen**: Scratch's pen blocks let a moving sprite leave a line behind it — pen down starts the line, pen up lifts it. Put turning and the pen together and shapes appear: lines at angles, corners, squares, and patterns no hand could rule so evenly. This is Bulgaria's first Scratch drawing lesson, and it stands on the robot artist's strokes: the same move-and-turn thinking, now with a pen that remembers the path.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: In Scratch, a sprite turns by degrees and draws with the pen: put the pen down, move and turn, and the sprite's path becomes a drawing. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: turn a sprite by a chosen number of degrees. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: put the pen down and draw a shape by moving and turning. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: say why 90 degrees is the corner turn of a square. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
