## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The proposition — a statement with a truth value** — A **proposition** (proposisi) is a statement that is **true or false** — not a question, not a command, not a wish: 'the lab has twenty computers' can be judged; 'open the lab' cannot. Every proposition carries a **truth value**, and logic's whole building stands on that one demand. Indonesia's SMK course opens its thinking chapter here because computing is built of propositions: every condition in every program is one, and the machine, like the logician, must always be able to say which of the two values it holds.
2. **The connectives — NOT, AND, OR, IF–THEN** — Propositions are joined by **connectives** into bigger statements whose truth is computed from their parts. **Negation** (negasi/ingkaran) flips the value: NOT p. **Conjunction** (konjungsi) joins with AND — true only when both parts are true. **Disjunction** (disjungsi) joins with OR — true when at least one part is true. **Implication** (implikasi) joins with IF–THEN — false only when the first is true and the second false — and its double, **biimplication**, says the two stand or fall together. These five are the complete grammar by which programs, circuits and arguments combine their truths.
