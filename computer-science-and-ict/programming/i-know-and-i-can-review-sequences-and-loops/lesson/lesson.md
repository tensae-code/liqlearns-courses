# I Know and I Can — Review: Sequences and Loops

## Do it first

Take one old puzzle from each kind — a sequence, a path, a loop drawing. Solve each without help. The one that stops you is the one this review sends you back to practise.

## I Know and I Can — Review: Sequences and Loops

Bulgaria's book stops every few lessons for **"Знам и мога" — I know and I can**. It is not a new topic; it is the mastery check the book builds in, where the learner proves the things the last lessons taught: building a **sequence** in order, moving the hero with **motion blocks**, **finding a path**, spotting **repeating actions**, and wrapping them in a **loop** for the robot artist. The review is honest work: each task is done alone, the answer is checked against what the blocks actually do, and anything not yet proved is named plainly and practised again. Passing this review means the first half of Grade 3 programming — everything before Scratch — is truly yours.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: This "Знам и мога" (I know and I can) lesson is the book's own mastery check: prove the sequences, motion blocks, paths, repeats and loops from the lessons before it — and find any one that is not proved yet. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: build a sequence and a path without help. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: spot a repeating part and wrap it in a loop with the right count. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: name honestly which earlier skill is not proved yet, and practise that one. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
