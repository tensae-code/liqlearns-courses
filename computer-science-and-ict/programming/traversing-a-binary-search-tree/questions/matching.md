# Traversing a Binary Search Tree — matching

Match each item on the left with the one it belongs to on the right.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What is a traversal?** → **Visiting every node of a tree in a fixed, recursive order**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What is the inorder order?** → **Left subtree, root, right subtree**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What is the preorder order?** → **Root first, then left subtree, then right subtree**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What is the postorder order?** → **Left subtree, right subtree, root last**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) What single thing differs between the three walks?** → **Where the visit sits — before, between, or after the subtree walks**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What is special about inorder on a BST?** → **It visits the values in sorted order**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) Why does inorder sort?** → **Left subtrees hold smaller values, right subtrees larger, and inorder reads left–root–right**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What is preorder good for?** → **Copying the tree — the root arrives before its subtrees**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) What is postorder good for?** → **Evaluating expressions, and deleting safely — children before their parent**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) What two jobs does the book say the BST joins?** → **Sorting and searching**
