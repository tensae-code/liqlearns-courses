# Lesson plan — LOGO procedures — giving a name to a set of commands

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Programming · **Lesson:** LOGO procedures — giving a name to a set of commands
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 12 — Programming in the Computer Science and ICT ladder (TITLES.md); stocked from Ethiopia's Grade 7 book, Unit 6 — Logic Oriented Graphics Oriented (LOGO) Programming. The ladder is the recommended order, never a forced one.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a procedure is a set of instructions given a descriptive one-word name, so one word does the work of a whole sequence — and it must be defined before use, or LOGO answers that the word is not in its vocabulary.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the drawing-commands lesson — single commands must be known before sets of them can be named.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or in steps — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: LOGO drawing commands — fd, bk, rt, lt, cs.  Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define a procedure.
2. Say what a primitive procedure is.
3. Give the book's SQUARE example both ways.
4. State the define-before-use rule and LOGO's answer when it is broken.

## What you need
A computer with MSW Logo open; paper and a pencil.
