# Sorting an Array

## Do it first

An array found is useful; an array in order is a tool. Mongolia's Grade 12 array strand teaches sorting by its most visible method — the bubble — slowly, pass by pass, until the pupil can write the whole program unaided, then sets the second method as their own work.

## Sub lesson 1 — Bubble sort — pass by pass

**Bubble sort** orders an array by **passes**: each pass walks along the array comparing **neighbouring pairs** and **swapping** any pair standing out of order, so the largest unplaced value **bubbles** to its place at the end. Pass follows pass — each shorter than the last, since the tail already stands sorted — until a pass makes **no swaps**, the array's own signal that order is complete. The method is taught by watching it think: trace a five-value array on paper and the sort's patience becomes visible — many small honest steps, none of them clever, arriving together at order.

In your mind's eye: bubble sort — pass by pass.

## Sub lesson 2 — The full program — and selection sort as your task

The book writes bubble sort as a **full program**: fill the array, sort it by the pass method, and **print it by a print subprogram** — the subprogram habit of the earlier chapter carrying the sorted result out. Then the book changes its voice: **selection sort** — repeatedly select the smallest remaining value and place it next — is **set as a task**, described by its idea and left to the pupil to build. Mongolia's two-step is the strand's teaching method in miniature: the first algorithm is given whole, traced and understood; the second is earned — the pupil who sorts by selection has stopped copying sorting and started knowing it.

In your mind's eye: the full program — and selection sort as your task.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: Walks the array swapping neighbouring pairs that stand out of order. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: The largest unplaced value — it bubbles to its place. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: The tail already stands sorted. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: A pass with no swaps. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
