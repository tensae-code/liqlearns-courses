# I Know and I Can — Final Review

## Do it first

Lay out the year: sequences, Scratch, loops, dialogue, projects, branching, variables, robots. Pick the task you would least like to be given. That pick tells you exactly what this review is for.

## I Know and I Can — Final Review

Both Bulgaria books end their programming with a closing **"Знам и мога. Преговор и обобщение" — I know and I can: review and summary**. In Grade 3 (Урок 32) it proves the whole year: sequences and loops from code.org, the Scratch environment, movement, pen drawing, positioning, stage and backdrops, sprites and costumes, sound, speech and dialogue, animation and project work. In Grade 4 (Урок 24) the same-named closing review proves that book's Scratch work — branching, operators, variables and synchronised sprites — before its game and journey projects. One lesson stocks both placements because the thing is the same: a final, honest proving of the course so far, where anything not yet proved is named and practised, and the summary gathers the year into a single "I know and I can" that the learner has earned.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: The final "Знам и мога" is the whole-course mastery review and summary: prove, in one sitting, the programming the year taught — and carry the same honest check into Grade 4's closing review. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: prove tasks from across the whole course, alone and checked. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: summarise what the year taught, in your own words. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: name anything not yet proved and practise it before moving on. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
