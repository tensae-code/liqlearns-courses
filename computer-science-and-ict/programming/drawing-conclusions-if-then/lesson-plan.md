# Lesson plan — Drawing Conclusions — If… Then…

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Programming · **Lesson:** Drawing Conclusions — If… Then…
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Turkmenistan (2026-11-14) — in the title Programming on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Turkmenistan's *Basics of Informatics, Grade 1*, because no existing lesson teaches this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** From a judgement you can draw a conclusion with if… then…: if the first part is so, then the second part follows — and a picture can be read the same way, composing a judgement from what the picture shows and concluding what comes next.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on True and False Judgements and on AND, OR, NOT — small statements a child can check — and builds the one new step: from what is so, to what follows.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. ## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **If… then… — a conclusion that follows** — A conclusion joins two judgements with if and then: if the first judgement is so, then the second follows — 'if it rains, then the ground gets wet' — and the conclusion is only as good as its first part.
2. **Composing judgements from pictures** — A picture shows judgements waiting to be said: look at what is in the picture, say the judgement it shows, then draw the conclusion — from a child holding an umbrella under dark clouds: if the clouds bring rain, then the umbrella keeps the child dry.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say a conclusion in the if… then… shape, with the if part first and the then part following it.
2. Say why the order matters: the then part must truly follow from the if part.
3. Look at a picture, compose a judgement from what it shows, and draw an if… then… conclusion from it.

## What you need
Pictures to read — the lesson's pictures, a picture book, or drawings the class makes — plus paper and a pencil.

## If the learner is stuck
Swaps the parts (wet ground, therefore rain) → return to the arrow of the lesson: ask 'which came first, and which followed?' — the first is the if, the follower is the then; a conclusion poured backwards is not trusted. Cannot start with a picture → name first, judge second, conclude third: say three things you see, turn one into a judgement, and only then reach for if… then….
