# Declaring Variables in Pascal and C++ — matching

Match each item on the left with the one it belongs to on the right.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) Where does Pascal declare variables?** → **In the var section, between the heading and begin**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) Can several names share a type in one line?** → **Yes — var a, b: integer;**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What three things does a declaration do?** → **Reserves memory, fixes the type, introduces the name**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What is a fresh variable's value?** → **Undefined — until the program assigns one**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) What error greets an undeclared variable?** → **The compiler refuses it — the beginner's first error message**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) Where may C++ declare a variable?** → **Wherever an instruction may stand — including in a for heading**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What can a C++ declaration carry?** → **An initial value — int a = 5; declares and assigns at once**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) How long does a C++ name exist?** → **From its declaration to the end of its block — its scope**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) What does Pascal's gathered section give instead?** → **A single census of a block's data, read at a glance**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) What will Grade 12 formalise from this?** → **Scope — the visibility domains of names**
