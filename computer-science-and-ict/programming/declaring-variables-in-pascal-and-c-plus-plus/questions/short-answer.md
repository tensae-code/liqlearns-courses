# Declaring Variables in Pascal and C++ — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) Where does Pascal declare variables?**
**Answer: In the var section, between the heading and begin**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) Can several names share a type in one line?**
**Answer: Yes — var a, b: integer;**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What three things does a declaration do?**
**Answer: Reserves memory, fixes the type, introduces the name**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What is a fresh variable's value?**
**Answer: Undefined — until the program assigns one**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) What error greets an undeclared variable?**
**Answer: The compiler refuses it — the beginner's first error message**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) Where may C++ declare a variable?**
**Answer: Wherever an instruction may stand — including in a for heading**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What can a C++ declaration carry?**
**Answer: An initial value — int a = 5; declares and assigns at once**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) How long does a C++ name exist?**
**Answer: From its declaration to the end of its block — its scope**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) What does Pascal's gathered section give instead?**
**Answer: A single census of a block's data, read at a glance**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) What will Grade 12 formalise from this?**
**Answer: Scope — the visibility domains of names**
