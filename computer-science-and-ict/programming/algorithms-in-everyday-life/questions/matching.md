**2. (Sub lesson 1) Why is a recipe an algorithm?** → **It gives ordered steps that finish a task — making the dish**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) Give an everyday order that must not be swapped.** → **Socks before shoes — some steps only work in their order**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) Does an algorithm need a computer?** → **No — people follow and invent algorithms every day**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What makes an everyday algorithm followable?** → **Each step is one clear action, in working order, that another person can follow without asking questions**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What must every algorithm do at the end?** → **End — it must not loop forever**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) How do you test an everyday algorithm?** → **Follow it literally, exactly as written, and find the step where the follower gets stuck**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) What is a stopping rule?** → **The condition that ends a repeated step — for example 'stir until the sugar has dissolved'**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) What do you fix when a follower gets stuck?** → **The unclear or missing step — not the follower**
