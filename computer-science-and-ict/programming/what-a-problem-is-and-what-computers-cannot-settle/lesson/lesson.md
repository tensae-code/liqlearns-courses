# What a problem is — and what computers cannot settle

## Do it first

Three problems from home: the effects of climate change on your town, road-traffic accidents and crossing the street safely, and what social media does to students. Could a computer, alone, solve any of them? Argue it with a partner before the book's verdict — then read the teacher's full keyed discussion of all three.

## What a problem is

In non-technical terms, a problem is a difficulty in understanding something, a matter difficult to solve or settle, a doubtful case, a complex task with doubt and uncertainty. The group work tests three big Ethiopian problems against what a computer can do — and concludes that such problems cannot be easily or directly resolved by a computer: they need individuals, local government, religious and community leaders. People first; computers are tools in their hands, not replacements.

## The three problems in full — teacher evidence

Climate change: extended dry seasons and heavy rainfall bring droughts and floods hitting rural and urban dwellers alike — floods destroy infrastructure and transport, drought brings hunger and famine, dries lakes and rivers, feeds wildfires, and rising temperature harms health directly and spreads disease vectors into the highlands; mitigations include cutting industrial gas emissions, planting trees and using renewable energy. Road traffic: Ethiopia is among the countries highly affected, and road accidents are globally the main killer of 15–29-year-olds; causes named are the poor road network, lack of road-safety knowledge, roads shared with cattle and donkey carts, and the age and condition of vehicles — and the guide turns it into walking-safety teaching: follow the rules and obey signs; walk on sidewalks where they exist, otherwise face traffic and keep as far from it as possible; cross at crosswalks or intersections looking in all directions, including for turning cars; if neither exists, find a well-lit place with the best view and wait for a safe gap; and watch for cars entering or leaving driveways and backing up in parking lots. Intercommunity conflict: causes are grouped as economic and political (decline, poverty, land fragmentation, unemployment), ethnic (contending interests over land and resources, cultural difference, administrative issues) and religious (lack of appreciation of pluralism) — with the verdict that such conflicts usually root in resources and intolerance, and the remedy of tolerance, trust, dialogue, mediation and community peace-building.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: a problem is a difficulty or complex task with doubt and uncertainty — and the big human problems cannot be easily or directly resolved by a computer. Then name the key words of this lesson — a problem, climate change effects, road accidents, who settles big problems — and say what each one means.
