# Hands-on - Machine language and assembly language

The lesson with your hands, not only your eyes.

## Two ways to say it

On paper, invent a tiny binary code: let 1 mean "stand" and 0 mean "sit". Write a short instruction string for a partner - three stands, two sits. Your partner reads it back as actions. Then write the same actions in word-symbols (STAND, SIT). Which version was quicker to write, and which quicker to read? That difference is the whole lesson.

## The translation chain

Three people play the chain: the first writes an instruction in mnemonics on a card; the second - the assembler - translates it into your class binary code; the third - the computer - obeys only the binary card and refuses the mnemonic card. Swap roles and play again.
