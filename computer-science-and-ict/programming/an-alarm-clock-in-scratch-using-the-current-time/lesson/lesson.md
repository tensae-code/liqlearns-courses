# An Alarm Clock in Scratch — Using the Current Time

## Do it first

Scratch can read the computer’s clock — the real year, hour and second, right now. Grade 9 builds two time machines on it: an alarm clock with snooze, and an analogue wall clock whose hands turn by formula, ringing at prayer times.

## Sub lesson 1 — The alarm — set, stop, snooze

The alarm clock reads the **current-time sensing blocks — year, month, date, day of week, hour, minute, second**. Its controls are **Set alarm / Stop / Snooze button sprites**; a **yes/no variable switches the ringing off**; a **custom block turns the weekday number into its name**; and **snooze silences the alarm for ten minutes**.

## Sub lesson 2 — The wall clock — hands pointed by formula

The wall clock keeps the computer’s time in analogue: **three hand sprites drawn as lines with their centre at one end**, each **pointed by a formula — seconds × 6 degrees, minutes × 6, hours × 30 + minutes × 0.5**. The project adds an **alarm at set times such as prayer times, and a date display**.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: The current-time sensing blocks give year, month, date, day of week, hour, minute and second: the alarm clock sets, stops and snoozes (ten minutes) with button sprites and a yes/no ringing variable, and the wall clock points its hands by formula — seconds × 6°, minutes × 6°, hours × 30° + minutes × 0.5°. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
