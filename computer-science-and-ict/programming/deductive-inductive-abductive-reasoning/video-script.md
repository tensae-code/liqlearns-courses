# Video script — Deductive, Inductive and Abductive Reasoning

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Reasoning (penalaran) moves between rules and cases, and its direction decides its strength. **Deductive** reasoning (deduktif) goes **from a rule to a case**: if the rule is true and the case falls under it, the conclusion is **certain** — all pupils in this class passed the test; Sari is in this class; Sari passed. **Inductive** reasoning (induktif) goes **from cases to a rule**: many observed cases suggest a general claim that is **likely, never certain** — every swan seen was white, so swans are white, until Australia. The SMK logic chapter teaches the pair as instruments: deduction proves, induction discovers.
**Abductive** reasoning (abduktif) goes **from a result to its most likely cause**: the floor is wet and the bucket is empty — probably the water was spilled. It is the reasoning of diagnosis — the technician's, the doctor's, the debugger's — and the weakest of the three in certainty, because another cause may explain the same result. Its strength is that it proposes what to check first. The chapter's craft is naming which reasoning a moment needs: prove with deduction, generalise with induction held humbly, and hunt causes with abduction — then verify what abduction proposes.
