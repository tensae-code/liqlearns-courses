# Designing a Maze Game — Story, Character and Goal

## Do it first

Before any maze exists, answer three questions: Who is travelling through the maze? Why? And what are they trying to reach? A game whose story, character and goal are clear can be built; a game that starts with random walls cannot.

## Sub lesson 1 — Plan the story first

The maze project begins on paper, not in blocks. **Plan the story**: who is the traveller, where must they get to, and what stands in their way? A few clear sentences are enough — a lost animal crossing the maze to reach home, a keeper collecting something precious from the far corner. The story decides everything after it: what the character looks like, what the goal is, even how hard the maze should feel. Write the story down before touching the paint editor.

[Picture — a picture of a paper plan with three written lines — who travels, why, what they must reach — and a small maze sketch under them]

## Sub lesson 2 — Draw a small game character

The game's traveller is a sprite you draw yourself. Keep it **small** — a maze's passages are narrow, and a big character cannot fit through them. Draw zoomed in: **zooming** enlarges your view so small work stays neat and the character's edges stay clean. Give the character some life with a **gradient fill** — a colour that shades from one tone to another — so it looks rounded rather than flat. The character must also be easy to see against the maze walls: a colour the walls do not use.

[Picture — a picture of a small game character being drawn zoomed-in in the paint editor, its body shaded with a gradient fill, beside the same character at true size on a maze corner]

## Sub lesson 3 — Draw the goal

Last, draw the **goal**: the thing or place the traveller must reach to win — drawn clearly enough that a player who has never seen your game understands at a glance, 'that is where I must get to'. The goal is its own sprite or drawing, in a colour and shape that stand apart from both the walls and the character. Story, character, goal: with those three designed, the maze itself can now be drawn — that is the next lesson's work.

[Picture — a picture of a maze corner with a bright, clearly drawn goal — a star-topped door — standing apart from the wall colour and the character]

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: design before programming — the story in a few sentences, a small character drawn zoomed-in with gradient fills, and a goal any new player understands at once. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
