# Lesson plan — Designing a Maze Game — Story, Character and Goal

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Programming · **Lesson:** Designing a Maze Game — Story, Character and Goal
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Finland (2026-11-14) — in the title Programming on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Finland's *Ensiaskeleet ohjelmointiin* (student book, Oppilaan kirja, Versio 2.0), because no existing lesson teaches this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A maze game is designed before it is programmed: first plan the story the game tells, then draw a small game character of your own (working zoomed-in, with gradient fills), then draw the goal the player must reach.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on drawing sprites in the paint editor; the design order — story, character, goal — is this lesson's whole content, taken from the start of Finland's maze-game project.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Plan the story first** — Plan a maze game's story in a few clear sentences.
2. **Draw a small game character** — Draw a small game character with zoom and gradient fills.
3. **Draw the goal** — Draw a clear goal a new player understands at a glance.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Plan a maze game's story in a few clear sentences.
2. Draw a small game character, using zoom and gradient fills.
3. Draw a clear goal for the player to reach.
4. Say why the design comes before the programming.

## What you need
A computer with Scratch, and paper and a pencil for the story plan.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Before any maze exists, answer three questions: Who is travelling through the maze? Why? And what are they trying to reach? A game whose story, character and goal are clear can be built; a game that starts with random walls cannot. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of a paper plan with three written lines — who travels, why, what they must reach — and a small maze sketch under them — the student studies the picture and says, for each part of it, which part of the lesson it shows.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of a small game character being drawn zoomed-in in the paint editor, its body shaded with a gradient fill, beside the same character at true size on a maze corner — the student studies the picture and says, for each part of it, which part of the lesson it shows.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of a maze corner with a bright, clearly drawn goal — a star-topped door — standing apart from the wall colour and the character — the student studies the picture and says, for each part of it, which part of the lesson it shows.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the lesson's key names and definitions aloud, in the order the lesson gives them — the student listens once through, then reads the same lines aloud along with the voice. (Audio is a described slot in this build; no audio file is made.)
3. **Say it to your companion** — the companion block in lesson/lesson.md.
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the lesson in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).

5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.

## If the learner is stuck
Character cannot fit the passages → it was drawn too big; shrink it or widen the maze. Goal looks like part of the wall → give it its own colour and shape, never the wall colour. Story vague → answer the three questions in writing: who, why, reach what.
