# Lesson plan — Counting How Many Times a Letter Appears in a Text

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Programming · **Lesson:** Counting How Many Times a Letter Appears in a Text
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Palestine (2026-11-14) — in the title Programming on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Palestine's Grade 9 Programming student book, because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A letter is counted by walking the text: letter-number-of reads one letter’s position at a time, length-of gives the text’s end, and a counter loop counts the matches — the worked example counts a letter in “The Dome of the Rock stands in Jerusalem”.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. ## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Letter by letter — position, length, counter** — Three tools walk the text: **letter number of** reads the letter at one position, **length of** gives how many letters the text holds, and a **counter loop** steps position by position, **counting the matches** — each time the letter at the position is the letter sought, the counter grows by one.
2. **The Dome of the Rock sentence — and the projects** — The worked example counts a letter in the sentence **“The Dome of the Rock stands in Jerusalem”**. The projects widen the same walk: **counting the vowel letters in an English sentence**, **checking an email address contains @**, and **finding a whole word** in a text.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
