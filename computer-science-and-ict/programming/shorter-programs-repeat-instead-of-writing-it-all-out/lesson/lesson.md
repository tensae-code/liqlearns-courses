# Shorter Programs — Repeat Instead of Writing It All Out

## Do it first

A program marches a character right, right, right, right — twelve ‘right’ blocks in a sad long tail. Before reading on, say the one-block idea that makes the tail vanish without changing a single step of the walk.

## Sub lesson 1 — Repetition is a pattern begging to fold

A passage that **repeats the same blocks** is a pattern, and a pattern can **fold**: ScratchJr's ‘Raccourcir le programme’ séance and Scratch's ‘Raccourcir un programme’ both hunt the same prey — a repeated group, counted, lifted into a **repeat** block with its count. The folded program is not a different program: run both, and the character does exactly the same dance. Shortening is refactoring in miniature: same behaviour, less text, clearer shape.

In your mind's eye: repetition is a pattern begging to fold.

## Sub lesson 2 — Why shorter is better — and when it isn't

Shorter programs are **read at a glance**, **repaired in one place** (fix the group once, not twelve times), and **extended by changing a number**. The séances keep the honesty clause: folding pays only when the repetition is real — different blocks wearing similar shapes must not be forced into one repeat with patches. The programmer's eye trained here — spot repetition, verify sameness, fold — is the same eye that later finds loops, sub-programs and patterns everywhere.

In your mind's eye: why shorter is better — and when it isn't.
