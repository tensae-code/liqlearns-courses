# Lesson plan — Choosing and Switching Backgrounds in Scratch

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Programming · **Lesson:** Choosing and Switching Backgrounds in Scratch
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Palestine (2026-11-14) — in the title Programming on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Palestine's Grade 6 Programming student book, because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A background is the picture the stage wears — chosen from the library to match the sprite and the story — and a project switches backgrounds, with waits, to move the action from place to place.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Choosing a background from the library** — Backgrounds are **inserted from the library**: the background is **the stage the action happens on**, so the choice is a storytelling choice — the book teaches **matching the background to the sprite**, a place that fits the character and the moment.
2. **Moving between backgrounds — the travelling parrot** — The travelling parrot carries the second half: the project **switches background** as the parrot travels, **wait blocks** pace the journey, and the parrot’s flight is finished with **bounce at the edge** and the **left-right rotation style** so it turns without flipping upside down.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
