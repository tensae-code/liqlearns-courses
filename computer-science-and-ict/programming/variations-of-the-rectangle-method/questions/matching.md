# Variations of the Rectangle Method — matching

Match each item on the left with the one it belongs to on the right.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What does averaging left and right sums cancel?** → **Their one-sidedness — one under-, the other over-estimates**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What shape is the average equal to?** → **The trapezoid areas on the subintervals**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What is the general lesson?** → **Two estimates with opposite errors are worth more together than either alone**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What is kept from the plain method?** → **The bracket — both sums still bound the truth**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) Is the average guaranteed inside the bracket?** → **Yes — it is the bracket's own middle point of the two sums**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What does doubling n let you watch?** → **The error's pace — how fast the sums approach the truth**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) By what factor does the averaged sums' error shrink when h halves?** → **About fourfold (against twofold for one-sided sums)**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What can the coarse–fine pair estimate?** → **Its own error — from the change between the two sums**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) What is the combination step beyond the course called?** → **Extrapolation — cancelling the leading error term**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) When is an approximate answer honourable?** → **When its error is estimated, not ignored**
