# Variations of the Rectangle Method — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What does averaging left and right sums cancel?**
**Answer: Their one-sidedness — one under-, the other over-estimates**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What shape is the average equal to?**
**Answer: The trapezoid areas on the subintervals**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What is the general lesson?**
**Answer: Two estimates with opposite errors are worth more together than either alone**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What is kept from the plain method?**
**Answer: The bracket — both sums still bound the truth**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) Is the average guaranteed inside the bracket?**
**Answer: Yes — it is the bracket's own middle point of the two sums**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What does doubling n let you watch?**
**Answer: The error's pace — how fast the sums approach the truth**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) By what factor does the averaged sums' error shrink when h halves?**
**Answer: About fourfold (against twofold for one-sided sums)**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What can the coarse–fine pair estimate?**
**Answer: Its own error — from the change between the two sums**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) What is the combination step beyond the course called?**
**Answer: Extrapolation — cancelling the leading error term**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) When is an approximate answer honourable?**
**Answer: When its error is estimated, not ignored**
