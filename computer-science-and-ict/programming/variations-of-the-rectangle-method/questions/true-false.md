# Variations of the Rectangle Method — true or false

Say true or false, and fix the false ones in your own words.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: Their one-sidedness — one under-, the other over-estimates**
**Answer: True**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: Their one-sidedness — one under-, the other over-estimates** — as the answer to: What shape is the average equal to?
**Answer: False — The trapezoid areas on the subintervals**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: Two estimates with opposite errors are worth more together than either alone**
**Answer: True**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: Two estimates with opposite errors are worth more together than either alone** — as the answer to: What is kept from the plain method?
**Answer: False — The bracket — both sums still bound the truth**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: Yes — it is the bracket's own middle point of the two sums**
**Answer: True**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: Yes — it is the bracket's own middle point of the two sums** — as the answer to: What does doubling n let you watch?
**Answer: False — The error's pace — how fast the sums approach the truth**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: About fourfold (against twofold for one-sided sums)**
**Answer: True**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: About fourfold (against twofold for one-sided sums)** — as the answer to: What can the coarse–fine pair estimate?
**Answer: False — Its own error — from the change between the two sums**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: Extrapolation — cancelling the leading error term**
**Answer: True**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: Extrapolation — cancelling the leading error term** — as the answer to: When is an approximate answer honourable?
**Answer: False — When its error is estimated, not ignored**
