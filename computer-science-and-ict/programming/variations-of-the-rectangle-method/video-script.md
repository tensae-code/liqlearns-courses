# Video script — Variations of the Rectangle Method

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

For a monotone stretch, the **left sum under- and the right sum over-estimates** (or the reverse): their **average** cancels the one-sidedness — and that average is exactly the area of **trapezoids** standing on the subintervals, the staircase promoted to a ramp. The variation's lesson generalises: **two estimates with opposite errors are worth more together than either alone**, and the bracket (both sums reported) still bounds the truth, so nothing of the plain method's honesty is spent — the average is a better answer inside a kept guarantee.
The second variation is systematic: **compute the sum at n, then at 2n**, and watch the change: for the rectangle family the error shrinks roughly by a known factor (about **fourfold** for the midpoint/trapezoid averages when h halves, against twofold for one-sided sums) — so successive sums **estimate their own error**, and the refined pair (coarse, fine) can be **combined** to cancel the leading error term (the extrapolation step beyond this course). The chapter closes with its ethic complete: an approximate answer is honourable exactly when its **error is estimated, not ignored**.
