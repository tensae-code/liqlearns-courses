Placement choice: this lesson was built new for the ICT expansion (Kazakhstan, 2026-11-14) because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree was checked for the slug and the live lesson content was checked for near matches before building. It sits in the Programming title (programming/), following the book's own order. Provenance scope note: the placements stocked here are from Kazakhstan's okulyk.kz (OKULYK) textbooks, recommended by the Ministry of Education and Science of the Republic of Kazakhstan — the Digital Literacy and Informatika lines of the Almatykitap, Arman-PV and Atamura teams and the Nazarbayev Intellectual Schools (NIS) books, all student books except the two Kadirkulov teacher guides named in the entries below, whose placements are stocked exactly as filed. Many books exist in parallel language editions (Kazakh / Russian / Uzbek / Uyghur / English) of the same textbook: each distinct book is taught once, and every distinct edition is named and cited in the book entry. Kazakhstan's own ways are kept: one textbook line in up to five parallel language editions, the okulyk.kz digital library as the books' home, Scratch from Grade 1 and robotics strands (LEGO WeDo 2.0 and Mindstorms EV3) from Grade 1–2, and every keyword given in Kazakh–Russian–English in the early grades. Media slots in lesson/lesson.md are described slots only ([Picture — …]); no picture numbers are invented.


Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G7 Informatika): Grade 7 by the Almatykitap team is measurement and memory first (units, file formats and file sizes), then networks with antivirus security and the user interface, a long spreadsheet part that ends in modelling processes in Excel, and Python taught through files and branching — nested and compound conditions included. The creative-tasks lesson 2.4 (printed p. 40) is not placed.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukhametzhanova G7 Informatika, 2017 edition): the 2017 Atamura Grade 7 book adds two things its rivals lack — file archiving (creating archives and extracting them) in the first part, and a fifth part on 3D modelling of objects and events; its programming part works with programming languages and systems in general (the glossary's syntax examples are C++: main, cout, cin) and includes designing the project's interface.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukhametzhanova G7 Informatika, updated edition): the updated-content revision of the Atamura Grade 7 book keeps the 2017 skeleton (units, archives, networks, Excel, practical programming) but replaces the general programming part with Python: working with files, branching, nested and compound conditions and organising a choice; an appendix teaches building electronic learning games with a game constructor (Editor and Class versions).

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G7 Informatika, 2019 edition): Almatykitap's other Grade 7 book (2019, printed in Turkey) follows the same five-part frame as the Atamura 2017 book — units and memory, networks and antivirus, spreadsheets with conditional formatting and modelling, programming solutions with interface design (its bibliography is Lazarus/Delphi-based), and 3D modelling of objects and events in SketchUp.

Kazakhstan's own way (Ataeva & Vyshkova G8 Informatics, NIS): the NIS Grade 8 course is the most distinctive of the wave: vector graphics flows into geographic information systems (planning an excursion, making maps, visualising data in GIS), spreadsheets are used to solve logical problems, loops are taught through mathematics, physics, business and multimedia problems, and the year ends with a full project cycle — six stages, presentation, documentation, defence — plus a mini-project on artificial intelligence.

Kazakhstan's own way (Zhunusov G7 Informatics, English-medium): the English-medium Grade 7 book teaches the updated national programme in English with trilingual terminology boxes — spreadsheets are Google Sheets (a "My to-do list" first spreadsheet), and practical programming is three worked games: a Quiz game, Rock Paper Scissors, a Guessing game and a Camel game, each split into algorithm-development and programming lessons.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G7 «Информатика»): the Almatykitap Grade 7 course pairs every theory section with «Творческие задания» and test tasks, teaches spreadsheets up to conditional formatting and process modelling, and does its Python through files: reading/writing files and file functions come before branching, nested and compound conditions.

Kazakhstan's own way (Zhunusov G7 «INFORMATICS»): the Astana-kitap Grade 7 English-medium book teaches spreadsheets in Google Sheets, files and archiving before networks, Python file work at the start of the programming chapter, and a practical chapter that builds three named games — a quiz, Rock Paper Scissors, a guessing game and a Camel game — each taken from problem statement to testing.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G7 «Информатика» 2019 edition): the 2019 Almatykitap Grade 7 book is not the 2023 Python book (Book 15): after measurement, networks and spreadsheets it teaches programming through the Lazarus project interface — interface elements, data types, branching, nested and compound conditions — and ends with 3D modelling of objects and events (house, car, table, chair, window, glass as the final tasks).

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukametzhanova G7 «Информатика» 2017 edition): the Atamura Grade 7 book runs the same state programme as the Almatykitap one (Book 27) but adds a unit on converting between units of information, teaches the programming in C++, and likewise ends in 3D modelling of objects and events.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukametzhanova G7 «Информатика» 2021 edition): the 2021 Atamura revision of the Grade 7 book drops the 2017 edition's 3D modelling and C++, and follows the renewed programme instead: Python from files and branching through nested and compound conditions and choice, ending in a practical programming section.

Kazakhstan's own way (Salgaraeva G7 «Информатика»): the Arman-PV Grade 7 book doubles almost every theory unit with a named practicum — units of information, user interface, tables, files, branching, nested and compound conditions each get their own practice unit — and its spreadsheet section adds building formulas for calculations in the sheet.

Kazakhstan's own way (Atayeva/Vyushkova/Zhurbenko G8 «Информатика» NIS): the NIS Grade 8 book is the widest of the set: a whole section on vector graphics and geographic information systems (planning an excursion, making maps, visualising data in GIS), octal and hexadecimal coding, loops applied separately to mathematics, physics, business and animation, strings and subroutines, and a nine-step project method ending in a mini-project on artificial intelligence.
