# Prototyping and Rapid Application Development

## Do it first

The life cycle is careful — sometimes too careful. Fiji's Year 13 chapter closes with the faster roads: prototyping, where a rough version teaches what the real one must be, and rapid application development, which builds the whole system that way. This lesson is the alternative — and when it wins.

## Sub lesson 1 — Prototyping — build a rough one to learn from

A **prototype** is a **quick, rough, working version** of a system — or of its screens and reports — built **so users can try it and react**. Users who cannot say what they want from a description can say it at once from a prototype: wrong field, missing report, button in the wrong place. The prototype is then **improved in rounds** — shown, corrected, shown again — or used as the clearest possible requirements statement for a full build. Fiji's insight is about people, not programs: reaction is easier than imagination.

In your mind's eye: prototyping — build a rough one to learn from.

## Sub lesson 2 — RAD — the whole build at prototype speed

**Rapid Application Development (RAD)** turns the prototype's way into the whole method: users and builders work **together in short cycles**, building the system piece by piece, each piece tried by its real users as soon as it stands, with the plan allowed to change as the trying teaches. Its gains are speed and fit — a system its users helped shape. Its price is discipline's opposite risk: a loosely run RAD build can wander, half-documented and hard to maintain. Fiji's closing judgment is the senior course's balance: the life cycle for systems that must be right; prototyping and RAD where learning fast matters more.

In your mind's eye: rAD — the whole build at prototype speed.
