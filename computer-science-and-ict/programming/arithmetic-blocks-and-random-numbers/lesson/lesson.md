# Arithmetic Blocks and Random Numbers

## Do it first

Ask a friend to pick a number between 1 and 10 without telling you. Chance just entered the game. Programs can do the same pick — and they can also add, take away, multiply and divide while they run.

## Arithmetic Blocks and Random Numbers

Scratch's green **operator blocks** give programs a calculator inside them. The **arithmetic blocks** — add, subtract, multiply, divide — take two numbers and answer with the result, and that answer can go anywhere a number can go: into a move, a wait, a say. The **random block (случайни числа)** picks a number between two limits by chance, different every run — the block behind dice games, guessing games and surprises. The lesson joins them the way programs do: compute something, or chance something, then **use the answer** in the next block. Reading skill: given an arithmetic block with its numbers, say its answer before running; given a random block, say the range the answer must fall in, even though the exact number is chance.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Operator blocks do arithmetic inside a program — add, subtract, multiply, divide — and a random block picks a number by chance, so a program can count and can surprise. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: build an arithmetic block and say its answer before running. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: put an arithmetic answer to work inside another block. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: set a random block's limits and say the range its answer must fall in. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
