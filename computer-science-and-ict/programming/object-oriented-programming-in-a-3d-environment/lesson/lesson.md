# Object-Oriented Programming in a 3D Environment

## Do it first

Grade 10 meets object-oriented programming standing inside a world: characters on a stage, each an object, each answering methods — wave, turn, move — that you can watch happening. The abstract idea becomes a play you direct.

## Sub lesson 1 — Objects you can see

In the 3D environment every character and prop on the stage is an **object** of a **class** the gallery offers (a person, an animal, a vehicle), carrying the class's **properties** (its size, colour, position) which the learner sets, and its **methods** (its ready actions — move, turn, say) which the learner calls. Because the object stands visible, the OOP vocabulary stops being vocabulary: a property is the slider you just changed; a method is the movement you just watched.

## Sub lesson 2 — Directing the play — methods in order, worlds that answer

The learner builds a small **scene as a program**: objects placed, then methods called **in sequence** — and, as the environment grows, in answer to events — so the 3D world becomes the gentlest first meeting with the two great ideas of the senior course: **object-orientation** (the world as acting things) and **event-driven** working (the world answering what happens). Greece places this chapter beside mobile-app programming in the same unit, 'creating applications', so Grade 10's learners meet modern programming's shapes by making worlds before they meet its text.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: An object of a class the gallery offers. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: Properties (size, colour, position) and methods (ready actions). Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: The slider you just changed. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: The movement you just watched. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
