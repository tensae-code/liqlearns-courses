# Flowchart symbols

## Do it first

Seven shapes, seven jobs. One starts and ends. One asks a question with more than one way out. One lets information in or out. Before the table is opened, guess which shape you would draw for 'Is it raining?' — the lesson's table will judge your guess.

## The symbols, first four

Terminator / Start–End: marks the starting or ending point, holding the word Start or End — always used at an algorithm's beginning and end. Action / Process rectangle: a single step, or a whole sub-process within a larger one. Decision diamond: a branching point — different decisions emerge from different points of the diamond. Input/Output parallelogram: material or information entering or leaving the system.

## The symbols, last three

Connector: a small circle — the flow continues where a matching symbol with the same letter is placed. Flow Line: the arrows showing sequence and direction. Subroutine / module: a sequence of actions performing a specific task within a larger process, describable in more detail on a separate flowchart — the predefined process. These seven are the alphabet of every flowchart in this unit.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: the seven basic flowchart symbols: terminator, process rectangle, decision diamond, input/output parallelogram, connector, flow line and subroutine. Then name the key words of this lesson — the terminator, the decision diamond, the input/output symbol, the connector — and say what each one means.
