**Acceptance count: taught in 8 countries (Ethiopia, Sudan, Bhutan, Libya, Mongolia, Sri Lanka, Bangladesh, Jordan).**

Sudan's own way (Grade 11): a university-style secondary text (single named author, Dr Awad Haj Ali of the University of Gezira) that teaches systems work before code — file types and file security in a floppy-disk/UPS world, a complete spare-parts-factory system as the running example, the algorithm named for al-Khwarizmi, desk-checking with real sales data, and then a full BASIC course (numbered lines, READ/DATA, GOSUB, DEF FN) with payroll and mean/variance programs — data processing taught as a school subject in its own right.

Sudan's own way (Grade 9): the whole Grade 9 book is programming — concepts and Pascal-style examples first, then algorithms and flowcharts, then a full Scratch course ending in a designed project. The Bakht Al-Ruda ICT series runs in the same pattern from Grade 4 to Grade 9.

Bhutan's own way (Class VII, Literacy with ICT student book): Social media is taught hands-on — learners create a Facebook account, post with audience settings, and follow netiquette and an Acceptable Use Policy; backup is taught to Google Drive, and the end-of-year experience is shared on Facebook.

Libya's own way (Grade 11 Term 1, Information Technology student book): a programming year taught in old-school order — problem solving and algorithms, flowcharts, memory/variables, then Visual Basic statement by statement (LET, PRINT, INPUTBOX, IF, SELECT CASE, FOR…NEXT, DO…WHILE, DO…UNTIL), every chapter ending in graded exercises. The book is printed for BOTH the Science and Humanities sections (للقسمين العلمي والأدبي), although the records label it Humanities only — cited as printed, and taught to Science and Humanities together.

Mongolia's own way (Grade 9): Grade 9 turns outward to society and real tools — the information society taught through Mongolia's own shift, open-source software through Soyombo Linux (Mongolia's own Linux) and Mongolian OpenOffice, copyright through Mongolia's 2006 copyright law; algorithms are drawn as flowcharts and run in Turtle Art; the long spreadsheet unit grades pupils with nested IFs, builds Fibonacci sequences, grows a 500,000-tögrög bank deposit, and charts the depth and area of Mongolia's great lakes (Khuvsgul, Uvs, Khar-Us); e-services are the real Mongolian ones — the ТҮЦ service kiosk, bookstore.mn and internet banking.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 6 Reading Book): a story/dialogue-led "Reading Book" with a talking computer character; every chapter cross-refers to numbered Workbook activities; local sectors (school office, ATM, ICU, factories, agriculture) and the 3R e-waste method are taught from Grade 6.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 11, Tamil): a full Visual Basic 6.0 programming course inside the O/L book (IDE, controls, data types, Select Case, arrays), an SDLC chapter, Photoshop-based multimedia, hand-coded HTML with Sri Lankan examples (the Sinharaja rainforest page, ucsc.cmb.ac.lk addresses, sltnet.lk domains), and sector chapters (agriculture, industry, government, commerce) before malware and health.

Switzerland's own way (Grades 7–8, Décodage 7e–8e): at 7e–8e the Décodage course becomes a real computer-science course under the Plan d'études romand EN 22/23: Thymio VPL missions (including Morse communication and self-driving-car autonomy levels), a full Scratch discovery course (movement, shortening programs, sub-programs as own blocks, remixing), a Scratch video-game project run in “studios” with a cahier des charges, data work (Huffman-style compression via Zip la marmotte, binary/octal/hex/ASCII codings, file sizes), access security (passwords, file encryption, phishing, authentication), home networks at two levels (IP addresses; message routing, packets, TCP and encryption), and inquiries into sorting, sorting networks, Caesar decryption and parity-bit error detection. Médias/Usages covers social networks and digital identity/footprint, information sources, cyberbullying and the environmental cost of digital technology.

Bangladesh's own way (HSC ICT, Classes XI–XII and Alim): one compulsory ICT book for every HSC and Alim student in the country, saturated with Bangladesh itself: Bangabandhu Satellite-1 and its ground stations, bdjobs and chakri.com job portals, Daraz and Bikroy e-commerce, the Walton factory's automated production line, the National ID card used to teach primary keys and its database used to teach RDBMS at work, a full Bangla Unicode table and a worked example of putting Bangla text on a web page with meta charset, the national letter-grade scale (A+ from 80) as the else-if programme, "I Love Bangladesh" as the loop example text, an algorithm for reaching college by bicycle or rickshaw, and the Digital Bangladesh programme — National Data Centre, hi-tech parks, Startup Bangladesh and Bangladesh Bank's QR-based Cashless Bangladesh. C is the HSC programming language, taught with scanf/printf in Code::Blocks.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 7 G7T2 Student): Citizenship comes before code in Term 2 — digital citizenship, intellectual property with the actual patent-application procedures, data privacy law, and balanced use of communication tools — then a pure algorithms unit with no computer needed (writing conditions, tracking, representation, flowcharts, improving algorithms).

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 6 G6 teacher): The guide reorganises the student book's 24 §s into 13 standard themes (6-1 to 6-13), each with lesson topic, outcome codes, cross-cutting competencies and practical work; it closes with a thematic plan, a worked sample lesson plan (§3) and slide-presentation assessment rubrics.
