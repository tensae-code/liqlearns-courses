# Computational thinking

## Do it first

One big block: 'Organise the school day.' Nobody can do it in one move. But break it — wake, wash, eat, travel, lessons — and each piece is easy. This lesson's thinking does exactly that to problems, and the research that goes with it names the skills of the century.

## The thought process

Life in the 21st century demands good problem solvers, drawing on critical thinking, communication, collaboration, creativity and innovation. Computational thinking is the thought process of formulating a problem and expressing its solution so that a computer, human or machine can effectively carry it out — breaking problems into simple steps and synthesising simple steps into bigger solutions.

## 21st-century skills in full — teacher evidence

The research answer prints the full framework (from the P21 Framework for 21st Century Learning): twelve abilities in three categories. Learning skills — the four Cs: critical thinking, creativity, collaboration, communication — the mental processes for adapting to a modern work environment. Literacy skills — IMT: information literacy, media literacy, technology literacy — discerning facts, publication outlets and the technology behind them, separating trustworthy sources from the misinformation flooding the Internet. Life skills — FLIPS: flexibility, leadership, initiative, productivity, social skills — the intangible personal and professional qualities of everyday life. And the bridge to this unit: critical thinking is another face of problem solving — reasoning effectively, thinking systematically, and evaluating alternative solutions, including for non-familiar problems.
