# Hands-on — Number Systems and Conversions

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Count like a computer
Hold up one hand. Each finger is a bit: finger down is 0, finger up is 1, with place values 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 from thumb to little finger. Count from 0 to 20 on your fingers, saying each binary pattern aloud. Your hand can count to 31 — check what pattern 31 is.

## The conversion ladder
On paper, take the number 45. Convert it to binary by repeated division by 2. Convert the binary answer to hexadecimal by grouping in fours. Then convert the hexadecimal back to binary, and the binary back to decimal by place values. You must land on 45 — if you do not, find the step where the ladder broke.

## In the simulator
If your school has TINA or Circuit Wizard, open a blank simulation and find where binary values are entered and shown. Enter 10110 and say its decimal and hexadecimal forms before the tool shows them. Montenegro's book pairs this theory with simulation from the start — the habit is: predict first, then let the tool check you.
