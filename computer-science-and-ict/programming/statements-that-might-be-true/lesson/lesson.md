# Statements That Might Be True — Uncertain Statements

## Do it first

Say: 'It will rain tomorrow.' Is that true? Is it false? Nobody knows yet — it might be true. This lesson teaches uncertain statements: sayings that are not surely true and not surely false.

## Statements That Might Be True — Uncertain Statements

A **statement** is a saying that tells something about the world. Some statements are **true**: 'The sun rises in the morning.' Some are **false**: 'Fish can fly.'

But some statements are **uncertain** — they **might be true**. 'It will rain tomorrow' might be true and might be false; we must wait and see. 'Our team will win the game' is uncertain until the game is played. 'There is a stone in this closed box' is uncertain until the box is opened — the stone either is there or is not, but we do not know yet.

An uncertain statement is not a mistake. It becomes true or false when we **learn more** or when the event **happens**. Sorting statements into true, false and uncertain is careful thinking: never call a 'might be' statement true before you know, and never call it false either.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Some statements are neither surely true nor surely false: an uncertain statement might be true — it depends on what happens or on what we do not yet know. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: say what an uncertain statement is.
2. Show it, don't just say it: sort statements into true, false and uncertain.
3. Show it, don't just say it: say what makes an uncertain statement become true or false: learning more, or the event happening.
