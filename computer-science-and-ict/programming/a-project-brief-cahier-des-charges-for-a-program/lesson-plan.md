## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What a cahier des charges says** — A **cahier des charges** is the brief a project is built against: the **product** (what game, what kind), the **players** (who it is for), the **required behaviours** (rules, scoring, win/lose, levels), the **limits** (time, tools, content rules), and the **acceptance** — how the studio and its client will judge ‘done’. Fiche 4 gives the video-game project's brief its form, because studios that write it argue once, on paper, instead of at delivery.
2. **Working to the brief** — During the build, the brief is the studio's court: scope questions (‘shall we add a second level?’) are answered by reading it, not by enthusiasm; playtest findings are sorted into ‘brief broken’ (must fix) and ‘brief silent’ (studio's choice, or a brief amendment, written and agreed). The delivered game is judged against the delivered brief — a professional mirror the scenario holds up so pupils feel the difference between ‘I like it’ and ‘it meets its charges’.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
