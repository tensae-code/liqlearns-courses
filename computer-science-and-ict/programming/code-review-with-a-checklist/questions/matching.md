**1. (Sub lesson 1) What is code review?** → **Another programmer reads code before it is accepted, looking for faults, risks and unreadable places**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) Why can't the writer review their own code as well?** → **The writer reads what they meant, not what they wrote — the reviewer reads what is actually there**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What three things does the reviewer hunt?** → **Faults, risks, and unreadable places**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) Why use a checklist at all?** → **It makes review complete instead of impressionistic — nothing depends on the reviewer's mood or memory**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) Name the four sections of a good checklist.** → **Correctness, readability, safety, and tests**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What form is each checklist item?** → **A yes/no question, answerable from the code**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) How does the checklist learn?** → **It is revised whenever a real fault escapes review — the miss becomes a new item**
