# Video script — Debugging — Finding and Fixing the Wrong Instruction

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

**Debugging** is finding and fixing the wrong instruction in a program. The method is patient: **run the program slowly**, watching each step, until the mouse does something the plan did not intend — that step's arrow is the suspect. Then ask what is wrong with it: is it **pointing the wrong way**, is it **one step too many or too few**, or is it **standing in the wrong place** in the row? Most wrong instructions are one of these three, and each has its own fix.
The fixes match the faults. A step too many is **removed**; a wrong-direction arrow is **replaced** with the right one; steps in the wrong place are **reordered** — swapped until the row reads as the road should go. Then the rule that closes every debugging: **run the program again**, from the start, all the way — a fix is only a guess until the mouse reaches the cheese by the corrected program. Find, fix, run again: the loop every programmer in this course will live by.
