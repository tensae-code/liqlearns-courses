# Lesson plan — Recursive procedures — a procedure that calls itself

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Programming · **Lesson:** Recursive procedures — a procedure that calls itself
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 12 — Programming in the Computer Science and ICT ladder (TITLES.md); stocked from Ethiopia's Grade 8 book, Unit 6 — Logic Oriented Graphics Oriented (LOGO) Programming. The ladder is the recommended order, never a forced one.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a recursive procedure is one that invokes a copy of itself — a call of the procedure within the procedure — and the book's star example, examined honestly: its code repeats with REPEAT and never calls itself.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the procedures lessons — a procedure must be known before a procedure that calls itself can be understood.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or in steps — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: LOGO procedures — giving a name to a set of commands; Creating a LOGO procedure in the Editor. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define a recursive procedure in the book's words.
2. Ground the idea in the book's everyday procedures — the waking-up steps.
3. Read the star procedure's code and say what it actually does, line by line.
4. Tell a truly recursive procedure from one that only repeats.

## What you need
A computer with MSW LOGO open; paper and a pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner writes the steps they followed this morning from waking to school — a procedure from their own life — before the word recursive is named (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the parts below, in order (lesson/lesson.md).
