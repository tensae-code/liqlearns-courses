# Video script — Graph Measures — Distance, Centre and Diameter

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

How spread out is a network? Measure it.

The distance between two nodes is the shortest path joining them. Count its edges.

From one node, spread out in waves: neighbours at one, their neighbours at two. The largest distance you reach is that node's eccentricity — its distance from the far end of its world.

The node with the smallest eccentricity is the centre. That smallest value is the radius. The largest distance anywhere is the diameter.

And here is the wonder: the world's social graph is enormous — and its diameter is tiny. About six handshakes join any two strangers.
