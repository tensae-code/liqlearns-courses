## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Natural languages — grown, rich, ambiguous** — A **natural language** (Greek, English, every mother tongue) **grew** with its people: enormous vocabulary, grammar full of history, and **ambiguity everywhere** — one sentence honestly meaning two things, its sense carried by context, tone and shared life. That richness is its glory for people and its impossibility for machines: a computer given an ambiguous instruction does not know which meaning was meant, and guessing is not obeying.
2. **Artificial languages — built, small, exact** — An **artificial language** is **built on purpose** with a **small fixed vocabulary and exact rules**: a programming language (and ΓΛΩΣΣΑ among them) is one, and so are mathematics' notation and the sign systems of science. Every sentence of an artificial language means **one thing**, checkable by its rules alone — which is exactly the property that lets a machine translate and execute it without judgement. The chapter's balance: people will keep *thinking* in natural languages; artificial languages are the narrow, honest bridge their thoughts cross to reach the machine.
