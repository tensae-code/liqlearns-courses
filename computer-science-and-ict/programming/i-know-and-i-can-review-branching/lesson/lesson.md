# I Know and I Can — Review: Branching

## Do it first

Take one branching task: a question, two ways. Run it answering yes; run it answering no. If either run fails, the review has found its work.

## I Know and I Can — Review: Branching

Grade 4's first **"Знам и мога" (I know and I can)** check proves the **branching** strand: the **branching block in Scratch** (from Урок 7, stocked in the loops-and-conditions lesson) and the **branched algorithm** (Урок 8), exercised through the lesson's theme — **"Пътешествие из България" (A journey through Bulgaria)** — where decisions about a journey are the branching tasks. As always in this book, the tasks are done alone and checked against what the script actually does on **both** answers; a branch proved only on its yes-way is not proved. Anything not proved is named and practised again.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: This "Знам и мога" mastery check proves the branching work — the branching block and branched algorithms — inside a "Journey through Bulgaria" theme. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: prove a branching block task on both its yes and its no runs. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: prove a branched algorithm with both ways written and working. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: name any branching skill not yet proved and practise it. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
