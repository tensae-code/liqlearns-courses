# Lesson plan — Making Sprites Talk — Say Blocks and Dialogue in Scratch

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Programming · **Lesson:** Making Sprites Talk — Say Blocks and Dialogue in Scratch
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Palestine (2026-11-14) — in the title Programming on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Palestine's Grade 6, Grade 7, Grade 8 and Grade 9 Programming student books, because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The say block puts words in a speech bubble; say-for-seconds times the line; waits keep speakers from overlapping; and dialogue can be run by broadcasts, each sprite speaking or playing its recorded line on its cue. Palestine’s note stands: Arabic appears reversed in Scratch, because Scratch writes left-to-right.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The say block — one sprite speaks** — The **say block** gives a sprite words in a bubble — Grade 7’s lion announces “I am the lion” — and **say for seconds** holds the line for a set time. Long speech is **spaced** so it can be read. Grade 6 keeps the local note: **Arabic appears reversed in Scratch**, because Scratch writes left-to-right.
2. **Dialogue — turns, waits and broadcasts** — A conversation is timed turns: **say-for-seconds plus waits** so one speaks while the other is silent and speakers do not overlap — the Abby and Maya conversation, the homeland dialogue project. Grade 9 runs the turns by **broadcasts**: in the race of the conceited one and the persistent one, each sprite’s **recorded line plays on its cue**.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
