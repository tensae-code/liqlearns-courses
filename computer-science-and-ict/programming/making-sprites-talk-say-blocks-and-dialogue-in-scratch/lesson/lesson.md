# Making Sprites Talk — Say Blocks and Dialogue in Scratch

## Do it first

“I am the lion.” One block gives a sprite those words. Two sprites, timed turns and recorded voices give a whole play — the race of the conceited cat and the persistent mouse. This lesson is the say block, from one line to a full dialogue.

## Sub lesson 1 — The say block — one sprite speaks

The **say block** gives a sprite words in a bubble — Grade 7’s lion announces “I am the lion” — and **say for seconds** holds the line for a set time. Long speech is **spaced** so it can be read. Grade 6 keeps the local note: **Arabic appears reversed in Scratch**, because Scratch writes left-to-right.

## Sub lesson 2 — Dialogue — turns, waits and broadcasts

A conversation is timed turns: **say-for-seconds plus waits** so one speaks while the other is silent and speakers do not overlap — the Abby and Maya conversation, the homeland dialogue project. Grade 9 runs the turns by **broadcasts**: in the race of the conceited one and the persistent one, each sprite’s **recorded line plays on its cue**.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: The say block puts words in a speech bubble; say-for-seconds times the line; waits keep speakers from overlapping; and dialogue can be run by broadcasts, each sprite speaking or playing its recorded line on its cue. Palestine’s note stands: Arabic appears reversed in Scratch, because Scratch writes left-to-right. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
