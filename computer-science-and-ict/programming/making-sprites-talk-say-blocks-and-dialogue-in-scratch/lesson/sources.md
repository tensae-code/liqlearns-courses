**Acceptance count: taught in 2 countries (Palestine, Kazakhstan).**

Placement choice: this lesson was built new for the ICT expansion (Palestine, 2026-11-14) because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree was checked for the slug and for near matches before building. The placements file named this slug directly (NEW LESSON NEEDED — programming/making-sprites-talk-say-blocks-and-dialogue-in-scratch). It sits in the Programming title (programming/), following the book's own order.

Palestine’s own way (Grade 6, Programming): Grade 6 programming is a full Scratch course (MIT, named in the book) in four units — basics, movement and costumes, sprites communicating, projects. Lessons after Unit 1 are deliberately non-cumulative: pupils imagine, program, then share their product in exhibitions and competitions. A local touch is kept: the “say” lesson notes Arabic text appears reversed because Scratch writes left-to-right.

Palestine’s own way (Grade 7, Programming): the Scratch course deepens in three units — programming graphics, sprites and backgrounds, sprite stories and dialogues. Local grounding is explicit: the pen unit opens by asking whether Scratch can draw ornaments like the decorations of Al-Aqsa Mosque; the stage is explained as a theatre whose actors (sprites) follow the director exactly and in order.

Palestine’s own way (Grade 8, Programming): Grade 8 Scratch is organised around making things — Unit 1 programs drawings and ornaments (the unit opens with the ornaments of Jerusalem’s churches and the Dome of the Rock), Unit 2 weaves interactive stories with sound and dialogue (including a dialogue project on love of the homeland), Unit 3 designs real games with variables, operators and sensing.

Palestine’s own way (Grade 9, Programming): Grade 9 is a full applications course in Scratch — after a basics unit it builds real things: a photo editor, a video player, a phone PIN lock, a bank account, a library sign-up/login, an alarm clock, an elevator, a task list, a wall clock that can ring at prayer times. Its own workarounds are content too: Scratch 2 cannot type Arabic in its paint editor, so Arabic text is written in MS Paint, saved as a picture and uploaded; the news-ticker lesson’s breaking-news example is the Ministry’s own decision to teach programming in Grades 5–9; the letter-counting lesson’s example sentence says the Dome of the Rock stands in Jerusalem.

Morocco's own way (Grade 4): Grade 4 stays inside Scratch but moves from the interface to behaviour — motion, looks, sound control and event handling taught block-family by block-family, then combined in two animated scenes (a city tour; an aquarium). (teacher guide — evidence)


Kazakhstan's own way (Kobdikova G2 Digital Literacy): the same six-part national Grade 2 frame as the Almatykitap book, but the tools differ: the "word by word" part builds a spoken-text dialogue between two Scratch characters, sound is recorded and trimmed in the Windows Voice Recorder app (not WavePad), media players AIMP and Windows Media Player are named, the keyboard trainer is the online typingstudy.com Kazakh course, the text editor is WordPad, and the Dancing Robot dances to the Kazakh folk melody "Kamajai" loaded through the EV3 Sound Editor.

Kazakhstan's own way (Isabaeva G2 Digital Literacy): a third Grade 2 course on the same six-part national frame, from the Kokzhiek/Atamura side: the keyboard trainer is RapidTyping 5 with full finger-placement rules, sound is edited in Wave Editor (wave-editor.com) by selecting waveform segments, voice recording also uses a smartphone dictaphone, and the final part has no text-editor lesson — the project algorithm itself is the writing task. Unnumbered end-of-part project and summary pages (printed pp. 27, 50, 84, 99, 114, 136–137) are not placed separately.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kopeeva G5 Informatika): Grade 5 Informatika (the older, pre-"Digital Literacy" subject name) is built around Scratch as a "game programming environment": a whole chapter runs from a first program through blocks, animation graphics, sound effects and dialogue to linear/branching/cyclic commands; earlier chapters pair Internet safety with data protection, and the year ends with print-ready project work — a Word newspaper page with WordArt and columns, presented as slides.

Kazakhstan's own way (Isabaeva G2 «Цифровая грамотность»): six sections each built round a cross-cutting theme («Всё обо мне», «Моя семья и друзья», «Моя школа»/«Мой родной край», «В здоровом теле — здоровый дух», «Традиции и фольклор», «Окружающая среда»/«Путешествие»); every section ends in a project, and the year ends with a build-and-programme «Танцующий робот» (Dancing Robot) project in Scratch + LEGO Mindstorms EV3 (the book's own resource list names scratch.mit.edu and the Mindstorms EV3 software).

Kazakhstan's own way (Kopeeva G5 «Информатика»): the year runs safety → internet safety → information and its processing → a long Scratch chapter, and it closes not with a test but with two production projects: a printed newspaper page made in a word processor (page setup, columns, WordArt, page numbers) and a defended Scratch project published to the Scratch community site.
