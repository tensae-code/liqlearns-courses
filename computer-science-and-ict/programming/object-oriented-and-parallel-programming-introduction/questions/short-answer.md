# Object-Oriented and Parallel Programming — Introduction — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What is an object, in this introduction?**
**Answer: A thing holding its data (properties) and its actions (methods) together**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) How do objects work together?**
**Answer: They send messages to each other**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) Against what is OOP set?**
**Answer: One long procedure marching over naked data**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) Name a world it models honestly.**
**Answer: A school (or a shop, or a game)**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) Which chapter introduces it?**
**Answer: Greece Grade 12 Anaptyxi Efarmogon, Chapter 6**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What is parallel programming?**
**Answer: Programs whose parts run at the same time, splitting a job across processors**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What does parallelism buy?**
**Answer: Falling wall-clock time, where the job truly divides**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What is its new difficulty?**
**Answer: Coordination — sharing data carefully and joining at the right places**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) What is bought if coordination fails?**
**Answer: Speed paid for with answers that change from run to run**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) What closes the chapter?**
**Answer: The programming environments where each road is worked**
