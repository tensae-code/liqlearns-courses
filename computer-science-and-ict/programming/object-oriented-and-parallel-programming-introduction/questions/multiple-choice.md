# Object-Oriented and Parallel Programming — Introduction — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What is an object, in this introduction?**
a) A thing holding its data (properties) and its actions (methods) together  b) They send messages to each other  c) One long procedure marching over naked data  d) A school (or a shop, or a game)
**Answer: a**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) How do objects work together?**
a) One long procedure marching over naked data  b) They send messages to each other  c) A school (or a shop, or a game)  d) Greece Grade 12 Anaptyxi Efarmogon, Chapter 6
**Answer: b**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) Against what is OOP set?**
a) A school (or a shop, or a game)  b) Greece Grade 12 Anaptyxi Efarmogon, Chapter 6  c) One long procedure marching over naked data  d) Programs whose parts run at the same time, splitting a job across processors
**Answer: c**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) Name a world it models honestly.**
a) Greece Grade 12 Anaptyxi Efarmogon, Chapter 6  b) Programs whose parts run at the same time, splitting a job across processors  c) Falling wall-clock time, where the job truly divides  d) A school (or a shop, or a game)

**5. (Sub lesson 1) Which chapter introduces it?**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What is parallel programming?**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What does parallelism buy?**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What is its new difficulty?**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) What is bought if coordination fails?**
