# Lesson plan — Project — A Maths Quiz in Scratch

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Programming · **Lesson:** Project — A Maths Quiz in Scratch
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Morocco (2026-11-14) — in the title Programming on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Morocco's Grade 6 “J'excelle en informatique” teacher guide, because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson. This lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence only (Morocco), named in lesson/sources.md, counted nowhere.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** One round of the quiz is a complete circuit: the program asks a maths question — built with operators, so the numbers can change every game, a random pair of numbers and an operation — captures the player's answer, works out the true answer with the same operators, and compares the two with a condition: if they match, the player is told they are right; if not, the true answer is shown. A quiz of one question is a moment; the project makes a game of rounds, and a score variable keeps the count: set to zero at the start, changed by one for every right answer, and shown at the end as the player's result.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.
