## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Program and programmer** — Sudan's Grade 9 book — a whole book about programming — opens with its concepts. A **program** is a set of instructions, written to solve a problem or do a task, that the computer carries out in order. The **programmer** is the person who writes it: understanding the problem, planning the steps and giving them to the machine in a form it accepts. Programming is this whole craft — not the typing of code, but the thinking the code carries.
2. **The parts of a programming language** — A program is written in a **programming language**, and the book names what a language is made of: its **words** — the reserved commands the language allows; its **symbols** — the signs for operations and values; and its **rules** — the grammar that fixes how words and symbols may be joined into instructions the computer understands. Break the rules and the instruction is refused, exactly as a sentence with broken grammar fails between people. Every language met later in the book — and the Scratch blocks after — is examined against these same parts.
