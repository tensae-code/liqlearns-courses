# What Programming Is — Concepts and the Parts of a Language

## Do it first

Before Sudan's Grade 9 pupils write a single instruction, their book stops them at the door and asks: what is a program, who writes it, and what is the language it is written in made of? This lesson is that doorway.

## Sub lesson 1 — Program and programmer

Sudan's Grade 9 book — a whole book about programming — opens with its concepts. A **program** is a set of instructions, written to solve a problem or do a task, that the computer carries out in order. The **programmer** is the person who writes it: understanding the problem, planning the steps and giving them to the machine in a form it accepts. Programming is this whole craft — not the typing of code, but the thinking the code carries.

## Sub lesson 2 — The parts of a programming language

A program is written in a **programming language**, and the book names what a language is made of: its **words** — the reserved commands the language allows; its **symbols** — the signs for operations and values; and its **rules** — the grammar that fixes how words and symbols may be joined into instructions the computer understands. Break the rules and the instruction is refused, exactly as a sentence with broken grammar fails between people. Every language met later in the book — and the Scratch blocks after — is examined against these same parts.
