# Repeating Actions — Do It Again

## Do it first

Clap this pattern: clap, clap, stamp — clap, clap, stamp. You did not invent six new actions; you did three actions twice. Finding the part that repeats is this lesson's whole skill.

## Repeating Actions — Do It Again

Paths grow longer, and inside the long paths the book points at something new: **repeating actions**. The hero walks the same stretch twice, turns the same corner again, and the row of blocks starts to look like a song with a chorus — a part that comes back. This lesson teaches the eye to find that part: read a long row of actions, mark where a group of actions ends and begins again, and say how many times the group repeats. Nothing new is run yet — the loop itself is the next lesson — but every loop ever written starts with this noticing: *these actions repeat, this many times*.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Some actions in a path repeat: the same move comes again and again, and noticing the repeating part is the step before loops. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: find the repeating part inside a long row of actions. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: say how many times the repeating part comes. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: shorten a written row of actions by naming its repeating part. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
