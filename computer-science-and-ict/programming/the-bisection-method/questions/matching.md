# The Bisection Method — matching

Match each item on the left with the one it belongs to on the right.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What does bisection do at each step?** → **Halves the bracket and keeps the half whose ends still change sign**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) How is the kept half decided?** → **By the sign of f at the midpoint — f(a)·f(c) < 0 keeps [a, c]**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) Is the root itself ever computed?** → **No — it is cornered; a cage-point is reported with the cage's width as error bound**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What starts the method?** → **A bracket from separation — f of opposite signs at the ends**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) What is reported at the end?** → **Any point of the final bracket (say its midpoint), with the bracket's width as the error**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What is the bracket's width after n steps?** → **(b − a) / 2ⁿ**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) How many steps squeeze [0, 1] below 10⁻³?** → **10 — because 2¹⁰ = 1024**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What can bisection never do?** → **Run away, divide by a slope, or be fooled by the function's shape**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) What is its price?** → **A fixed rate — one binary digit of precision per step**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) What is bisection, in the chapter's verdict?** → **The benchmark of honesty — certainty first, speed as a luxury**
