# Developing a Program Step by Step

## Do it first

A program is not typed in one rush from a blank screen. Malaysia's Grade 7 book teaches program development as a staircase of steps, climbed in order. In this lesson you learn the staircase — and why skipping steps is what makes programs fail.

## Sub lesson 1 — Understand and plan before building

Development opens away from the keyboard: **understand the problem** — what the program must do, what goes in and what must come out — then **plan the steps** (the algorithm, in pseudocode or flowchart) that turn the input into the output. Only then is the program **built** — written in the language or blocks the project uses, following the plan. Malaysia's order is the discipline: a program built from a plan can be checked against the plan; a program built from a guess can only be checked against another guess.

In your mind's eye: understand and plan before building.

## Sub lesson 2 — Test, fix, and test again

A built program is **tested**: run with known inputs whose right outputs are already worked out by hand, and the program's outputs compared. A mismatch is a **fault found**, not a failure of the whole work — the fault is located, **fixed**, and the program tested again, including the tests that passed before, because a fix can break a neighbour. Development ends when the tests pass and the program does the understood problem's job — the staircase's top is the problem you started with, solved.

In your mind's eye: test, fix, and test again.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: Understand the problem, plan the steps, build the program, test it, fix what the tests expose. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: What the program must do — what goes in and what must come out. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: Pseudocode or flowchart — the algorithm. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: Run with known inputs whose right outputs are worked out by hand, and compare. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
