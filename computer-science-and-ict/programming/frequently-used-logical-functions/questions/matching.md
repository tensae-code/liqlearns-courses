# Frequently Used Logical Functions — matching

Match each item on the left with the one it belongs to on the right.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What is NAND?** → **NOT-AND — 0 only when both inputs are 1**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What is NOR?** → **NOT-OR — 1 only when both inputs are 0**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What does 'universal' mean for NAND and NOR?** → **Each alone can build NOT, AND and OR — whole circuits from one gate kind**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) How is NAND's truth table written?** → **Take AND's table and flip every output**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) Why are real chips often NAND chips?** → **One universal gate kind simplifies manufacture**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) When does XOR give 1?** → **When the inputs differ**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What is XNOR?** → **XOR negated — 1 when the inputs agree**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) Where does XOR work inside arithmetic?** → **As the sum bit of an adder (carry aside)**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) What is XNOR used for?** → **Testing equality of bits**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) How is any of the four proved?** → **By its four-row truth table**
