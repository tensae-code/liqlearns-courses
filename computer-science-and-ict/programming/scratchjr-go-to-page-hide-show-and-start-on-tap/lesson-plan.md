**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Growing a ScratchJr story across pages — Go to Page, hiding and showing sprites, and Start on Tap

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Go to Page — a story in rooms, and Hide–Show** — **Go to Page** moves the story to another **page** of the project — ScratchJr's scenes: the sprite's script sends the reader to the next place, and the new page's sprites take up the tale. With it come **Hide and Show**: a sprite can be **hidden** — present but invisible — and **shown** again by a block, which is how surprises, entrances and disappearances are made.
2. **Start on Tap — the reader's finger as a trigger** — **Start on Tap** starts a script when the reader **taps that sprite**. The story stops being only watched and becomes handled: tap the door and it opens; tap the animal and it calls. Kuwait places tap beside pages and hiding because together they make the book interactive — the reader moves the story by moving between pages and touching the cast.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
