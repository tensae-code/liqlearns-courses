# ScratchJr — Go to Page, Hide–Show and Start on Tap

## Do it first

A story longer than one screen needs rooms. Kuwait's third ScratchJr lesson gives the story pages to move between, sprites that can vanish and return, and a trigger the reader's own finger fires. This lesson is those three powers.

## Sub lesson 1 — Go to Page — a story in rooms, and Hide–Show

**Go to Page** moves the story to another **page** of the project — ScratchJr's scenes: the sprite's script sends the reader to the next place, and the new page's sprites take up the tale. With it come **Hide and Show**: a sprite can be **hidden** — present but invisible — and **shown** again by a block, which is how surprises, entrances and disappearances are made.

In your mind's eye: go to Page — a story in rooms, and Hide–Show.

## Sub lesson 2 — Start on Tap — the reader's finger as a trigger

**Start on Tap** starts a script when the reader **taps that sprite**. The story stops being only watched and becomes handled: tap the door and it opens; tap the animal and it calls. Kuwait places tap beside pages and hiding because together they make the book interactive — the reader moves the story by moving between pages and touching the cast.

In your mind's eye: start on Tap — the reader's finger as a trigger.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: Moves the story to another page (scene) of the project. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: One scene or room of the story. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: Make a sprite invisible, then visible again. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: No — present but invisible. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
