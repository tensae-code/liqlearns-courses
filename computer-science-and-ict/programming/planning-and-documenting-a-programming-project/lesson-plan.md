**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Programming · **Lesson:** Planning and Documenting a Programming Project
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Kuwait (2026-11-14) — in the title Programming on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Kuwait's student books (Ministry of Education, State of Kuwait), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree and live lesson content were checked for the slug and for near matches before building.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Kuwait's series project-method lesson, taught in every year from Grade 6 to Grade 12 — the project's goal and skill areas, the team and its shared tasks, the weekly plan or timetable of stages, and scientific documentation with references (APA style at the senior grades)
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The goal, the skills and the team — what the project is and who does what** — Every Kuwait project begins by naming its **goal** — the product to be made, in words a stranger could judge — and the **skill areas** it will use, so the team knows what it must already have or go and learn. The work is then **shared**: team roles and tasks are divided by name, so each part of the product has an owner, and the **teamwork skills** — agreeing, reporting, helping across roles — are taught as part of the method, not hoped for.
2. **The weekly plan — and documenting the project with references** — The project runs on a **plan**: a weekly plan or timetable of stages that says what will exist by when, so lateness is visible while it can still be repaired. And it ends in **documentation**: a scientific record of what was made and how, with **references** to the sources used — in APA style at the senior grades — because a product whose making cannot be shown cannot be trusted, taught from, or built on. Plan the work, share the work, time the work, document the work: the series' whole method in four verbs.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
