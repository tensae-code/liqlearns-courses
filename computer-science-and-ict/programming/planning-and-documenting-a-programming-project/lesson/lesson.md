# Planning and Documenting a Programming Project

## Do it first

One lesson in Kuwait's series is taught again and again, in every year and beside every kind of product — a Scratch map, a design, a 3D model, a website, a Python product, a database project, a Visual Basic program. It is the project method itself. This lesson gathers the series' eight teachings of it into one: how a programming project is planned, shared, timed and documented.

## Sub lesson 1 — The goal, the skills and the team — what the project is and who does what

Every Kuwait project begins by naming its **goal** — the product to be made, in words a stranger could judge — and the **skill areas** it will use, so the team knows what it must already have or go and learn. The work is then **shared**: team roles and tasks are divided by name, so each part of the product has an owner, and the **teamwork skills** — agreeing, reporting, helping across roles — are taught as part of the method, not hoped for.

## Sub lesson 2 — The weekly plan — and documenting the project with references

The project runs on a **plan**: a weekly plan or timetable of stages that says what will exist by when, so lateness is visible while it can still be repaired. And it ends in **documentation**: a scientific record of what was made and how, with **references** to the sources used — in APA style at the senior grades — because a product whose making cannot be shown cannot be trusted, taught from, or built on. Plan the work, share the work, time the work, document the work: the series' whole method in four verbs.
