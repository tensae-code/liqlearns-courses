# Drawing with Loops — and Jump Instructions — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What is a loop's natural drawing work?**
**Answer: A drawing with a repeating part — a row of houses, a fence, a pattern**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What decides how long a pattern runs?**
**Answer: The loop's count**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What does changing the count change?**
**Answer: The length of the pattern — the same program draws more or fewer repeats**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What two things does a loop separate?**
**Answer: What repeats, and how many times**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) Name a repeating pattern from the lesson.**
**Answer: Fence planks (also: window rows, roof tiles, dotted borders)**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What is a jump instruction?**
**Answer: A move without drawing — the pen lifted, no line made**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) Why is the jump needed?**
**Answer: Without it every move drags a line and separate drawings smear together**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) How is a dotted line drawn with loops and jumps?**
**Answer: Draw, jump, draw, jump — repeated in a loop**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) What is the jump compared to in the lesson?**
**Answer: The silence between words**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) Does the jump appear in the finished drawing?**
**Answer: No — it makes nothing, on purpose; only its effect (the gap) shows**
