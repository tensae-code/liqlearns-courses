Switzerland's own way (Grades 1–2, Décodage 1re–2e): Vaud's Romandie digital-education curriculum is taught in three axes — Science informatique (SI), Médias (M), Usages (U) — almost entirely UNPLUGGED at this age: algorithms are learned with the body (robot game, crane game, hand-washing, getting dressed, a crêpe recipe), a physical sorting machine, and the Blue-Bot floor robot; the class also builds a collective digital “charter book” of good digital habits across the whole cycle.

Switzerland's own way (Grades 3–4, Décodage 3e–4e): the 3e–4e volume repeats the 1re–2e unplugged activities at a higher level (loops and debugging join the robot and crane games and the sorting machine) and adds three new strands: Thymio robotics, a full ScratchJr scenario, and media “parcours” — Parcours des bestioles (how images influence emotions), Parcours du partage (image rights, digital traces, thinking before sharing) and Parcours de la pub (recognising and creating advertising). Pixel paravent introduces binary coding as transmitting a black-and-white image across a screen with an agreed code; Deviner la carte teaches the conditional through a magic trick and its logigramme.

Switzerland's own way (Grades 5–6, Décodage 5e–6e): Cycle 2 moves from unplugged games to real tools while keeping the game frame: the imaginary city “Square CT” teaches classification, decision trees, conditionals, indentation and flowcharts (logigrammes); ScratchJr tells emotion and climate stories; the Swiss Thymio robot is programmed in VPL; data is taught through binary pixel coding and colour coding; and “Enquêtes” (inquiries) teach addition-as-algorithm, debugging, sorting, the Nim winning strategy, Caesar ciphers and sensor experiments. Médias/Usages scenarios cover cyberbullying, screen practices, news and media stereotypes.

Switzerland's own way (Grades 7–8, Décodage 7e–8e): at 7e–8e the Décodage course becomes a real computer-science course under the Plan d'études romand EN 22/23: Thymio VPL missions (including Morse communication and self-driving-car autonomy levels), a full Scratch discovery course (movement, shortening programs, sub-programs as own blocks, remixing), a Scratch video-game project run in “studios” with a cahier des charges, data work (Huffman-style compression via Zip la marmotte, binary/octal/hex/ASCII codings, file sizes), access security (passwords, file encryption, phishing, authentication), home networks at two levels (IP addresses; message routing, packets, TCP and encryption), and inquiries into sorting, sorting networks, Caesar decryption and parity-bit error detection. Médias/Usages covers social networks and digital identity/footprint, information sources, cyberbullying and the environmental cost of digital technology.

Vietnam's own way (Grade 7 Tin học 7): Grade 7 is the spreadsheet year (five Excel lessons in a row, in Windows/Excel/PowerPoint named openly) and its computer-science strand is pure algorithms with no code at all — sequential search, binary search and sorting acted out and flowcharted before any language appears.

Vietnam's own way (Grade 11 Tin học 11 Khoa học máy tính): from Grade 11 the two orientations are separate books but share a common core (Bài 1–16 here: operating systems, inside the computer, and a full databases strand ending at SQL); the CS-only second half is algorithm engineering in Python — arrays, search and sort, time complexity, stepwise refinement, modular design and building code libraries.


Kazakhstan's own way (Salgaraeva G10 Informatika, natural-science track): in Grade 10 the subject splits by track; this natural-science book is the deepest in the set — networks with IP, domains and VPN, number systems and logic with truth tables and the computer's logical elements, procedures/functions, strings, files, sorting methods and graph algorithms, a full HTML/CSS/scripts web part, and information systems from Big Data to linking web pages with a database. Every topic is doubled with a practicum paragraph.

Kazakhstan's own way (Zhunusov G10 Computer Science, English-medium): the English-medium Grade 10 book is the most technical of the wave: the CIA triad and three separate authentication lessons (passwords twice, biometrics), encoding standards, Python dictionaries, DFS/BFS graph traversal, and a full SQL chapter in MySQL (CREATE, INSERT, SELECT/WHERE, MIN/MAX, JOIN, GROUP BY, SELECT INTO) before a JavaScript chapter. (Its preface is the publisher's generic science template, left over from another book.)

Kazakhstan's own way (Kolyeva G9 Informatika): the Mektep Grade 9 book is the Google-cloud book: joint work on documents and presentations in Google services with shared-access settings and network etiquette, a practical chapter on choosing and pricing a computer and its software, databases built inside spreadsheets, then Python lists (search, replace, sort, insert/delete, two-dimensional) and 2D games with PyGame.

Kazakhstan's own way (Yerzhanov G9 Informatics, English-medium): the English-medium twin of the Mektep Grade 9 book — the same four chapters (computer literacy with netiquette and pricing a computer, database basics with pivot tables and a "family shop" mini-project, Python lists, PyGame 2D games ending in a Program Arcade Game) taught in English with trilingual terminology.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukhametzhanova G9 Informatika): the Atamura Grade 9 book covers the same ground as the Mektep book (information properties and joint document work, choosing a computer, databases, Python lists, PyGame) but in longer, deeper lessons — each topic runs seven to ten pages instead of two to four.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G9 Informatika): Almatykitap's Grade 9 book splits sorting and filtering into separate lessons plus an advanced-filter lesson, gives data input/output in one-dimensional arrays its own lesson, and has the fullest PyGame part of the wave — seven lessons ending in a complete "Footballer" game with sprite-collision detection.
