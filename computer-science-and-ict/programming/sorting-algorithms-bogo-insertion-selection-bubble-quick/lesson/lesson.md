# Sorting Algorithms — Bogo, Insertion, Selection, Bubble, Quick

## Do it first

Shuffle eight numbered cards. Sort them. Shuffle again — sort them a completely different way, with different hands doing different work. Before reading on, count how many genuinely different sorting methods your class can invent in two minutes.

## Sub lesson 1 — Many ways to sort

The annexes' scandal and gift is plurality: **there are several ways to sort (il existe plusieurs façons de trier)**. **Stupid sort (tri stupide / bogo)** shuffles at random and checks — a joke that teaches by absurdity. **Insertion sort** grows a sorted hand card by card, slotting each new card into place. **Selection sort** repeatedly picks the smallest remaining. **Bubble sort** compares neighbours and swaps, pass after pass. **Quick sort** partitions around a pivot and conquers each side. Five methods, one job, wildly different costs.

## Sub lesson 2 — Networks and the question of ‘best’

The annexes add **sorting networks (réseaux de tri)**: fixed rows of comparators that sort in parallel, whatever the input — a machine made of comparisons. With plurality comes the inquiry's question — **which way is best?** — whose honest answer is “it depends”: on the number of items, how scrambled they already are, and what a comparison costs. Meeting five sorts early vaccinates against the beginner's superstition that every problem has exactly one method with exactly one shape.
