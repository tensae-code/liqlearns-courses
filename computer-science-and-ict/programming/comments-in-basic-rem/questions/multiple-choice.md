# Comments in BASIC — REM — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What does REM stand for?**
a) Remark  b) A comment — a note to the program's human readers  c) The people — the programmer later, or another programmer reading the program  d) Why — not a repetition of what the line does
**Answer: a) Remark**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What is a REM line?**
a) Ignores them completely — they change, print and compute nothing  b) A comment — a note to the program's human readers  c) Because a program nobody can read is a program nobody can fix  d) At the head of the program and before any part whose purpose is not obvious
**Answer: b) A comment — a note to the program's human readers**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What does the computer do with REM lines?**
a) The people — the programmer later, or another programmer reading the program  b) Why — not a repetition of what the line does  c) Ignores them completely — they change, print and compute nothing  d) The program's name, its job and its writer
**Answer: c) Ignores them completely — they change, print and compute nothing**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) Who are comments for?**
a) Because a program nobody can read is a program nobody can fix  b) At the head of the program and before any part whose purpose is not obvious  c) Comment the thinking, not every line  d) The people — the programmer later, or another programmer reading the program
**Answer: d) The people — the programmer later, or another programmer reading the program**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) Why does the book give comments their own section?**
a) Because a program nobody can read is a program nobody can fix  b) Why — not a repetition of what the line does  c) The program's name, its job and its writer  d) It hides its own code
**Answer: a) Because a program nobody can read is a program nobody can fix**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What should a good comment say?**
a) At the head of the program and before any part whose purpose is not obvious  b) Why — not a repetition of what the line does  c) Comment the thinking, not every line  d) Remark
**Answer: b) Why — not a repetition of what the line does**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) Where do comments belong?**
a) The program's name, its job and its writer  b) It hides its own code  c) At the head of the program and before any part whose purpose is not obvious  d) A comment — a note to the program's human readers
**Answer: c) At the head of the program and before any part whose purpose is not obvious**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What goes in the head comment?**
a) Comment the thinking, not every line  b) Remark  c) Ignores them completely — they change, print and compute nothing  d) The program's name, its job and its writer
**Answer: d) The program's name, its job and its writer**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) What is the rule of proportion?**
a) Comment the thinking, not every line  b) It hides its own code  c) A comment — a note to the program's human readers  d) The people — the programmer later, or another programmer reading the program
**Answer: a) Comment the thinking, not every line**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) What does a program drowned in remarks do?**
a) Remark  b) It hides its own code  c) Ignores them completely — they change, print and compute nothing  d) Because a program nobody can read is a program nobody can fix
