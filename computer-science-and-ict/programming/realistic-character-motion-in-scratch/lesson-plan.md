## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Speed that changes — acceleration and easing** — Real movement **starts slow, hurries, and settles**: the unit builds that feel by keeping the sprite's speed in a **variable** and **changing the speed a little each step** — a small addition while a key is held (**acceleration**), a small subtraction when it is released (**slowing to rest**). The same blocks that moved the sprite before now move it by a *changing* amount, and the difference is visible at once: the character leans into its journey instead of teleporting politely along it.
2. **Falling and turning — gravity's feel in blocks** — The unit's second believability is **falling**: a jumping or dropped character's downward speed **grows** each step (gravity adds and adds) until it lands, where the growth must stop — a landing check is the difference between a jump and a disappearance through the floor. Turning likewise gains life when the **costume and direction answer the movement** (face the way you travel; change costume as you run). The lesson's rule collects them: believable motion is ordinary motion blocks **plus variables for the physics the eye expects**.

1. **Do it first** — A sprite that glides at one speed in a straight line looks like a sticker, not a creature. Grade 8's motion lesson teaches the tricks that make movement believable — change, not constancy, is what eyes believe. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
