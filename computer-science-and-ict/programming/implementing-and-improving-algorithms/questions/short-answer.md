# Implementing and Improving Algorithms — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What is implementing?**
**Answer: Expressing an algorithm in an executable form without changing its logic**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) Against what is an implementation tested?**
**Answer: The algorithm — same inputs, same outputs, every step accounted**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) Name three translation faults.**
**Answer: A flipped condition, a dropped step, a moved loop end**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) How are translation faults caught?**
**Answer: Tracking the implementation against the algorithm, row by row**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) What may implementation not change?**
**Answer: The algorithm's logic**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) When is an algorithm improved?**
**Answer: Same answer with less — fewer steps, less memory, clearer working**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) With what does improvement begin?**
**Answer: Counting — steps for this size, and how the count grows**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) Name three wastes to hunt.**
**Answer: Repeated steps, searches that could stop early, work for impossible cases**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) How must an improvement be proved?**
**Answer: Tracked and tested on the same cases**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) What has a 'faster' algorithm that answers differently done?**
**Answer: Changed the destination, not improved the road**
