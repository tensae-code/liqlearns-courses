Provenance scope note: the placements stocked here are from Kazakhstan's okulyk.kz (OKULYK) textbooks, recommended by the Ministry of Education and Science of the Republic of Kazakhstan — the Digital Literacy and Informatika lines of the Almatykitap, Arman-PV and Atamura teams and the Nazarbayev Intellectual Schools (NIS) books, all student books except the two Kadirkulov teacher guides named in the entries below, whose placements are stocked exactly as filed. Many books exist in parallel language editions (Kazakh / Russian / Uzbek / Uyghur / English) of the same textbook: each distinct book is taught once, and every distinct edition is named and cited in the book entry. Kazakhstan's own ways are kept: one textbook line in up to five parallel language editions, the okulyk.kz digital library as the books' home, Scratch from Grade 1 and robotics strands (LEGO WeDo 2.0 and Mindstorms EV3) from Grade 1–2, and every keyword given in Kazakh–Russian–English in the early grades. Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G8 Informatika, Lazarus edition): Grade 8 by the Almatykitap team measures information two ways (the probabilistic and alphabetic approaches, with Hartley-formula problems), works Excel hard (absolute/relative references, built-in functions, data analysis, applied problems), and teaches programming in the Lazarus IDE — case, for, nested, repeat-until and while operators, tracing, and procedures and functions — before a final part that runs the whole problem-solving cycle from statement to tested program.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G8 Informatika, Python edition): the newer, shorter Grade 8 edition from the same team keeps the measurement, health and Excel parts but replaces the Lazarus IDE with Python: for and while loops, nested loops, continue, break and loop-else, algorithm tracing, and a closing part on practical programming that ends with olympiad-style tasks.

Kazakhstan's own way (Salgaraeva G8 Informatika, 2018 edition): the Arman-PV Grade 8 book runs the shared national frame in 34 numbered paragraphs with a test after every part: two paragraphs split the processor from its characteristics, the Excel part goes furthest of the three publishers (data formats, text and logical functions, plotting and restyling function graphs), and the Lazarus part adds algorithm tracing and a practicum on typical programming errors.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G7 Informatika): Grade 7 by the Almatykitap team is measurement and memory first (units, file formats and file sizes), then networks with antivirus security and the user interface, a long spreadsheet part that ends in modelling processes in Excel, and Python taught through files and branching — nested and compound conditions included. Kazakhstan's own way (Salgaraeva G8 Informatika, updated edition): the updated-content revision of the Arman-PV Grade 8 book keeps the measurement, health and Excel parts of the 2018 edition (with a fuller glossary — digital reputation, worms, DoS/DDoS attacks, the Hartley formula) but replaces the Lazarus part with Python loops (while, for, continue, break, loop-else, tracing, typical errors) and ends with practical programming.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukhametzhanova G8 Informatika): the Atamura Grade 8 book is the leanest of the three publishers — five parts only, tasks ranked by Bloom's taxonomy, and a rich glossary that reaches from pivot tables, sparklines and data consolidation in Excel to botnets, phishing, cyberbullying and griefing in the safety part; Python loops include loop-else and trace tables.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukhametzhanova G7 Informatika, updated edition): the updated-content revision of the Atamura Grade 7 book keeps the 2017 skeleton (units, archives, networks, Excel, practical programming) but replaces the general programming part with Python: working with files, branching, nested and compound conditions and organising a choice; an appendix teaches building electronic learning games with a game constructor (Editor and Class versions).
