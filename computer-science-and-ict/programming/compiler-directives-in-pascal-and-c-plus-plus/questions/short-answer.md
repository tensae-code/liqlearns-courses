# Compiler Directives in Pascal and C++ — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What is a compiler directive?**
**Answer: An instruction to the compiler — changing how the program is built, executing nothing at run time**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) How does Pascal write directives?**
**Answer: As special comments — {$R+} and its kin**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What does {$R+} switch on?**
**Answer: Range checking**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) Over what text does a directive rule?**
**Answer: The text after it, until switched again**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) What changes if all directives are removed?**
**Answer: No algorithm — only the safety and shape of the building**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What runs before C++ compilation?**
**Answer: The preprocessor, driven by lines beginning with #**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What does #include do?**
**Answer: It inserts a whole file's text — a library's header**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What does #define do?**
**Answer: It names a text substitution used through the program**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) What do #ifdef and #endif do?**
**Answer: They include or exclude parts of the text by condition**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) What is #include <iostream>, in the lesson's phrase?**
**Answer: A directive — the program's first word is spoken to the builder**
