# My First Program — Guiding the Mouse Through a Maze

## Do it first

The mouse must reach the cheese, and the walls do not forgive a wrong turn. Moldova's Grade 1 book makes this the child's first program: not typing, but a row of arrows planned before a single step is taken.

## Sub lesson 1 — Arrow instructions — the program's words

The program is written in **arrows**: each arrow is one instruction — move one square forward in the arrow's direction. The arrows are laid **in a row, in order**, and the mouse will follow them exactly as laid: first the first, then the second, to the last. Writing the program means **planning the whole road before moving**: look at the maze, find the path from start to cheese, and only then lay the arrows. A program is thought first and run second.

## Sub lesson 2 — Running the program — and the guided stages

To **run** the program, the mouse walks it: read the first arrow, move; read the next, move — until the last arrow or a wall stops the walk. The book then moves the same skill onto the screen in **guided online stages**: the same arrow programs, slightly harder mazes, each stage checking the walk at once. Whether on paper or on screen, the discipline is one: plan, write, run, and when the mouse hits a wall, the program — not the mouse — is wrong.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: One instruction — move one square in the arrow's direction. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: In a row, in order — first, second, to the last. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: Before moving — the whole path is found first. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: It follows the arrows exactly as laid. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
