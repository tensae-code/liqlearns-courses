# Searching an Array — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) How does linear search look?**
**Answer: It walks the array in order, testing each element against the wanted value**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What is the found-index variable?**
**Answer: The variable reporting where the value was found — or the agreed 'nothing found' sign**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What are the search's two duties?**
**Answer: To report the place, or to report absence — both are knowledge**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What is wrong with the first version?**
**Answer: It walks on after success, finishing the array out of habit**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) What is the improvement?**
**Answer: Stop at the first match — end the walk when the found-index is set**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What saving does stopping make?**
**Answer: On average, half the array unwalked**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What is the larger lesson of the improvement?**
**Answer: A correct first version is a floor, not a ceiling — algorithms are improvable**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What questions do the book's programs ask the array?**
**Answer: Is this present; where does this stand**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) How do the two array lessons pair?**
**Answer: Sorting makes the array orderly; searching makes it answerable**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) In what spirit are the methods taught?**
**Answer: To be understood, traced and improved — not memorised**
