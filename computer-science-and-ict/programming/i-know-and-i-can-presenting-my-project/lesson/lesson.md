# I Know and I Can — Presenting My Project

## Do it first

Stand beside your best project and imagine the class asking: "How does this part work?" For every part you can answer, the year is proved. Any part you cannot explain is the last gap to close.

## I Know and I Can — Presenting My Project

Grade 4 closes with **"Знам и мога. Представям проект" (I know and I can: I present my project)** — the mastery check in its final form: not a test page, but a **presentation**. The learner **presents a finished project** — the computer game or the Journey through Bulgaria — running it for an audience, **explaining how it works** part by part (control, variables, decisions, synchronising, backdrops), and **answering questions** about it. Presenting is a stricter proof than building: a part you merely copied cannot be explained, so the presentation shows honestly what is truly known. The lesson teaches the presenting craft itself: start from the plan, run the project slowly enough to follow, name the skills inside it, and finish by saying what you would improve next.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: The year ends with "Знам и мога. Представям проект" — the mastery check as a presentation: run your project for an audience, explain how it works, and answer for every part of it. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: present a finished project to an audience, running it so they can follow. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: explain how each part of the project works, naming the skills inside it. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: answer questions about the project and name what you would improve next. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
