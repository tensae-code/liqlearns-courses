# Making Your Own Blocks in Scratch

## Do it first

You have drawn the same square eleven times. Scratch lets you name that stack once — “square” — with a hole for the side’s size, and then use your own block like any block Scratch came with. Grade 9’s additional-blocks lesson is the course’s graduation in tool-making.

## Sub lesson 1 — Defining your own block — “square” and “sum”

**Defining your own block** means naming a stack and keeping it as a block: the book defines **“square”** and **“sum”**, each **with number inputs**, so the same block draws any size of square or adds any pair. Once defined, your block is **used like any built-in block**.

## Sub lesson 2 — A custom block with inputs — draw the triangle

The triangle build shows why inputs matter: a custom **“draw the triangle” block taking the two sides as inputs** draws any right-angled triangle the main program names — the checking of Pythagoras happens in the lesson on the if statement, and the drawing lives inside the custom block, called with each new pair of sides.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Your own block is defined once — a name, and number inputs if it needs them — and then used like any built-in block: the book defines “square” and “sum”, and a “draw the triangle” block taking the two sides as inputs. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
