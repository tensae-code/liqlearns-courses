**Acceptance count: taught in 4 countries (Palestine, Bhutan, Kuwait, Greece).**

Placement choice: this lesson was built new for the ICT expansion (Palestine, 2026-11-14) because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree was checked for the slug and for near matches before building. The placements file named this slug directly (NEW LESSON NEEDED — programming/making-your-own-blocks-in-scratch). It sits in the Programming title (programming/), following the book's own order.

Palestine’s own way (Grade 9, Programming): Grade 9 is a full applications course in Scratch — after a basics unit it builds real things: a photo editor, a video player, a phone PIN lock, a bank account, a library sign-up/login, an alarm clock, an elevator, a task list, a wall clock that can ring at prayer times. Its own workarounds are content too: Scratch 2 cannot type Arabic in its paint editor, so Arabic text is written in MS Paint, saved as a picture and uploaded; the news-ticker lesson’s breaking-news example is the Ministry’s own decision to teach programming in Grades 5–9; the letter-counting lesson’s example sentence says the Dome of the Rock stands in Jerusalem.

Bhutan's own way (Class VIII, Literacy with ICT student book): The final primary/middle year is project-led — a full Audacity audio unit (record, edit, mix, share), information ethics taught through Copyright, Creative Commons and software piracy, and Scratch taught to game level with lists, custom blocks (functions) and logical operators.

Kuwait's own way (Grade 6 Digital Intelligence Term 1): the first intermediate year is entirely creative computing — a full advanced-Scratch ladder (sensing, variables, broadcasting, lists, cloning, custom blocks, AI extensions) framed by Kuwaiti themes (volunteer work, famous mosques, the governorates), followed by a complete virtual-robotics unit in Open Roberta Lab that ends in a Champions Challenge.

Greece's own way (Grade 8, Pliroforiki B' Gymnasiou, 2024): Grade 8 moves from bits/bytes and data rates into networks, then data analysis in spreadsheets and presentations, then modular Scratch programming (own blocks with parameters, realistic character motion), list data structures, a full AI unit training a neural network, and ICT's changes to the economy (e-commerce, Industry 4.0, new jobs, sustainability).

Switzerland's own way (Grades 7–8, Décodage 7e–8e): at 7e–8e the Décodage course becomes a real computer-science course under the Plan d'études romand EN 22/23: Thymio VPL missions (including Morse communication and self-driving-car autonomy levels), a full Scratch discovery course (movement, shortening programs, sub-programs as own blocks, remixing), a Scratch video-game project run in “studios” with a cahier des charges, data work (Huffman-style compression via Zip la marmotte, binary/octal/hex/ASCII codings, file sizes), access security (passwords, file encryption, phishing, authentication), home networks at two levels (IP addresses; message routing, packets, TCP and encryption), and inquiries into sorting, sorting networks, Caesar decryption and parity-bit error detection. Médias/Usages covers social networks and digital identity/footprint, information sources, cyberbullying and the environmental cost of digital technology.
