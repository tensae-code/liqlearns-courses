# Lesson plan — What makes an algorithm good — six characteristics

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Programming · **Lesson:** What makes an algorithm good — six characteristics
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 6 — Fundamentals of Programming (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** an algorithm must be clear and unambiguous, well-defined in inputs and outputs, finite, feasible and language-independent — and even a good one costs time to write and shows branching and looping only with difficulty.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on What an algorithm is (the Grade 10 growth in this title) — the learner can already say what an algorithm is. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or in steps — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: What an algorithm is (the Grade 10 growth of From Polya to programs — the algorithm). Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. List the six characteristics of an algorithm.
2. Say the advantages: easy to understand, stepwise, breaks problems into pieces.
3. Say the disadvantages: slow to write; branching and looping hard to show.
4. Test a new instruction set against the six and name its failures.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Counts 'finite' as 'short' → finite means it ends at all; a long algorithm that ends passes, a short one that loops forever fails.
- Thinks language-independent means 'no language' → it means not tied to ONE language; the same algorithm works in any.
