**Acceptance count: taught in 14 countries (Ethiopia, China, Sudan, Uzbekistan, Malaysia, Mongolia, Greece, Moldova, Bangladesh, Jordan, Armenia, Indonesia, South Sudan, Pakistan).**

Book error taught corrected: the student book prints “fineness” for this characteristic (printed p. 210, in the worked-algorithms summary of the same list) — the word meant is **finiteness**, and the lesson teaches finiteness. The unit's outcomes header also spells the unit “FUNDAMENTALS OF PROGRAMING” with one M (a misprint); the lessons use the correct spelling, Fundamentals of Programming.

Sudan's own way (Grade 9): the whole Grade 9 book is programming — concepts and Pascal-style examples first, then algorithms and flowcharts, then a full Scratch course ending in a designed project. The Bakht Al-Ruda ICT series runs in the same pattern from Grade 4 to Grade 9.


Uzbekistan's own way (Grade 9): grade 9 is the capstone — computer graphics, Access, Excel, then a nine-lesson algorithms block and a 28-lesson Turbo Pascal 7.0 course that runs from the alphabet of the language to files, procedures and graphics mode, closing with the Internet. Every chapter ends with a dedicated repetition lesson, placed here to that chapter's main lesson.

Malaysia's own way (Grade 10, SK Tingkatan 4): the upper-secondary Sains Komputer (SK — Computer Science) elective switches language to Java (NetBeans) and is organised as three big project-style chapters — Programming, Relational Databases (Access, ERD, normalisation to 3NF, macros/switchboard, a full SDLC database project), and Human-Computer Interaction with screen-design prototyping and quantitative evaluation (prototypes built in Java NetBeans).

Mongolia's own way (Grade 11): a deep, engineering-style systems book (CPU registers, disk access-time calculations) that turns practical fast — pupils price up a computer from Mongolian web shops (pc-mall.mn, bsb.mn, itzone.mn); the web chapter builds a real "Our School" site and an "Animals of the Mongolian Red Book" page; programming is Microsoft Small Basic, and its loops-and-graphics work draws Mongolian ornament — the alkhan khee meander, the khaan buguivch knot and the ulzii endless knot — with code; the book closes by comparing every Small Basic construct with Python and C side by side.

Mongolia's own way (Grade 12): the final book measures everything against Mongolia itself — the ITU index and Mongolia's slipping rank in it, 36,760 km of fibre to 299 soums, the Mongolsat-1 satellite, IoT chips on livestock, telemedicine at the State Central Hospital, the ХУР state data-exchange and ТҮЦ service machines, nano-products from horse bone and ger covers — and its chapter projects are real Mongolian systems: a school ICT-development survey, a study of the ebarimt VAT-receipt system, and a Small Basic "Bouncing Ball" game; programming examples draw the alkhan khee meander with a Turtle subprogram.

Greece's own way (Grade 11, Eisagogi stis Arches tis Epistimis ton H/Y B' Lykeiou): a theory-first introduction to computer science in three units — basic concepts (theoretical vs applied CS), theoretical CS (problem, algorithms in depth including complexity/computability, data structures operations, debugging logic errors and documentation, programming paradigms and structured programming, software life cycle), and applied CS (operating systems in depth, information systems with databases and SQL/XML, networks, AI).

Moldova's own way (Grade 8, Informatică): Grade 8 pairs a full office-grade Word course (styles, templates, mail merge, formulas) with algorithmic thinking taught formally — executors, subalgorithms, counted/conditional loops, branching — then implemented instruction by instruction in Pascal, closing with image editing in Paint 3D and GIMP.

Bangladesh's own way (Grade 6, Digital Technology): the Competency-based National Curriculum 2022 book is organised as nine project "Learning Experiences" done in groups through stories, games and fieldwork — no conventional chapters; even learners without a computer at home learn technology through experience around school and home. The NCTB Digital Technology line runs in parallel English and Bangla editions; placements are cited from the English Version with the Bangla Version parallel spot-checked.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 7 G7T2 Student): Citizenship comes before code in Term 2 — digital citizenship, intellectual property with the actual patent-application procedures, data privacy law, and balanced use of communication tools — then a pure algorithms unit with no computer needed (writing conditions, tracking, representation, flowcharts, improving algorithms).

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 5 G5 student (Antares)): One of two different Grade 5 books in the set (this one by Velijanyan & Ayvazyan, Antares); it runs as numbered § lessons mixing information theory, careful judgement of fake vs true information, and a long practical spine — text editor, spreadsheets, algorithms and variables — ending in teamwork principles.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 10 G10 student): The senior-school series (Stepanyan, Torosyan, Martirosyan, Amirkhanyan, Matinyan) is built on four ideas around digital tools and collaboration; Grades 10–12 switch the programming language to C++ and go deep: Boolean logic, translators, types, code quality, debugging, procedures, recursion, divide-and-conquer, testing, development stages, AI and data.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 10 G10 Informatika student): Senior-high Informatika opens with real data work — multi-variable search, data collection by Python web scraping in Google Colab (pandas/Plotly) — then algorithms chosen for real-life problems, office automation, IT careers/certifications, and information security with classical ciphers.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 10 G10 Informatika SMK Sem2): Semester 2 turns to algorithms in depth, the social side of informatics (history of IT, intellectual property, professions), and a full informatics project-management chapter — plan, monitor, evaluate.

South Sudan's own way (Grade 11 S3 Student): Year 3 is creation and programming: learners build and run a real personal website/blog end-to-end in WordPress (hosting, domain, themes, posts, legal issues and comment moderation), study social networks platform by platform (LinkedIn, Google+ and the Students Circle Network, with South Sudanese business use), then learn programming through data types, arrays and structures in C/C++ and writing programs in Notepad, closing on ethical conduct and intellectual property.


Pakistan's own way (Grade 7 Computer Science 7): Grade 7 opens with emerging technologies and a deep ports-and-peripherals hardware treatment, then teaches email all the way to DKIM/SPF/DMARC authentication and POP3/IMAP/SMTP protocols; binary data representation (ASCII/Unicode, RGB colour, sound sampling) is taught inside the Programming unit, and entrepreneurship becomes business-plan craft — promotion, value proposition, quality assurance, SEO and platform business models, with Twitter and Snapchat origin stories.
