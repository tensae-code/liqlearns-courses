# Lesson plan — ScratchJr — Sound and Message Blocks

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Programming · **Lesson:** ScratchJr — Sound and Message Blocks
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Kuwait (2026-11-14) — in the title Programming on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Kuwait's student books (Ministry of Education, State of Kuwait), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree and live lesson content were checked for the slug and for near matches before building.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Giving ScratchJr stories a voice — the Pop and recorded-sound blocks — and sprites that talk to each other with Send Message and Start on Message
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Sound blocks — Pop and your own recorded sound** — ScratchJr's sound blocks make a sprite **make a noise at the moment its script reaches the block**: the ready-made **Pop** for a quick effect, and **recorded sound** — the pupil's own voice or a real-world sound, captured with the device — for everything a library pop cannot say. Sound is timed by its place in the script, like every block: the pop lands where the block stands.
2. **Message blocks — Send Message and Start on Message** — A **message** is ScratchJr's signal between sprites: **Send Message** sends a coloured signal out, and **Start on Message** starts the script of any sprite waiting for that same colour. One sprite finishing its line can wake the next speaker — dialogue and turn-taking without the reader touching anything. Messages must match by colour: a script waiting on red hears nothing sent on blue.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
