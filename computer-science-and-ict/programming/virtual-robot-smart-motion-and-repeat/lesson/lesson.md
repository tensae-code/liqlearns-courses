# Virtual Robot — Smart Motion and Repeat

## Do it first

A car steers with a wheel the driver turns. Kuwait's virtual robot has no such wheel — yet it corners. The Grade 6 robotics unit's second lesson is that secret, and the repetition that makes motion programs short. This lesson is steering without a wheel, and Repeat.

## Sub lesson 1 — Steering without a steering wheel — two motors, two speeds

The virtual robot steers by its **two drive motors**: driving both at the same speed goes straight; driving one **slower** bends the path toward it; stopping one and driving the other **spins** the robot on the spot. There is no steering wheel because the steering is in the speeds — the 'secret of smart motion' the book's title names. A programmer steers by numbers: the pair of speeds is the corner's sharpness.

## Sub lesson 2 — Smart repetition — Repeat

Missions repeat their shapes — four sides, six laps, ten passes — and the **Repeat** block writes the repetition **once**: the motion blocks inside it run the counted number of times. Smart repetition is the square drawn as one side plus Repeat 4 instead of four copied sides: shorter to write, safer to change — mend the side once and every side mends. The lesson pairs the two teachings because missions need both: true steering, written briefly.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: By its two drive motors — different speeds bend the path. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: Both motors at the same speed. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: Driving one motor slower than the other. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: Stop one motor and drive the other. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
