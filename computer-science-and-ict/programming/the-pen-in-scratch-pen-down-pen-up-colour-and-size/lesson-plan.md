# Lesson plan — The Pen in Scratch — Pen Down, Pen Up, Colour and Size

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Programming · **Lesson:** The Pen in Scratch — Pen Down, Pen Up, Colour and Size
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Palestine (2026-11-14) — in the title Programming on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Palestine's Grade 6, Grade 7, Grade 8 and Grade 9 Programming student books, because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Scratch’s pen draws as the sprite moves: pen down starts the line, pen up lifts it, clear wipes the stage — and the pen’s colour (200+ shades, or a number, or a colour touched on screen) and size are set by blocks.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Pen down, pen up, clear — and the pen’s colour and size** — The pen’s first blocks are three: **pen down** puts the pen on the stage so movement draws, **pen up** lifts it so movement is invisible, and **clear** wipes the drawing away. On top of these sit the settings: **pen colour**, chosen across **200+ shades** in Grade 6 and set **by number** or **by touching a colour on screen** in Grade 8, with **pen shade** light or dark, and **pen size** set and changed by blocks.
2. **The pen at work — ornaments, stairs, and free writing** — The books put the pen to four kinds of work: Grade 7 draws **lines and ornaments** (opening with the question of ornaments like Al-Aqsa Mosque’s), Grade 6 combines the pen with **movement, costumes and resizing**, Grade 8 draws **stairs and a house**, and Grade 9 writes **freehand on the stage** — a pencil sprite whose costume centre is its tip follows the mouse, pen down while the mouse is pressed, with a clear sprite as the board wiper and an eraser sprite in the poetry project.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
