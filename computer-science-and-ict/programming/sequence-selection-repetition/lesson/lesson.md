# Sequence, selection, repetition

## Do it first

Three dances. One goes step by step, straight to the end. One forks: if this, go left; if that, go right. One circles back and does it again. Watch the GIF circle — that circling is a whole third of how programs flow. Name which dance each of your morning routines dances.

## Sequence and selection

Sequence: instructions execute top-down in the order placed. Selection, or condition: instructions execute or jump depending on a decision's outcome — the umbrella question (opened outside, not inside), and the promotion question: a Grade 9 average under 50% blocks Grade 10. One path is taken; the other is not.

## Repetition

Repetition: instructions execute repeatedly — a fixed number of times, or until a condition occurs — as in adding a list of 100 numbers. No pupil writes a hundred additions; the loop writes one addition and goes round. These three — sequence, selection, repetition — are the whole grammar of flow: every flowchart and script in this unit is built from them.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: instruction flow comes in three kinds: sequence — in order; selection — depending on a decision; repetition — again and again, a fixed number of times or until a condition occurs. Then name the key words of this lesson — sequence, selection, repetition, the promotion example — and say what each one means.
