# The N-Queens Problem — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What must a queens placement avoid?**
**Answer: Any two queens sharing a row, a column, or a diagonal**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) Which small boards have no solution?**
**Answer: N = 2 and N = 3**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) How many solutions has the classic N = 8?**
**Answer: 92**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) Why does the book close the strand with this problem?**
**Answer: Every part of backtracking is visible in it — choices, conditions, stepping back**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) What does a queen attack along?**
**Answer: Her row, her column, and both diagonals**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What decision simplifies the search?**
**Answer: Place one queen per row, row by row — rows then can never clash**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What still needs checking at each placement?**
**Answer: The column and both diagonals**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What happens when a row has no safe column?**
**Answer: Backtrack — move the previous row's queen to her next column and descend again**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) When is a solution found?**
**Answer: When a full board stands — every row holding a safe queen**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) When is 'no solution' proven?**
**Answer: When row one's columns are exhausted**
