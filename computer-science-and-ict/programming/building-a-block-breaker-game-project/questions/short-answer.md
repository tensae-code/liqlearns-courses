# Building a Block Breaker Game — Project — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What are a block breaker's named parts?**
**Answer: A paddle, a ball, bricks, and the rules that bind them**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What do the rules cover?**
**Answer: Bounce angles, a score for bricks, and lives lost when the ball passes the paddle**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) Why does Egypt develop the game in two parts?**
**Answer: A game is built in working slices — each part ends running**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What does part one deliver?**
**Answer: The field, paddle and moving ball — the game exists**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) What does part two add?**
**Answer: Bricks, collisions, score and lives — the game is a game**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) Why fix faults in part one before building part two?**
**Answer: A fault under later work becomes an excavation instead of a lesson**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What must the summary do?**
**Answer: Say in order what was built, how each part works, and what the hard problems were and how they fell**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What happens in the development presentation?**
**Answer: The game is demonstrated live and its choices explained and defended before an audience**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) Why can copied code not pass the summary?**
**Answer: The summary tests understanding of the makers' own build, not the code's existence**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) What standard does Egypt set for AI-assisted projects?**
**Answer: The makers must be able to explain, summarise and present the work as their own**
