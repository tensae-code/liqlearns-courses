# The Alphabet, Vocabulary and Keywords of Pascal and C++

## Do it first

Grade 10's formal course inventories the languages' raw material. Everything either language will ever say is built from a small alphabet and a fixed treasury of reserved words — here they are, side by side.

## Sub lesson 1 — Alphabet and special symbols — the raw material

A language's **alphabet** is the set of characters its texts may use: the Latin letters, the digits, and the **special symbols** — + − * / = < > ( ) [ ] { } ; : , . ' and their kin. Pascal and C++ share most of the alphabet; C++ adds symbols Pascal lacks (#, %, &, |, ~) for its own operations. **Separators and blanks** divide the symbols into words. Nothing outside the alphabet may appear in a program — a stray character is the compiler's first and simplest error.

## Sub lesson 2 — Keywords — the reserved treasury

A language's **vocabulary** is built from its alphabet, and at its centre stand the **keywords (reserved words)**: words with a fixed meaning in the language — Pascal's program, begin, end, var, if, while; C++'s int, return, if, while, class — which the programmer **may not use as identifiers**. Keywords are few (dozens, not thousands) and are the language's skeleton: every program text is keywords, identifiers and symbols woven together. Pascal is case-insensitive for them (BEGIN = begin); C++ is case-sensitive (Main is not main) — the pair's first practical difference.
