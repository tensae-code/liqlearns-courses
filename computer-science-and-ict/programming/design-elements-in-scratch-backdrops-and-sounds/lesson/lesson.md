# Design Elements in Scratch — Backdrops and Sounds

## Do it first

A Scratch project is not only code: it is a stage dressed and a world sounded. This lesson learns the two design elements that dress it — backdrops and sounds.

## Sub lesson 1 — Backdrops — the stage's clothing

A **backdrop** is the stage's background picture: chosen from Scratch's library, painted in its editor, uploaded, or taken by camera. A project may hold **many backdrops** and switch between them — by block, by event — so one stage becomes a street, then a room, then the moon. Backdrops are design with consequences: the sprite's colours must read against them, and a switch of backdrop is the cheapest scene-change in all of theatre.

In your mind's eye: backdrops — the stage's clothing.

## Sub lesson 2 — Sounds — the project's voice

A project holds **sounds** per sprite and for the stage: chosen from the library, **recorded** in Scratch, or uploaded — and played, looped, stopped and volumed by blocks. Sound is information as much as decoration: the click that confirms, the alarm that warns, the music that sets the scene's weather. Jordan's Grade 8 lesson teaches backdrops and sounds together as **design elements** because a project's quality is judged by eye and ear together — and because choosing them is the pupil's first work as a designer, not only a coder.

In your mind's eye: sounds — the project's voice.
