# Actions in Sequence — First, Then, Next

## Do it first

Think of washing your hands: first turn on the water, then wet your hands, then use soap, then rinse. Do them in the wrong order and it does not work. This lesson teaches sequences: actions in their right order.

## Actions in Sequence — First, Then, Next

A **sequence** is actions or events in an **order** — one after another. We say them with order words: **first**, **then**, **next**, and **last**.

Everyday life is full of sequences. Getting dressed: first underclothes, then shirt and trousers, then shoes — shoes first would be silly. Planting a seed: first dig, then put the seed in, then cover it, then water it. Each action waits for the one before it.

The order matters. Some actions can swap places and nothing breaks — but many cannot: you cannot rinse the soap before you put the soap on. When you tell a sequence, say every action, in its order, with nothing skipped. A missing step breaks the sequence just as a wrong order does.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Actions happen in a sequence — one after another in an order: first one action, then the next — and changing the order can break the result. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: say what a sequence is, using first, then, next and last.
2. Show it, don't just say it: put a jumbled set of everyday actions into their right order.
3. Show it, don't just say it: spot a missing step or a wrong order in a given sequence.
