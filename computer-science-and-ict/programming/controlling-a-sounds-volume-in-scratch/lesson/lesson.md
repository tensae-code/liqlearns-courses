# Controlling a Sound's Volume in Scratch

## Do it first

A sound in a project can be too loud for its moment or too soft to hear. Scratch hands the maker the volume itself. Grade 4's volume séance teaches setting how loud a sprite's sounds play.

## Sub lesson 1 — Volume — how loud, as a setting

**Volume** is **how loud a sound plays**, and in Scratch it is a **setting of the sprite**, given as a **percentage**: full volume is 100%, silence is 0%, and a quiet sound sits between. The volume belongs to the sprite whose sounds are playing — one sprite can be loud while another whispers in the same project. A sound played before any volume is set plays at the volume the sprite already has, so the setting is made **before the moment it must serve**.

[Picture — volume — how loud, as a setting]

## Sub lesson 2 — Setting and changing the volume

Two blocks do the work: **set volume** puts the loudness at an exact percentage, and **change volume** raises or lowers it **by an amount** from where it stands — the same set-and-change pair the course met with variables. With them a project can **fade**: lower the volume step by step until the music is gone, or raise a voice from a whisper as a character comes nearer. The blocks affect **the sounds the sprite plays after them**, in the order the script runs.
