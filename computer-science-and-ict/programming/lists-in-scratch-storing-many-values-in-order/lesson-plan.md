# Lesson plan — Lists in Scratch — Storing Many Values in Order

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Programming · **Lesson:** Lists in Scratch — Storing Many Values in Order
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Palestine (2026-11-14) — in the title Programming on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Palestine's Grade 9 Programming student book, because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A list is ordered data — every item with a position number: add, insert, item number of, replace, delete (deleting renumbers the rest), length of list, and contains. Parallel lists hold matched facts — questions/answers, names/numbers, tasks/dates — and counters loop over them.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The list and its operations** — **A list is ordered data, every item with a position number.** Its operations are the lesson’s kit: **add, insert, item number of, replace, delete** — **deleting renumbers the rest** — **length of list**, and **contains**. The first lists hold **names, fathers’ names and family names**, in order.
2. **Parallel lists at work — quiz, address book, budget, tasks** — The book’s builds pair lists up: a **quiz on three parallel lists** (questions, correct answers, the student’s answers) with **counters for right and wrong**; an **address book on six parallel lists** (name, mobile, city, home address, email, age) with **input checked as entered** — age 0–120, a mobile ten digits starting 05, asked again until valid — and **searching the names list**; **Amal’s expense tracking on two lists** (kind and price) totalled by **looping with a counter**, savings = income − total; and a **task list on two lists** (tasks and dates) with Add, Update, Delete-one and Delete-all buttons, updating first checking the position is inside the list.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
