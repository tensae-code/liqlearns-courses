# Lists in Scratch — Storing Many Values in Order

## Do it first

A variable holds one value. A class holds thirty names, a budget holds every expense, an address book holds six facts per person. Grade 9’s answer is the list — many values, in order, each with its number. Five of the book’s builds run on it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The list and its operations

**A list is ordered data, every item with a position number.** Its operations are the lesson’s kit: **add, insert, item number of, replace, delete** — **deleting renumbers the rest** — **length of list**, and **contains**. The first lists hold **names, fathers’ names and family names**, in order.

## Sub lesson 2 — Parallel lists at work — quiz, address book, budget, tasks

The book’s builds pair lists up: a **quiz on three parallel lists** (questions, correct answers, the student’s answers) with **counters for right and wrong**; an **address book on six parallel lists** (name, mobile, city, home address, email, age) with **input checked as entered** — age 0–120, a mobile ten digits starting 05, asked again until valid — and **searching the names list**; **Amal’s expense tracking on two lists** (kind and price) totalled by **looping with a counter**, savings = income − total; and a **task list on two lists** (tasks and dates) with Add, Update, Delete-one and Delete-all buttons, updating first checking the position is inside the list.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: A list is ordered data — every item with a position number: add, insert, item number of, replace, delete (deleting renumbers the rest), length of list, and contains. Parallel lists hold matched facts — questions/answers, names/numbers, tasks/dates — and counters loop over them. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
