# Lesson plan — End-of-Year Project — Picnic Story and Animation

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Programming · **Lesson:** End-of-Year Project — Picnic Story and Animation
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Bhutan (2026-11-14) — in the title Programming on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Bhutan's student books ("Literacy with ICT", Royal Education Council, Bhutan, Paro), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Typing a story in Notepad, drawing in Scratch and animating in Scratch
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The picnic story — words first in Notepad** — The project opens with **writing**: the picnic story is typed in **Notepad**, the plain text program, so the words come first with no formatting to hide behind. The story is planned before typing — who went, where, what happened — then typed, read back and corrected, and **saved as a file** with a clear name in the class folder, using the year's file skills.
2. **The picnic animation — drawing and movement in Scratch** — The story is then **drawn and animated in Scratch**: a backdrop for the picnic place, sprites for the people and things in the story, and motion and looks blocks to make the scene move — a walk to the picnic spot, food appearing, a greeting said. The finished project is shown to the class: story file and animation together, each part made with a skill the year taught separately.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
