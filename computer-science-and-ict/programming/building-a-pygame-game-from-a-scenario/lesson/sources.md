Provenance scope note: the placements stocked here are from Kazakhstan's okulyk.kz (OKULYK) textbooks, recommended by the Ministry of Education and Science of the Republic of Kazakhstan — the Digital Literacy and Informatika lines of the Almatykitap, Arman-PV and Atamura teams and the Nazarbayev Intellectual Schools (NIS) books, all student books except the two Kadirkulov teacher guides named in the entries below, whose placements are stocked exactly as filed. Many books exist in parallel language editions (Kazakh / Russian / Uzbek / Uyghur / English) of the same textbook: each distinct book is taught once, and every distinct edition is named and cited in the book entry. Kazakhstan's own ways are kept: one textbook line in up to five parallel language editions, the okulyk.kz digital library as the books' home, Scratch from Grade 1 and robotics strands (LEGO WeDo 2.0 and Mindstorms EV3) from Grade 1–2, and every keyword given in Kazakh–Russian–English in the early grades. Media slots in lesson/lesson.md are described slots only ([Picture — …]); no picture numbers are invented.


Kazakhstan's own way (Salgaraeva G9 «Информатика»): Grade 9 in the Arman-PV line is a maker's year: work with information (netiquette, shared documents), choosing and costing a real computer, databases built first in spreadsheets, then Python arrays, and a full PyGame 2D game — background, characters, sprite animation, keyboard control — built from a ready scenario.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukametzhanova G6 «Информатика»): the whole second half of Grade 6 is one long game-development arc — idea, scenario, drawing scenes and characters (vector editor), realising the scenario, sound, a title screen, improving the project — and then documenting the game properly with footnotes, hyperlinks and an automatically built table of contents.

Kazakhstan's own way (Shaniyev team G9 «INFORMATICS»): the Astana-kitap Grade 9 English-medium book is where Kazakhstan teaches databases properly — design, build, sort/filter, pivot tables and charts, ending in a "family shop" mini project — and then spends two chapters on Python lists (one- and two-dimensional, with search/swap/sort/insert/delete) and a full PyGame 2D games chapter.

Kazakhstan's own way (Salgaraeva G6 «Информатика»): the Arman-PV Grade 6 book is a game-making year wrapped in document skills: ergonomics and wireless networks first, then binary coding and encryption, vector graphics (drawing a «Жайлау» summer pasture), a Scratch game designed, built and given a sound track and title screen — and the year ends with footnotes, hyperlinks, copyright and plagiarism, a table of contents and the written documentation for the game.

Kazakhstan's own way (Atayeva/Vyushkova/Zhurbenko G8 «Информатика» NIS): the NIS Grade 8 book is the widest of the set: a whole section on vector graphics and geographic information systems (planning an excursion, making maps, visualising data in GIS), octal and hexadecimal coding, loops applied separately to mathematics, physics, business and animation, strings and subroutines, and a nine-step project method ending in a mini-project on artificial intelligence.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G9 «Информатика»): the Almatykitap Grade 9 book gives Google Drive collaborative editing its own unit, splits sorting from filtering and adds an advanced-filter unit in the database section, separates array input/output and the "main parameters" of a two-dimensional array into their own units, and ends the PyGame chapter with sprite collision detection and a football game.

Kazakhstan's own way (Zhunusov G8 «INFORMATICS»): the Astana-kitap English-medium Grade 8 book (2nd edition) opens with measuring information by Hartley's formula worked on the Kazakh alphabet in Cyrillic (42) and Latin (32) scripts, and its practical-programming chapter builds one game — the "Melting Snowman" — across four parts from algorithm design to testing.
