# HTML Frames

## Do it first

Before modern layouts, a web page could be a window of windows. Mongolia's Grade 11 book still teaches the classic mechanism in full — frames — as part of its complete HTML course. This lesson is that mechanism, taught as the book teaches it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The frameset — dividing the window

A **frameset** document holds no page content of its own: its **FRAMESET** element divides the browser window into a grid, declaring the divisions as **cols** (columns, by width) or **rows** (by height), in pixels, percents or the star share of what remains. Each cell of the grid is then filled by a **FRAME** element naming the page to show there. The visitor sees one window; the server sent several pages and one plan for joining them.

## Sub lesson 2 — Working with frames — what they were for

Frames solved a real problem of their era: a **menu frame** could stay fixed while the **content frame** changed, and a banner could head every page without being copied into each. Their costs are taught with them: a framed site has one address for many pages, so bookmarking and printing a single inner page fight the design — which is why later web practice replaced frames with page layouts. Mongolia teaches them as the book's complete-HTML duty: the pupil who can build a frameset understands how the early web was assembled, and reads old sites without mystery.
