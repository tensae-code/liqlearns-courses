# Finding a Path with Motion Blocks

## Do it first

Look at a short word written in big letters on a path, or a star at the end of a maze. Trace the route with your finger first, slowly. The route your finger found is the path your blocks must build.

## Finding a Path with Motion Blocks

This double lesson puts motion blocks to work on two jobs. In **"Подреди дума" (Spell a word)** the hero travels along the letters of a word, so the path itself spells something — every turn must be planned, or the word comes out broken. In **"Открий път" (Find a path)** the hero must reach a goal through a puzzle, and a **dice game** version makes the planning playful: the dice throws up moves, and you judge which moves belong in the path and in what order. The skill underneath both jobs is the same: look at the whole route first, break it into moves, build it from motion blocks, run it, and when the hero misses, change the path — not the goal.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Finding a path means planning the hero's route first — spelling a word and reaching a goal — then building exactly that route out of motion blocks, and testing it in a dice game of moves. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: plan a whole route with a finger or eye before building any blocks. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: build a path that spells a word, block by block. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: test a path, find where the hero misses, and fix the path. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
