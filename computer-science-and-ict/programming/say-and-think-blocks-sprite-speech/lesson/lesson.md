# Say and Think Blocks — Sprite Speech

## Do it first

Say "Hello!" out loud — that is speech everyone hears. Now think the word "Hello!" without speaking — nobody sees it, but it is in your mind. Sprites can do both, with different blocks.

## Say and Think Blocks — Sprite Speech

A sprite that only moves is half a character. The book now gives it a voice and a mind: **say blocks** put words in a speech bubble above the sprite, for as long as you set or until changed; **think blocks** put words in a thought bubble — the sprite's silent thoughts, which the audience reads but the other characters "do not hear". The lesson also teaches **entering text**: the words inside the block are yours to type, change and correct, so the sprite says exactly the sentence the story needs. Choosing say or think is a storytelling decision: what the world hears, and what only we know.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Say blocks give a sprite words in a speech bubble, think blocks give it silent thoughts, and typed text goes inside the block: the hero speaks, thinks, and takes in text you type. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: make a sprite say a typed sentence in a speech bubble. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: make a sprite think a sentence in a thought bubble. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: choose say or think for a story moment and say why. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
