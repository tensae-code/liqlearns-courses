# Separating the Solutions of Algebraic and Transcendent Equations — true or false

Say true or false, and fix the false ones in your own words.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: A continuous function changing sign — f(a) and f(b) of opposite signs**
**Answer: True**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: A continuous function changing sign — f(a) and f(b) of opposite signs** — as the answer to: What is separation?
**Answer: False — The hunt for brackets — one interval isolating each root**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: Tabulating f at steps and marking sign changes; graphing and reading crossings**
**Answer: True**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: Tabulating f at steps and marking sign changes; graphing and reading crossings** — as the answer to: What is a bracket?
**Answer: False — An interval holding a sign change**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: An even number of roots in one interval shows no sign change — finer steps watch for it**
**Answer: True**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: An even number of roots in one interval shows no sign change — finer steps watch for it** — as the answer to: When is separation complete?
**Answer: False — When each bracket holds exactly one root**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: By the function's behaviour inside — monotonicity, by graph or derivative sense — and by narrowing**
**Answer: True**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: By the function's behaviour inside — monotonicity, by graph or derivative sense — and by narrowing** — as the answer to: Why do refining methods demand separation first?
**Answer: False — They are local — given a two-root bracket, they converge to one and stay silent about the other**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: Separate first, refine second**
**Answer: True**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: Separate first, refine second** — as the answer to: What is separation's ethics in one line?
**Answer: False — Know how many answers exist before polishing any of them**
