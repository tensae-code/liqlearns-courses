# Separating the Solutions of Algebraic and Transcendent Equations — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What guarantees a root inside [a, b]?**
**Answer: A continuous function changing sign — f(a) and f(b) of opposite signs**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What is separation?**
**Answer: The hunt for brackets — one interval isolating each root**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) Name the two hunting methods.**
**Answer: Tabulating f at steps and marking sign changes; graphing and reading crossings**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What is a bracket?**
**Answer: An interval holding a sign change**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) What is the hunt's blind spot?**
**Answer: An even number of roots in one interval shows no sign change — finer steps watch for it**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) When is separation complete?**
**Answer: When each bracket holds exactly one root**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) How is singleness tested?**
**Answer: By the function's behaviour inside — monotonicity, by graph or derivative sense — and by narrowing**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) Why do refining methods demand separation first?**
**Answer: They are local — given a two-root bracket, they converge to one and stay silent about the other**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) State the chapter's order.**
**Answer: Separate first, refine second**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) What is separation's ethics in one line?**
**Answer: Know how many answers exist before polishing any of them**
