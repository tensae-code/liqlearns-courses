# Video script — Queues and Waiting Lines

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Every queue has the same three parts: **arrivals** (how often customers come, and how irregularly), the **queue** itself (the waiting line, usually **first in, first out**), and **service** (how many servers, and how long each service takes). The numbers that matter are **waiting time**, **queue length**, and how busy the servers stay. The trap is irregularity: even when arrivals average slower than service, a bunching of arrivals builds a line that takes long to clear — which is why the average alone, the lesson of the analysis chapters, never tells a queue's story.
A **queue simulation** runs the model minute by minute — or arrival by arrival — varying what the manager can change: one more server, faster service, arrivals spread by appointments. The findings are famously unfair: as a system nears its capacity, waiting time does not grow gently but **explodes**, so a service running at 95% busy can hold queues many times longer than one at 80%. Egypt places queues inside the simulation chapter because this is simulation's home ground — a real queue cannot be experimented on without experimenting on real people, but its model can be run a hundred ways before a single desk is moved.
