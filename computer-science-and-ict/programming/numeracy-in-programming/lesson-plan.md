## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Numbers are the programmer's material** — Programs are built of numbers even when they show words: counts, sizes, positions, times, scores. **Numeracy for programming** (literasi numerasi untuk pemrograman) is the number sense that work demands — knowing how big a value can grow, what a count means, when an answer is the wrong size to be true. Indonesia's Grade 8 book sets it as its own topic at the head of the programming chapter because the faults it prevents are the quietest in computing: a program rarely announces a wrong number; it just prints it, confidently, in the right font.
2. **Estimates, ranges and the sanity check** — The numerate programmer's habits are three. **Estimate** before computing — guess the answer's size, so a result ten times too big is caught by the eye. **Mind the ranges** — a byte holds so much, a counter overflows, a percentage of a small class is a handful of people; every type and every real quantity has its banks. **Sanity-check** the output — does this total exceed its parts, this average sit outside its values, this count come out negative? Numeracy is not more mathematics; it is mathematics standing guard over the program.
