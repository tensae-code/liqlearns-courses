# micro:bit Project — Introduce Yourself by Programming

## Do it first

The micro:bit ladder ends, as Kuwait's units end, in a project — but this one's subject is the programmer. 'Introduce yourself by programming' sets challenges in which the board says who its maker is. This lesson is that set of challenges.

## Sub lesson 1 — Letters, animals and the weather — the board speaks for you

The first challenges make the board a voice: **letters** — your initials and name lighting the grid; **animals** — a favourite creature drawn and animated in LEDs; and the **weather** — the board reading its sensors to report the warmth and light around it, your place described by its numbers. Each challenge is small; together they are an introduction no essay could give, because every fact arrives as a working program.

In your mind's eye: letters, animals and the weather — the board speaks for you.

## Sub lesson 2 — The button-guessing game — your program, played by others

The closing challenge is a **game**: the **button-guessing game**, in which the board sets a puzzle its player answers with the A and B buttons — the maker's program, played by somebody else. It gathers the whole ladder into one build: Basic frames, LED display, Input buttons, and the Logic of right and wrong answers. A project that introduces its maker ends properly when a stranger can pick the board up and meet the maker through play.

In your mind's eye: the button-guessing game — your program, played by others.
