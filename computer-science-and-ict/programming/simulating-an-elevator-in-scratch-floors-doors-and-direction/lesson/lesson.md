# Simulating an Elevator in Scratch — Floors, Doors and Direction

## Do it first

A new building needs its lift tested before anyone rides it. Grade 9 simulates the elevator in Scratch: floor numbers, doors that glide open, direction arrows — and logic that moves the lift one floor at a time, in the right direction, until it arrives.

## Sub lesson 1 — Floors, doors, buttons, arrows

The lift’s pieces are sprites with jobs: **floor-number sprites with several costumes** (one costume per floor number), **door sprites that glide open and closed**, **open/close buttons**, and **direction arrows** showing the lift’s travel. The stage becomes the lift shaft, drawn and working.

## Sub lesson 2 — Current floor, new floor, direction — moving floor by floor

Three variables steer: **current floor, new floor and direction**. The movement logic is careful logic — **repeat-until and wait-until with AND/OR** move the lift **floor by floor** towards the new floor, stop it on arrival, and only then glide the doors. A simulation must obey the real machine’s order: arrive first, doors after.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: The elevator simulation runs on floor-number sprites with several costumes, door sprites that glide open and closed, open/close buttons and direction arrows; current floor, new floor and direction variables steer it, and repeat-until and wait-until with AND/OR logic move the lift floor by floor. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
