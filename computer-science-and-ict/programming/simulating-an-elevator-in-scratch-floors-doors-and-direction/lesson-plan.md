# Lesson plan — Simulating an Elevator in Scratch — Floors, Doors and Direction

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Programming · **Lesson:** Simulating an Elevator in Scratch — Floors, Doors and Direction
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Palestine (2026-11-14) — in the title Programming on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Palestine's Grade 9 Programming student book, because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The elevator simulation runs on floor-number sprites with several costumes, door sprites that glide open and closed, open/close buttons and direction arrows; current floor, new floor and direction variables steer it, and repeat-until and wait-until with AND/OR logic move the lift floor by floor.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Floors, doors, buttons, arrows** — The lift’s pieces are sprites with jobs: **floor-number sprites with several costumes** (one costume per floor number), **door sprites that glide open and closed**, **open/close buttons**, and **direction arrows** showing the lift’s travel. The stage becomes the lift shaft, drawn and working.
2. **Current floor, new floor, direction — moving floor by floor** — Three variables steer: **current floor, new floor and direction**. The movement logic is careful logic — **repeat-until and wait-until with AND/OR** move the lift **floor by floor** towards the new floor, stop it on arrival, and only then glide the doors. A simulation must obey the real machine’s order: arrive first, doors after.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
