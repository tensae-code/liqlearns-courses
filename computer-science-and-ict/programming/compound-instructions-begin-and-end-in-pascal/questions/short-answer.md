# Compound Instructions — begin and end in Pascal — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What is a compound instruction?**
**Answer: A sequence of instructions in begin…end, treated as one instruction**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) How are the inside instructions separated?**
**Answer: By semicolons — none is needed after the last, before end**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What does C++ use for the same wrapper?**
**Answer: Braces { }**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) Where can a compound instruction stand?**
**Answer: Wherever the grammar asks for one instruction — if branches, loop bodies, case labels**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) What does a semicolon just before end add?**
**Answer: Only an empty instruction — harmless but needless**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) In 'if x > 0 then a := 1; b := 2;', what is conditional?**
**Answer: Only a := 1 — b := 2 runs always**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) How is the pair made to stand together?**
**Answer: Wrap both in begin…end as the if's one instruction**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What reading habit does the lesson teach?**
**Answer: Find the governing instruction's one instruction and draw its border before judging**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) What are most beginner logic errors, in the lesson's phrase?**
**Answer: Border errors — mistaking what a governing instruction commands**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) Does begin…end itself execute anything?**
**Answer: No — it groups; the inside instructions execute in order**
