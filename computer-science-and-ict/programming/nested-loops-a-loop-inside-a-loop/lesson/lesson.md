# Nested Loops — a Loop Inside a Loop

## Do it first

One loop repeats a line of work. Real work comes in grids — every row, and inside it every column. Kuwait's loops lesson ends with loops inside loops. This lesson is the nesting, and how to count it without lying to yourself.

## Sub lesson 1 — The nesting — the inner loop runs whole, every outer turn

A **nested loop** places one loop **inside** another's body. The law that governs it: the **inner loop runs to completion for every single turn of the outer loop** — so a 3-turn outer loop around a 4-turn inner loop runs the innermost body **12 times**, not 7. Kuwait teaches both family forms: a **while inside a while**, and a **for inside a for** — the second being the grid's natural shape, rows by the outer, columns by the inner.

In your mind's eye: the nesting — the inner loop runs whole, every outer turn.

## Sub lesson 2 — Reading nested loops — tables, grids and patterns

Nested loops are read by **tracing both counters**: write the outer's turn, then the inner's whole run beneath it, turn by turn, until the shape is visible — the multiplication table is the classic trace, every product the meeting of one outer number and one inner. Their work is the world's repeated rectangles: timetables, seating plans, printed patterns of stars in rows — wherever a job is 'for each row, for each thing in the row', a nested loop is already the answer, waiting to be recognised.

In your mind's eye: reading nested loops — tables, grids and patterns.
