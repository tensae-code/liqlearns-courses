# Lesson plan — Scrolling Backgrounds That Wrap Around in Scratch

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Programming · **Lesson:** Scrolling Backgrounds That Wrap Around in Scratch
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Palestine (2026-11-14) — in the title Programming on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Palestine's Grade 9 Programming student book, because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A scene wider than the stage is split into two sprites that keep gliding left and jump back to the right when their position passes a limit, so the background scrolls without ever ending.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Two sprites, one endless scene** — The trick is a cut: **a scene wider than the stage is split into two sprites**, laid edge to edge so the stage shows a join that should not be seen. Together the two pieces are the whole wide picture; the stage only ever shows one scene’s width of it.
2. **Glide left, jump back right** — Both sprites **keep gliding left**; when a sprite’s **position passes a limit** — its whole width gone off the left — it **jumps back to the right**, behind its partner, ready to glide through again. The two leapfrog forever, and **the background never ends**.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
