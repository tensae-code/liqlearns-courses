# Scrolling Backgrounds That Wrap Around in Scratch

## Do it first

The scene is wider than the stage: the road runs on past the screen’s edge. Grade 9’s animated-backgrounds lesson cuts the wide scene in two, and the two pieces chase each other forever — the background never ends.

## Sub lesson 1 — Two sprites, one endless scene

The trick is a cut: **a scene wider than the stage is split into two sprites**, laid edge to edge so the stage shows a join that should not be seen. Together the two pieces are the whole wide picture; the stage only ever shows one scene’s width of it.

[Picture — two sprites, one endless scene]

## Sub lesson 2 — Glide left, jump back right

Both sprites **keep gliding left**; when a sprite’s **position passes a limit** — its whole width gone off the left — it **jumps back to the right**, behind its partner, ready to glide through again. The two leapfrog forever, and **the background never ends**.

[Picture — glide left, jump back right]

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: A scene wider than the stage is split into two sprites that keep gliding left and jump back to the right when their position passes a limit, so the background scrolls without ever ending. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.
