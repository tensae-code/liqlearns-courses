# Plotting Graphs and Diagrams in PascalABC

## Do it first

Plotting a graph by hand takes a page of calculations and a careful hand. A program does the same work in a blink — for any function, any scale. In this lesson your PascalABC programs plot graphs from functions and diagrams from data.

## Sub lesson 1 — Plotting a function's graph

To **plot a graph**, the program needs axes first: lines for the horizontal and vertical axes, with a chosen **scale** — how many window points stand for one unit. Then it works the function like a table: take an x value, calculate its y, turn the pair into window coordinates, and mark the point; repeat in small steps and join the points, and the **curve** appears. The program does in a blink what graph paper does in a lesson — and with another scale or another step, it does it again differently.

[Picture — axes with a stated scale and a curve being plotted point by point across them]

## Sub lesson 2 — Diagrams from data

A **diagram** shows amounts, not a function: the program reads the data values and draws each as a bar or sector of the height or size the value deserves, in the same scale across the whole diagram — the comparison is the message. Both graph and diagram share one discipline: the picture must be **honest to the numbers** — axes and scales stated, steps even, heights proportional — because a plotted picture persuades faster than a table, and a wrong scale persuades wrongly. That is why this section closes the graphics work: pictures from programs are claims, and the programmer answers for them.

[Picture — a row of bars of different heights with one scale ruler standing beside them]
