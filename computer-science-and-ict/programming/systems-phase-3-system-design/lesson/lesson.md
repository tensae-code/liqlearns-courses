# Systems Phase 3 — System Design

## Do it first

Analysis said what; design now says how. Fiji's Phase 3 draws the new system completely — its data, its processes, its screens and reports — before a line of it is built. This lesson is the drawing.

## Sub lesson 1 — Design the inside — data and processes

**System design** turns the requirements into a **complete plan of the new system**. Its inside work designs the **data**: what files or tables will hold it, in what fields, under what codes — and the **processes**: what each part of the system does to the data, in what order, with what checks. At Viti this is where 'find stock in under a minute' becomes an actual stock file with an actual item code: the requirement's promise given a mechanism. Design mistakes found *here* are still pencil mistakes.

## Sub lesson 2 — Design the outside — inputs, outputs, and the whole checked

The outside work designs what people meet: the **inputs** — the forms and screens data enters by, laid out so entry is hard to get wrong — and the **outputs** — the screens and printed reports each user receives, carrying exactly their level's information. The phase ends with the whole design **checked against the requirements**, one by one: every 'must' of analysis either designed for, or openly missing. Fiji's design lesson is the blueprint discipline: builders who follow it in Phase 4 never have to guess what they are building.
