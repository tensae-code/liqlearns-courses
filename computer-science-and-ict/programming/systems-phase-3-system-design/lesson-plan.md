## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Design the inside — data and processes** — **System design** turns the requirements into a **complete plan of the new system**. Its inside work designs the **data**: what files or tables will hold it, in what fields, under what codes — and the **processes**: what each part of the system does to the data, in what order, with what checks. At Viti this is where 'find stock in under a minute' becomes an actual stock file with an actual item code: the requirement's promise given a mechanism. Design mistakes found *here* are still pencil mistakes.
2. **Design the outside — inputs, outputs, and the whole checked** — The outside work designs what people meet: the **inputs** — the forms and screens data enters by, laid out so entry is hard to get wrong — and the **outputs** — the screens and printed reports each user receives, carrying exactly their level's information. The phase ends with the whole design **checked against the requirements**, one by one: every 'must' of analysis either designed for, or openly missing. Fiji's design lesson is the blueprint discipline: builders who follow it in Phase 4 never have to guess what they are building.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
