## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Study the present — fact-finding** — **Systems analysis** studies the **current system** in detail: how work actually flows, what data is captured, where it hurts. Its tools are **fact-finding** — interviewing the people who do the work, questioning them in writing, watching the work happen, and reading the present system's documents and records. Fiji's analyst does not guess from an office: at Viti the analysis sits with the stock clerks and follows a stock card through its whole life, because the system that exists on paper and the system that exists at 4pm on a Friday are often two different systems.
2. **Fix the requirements — what the new system must do** — Analysis ends by writing the **requirements**: the exact statement of what the new system **must do** — the data it must keep, the processes it must carry, the outputs it must produce, and the limits it must respect. Requirements answer *what*, never *how*: 'any clerk must find a stock figure in under a minute' is a requirement; which program or screen achieves it belongs to design. Fiji's discipline is the phase's gift to every later phase — a wrong requirement fixed here costs a sentence; fixed in development, it costs the build.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
