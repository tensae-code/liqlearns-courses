# Algorithm Design Methods — Divide-and-Conquer, Dynamic Programming, Greedy — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What are divide-and-conquer's three moves?**
**Answer: Split into smaller copies, solve each, join the answers**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What is its worked example in the supplementary material?**
**Answer: Binary search and its variants**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) How does binary search split?**
**Answer: Halve the ordered list and decide which half holds the target**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) When does the method pay?**
**Answer: When splitting is cheap and the pieces are truly independent**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) Which chapter teaches the methods?**
**Answer: Greece Grade 12 Anaptyxi Efarmogon, Chapter 4**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) When does dynamic programming fit?**
**Answer: Problems whose pieces overlap — solve each piece once and store its answer**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) Where are the answers stored?**
**Answer: In a table the bigger pieces read**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What does the greedy method do?**
**Answer: Builds the answer one locally best step at a time, never revisiting**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) What is greedy's danger?**
**Answer: The locally best step may not serve the whole — it must be proved**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) Which book reworks them as activities?**
**Answer: The Grade 12 Anaptyxi workbook, Chapter 4**
