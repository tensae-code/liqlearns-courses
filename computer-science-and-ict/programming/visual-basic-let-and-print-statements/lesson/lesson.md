# Visual Basic — LET and PRINT Statements

## Do it first

Libya's Visual Basic course begins, statement by statement, with the pair every program needs first: a way to put a value into a variable, and a way to show what the program holds. This lesson is LET and PRINT, exactly as the Grade 11 book teaches them.

## Sub lesson 1 — LET — the assignment statement

**LET** assigns a value to a variable: `LET X = 5` puts 5 into X. The variable stands on the **left**, the value or expression on the **right**, and the equals sign here means **'becomes'**, not 'is equal to'. The book names the **common errors** with LET plainly: putting the variable on the right, trying to assign to a constant, and confusing assignment with a test of equality — the three mistakes every beginner makes, named before they harden.

## Sub lesson 2 — PRINT — showing what the program holds

**PRINT** shows output: a **number** (`PRINT 7`), a **text constant** in quotation marks, a **variable's value** (`PRINT X` shows what X holds, not the letter X), **string variables**, the **joining of two text variables**, and the **result of a maths expression** — PRINT works the expression out and shows the answer. Read a PRINT line by asking one question: is this item in quotes (shown as it stands), a variable (its value is shown), or an expression (its result is shown)?

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: Assigns a value to a variable. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: Becomes — X becomes 5; it is assignment, not a test of equality. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: The left; the value or expression stands on the right. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: Putting the variable on the right (any one of: assigning to a constant; confusing assignment with equality). Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
