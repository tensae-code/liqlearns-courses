# Video script — Visual Basic — LET and PRINT Statements

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

**LET** assigns a value to a variable: `LET X = 5` puts 5 into X. The variable stands on the **left**, the value or expression on the **right**, and the equals sign here means **'becomes'**, not 'is equal to'. The book names the **common errors** with LET plainly: putting the variable on the right, trying to assign to a constant, and confusing assignment with a test of equality — the three mistakes every beginner makes, named before they harden.
**PRINT** shows output: a **number** (`PRINT 7`), a **text constant** in quotation marks, a **variable's value** (`PRINT X` shows what X holds, not the letter X), **string variables**, the **joining of two text variables**, and the **result of a maths expression** — PRINT works the expression out and shows the answer. Read a PRINT line by asking one question: is this item in quotes (shown as it stands), a variable (its value is shown), or an expression (its result is shown)?
