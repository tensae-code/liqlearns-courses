# SDLC Models — Waterfall, Spiral and Agile

## Do it first

The life cycle's stages can be walked in different gaits: straight down like water, round and round like a spiral, or in short sprints. This lesson learns the three gaits and when each is chosen.

## Sub lesson 1 — Waterfall and Spiral — the planned gaits

The **Waterfall** model walks the stages **once, in order**, each finished before the next begins: right when requirements are fixed and known (a payroll for a settled law), helpless when they change mid-fall. The **Spiral** model walks the cycle **repeatedly**, each loop planning, weighing **risks**, building a part, and evaluating — growing the system in widening turns: right when the risks are large and the destination is learned by travelling.

## Sub lesson 2 — Agile — the responsive gait

**Agile** methods build in **short iterations (sprints)**: a small working part every few weeks, the customer reacting, the plan re-drawn each round. Their characteristics: **change welcomed** rather than feared, working software valued over documents, people and conversation over rigid process. Jordan's comparison closes with the chooser's grounds — how fixed are the requirements? how costly is a wrong turn? how present is the customer? — because a model is a bet about the future of the project, and each model bets differently.
