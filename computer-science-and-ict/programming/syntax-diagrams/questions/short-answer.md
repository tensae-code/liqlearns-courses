# Syntax Diagrams — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What stands in round-ended boxes?**
**Answer: Terminal symbols — written exactly as shown**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What stands in rectangular boxes?**
**Answer: Nonterminals — constructions with their own charts**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) How is a diagram read?**
**Answer: By following the arrows — a complete path collects a legal form**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What do branches and loops in the arrows mean?**
**Answer: Branches are choices; a loop back means the part may repeat**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) What is each construction's chart called as a whole?**
**Answer: Its syntax diagram**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) Do diagrams and BNF describe different grammars?**
**Answer: No — the same grammar, in two media**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What do diagrams show better?**
**Answer: Flow — repetition and choices are seen at a glance**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What is BNF's advantage?**
**Answer: It is shorter to write, easier to print and search**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) How does a pupil prove they own a rule?**
**Answer: By translating it between BNF and a diagram**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) Which manuals use both?**
**Answer: The programming languages' own manuals**
