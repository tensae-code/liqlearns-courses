# Synchronising Sprites' Actions

## Do it first

Two dancers must start their turn at the same moment. They count, they watch each other, one gives a signal. Sprites synchronise the same way: signals and waits, agreed timing.

## Synchronising Sprites' Actions

Sprites that each run their own script live in their own time. **Synchronising the sprites' actions (Синхронизиране на действията на героите)** brings them into one time. The tools are the ones dialogue taught — **waits** matched to a partner's action — and Scratch's **messages**: one sprite **broadcasts** a message when its part is done, and the other sprite's script starts **when it receives** that message. A message is surer than a guessed wait, because the second sprite starts on the event itself, not on a hope about timing. The learner synchronises pairs of actions — one ends, the other begins on the signal; two begin together on one broadcast — and learns to hunt the two classic faults: the gap (signal sent too late or wait too long) and the pile-up (signal missing, so actions overlap).

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Synchronising means sprites act together in time: messages and waits line their actions up, so one sprite starts exactly when another finishes — no gaps, no pile-ups. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: make one sprite start exactly when another finishes, using a message. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: start two sprites together on one broadcast. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: find and fix a gap or a pile-up in a synchronised scene. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
