# Drawing a first flowchart

## Do it first

A policy arrives from the Woreda Education Department, in words. Your job: turn it into a picture a machine could follow. This doing-lesson builds the chart in the book's order — policy first, symbols second, paths traced last. Prove each sub lesson before the next opens. The teacher's way of running these lessons — draw alone, then argue in pairs, swapping who draws and who argues — is how your class will work.

## Sub lesson 1 — The garden policy in words

Flowcharts can represent non-computational — human, manual — processes too. The worked example turns a Woreda Education Department environmental policy into a flowchart: schools with a garden have it cultivated by students and teachers as Nature study; a new garden is prepared with parents' participation. Two cases, decided by one question — that is flowchart-shaped already.

## Sub lesson 2 — Building the chart

The diagram uses Start and End terminators and a decision diamond — 'Has the school a botanical garden?' — with Yes and No arrows leading to the two process boxes: cultivate the garden that exists, or prepare a new one with parents. Draw it exactly so: terminator, diamond, two paths, one End.

## Sub lesson 3 — Tracing and testing the chart

Trace the chart twice — once down the Yes path, once down the No path — as the GIF does. Then the practicals: a speed-check algorithm (40 km/h and under is 'reasonable speed'; 40–60 is 'medium speed limit — be careful'; over 60 is 'excessive speed, high risk'), drawn as a flowchart; and a flowchart accepting two numbers and displaying their sum and product. The teacher's method for these lessons: draw on paper individually first, then discuss in pairs or small groups, swapping roles — the one who draws and the one who argues for each symbol change places — and where computers are scarce, algorithms are designed and argued unplugged, through role-plays; a visually impaired pupil joins understanding and planning by speaking the steps aloud or writing them in braille (teacher guide — evidence).

## Sub lesson 4 — Grade 10 worked flowcharts — add two numbers; positive or negative

Two complete Grade 10 solutions model the craft.

**Add two numbers.** The algorithm description — **read A and B, add them, assign the sum to C, display C** — is drawn as **start → input → process → output → end**: terminator, parallelogram (read A and B), rectangle (C = A + B), parallelogram (display C), terminator.

**Positive or negative.** Read **x**; then a **decision**: **if x is less than zero, write that it is negative; otherwise write that it is not negative**. Drawn, the **decision diamond splits the flow into its two message paths** — follow the marker in the GIF down the first chart, box by box, the way every flowchart is read.

## Sub lesson 4 — Watch the flow build

The chart is built **symbol by symbol, in order** — start, input, process, output, end — and read the same way: by following the flow.
