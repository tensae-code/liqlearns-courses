# Lesson plan — Drawing a first flowchart

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Programming · **Lesson:** Drawing a first flowchart
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 6 — Fundamentals of Programming (CURRICULUM.md) (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a flowchart can carry a human process too — the school garden policy becomes Start, a decision diamond, two process paths and End.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course — the learner knows what a computer, a network and the Internet are. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or in steps — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The garden policy in words** — schools with a garden have it cultivated; a new garden is prepared with parents' participation.
2. **Building the chart** — Start and End terminators and the decision diamond 'Has the school a botanical garden?' with Yes and No arrows to the two process boxes.
3. **Tracing and testing the chart** — the speed-check and sum-and-product practicals test the same skills on new problems.

4. **Grade 10 worked flowcharts — add two numbers; positive or negative** — two complete Grade 10 solutions: the add-two-numbers chart from its algorithm description, and the sign test whose decision diamond splits the flow into two message paths

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of this course. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Draw the garden-policy flowchart complete and correct.
2. Trace both paths and say what each reaches.
3. Build the speed-check and sum-and-product charts.
4. Draw the add-two-numbers flowchart from its algorithm description.
5. Draw the sign-test flowchart with its decision diamond and two message paths.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Where the lesson is a doing-lesson, a school computer if one is available — paper works too.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes up the key words → go back to the picture and point at each part while you say its name: the garden policy, the decision diamond's question, the speed-check bands, pair work in flowchart lessons.
- Cannot say why it matters → use the lesson's own example again, step by step, before trying a new one.
