# Scratch Movement Blocks — Move, Turn and Point

## Do it first

After the Scratch room itself, Cuba's first block family is movement: the blue blocks that walk the sprite around the stage. Grade 5 meets them in the programme and the student book, and Grade 6 works them again as its own chapter.

## Sub lesson 1 — Move and turn — steps and degrees

**Move** steps the sprite forward by the number of steps written in the block, in the direction it faces. **Turn** rotates the sprite by the **degrees** written in it — right or left — changing where it faces without moving its place. Steps and degrees are both numbers the programmer chooses: a longer walk is a bigger move number, a sharper corner is a bigger turn. Reading a movement script means adding up its steps and turns before running it and saying where the sprite will end.

## Sub lesson 2 — Point and glide — facing first, then travelling

**Point** sets the sprite's direction at once — right, up, left or down by its number — so the next move goes the chosen way. **Glide** moves the sprite **smoothly to a place over a number of seconds**, instead of jumping there: the sprite is seen travelling, and a longer time means a slower journey. Cuba's Grade 5 guide adds glide to the movement family inside the same unit as move and turn: face the right way first, then travel — by steps at once, or by gliding in time.
