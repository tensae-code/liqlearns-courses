# Lesson plan — Scratch Movement Blocks — Move, Turn and Point

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Programming · **Lesson:** Scratch Movement Blocks — Move, Turn and Point
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Cuba (2026-11-14) — in the title Programming on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Cuba's Mi Mundo Digital / Informática student books and teacher-side books (Ministerio de Educación / Editorial Pueblo y Educación), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Scratch's **movement blocks** move the sprite: **move** steps it forward, **turn** rotates it by degrees, **point** sets the direction it faces, and **glide** carries it smoothly to a place.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Move and turn — steps and degrees** — **Move** steps the sprite forward by the number of steps written in the block, in the direction it faces. **Turn** rotates the sprite by the **degrees** written in it — right or left — changing where it faces without moving its place. Steps and degrees are both numbers the programmer chooses: a longer walk is a bigger move number, a sharper corner is a bigger turn. Reading a movement script means adding up its steps and turns before running it and saying where the sprite will end.
2. **Point and glide — facing first, then travelling** — **Point** sets the sprite's direction at once — right, up, left or down by its number — so the next move goes the chosen way. **Glide** moves the sprite **smoothly to a place over a number of seconds**, instead of jumping there: the sprite is seen travelling, and a longer time means a slower journey. Cuba's Grade 5 guide adds glide to the movement family inside the same unit as move and turn: face the right way first, then travel — by steps at once, or by gliding in time.
