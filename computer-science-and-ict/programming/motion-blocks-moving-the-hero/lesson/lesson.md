# Motion Blocks — Moving the Hero

## Do it first

Point to the way you want a character on the screen to go. Say the moves out loud — forward, forward, turn. Each move you named can be a block: that is what motion blocks are.

## Motion Blocks — Moving the Hero

After sequences, the book gives the steps a new form: **motion blocks**. A motion block is one movement written as a block — move forward, turn — and the hero (the character in the code.org Course 1 puzzles) does exactly the movement the block says. Blocks snap together in a row, and the hero follows the row from the first block to the last, just as it followed a spoken sequence. The number inside a move can matter: a longer move carries the hero further. Reading a row of motion blocks before running it — saying where the hero will end — is the habit this lesson builds: first read the blocks, then run them, then check whether the hero went where the blocks promised.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Motion blocks are the blocks that move the hero: each block is one movement, and snapping motion blocks together builds the hero's whole path. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: say what a motion block does. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: build a short path for the hero out of motion blocks. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: read a row of motion blocks and say where the hero will end before running it. Do this with a real example from your own work or your own school day, and write or say how you know it is right.
