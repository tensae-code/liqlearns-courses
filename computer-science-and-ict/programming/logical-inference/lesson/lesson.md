# Logical Inference

## Do it first

Logical inference — drawing a conclusion that must follow from given propositions: the rules by which true premises yield a true conclusion

## Sub lesson 1 — Inference — conclusions that must follow

**Inference** (inferensi) is reasoning's engine: from propositions accepted as true, a conclusion is **drawn** that those propositions **force**. If every programmer in the room passed, and Dedi is a programmer in the room, then Dedi passed — not probably, but by the shape of the premises. Indonesia's SMK logic chapter builds inference after the connectives on purpose: the connectives build propositions, and inference is what propositions are *for* — in law, in mathematics, and in the expert systems and program logic that close the book's road.

In your mind's eye: inference — conclusions that must follow.

## Sub lesson 2 — Valid and invalid — the shape, not the story

An inference is judged by its **shape**, not by whether its story sounds true. It is **valid** when true premises cannot leave the conclusion false; it is **invalid** — however believable — when the same shape lets a false conclusion slip through (from 'some pupils passed' and 'Dedi is a pupil', Dedi's passing does not follow). The chapter's discipline is to test arguments by replacing their content with letters and watching the shape alone — the habit that keeps a reasoner, and later a programmer writing conditions, from being persuaded by a story into a hole in the logic.

In your mind's eye: valid and invalid — the shape, not the story.
