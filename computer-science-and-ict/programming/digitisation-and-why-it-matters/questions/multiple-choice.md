# Digitisation and Why It Matters — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) Digitisation is —**
a) buying a computer  b) turning continuous real-world things into binary data  c) deleting paper records  d) drawing pictures on a screen
**Answer: b) turning continuous real-world things into binary data** — Sound, pictures and text are measured and written as 0s and 1s.

**2. (Sub lesson 1) A digitised picture is held as —**
a) a tiny photograph inside the machine  b) numbers for the colour of each tiny dot  c) a smooth sheet of light  d) ink levels
**Answer: b) numbers for the colour of each tiny dot** — Each dot — pixel — gets its colour as numbers.

**3. (Sub lesson 2) Which power does digitised data gain over the original photograph?**
a) It fades more slowly but still fades  b) Copies of it are exact, however many are made  c) It can only be sent once  d) It becomes heavier
**Answer: b) Copies of it are exact, however many are made** — The numbers are the thing; numbers copy perfectly.

**4. (Sub lesson 2) Why must digitisation come before data processing and AI?**
a) It is chapter one by tradition  b) Programs can only work on data — digitisation is how the world becomes data  c) Computers refuse paper  d) Teachers prefer it
**Answer: b) Programs can only work on data — digitisation is how the world becomes data** — Digitisation is the doorway; everything else is on its far side.

**5. (Sub lesson 2) Which of these is digitisation?**
a) Reading a letter aloud  b) Recording a voice note on a phone  c) Writing a letter with a pen  d) Singing in a hall
**Answer: b) Recording a voice note on a phone** — The continuous voice becomes binary data.
