# Counters and Accumulators

## Do it first

Counting and totalling are the two jobs almost every program does. Cuba's Grade 9 foundations teach them as a named pair — *contadores y acumuladores* — right after variables and expressions, before the flowchart chapter puts them to work.

## Sub lesson 1 — Counters — one more, each time

A **counter** starts at an opening value — usually **zero** — and every time the event it watches happens, it **grows by exactly 1**: counter = counter + 1. At any moment the counter **tells how many times** the event has happened so far: pupils entered, pages read, goals scored. The counter does not care what happened, only how often. Forgetting to start it — or starting it at the wrong value — is the classic counter fault: every count after is off by that much.

## Sub lesson 2 — Accumulators — a running total

An **accumulator** also starts opened — usually at **zero** — but it grows by **the value that arrives**: total = total + new value. It answers not how many, but **how much altogether**: the sum of marks, the cost of the shopping, the litres used. Counter and accumulator usually work side by side in one loop — the counter counts the values while the accumulator totals them — and together they give the average: total ÷ count. Cuba pairs them in one section because real programs so rarely use one without the other.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: Counts by adding 1 each time an event happens. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: At zero. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: Adds each arriving value into a running total. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: By the value that arrives, not by 1. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
