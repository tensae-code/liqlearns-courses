# Lesson plan — Wait Blocks — Pausing Between Steps in Scratch

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Programming · **Lesson:** Wait Blocks — Pausing Between Steps in Scratch
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Palestine (2026-11-14) — in the title Programming on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Palestine's Grade 7 Programming student book, because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** A wait block pauses a script for a set time before the next block runs — it controls the timing of execution, pacing dialogue, movement and drawing so each step can be seen and read.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The wait period — timing of execution** — Grade 7 teaches the **wait period** inside its repetitions lesson: a **wait block** holds the script still for the seconds written on it, and the next block runs only when the wait ends. Waits **control the timing of execution** — the same script, with different waits, tells its story at a different speed.
2. **Waits at work — dialogue, journeys, repetition** — Once taught, the wait quietly runs the rest of the course: speakers **wait while the other talks**, the travelling parrot’s journey is **paced by waits**, and repeated drawing becomes watchable when a wait sits inside the repeat. The question is never whether to wait, but how long — long enough to read, short enough to keep the story alive.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Two lines of dialogue spoken in the same instant are noise. A drawing that appears all at once cannot be watched. The block that fixes both is the smallest one in this lesson: wait. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
