## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Following instructions step by step** — A program is **instructions followed in order**, one step at a time. In Jordan's Grade 1 Programming Hour, a character must cross a grid or a maze: the class learns that the character does **exactly** what is said — no guessing, no kindness — so each instruction must be complete and in its right place. When the character goes wrong, the fault is in the instructions, never in the character: the first and kindest form of debugging, learned at six years old.
2. **Direction commands — forward, turn left, turn right** — The commands are directions: **forward** (a stated number of steps), **turn left**, **turn right** — a tiny language whose words the whole class shares. A route is a **sequence** of these commands; guiding a character with words along a worksheet maze — 'forward 2, turn left, forward 1' — is writing a program in speech. The teacher guide's own fallback carries the lesson's philosophy: if no computers are available, print the pictures and do the direction work on paper, because **the goal is the thinking, not the device**.

1. **Do it first** — No computer is needed for the first programming of all: one child is the character, another is the programmer, and the floor is the world. This lesson learns coding with the body — directions given, followed, and debugged aloud. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
