**Acceptance count: taught in 11 countries (Ethiopia, Finland, Palestine, Sudan, Cuba, Bhutan, Sri Lanka, Rwanda, Vietnam, Pakistan, Kazakhstan).**

Teacher-guide answers for this lesson's review items were checked against the guide's closing answer keys (teacher guide — evidence; student book Unit review answers keyed in the guide, Unit 5 printed p. 82 and Unit 6 printed pp. 112–113).

Saudi Arabia's own way (Grade 4, Digital Skills): the primary Digital Skills course (Binary Logic) is built as three units per term with the same guide pattern as the intermediate books (outcomes, important points, steps, answers, unit project, "Test Yourself"), files on the Ain platform, and a strong Arabic/English bilingual-typing strand — pupils switch the keyboard between Arabic and English with Alt+Shift and learn text direction, hamza and Arabic punctuation on the keyboard alongside Word skills; Scratch is introduced in Term 1 through algorithms from daily life. This citation is PARTIAL — Term 1 only. (teacher guide — evidence)

Palestine’s own way (Grade 6, Programming): Grade 6 programming is a full Scratch course (MIT, named in the book) in four units — basics, movement and costumes, sprites communicating, projects. Lessons after Unit 1 are deliberately non-cumulative: pupils imagine, program, then share their product in exhibitions and competitions. A local touch is kept: the “say” lesson notes Arabic text appears reversed because Scratch writes left-to-right.

Palestine’s own way (Grade 8, Programming): Grade 8 Scratch is organised around making things — Unit 1 programs drawings and ornaments (the unit opens with the ornaments of Jerusalem’s churches and the Dome of the Rock), Unit 2 weaves interactive stories with sound and dialogue (including a dialogue project on love of the homeland), Unit 3 designs real games with variables, operators and sensing.

Palestine’s own way (Grade 9, Programming): Grade 9 is a full applications course in Scratch — after a basics unit it builds real things: a photo editor, a video player, a phone PIN lock, a bank account, a library sign-up/login, an alarm clock, an elevator, a task list, a wall clock that can ring at prayer times. Its own workarounds are content too: Scratch 2 cannot type Arabic in its paint editor, so Arabic text is written in MS Paint, saved as a picture and uploaded; the news-ticker lesson’s breaking-news example is the Ministry’s own decision to teach programming in Grades 5–9; the letter-counting lesson’s example sentence says the Dome of the Rock stands in Jerusalem.

Sudan's own way (Grade 9): the whole Grade 9 book is programming — concepts and Pascal-style examples first, then algorithms and flowcharts, then a full Scratch course ending in a designed project. The Bakht Al-Ruda ICT series runs in the same pattern from Grade 4 to Grade 9.

Cuba's own way (Grade 6): Grade 6 adds the three productivity firsts in one year — digital presentations, Scratch animation with event/control blocks, and spreadsheets with formulas and charts — closing with a TIC chapter on searching and processing digital information; computer generations are taught inside Chapter 1.

Bhutan's own way (Class VIII, Literacy with ICT student book): The final primary/middle year is project-led — a full Audacity audio unit (record, edit, mix, share), information ethics taught through Copyright, Creative Commons and software piracy, and Scratch taught to game level with lists, custom blocks (functions) and logical operators.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 8 Reading Book): number systems and binary conversion open Grade 8; "physical computing" is logic gates taught through water-valve and street-lamp analogies; Scratch selection structures and mobile/smart-device apps sit in the Programming chapter.

Rwanda's own way (S2 ICT Teacher Guide, KLB): S2 moves to data protection, ICT in finance (including Rwandan mobile banking risks and biometrics), spreadsheets in depth, a second ArcGIS unit, and Scratch game design with sprite interaction tables and score/level rules. (teacher guide — evidence)

Rwanda's own way (S3 ICT Learner's Book, Fountain): the same ten units as the S3 guide taught as long screenshot-by-screenshot practicals rooted in Rwandan school life (a GS Bumbogo parents'-meeting mail merge, an administration map of Rwanda built in ArcGIS, an environmental-protection presentation, 'Rwanda Nziza' hyperlink decks), closing with a complete Scratch platform game, Hard Journey.

Rwanda's own way (S3 ICT Teacher Guide, Fountain): the S3 guide (a different publisher from the KLB S1/S2 set) organises the year into four study areas — application software, GIS/spreadsheets/presentations, graphics and multimedia, networks and programming — ending in platform game programming in Scratch. (teacher guide — evidence)

Rwanda's own way (S2 ICT Student Book, KLB): the student companion to the S2 guide — the same nine units, taught through Rwandan financial life (mobile banking, ATMs, stock exchange) and Scratch game projects (Helicopter Game, Moving Eyes Game).

Vietnam's own way (Grade 4 Tin học 4): Grade 4 is where the visual programming language first appears (its preface announces it), software legality is taught as a child's rule — only use software when you have permission — and the applied theme offers alternative Bài 12A (multimedia tools) or 12B (typing-trainer software).

Vietnam's own way (Grade 5 Tin học 5): Grade 5 splits its applied theme into two alternative tracks — track A makes digital products with graphics software, track B follows tutorial videos to make physical craft products — and its programming theme is taught in a visual block language as pure structures first (sequence, loop, branch), then variables and expressions, ending with turning a written script/storyboard into a program.

Vietnam's own way (Grade 9 Tin học 9): Grade 9 teaches simulation software (PhET, Geometer's Sketchpad) as a way to learn by experiment, and its applied fork is advanced Excel (data validation, COUNTIF/SUMIF/IF, a family-finance workbook project) versus a full video-production track in Video Editor from storyboard to export.


Pakistan's own way (Grade 6 Computer Science 6): every unit opens with Students Learning Outcomes, is dotted with "Do you Know?", "Extra Bit!" and "Tips" boxes, and closes with Summary, MCQ/short/detailed exercises, project-based and activity-based questions, with an answers key at the back. Digital skills (Windows desktop, file management, Paint 3D, a typing tutor) are taught hands-on BEFORE programming; digital citizenship is framed through Pakistani citizenship first; the entrepreneurship unit teaches from Pakistani founders (Foodpanda/EatOye, Shopsy.pk, Zero Makeup, Investors Lounge).

Pakistan's own way (Grade 7 Computer Science 7): Grade 7 opens with emerging technologies and a deep ports-and-peripherals hardware treatment, then teaches email all the way to DKIM/SPF/DMARC authentication and POP3/IMAP/SMTP protocols; binary data representation (ASCII/Unicode, RGB colour, sound sampling) is taught inside the Programming unit, and entrepreneurship becomes business-plan craft — promotion, value proposition, quality assurance, SEO and platform business models, with Twitter and Snapchat origin stories.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kobdikova G1 Digital Literacy): every keyword is given in three languages (Kazakh–Russian–English); the same national curriculum frame as the Almatykitap Grade 1 book (information etiquette / programming / robots, themes "Journey" and "Traditions and folklore") but this Arman-PV team teaches robotics on LEGO Mindstorms Education EV3 instead of WeDo 2.0, and Scratch through Kazakh folklore heroes (Aldar Kose, Makta Kyz) and national instruments (dombyra, kobyz, zhetigen); each project lesson is tiered A/B/C by difficulty.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G3 Digital Literacy): Grade 3 is built around making a whole Scratch game (scenario, scenes, characters, costumes) and an EV3 robotics project, with Word and PowerPoint taught as the project's tools; "Check yourself! Creative tasks" lessons close each section (printed pp. 29, 59, 95, 126 — assessment pages, not placed).

Kazakhstan's own way (Kobdikova G4 ICT): Grade 4 ICT deepens every primary strand at once: Scratch grows variables, logical and comparison operators on the way to "My game"; EV3 robotics is taught through its sensors (colour, ultrasonic) towards labyrinth and kegeling; video is a real subject in the NCH VideoPad editor; and the final part pairs e-mail file exchange with password reliability and "computers of the future". Augmented-reality markers (the AR+ app from arman-pv.kz) animate pictures through a phone.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukhametzhanova G6 Informatika, 2018 edition): the 2018 Atamura Grade 6 course is built around making a real computer game across two whole parts — idea, scenario, stage and characters, implementation, sound accompaniment, a start page and improvements — with vector graphics in Inkscape before it and full Word documentation after it: footnotes, hyperlinks, an automatic contents and a formal game design document that teaches copyright and citing authors.

Kazakhstan's own way (Salgaraeva & Ilyasova G6 Informatika): the Arman-PV Grade 6 book runs the full 2018 programme in numbered paragraphs with a test after every part: systems and networks, presenting information (transmission, encryption, binary coding of text), vector graphics with a "Zhailau" drawing practicum, then the two-part game build in the Scratch environment, and a final Word part that adds references, copyright and plagiarism, a referat mini-project and the game document.

Kazakhstan's own way (Isabaeva G2 «Цифровая грамотность»): six sections each built round a cross-cutting theme («Всё обо мне», «Моя семья и друзья», «Моя школа»/«Мой родной край», «В здоровом теле — здоровый дух», «Традиции и фольклор», «Окружающая среда»/«Путешествие»); every section ends in a project, and the year ends with a build-and-programme «Танцующий робот» (Dancing Robot) project in Scratch + LEGO Mindstorms EV3 (the book's own resource list names scratch.mit.edu and the Mindstorms EV3 software).

Kazakhstan's own way (Kobdikova G2 «Цифрлиқ саватлиқ»): a second, parallel Grade 2 textbook from the Arman-PV team follows the same state programme as Book 2 but re-orders it (browser search before files, two sound-editing lessons, robotics taught as «Робототехника: сенсорлар») and writes project ideas in a text editor before building them.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kobdikova G1 «Цифрлық сауаттылық»): the Arman-PV Grade 1 book teaches programming through Scratch from §6 (creating a project, the «Қош келдің, Наурыз!» project, saving/opening a project, a «Музыкалық аспаптар» project) where the Almatykitap book uses an unnamed game environment, and its safety section adds a lesson on unwanted contacts online.

Kazakhstan's own way (Grade 1 teacher guide «Цифровая грамотность. Методическое руководство» 2023 (Russian + Kazakh editions)): the 2023 guide restructures Grade 1 against the 2021 student book (Book 5): robotics disappears, replaced by a drawing section («Мой первый рисунок», taught in Tux Paint in the Kazakh plans) and a separate algorithms section (concept of algorithm, executors and their command systems, kinds of algorithms) before Scratch programming.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kobdikova G4 «Ақпараттық-коммуникациялық технологиялар»): Grade 4 ICT is five project-driven sections with cross-cutting patriotic/cultural themes: a Scratch game the pupil designs from scenario to finished game, LEGO robotics with colour and ultrasonic sensors ending in labyrinth and kegelring challenges, video making in VideoPad, presentations with sound/video/animation, and a closing «Компьютеры будущего» section that is really about sending data, e-mail attachments and password strength; AR (augmented reality) tasks run through the book via a companion CD/app.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukametzhanova G6 «Информатика»): the whole second half of Grade 6 is one long game-development arc — idea, scenario, drawing scenes and characters (vector editor), realising the scenario, sound, a title screen, improving the project — and then documenting the game properly with footnotes, hyperlinks and an automatically built table of contents.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G3 «Цифровая грамотность»): the Almatykitap Grade 3 digital literacy book is built as projects: a Scratch game built scene by scene, then a robotics project that oddly starts in Word (the project document is written and illustrated first) before the robot arm moves, and a cleaner robot is built; presentations and a photo collection close the year.

Kazakhstan's own way (Salgaraeva G6 «Информатика»): the Arman-PV Grade 6 book is a game-making year wrapped in document skills: ergonomics and wireless networks first, then binary coding and encryption, vector graphics (drawing a «Жайлау» summer pasture), a Scratch game designed, built and given a sound track and title screen — and the year ends with footnotes, hyperlinks, copyright and plagiarism, a table of contents and the written documentation for the game.
