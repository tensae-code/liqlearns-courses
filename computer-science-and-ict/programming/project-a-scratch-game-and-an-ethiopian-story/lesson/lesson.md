# Project: a Scratch game and an Ethiopian story

## Do it first

Catch, Pong, Flappy Bird, Pac-Man, Mario Bros — five famous games, all built from the blocks you now own. Your project: a game of your own, then a story Ethiopia needs told. Watch the GIF's bowl slide for its apple — that one moving bowl is a whole game in embryo. Plan first: actors, actions, stages.

## Designing the game

Design your own game in Scratch, studying existing ones — Catch, Pong, Flappy Bird, Pac-Man and Mario Bros are named. A game needs actors / sprites (an apple and a bowl for Catch), a script of actions (the bowl moving horizontally to collect falling apples), and stages that change as needed.

## Telling Ethiopian stories — and how the project is run, teacher evidence

Then storytelling: Ethiopia's touristic attractions and cultural stories, and corrective stories for younger children about problems such as drug addiction, improper walkway use in towns, and abuse of children and female students — determine characters, scenes and dialogues, and use wait and broadcast blocks to make the story interactive and shareable online. Both demand careful planning, implementation and teamwork — a problem-solving strategy in themselves. The teacher's structure for running it: the teacher first builds a project guide with a video to watch and story-building instructions — the Scratch catch-game cards for the game, and a storytelling video about UN Sustainable Development Goal 3 for the story; pupils get 3 to 5 days, with a laboratory hour arranged in their free time; they are told in advance how they will be evaluated, from a rubric the teacher develops (the ScratchEd rubric is the named model); and the project closes with a written report in word-processing software — how the group understood the problem, the design or storyboard, the division of work among members, the stages ('sprints') and scripts used, screenshots of the game or story, and the group's reflection afterwards — plus a slide presentation of the report with a live demonstration of the game to the teacher and classmates (teacher guide — evidence).
