## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The inspection — locks, updates, permissions** — The investigation asks its title question with a checklist: Is the phone **locked** — PIN, pattern or fingerprint — and does the lock engage quickly? Are its system and apps **updated**, closing the holes the news reports? Which apps hold **permissions** they do not need — a torch that reads contacts, a game that watches location? What **accounts** stay signed in, and would a thief holding the unlocked phone hold the family's bank too? Each question is answerable in the phone's own settings, which is why the lesson is an investigation and not a lecture.
2. **The repair — and the family's habits** — Then the findings are **repaired with the family's agreement**: a real lock set, permissions withdrawn one by one, updates run, the banking app behind its own lock, and a **backup** made of the photographs that exist nowhere else. The session's last work is the family's **habits**: unknown links unclicked whoever sent them, OTP codes never shared with any caller (the bank never asks — a caller who asks is the crime in progress), and the phone's owner able to say what to do in the first hour after a loss. Safety, in this lesson, is a family that has rehearsed.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — The most important computer in most families is not guarded by an IT department. It holds the banking, the photographs, the identities — and it is carried by a person who never read its settings. Grade 8 sends its learners home as that phone's first security officer. This lesson is their inspection. (lesson/lesson.md).
