# Encryption — the Caesar and Rail Fence Ciphers — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What are plaintext and ciphertext?**
**Answer: Readable data; its encrypted unreadable form**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What two things does encryption use?**
**Answer: A method and, usually, a key**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) How do substitution and transposition differ?**
**Answer: Substitution replaces letters; transposition rearranges their order**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) To which families do Caesar and Rail Fence belong?**
**Answer: Substitution; transposition**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) What are modern ciphers, in the lesson's phrase?**
**Answer: These two ideas industrialised**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) Encrypt HELLO with Caesar key 3.**
**Answer: KHOOR**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What is Caesar's weakness?**
**Answer: A tiny key space — 25 shifts to try**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) How does Rail Fence write its message?**
**Answer: In a zigzag across rails, read off row by row**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) What does Rail Fence hide, and keep?**
**Answer: Hides the order; keeps the letters themselves**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) What must real systems hide?**
**Answer: Both the letters' identities and their order**
