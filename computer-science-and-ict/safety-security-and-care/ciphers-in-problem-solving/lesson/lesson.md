# Ciphers in Problem Solving

## Do it first

A cipher alone is a trick; inside a problem it becomes a tool. Malaysia's cryptography unit closes by setting problems whose solution is a cipher at work. In this lesson you solve with ciphers — stating the need, choosing, applying, and checking that the secret survives the journey.

## Sub lesson 1 — The problem shape — need, choice, application

A cipher problem states a **situation with a secrecy need**: a message must travel through hands that must not read it, and arrive readable by its one intended reader. Solving follows the unit's whole chain: state the need (how secret, how long, whose hands), **choose** the method by the need, **apply** it exactly (encrypt letter by letter with the chosen key or table), and hand over ciphertext plus — by a separate safe path — the key.

In your mind's eye: the problem shape — need, choice, application.

## Sub lesson 2 — Checking the solution — decrypt and judge

The solution is **checked from the reader's end**: the intended reader decrypts with the key and must recover the plaintext **exactly** — one wrong letter means the application, not the idea, failed, and the encryption is reworked. Then the solver judges the holding: could an unintended hand, with the time the secret must last, break this method? If yes, the choice was wrong for the need and the problem is solved again with a stronger method. Malaysia's problems end with that judgement written down — a cipher solution is a claim, and the claim must be examined.

In your mind's eye: checking the solution — decrypt and judge.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: A situation with a secrecy need — a message that must travel unreadable and arrive readable by one reader. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: State the need, choose the method, apply it exactly, hand over ciphertext and key by separate paths. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: By a separate safe path — never with the ciphertext. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: From the reader's end — decrypt and recover the plaintext exactly. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
