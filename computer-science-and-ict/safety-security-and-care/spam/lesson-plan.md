## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What spam is** — **Spam** is **unwanted mail sent in bulk** — the same message pushed to thousands or millions of addresses that never asked for it: advertisements for worthless or forbidden goods, false prizes and lotteries, chain pleas, and mail whose sender's name and address are forged. It exists because sending costs almost nothing: if a million messages win ten buyers, the spam has paid. Every inbox pays the spam's real costs instead — in time, in storage and network load, and in the true messages buried under the flood.
2. **What spam carries, and the defences** — Spam is also a **delivery vehicle**: its attachments and links carry **malware and phishing** — the fake bank page of the chapter's scam section often arrives as spam. The defences are the user's and the system's together: the mail service's **spam filter**, which learns the flood's shapes and files it apart; the user's discipline — **never reply, never buy, never click or open attachments, never give the address carelessly**, and mark spam as spam so the filter learns. Replying is the one unforgivable act: it proves a person reads that address, and the address is then sold on as live. Laos teaches spam inside its privacy chapter because the flood begins where addresses are given away.
