# Lesson plan — Passwords — making a strong one

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Safety, Security and Care · **Lesson:** Passwords — making a strong one
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 13 — Safety, Security and Care in the Computer Science and ICT ladder (TITLES.md); stocked from Ethiopia's Grade 7 book, Unit 5 — Security and Safety of Computer. The ladder is the recommended order, never a forced one.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a password is a sequence of characters that verifies who you are — the first line of defence — and a strong one is unique to the account, at least 8 characters mixing lowercase, uppercase, numbers and symbols, told to no one.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the accounts lesson — the password is the account's guard, taken up on its own because the book gives it its own teaching.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: User accounts — Administrator, Standard, Guest.  Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define a password and say what it is the first line of.
2. Give the three strong-password rules.
3. Build a password that passes all three rules.
4. Explain the unlocked-door comparison.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil — never write down a real password you use; practise with invented ones.

## If the learner is stuck
- Uses a name or a birthday → guessable by anyone who knows you; strength is being hard to guess, and the mix is how.
- Uses one password everywhere → the book's first rule is a unique password for the account.
