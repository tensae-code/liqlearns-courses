**Acceptance count: taught in 8 countries (Ethiopia, Tanzania, China, Sudan, Laos, Greece, Moldova, Kazakhstan).**

Grade 10 growth added 2026-11-12 from the Grade 10 student book, Unit 5, and the Grade 10 teacher guide's Unit 5 review key (printed pp. 101–102), which also served as a verified answer source for this build's Grade 10 Unit 5 question files (the guide keys every unit's objective items — Unit 5 at printed p. 101, Unit 6 at printed p. 116). Book error flagged in the unit's student placements and handled here: the Unit 5 figure sequence in the student book jumps to “Figure 5.14” for its cyber-attack picture; no lesson in this growth cites that figure number — the picture's content is taught, the wrong number is not.

Sudan's own way (Grade 6): the final primary book is built around real Sudanese systems — networks and cloud first, then a full security unit taught through named children's everyday internet dilemmas, and a worked example of the ipSchools school-management system (with a real Bakht Al-Ruda school results table) before closing with computers in banks and police work.

Laos's own way (Grade 10 ICT student book): the upper-secondary course turns conceptual — a full information-systems syllabus (organisations, TPS/MIS/DSS/EIS/expert/office systems, the development life cycle), then the series' deepest security chapter (firewalls, cookies, spyware, spam, employee monitoring, privacy law), closing with artificial intelligence, with DOS and Unix taught alongside Windows in the senior years' background.

Greece's own way (Grade 7, Pliroforiki A' Gymnasiou, 2024): the new Gymnasium informatics course is built around algorithms from the first page (a Python binary example in Unit 1), then hardware/software, the internet with a full copyright/Creative Commons/AI-copyright sequence, cybersecurity with cryptography, word processing, algorithmics, Scratch storytelling/chatbot projects, and a long society unit (robots, drones, autonomous cars, e-government, AI in education/medicine).

Moldova's own way (Grade 7, Informatică): lower-secondary Informatica is a real textbook discipline, not computer-skills practice — number systems, text/image/sound encoding and data types in Pascal sit alongside GUI literacy, and two full chapters are given to behaviour, law and information culture in virtual spaces.


Kazakhstan's own way (Salgaraeva G10 Informatika, natural-science track): in Grade 10 the subject splits by track; this natural-science book is the deepest in the set — networks with IP, domains and VPN, number systems and logic with truth tables and the computer's logical elements, procedures/functions, strings, files, sorting methods and graph algorithms, a full HTML/CSS/scripts web part, and information systems from Big Data to linking web pages with a database. Every topic is doubled with a practicum paragraph.

Kazakhstan's own way (Salgaraeva G10 Informatika, humanities track): the humanities-track Grade 10 book replaces programming and databases with media production: a short information-security part, then shooting and editing video as a project, design theory ("good design", graphics for the web, developing a site's design), and building, publishing and promoting a real website. Each part closes with sample summative-assessment tasks.

Kazakhstan's own way (Zhunusov G10 Computer Science, English-medium): the English-medium Grade 10 book is the most technical of the wave: the CIA triad and three separate authentication lessons (passwords twice, biometrics), encoding standards, Python dictionaries, DFS/BFS graph traversal, and a full SQL chapter in MySQL (CREATE, INSERT, SELECT/WHERE, MIN/MAX, JOIN, GROUP BY, SELECT INTO) before a JavaScript chapter. (Its preface is the publisher's generic science template, left over from another book.)

Kazakhstan's own way (Ataeva & Vyshkova G8 Informatics, NIS): the NIS Grade 8 course is the most distinctive of the wave: vector graphics flows into geographic information systems (planning an excursion, making maps, visualising data in GIS), spreadsheets are used to solve logical problems, loops are taught through mathematics, physics, business and multimedia problems, and the year ends with a full project cycle — six stages, presentation, documentation, defence — plus a mini-project on artificial intelligence.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kolyeva G10 Informatika, humanities track): Mektep's humanities-track Grade 10 book mirrors the Arman-PV one (information security, video content, design theory, web design) and adds a fifth part no other humanities book has: artificial intelligence and blockchain — machine-learning principles, how neural networks are organised and work, the fields where AI is used, and blockchain technology.

Kazakhstan's own way (Heathcote ICT Grade 10): an imported British ICT course (Express Publishing's Grammar Schools series) adapted for Kazakhstan — ten modules that include two topics the national books do not teach at all: expert and control systems, and developing a mobile app.
