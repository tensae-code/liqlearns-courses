# Lesson plan — Choosing the Best Cipher Method

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Safety, Security and Care · **Lesson:** Choosing the Best Cipher Method
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Malaysia (2026-11-14) — in the title Safety, Security and Care on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Malaysia's student books (Kementerian Pendidikan Malaysia), because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree and live lesson content were checked for the slug and for near matches before building.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** How to choose the best cipher method for a purpose — matching the method's strength, ease and cost to what the message needs (Malaysia, Tingkatan 3)
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The choice starts from the message** — Choosing begins with the **message's needs**: how secret must it stay, and **for how long** (a surprise party's secret must last days; an exam paper's, until the exam); who must use the method — children by hand, or a bank's computers; and what a mistake would cost. A method stronger than the need wastes effort and invites use-errors; a method weaker than the need is not a protection at all. The needs are written first, because 'best' has no meaning until they are stated.
2. **Matching method to need — and defending it** — The choice is then a **match**: read the comparison table against the written needs and pick the method whose strengths cover the needs and whose weaknesses the purpose can bear. Malaysia's form for the answer is a **defence**: the chosen method, the need it answers, and the rejected methods with the reason each was rejected. A choice that cannot be defended in that form has not been made — it has been guessed. The unit's deepest point is that security is this judgement, repeated for every message.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
