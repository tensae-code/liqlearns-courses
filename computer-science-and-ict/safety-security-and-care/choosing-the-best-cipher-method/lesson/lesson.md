# Choosing the Best Cipher Method

## Do it first

Compared methods still leave a decision: which one for this message? Malaysia's unit ends the comparison in a choice, defended by the message's needs. In this lesson you learn to make that choice — best means best for this purpose, not best in general.

## Sub lesson 1 — The choice starts from the message

Choosing begins with the **message's needs**: how secret must it stay, and **for how long** (a surprise party's secret must last days; an exam paper's, until the exam); who must use the method — children by hand, or a bank's computers; and what a mistake would cost. A method stronger than the need wastes effort and invites use-errors; a method weaker than the need is not a protection at all. The needs are written first, because 'best' has no meaning until they are stated.

In your mind's eye: the choice starts from the message.

## Sub lesson 2 — Matching method to need — and defending it

The choice is then a **match**: read the comparison table against the written needs and pick the method whose strengths cover the needs and whose weaknesses the purpose can bear. Malaysia's form for the answer is a **defence**: the chosen method, the need it answers, and the rejected methods with the reason each was rejected. A choice that cannot be defended in that form has not been made — it has been guessed. The unit's deepest point is that security is this judgement, repeated for every message.

In your mind's eye: matching method to need — and defending it.
