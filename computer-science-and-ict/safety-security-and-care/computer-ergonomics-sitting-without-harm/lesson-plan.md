# Lesson plan — Computer ergonomics — sitting without harming your body

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Safety, Security and Care · **Lesson:** Computer ergonomics — sitting without harming your body
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 13 — Safety, Security and Care in the Computer Science and ICT ladder (TITLES.md); stocked from Ethiopia's Grade 8 book, Unit 5 — Computer Ergonomics. The ladder is the recommended order, never a forced one.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** computer ergonomics is the study of how users and their bodies interact with a computer, so the health risks of improper sitting are made as small as possible — and bad sitting has named harms.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Grade 7 safety lessons — the learner already knows wrong use can hurt the user; this lesson names the sitting harms and the injury behind them.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Caring for a computer — safety before cleaning (Grade 7 safety). Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what computer ergonomics deals with, and why it matters.
2. Name the harms of inappropriate seating: muscle contraction, pain, injury and backaches.
3. Explain an overuse injury — where it strikes (elbow, wrist, hand) and its five symptoms (teacher guide — evidence).
4. Judge two sitting pictures and pick the appropriate sitting style.

## What you need
No equipment for this lesson — eyes, a chair, and your own body to check against.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner sits at a desk the way they usually sit, notices where their body feels strain, and looks at two sitting pictures to pick the appropriate one (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks ergonomics is about the computer, not the body → it deals with the interaction of users and their bodies with a computer; the machine is only half of it.
- Cannot feel any harm in their own sitting → harms build over many hours a day, often without the user knowing the consequences; that is why the pictures and the symptom list matter.
