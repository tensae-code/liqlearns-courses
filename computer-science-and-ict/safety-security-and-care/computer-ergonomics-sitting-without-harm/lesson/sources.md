**Acceptance count: taught in 4 countries (Ethiopia, Mozambique, Afghanistan, Uzbekistan).**

Afghanistan's own way (Grade 11): grade 11 is the health-and-PowerPoint year — a full chapter on computer health and strain-relief exercises, then Word finished off (Format, Insert, Edit) before a five-lesson PowerPoint block and a first step onto the Web.

The student book prints Activity 5.2 before Activity 5.1 (printed p. 82) — the activities are taught here in the book's intended order, 5.1 then 5.2, and the swap is flagged here rather than taught. The Unit 5 answer key in the teacher guide (printed pp. 47–48) was used as the verified-answer check for this unit's question files. The teacher guide's Unit 5 period figures contradict each other: its overview says "seven (5) periods", its unit table lists only three sub-topics totalling 5 periods and omits §5.2 Workstation Ergonomics, and its four body sections are allotted 2+1+1+2 = 6 periods. That contradiction is filed here as an open discrepancy — none of its figures is taught as settled in this lesson.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Grade 9): one TIA teacher guide carries two ICT subjects side by side — Communication Technology (networks, ergonomics, safety, societal impact) and Computer Technology (architecture, software) — organised by content standards and numbered benchmarks (9.4.x / 9.5.x), with the design brief used as the problem-solving method for both a product and a simple network, and typing assessed at 20 wpm with 80% accuracy. (teacher guide — evidence, PARTIAL)


Uzbekistan's own way (Grade 5): informatics opens with a full lesson of safety and hygiene before the machine is even described, and Paint gets five of the seventeen lessons — drawing is the grade-5 child's main creative tool, with keyboard training on a typing trainer in between. The Boltaev-team informatics line runs from keyboard trainers and Paint in Grades 5–6 to a full Pascal course in Grades 8–9; this book is its Russian-language edition.

Uzbekistan's own way (Grade 8): grade 8 is theory-first — number systems, coding and Boolean logic before the machine's own story — then a full operating-system chapter and a five-lesson Word course; safety rules return as a closing appendix.

Cuba's own way (Grade 2): Play-based (lúdico) introduction through the graficador (Paint-type drawing program) and educational software; correct workstation posture/ergonomics is taught explicitly in Grade 2, and files/folders are introduced intuitively through saving and opening drawings in a class-group folder. (teacher guide — evidence)
