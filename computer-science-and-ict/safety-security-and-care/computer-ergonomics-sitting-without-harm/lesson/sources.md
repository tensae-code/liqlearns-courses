**Acceptance count: taught in 9 countries (Ethiopia, Mozambique, Afghanistan, Uzbekistan, Fiji, Bhutan, Mongolia, Sri Lanka, Moldova).**

Afghanistan's own way (Grade 11): grade 11 is the health-and-PowerPoint year — a full chapter on computer health and strain-relief exercises, then Word finished off (Format, Insert, Edit) before a five-lesson PowerPoint block and a first step onto the Web.

The student book prints Activity 5.2 before Activity 5.1 (printed p. 82) — the activities are taught here in the book's intended order, 5.1 then 5.2, and the swap is flagged here rather than taught. The Unit 5 answer key in the teacher guide (printed pp. 47–48) was used as the verified-answer check for this unit's question files. The teacher guide's Unit 5 period figures contradict each other: its overview says "seven (5) periods", its unit table lists only three sub-topics totalling 5 periods and omits §5.2 Workstation Ergonomics, and its four body sections are allotted 2+1+1+2 = 6 periods. That contradiction is filed here as an open discrepancy — none of its figures is taught as settled in this lesson.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Grade 9): one TIA teacher guide carries two ICT subjects side by side — Communication Technology (networks, ergonomics, safety, societal impact) and Computer Technology (architecture, software) — organised by content standards and numbered benchmarks (9.4.x / 9.5.x), with the design brief used as the problem-solving method for both a product and a simple network, and typing assessed at 20 wpm with 80% accuracy. (teacher guide — evidence, PARTIAL)


Uzbekistan's own way (Grade 5): informatics opens with a full lesson of safety and hygiene before the machine is even described, and Paint gets five of the seventeen lessons — drawing is the grade-5 child's main creative tool, with keyboard training on a typing trainer in between. The Boltaev-team informatics line runs from keyboard trainers and Paint in Grades 5–6 to a full Pascal course in Grades 8–9; this book is its Russian-language edition.

Uzbekistan's own way (Grade 8): grade 8 is theory-first — number systems, coding and Boolean logic before the machine's own story — then a full operating-system chapter and a five-lesson Word course; safety rules return as a closing appendix.

Cuba's own way (Grade 2): Play-based (lúdico) introduction through the graficador (Paint-type drawing program) and educational software; correct workstation posture/ergonomics is taught explicitly in Grade 2, and files/folders are introduced intuitively through saving and opening drawings in a class-group folder. (teacher guide — evidence)

Fiji's own way (Years 7–8 Computer Education student book): A practical, exercise-driven primary book built around a class "students folder" of set files (work is saved as U<unit>-Ex<n>-<FirstName>); local Fijian names and schools throughout (e.g. Babasiga Secondary School), Internet searching is practised on the "Map of Fiji" with kids' search engines, and a full unit covers computers in Fijian society including Braille keyboards for blind learners. PARTIAL: the PDF as received has no cover, title page, contents or imprint — the PARTIAL label travels with every citation of this book.

Fiji's own way (Year 11 Computer Education student book): The first locally written text of its kind (the book's Preface): written by a panel of Computer Studies officers and University of the South Pacific lecturers for the new Year 11 Computer Education syllabus trialled in 2014; each chapter uses "Discovery Learning" prompts, and the database chapter is a full Access 2007 practical building a Student database in a "Lab Chapter 3" folder.

Bhutan's own way (Class IV, Literacy with ICT student book): Competency-based "Literacy with ICT" (not procedure drill): every chapter opens with learning objectives, mixes off-machine "Try This" with "Try This On Machine", and closes with Now You Know / Check Your Progress / Explore Further; content is localised (Bhutanese names, kuenselonline, education.gov.bt, Bhutan's 1999 Internet start) and one class per week across the year.

Bhutan's own way (Class VI, Literacy with ICT student book): School computing runs on Google's G Suite for Education — email is Gmail on the school account, collaboration is Google Classroom, and the end-of-year food-chain project is agreed in Classroom, built in Word, password-protected, cited, filed in folders and reported to the teacher by email.

Mongolia's own way (Grade 6): ICT is built on Mongolian examples throughout — information and objects taught through the Mazaalai (Gobi bear), the ger (with a paper origami model), Mongolian pattern (khee) motifs and the national flag; touch typing is taught on the Mongolian Cyrillic keyboard layout with local tutor programs (MonTT, Fast type); web work uses Mongolian sites (surguuli.edu.mn, kids.gogo.mn, tsag-agaar.mn).

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 6 Reading Book): a story/dialogue-led "Reading Book" with a talking computer character; every chapter cross-refers to numbered Workbook activities; local sectors (school office, ATM, ICU, factories, agriculture) and the 3R e-waste method are taught from Grade 6.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 11, Tamil): a full Visual Basic 6.0 programming course inside the O/L book (IDE, controls, data types, Select Case, arrays), an SDLC chapter, Photoshop-based multimedia, hand-coded HTML with Sri Lankan examples (the Sinharaja rainforest page, ucsc.cmb.ac.lk addresses, sltnet.lk domains), and sector chapters (agriculture, industry, government, commerce) before malware and health.

Moldova's own way (Grade 7, Informatică): lower-secondary Informatica is a real textbook discipline, not computer-skills practice — number systems, text/image/sound encoding and data types in Pascal sit alongside GUI literacy, and two full chapters are given to behaviour, law and information culture in virtual spaces.
