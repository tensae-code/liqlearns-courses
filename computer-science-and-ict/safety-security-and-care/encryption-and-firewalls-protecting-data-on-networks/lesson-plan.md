## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Encryption — scrambling the data itself** — **Encryption of information** turns readable data into a **scrambled form** before it travels, so anyone who intercepts it on the network holds something they **cannot read**; only the intended receiver, holding the means to unscramble, restores it. Encryption protects the **data itself**, wherever it goes — the letter is sealed, not just the road guarded. It answers the network's first danger: listening and copying in transit.
2. **The firewall — guarding the gate** — The **firewall** guards the **gate** between a network and the outside: it **examines traffic passing in and out** and **allows or blocks it by its rules**, so unwanted connections are turned away before they reach the machines inside. The two defences are partners, not alternatives: the firewall decides **what may pass**, encryption decides **what passing data reveals**. Libya teaches them in one section because a network needs both answers at once.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
