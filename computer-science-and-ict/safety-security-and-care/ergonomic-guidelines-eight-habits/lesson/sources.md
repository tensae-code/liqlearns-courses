**Acceptance count: taught in 3 countries (Ethiopia, Bhutan, Kazakhstan).**

The teacher guide's Unit 5 answer key (printed pp. 47–48) was used as the verified-answer check for the Unit 5 question files. The teacher guide's Unit 5 period figures contradict each other: its overview says "seven (5) periods", its unit table lists only three sub-topics totalling 5 periods and omits §5.2 Workstation Ergonomics, and its four body sections are allotted 2+1+1+2 = 6 periods. That contradiction is filed here as an open discrepancy — none of its figures is taught as settled in this lesson.

Bhutan's own way (Class VI, Literacy with ICT student book): School computing runs on Google's G Suite for Education — email is Gmail on the school account, collaboration is Google Classroom, and the end-of-year food-chain project is agreed in Classroom, built in Word, password-protected, cited, filed in folders and reported to the teacher by email.


Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G5 Informatika): Grade 5 Informatika by the Almatykitap team drops Scratch entirely: information theory first (transmission, encryption, binary, graphics in binary code), then raster work in Paint against vector work with curves, and half the year is EV3 robotics beyond simple movement — the gyroscopic sensor, line following and robo-sumo competitions.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kobdikova G5 Informatika): the Arman-PV Grade 5 book teaches the same five national sections as the other two publishers, but every second paragraph is a practicum or a "creating a project" lesson, and the safety section ends in cloud skills — placing, changing and downloading shared-access files — plus mini-projects on setting passwords on documents and preparing a document for print. The vector editor is Inkscape (named in the glossary), the raster editor Paint.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukhametzhanova G5 Informatika, 2020 edition): the Atamura team's 2020 Grade 5 book follows the same five national sections in numbered-paragraph form (1.1–5.5) with end-of-section final tasks; its graphics strand works in Paint for raster and Inkscape for vector (canvas size, SVG, Bezier pen), and the introduction also promises a first taste of 3D technologies (3D model, 3D editor, 3D printing) inside the graphics work.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G6 Informatika): Grade 6 by the Almatykitap team drops the Scratch-game strand of the 2018 books: after systems/networks (including a lesson on Internet addiction) it teaches 3D modelling and 3D printing, a first Python course (IDE, syntax, data types, input/output, linear programs), and academic Word work — footnotes, hyperlinks, an automatic table of contents, copyright and plagiarism, and how to write a referat.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukhametzhanova G6 Informatika, 2018 edition): the 2018 Atamura Grade 6 course is built around making a real computer game across two whole parts — idea, scenario, stage and characters, implementation, sound accompaniment, a start page and improvements — with vector graphics in Inkscape before it and full Word documentation after it: footnotes, hyperlinks, an automatic contents and a formal game design document that teaches copyright and citing authors.

Kazakhstan's own way (Salgaraeva & Ilyasova G6 Informatika): the Arman-PV Grade 6 book runs the full 2018 programme in numbered paragraphs with a test after every part: systems and networks, presenting information (transmission, encryption, binary coding of text), vector graphics with a "Zhailau" drawing practicum, then the two-part game build in the Scratch environment, and a final Word part that adds references, copyright and plagiarism, a referat mini-project and the game document.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukhametzhanova G5 Informatika, 2017 edition): the 2017 Atamura Grade 5 book follows the older national programme shared with the Arman-PV book of the same year: computer safety, information and raster graphics, algorithms through labyrinths (real and virtual), a first Scratch program and "living graphics", and project work ending in a print-ready document; a full Scratch block dictionary is printed as an appendix (printed pp. 119–127). The book was written with Microsoft "Partnership in Education" material.

Kazakhstan's own way (Salgaraeva & Kopeeva G6 Informatika): the Arman-PV Grade 6 book follows the newer programme (systems and networks, 3D printing, Python, academic Word work) in 30 paragraphs with a test after each section; the 3D editor is SketchUp (the book's resource list points to gsketchup.ru and python.org), the history of computing is split into two paragraphs, and almost every topic is doubled with a "creating a project" paragraph.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukhametzhanova G6 Informatika, 2020 edition): the 2020 Atamura Grade 6 book drops the game project of its 2018 predecessor and follows the newer programme in four parts; its 3D strand is taught in Paint 3D (with Remix 3D models, the Mixed Reality Viewer and 3D-printer safety rules), and the Python part works in IDLE with type conversion (int/float) given its own space.

Kazakhstan's own way (Vyshkova & Zhurbenko G7 Informatics, NIS): the Nazarbayev Intellectual Schools course is a different programme from the national one: business documents, databases inside the information-processing part, logo design and GIF animation in raster graphics, a whole chapter on modelling the world (including forecasting social problems from data), client–server networks, utilities, and linear-structure programming ending in building your own calculator. Lessons run two pages each with research-style tasks.

Kazakhstan's own way (Ataeva & Vyshkova G8 Informatics, NIS): the NIS Grade 8 course is the most distinctive of the wave: vector graphics flows into geographic information systems (planning an excursion, making maps, visualising data in GIS), spreadsheets are used to solve logical problems, loops are taught through mathematics, physics, business and multimedia problems, and the year ends with a full project cycle — six stages, presentation, documentation, defence — plus a mini-project on artificial intelligence.
