## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The computer level and the network level** — At the **computer level**, security guards **one machine**: its accounts and passwords, its updated programs, its antivirus, who may sit at it. At the **network level**, security guards **the travelling and the gates**: the connections' protection, the **firewall** deciding what may pass, the watching for attacks aimed at many machines at once. A fault at one level is not excused by the other: a guarded network carrying an unguarded laptop is a wall with the door open.
2. **The information-systems level — people, data and rules** — At the **information-system level**, security guards the **whole working system** — its **data** (who may read, change and delete what, with backups when the worst happens), its **people** (their training, their duties, the rules they sign), and its **procedures** (what happens on the bad day, decided before it comes). The unit's application rule closes the levels: real security is **all three rings at once**, because attackers choose the weakest ring, and the levels are a checklist for finding it before they do.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

1. **Do it first** — Grade 7's cybersecurity unit does not leave 'be secure' as one fog. It levels it: what must be secured on this machine, what on the network between machines, and what on the whole system of people and data — three rings, each with its own guards. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of the computer level and the network level as the lesson describes it — the student studies the picture and names what each part shows.
