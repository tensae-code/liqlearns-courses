**Acceptance count: taught in 14 countries (Ethiopia, Afghanistan, Saudi Arabia, Iraq, Bhutan, Libya, Mongolia, Sri Lanka, Rwanda, Laos, Indonesia, South Sudan, Pakistan, Kazakhstan).**

Afghanistan's own way (Grade 12): the final year turns outward — computers in society (e-commerce, communications, even military systems), system software and virus protection, then Excel for the first time and a closing block on the Web, e-mail and browsers (taught through Internet Explorer).

Mauritania's own way (Grade 8): The second year is organised as five applied units of 35 thirty-minute fiches — image processing first (Paint/GIMP/Photoshop, bitmap vs vector), then internet ethics, a full Excel data-processing unit ending in a practical project, web source-checking and responsible downloading, and finally email — each unit closed by evaluation and support fiches with full answer keys at the end.

Saudi Arabia's own way (Grade 3, continuing education): a workbook-style book for the continuing-education (adult/lifelong learning) track — every one of the 16 lessons opens with "I want to learn" objectives and closes with written exercises, and everything is taught on an older Windows desktop (Start menu, "My Computer", Recycle Bin) with Notepad as the only application, worked through step-by-step practicals in the school lab.

Iraq's own way (Grade 10): the preparatory stage turns practical and examinable — a whole unit on computer maintenance (preventive vs corrective, hardware and software, hard-disk management with defragmentation, ScanDisk, partitions and FAT32 vs NTFS), then PowerPoint and Microsoft Publisher taught tab by tab, looping control structures in Scratch, and a final unit pairing HTML web-page building with information security.

Bhutan's own way (Class VIII, Literacy with ICT student book): The final primary/middle year is project-led — a full Audacity audio unit (record, edit, mix, share), information ethics taught through Copyright, Creative Commons and software piracy, and Scratch taught to game level with lists, custom blocks (functions) and logical operators.

Libya's own way (Grade 12 Humanities, Information Technology student book): the Humanities final year pairs the social/legal side of ICT (data security, e-government, computer law and software licences) with a long multimedia-and-PowerPoint practical; the closing projects are Libyan — a deck about Libyan dishes and a deck about Libya's archaeological cities and landmarks.

Libya's own way (Grade 12 Science, Information Technology student book): the Science section shares the Humanities book's first three chapters (security, e-government, computer law) word-for-structure, then diverges into a full second Visual Basic course — the VB environment and form controls, arrays, subroutines and built-in functions, and the remaining toolbox controls. Visual Basic is the Grade 12 programming language in both sections.

Mongolia's own way (Grade 7): the whole applications half is one long project — research and produce a printed booklet "Усаа хайрлая" (Cherish the Water) in Word, a Paint.NET layered picture, and a class PowerPoint; image work uses the free Paint.NET (downloaded from getpaint.net) rather than MS Paint; the e-communication chapter teaches Yahoo mail and Yahoo Messenger, Mongolian history of the örtöö relay post and the @ sign, and makes correct Latin transliteration of Mongolian Cyrillic (standard MNS 5217:2003) an ethics topic in its own right.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 11, Tamil): a full Visual Basic 6.0 programming course inside the O/L book (IDE, controls, data types, Select Case, arrays), an SDLC chapter, Photoshop-based multimedia, hand-coded HTML with Sri Lankan examples (the Sinharaja rainforest page, ucsc.cmb.ac.lk addresses, sltnet.lk domains), and sector chapters (agriculture, industry, government, commerce) before malware and health.

Rwanda's own way (S2 ICT Teacher Guide, KLB): S2 moves to data protection, ICT in finance (including Rwandan mobile banking risks and biometrics), spreadsheets in depth, a second ArcGIS unit, and Scratch game design with sprite interaction tables and score/level rules. (teacher guide — evidence)

Rwanda's own way (S2 ICT Student Book, KLB): the student companion to the S2 guide — the same nine units, taught through Rwandan financial life (mobile banking, ATMs, stock exchange) and Scratch game projects (Helicopter Game, Moving Eyes Game).

Laos's own way (Grade 10 ICT student book): the upper-secondary course turns conceptual — a full information-systems syllabus (organisations, TPS/MIS/DSS/EIS/expert/office systems, the development life cycle), then the series' deepest security chapter (firewalls, cookies, spyware, spam, employee monitoring, privacy law), closing with artificial intelligence, with DOS and Unix taught alongside Windows in the senior years' background.

Laos's own way (Grade 12 ICT student book): the final year is a modern, applied course — problem-solving method, a long English-titled internet-safety sequence (Internet Safety, Email Tips for Scams and Spam, Smart Social Networking, Cyber harassment/Stalking/Addiction), data summaries and PivotTables for decision-making, copyright and Creative Commons licensing, a full PowerPoint course, and choosing technology for your future occupation.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 10 G10 Informatika student): Senior-high Informatika opens with real data work — multi-variable search, data collection by Python web scraping in Google Colab (pandas/Plotly) — then algorithms chosen for real-life problems, office automation, IT careers/certifications, and information security with classical ciphers.

South Sudan's own way (Grade 9 S1 Student): Opens ICT at secondary level entirely through doing — the whole first year is Microsoft Office application skills (Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Publisher, Paint) taught as step-by-step keyboard/mouse procedures with local data (South Sudan state populations, local disease statistics for charts), then internet use, hands-on troubleshooting, and Windows access control/security settings.


Pakistan's own way (Grade 8 Computer Education 8): the Grade 8 book is the older National-Curriculum-2006 Punjab book (not SNC): it teaches networks through physical media and cutting-edge wireless (cellular, satellite, GPS, Bluetooth), a whole unit of hands-on antivirus management (definitions, updates, an AVG whole-computer scan), Excel 2007 taught through three full worked reports (monthly expenses, salaries, school results), and programming in GW-BASIC with line numbers — the pre-SNC Punjab way into code.

Pakistan's own way (Grade 11 Computer Science and Entrepreneurship 11): the new-curriculum Grade 11 book runs the whole arc from making software to selling it: SDLC with UML and design patterns, a full Python course (through OOP, files and exceptions), algorithms with complexity classes (P, NP, NP-hard), data analytics with regression and clustering, cloud and blockchain, and a closing entrepreneurship unit — every unit grounded in Pakistani examples (Daraz, Foodpanda, Careem, Pakola, NADRA, the Personal Data Protection Bill, NR3C-FIA).

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G7 «Информатика»): the Almatykitap Grade 7 course pairs every theory section with «Творческие задания» and test tasks, teaches spreadsheets up to conditional formatting and process modelling, and does its Python through files: reading/writing files and file functions come before branching, nested and compound conditions.

Kazakhstan's own way (Zhunusov G7 «INFORMATICS»): the Astana-kitap Grade 7 English-medium book teaches spreadsheets in Google Sheets, files and archiving before networks, Python file work at the start of the programming chapter, and a practical chapter that builds three named games — a quiz, Rock Paper Scissors, a guessing game and a Camel game — each taken from problem statement to testing.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G7 «Информатика» 2019 edition): the 2019 Almatykitap Grade 7 book is not the 2023 Python book (Book 15): after measurement, networks and spreadsheets it teaches programming through the Lazarus project interface — interface elements, data types, branching, nested and compound conditions — and ends with 3D modelling of objects and events (house, car, table, chair, window, glass as the final tasks).

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukametzhanova G7 «Информатика» 2017 edition): the Atamura Grade 7 book runs the same state programme as the Almatykitap one (Book 27) but adds a unit on converting between units of information, teaches the programming in C++, and likewise ends in 3D modelling of objects and events.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukametzhanova G7 «Информатика» 2021 edition): the 2021 Atamura revision of the Grade 7 book drops the 2017 edition's 3D modelling and C++, and follows the renewed programme instead: Python from files and branching through nested and compound conditions and choice, ending in a practical programming section.
