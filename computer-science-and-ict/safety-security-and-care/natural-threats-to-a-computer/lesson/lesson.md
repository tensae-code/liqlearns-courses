# Natural threats to a computer

## Do it first

Smoke in the computer laboratory. What do you save first — yourself, or the machine in front of you? Answer honestly, then read what the book orders, and the laboratory procedure that goes with it.

## Fire, temperature, lightning

Earthquakes, flooding and lightning storms lead to fires, extreme temperatures and electric shocks, causing physical damage and loss of data. Fire: flames damage directly, heat melts sensitive components, and smoke damages the CPU fan, causing overheating — and in a fire, get yourself to safety before rescuing the computer. Extreme temperature: computers work within a moderate range; excessive heat or cold malfunctions or breaks components, and after drastic changes the machine should return to room temperature before use. Lightning: a strike need not touch the hardware — it creates an electric surge, a spike, through the power supply that can destroy crucial components, including the motherboard.

## Fire in the computer laboratory — teacher evidence

The teacher's keyed procedure is lab-specific. On a fire outbreak in the lab or server room: switch off the main electrical supply; vacate the room; inform the person in charge — the laboratory coordinator or teacher; call for help; and try to extinguish only with the appropriate fire extinguisher, never water. Prevention: keep the computer room free of stored combustible material — boxes, packaging and manuals are stored elsewhere; inspect power cords and repair frayed or damaged ones at once; keep systems cleaned of dust, because dust is kindling; train everyone and display the fire emergency plan; and schedule regular inspection of the fire protection systems. The consequences are stated plainly: computers do not survive fires — if the flames spare the case and boards, the heat can melt the hard drive and the solder — and a power surge can damage the motherboard and hard drive and slow performance.
