# Social media — what it gives a student, what it costs

## Do it first

Hold a balance in your mind. On one side, everything social media gives you. On the other, everything it takes. Which side is heavier for a student — and does the answer change at examination time? This lesson is the teacher's full keyed balance sheet, both sides at full length.

## What it gives

Connectivity with people worldwide — new friends, shared thoughts, family kept close. Collaboration, especially for shy pupils who find it easier to work with peers online than to speak in class. Plentiful helpful information. Learning, including from home in a pandemic, and from teachers who help on social media. And entertainment and stress relief, including games and the pleasure of comments on one's posts.

## What it costs — and the two sharings never to make

Addiction that distracts from study, with pupils scrolling feeds and posting instead of attending. Damage to close relationships, as distant friendships displace family and nearby friends. Cyberbullying through anonymous and fake accounts, bringing anxiety, depression and stress. Inappropriate content, including pornography, reaching underage pupils intentionally or by accident while gaming or scrolling — with monitoring named as the parents' responsibility. And health harm from day-and-night sitting — obesity and sacrificed sleep leading to sleep disorders. The same answer keys two sharings never to make: personal information — address, photo, phone number, birthday — raises the risk of identity theft, stalking and harassment, and pictures can be copied, altered and spread without consent unless privacy settings limit access; and sharing passwords invites hacked accounts and misused information.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: social media gives a student connectivity, collaboration, information, learning and entertainment — and can cost study, close relationships, safety, innocence and health. Then name the key words of this lesson — connectivity, collaboration online, addiction, the two sharings never to make — and say what each one means.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write your own balance sheet for one week of your social media use.
2. Say which harm is easiest to miss, and why.
3. Explain how privacy settings limit the copying and spreading of pictures.
