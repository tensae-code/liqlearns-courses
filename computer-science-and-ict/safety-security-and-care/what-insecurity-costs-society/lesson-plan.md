# Lesson plan — What insecurity costs — impact on society

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Safety, Security and Care · **Lesson:** What insecurity costs — impact on society
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Information and Computer Security (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** security risks come from intentional or unintentional misuse of computers, network resources and the internet — and what they cost is hardware, software, confidential information, data integrity, and the availability of the network itself.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Grade 10 opening lesson of this unit — the learner knows what information security and computer security defend. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or in steps — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of this course, especially Information security and computer security (the Grade 10 growth of What security and computer security mean). Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the two sources of security risk: intentional and unintentional misuse.
2. List the five costs of violated security: hardware, software, confidential information, data integrity, availability.
3. Say what hackers range over, from stealing passwords to remote control.
4. Describe what an unsecured school system suffers (teacher guide — evidence).
5. State that violating information and computer security is punishable.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Counts only broken machines → walk the five costs one by one and ask, for each: does the school feel this one?
- Thinks only attacks count → unintentional misuse is in the book's own first line: a careless act opens the same door.
