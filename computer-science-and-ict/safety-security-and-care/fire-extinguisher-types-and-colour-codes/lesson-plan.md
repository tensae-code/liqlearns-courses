## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Fire classes and extinguisher types** — Fires are classed by **what is burning** — ordinary materials, flammable liquids, gases, and **electrical equipment**, the class a computer lab fears most. Each **extinguisher type** is filled for certain classes: **water** cools ordinary fires but must never touch electricity or liquid fuel; **foam** smothers liquids; **dry powder** knocks down most classes; and **carbon dioxide (CO₂)** — the lab's extinguisher — smothers electrical fires **without leaving residue** on the machines.
2. **Colour codes — reading the extinguisher at a glance** — Because a fire leaves no time to read manuals, extinguishers carry a **colour code**: the body or its band announces the type — **red for water**, **cream for foam**, **blue for dry powder**, **black for CO₂** (the standard coding the guide teaches). The drill the guide intends: read the colour **before** you lift it, match it to the fire's class, and if no colour matches the fire, **get out and raise the alarm instead** — the code's last lesson is knowing when not to fight.
