# Comparing Cipher Methods — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What shape do all the unit's methods share?**
**Answer: A rule that turns plaintext into ciphertext, with some key or pattern**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) How does a shift (Caesar) method work?**
**Answer: It moves every letter by the same number of places**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) How does a substitution method differ?**
**Answer: Each letter is swapped for another by a fixed table — not necessarily in alphabet order**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What does a transposition method change?**
**Answer: The letters' order — the letters themselves are kept**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) Why does comparison start with the working?**
**Answer: Strength and weakness both follow from how the method works**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) On what grounds are methods compared?**
**Answer: Strength, what the method hides, and ease of correct use**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) Why is a shift weak on strength?**
**Answer: Only 25 keys exist to try**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What clues does substitution leave?**
**Answer: Word lengths and letter frequencies**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) What does transposition leave visible?**
**Answer: The letters themselves — only their order is hidden**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) Can a method be simply 'better'?**
**Answer: No — it is stronger here, weaker there, easier for this user; the table gives reasons**
