# Comparing Cipher Methods

## Do it first

Malaysia's cryptography unit does not teach one cipher and stop. It lines the methods up and compares them. In this lesson you learn the grounds of comparison — so 'which cipher?' becomes a question with reasons, not tastes.

## Sub lesson 1 — The methods on the table

The unit's methods share one shape — a rule that turns plaintext into ciphertext with some key or pattern — but differ in their working: a **shift (Caesar) method** moves every letter by the same number of places; a **substitution method** swaps each letter for another by a fixed table, not necessarily in alphabet order; a **transposition method** keeps the letters but rearranges their order. Comparing starts by naming each method's working exactly, because strength and weakness both follow from the working.

## Sub lesson 2 — The grounds of comparison

Methods are compared on stated grounds: **strength** — how much work breaking the method takes (a shift has only 25 keys to try; a full substitution table has vastly more); **what it hides** — substitution hides letter identities but leaves word lengths and letter frequencies as clues, transposition hides the reading order but leaves the letters themselves; **ease** — how simply it can be used correctly by hand. Malaysia's comparison is a table with reasons in every cell: a method is never just 'better' — it is stronger here, weaker there, easier for this user.
