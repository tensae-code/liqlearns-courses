# Cleaning monitor, keyboard and mouse

## Do it first

Look at the three devices you touch most: the monitor's screen, the keyboard's gaps, the mouse's underside. Each gathers its own dirt, and each has its own cleaning procedure — in its own order. Guessing the order is how screens get damaged. Learn the book's three procedures exactly.

## Sub lesson 1 — Cleaning the monitor

In the book's order: **turn off the computer; unplug the monitor; wipe the screen with a soft, clean cloth moistened with water.** And the rule that protects the screen: **never spray liquid directly on the screen** — liquid can leak in and damage the parts inside. The cloth carries the water, never the spray. Follow the GIF: off, unplug, wipe.

## Sub lesson 2 — Cleaning the keyboard

A keyboard gathers **dust, hair and lint** between its keys. **Sticky dirt** is removed with **cotton moistened with alcohol**, wiped **gently around the keys**. Gentle is part of the procedure: keys are small moving parts, not a floor to scrub.

## Sub lesson 3 — Cleaning the mouse

In the book's order: **unplug the mouse; wipe the top and the bottom with a cotton cloth moistened with rubbing alcohol; let all parts dry before reconnecting it.** And the warning that stands over all three procedures: **water conducts electricity**, so wet cleaning of electronics carries a risk of serious injury or death — which is why every procedure starts with unplugging, and ends only when parts are dry.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion all three procedures, each in its order, with the two golden rules: never spray the screen; dry before reconnecting.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the monitor procedure from memory, numbered.
2. Say what gathers in a keyboard and how sticky dirt is removed.
