# Assessing Risk in Information Exchange

## Do it first

Before Grade 6's class builds its human chain against information risk, it does the quieter work this lesson teaches: finding out what the risks actually are. You cannot guard what you have not mapped — so the class surveys its own information world first.

## Sub lesson 1 — Surveying the exchange — media, paths, risks

Information **exchanges** through **media** — spoken, written, printed, digital — and every path has its watchers: a spoken secret crosses a room; a written one crosses every hand that carries it; a digital one crosses networks of strangers. The class's **risk survey** walks its own exchanges — what do we send, by what medium, who could intercept, copy or forward it — and records what it finds. The survey's law is the security course's first: risk lives not in the information alone, nor the medium alone, but in the meeting of the two.

## Sub lesson 2 — The expert interview — and classifying information

The class then **interviews an ICT expert** — preparing questions in advance (the survey's gaps become the questions), listening for the risks the class had not imagined, and bringing the answers home as evidence. Last, the findings harden into a tool: information **classified** by the care it demands — public, private, secret — so that every future exchange can ask the classification first and choose its medium by the answer. Assessment ends in classification, and classification becomes the habit the human-chain lesson then defends in public.
