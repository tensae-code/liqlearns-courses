## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Wireless security — the network with no walls** — A **wireless network** sends its traffic through the air, **beyond the room it serves** — anyone within reach of the signal can listen for it, which no wall prevents. Its security therefore stands on **encryption and the network password**: the wireless password is not a courtesy but the lock on a door that opens onto the street, and an **open (password-free) network** lets any listener read what travels unencrypted and join the network as if invited. The same suspicion applies in reverse: a **free public wireless** offered to your device may itself be the trap — a name copied from the café's real network, harvesting whoever joins.
2. **Mobile device security — the computer in the pocket** — The **mobile phone** is a full computer carrying mail, accounts, money and photographs, and its security is the computer's security shrunk to a pocket: a **lock (PIN, pattern or fingerprint)** on the device itself, because a lost unlocked phone is every account lost at once; **care with apps** — taken from the proper store, their permissions read; the **wireless and Bluetooth radios off or guarded** in strange places; and **backups and a find/wipe service** ready before the loss, so a stolen phone is a lost object, not a lost life. Laos ends the sequence here because this is the device its graduates will actually defend.
