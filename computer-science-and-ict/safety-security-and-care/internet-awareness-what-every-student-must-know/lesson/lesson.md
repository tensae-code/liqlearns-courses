# Internet awareness — what every student must know

## Do it first

Read this claim: 'Everything on the Internet is checked by someone before it is published.' True or false? Your answer decides how safely you will use the biggest library in the world. This lesson is the long awareness list — the knowledge the securing steps stand on.

## A powerful tool, not a safe one by itself

The Internet is a powerful tool to use wisely — a vast library, worldwide communication, a creative outlet. But not all its information is valid or appropriate. Sexually explicit or violent material can harm. Content can promote hatred, intolerance and dangerous or illegal activities — self-injury, gambling, illegal drugs. Messages and people are not always who they seem: chat-room and blog contacts may not be who they appear to be, and email can carry malicious code.

## What must never be shared — and when to get help

Predators try to win trust, pretend to be students themselves, lure pupils into false security and alienate them from family. Pupils must know which information is safe to share online and which never — never reveal where you live or attend school. Even messages to known friends leave electronic footprints others can misuse. Posting personal information and photos lets predators make contact and groom, and photos can be misused or altered. Gaming sites can attract predators and bullies, and some games hold pornographic or violent images — talk to parents about what is acceptable. And pupils must learn to judge whether a download is legal and free of malicious code — and to get adult help in dangerous situations.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: the Internet is a powerful tool to use wisely — and a student must know what it holds, who may be pretending, what must never be shared, and when to get adult help. Then name the key words of this lesson — electronic footprints, grooming, a safe download, never shared — and say what each one means.
