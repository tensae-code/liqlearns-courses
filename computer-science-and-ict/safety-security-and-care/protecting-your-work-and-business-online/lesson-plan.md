## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What is at stake — accounts, money, name** — A worker's or small business's online **affairs** stand on three assets. Its **accounts** — the mail, shop, bank and social accounts everything else hangs from. Its **money** — payments received and sent through those accounts. And its **name** — the reputation customers trust, kept in its pages and reviews. An attacker who takes the mail account can reset the others from it; a scammer who clones the business's page steals the name while the business still sleeps. Laos teaches the three together because they fall together: the defence must be planned as one wall, not three fences.
2. **The defences — strong doors, separate keys, watchful eyes** — The section's protection is the course's security craft aimed at one's own affairs: **strong, separate passwords** for each important account (the mail account strongest of all, since it resets the rest), **two-step proof** wherever it is offered, **payment only through traced channels** with records kept, **backups of the business's own records and pages** kept apart, and a **watching habit** — checking account activity and the business's public face regularly, so a clone page or a strange transaction is found in hours, not months. Under them all, the sequence's standing rule: what your livelihood stands on gets the strongest door you have.
