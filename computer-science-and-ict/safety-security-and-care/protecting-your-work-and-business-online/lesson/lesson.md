# Protecting Your Work and Business Online

## Do it first

The safety sequence's sixth section turns to what a person has built: for the Grade 12 learner about to work and trade online, the question is no longer only 'am I safe' but 'is my work safe'. This lesson defends the affairs.

## Sub lesson 1 — What is at stake — accounts, money, name

A worker's or small business's online **affairs** stand on three assets. Its **accounts** — the mail, shop, bank and social accounts everything else hangs from. Its **money** — payments received and sent through those accounts. And its **name** — the reputation customers trust, kept in its pages and reviews. An attacker who takes the mail account can reset the others from it; a scammer who clones the business's page steals the name while the business still sleeps. Laos teaches the three together because they fall together: the defence must be planned as one wall, not three fences.

## Sub lesson 2 — The defences — strong doors, separate keys, watchful eyes

The section's protection is the course's security craft aimed at one's own affairs: **strong, separate passwords** for each important account (the mail account strongest of all, since it resets the rest), **two-step proof** wherever it is offered, **payment only through traced channels** with records kept, **backups of the business's own records and pages** kept apart, and a **watching habit** — checking account activity and the business's public face regularly, so a clone page or a strange transaction is found in hours, not months. Under them all, the sequence's standing rule: what your livelihood stands on gets the strongest door you have.
