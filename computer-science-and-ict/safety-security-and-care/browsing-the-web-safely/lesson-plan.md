## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Know where you are — address and padlock** — Safe browsing begins before the page loads: **read the address**. The name you meant is the name in the address bar — a famous name with letters added, swapped or misspelled is a **copycat site**, built to harvest the details meant for the real one. On any page that asks for a password or personal detail, look for the **padlock / https** that says the road to the site is encrypted, and treat its absence on such a page as a stop sign. Laos's order is a traveller's order: check the destination's name and the road's safety before handing anything over.
2. **Browse as a guest who can leave — downloads, pop-ups and traces** — On the page itself, the safe browser behaves as a **guest who can leave**: **pop-ups** that announce prizes, infections or urgent updates are **closed, never obeyed** — a real warning never arrives as a web page shouting. **Downloads** are taken only from the maker's own site, and a file the page pushes unasked is refused. What is typed into a strange page is typed as if it will be kept — because it may be: forms on unknown sites are given the **least information the errand truly needs**, and accounts are not made where a guest visit would do. The sequence's rule under all of it: browsing is safe when every step is a choice, and unsafe the moment the page starts making the choices.
