# Password attacks

## Do it first

Five thieves want one password. One tries every word in a dictionary. One tries everything, letter by letter, for as long as it takes. One simply watches you type. Sort their patience: which is fastest when it works, which never needs a computer at all?

## Trying and computing

A dictionary attack tries possible passwords from a dictionary — the words people actually choose. A brute-force attack decodes by trial and error — every combination, which is time-consuming. A rainbow table uses pre-computed hash values to find passwords — the trying is done in advance, stored, and looked up.

## Recording and watching

A key-logger attack tracks keyboard movements — the password is stolen as it is typed, which is why the key logger also appeared in the malware family. And shoulder surfing needs no technology at all: physically looking over the user's shoulder while they type.
