# Safety Rules in the Computer Room

## Do it first

Two Uzbekistan books print the same rules — Grade 5 opens with them, Grade 8 closes with them as an appendix. First lesson and last word agree: the computer room has rules, and they come before the work.

## Sub lesson 1 — Rules before switching on

The **safety rules** (правила техники безопасности) begin before the machine: enter the computer room calmly and with the teacher's permission, take your own place, touch nothing that is not yours to touch, and never work with wet hands or while eating and drinking near the machines. Cables stay as they lie — a pupil does not plug, unplug or move equipment unless the teacher says so.

## Sub lesson 2 — Rules while working and after

While working: sit straight at the set distance, report any smell, spark, strange sound or damage **at once** and stop, and never open the system unit. After work: close your programs, leave the place tidy, and push the chair in. Grade 8 prints the same rules again as an appendix — a pupil who met them at ten meets them again at fourteen, because the machines grew but the rules did not change.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: The **safety rules** (правила техники безопасности) begin before the machine: enter the computer room calmly and with the teacher's permission, take your own place, touch nothing that is not yours to touch, and never work with wet hands or while eating and drinking near the machines. While working: sit straight at the set distance, report any smell, spark, strange sound or damage **at once** and stop, and never open the system unit. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: Before the machine is switched on. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: The teacher. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: Any smell, spark, strange sound or damage. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: The system unit. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
