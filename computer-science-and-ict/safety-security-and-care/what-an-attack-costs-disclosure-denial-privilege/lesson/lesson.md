# What an attack costs — disclosure, denial, privilege

## Do it first

A warning letter is pinned on the staff noticeboard — internal, private. By evening a photograph of it is on social media for the whole town. Nobody stole the letter; nobody changed it. So what, exactly, was lost? Hold that question while the last three losses are named — then judge Halima's case with all seven.

## Disclosure, denial, privilege

Loss five: disclosure of information — dissemination to anyone unauthorised. A warning letter pinned on the school's internal noticeboard, photographed and posted on social media, is unethical disclosure. (A figure in the book shows cyber-disclosure rising in American secondary schools, offered as a warning of the emerging situation in Ethiopia.) Loss six: denial of use — the intentional degradation or blocking of resources so intended users cannot reach them: the DoS flood, filed as a loss. Loss seven: elevation of privilege — using a system weakness to gain rights that should not be available: a student entering the academic record system on a teacher's higher-privilege account, made possible by guessable passwords, theft or hijacking, and keystroke logging — recording the keys struck on a keyboard.

## Halima's case — the answer key, teacher evidence

Halima, an Ethiopian online shopper, finds purchases she never made and double charges on her mobile banking. The teacher's key names the mechanism and the remedy: she has suffered unauthorised access to her bank account, and lost the confidentiality of her account information. The likely causes: careless handling of her bank details — sharing them, or leaving them in a public place; using a less-trusted or fake online shop; or a computer or phone carrying adware and viruses that sniff personal information and send it to third parties. Her protections: keep bank information in a secured place; run antivirus and an adware cleaner on her devices; and check the trustworthiness of online shops and services before buying and paying.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: the last three losses are: disclosure of information, denial of use, and elevation of privilege — and Halima's shopping case shows them in real life. Then name the key words of this lesson — disclosure of information, denial of use, elevation of privilege, keystroke logging — and say what each one means.
