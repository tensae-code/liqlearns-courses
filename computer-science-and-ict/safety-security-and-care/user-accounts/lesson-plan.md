# Lesson plan — User accounts — Administrator, Standard, Guest

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Safety, Security and Care · **Lesson:** User accounts — Administrator, Standard, Guest
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 13 — Safety, Security and Care in the Computer Science and ICT ladder (TITLES.md); stocked from Ethiopia's Grade 7 book, Unit 5 — Security and Safety of Computer. The ladder is the recommended order, never a forced one.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** computer security protects systems and information from unauthorised use; a user account is an identity of user name plus password, and Windows offers three kinds — Administrator, Standard and Guest — with different powers.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the routines lessons — the accounts are what you sign in to at start-up and out of at the end.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or in steps — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Computer security and the account** — security protects against theft of and damage to hardware and information, and disruption of service; an account is a user name plus password for logging in
2. **The three kinds of account** — Administrator controls the whole computer; Standard does everyday tasks without installing; Guest is anyone, not recognised by name

3. **The accounts at Grade 10 depth — powers, and checking who is there** — the account as settings and permissions; the three types tabled by power, with the daily-use warning; and the route for checking existing accounts

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Starting up a computer, and Shut down, Sleep and Sign Out.  Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what computer security protects, and its three threats.
2. Define a user account: user name plus password.
3. Give each account kind's powers and limits.
4. Say which account everyday work belongs on.
5. Define a user account as settings and permissions.
6. Give the Grade 10 powers table for Administrator, Standard and Guest.
7. Run the account-checking route in order.

## What you need
A computer with user accounts if you can look at one; paper and a pencil.
