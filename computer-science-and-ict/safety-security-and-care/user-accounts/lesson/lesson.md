# User accounts — Administrator, Standard, Guest

## Do it first

One school computer, thirty pupils, and everyone able to do everything — install anything, change anything, open anyone's work. What goes wrong? This lesson is about the guard the book teaches: accounts, and why they are not all given the same power.

## Sub lesson 1 — Computer security and the account

**Computer security** is protecting computer systems and information from **unauthorised use** — against **theft of or damage to hardware**, **theft of or damage to information**, and **disruption of service**. The protection taught here is **passwords on user accounts**. A **user account** is an identity — a **user name plus a password** — for logging in: the machine knows who you are, and what you may do, by your account.

## Sub lesson 2 — The three kinds of account

Windows offers three kinds. The **Administrator** controls the whole computer: can install programs and let others use the machine. A **Standard** account is for everyday tasks: it runs existing programs, but **cannot install programs or make big changes**. A **Guest** account lets anyone sign in — the guest is **not recognised by name**. Power is given by need: the administrator holds the keys because someone must; everyone else works safely inside limits.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the three account kinds, from most power to least, with one power and one limit for each.
