# Securing your computer — the checklist

## Do it first

Seven defences stand between your computer and everything in the last ten lessons. Before they are listed, write your own seven: what do you actually do today? Then measure your list against the book's checklist — and against the teacher's longer one that follows it.

## The book's checklist

Install reliable, reputable security and anti-virus software. Activate the firewall — a security guard between the Internet and your local network. Stay up to date and perform software updates as soon as they appear. Avoid clicking email attachments unless you know the source. Change passwords regularly, using a unique combination of numbers and letters with capital and small letters — at least 15 characters is advised — and different passwords for different accounts: email, e-learning, Facebook. Use the Internet with caution, ignoring pop-ups and drive-by downloads while surfing. And perform daily full system scans, with a periodic system backup schedule, so data is retrievable when something happens to the computer. Two activities in the student book are both numbered 5.4 — this checklist's activity is the second of them; both are taught, in the book's order.

## The teacher's longer checklist — teacher evidence

The keyed answer adds concrete habits: limit personal information shared online, change privacy settings and do not use location features. Keep applications and the operating system up to date. Build strong passwords from upper- and lower-case letters, numbers and special characters, and use a password manager and two methods of verification. Lock down social media, because social-engineering criminals need only a few data points. Use a strong home or school network — a strong encryption password, a VPN, and passwords such as Wi-Fi changed regularly. Treat anything that urges immediate action, sounds too good to be true or asks for personal information as suspicious — think before clicking, and when in doubt do not click. Never share PINs or passwords, and prefer devices with biometric scans. Check account statements and credit reports regularly. Share financial information only on secure sites beginning https:// and never on a site with an invalid certificate. Run antivirus, antimalware and firewalls. Back up files regularly into an encrypted file or encrypted storage device. Never click links in texts or emails from strangers. Remember: the government will not call, text or contact you on social media about money owed. And expect scammers to exploit financial fears with work-from-home, debt-consolidation and student-loan offers.

## Grade 10 depth — the two families of prevention, and the technical table whole

Prevention strategies split into **technical** and **non-technical**. The technical table is taught whole — seven measures:

1. **Taking data backup** — multiple copies on external hard drive, CD, flash memory or an online/cloud backup site, ready for any loss.
2. **Installing protective electrical devices** — such as a **UPS**, battery backup in an outage.
3. **Installing antivirus and antispyware programs** — against internet-borne spyware and viruses.
4. **Installing a firewall** — against unauthorised access.
5. **Encrypting data** — so lost, stolen or hacked files cannot be read.
6. **Installing antivirus software** proper — scanning for, detecting and removing viruses, worms and spyware.
7. **Creating and changing user accounts** — so settings and permissions bound what each user can do.

## Grade 10 depth — copy protection, and seven ways to protect data (teacher guide — evidence)

The teacher guide's key adds a protection family the student book does not name: **copy protection**, whose components are **encryption** (content usable only with the correct key), **DRM controls** (technology that manages access to copyrighted material), **licensing**, and **watermarks**.

And it keys **seven ways to protect programs and data**: encrypt your data; **back it up**; use the **cloud** as a backup option; **anti-malware protection**; make **old computers' hard drives unreadable**; install an **up-to-date operating system**; **automate software updates**. Non-technical prevention is keyed as **administrative policies, procedures and standards with assigned responsibilities**.
