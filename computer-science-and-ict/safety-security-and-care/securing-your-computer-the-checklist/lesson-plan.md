# Lesson plan — Securing your computer — the checklist

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Safety, Security and Care · **Lesson:** Securing your computer — the checklist
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Information and Computer Security (CURRICULUM.md) (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the practical steps: reputable security software, the firewall on, updates at once, care with attachments, long unique passwords, caution with pop-ups, daily scans and a backup schedule.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier lessons of this course — the learner knows what a computer, a network and the Internet are. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or in steps — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of this course. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Recite the book's seven securing steps.
2. Apply the teacher's longer checklist to your own accounts and devices.
3. Run the checklist on a real machine and report what was missing.
4. Split prevention into technical and non-technical, and list the seven technical measures.
5. Name copy protection's four components and the guide's seven ways to protect data (teacher guide — evidence).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Where the lesson is a doing-lesson, a school computer if one is available — paper works too.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner writes their own seven defences first, then measures them against the book's checklist and the teacher's longer keyed checklist (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the parts below, in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 20920]: a picture of securing your computer — the checklist poster — the student studies each part of the picture and names what it shows.

- Mixes up the key words → go back to the picture and point at each part while you say its name: the firewall, the password rule here, daily scans and backups, think before you click.
