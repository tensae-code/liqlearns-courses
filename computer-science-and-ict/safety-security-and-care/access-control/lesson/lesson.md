# Access Control

## Do it first

Security begins at the door. Laos's Grade 10 security chapter, after naming its threats, turns to the defences — and the first defence it builds is access control: the system that decides who gets in. This lesson guards the door.

## Sub lesson 1 — What access control is

**Access control** is a system's way of **letting the right people in and keeping everyone else out** — and, once inside, of giving each person **only the powers their work needs**. It answers two questions in order: **who are you** (identification and proof — an account name with its password, a card, a fingerprint), and **what may you do** (the permissions attached to that identity — read only, change, administer). A system without access control does not have users; it has visitors, any of whom may do anything.

## Sub lesson 2 — Access control at work — least power, kept records

Working access control follows the rule of **least power**: every account gets the smallest set of permissions its job requires — the clerk who enters marks is not given the power to delete pupils — because every extra power is an extra loss waiting for a stolen password. Beside the door stands its memory: the system **logs who entered and what they did**, so misuse can be traced to an account and a moment. Laos teaches access control first among its security measures because the chapter's other defences — antivirus, firewall, backup — all assume this one: they protect a system whose door is already guarded.
