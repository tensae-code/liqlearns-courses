**Acceptance count: taught in 10 countries (Ethiopia, Uganda, Yemen, Saudi Arabia, Bhutan, Sri Lanka, Jordan, Armenia, Vietnam, Pakistan).**

Yemen's own way (Grade 10): a skills ladder — every unit alternates short teaching topics with a named تدريب (guided training) and نشاط (activity), and Windows itself is taught as a school subject for a full unit before any application is opened.

Saudi Arabia's own way (Grade 3, continuing education): a workbook-style book for the continuing-education (adult/lifelong learning) track — every one of the 16 lessons opens with "I want to learn" objectives and closes with written exercises, and everything is taught on an older Windows desktop (Start menu, "My Computer", Recycle Bin) with Notepad as the only application, worked through step-by-step practicals in the school lab.

Morocco's own way (Grade 1): ICT from age 6 built entirely on handling real hardware first — plug in, switch on, draw with the mouse, type in Word — with every unit taught in the same five-step rhythm (mobilisation, découverte, application, évaluation, réemploi) in pairs/groups, and files taught through paper folders and plastic sleeves before the screen. (teacher guide — evidence)

Bhutan's own way (Class IV, Literacy with ICT student book): Competency-based "Literacy with ICT" (not procedure drill): every chapter opens with learning objectives, mixes off-machine "Try This" with "Try This On Machine", and closes with Now You Know / Check Your Progress / Explore Further; content is localised (Bhutanese names, kuenselonline, education.gov.bt, Bhutan's 1999 Internet start) and one class per week across the year.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 6 Reading Book): a story/dialogue-led "Reading Book" with a talking computer character; every chapter cross-refers to numbered Workbook activities; local sectors (school office, ATM, ICU, factories, agriculture) and the 3R e-waste method are taught from Grade 6.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 1 G1 Workbook): At Grade 1, digital skills are taught inside an integrated activity book — six "blocks" (لبنات) of exploratory tasks where MS Paint is the working tool for position/direction, numbers, movement and shapes, ending in an unplugged "Programming Hour" project of direction commands.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 2 G2 student): A story-led primary book built around two Grade 2 characters (Hayk and Arpi); every lesson uses fixed activity "rooms" (key knowledge, key words, skills/tasks) and QR codes link to audio/video material; safety and health come before any machine skills.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 2 G2 teacher): Companion to Book 1 in the Zangak guide template: Grade 2 standard outcomes, an exemplary programme, a 32-hour thematic plan covering the student book's twelve themes with practical works and two summary tests, sample lesson plans (the first teaches the "Important commands" lesson — select, cut, copy, paste, undo, redo), and worked explanations of the book's electronic tasks, opening with correct sitting posture at the computer.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 3 G3 student): Grade 3 (Stepanyan, Amirkhanyan, Matinyan) keeps the Hayk-and-Arpi story frame and QR-linked media: device safety, technology around us, picture and text editors, e-mail with attachments, input/output devices, keyboard keys, data collection with programs, then a Scratch strand — environment, first program, pictures, repeated actions, and game scores as a first step into variables.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 3 G3 teacher): Companion to Book 3 in the Zangak guide template: Grade 3 standard outcomes, exemplary programme, thematic planning that opens with e-security and health (switching the computer on, off and restarting), sample lesson plans (the first is Theme 6, keyboard shortcuts for fast work), and explanations of the textbook's electronic tasks.

Vietnam's own way (Grade 3 Tin học 3): Informatics starts in Grade 3 not with the machine but with information in daily life — an alarm clock ringing is "information" that leads to a decision — and only then meets the computer; problem-solving (Chủ đề 6) is taught unplugged as ordering the steps of everyday jobs, with and without conditions, before any code exists.


Pakistan's own way (Grade 6 Computer Science 6): every unit opens with Students Learning Outcomes, is dotted with "Do you Know?", "Extra Bit!" and "Tips" boxes, and closes with Summary, MCQ/short/detailed exercises, project-based and activity-based questions, with an answers key at the back. Digital skills (Windows desktop, file management, Paint 3D, a typing tutor) are taught hands-on BEFORE programming; digital citizenship is framed through Pakistani citizenship first; the entrepreneurship unit teaches from Pakistani founders (Foodpanda/EatOye, Shopsy.pk, Zero Makeup, Investors Lounge).
