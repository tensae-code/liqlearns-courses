Placement choice: this lesson was built new for the ICT expansion (Kazakhstan, 2026-11-14) because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree was checked for the slug and the live lesson content was checked for near matches before building. It sits in the Safety, Security and Care title (safety-security-and-care/), following the book's own order. Provenance scope note: the placements stocked here are from Kazakhstan's okulyk.kz (OKULYK) textbooks, recommended by the Ministry of Education and Science of the Republic of Kazakhstan — the Digital Literacy and Informatika lines of the Almatykitap, Arman-PV and Atamura teams and the Nazarbayev Intellectual Schools (NIS) books, all student books except the two Kadirkulov teacher guides named in the entries below, whose placements are stocked exactly as filed. Many books exist in parallel language editions (Kazakh / Russian / Uzbek / Uyghur / English) of the same textbook: each distinct book is taught once, and every distinct edition is named and cited in the book entry. Kazakhstan's own ways are kept: one textbook line in up to five parallel language editions, the okulyk.kz digital library as the books' home, Scratch from Grade 1 and robotics strands (LEGO WeDo 2.0 and Mindstorms EV3) from Grade 1–2, and every keyword given in Kazakh–Russian–English in the early grades. Media slots in lesson/lesson.md are described slots only ([Picture — …]); no picture numbers are invented.


Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G5 Informatika): Grade 5 Informatika by the Almatykitap team drops Scratch entirely: information theory first (transmission, encryption, binary, graphics in binary code), then raster work in Paint against vector work with curves, and half the year is EV3 robotics beyond simple movement — the gyroscopic sensor, line following and robo-sumo competitions.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kobdikova G5 Informatika): the Arman-PV Grade 5 book teaches the same five national sections as the other two publishers, but every second paragraph is a practicum or a "creating a project" lesson, and the safety section ends in cloud skills — placing, changing and downloading shared-access files — plus mini-projects on setting passwords on documents and preparing a document for print. The vector editor is Inkscape (named in the glossary), the raster editor Paint.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukhametzhanova G5 Informatika, 2020 edition): the Atamura team's 2020 Grade 5 book follows the same five national sections in numbered-paragraph form (1.1–5.5) with end-of-section final tasks; its graphics strand works in Paint for raster and Inkscape for vector (canvas size, SVG, Bezier pen), and the introduction also promises a first taste of 3D technologies (3D model, 3D editor, 3D printing) inside the graphics work.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukhametzhanova G6 Informatika, 2018 edition): the 2018 Atamura Grade 6 course is built around making a real computer game across two whole parts — idea, scenario, stage and characters, implementation, sound accompaniment, a start page and improvements — with vector graphics in Inkscape before it and full Word documentation after it: footnotes, hyperlinks, an automatic contents and a formal game design document that teaches copyright and citing authors.

Kazakhstan's own way (Salgaraeva & Ilyasova G6 Informatika): the Arman-PV Grade 6 book runs the full 2018 programme in numbered paragraphs with a test after every part: systems and networks, presenting information (transmission, encryption, binary coding of text), vector graphics with a "Zhailau" drawing practicum, then the two-part game build in the Scratch environment, and a final Word part that adds references, copyright and plagiarism, a referat mini-project and the game document.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukametzhanova G6 «Информатика»): the whole second half of Grade 6 is one long game-development arc — idea, scenario, drawing scenes and characters (vector editor), realising the scenario, sound, a title screen, improving the project — and then documenting the game properly with footnotes, hyperlinks and an automatically built table of contents.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G5 «Информатика»): Grade 5 in the Almatykitap line is a robotics year: after information representation (encryption, binary, binary-coded graphics) and raster/vector graphics, two full sections run LEGO robots — gyro sensor, turns, line following and robo-sumo competitions — before a closing safety section that ends with shared folders and mini-projects. Every term is glossed in three languages (Kazakh/Russian/English) in the margin.

Kazakhstan's own way (Salgaraeva G6 «Информатика»): the Arman-PV Grade 6 book is a game-making year wrapped in document skills: ergonomics and wireless networks first, then binary coding and encryption, vector graphics (drawing a «Жайлау» summer pasture), a Scratch game designed, built and given a sound track and title screen — and the year ends with footnotes, hyperlinks, copyright and plagiarism, a table of contents and the written documentation for the game.

Kazakhstan's own way (Atayeva/Vyushkova/Zhurbenko G8 «Информатика» NIS): the NIS Grade 8 book is the widest of the set: a whole section on vector graphics and geographic information systems (planning an excursion, making maps, visualising data in GIS), octal and hexadecimal coding, loops applied separately to mathematics, physics, business and animation, strings and subroutines, and a nine-step project method ending in a mini-project on artificial intelligence.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukametzhanova G5 «Информатика» 2020 edition): the 2020 Atamura revision of the Grade 5 book is rebuilt on the renewed programme — the same five sections as the Almatykitap book (Book 18): presenting information with encryption and binary, raster and vector graphics with curves, robotics with the gyroscope sensor and turns, robot competitions (line following and robo-sumo), and computer safety moved to the end.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kobdikova G5 «Информатика»): the Arman-PV Grade 5 book is the practicum twin of the same programme as Books 18 and 35 — nearly every unit comes in a theory + practice pair, and it adds a block the others lack: sharing files (placing, changing and downloading shared files), setting a password on documents, and organising a document effectively for print, taught as "mini-project works".
