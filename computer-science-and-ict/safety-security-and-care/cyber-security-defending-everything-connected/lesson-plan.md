# Lesson plan — Cyber security — defending everything connected

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Safety, Security and Care · **Lesson:** Cyber security — defending everything connected
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Information and Computer Security (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** cyber security applies technologies, processes and controls to protect systems, networks, programs, devices and data from cyber-attacks — across network security, information security, operational security, disaster recovery and end-user education.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the whole of this unit so far — threats, prevention and habits. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or in steps — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier lessons of this unit. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define cyber security: technologies, processes and controls protecting systems, networks, programs, devices and data.
2. Name the five categories: network, information, operational, disaster recovery, end-user education.
3. State the full-size stakes: shutdowns, military failure, secrets, medical records, paralysed networks.
4. Define cybercrime and its main motive.
5. List what attackers are after and draw the cyber security / cybercrime-fighting line (teacher guide — evidence).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks cyber security is just antivirus → antivirus is one tool; the definition is technologies, processes AND controls across five categories.
- Mixes cybercrime with cyber security → crime is the attack side (mostly for profit); security is the defence side.
