# Confidentiality, integrity, availability — the CIA triad

## Do it first

Here is a pupil's mark sheet. Three promises must hold at once: only the right people may see it; nobody may quietly change it; and it must be there, ready, on the day the pupil needs it. If any one promise breaks, security has failed. This lesson names the three promises.

## The three principles

Computer security is mainly concerned with three areas, drawn as one triangle of triple constraints — the CIA triad. Confidentiality: information is available only to the intended audience — only authorised users and processes can access or modify data; privacy, protecting personal data, is its close relative. Integrity: data can be trusted — it is maintained in a correct state, not wrongly changed; it is correct, authentic and reliable. Availability: information is available to authorised users whenever they require it — systems, networks and devices kept up and running.

## The school example — teacher evidence

The teacher's keyed example grounds all three in one school. A pupil's marks must not be altered by a third party — not by the school director and not by another pupil — and must not be disclosed to anyone except the pupil and the homeroom teacher the pupil allows: that is confidentiality and integrity together. And information must reach the correct receiver on time when required — an ICT service intended for disabled students must be provided for them on time when they require it: that is availability.

## Grade 10 depth — nine habits against cyber-attack, and who to call

The unit's protection model is completed by a checklist of **nine habits** against cyber-attack:

1. **Use strong passwords.** 2. **Secure your computer.** 3. **Be social-media savvy.** 4. **Secure your mobile devices.** 5. **Install the latest operating system updates.** 6. **Protect your data.** 7. **Secure your wireless network.** 8. **Protect your e-identity.** 9. **Avoid being scammed.**

Above the list stands the human rule — **call the right person for help**: illegal internet content or a suspected computer crime, identity theft or a commercial scam goes to the **local police**; maintenance or software-installation help goes to the **service provider or a certified computer technician**.

And the aim of it all is the triad this lesson is named for: protection aims at **confidentiality** (no unauthorised disclosure or access), **integrity** (no unauthorised modification or damage) and **availability** — whose failure is **denial of service**.
