# Who attacks — criminals, hackers, insiders, mistakes

## Do it first

Five attacks, five attackers. One wants money. One wants the thrill of the puzzle. One works inside the building and holds a key. One meant no harm at all — and one sits in a classroom like yours. Guess which of the five causes damage without wanting to, before the list is opened.

## Criminals, hackers, insiders

Online criminals identify what can be appropriated — stealing and selling sensitive data, and holding systems and information to ransom. Hackers have varying expertise and are often untargeted — testing their skills, or causing disruption for its own sake. Malicious insiders use their access to an organisation's data or networks — stealing sensitive information for competitors, for example.

## Mistakes and pupils

Honest mistakes belong on the list too: people with the best intentions err — emailing something sensitive to the wrong address is the book's example. And school pupils or students are named plainly: some enjoy the challenge of testing their cyber skills. Knowing all five matters because the defence differs: locks stop outsiders, but only care and training stop mistakes.

## Say it to your companion

Then name the key words of this lesson — online criminals, hackers, malicious insiders, honest mistakes — and say what each one means.
