# Video script — Authentication — Proving Who You Are, and Two-Factor Authentication

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

**Authentication** is proving **who you are** to a system, before it lets you in as that person. Proofs come in three **factors**: something you **know** (a password, a PIN), something you **have** (a phone, a card, a key), and something you **are** (a fingerprint, a face — biometrics). One factor is one lock; the lesson's weight is that each factor can fail in its own way — a password can be guessed, a phone stolen, a face spoofed — so serious doors ask for more than one kind of proof.
**Two-factor authentication (2FA)** asks for proofs from **two different factors** — most often the password you know **plus** a short-lived code on the phone you have. Kuwait teaches it as worksheets, hands on: setting up **Google Authenticator**, which generates the changing codes, and the two-factor protection in the **Ministry of Education app** the pupils themselves use. A code that changes every few seconds cannot be replayed tomorrow — that expiry is what the second factor buys.
