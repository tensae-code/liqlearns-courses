## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Physical means — guarding the thing itself** — **Physical protection** guards data by guarding its **carriers and rooms**: locks and guards for server rooms, controlled entry (cards, logs), safes and locked cabinets for media and backups, protection against fire, flood and power failure (alarms, extinguishers, UPS), and care with the machine's body — cables, screens facing walls, devices not left open and logged-in. A stolen, burned or drowned computer needs no hacker: the physical means answer the threats that never touch a network.
2. **Digital means — guarding the data itself** — **Digital protection** guards the data in its own realm: **passwords and access control** (only the authorised reach it), **encryption** (reached data reads as noise), **anti-malware and updates** (the known weapons blunted), **firewalls** (the network's gate), **backups** (the data survives its own destruction), and **monitoring** (the watch kept). Jordan teaches the two materials as one lesson because real protection is their weave: the encrypted disk in the locked room, the backed-up file behind the guarded account — a threat stopped by whichever material it meets first.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
