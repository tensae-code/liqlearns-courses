# Privacy and security — rights vs locks

## Do it first

Two sentences. First: “I can ask the company to show me what it holds about me, and to fix what is wrong.” Second: “A padlock of software stops a stranger reading my files.” One of these is about **your rights**. The other is about **keeping attackers out**. Decide which is which — this lesson gives the two their proper names: privacy, and security.

## Privacy — your rights over your data

**Privacy** safeguards people's **right to manage their own data** — to **access** what firms hold about them and to **correct or erase errors** in it. Privacy is about **rights**: how personal information is **acquired, used and maintained**, and **who may access it**.

So **data privacy** is the **proper handling, processing, storage and use of personal information** — and the **rights of the individual** in it.

## Security — the measures that guard it

**Security** guards against **leaks from malware and breaches**. Security is the **measures** — the policies, methods and means — that stop **third parties reaching personal data without authorisation**.

So **data security** is the **policies, methods and means that secure personal data**. Privacy says what should happen to your data and what you may demand; security is the lock-work that makes sure only the authorised reach it.

## Telling them apart

Test any case with two questions. Is it about **what a person may see, correct or erase** — their rights? That is **privacy**. Is it about **stopping an unauthorised third party getting in** — a measure, a lock, a guard? That is **security**. The group exercise in the book tests the distinction against a real Gmail account's privacy and security practice — one account, both questions asked of it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion one privacy right and one security measure, then ask them: a firm lets you erase wrong data it holds about you — privacy or security? Why?
