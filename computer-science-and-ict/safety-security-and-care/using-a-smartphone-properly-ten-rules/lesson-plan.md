# Lesson plan — Using a smartphone properly — ten rules

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Safety, Security and Care · **Lesson:** Using a smartphone properly — ten rules
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 13 — Safety, Security and Care in the Computer Science and ICT ladder (TITLES.md); stocked from Ethiopia's Grade 8 book, Unit 5 — Computer Ergonomics. The ladder is the recommended order, never a forced one.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the book's ten tips for proper use of a smartphone, to reduce the health risks of a device that has become commonplace.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the seven handling rules — this lesson is the book's own checklist form of the same care, widened to roads, public places and messages.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or in steps — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Handling portable devices safely — signal, texts and landlines. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name all ten proper-use rules from memory.
2. Match each rule to the situation it governs — road, public place, message, body, break.
3. Judge a described phone habit against the ten rules.

## What you need
The ten-rule list and your own phone habits to judge against it.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner writes down their own five phone habits, good or bad, before the book's ten rules are shown (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the parts below, in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 12309]: a picture of the ten rules as a checklist beside a phone — the student ticks the rules their own habits already keep.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the ten rules aloud, one by one — the student counts them off on their fingers.
3. **Say it to your companion** — explain the lesson back in your own words (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the lesson retold in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-or-false, fill-in-the-blank — served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the doing block at the end of lesson/lesson.md.
