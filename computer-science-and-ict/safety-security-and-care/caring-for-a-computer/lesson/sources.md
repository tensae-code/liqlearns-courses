**Acceptance count: taught in 11 countries (Ethiopia, Tanzania, Eritrea, Mozambique, Uganda, Saudi Arabia, Sudan, Bhutan, Sri Lanka, Rwanda, Moldova).**

Saudi Arabia's own way (Grade 3, continuing education): a workbook-style book for the continuing-education (adult/lifelong learning) track — every one of the 16 lessons opens with "I want to learn" objectives and closes with written exercises, and everything is taught on an older Windows desktop (Start menu, "My Computer", Recycle Bin) with Notepad as the only application, worked through step-by-step practicals in the school lab.

Sudan's own way (Grade 4): a gentle first ICT book in six short units that ends not with applications but with the keyboard and mouse themselves — hand skills before software. The Bakht Al-Ruda ICT series runs in the same pattern from Grade 4 to Grade 9.

Bhutan's own way (Class IV, Literacy with ICT student book): Competency-based "Literacy with ICT" (not procedure drill): every chapter opens with learning objectives, mixes off-machine "Try This" with "Try This On Machine", and closes with Now You Know / Check Your Progress / Explore Further; content is localised (Bhutanese names, kuenselonline, education.gov.bt, Bhutan's 1999 Internet start) and one class per week across the year.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 7 Reading Book): the CPU is taught through a hotel-kitchen/chef analogy; programme development (decomposition, flowcharts, variables, bugs) precedes presentation software; HTML page-building is already in Grade 7.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 7 Workbook): a companion workbook mirroring the Reading Book chapter-for-chapter (CPU, OS, security, word processing, program development, presentations, internet).

Rwanda's own way (S1 ICT Student Book, KLB): competency-based units each opening with a Key Unit Competency and keywords; a full GIS/ArcGIS unit and a Scratch music-making unit sit inside the first secondary year, and ICT use is grounded in Rwandan daily life.

Moldova's own way (Grade 1, Educație Digitală): digital education starts at age 6–7 with no keyboard skills assumed — information, devices and staying safe first, told through two child guides (Ana and Ștefan), and programming introduced unplugged (instructions, algorithm, a first maze program, debugging) before any real software. The Digital Education module sits inside Technological Education in the early grades.
