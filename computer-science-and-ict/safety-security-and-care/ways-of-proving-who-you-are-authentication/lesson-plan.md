## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Three families of proof** — **Authentication** is proving identity to a system, and its methods sort into three families: something you **know** (a password, a PIN, a secret answer), something you **have** (a card, a key, a phone that receives codes), and something you **are** (a fingerprint, a face). The activity's fiches compare the methods' **characteristics**: what can be forgotten, stolen, copied, shared — or lost with the body itself. Each family fails differently, which is exactly why serious systems combine two.
2. **Logging in, judged** — A login page is authentication at work: type the password (know), perhaps confirm on the phone (have), perhaps touch the sensor (are). Judging a method means asking the fiche's questions: How secret is it really? Who else could present it? What happens when it is lost — can it be changed like a password, or is it yours for life like a fingerprint? Strong access is not the fanciest method; it is the combination whose failures you have thought through.
