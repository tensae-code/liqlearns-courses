# Ways of Proving Who You Are — Authentication

## Do it first

A website asks: prove it is you. You type a password — one kind of proof. Before reading on, name two other proofs you have used or seen: something in your pocket, something in your body.

## Sub lesson 1 — Three families of proof

**Authentication** is proving identity to a system, and its methods sort into three families: something you **know** (a password, a PIN, a secret answer), something you **have** (a card, a key, a phone that receives codes), and something you **are** (a fingerprint, a face). The activity's fiches compare the methods' **characteristics**: what can be forgotten, stolen, copied, shared — or lost with the body itself. Each family fails differently, which is exactly why serious systems combine two.

In your mind's eye: three families of proof.

## Sub lesson 2 — Logging in, judged

A login page is authentication at work: type the password (know), perhaps confirm on the phone (have), perhaps touch the sensor (are). Judging a method means asking the fiche's questions: How secret is it really? Who else could present it? What happens when it is lost — can it be changed like a password, or is it yours for life like a fingerprint? Strong access is not the fanciest method; it is the combination whose failures you have thought through.

In your mind's eye: logging in, judged.
