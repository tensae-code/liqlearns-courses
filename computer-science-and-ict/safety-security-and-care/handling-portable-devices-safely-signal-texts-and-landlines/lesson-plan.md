# Lesson plan — Handling portable devices safely — signal, texts and landlines

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Safety, Security and Care · **Lesson:** Handling portable devices safely — signal, texts and landlines
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 13 — Safety, Security and Care in the Computer Science and ICT ladder (TITLES.md); stocked from Ethiopia's Grade 8 book, Unit 5 — Computer Ergonomics. The ladder is the recommended order, never a forced one.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** rules 4–7 for careful handling of portable devices: switch off data and wireless, avoid calls where signal is low, prefer texts to calls, and use landline telephones for long conversations.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on rules 1–3 — calls, headsets, distance — and completes the book's seven handling rules.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Handling portable devices safely — calls, headsets, distance. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State rules 4–7 for handling portable devices safely.
2. Explain why a phone works harder — and transmits more — where signal is low.
3. Say why texting is safer than a long call, and when a landline is the best choice.

## What you need
A phone (real or drawn) for the toggles, and a partner for the call-or-text choices.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner lists every wireless switch on a phone they can name, and says which of them are on right now for no reason (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the parts below, in order (lesson/lesson.md).

3. **Say it to your companion** — explain the lesson back in your own words (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the lesson retold in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks low signal only means a weak voice → the device raises its transmission power to stay connected to the tower; that raised power is exactly what the rule avoids.
