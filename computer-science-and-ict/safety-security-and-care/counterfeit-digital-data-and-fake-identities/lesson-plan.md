# Lesson plan — Counterfeit Digital Data and Fake Identities

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Safety, Security and Care · **Lesson:** Counterfeit Digital Data and Fake Identities
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Bangladesh (2026-11-14) — in the title Safety, Security and Care on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). **The one thing this lesson teaches:** counterfeit digital data and fake identities: data and identities manufactured to deceive — doctored records, forged profiles, copied persons — and a classroom team of investigators learning to expose them (Bangladesh Grade 8 Digital Technology, Learning Experience 2, Session 3).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Counterfeit data — records that lie** — **Counterfeit digital data** is data made or altered to deceive: a marks sheet with raised numbers, a certificate with a changed name, a screenshot of a payment never made. Its danger is the copy's perfection — the forged file prints as cleanly as the true one. Its enemies are the **original's keepers**: the issuing office's record, the system's log, the second source that never met the forger. Counterfeit data is defeated not by looking harder at the copy but by **checking against the source** — the verification habit wearing a detective's coat.
2. **Fake identities — persons that are not; and the investigators** — A **fake identity** goes further: a whole profile of a person who does not exist, or the stolen face and name of one who does — built to befriend, to borrow trust, to sell, to spy. Its tells are an investigator's list: a history that starts yesterday, friends who are strangers to each other, photographs that appear elsewhere under other names (the verification lesson's reverse search), and requests that hurry or flatter. The Grade 8 teams work as **classroom investigators**: given cases, they gather evidence, test the identity against sources, and present the verdict with its proof — suspicion is not a verdict; evidence is.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Paper money is counterfeited; so is everything digital money's cousins are made of — a certificate, a profile, a screenshot, a person. The digital copy is perfect, which is the problem: nothing about a fake file looks fake. Grade 8 answers by training investigators, and this lesson is their training. (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the lesson text in lesson/lesson.md, in its parts in order.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of counterfeit data — records that lie as the lesson describes it — the student studies the picture and names what each part shows.
   - ADD GIF: a GIF of fake identities — persons that are not; and the investigators in action — the student watches once, then says what changed and why.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the lesson's key names and definitions aloud, in the order the lesson gives them — the student listens once through, then reads the same lines aloud along with the voice. (Audio is a described slot in this build; no audio file is made.)
3. **Say it to your companion** — the companion block in lesson/lesson.md.
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the lesson in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-false, matching, short-answer — served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the doing block at the end of lesson/lesson.md.
