# Counterfeit Digital Data and Fake Identities — true or false

Say true or false, and fix the false ones in your own words.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: Data made or altered to deceive**
**Answer: True**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: Data made or altered to deceive** — as the answer to: Name two examples.
**Answer: False — A raised marks sheet and a payment screenshot never made (also a changed certificate)**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: The copy is perfect — nothing about it looks fake**
**Answer: True**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: The copy is perfect — nothing about it looks fake** — as the answer to: Who are counterfeit data's enemies?
**Answer: False — The original's keepers — the issuing record, the log, the second source**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: By checking against the source, not by staring at the copy**
**Answer: True**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: By checking against the source, not by staring at the copy** — as the answer to: What is a fake identity?
**Answer: False — A profile of a person who does not exist — or a stolen person's face and name**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: A history starting yesterday, and photographs found under other names (also stranger-friends, hurrying requests)**
**Answer: True**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: A history starting yesterday, and photographs found under other names (also stranger-friends, hurrying requests)** — as the answer to: Which earlier tool exposes reused photographs?
**Answer: False — Reverse image search**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: Evidence — suspicion is not a verdict**
**Answer: True**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: Evidence — suspicion is not a verdict** — as the answer to: What do the classroom investigators present?
**Answer: False — The verdict with its proof**
