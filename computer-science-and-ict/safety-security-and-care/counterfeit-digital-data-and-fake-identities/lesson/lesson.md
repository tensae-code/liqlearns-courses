# Counterfeit Digital Data and Fake Identities

## Do it first

Paper money is counterfeited; so is everything digital money's cousins are made of — a certificate, a profile, a screenshot, a person. The digital copy is perfect, which is the problem: nothing about a fake file looks fake. Grade 8 answers by training investigators, and this lesson is their training.

## Sub lesson 1 — Counterfeit data — records that lie

**Counterfeit digital data** is data made or altered to deceive: a marks sheet with raised numbers, a certificate with a changed name, a screenshot of a payment never made. Its danger is the copy's perfection — the forged file prints as cleanly as the true one. Its enemies are the **original's keepers**: the issuing office's record, the system's log, the second source that never met the forger. Counterfeit data is defeated not by looking harder at the copy but by **checking against the source** — the verification habit wearing a detective's coat.

In your mind's eye: counterfeit data — records that lie.

## Sub lesson 2 — Fake identities — persons that are not; and the investigators

A **fake identity** goes further: a whole profile of a person who does not exist, or the stolen face and name of one who does — built to befriend, to borrow trust, to sell, to spy. Its tells are an investigator's list: a history that starts yesterday, friends who are strangers to each other, photographs that appear elsewhere under other names (the verification lesson's reverse search), and requests that hurry or flatter. The Grade 8 teams work as **classroom investigators**: given cases, they gather evidence, test the identity against sources, and present the verdict with its proof — suspicion is not a verdict; evidence is.
