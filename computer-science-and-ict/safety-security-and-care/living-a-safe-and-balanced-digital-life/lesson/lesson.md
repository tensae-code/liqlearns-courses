# Living a Safe and Balanced Digital Life

## Do it first

The security lessons guard the machine and the data. The last session guards the person: the eyes, the sleep, the temper, the hours. A digital life can be perfectly secure and quietly miserable — this lesson is about the other half of safe.

## Sub lesson 1 — Balance — time and body

**Balance** begins with **time**: the phone's hours measured honestly (the device itself reports them) and budgeted like money — school, family, play and sleep paid first, the feed given what remains, not the reverse. The **body** keeps its accounts too: eyes rested by distance and breaks, posture kept, the device put down before sleep so the night can do its work. None of this is the machine's setting; it is the owner's, which is why the book teaches it as a life skill in a technology book — the technology will not teach it at all.

## Sub lesson 2 — Balance — mind and friendship; the safe life whole

The **mind**'s balance is the feed's diet: what angers and frightens is served most because it holds us longest, so the balanced citizen curates — follows what nourishes, mutes what poisons, and remembers the misinformation lessons when the feed performs its certainties. **Friendship**'s balance keeps the digital in its place: present with the people present, the phone face-down in company, online kindness held to the standard of the room. Safe *and* balanced — the experience's two words are the whole curriculum of the digital citizen in miniature: protected from the world's harms, and master of the tool that could otherwise master them.
