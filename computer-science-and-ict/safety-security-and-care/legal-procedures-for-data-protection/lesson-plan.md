## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What the law does for data** — Data-protection **law** sets the duties around personal data: who may **collect** it and for what purposes, how it must be **kept** (secured, accurate, not kept forever), whose **consent** is needed, and what **rights** the person it describes holds — to know, to correct, to object. Legal procedures are the law's working form: the **steps and documents** by which the duties are actually done — permissions obtained, breaches reported, complaints filed, penalties applied.
2. **Procedures in practice** — In practice the procedures touch everyone: an organisation **registers and documents** what personal data it holds and why; a **breach** must be reported to the authority and the affected people within the law's terms; a citizen **requests** to see or correct their data by a stated route; and **violations** meet stated consequences — fines and liability — decided by stated bodies. Jordan's Grade 7 lesson teaches procedures, not slogans, because protection a citizen cannot operate is protection on paper only: the pupil learns the route as well as the right.
