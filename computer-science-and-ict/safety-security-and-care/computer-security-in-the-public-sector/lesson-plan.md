## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Why public-sector security is its own subject** — Government systems differ from a firm's in what they hold and whom they answer to: **citizens' records** — identity, land, tax, health and education data the citizen **cannot choose to take elsewhere** — and **public services** whose failure stops a whole province's paperwork, not one shop's sales. The data is the people's, held in trust; a breach is not a commercial loss but a **broken trust between the state and its citizens**. That is why the book gives the sector its own closing section, after the private and personal measures.
2. **The measures — policy, people and systems together** — The public sector's measures run on three legs. **Policy**: written security rules for every office — who may access which records, how media and passwords are handled, what happens when rules break — the same in every office, because the citizen's record travels between all of them. **People**: staff **trained** to the rules and **accountable** under them, with access given by duty, not by rank or favour. **Systems**: the chapter's own defences at public scale — **access control, backups, firewalls, antivirus, and logs that are actually reviewed**. Laos closes the chapter here deliberately: the learner who will staff these offices meets their duty before they meet their desk.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
