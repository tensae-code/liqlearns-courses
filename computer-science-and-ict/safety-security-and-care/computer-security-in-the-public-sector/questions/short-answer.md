# Computer Security in the Public Sector — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What do government systems hold that a firm's do not?**
**Answer: Citizens' records the citizen cannot choose to take elsewhere**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) Name three kinds of citizen record named.**
**Answer: Identity, land and tax records (also health, education)**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What is a public-sector breach, beyond a loss?**
**Answer: A broken trust between the state and its citizens**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) Why must office rules be the same everywhere?**
**Answer: A citizen's record travels between all the offices**

**5. (Sub lesson 2) What are policy's subjects?**
**Answer: Who may access records, and how media and passwords are handled**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) How is staff access given in the measures?**
**Answer: By duty, not by rank or favour**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) Name the system defences at public scale.**
**Answer: Access control, backups, firewalls, antivirus and reviewed logs**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What are the measures' three legs?**
**Answer: Policy, people and systems**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) Why does the book close its security chapter here?**
**Answer: Its learners will staff these offices — duty before desk**

**10. (Sub lesson 1) Which chapter teaches this?**
**Answer: Laos Grade 10 ICT, Chapter 3 §5**
