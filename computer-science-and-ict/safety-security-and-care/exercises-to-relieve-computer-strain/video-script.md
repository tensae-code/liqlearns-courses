# Video script — Exercises to Relieve Computer Strain

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Computer work strains the body quietly: the **eyes** tire from holding one distance and blinking too little, the **neck and shoulders** tighten from holding still, the **back** suffers a held posture, and the **wrists and fingers** repeat the same small movements hundreds of times.
None of it hurts in the first minute — that is the trap.
Strain is the price of stillness, and it is paid either in small change during the work — short breaks and exercises — or all at once later, as pain.
The book puts this lesson inside its health chapter, straight after healthy computer use: first set the workplace right, then move the body the work has held.
The exercises are short and done at the desk.
For the **eyes**: look far away across the room and back, roll the eyes gently, and rest them closed or covered for a moment.
For **neck and shoulders**: turn the head slowly side to side, drop each ear toward its shoulder, roll the shoulders in circles, and shrug up and release.
For the **back**: stand, stretch the arms overhead, and bend gently.
For **wrists and fingers**: make fists and open the fingers wide, rotate the wrists, and shake the hands out.
The working rule is rhythm, not marathon: a **short break with exercises every half hour or so of work**, and a stop at once if anything hurts sharply — exercises relieve strain, they are not a test to push through.
