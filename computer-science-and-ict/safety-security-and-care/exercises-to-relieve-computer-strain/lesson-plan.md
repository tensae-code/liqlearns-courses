# Lesson plan — Exercises to Relieve Computer Strain

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Safety, Security and Care · **Lesson:** Exercises to Relieve Computer Strain
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Afghanistan (2026-11-14) — in the title Safety, Security and Care on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Afghanistan's *Computer* Grade 11, because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Why computer work strains the body** — Computer work strains the body quietly: the **eyes** tire from holding one distance and blinking too little, the **neck and shoulders** tighten from holding still, the **back** suffers a held posture, and the **wrists and fingers** repeat the same small movements hundreds of times. None of it hurts in the first minute — that is the trap. Strain is the price of stillness, and it is paid either in small change during the work — short breaks and exercises — or all at once later, as pain. The book puts this lesson inside its health chapter, straight after healthy computer use: first set the workplace right, then move the body the work has held.
2. **The exercises themselves** — The exercises are short and done at the desk. For the **eyes**: look far away across the room and back, roll the eyes gently, and rest them closed or covered for a moment. For **neck and shoulders**: turn the head slowly side to side, drop each ear toward its shoulder, roll the shoulders in circles, and shrug up and release. For the **back**: stand, stretch the arms overhead, and bend gently. For **wrists and fingers**: make fists and open the fingers wide, rotate the wrists, and shake the hands out. The working rule is rhythm, not marathon: a **short break with exercises every half hour or so of work**, and a stop at once if anything hurts sharply — exercises relieve strain, they are not a test to push through.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
