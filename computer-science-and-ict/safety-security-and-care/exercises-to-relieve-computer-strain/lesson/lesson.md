# Exercises to Relieve Computer Strain

## Do it first

Your shoulders ache, your eyes sting — the computer will not rest you; you must rest yourself. In this lesson you learn the strain-relief exercises Afghanistan teaches its computer students.

## Sub lesson 1 — Why computer work strains the body

Computer work strains the body quietly: the **eyes** tire from holding one distance and blinking too little, the **neck and shoulders** tighten from holding still, the **back** suffers a held posture, and the **wrists and fingers** repeat the same small movements hundreds of times. None of it hurts in the first minute — that is the trap. Strain is the price of stillness, and it is paid either in small change during the work — short breaks and exercises — or all at once later, as pain. The book puts this lesson inside its health chapter, straight after healthy computer use: first set the workplace right, then move the body the work has held.

## Sub lesson 2 — The exercises themselves

The exercises are short and done at the desk. For the **eyes**: look far away across the room and back, roll the eyes gently, and rest them closed or covered for a moment. For **neck and shoulders**: turn the head slowly side to side, drop each ear toward its shoulder, roll the shoulders in circles, and shrug up and release. For the **back**: stand, stretch the arms overhead, and bend gently. For **wrists and fingers**: make fists and open the fingers wide, rotate the wrists, and shake the hands out. The working rule is rhythm, not marathon: a **short break with exercises every half hour or so of work**, and a stop at once if anything hurts sharply — exercises relieve strain, they are not a test to push through.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Short exercises relieve the strain computer work puts on the eyes, neck, shoulders, back, wrists and fingers: Afghanistan's Grade 11 book teaches them as their own lesson inside its health chapter, because the relief must be practised, not just known. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: Holding one distance and blinking too little. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: Repeat the same small movements. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: Look far away and back, roll the eyes gently, and rest them closed. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: Standing, stretching the arms overhead, and bending gently. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
