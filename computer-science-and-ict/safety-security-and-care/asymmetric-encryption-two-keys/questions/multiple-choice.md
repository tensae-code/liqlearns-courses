# Asymmetric Encryption — Two Keys — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) To send a secret message to Ana, you encrypt it with —**
a) Your public key  b) Ana's public key  c) Your private key  d) Ana's private key
**Answer: b) Ana's public key** — Encrypt with the receiver's public key; only the receiver's private key can open it.

**2. (Sub lesson 1) The private key must —**
a) Be published  b) Travel with the message  c) Never leave its owner  d) Change every message
**Answer: c) Never leave its owner** — The private key's whole power comes from never travelling.

**3. (Sub lesson 1) Ben locks a message with Ana's padlock (public key). Who can open it?**
a) Ben  b) Anyone with a public key  c) Only Ana, with her private key  d) Nobody
**Answer: c) Only Ana, with her private key** — Not even the locker can reopen what the pair locked.

**4. (Sub lesson 1) Knowing someone's public key lets an attacker —**
a) Work out the private key easily  b) Decrypt their messages  c) Encrypt messages to them — which anyone may do  d) Sign as them
**Answer: c) Encrypt messages to them — which anyone may do** — Locking is public work; unlocking is the protected direction.

**5. (Sub lesson 2) Asymmetric encryption's main cost is —**
a) It needs paper  b) It is slow compared with symmetric encryption  c) Keys are heavy  d) It only works at night
**Answer: b) It is slow compared with symmetric encryption** — Heavy mathematics — too slow for bulk data, perfect for delivering a key.

**6. (Sub lesson 2) In a real protected web connection, asymmetric encryption is used to —**
a) Encrypt the whole visit  b) Carry a fresh symmetric key safely, then symmetric encryption carries the data  c) Replace passwords  d) Draw the padlock icon only
**Answer: b) Carry a fresh symmetric key safely, then symmetric encryption carries the data** — The marry-the-two design: each method does the job it is best at.
