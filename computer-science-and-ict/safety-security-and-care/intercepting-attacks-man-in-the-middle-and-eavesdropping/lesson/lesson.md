# Intercepting attacks — man in the middle and eavesdropping

## Do it first

Two friends message each other across an open café Wi-Fi. A third person sits between them, reading every word — and neither friend notices anything slow or strange. If nothing looks wrong, how would you ever know? That is the danger this lesson names, twice over.

## Man in the middle

A man-in-the-middle attack intercepts communication between people and steals data from their conversation. Open Wi-Fi networks are the most common place for it — the convenient free connection is exactly where a stranger can sit in the middle.

## Eavesdropping — sniffing, snooping

Eavesdropping, also called sniffing or snooping, is when a hacker intercepts, deletes or modifies data transmitted between two devices, exploiting unsecured, unencrypted network communications — typically when a user sends sensitive business data over an open network. It is hard to spot because a listening device may not affect the performance of devices or networks at all: nothing slows, nothing breaks, and the data is gone.
