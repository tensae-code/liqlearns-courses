# Video script — Breaking a Code With Frequency Analysis

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Every language uses its letters **unequally**: in French, E towers over the rest; in English, E, T and A lead. A substitution cipher replaces each letter faithfully — so the cipher's most frequent symbol **is** the language's most frequent letter, wearing a mask. **Frequency analysis (analyse fréquentielle)** counts the cipher symbols, ranks them, and matches the ranking against the language's known habits: the first mask falls, then its neighbours.
One identified letter unlocks patterns: a one-letter word must be A or I (in English); a doubled symbol inside a word narrows its owner; common pairs and endings confirm or correct the guesses. Each new letter makes the next easier — the text opens like ice cracking — until the whole message reads plain. The inquiry's lesson for cipher-makers is the mirror: a cipher that preserves the language's habits carries its own key in its statistics.
