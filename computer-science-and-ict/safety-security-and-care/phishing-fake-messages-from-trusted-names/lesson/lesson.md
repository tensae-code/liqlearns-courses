# Phishing — fake messages from trusted names

## Do it first

An email arrives from 'your bank'. It has the bank's name, its colours, an urgent warning — and one small wrong letter in the sender's address. Everything is real except the sender. What would you check before you clicked anything in it?

## The fake that looks real

Phishing is sending fraudulent communications that appear to come from a reputable source, usually by email. The message borrows a trusted name — a bank, a company, a service you know — precisely so that you will not look twice.

[Picture 20911 — Phishing — a genuine-looking email, a false sender]

## What it wants

Its aim is to steal sensitive data, such as credit-card and login information — or to install malware on the victim's machine. It is a common attack, and the book's charge is direct: everyone should learn to recognise it in order to protect themselves. Recognition is the defence.
