# Social engineering — tricking the person, not the machine

## Do it first

Your phone rings. 'This is your mobile company. Your device is in danger — quickly, tell me your details so we can protect you.' The voice is calm, official, urgent. Nothing on your phone has been hacked — so what, exactly, is being attacked?

## The trick

Social engineering creates a social situation to extract information from the user. No code is written, no system is broken into: a situation is built — an authority, an emergency, a favour — inside which handing over information feels like the natural, helpful thing to do.

## The book's example

A call supposedly from the mobile company says: 'Your device is in danger.' The victim blindly trusts, and hands over all their information without any verification. The two failures are the lesson: blind trust, and no verification. A real company can be checked — call it back on its own number — and that one habit breaks the trick.
