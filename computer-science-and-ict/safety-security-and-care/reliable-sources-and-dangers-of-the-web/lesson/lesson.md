# Reliable Sources and Dangers of the Web

## Do it first

Mauritania's Grade 8 course gives a whole fiche to this: reliability of sources and dangers of the web. This lesson teaches the checks before the trust.

## Sub lesson 1 — Checking whether a source is reliable

A **reliable source** can answer four questions: **Who** wrote this — a named person or body with a reason to know? **When** — is it in date for the question you are asking? **Why** — to inform, or to sell, mock or deceive? And **does it agree** with other trustworthy sources? A school, ministry or maker's own site carries weight a stranger's page does not; a claim found in only one place stays a claim, not a fact, until a second source confirms it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Dangers of the web

The web's **dangers** include false information dressed as news, pages that imitate trusted ones to steal details, downloads carrying harmful programs, and strangers whose friendly contact aims at harm. The defences are habits: check the source before believing or sharing, keep private information private, download only from rightful sources, and tell a trusted adult when anything online frightens, pressures or confuses you. Sharing an unchecked claim spreads the danger — checking first stops it with you.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Not everything on the web deserves belief: a reliable source can be named, checked and matched against others, while the web's dangers — false information, traps and harmful contact — are beaten first by that same habit of checking. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: Who wrote it, when, why — and whether others agree. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: A claim, not a fact — until a second source confirms it. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: False information, and pages imitating trusted ones to steal details. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: Check the source first. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
