## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Checking whether a source is reliable** — A **reliable source** can answer four questions: **Who** wrote this — a named person or body with a reason to know? **When** — is it in date for the question you are asking? **Why** — to inform, or to sell, mock or deceive? And **does it agree** with other trustworthy sources? A school, ministry or maker's own site carries weight a stranger's page does not; a claim found in only one place stays a claim, not a fact, until a second source confirms it.
2. **Dangers of the web** — The web's **dangers** include false information dressed as news, pages that imitate trusted ones to steal details, downloads carrying harmful programs, and strangers whose friendly contact aims at harm. The defences are habits: check the source before believing or sharing, keep private information private, download only from rightful sources, and tell a trusted adult when anything online frightens, pressures or confuses you. Sharing an unchecked claim spreads the danger — checking first stops it with you.
