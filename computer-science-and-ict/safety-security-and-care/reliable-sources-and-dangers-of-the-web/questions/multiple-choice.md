# Reliable Sources and Dangers of the Web — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What four questions test a source?**
a) Who wrote it, when, why — and whether others agree  b) A school, ministry or maker's own site  c) Its information is recent enough for the question being asked  d) Check the source first
**Answer: a) Who wrote it, when, why — and whether others agree**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What kind of sites carry the most weight?**
a) A claim, not a fact — until a second source confirms it  b) A school, ministry or maker's own site  c) False information, and pages imitating trusted ones to steal details  d) Tell a trusted adult
**Answer: b) A school, ministry or maker's own site**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) A claim found in only one place is…**
a) A page made to sell or deceive informs differently from one made to inform  b) Steal your details by looking trusted  c) A claim, not a fact — until a second source confirms it  d) It spreads the danger to everyone who receives it
**Answer: c) A claim, not a fact — until a second source confirms it**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) Why does the 'why' question matter?**
a) Its information is recent enough for the question being asked  b) Check the source first  c) Who wrote it, when, why — and whether others agree  d) A page made to sell or deceive informs differently from one made to inform
**Answer: d) A page made to sell or deceive informs differently from one made to inform**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) What does 'in date' mean for a source?**
a) Its information is recent enough for the question being asked  b) False information, and pages imitating trusted ones to steal details  c) Tell a trusted adult  d) A school, ministry or maker's own site
**Answer: a) Its information is recent enough for the question being asked**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) Name two dangers of the web.**
a) Steal your details by looking trusted  b) False information, and pages imitating trusted ones to steal details  c) It spreads the danger to everyone who receives it  d) A claim, not a fact — until a second source confirms it
**Answer: b) False information, and pages imitating trusted ones to steal details**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What do imitating pages try to do?**
a) Check the source first  b) Who wrote it, when, why — and whether others agree  c) Steal your details by looking trusted  d) A page made to sell or deceive informs differently from one made to inform
**Answer: c) Steal your details by looking trusted**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What is the defence habit before sharing?**
a) Tell a trusted adult  b) A school, ministry or maker's own site  c) Its information is recent enough for the question being asked  d) Check the source first
**Answer: d) Check the source first**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) What should you do when something online frightens or pressures you?**
a) Tell a trusted adult  b) It spreads the danger to everyone who receives it  c) A claim, not a fact — until a second source confirms it  d) False information, and pages imitating trusted ones to steal details
**Answer: a) Tell a trusted adult**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) What does sharing an unchecked claim do?**
a) Who wrote it, when, why — and whether others agree  b) It spreads the danger to everyone who receives it  c) A page made to sell or deceive informs differently from one made to inform  d) Steal your details by looking trusted
