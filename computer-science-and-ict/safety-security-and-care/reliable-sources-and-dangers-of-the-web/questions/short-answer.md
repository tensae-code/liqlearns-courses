# Reliable Sources and Dangers of the Web — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What four questions test a source?**
**Answer: Who wrote it, when, why — and whether others agree**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What kind of sites carry the most weight?**
**Answer: A school, ministry or maker's own site**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) A claim found in only one place is…**
**Answer: A claim, not a fact — until a second source confirms it**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) Why does the 'why' question matter?**
**Answer: A page made to sell or deceive informs differently from one made to inform**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) What does 'in date' mean for a source?**
**Answer: Its information is recent enough for the question being asked**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) Name two dangers of the web.**
**Answer: False information, and pages imitating trusted ones to steal details**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What do imitating pages try to do?**
**Answer: Steal your details by looking trusted**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What is the defence habit before sharing?**
**Answer: Check the source first**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) What should you do when something online frightens or pressures you?**
**Answer: Tell a trusted adult**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) What does sharing an unchecked claim do?**
**Answer: It spreads the danger to everyone who receives it**
