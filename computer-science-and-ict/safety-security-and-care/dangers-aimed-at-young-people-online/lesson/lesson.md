# Dangers aimed at young people online

## Do it first

Three phones. On one, a cruel comment spreads. On the second, a friendly stranger is not who they claim to be. On the third, a pupil posts their school, their street and their photo for the whole world. Different harms — but all three aim at the same target. Who?

## Bullying and predators

Cyberbullying is ridiculing or humiliating young people on social media and game platforms — sending, posting or sharing negative, harmful, false or mean content, including sharing someone's private information to embarrass or humiliate them; some of it crosses into unlawful or criminal behaviour. Cyber predators are people who exploit younger people online for sexual and other purposes, often pretending to be someone else or lying about themselves to gain trust.

## Posting too much

The third danger is the young person's own posting: pupils who do not yet understand social boundaries post personally identifiable information — in social-media profiles, for example — that should not be public. The lesson closes with the book's plain warning: religious fundamentalists, extremists, terrorists and sex traffickers find the Internet a safe zone for reaching the young — which is why these three dangers are taught by name.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: three dangers are aimed especially at the young: cyberbullying, cyber predators, and young people posting private information they do not yet understand should stay private. Then name the key words of this lesson — cyberbullying, cyber predators, personally identifiable information, posting private information — and say what each one means.
