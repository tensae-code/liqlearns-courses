# Denial of Service — flooding a service shut

## Do it first

A bank door with a hundred people pushing to enter — but ninety-nine of them want nothing inside. They only stand there so the real customers cannot get in. Watch the GIF, then say: who is harmed, and how, when nobody breaks anything at all?

## The flood

A Denial of Service attack — DoS — hurts a system by flooding its target with requests, stopping regular users from connecting. The service itself is not stolen or changed; it is denied — network-based services cannot be provided in a timely manner because the doorway is jammed with requests that want nothing.

[Picture 20908 — Denial of Service — a door jammed by fake requests]

## The bank example

A DoS attack on a bank's IT infrastructure could stop ATM and CBE Birr services. Nothing is taken from the bank's vault — but customers are disappointed, they cannot reach their own money, and the bank's reputation is damaged. Denial is the damage.
