# Lesson plan — Setting up a workstation — the healthy angles

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Safety, Security and Care · **Lesson:** Setting up a workstation — the healthy angles
**Place in the curriculum:** Title 13 — Safety, Security and Care in the Computer Science and ICT ladder (TITLES.md); stocked from Ethiopia's Grade 8 book, Unit 5 — Computer Ergonomics. The ladder is the recommended order, never a forced one.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the exact workstation set-up: the angles and alignments that prevent soft-tissue injuries and muscle disorders — plus what long screen work does to the eyes, and how to judge a sitting.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the ergonomics introduction — the learner knows bad sitting harms; this lesson gives the settings that prevent the harm.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. ## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Computer ergonomics — sitting without harming your body. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Set a workstation by the eleven principles — elbows, shoulders, wrists, joints, feet, head, eyes, seat, backrest, keyboard and mouse, laptop.
2. Name computer vision syndrome and its symptoms (teacher guide — evidence).
3. Judge the paired sitting figures and say which style is appropriate, and why (teacher guide — evidence).

## What you need
A chair, a desk, a screen and keyboard (real or drawn life-size on paper), and a partner to check your angles.

## If the learner is stuck
- Sets the screen first and forgets the chair → the set-up starts with the body: seat, feet, joints, then screen and keyboard come to the body, not the other way round.
- Thinks eye strain is just tiredness → long screen work has a named syndrome with named symptoms; the set-up (eyes at the top third of the screen, screen position against reflections) is its prevention.
