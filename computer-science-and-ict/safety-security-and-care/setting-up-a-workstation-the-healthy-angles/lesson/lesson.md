# Setting up a workstation — the healthy angles

## Do it first

Sit at a workstation exactly as it stands. Do not fix anything yet. Now — adjusting one thing at a time — set the chair, the screen and the keyboard into the correct positions. This lesson gives the exact settings to adjust to. Setting up is a sequence of adjustments, and the order below is the order to make them in.

## Sub lesson 1 — The set-up principles

Long computer use can cause muscle diseases and injuries leading to disorders. The goal of ergonomics is to **prevent soft-tissue injuries and muscle disorders** — so both the workstation and the sitting must be right. The recommended set-up:

- **Elbows above the desk at 90–110 degrees.**
- **Shoulders relaxed, not hunched.**
- **Wrists in line with forearms.**
- **Hips, knees and ankles at 90 degrees** while seated.
- **Feet flat on the ground or a footrest.**
- **Head upright, with ears aligned with shoulders.**
- **Eyes looking at the top third of the screen.**
- **Seat length enough to support beneath the thighs.**
- **Backrest angled at 90–110 degrees, with lumbar support in line with the lower back.**
- **Keyboard aligned with your nose, and the mouse gripped loosely.**
- **A laptop used with a riser, an external keyboard and an external mouse.**

## Sub lesson 2 — Your eyes, and judging a sitting (teacher guide — evidence)

Your eyes pay for long screen work too. Everyone spending long periods at a computer — at work, at school or at home — risks **headaches, burning eyes, red eyes and a stiff neck**. Together these make up **computer vision syndrome**. Prolonged computer work can cause physical stress that could eventually lead to a disability — the set-up in Sub lesson 1 is how you hold it off.

Now judge. In the paired figure, two pupils sit in two styles. The verdict: **the left-hand sitting style is the appropriate one** — likely free of muscle injuries. **The right-hand style is wrong and harsh for health**, causing back pain and muscle injury. So the left style is the recommended way of sitting.
