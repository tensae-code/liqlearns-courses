# Lesson plan — Formulating a Cyber Security Policy

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Safety, Security and Care · **Lesson:** Formulating a Cyber Security Policy
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Bangladesh (2026-11-14) — in the title Safety, Security and Care on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). **The one thing this lesson teaches:** formulating a cyber security policy: a class writes its own security policy — the rules, roles and responses that govern its digital life — and carries cyber awareness beyond its own walls (Bangladesh Grade 7 Digital Technology, Learning Experience 4, Sessions 5–6).
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The policy — rules with reasons** — A **cyber security policy** is a written agreement that states: what must be **protected**, the **rules** of protection (passwords, sharing, downloads, strangers — the course's lessons gathered into clauses), who holds which **role** (who keeps accounts, who receives reports), and what happens when a rule is **broken** or an attack succeeds — the response, written before the panic. Each clause carries its **reason** beside it, because a policy whose reasons are known is followed when nobody watches, which is the only following that protects anyone.
2. **Cyber awareness — the policy goes public** — The final session turns outward: **cyber awareness** — the class carries its policy's teaching to the school and families, as the verification campaign carried checking: posters, a presentation, the policy's best clauses translated for younger children. A policy locked in a drawer protects one classroom's intentions; awareness multiplies it into a school's habits. The experience closes where the whole ICT course aims: citizens who do not merely survive the digital world but govern their corner of it — and teach the governing.
