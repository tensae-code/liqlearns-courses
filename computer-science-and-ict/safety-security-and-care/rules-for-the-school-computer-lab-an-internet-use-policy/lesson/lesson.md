# Rules for the school computer lab — an Internet-use policy

## Do it first

Imagine the laboratory door carries one poster with every rule a pupil needs. Eight rules. Before you read the teacher's ready-made policy, draft your own eight — then compare: which of yours are missing, and which of the book's did you forget?

## The policy, first half

Computers, network and Internet are for educational purposes only, and online material is to be educational, not entertainment. A pupil never reveals full name, address, telephone number or other personal information on a school computer. Pupils never agree to meet someone contacted through the Internet without parental permission. A pupil who meets dangerous or inappropriate material, or anything that makes them uncomfortable, tells the teacher.

## The policy, second half

A pupil who spots a security problem notifies the teacher or building administrator at once — and does not demonstrate the problem to others or access unauthorised material. Online passwords are strong: capitals, small letters, numbers and special characters, more than 8 characters. Pupils do not share passwords, use others' passwords or accounts, or try to circumvent network security. And work found on the Internet is not plagiarised.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: a school laboratory's Internet-use policy: educational use only, no personal information revealed, no meetings with online contacts without permission, problems reported to the teacher, strong passwords, and no plagiarism. Then name the key words of this lesson — educational purposes only, never revealed, report, don't demonstrate, strong passwords in the policy — and say what each one means.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the eight rules as a poster, in your own clear words.
2. Judge three situations against the policy and cite the rule each time.
3. Say why 'report, don't demonstrate' protects the reporter too.
