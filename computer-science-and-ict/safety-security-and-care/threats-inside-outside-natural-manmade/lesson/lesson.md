# Threats — inside, outside, natural, man-made

## Do it first

Four troubles reach one school in a week: a storm cuts the power, a pupil deletes a file by mistake, an outsider tries to break in, and an insider copies the marks file. Before you judge any of them, sort them: where did each come from, and was it meant?

## Where threats come from

Computer security threats come from internal sources — inside an institution — and from external ones outside it. They also come from environmental and natural factors, as well as from human and technical (man-made, artificial) ones. The next lessons take the sorts one by one: first the natural threats, then the attacks that arrive through the Internet.

## Meant, or not

Attacks can be malicious or non-malicious. And they can be accidental — a mistake, an unintended action — or intentional, done deliberately. A deleted file is damage either way, but the sorting matters: you defend against a storm, a mistake and a deliberate attack in three different ways.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: security threats come from inside and outside an institution, and from natural factors as well as human and technical ones — and attacks can be malicious or not, accidental or deliberate. Then name the key words of this lesson — internal threats, external threats, accidental attacks, intentional attacks — and say what each one means.
