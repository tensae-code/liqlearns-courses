# Encryption and RSA Public-Key Cryptography — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) Ciphertext is —**
a) the key itself  b) plaintext after encryption has scrambled it  c) a message that was never sent  d) the receiver's address
**Answer: b) plaintext after encryption has scrambled it** — Encryption turns plaintext into ciphertext using a key.

**2. (Sub lesson 1) The hard question symmetric encryption raises is —**
a) how to write the message  b) how two people agree the shared key without an eavesdropper hearing it  c) how to print the message  d) which computer is faster
**Answer: b) how two people agree the shared key without an eavesdropper hearing it** — One shared key must somehow be shared secretly — public-key encryption answers this.

**3. (Sub lesson 1) In public-key encryption, the public key —**
a) only unlocks  b) only locks, and may be shared with everyone  c) must be kept as secret as the private key  d) is the message
**Answer: b) only locks, and may be shared with everyone** — Publish the padlock; keep the key.

**4. (Sub lesson 2) RSA's safety rests on —**
a) nobody knowing how RSA works  b) multiplying two primes being easy while factoring their product is practically impossible  c) using very long passwords only  d) the internet being trustworthy
**Answer: b) multiplying two primes being easy while factoring their product is practically impossible** — The method is published; the factoring is the wall.

**5. (Sub lesson 2) The private key in RSA is worked out from —**
a) the product alone  b) the two prime numbers themselves  c) the sender's name  d) the ciphertext
**Answer: b) the two prime numbers themselves** — Whoever knows the primes can unlock; the public sees only the product.
