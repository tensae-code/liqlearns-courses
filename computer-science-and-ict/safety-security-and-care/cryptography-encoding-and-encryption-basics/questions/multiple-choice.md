# Cryptography — Encoding and Encryption Basics — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What does encoding do?**
a) Rewrites information in another agreed form  b) No — about form; anyone with the code can read it back  c) Letters becoming numbers, or numbers becoming binary  d) Protection works on the represented form of the information
**Answer: a) Rewrites information in another agreed form**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) Is encoding about secrecy?**
a) Letters becoming numbers, or numbers becoming binary  b) No — about form; anyone with the code can read it back  c) Protection works on the represented form of the information  d) Anyone who knows the agreed code
**Answer: b) No — about form; anyone with the code can read it back**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) Give an example of encoding from the lesson.**
a) Protection works on the represented form of the information  b) Anyone who knows the agreed code  c) Letters becoming numbers, or numbers becoming binary  d) The readable message before encryption
**Answer: c) Letters becoming numbers, or numbers becoming binary**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) Why must a computer encode before it can protect?**
a) Anyone who knows the agreed code  b) The readable message before encryption  c) The scrambled, unreadable result of encryption  d) Protection works on the represented form of the information
**Answer: d) Protection works on the represented form of the information**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) Who can decode an encoded message?**
a) Anyone who knows the agreed code  b) The readable message before encryption  c) The scrambled, unreadable result of encryption  d) The right key
**Answer: a) Anyone who knows the agreed code**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What is plaintext?**
a) The scrambled, unreadable result of encryption  b) The readable message before encryption  c) The right key  d) They are long enough that guessing is hopeless
**Answer: b) The readable message before encryption**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What is ciphertext?**
a) The right key  b) They are long enough that guessing is hopeless  c) The scrambled, unreadable result of encryption  d) Banking and messaging (any two from the lesson)
**Answer: c) The scrambled, unreadable result of encryption**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What does decryption need?**
a) They are long enough that guessing is hopeless  b) Banking and messaging (any two from the lesson)  c) Rewrites information in another agreed form  d) The right key
**Answer: d) The right key**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) What makes modern keys safe?**
a) They are long enough that guessing is hopeless  b) Banking and messaging (any two from the lesson)  c) Rewrites information in another agreed form  d) No — about form; anyone with the code can read it back
**Answer: a) They are long enough that guessing is hopeless**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) Name two things encryption protects.**
a) Rewrites information in another agreed form  b) Banking and messaging (any two from the lesson)  c) No — about form; anyone with the code can read it back  d) Letters becoming numbers, or numbers becoming binary
