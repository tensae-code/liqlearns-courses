# Cryptography — Encoding and Encryption Basics — true-false

Say whether each statement is true or false.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "What does encoding do?" is: Rewrites information in another agreed form**
**Answer: True**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "Is encoding about secrecy?" is: Anyone who knows the agreed code**
**Answer: False** — No — about form; anyone with the code can read it back

**3. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "Give an example of encoding from the lesson." is: Letters becoming numbers, or numbers becoming binary**
**Answer: True**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "Why must a computer encode before it can protect?" is: The scrambled, unreadable result of encryption**
**Answer: False** — Protection works on the represented form of the information

**5. (Sub lesson 1) True or false: the answer to "Who can decode an encoded message?" is: Anyone who knows the agreed code**
**Answer: True**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "What is plaintext?" is: They are long enough that guessing is hopeless**
**Answer: False** — The readable message before encryption

**7. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "What is ciphertext?" is: The scrambled, unreadable result of encryption**
**Answer: True**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "What does decryption need?" is: Rewrites information in another agreed form**
**Answer: False** — The right key

**9. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "What makes modern keys safe?" is: They are long enough that guessing is hopeless**
**Answer: True**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) True or false: the answer to "Name two things encryption protects." is: Letters becoming numbers, or numbers becoming binary**
**Answer: False** — Banking and messaging (any two from the lesson)
