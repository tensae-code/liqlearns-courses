# Choosing your CIA priorities

## Do it first

Two buildings. A school wants its knowledge resources trustworthy and open to every student. A military base wants its information seen by almost nobody. Both use the same triangle from the last lesson — but they lean it in opposite directions. Which way does each one lean?

## The same triangle, different leans

Achieving all three of the triad at once can be costly, so organisations choose focuses by priority. Schools usually aim at trustworthy knowledge resources open to the wider student community — integrity and availability first. Military institutions focus on confidentiality and integrity — who may see it, and that it is never wrongly changed.

## Apply it to your own school

The group work applies the triad at home: who may see or modify examination results in your school? Can a director or a homeroom teacher change marks — and should they be able to? And what Internet support does the school offer to students with disability? Answering those three questions is choosing your school's CIA priorities in real life.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: achieving all three parts of the triad at once can be costly, so organisations choose focuses by priority — schools and military institutions choose differently. Then name the key words of this lesson — CIA priorities, a school's lean, a military lean, examination results — and say what each one means.
