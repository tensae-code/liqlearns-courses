## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Why transfer is the exposed moment** — In **transfer**, data leaves its guarded home and crosses **networks it does not own**: cables, airwaves, other companies' machines. On the road it can be **copied by watchers** (a public network's other users, a compromised stop), **changed** (an altered mark, a swapped account number), or **delayed and lost**. Jordan's importance argument is arithmetic: a school may store data carefully for years and lose it in the three seconds of one careless sending — protection that ends at the door is protection unfinished.
2. **Securing the journey — the standing means** — The means answer the road's dangers one by one: **encryption in transit** (the sealed envelope — HTTPS, VPNs, encrypted channels: watchers copy noise), **integrity checks** (hashes and signatures: a changed message is detected), **verified endpoints** (certificates: the receiver is truly the receiver), **authorised channels** (approved services, not a stranger's free tool), and **minimal sending** (only the data the journey needs travels). The lesson closes the transfer trilogy the unit builds: protect data stored, protect data used, protect data moving — the same data, three postures, one duty.
