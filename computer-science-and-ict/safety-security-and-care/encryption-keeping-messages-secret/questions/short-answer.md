# Encryption — Keeping Messages Secret — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What is encryption?**
**Answer: Turning a readable message into a scrambled ciphertext that reveals nothing without the secret**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What are plaintext and ciphertext?**
**Answer: The readable message, and its scrambled form**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What two things does encryption work with?**
**Answer: A method — the scrambling rule — and a key, the secret value**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What is decryption?**
**Answer: The journey back from ciphertext to plaintext, possible only with the key**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) May the method be publicly known?**
**Answer: Yes — safety rests on the key's secrecy, not on hiding the method**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What ground does encryption let messages cross?**
**Answer: Untrusted ground — networks anyone can listen to**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) Name two honest limits of encryption**
**Answer: A weak method or guessable key falls to patient trying; the key must itself reach the receiver safely (also: content is protected, not the message's existence)**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What can watchers still see despite encryption?**
**Answer: Who wrote to whom, and when**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) How does Egypt introduce encryption?**
**Answer: Through Python — pupils write a first encryption program, in the Grade 8 Term 2 unit**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) What does making a cipher teach that reading about one does not?**
**Answer: Its limits — learned honestly by building it**
