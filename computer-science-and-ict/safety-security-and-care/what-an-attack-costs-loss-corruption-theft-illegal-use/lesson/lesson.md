# What an attack costs — loss, corruption, theft, illegal use

## Do it first

A school's marks are raised by an intruder. A drive fails and a term's work is gone. Someone runs a private business over the school Internet. Nothing here looks alike — yet the book files all of it under one heading: what an attack costs. Impacts, the book says, can be psychological, financial, legal or political. Sort the three cases as you read the four losses.

## Loss and corruption

Whatever a threat's origin, its impacts are psychological, financial, legal or political. Loss one: destruction or loss of information — destroyed components, data lost to a failed hard disk. Loss two: corruption of information — unauthorised alteration of stored files: adding or reducing student marks in the school database, or posting documents the school considers illegal or unacceptable on its social-media channels.

## Theft of services and illegal usage

Loss three: theft of services — unauthorised use of computer or network services, such as using school Internet access granted for education to run unrelated online marketing. Loss four: illegal usage — using the system's normal function for the attacker's other purpose: spreading texts or videos promoting inter-communal or inter-religious conflict over school Internet is the example, and it can be a criminal act.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: the first four losses an attack causes are: destruction or loss of information, corruption of information, theft of services, and illegal usage. Then name the key words of this lesson — destruction or loss of information, corruption of information, theft of services, illegal usage — and say what each one means.
