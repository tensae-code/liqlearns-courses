# Cleaning a computer — why, how often, with what

## Do it first

Look closely at a computer — its vents, its fan openings, the gaps between keys. What is gathering there? Dust. It looks harmless. This lesson explains why it is not, how often to fight it, and with what.

## Sub lesson 1 — Why and how often

Cleaning helps the computer **run smoothly and last longer**. Dust is the enemy, and it works four ways: it **insulates** (wraps parts in a warm coat), **blocks air circulation**, causes **overheating** — and conductive dust can **short-circuit** parts, or even **cause fire**. The standing rule: **clean the outside with a dry cloth at least once a week.**

## Sub lesson 2 — The cleaning kit

The book's kit table: a **small brush** for dusting electrical components; **cleaning wipers** for the system-unit casing, the monitor and the keyboard; **compressed air** for blasting dust out — watch the GIF: the dust leaves, and air can flow again; and a **CD-cleaning kit** for dust and grease on CDs — CDs themselves are kept in cases and handled by their edges. The teacher guide adds three kit items pupils may name from home: **microfiber cloths, a screwdriver and cotton cloths** — know them as guide additions to the book's four.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion why dust is dangerous, the weekly rule, and each kit item's job.
