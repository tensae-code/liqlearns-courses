# Computer Science and ICT course — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

Computer Science and ICT is one world course (his architecture
ruling, 2026-11-11: the thing itself is the same everywhere;
only the way of teaching differs, and the way shows inside as
examples — under that principle Computer Science and ICT is one
world leveled course, not 200 country courses). The first
stocked way is Ethiopia's: concepts first, then one hands-on
skill per unit, every unit opening with a brainstorm that links
to what the learner already knows before any definition is
given. That brainstorm-first rhythm is kept as teaching
throughout the course.

**First Steps → Hardware → Software → Files and Folders → Word
Processing → Spreadsheets → Presentations → Internet and the
Web → Networks → Multimedia → Databases → Programming →
Safety, Security and Care → Computers in Society → New
Technologies**

That ladder IS the path order (recommended, never forced — a
learner can jump in higher; the "start here instead" path
points down this ladder).

The titles, in ladder order:

1. **First Steps** — what ICT and IT are, the five elements of
   an ICT system, data becoming information (and on up the
   pyramid to knowledge and wisdom), ICT in daily life, what a
   computer is, its four functions, its generations, its kinds
   and its characteristics.
2. **Hardware** — the part you can touch: input devices, the
   CPU and what is inside it, output devices, storage devices
   and the units data is measured in.
3. **Software** — instructions that make hardware work: system
   software and the operating system, utilities, drivers, and
   application software for particular jobs.
4. **Files and Folders** — what a file is, folders and drives,
   File Explorer, creating, copying, moving, renaming, deleting
   and searching for files, file extensions and paths, backup
   and compression.
5. **Word Processing** — writing on a computer: opening and
   closing a word processor, its window, typing, saving, copy
   and cut and paste, formatting, indentation and alignment,
   lists, page breaks, headers and footers, tables and pictures
   in a document.
6. **Spreadsheets** — data in rows, columns and cells; formulas
   that start with =, cell references, the fill handle,
   functions, sorting, filtering, charts, freezing panes and
   formatting cells.
7. **Presentations** — slides that show your ideas: inserting
   and laying out slides, themes and readable design, copying
   and deleting slides, transitions, animation and the slide
   master.
8. **Internet and the Web** — the internet as a network of
   networks, browsers and search engines, web addresses, pages
   and websites and hyperlinks, internet services and
   protocols, email, and building web pages with HTML.
9. **Networks** — what a network is and the devices that carry
   it, the kinds of network (PAN, LAN, MAN, WAN), topologies,
   peer-to-peer and client-server, cables and wireless signals,
   telecommunications, mobile and satellite networks, and how
   data travels.
10. **Multimedia** — what multimedia is, its five components,
    where it is used, how a multimedia piece is produced, and
    making one: drawing, keyframe animation, text, images,
    sound and video.
11. **Databases** — from separate files to the database
    approach, entities and relationships, drawing an ERD,
    tables of rows and columns, keys, building databases and
    asking them questions with queries and SQL, and XML.
12. **Programming** — programming languages, LOGO and its
    turtle (commands, procedures, variables, recursion,
    conditionals and loops), and Python: the shell, scripts,
    variables and the rules for names.
13. **Safety, Security and Care** — caring for a computer and
    cleaning it safely, starting up and shutting down, user
    accounts and strong passwords, ergonomics and healthy
    habits, troubleshooting, and preventive maintenance.
14. **Computers in Society** — e-government, e-banking,
    e-learning, e-commerce, telemedicine and teleconferencing,
    social networks and their benefits and risks, ethics and
    law, privacy, intellectual property, digital identity,
    digital citizenship and working together online.
15. **New Technologies** — emerging technologies: artificial
    intelligence and machine learning, robotics, natural
    language processing, expert systems, augmented and virtual
    reality, data science and big data, cloud computing, fog
    computing and the Internet of Things.
