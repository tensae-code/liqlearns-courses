**Acceptance count: taught in 6 countries (Ethiopia, Yemen, Bhutan, Sri Lanka, Laos, Armenia).**

Yemen's own way (Grade 10): a skills ladder — every unit alternates short teaching topics with a named تدريب (guided training) and نشاط (activity), and Windows itself is taught as a school subject for a full unit before any application is opened.

The book's own shape is filed in this lesson, as the build brief directs: the six-unit road map and the brainstorm-first rhythm are the student book's own way of running the course (placement item 1). The guide's matching shape — the 72-period budget (Unit 1 taking 12 periods) and the brainstorm, then teacher conclusion, then group work, then summarise rhythm — is filed here as teacher evidence (guide placement item 1). The student book prints "Window Explorer" for Windows Explorer in this unit; the lessons teach the correct name, Windows Explorer, and the slip is flagged again in the File Explorer lessons it touches. Question answers were checked against the teacher guide's Unit 1 answer keys (printed pp. 17–19) (teacher guide — evidence).

Grade 10 growth (2026-11-12, Wave 3 band D1): this lesson was grown, not duplicated — the Grade 10 book covers the same ground at greater depth, so its file-management teaching, its own six-unit road map (placement item 1, the book's-own-shape item, filed in this first grown lesson as the brief directs) and the guide's disk-against-drive note were added as Sub lesson 4. Question answers for the new sub lesson were checked against the teacher guide's Unit 1 keys (printed pp. 2–3, 18–21) (teacher guide — evidence).

Morocco's own way (Grade 1): ICT from age 6 built entirely on handling real hardware first — plug in, switch on, draw with the mouse, type in Word — with every unit taught in the same five-step rhythm (mobilisation, découverte, application, évaluation, réemploi) in pairs/groups, and files taught through paper folders and plastic sleeves before the screen. (teacher guide — evidence)

Bhutan's own way (Class IV, Literacy with ICT student book): Competency-based "Literacy with ICT" (not procedure drill): every chapter opens with learning objectives, mixes off-machine "Try This" with "Try This On Machine", and closes with Now You Know / Check Your Progress / Explore Further; content is localised (Bhutanese names, kuenselonline, education.gov.bt, Bhutan's 1999 Internet start) and one class per week across the year.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 6 Reading Book): a story/dialogue-led "Reading Book" with a talking computer character; every chapter cross-refers to numbered Workbook activities; local sectors (school office, ATM, ICU, factories, agriculture) and the 3R e-waste method are taught from Grade 6.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 6 Workbook): a companion workbook whose numbered activities (1.1–6.2) are referenced activity-by-activity from the Reading Book; fill-in, labelling and categorising exercises rather than separate theory.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 7 Reading Book): the CPU is taught through a hotel-kitchen/chef analogy; programme development (decomposition, flowcharts, variables, bugs) precedes presentation software; HTML page-building is already in Grade 7.

Laos's own way (Grade 6 ICT student book): a theory-only foundation year — no hands-on computer practicals at all; ICT is introduced through Lao daily-life examples (TV remote, household appliances) and every chapter opens with objectives, guiding questions and a discussion activity (ກິດຈະກໍາ) before the exposition. The RIES ICT line runs ມ.1–ມ.7 (Grades 6–12).

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 2 G2 student): A story-led primary book built around two Grade 2 characters (Hayk and Arpi); every lesson uses fixed activity "rooms" (key knowledge, key words, skills/tasks) and QR codes link to audio/video material; safety and health come before any machine skills.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 4 G4 student): Grade 4 (Stepanyan, Amirkhanyan, Matinyan) opens with e-security and health (recognising device malfunctions) and what to tell about yourself online, then purposeful documents (paragraph formats, inserted pictures, tables), slide presentations, e-mail, files, solving technical problems, ways of picturing data, and a Scratch strand of story animation, conditions, loops and variables.
