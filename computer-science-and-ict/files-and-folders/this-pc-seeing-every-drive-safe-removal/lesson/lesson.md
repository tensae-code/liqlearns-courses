# This PC — seeing every drive; safe removal

## Do it first

A flash drive is in the computer, with files on it. The lesson you are about to learn exists because of one small habit: how the stick leaves the machine. Before the safe way is shown, say what you do now when you finish with a flash drive — and what could go wrong with the files if the computer was still busy with it.

## Seeing all your drives

All **mounted** drives are viewed in one place:

1. Open **File Explorer** — **Windows key + E** is the fast way, from Opening File Explorer.
2. Choose **This PC** in the **left pane**.
3. The drives appear **on the right**.

[Picture 12553 — the This PC window with three drives shown]

Hard disk, optical drive, flash drive — every drive the computer can see, in one window.

## Removing a flash drive safely

A flash drive must **not simply be pulled out**. The book's order:

1. **Right-click** the flash drive.
2. Choose **Disconnect/Eject** first.
3. **Then** remove it.

[GIF 12554 — the Eject click and the safe-to-remove moment before the stick is pulled]

Why the wait? So **files on it are not damaged**. The eject tells the computer to finish with the stick before your hand takes it.

The same right-click menu carries one more small power: the flash drive can also be **renamed** from it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion how to see every drive in This PC. Then give the safe-removal order and the reason: right-click, Disconnect/Eject, then — and only then — what?

## Do It Yourself

1. Open File Explorer with Windows key + E, choose This PC, and read every drive aloud with its letter.
2. Put a practice file on a flash drive. Right-click the drive, choose Disconnect/Eject, then remove the stick.
3. Rename the flash drive from its right-click menu to a name of yours. Eject it safely again afterwards.
