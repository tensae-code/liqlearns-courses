# Compressing and uncompressing files — zipping

## Do it first

A folder is too big to send and too slow to copy. You may not delete anything from it, and everything in it must arrive whole. What can be done to the folder itself? Do that thinking first — then read what the tool is called.

## Sub lesson 1 — Zipping — squeezing a file or folder

**Compression** — also called **zipping** — shrinks a file's **logical size** while keeping the **same data**. Two gains follow: it **saves disk space**, and it makes **transfer over a network or the internet faster**. Several files can also be combined into **one zipped folder** for sharing, and zipped folders are worked with in Windows just like ordinary folders.

To zip, in this order:

1. **Locate** the file or folder.
2. **Right-click** it (or press and hold it).
3. Point to **Send to** and choose **Compressed (zipped) folder**.

A zipped folder of the **same name** appears in the **same place**. Nothing has left; a smaller twin has arrived.

## Sub lesson 2 — Unzipping — opening the package back up

To unzip the whole package: **right-click** the zipped folder and choose **Extract All**, then follow the instructions. To take out just one item: **open** the zipped folder and **drag or copy** the single item out.

The popular tools named for this work are **WinRAR, WinZip and 7-Zip**. The guide keys the gain in numbers (teacher guide — evidence): zipping can save **up to 80 percent or more** of hard-disk space and cuts email transmission time sharply.

The book's own practical ties it together: write and save a paragraph about the **Ethiopian Renaissance Dam**, zip it, unzip it onto the desktop, add a file to the zip, and copy the zip to external storage.
