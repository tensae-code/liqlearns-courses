**Acceptance count: taught in 7 countries (Ethiopia, Belarus, Bhutan, Mongolia, Greece, Indonesia, Kazakhstan).**

Belarus's own way (Grade 10): the Zabaroŭski–Pupcaŭ course treats informatics as applied mathematics with office tools — a full-year arc from data archiving through Pascal strings and information modelling to Excel and 2010-era Internet communication (NetMeeting, Skype, blogs).

Book error, taught corrected: on the student book's printed p. 23, Practical Exercise 1.3 is printed before Practical Exercise 1.2. The lesson teaches the practicals in the book's intended order (the writing-and-zipping practical sequence as the exercises build on each other) and flags the swapped printing here. No other error touches this lesson's pages.

Bhutan's own way (Class VII, Literacy with ICT student book): Social media is taught hands-on — learners create a Facebook account, post with audience settings, and follow netiquette and an Acceptable Use Policy; backup is taught to Google Drive, and the end-of-year experience is shared on Facebook.

Mongolia's own way (Grade 8): Grade 8 is built around two long projects — a folded leaflet on Ulaanbaatar's air pollution (Word templates, styles, WordArt, charts and SmartArt) that then becomes a PowerPoint presentation with its own master slide and hyperlink buttons, and a Paint.NET collage of the five-snout livestock (таван хошуу мал) in a steppe landscape. Models are taught on Mongolian material (the livestock, Rutherford's atom, a virus-growth formula); spreadsheets open with the chessboard-and-wheat legend and count Mongolia's real 2014 livestock by aimag; social networks are taught on Facebook and Twitter ("жиргээ") with Mongolian examples.

Greece's own way (Grade 11 EPAL, Basika Themata Pliroforikis — student notes): the EPAL (vocational) informatics-sector notes go far deeper into digital technology than the general track — full data representation (number systems and base conversion, character encoding, compression, integer representation and overflow), image/sound/video representation and editing, animation, VR/AR, 3D printing and scanning, then the electronics ladder (Boolean algebra, logic gates, combinational and sequential circuits, adders, flip-flops, counters, A/D and D/A converters, integrated circuits and the CPU), ending with digital-technology applications sector by sector and ICT professions.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 7 G7 Informatika student): Kurikulum Merdeka Informatika built on four elements (Berpikir Komputasional, Literasi Digital, Analisis Data, Algoritma dan Pemrograman), mixing plugged and unplugged activities with coded activity IDs (e.g. VII-BK, VII-LD) and QR-linked resources; Grade 7 covers only BK + Literasi Digital + data handling.


Kazakhstan's own way (Mukhametzhanova G7 Informatika, 2017 edition): the 2017 Atamura Grade 7 book adds two things its rivals lack — file archiving (creating archives and extracting them) in the first part, and a fifth part on 3D modelling of objects and events; its programming part works with programming languages and systems in general (the glossary's syntax examples are C++: main, cout, cin) and includes designing the project's interface.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukhametzhanova G7 Informatika, updated edition): the updated-content revision of the Atamura Grade 7 book keeps the 2017 skeleton (units, archives, networks, Excel, practical programming) but replaces the general programming part with Python: working with files, branching, nested and compound conditions and organising a choice; an appendix teaches building electronic learning games with a game constructor (Editor and Class versions).

Kazakhstan's own way (Zhunusov G7 Informatics, English-medium): the English-medium Grade 7 book teaches the updated national programme in English with trilingual terminology boxes — spreadsheets are Google Sheets (a "My to-do list" first spreadsheet), and practical programming is three worked games: a Quiz game, Rock Paper Scissors, a Guessing game and a Camel game, each split into algorithm-development and programming lessons.

Kazakhstan's own way (Zhunusov G7 «INFORMATICS»): the Astana-kitap Grade 7 English-medium book teaches spreadsheets in Google Sheets, files and archiving before networks, Python file work at the start of the programming chapter, and a practical chapter that builds three named games — a quiz, Rock Paper Scissors, a guessing game and a Camel game — each taken from problem statement to testing.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukametzhanova G7 «Информатика» 2017 edition): the Atamura Grade 7 book runs the same state programme as the Almatykitap one (Book 27) but adds a unit on converting between units of information, teaches the programming in C++, and likewise ends in 3D modelling of objects and events.

Kazakhstan's own way (Mukametzhanova G7 «Информатика» 2021 edition): the 2021 Atamura revision of the Grade 7 book drops the 2017 edition's 3D modelling and C++, and follows the renewed programme instead: Python from files and branching through nested and compound conditions and choice, ending in a practical programming section.

Kazakhstan's own way (Salgaraeva G7 «Информатика»): the Arman-PV Grade 7 book doubles almost every theory unit with a named practicum — units of information, user interface, tables, files, branching, nested and compound conditions each get their own practice unit — and its spreadsheet section adds building formulas for calculations in the sheet.
