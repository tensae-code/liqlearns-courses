**Acceptance count: taught in 7 countries (Ethiopia, Turkmenistan, Azerbaijan, Afghanistan, Yemen, Sudan, Uzbekistan).**

Yemen's own way (Grade 10): a skills ladder — every unit alternates short teaching topics with a named تدريب (guided training) and نشاط (activity), and Windows itself is taught as a school subject for a full unit before any application is opened.

Afghanistan's own way (Grade 10): a Windows XP-era office apprenticeship — one full chapter per application, taught menu by menu (File, Edit, Insert, Format), with the Dari keyboard language added as its own lesson; networks arrive only in the final chapter.

No book error touches this lesson's content. Question answers were checked against the teacher guide's Unit 1 answer keys (printed pp. 17–19) (teacher guide — evidence).

Mauritania's own way (Grade 8): The second year is organised as five applied units of 35 thirty-minute fiches — image processing first (Paint/GIMP/Photoshop, bitmap vs vector), then internet ethics, a full Excel data-processing unit ending in a practical project, web source-checking and responsible downloading, and finally email — each unit closed by evaluation and support fiches with full answer keys at the end.

Saudi Arabia's own way (Grade 4, Digital Skills): the primary Digital Skills course (Binary Logic) is built as three units per term with the same guide pattern as the intermediate books (outcomes, important points, steps, answers, unit project, "Test Yourself"), files on the Ain platform, and a strong Arabic/English bilingual-typing strand — pupils switch the keyboard between Arabic and English with Alt+Shift and learn text direction, hamza and Arabic punctuation on the keyboard alongside Word skills; Scratch is introduced in Term 1 through algorithms from daily life. This citation is PARTIAL — Term 1 only. (teacher guide — evidence)

Morocco's own way (Grade 2): Grade 2 goes online before it goes deep on the machine — browser and search first, then saving what the web gives (page, image, video), then creating in Paint and WordPad, with removable-disk file handling taught as a whole unit of its own. (teacher guide — evidence)

Sudan's own way (Grade 5): a short, highly practical Arabic primary series that revises the previous grade first, then works entirely inside Windows and its built-in apps (Calculator, Paint) before reaching Word — every lesson ends with hands-on activities and each unit with an assessment.


Uzbekistan's own way (Grade 5): informatics opens with a full lesson of safety and hygiene before the machine is even described, and Paint gets five of the seventeen lessons — drawing is the grade-5 child's main creative tool, with keyboard training on a typing trainer in between. The Boltaev-team informatics line runs from keyboard trainers and Paint in Grades 5–6 to a full Pascal course in Grades 8–9; this book is its Russian-language edition.

Uzbekistan's own way (Grade 9): grade 9 is the capstone — computer graphics, Access, Excel, then a nine-lesson algorithms block and a 28-lesson Turbo Pascal 7.0 course that runs from the alphabet of the language to files, procedures and graphics mode, closing with the Internet. Every chapter ends with a dedicated repetition lesson, placed here to that chapter's main lesson.

Cuba's own way (Grade 2): Play-based (lúdico) introduction through the graficador (Paint-type drawing program) and educational software; correct workstation posture/ergonomics is taught explicitly in Grade 2, and files/folders are introduced intuitively through saving and opening drawings in a class-group folder. (teacher guide — evidence)

Cuba's own way (Grade 5): The Grade 5 programme is built around extracting information from Cuban educational software, encyclopedias and EcuRed into word-processor documents, then Scratch — drawing/animation first (Unit 3), control/movement/appearance blocks in Unit 4. (teacher programme — evidence)

Cuba's own way (Grade 5): Same Grade 5 content as the Programa, recast as per-unit teaching tables with hour allocations; Unit 4 is renamed “Aprendiendo y creando” and taught purely as Scratch control, movement and appearance blocks. (teacher guide — evidence)
