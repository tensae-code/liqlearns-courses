# Drives — where files live

## Do it first

Switch the computer off in your mind. Your saved files are still somewhere — inside something. What? And when a word processor opens, where does it come from, and where do your saves go back to? Answer as best you can. This lesson names the hardware that holds everything — and clears up its most famous confusion.

## Drives — what a drive is

A **drive** is the **hardware component that stores and retrieves data** — files, programs and the operating system itself.

The book's example makes it plain: a **word processor is loaded from the hard drive** when you open it, and your work **saves back to a drive** when you save it. Nothing you keep lives anywhere else.

Systems name drives differently:

- **Windows** names drives **by letter** — **C:**, **D:**, **F:**.
- **Linux** uses instead **a tree beginning at the root '/'**.

## Drive is not driver

The book warns against one confusion, and it is worth learning cold:

- A **drive stores**.
- A **driver** is **software code** that lets a **hardware device talk to the operating system**.

Without drivers, the computer cannot send and receive data correctly to devices such as a **printer** or a **flash drive**. Same sound, different things: one is hardware that holds; the other is software that translates.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what a drive is and how Windows and Linux each name drives. Then give the difference in one breath: a drive does what — a driver does what?
