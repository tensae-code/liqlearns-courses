# Navigating with the Address bar

## Do it first

Open a folder, then a subfolder inside it, then one inside that. You are three levels deep. Now get back to the top — any way you can — and count what it cost you. There is a bar at the top of the window that was showing you the whole road the entire time. This lesson teaches you to travel by it.

## Getting around with the Address bar

The **Address bar** shows the **full path of the current folder** — every folder you passed through, written in a line: the road you are on.

[Picture 12540 — an address bar path with one folder's drop-down open]

Three ways to travel by it:

- **Click a folder name in the bar.** You jump straight back to that folder — the previously visited folders are also reached with the **Back and Forward buttons**.
- **Click the right-pointing arrow beside a folder name.** The arrow turns downward and **lists that folder's contents** — its subfolders — for jumping into.
- Pick the subfolder from the list and you land inside it, without opening the folders in between one by one.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what the Address bar shows. Then explain the arrow: what happens when you click the right-pointing arrow beside a folder name, and what the downward arrow means?

## Do It Yourself

1. Go three folders deep. Read the Address bar aloud — the full path, folder by folder.
2. Click a folder name in the bar and jump back to it. Then use Back and Forward to revisit a folder.
3. Click the arrow beside a folder name and jump into one of its subfolders from the drop-down list.
