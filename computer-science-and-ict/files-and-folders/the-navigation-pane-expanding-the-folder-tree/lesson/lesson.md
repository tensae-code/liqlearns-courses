# The Navigation pane — expanding the folder tree

## Do it first

Open File Explorer and look only at the left pane. It is a tree of folders. Beside some folders sits a small triangle; beside others, nothing. Before anyone explains, guess: why do some folders carry a triangle and others none? This lesson gives the code — three signs, and one click that flips between them.

## The Navigation pane and its triangles

The left-hand pane is the **Navigation pane**. It shows the **folder tree**, with main sections **Quick Access**, **OneDrive**, **This PC** and **Network**. Selecting a folder in the pane shows its contents in the list pane beside it.

The triangles are a code of three signs:

- **No triangle** — the folder has **no subfolders**.
- **Right-pointing triangle** — subfolders are **hidden inside**.
- **Down-pointing triangle** — the subfolders are **displayed**.

**Clicking the triangle toggles** between the two: hidden becomes displayed, displayed becomes hidden.

## Pinning a folder to Quick Access (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide adds one small procedure to this lesson: pin any **frequently used folder** so it is always at hand.

1. **Right-click** the folder.
2. Choose **'Pin to Quick access'**.

[Picture 12543 — the right-click menu with Pin to Quick access highlighted, beside the folder in Quick access]

From then on the folder **opens at any time from the Quick access area** — no travelling the tree to reach it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the three triangle signs and what each means. Then walk the pinning steps: right-click, and what do you choose — and where does the folder live afterwards?

## Do It Yourself

1. In the Navigation pane, find one folder of each kind: no triangle, right-pointing, down-pointing. Name what each sign promises.
2. Click a right-pointing triangle and watch the subfolders unfold. Click it again to hide them.
3. Pin a folder you use often to Quick access. Open it from Quick access to prove the pin works.
