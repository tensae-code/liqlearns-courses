**Acceptance count: taught in 10 countries (Ethiopia, Belarus, Uzbekistan, Bhutan, Libya, Laos, Greece, Jordan, Pakistan, Kazakhstan).**

Belarus's own way (Grade 10): the Zabaroŭski–Pupcaŭ course treats informatics as applied mathematics with office tools — a full-year arc from data archiving through Pascal strings and information modelling to Excel and 2010-era Internet communication (NetMeeting, Skype, blogs).

No book error touches this lesson's content. The Tip box is marked in the lesson as ours, not the book's, per the build brief. Question answers were checked against the teacher guide's Unit 1 answer keys (printed pp. 17–19) (teacher guide — evidence).

Grade 10 growth (2026-11-12, Wave 3 band D1): grown, not duplicated, as Sub lesson 3. Book error flagged: the student book uses Figure 1.5 for two different figures in Unit 1; this lesson cites no figure number for its Save As picture and teaches from the pictured content, as the brief directs. The book also prints 'Covert' for convert on printed p. 20 — taught corrected as convert wherever the word is used (and flagged in full in the File conversion lesson's sources).


Uzbekistan's own way (Grade 6): the whole of grade 6 is one application taught to mastery — MS Word from its interface to formulas and print — on the principle that one tool learned deeply beats many tools skimmed.

Uzbekistan's own way (Grade 8): grade 8 is theory-first — number systems, coding and Boolean logic before the machine's own story — then a full operating-system chapter and a five-lesson Word course; safety rules return as a closing appendix.

Cuba's own way (Grade 2): Play-based (lúdico) introduction through the graficador (Paint-type drawing program) and educational software; correct workstation posture/ergonomics is taught explicitly in Grade 2, and files/folders are introduced intuitively through saving and opening drawings in a class-group folder. (teacher guide — evidence)

Cuba's own way (Grade 5): The Grade 5 programme is built around extracting information from Cuban educational software, encyclopedias and EcuRed into word-processor documents, then Scratch — drawing/animation first (Unit 3), control/movement/appearance blocks in Unit 4. (teacher programme — evidence)

Cuba's own way (Grade 5): Same Grade 5 content as the Programa, recast as per-unit teaching tables with hour allocations; Unit 4 is renamed “Aprendiendo y creando” and taught purely as Scratch control, movement and appearance blocks. (teacher guide — evidence)

Bhutan's own way (Class IV, Literacy with ICT student book): Competency-based "Literacy with ICT" (not procedure drill): every chapter opens with learning objectives, mixes off-machine "Try This" with "Try This On Machine", and closes with Now You Know / Check Your Progress / Explore Further; content is localised (Bhutanese names, kuenselonline, education.gov.bt, Bhutan's 1999 Internet start) and one class per week across the year.

Libya's own way (Grade 8, Computer student book): a three-unit arc — computer history/generations and number systems first, then a whole practical unit built around the scanner (scan, save, copy, delete, scan via Paint), then Microsoft Excel 2007 taught step by step with screenshots. The basic-education line is الحاسوب (Computer) in Grades 8–9.

Laos's own way (Grade 9 ICT student book): the first fully practical year — a screenshot-led Windows XP, Word and Excel course where every numbered topic is a click-by-click procedure (the Excel half even teaches the Chart Wizard and worksheet Page Setup with its Orientation/Paper Size/Page Numbering table in English). Windows XP-era office teaching runs through the middle years.

Greece's own way (Grade 7, Pliroforiki A' Gymnasiou, 2024): the new Gymnasium informatics course is built around algorithms from the first page (a Python binary example in Unit 1), then hardware/software, the internet with a full copyright/Creative Commons/AI-copyright sequence, cybersecurity with cryptography, word processing, algorithmics, Scratch storytelling/chatbot projects, and a long society unit (robots, drones, autonomous cars, e-government, AI in education/medicine).

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 2 G2 Workbook): At Grade 2 the digital tool widens from Paint to Office: every block's learning product is a PowerPoint presentation, Word is used for written work, and keyboard finger placement and saving-after-every-edit are taught as habits.


Pakistan's own way (Grade 6 Computer Science 6): every unit opens with Students Learning Outcomes, is dotted with "Do you Know?", "Extra Bit!" and "Tips" boxes, and closes with Summary, MCQ/short/detailed exercises, project-based and activity-based questions, with an answers key at the back. Digital skills (Windows desktop, file management, Paint 3D, a typing tutor) are taught hands-on BEFORE programming; digital citizenship is framed through Pakistani citizenship first; the entrepreneurship unit teaches from Pakistani founders (Foodpanda/EatOye, Shopsy.pk, Zero Makeup, Investors Lounge).

Kazakhstan's own way (Sagimbaeva G1 «Цифровая грамотность»): two cartoon guides (Arman and Saule, themselves first-graders) lead the year; lessons are told as a journey, QR codes open lesson videos, and from lesson 10 the class builds and programmes LEGO-style robots (windmill model, labyrinth runs).

Kazakhstan's own way (Kobdikova G1 «Цифрлық сауаттылық»): the Arman-PV Grade 1 book teaches programming through Scratch from §6 (creating a project, the «Қош келдің, Наурыз!» project, saving/opening a project, a «Музыкалық аспаптар» project) where the Almatykitap book uses an unnamed game environment, and its safety section adds a lesson on unwanted contacts online.

Kazakhstan's own way (Grade 1 teacher guide «Цифровая грамотность. Методическое руководство» 2023 (Russian + Kazakh editions)): the 2023 guide restructures Grade 1 against the 2021 student book (Book 5): robotics disappears, replaced by a drawing section («Мой первый рисунок», taught in Tux Paint in the Kazakh plans) and a separate algorithms section (concept of algorithm, executors and their command systems, kinds of algorithms) before Scratch programming.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kopeeva G3 «Ақпараттық-коммуникациялық технологиялар»): Grade 3 ICT is a tools year organised by cross-cutting themes («Менің мектебім», «Сәулет», «Өнер»…): text, then sound, then computer pictures in a graphics editor, then a mixed document that combines them, then internet safety/search and presentations — with a practicum closing almost every section.
