**Acceptance count: taught in 4 countries (Ethiopia, Saudi Arabia, Moldova, South Sudan).**

No book error touches this lesson's content. Question answers were checked against the teacher guide's Unit 1 answer keys (printed pp. 17–19) (teacher guide — evidence).

Saudi Arabia's own way (Grade 3, continuing education): a workbook-style book for the continuing-education (adult/lifelong learning) track — every one of the 16 lessons opens with "I want to learn" objectives and closes with written exercises, and everything is taught on an older Windows desktop (Start menu, "My Computer", Recycle Bin) with Notepad as the only application, worked through step-by-step practicals in the school lab.

Moldova's own way (Grade 2, Educație Digitală): Grade 2 builds the idea of information quantity (bit, byte, binary drawing) before any office software, teaches the keyboard including touch-typing posture, and keeps programming unplugged-but-precise: loops, jump (pen-up) instructions and multi-loop programs for the mouse, the painter and the hedgehog. The Digital Education module sits inside Technological Education.

Moldova's own way (Grade 7, Informatică): lower-secondary Informatica is a real textbook discipline, not computer-skills practice — number systems, text/image/sound encoding and data types in Pascal sit alongside GUI literacy, and two full chapters are given to behaviour, law and information culture in virtual spaces.

South Sudan's own way (Grade 11 S3 Student): Year 3 is creation and programming: learners build and run a real personal website/blog end-to-end in WordPress (hosting, domain, themes, posts, legal issues and comment moderation), study social networks platform by platform (LinkedIn, Google+ and the Students Circle Network, with South Sudanese business use), then learn programming through data types, arrays and structures in C/C++ and writing programs in Notepad, closing on ethical conduct and intellectual property.
