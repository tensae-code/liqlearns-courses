**Acceptance count: taught in 14 countries (Ethiopia, Eritrea, Yemen, Saudi Arabia, Uzbekistan, Cuba, Bhutan, Mongolia, Sri Lanka, Rwanda, Greece, Indonesia, Pakistan, Kazakhstan).**

Yemen's own way (Grade 10): a skills ladder — every unit alternates short teaching topics with a named تدريب (guided training) and نشاط (activity), and Windows itself is taught as a school subject for a full unit before any application is opened.

No book error touches this lesson's content. The teacher-guide part is the guide's printed concluding frame for the opening brainstorm — the paper-versus-computer verdict and the manila-dossier classroom resource — filed as teacher evidence, exactly as placed. Question answers were checked against the teacher guide's Unit 1 answer keys (printed pp. 17–19) (teacher guide — evidence).

Mauritania's own way (Grade 7): Informatics is taught for the first time in 1AS as thirty 30-minute teacher fiches moving hardware → information systems (illustrated with Mauritanian systems Bankily, Masrvi, Houwiyeti and the Ministry's Sirage platform) → a long Microsoft Word sequence → a full unit on internet ethics before browsers and search are even introduced.

Saudi Arabia's own way (Grade 3, continuing education): a workbook-style book for the continuing-education (adult/lifelong learning) track — every one of the 16 lessons opens with "I want to learn" objectives and closes with written exercises, and everything is taught on an older Windows desktop (Start menu, "My Computer", Recycle Bin) with Notepad as the only application, worked through step-by-step practicals in the school lab.

Morocco's own way (Grade 1): ICT from age 6 built entirely on handling real hardware first — plug in, switch on, draw with the mouse, type in Word — with every unit taught in the same five-step rhythm (mobilisation, découverte, application, évaluation, réemploi) in pairs/groups, and files taught through paper folders and plastic sleeves before the screen. (teacher guide — evidence)


Uzbekistan's own way (Grade 8): grade 8 is theory-first — number systems, coding and Boolean logic before the machine's own story — then a full operating-system chapter and a five-lesson Word course; safety rules return as a closing appendix.

Cuba's own way (Grade 2): Play-based (lúdico) introduction through the graficador (Paint-type drawing program) and educational software; correct workstation posture/ergonomics is taught explicitly in Grade 2, and files/folders are introduced intuitively through saving and opening drawings in a class-group folder. (teacher guide — evidence)

Cuba's own way (Grade 5): Student text mirrors the programme in four chapters; word-processing is taught as a research workflow (synonyms, symbols, searching, copying texts and images from sources), and Scratch is introduced through its paint editor before movement/appearance/control blocks.

Cuba's own way (Grade 7): Secondary Informática opens by contrasting proprietary Windows with Cuba's free operating system Nova; programming continues the primary Scratch line, here centred on the Scratch pen tool for drawing geometric figures and the linear function; word-processor work ends by exporting documents to HTML for the web.

Bhutan's own way (Class VI, Literacy with ICT student book): School computing runs on Google's G Suite for Education — email is Gmail on the school account, collaboration is Google Classroom, and the end-of-year food-chain project is agreed in Classroom, built in Word, password-protected, cited, filed in folders and reported to the teacher by email.

Mongolia's own way (Grade 7): the whole applications half is one long project — research and produce a printed booklet "Усаа хайрлая" (Cherish the Water) in Word, a Paint.NET layered picture, and a class PowerPoint; image work uses the free Paint.NET (downloaded from getpaint.net) rather than MS Paint; the e-communication chapter teaches Yahoo mail and Yahoo Messenger, Mongolian history of the örtöö relay post and the @ sign, and makes correct Latin transliteration of Mongolian Cyrillic (standard MNS 5217:2003) an ethics topic in its own right.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 6 Reading Book): a story/dialogue-led "Reading Book" with a talking computer character; every chapter cross-refers to numbered Workbook activities; local sectors (school office, ATM, ICU, factories, agriculture) and the 3R e-waste method are taught from Grade 6.

Rwanda's own way (S1 ICT Student Book, KLB): competency-based units each opening with a Key Unit Competency and keywords; a full GIS/ArcGIS unit and a Scratch music-making unit sit inside the first secondary year, and ICT use is grounded in Rwandan daily life.

Greece's own way (Grade 7, Pliroforiki A' Gymnasiou, 2024): the new Gymnasium informatics course is built around algorithms from the first page (a Python binary example in Unit 1), then hardware/software, the internet with a full copyright/Creative Commons/AI-copyright sequence, cybersecurity with cryptography, word processing, algorithmics, Scratch storytelling/chatbot projects, and a long society unit (robots, drones, autonomous cars, e-government, AI in education/medicine).

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 7 G7 Informatika student): Kurikulum Merdeka Informatika built on four elements (Berpikir Komputasional, Literasi Digital, Analisis Data, Algoritma dan Pemrograman), mixing plugged and unplugged activities with coded activity IDs (e.g. VII-BK, VII-LD) and QR-linked resources; Grade 7 covers only BK + Literasi Digital + data handling.


Pakistan's own way (Grade 6 Computer Science 6): every unit opens with Students Learning Outcomes, is dotted with "Do you Know?", "Extra Bit!" and "Tips" boxes, and closes with Summary, MCQ/short/detailed exercises, project-based and activity-based questions, with an answers key at the back. Digital skills (Windows desktop, file management, Paint 3D, a typing tutor) are taught hands-on BEFORE programming; digital citizenship is framed through Pakistani citizenship first; the entrepreneurship unit teaches from Pakistani founders (Foodpanda/EatOye, Shopsy.pk, Zero Makeup, Investors Lounge).

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G2 Digital Literacy): one Grade 2 year mixes Scratch, a word processor, the WavePad sound editor and Lego Mindstorms EV3 robotics, and ends with a built-and-defended robotics project ("Dancing Robot"); every section closes with research and creative task pages (printed pp. 18, 34, 58).

Kazakhstan's own way (Kobdikova G2 Digital Literacy): the same six-part national Grade 2 frame as the Almatykitap book, but the tools differ: the "word by word" part builds a spoken-text dialogue between two Scratch characters, sound is recorded and trimmed in the Windows Voice Recorder app (not WavePad), media players AIMP and Windows Media Player are named, the keyboard trainer is the online typingstudy.com Kazakh course, the text editor is WordPad, and the Dancing Robot dances to the Kazakh folk melody "Kamajai" loaded through the EV3 Sound Editor.

Kazakhstan's own way (Isabaeva G2 Digital Literacy): a third Grade 2 course on the same six-part national frame, from the Kokzhiek/Atamura side: the keyboard trainer is RapidTyping 5 with full finger-placement rules, sound is edited in Wave Editor (wave-editor.com) by selecting waveform segments, voice recording also uses a smartphone dictaphone, and the final part has no text-editor lesson — the project algorithm itself is the writing task. Unnumbered end-of-part project and summary pages (printed pp. 27, 50, 84, 99, 114, 136–137) are not placed separately.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kopeeva G3 ICT): the older Grade 3 "ICT" subject (not Digital Literacy): no programming or robotics at all — the year is computer basics, WordPad-style text work, sound recording, a long Paint strand (tools, shapes, repeating patterns, palette, printing, mixed text-and-picture documents), Internet safety and search, and a full presentation strand ending with sound inside slides.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G3 ICT): the Almatykitap team's Grade 3 ICT runs the same eight cross-cutting themes as the Arman-PV book (Living nature; What is good, what is bad; Time; Architecture; Art; Outstanding personalities; Water — source of life; Culture of rest. Holidays) but as 34 short lessons with named mini-project defences ("My idol"); the glossary names Tux Paint as the children's raster editor and WordPad as the text editor. The mixed review lesson 21 ("Creative tasks: searching, typing, drawing", printed p. 53) is not placed.

Kazakhstan's own way (Isabaeva G2 «Цифровая грамотность»): six sections each built round a cross-cutting theme («Всё обо мне», «Моя семья и друзья», «Моя школа»/«Мой родной край», «В здоровом теле — здоровый дух», «Традиции и фольклор», «Окружающая среда»/«Путешествие»); every section ends in a project, and the year ends with a build-and-programme «Танцующий робот» (Dancing Robot) project in Scratch + LEGO Mindstorms EV3 (the book's own resource list names scratch.mit.edu and the Mindstorms EV3 software).

Kazakhstan's own way (Kobdikova G2 «Цифрлиқ саватлиқ»): a second, parallel Grade 2 textbook from the Arman-PV team follows the same state programme as Book 2 but re-orders it (browser search before files, two sound-editing lessons, robotics taught as «Робототехника: сенсорлар») and writes project ideas in a text editor before building them.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G2 teacher guide «Цифровая грамотность. Методическое руководство» (Russian + Kazakh editions)): the guide teaches the same Grade 2 year as full scripted lesson plans (objectives referenced to the state programme codes, e.g. 2.1.3.1, differentiated tasks for all/most/some pupils, formative-assessment notes); its section names differ slightly from the student books («Текст и мультимедиа», «Роботы в нашей жизни»), and it adds lessons the student books fold together — robot wheel and movement blocks, the touch sensor, and exchanging data between programmes.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kopeeva G5 «Информатика»): the year runs safety → internet safety → information and its processing → a long Scratch chapter, and it closes not with a test but with two production projects: a printed newspaper page made in a word processor (page setup, columns, WordArt, page numbers) and a defended Scratch project published to the Scratch community site.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kopeeva G3 «Ақпараттық-коммуникациялық технологиялар»): Grade 3 ICT is a tools year organised by cross-cutting themes («Менің мектебім», «Сәулет», «Өнер»…): text, then sound, then computer pictures in a graphics editor, then a mixed document that combines them, then internet safety/search and presentations — with a practicum closing almost every section.

Kazakhstan's own way (Atayeva/Vyushkova/Zhurbenko G8 «Информатика» NIS): the NIS Grade 8 book is the widest of the set: a whole section on vector graphics and geographic information systems (planning an excursion, making maps, visualising data in GIS), octal and hexadecimal coding, loops applied separately to mathematics, physics, business and animation, strings and subroutines, and a nine-step project method ending in a mini-project on artificial intelligence.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G3 «Ақпараттық-коммуникациялық технологиялар»): the older Almatykitap Grade 3 ICT book (2018, a different book from the 2021 Digital Literacy, Book 23) is organised by cross-curricular themes — Living Nature, What is Good and What is Bad, Time, Architecture, Art, Outstanding People, Water is the Source of Life, Culture of Rest — and teaches sound (playback, effects, recording, processing) and the graphics editor inside them, ending every theme with a defended mini-project.
