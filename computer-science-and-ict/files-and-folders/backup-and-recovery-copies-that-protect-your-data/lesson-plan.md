# Lesson plan — Backup and recovery — copies that protect your data

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Files and Folders · **Lesson:** Backup and recovery — copies that protect your data
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — Organization of Files (Grade 10 depth) — in the title Files and Folders on the ICT course ladder (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** backup and recovery (operational recovery) means making and storing copies of data so people and organisations are protected against loss: a backup is a digital copy of what matters most, kept on a separate system or medium, and restoring copies it back so work can continue from an earlier, safe point.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the drive lessons: the learner knows separate drives and cloud drives exist; this lesson gives them their protective job. The next lesson takes a backup step by step.

- Recommended first: Drives and drive letters — and drive vs driver, in this same title. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a backup is and where it must live.
2. Name the four loss dangers a backup protects against.
3. Say what restoring is and when it happens.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else is needed to learn it; the doing lesson after this one needs a computer and an external drive.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — name the files on a school or home computer whose loss would hurt most — photos, records, assignments — before the word backup is given (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Keeps the 'backup' on the same disk → a copy that dies with the original is no backup; separate system or medium is the whole point.
- Mixes backup and restoring → backup makes the copy before trouble; restoring spends it after.
