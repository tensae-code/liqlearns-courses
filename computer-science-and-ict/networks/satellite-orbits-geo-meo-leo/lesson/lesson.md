# Satellite orbits — GEO, MEO, LEO

## Do it first

Picture three shelves around the earth: one very high, one in the middle, one low. Every satellite sits on one shelf. Which shelf is which — and why are the gaps between them empty?

## Sub lesson 1 — Three orbit paths

An artificial satellite follows an **orbit — equatorial, inclined or polar**. The guide's key adds why a GEO satellite's orbit is **equatorial**: it needs to **stay fixed over one spot** (teacher guide — evidence).

## Sub lesson 2 — GEO, MEO and LEO by height

By orbit height satellites fall into three categories: **geostationary Earth orbit (GEO)**, at the **single altitude of 35,786 km**; **medium Earth orbit (MEO)**, **between 5,000 and 15,000 km**; and **low Earth orbit (LEO)**, **normally below 2,000 km**.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Van Allen belts bar the gaps

The gaps are **not empty by accident**: two **Van Allen belts** of **charged particles** would **destroy a satellite orbiting inside them**, and **MEO sits between the belts**.
