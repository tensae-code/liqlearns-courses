# Lesson plan — Satellite orbits — GEO, MEO, LEO

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Networks · **Lesson:** Satellite orbits — GEO, MEO, LEO
**Place in the curriculum:** Title — Networks in the Computer Science and ICT ladder (TITLES.md); stocked from Ethiopia's Grade 10 book, Unit 2 — Computer Network. The ladder is the recommended order, never a forced one.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** By height, satellites are **GEO at 35,786 km**, **MEO between 5,000 and 15,000 km**, and **LEO below 2,000 km** — with the **Van Allen belts** barring the gaps.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Satellite networks — this lesson sorts those satellites by the height of their orbits.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Three orbit paths** — equatorial, inclined, polar; GEO's equatorial reason
2. **GEO, MEO and LEO by height** — 35,786 km; 5,000–15,000 km; below 2,000 km
3. **The Van Allen belts bar the gaps** — the belts destroy satellites inside them; MEO sits between

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Satellite networks — uplink, downlink, transponder. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the three orbit paths.
2. Give each category's height range.
3. Say why the gaps hold no satellites and where MEO sits.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil.
