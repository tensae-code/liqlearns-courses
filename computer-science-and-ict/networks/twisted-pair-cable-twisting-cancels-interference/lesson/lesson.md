# Twisted-pair cable — twisting cancels interference

## Do it first

Take two loose strings. Lay them side by side, straight. Now twist them round each other. The twisted pair is not decoration — this lesson shows what the twist does to noise.

## Sub lesson 1 — Guided media carry the signal inside a conduit

**Guided media** carry the signal inside a **physical conduit** that **directs and contains** it. **Twisted pair** and **coaxial** cable use **copper conductors** carrying **electric current**; **fibre** carries **light** instead.

[Picture 14502 — twisted pair, twisting cancels interference]

## Sub lesson 2 — Two wires, one signal, one ground

A **twisted pair** is **two insulated copper conductors twisted together**. One wire **carries the signal**; the other acts as the **ground reference**.

## Sub lesson 3 — Why the twist cancels interference

**Interference** and **crosstalk** strike two **parallel** wires **unequally** — the wire nearer the noise source suffers more. **Twisting swaps which wire is nearer the noise source at every turn**, so **both wires suffer equally**, and the unwanted effect **cancels at the receiver**.
