# Tree Topology — a Hierarchy of Networks

## Do it first

A school network outgrows its single star: too many rooms, too many cables to one centre. The answer is to grow the star into a tree — centres joined to a centre, branches under branches. This lesson studies that grown shape, the topology of nearly every real large network.

## Sub lesson 1 — The tree — stars upon a backbone

In **tree topology** — also called **hierarchical topology** — nodes are arranged in levels: a **root** device at the top connects to the central devices of **star groups** beneath it, which connect onward to their own devices or further sub-stars, like a family tree or a company's chart. It is honestly a hybrid of the shapes you know: stars at the leaves, a bus-like backbone at the trunk. Cabling follows the hierarchy, and each level's centre manages its own branch.

## Sub lesson 2 — Strengths — and the root's weakness

The tree's gifts are scale and order: it **grows branch by branch** without rewiring the whole; a fault in one branch can be **isolated** while the rest work on; and its shape matches how institutions themselves are built, so networks and organisations understand each other. Its weakness is written in its name: the **root and the upper trunks carry everything** — a failure near the top fells every branch beneath it, and the top links bear the heaviest traffic. Real builders answer with strong root devices and backup links at the trunk — protecting the tree where the tree is weakest.
