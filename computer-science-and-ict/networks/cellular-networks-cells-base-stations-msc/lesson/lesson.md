# Cellular networks — cells, base stations, MSC

## Do it first

Walk across town while talking on a phone. The call does not break — yet you left the first tower far behind. Something handed your call along. This lesson names the handers.

## Sub lesson 1 — Cells and base stations

A **cellular (mobile) network** divides its service area into **small regions called cells**, each with an **antenna controlled by a fixed transceiver — the base station (BS)**. So the provider can **locate and track a caller, assign a channel, and hand the call to the next base station** as the caller moves.

## Sub lesson 2 — The mobile switching centre

All base stations are coordinated by a **mobile switching centre (MSC)** — the **computerised office that connects calls, records call information and handles billing**, and **links the cells to the telephone central office**. The Ethiopian field practical visits a local **Ethio telecom** office to summarise the services of a cellular system.

## Sub lesson 3 — Handoffs and WiMax keyed (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide's discussion key adds: a **hard handoff** means a mobile talks to **one base station at a time**; a **soft handoff** means **two at once**. It also supplies **WiMax** — a **wireless WAN built in star topology from a base station to fixed or mobile subscriber stations** — a definition found nowhere in the student book's body.
