# Lesson plan — How wireless signals travel — ground, sky and line-of-sight

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Networks · **Lesson:** How wireless signals travel — ground, sky and line-of-sight
**Place in the curriculum:** Title — Networks in the Computer Science and ICT ladder (TITLES.md); stocked from Ethiopia's Grade 10 book, Unit 2 — Computer Network. The ladder is the recommended order, never a forced one.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Wireless signals travel three ways: **hugging the earth** (ground), **bouncing off the ionosphere** (sky), or **straight, antenna to antenna** (line-of-sight).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Wired and wireless media — the learner knows unguided media broadcast through free space; this lesson gives their three paths.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Unguided media — broadcast through free space** — waves through free space, no conductor, anyone with a suitable device can receive
2. **Ground and sky propagation** — ground hugs the curve, distance grows with power; sky bounces off the ionosphere, farther on less power
3. **Line-of-sight propagation** — very high frequencies in straight lines; directional, tall or close antennas

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what unguided media broadcast and who can receive it.
2. Tell ground from sky propagation by path and power.
3. Say what line-of-sight demands of its antennas.

## If the learner is stuck
- Ground = along the ground. Sky = up to the sky (ionosphere) and back. Line-of-sight = straight line, eye to eye — if the earth curves between the antennas, they must be taller or closer.
