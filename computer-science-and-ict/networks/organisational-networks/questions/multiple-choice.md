# Organisational Networks — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What is an intranet?**
a) A private network inside one organisation, using internet ways — pages, browsers, email  b) Only the organisation's own people  c) Its notices, shared files and internal mail  d) Exactly its wall — internet tools with the public left outside
**Answer: a) A private network inside one organisation, using internet ways — pages, browsers, email**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) Who may enter an intranet?**
a) Its notices, shared files and internal mail  b) Only the organisation's own people  c) Exactly its wall — internet tools with the public left outside  d) Yes — web pages and browsers, on a private network
**Answer: b) Only the organisation's own people**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What does a school intranet carry?**
a) Exactly its wall — internet tools with the public left outside  b) Yes — web pages and browsers, on a private network  c) Its notices, shared files and internal mail  d) Part of an intranet opened to chosen outsiders
**Answer: c) Its notices, shared files and internal mail**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What is the intranet's value, in Fiji's phrase?**
a) Yes — web pages and browsers, on a private network  b) Part of an intranet opened to chosen outsiders  c) Suppliers checking orders, or parents reading reports  d) Exactly its wall — internet tools with the public left outside
**Answer: d) Exactly its wall — internet tools with the public left outside**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) Are intranet pages web pages?**
a) Yes — web pages and browsers, on a private network  b) Part of an intranet opened to chosen outsiders  c) Suppliers checking orders, or parents reading reports  d) To their part only, by account and password
**Answer: a) Yes — web pages and browsers, on a private network**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What is an extranet?**
a) Suppliers checking orders, or parents reading reports  b) Part of an intranet opened to chosen outsiders  c) To their part only, by account and password  d) The internet itself — the public network everyone may enter
**Answer: b) Part of an intranet opened to chosen outsiders**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) Give an extranet example.**
a) To their part only, by account and password  b) The internet itself — the public network everyone may enter  c) Suppliers checking orders, or parents reading reports  d) Intranet (ours), extranet (ours, shared by invitation), internet (everyone's)
**Answer: c) Suppliers checking orders, or parents reading reports**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) How are extranet visitors let in?**
a) The internet itself — the public network everyone may enter  b) Intranet (ours), extranet (ours, shared by invitation), internet (everyone's)  c) A private network inside one organisation, using internet ways — pages, browsers, email  d) To their part only, by account and password
**Answer: d) To their part only, by account and password**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) What is the third circle?**
a) The internet itself — the public network everyone may enter  b) Intranet (ours), extranet (ours, shared by invitation), internet (everyone's)  c) A private network inside one organisation, using internet ways — pages, browsers, email  d) Only the organisation's own people
**Answer: a) The internet itself — the public network everyone may enter**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) Name Fiji's three circles.**
a) A private network inside one organisation, using internet ways — pages, browsers, email  b) Intranet (ours), extranet (ours, shared by invitation), internet (everyone's)  c) Only the organisation's own people  d) Its notices, shared files and internal mail
**Answer: b) Intranet (ours), extranet (ours, shared by invitation), internet (everyone's)**
