# Organisational Networks

## Do it first

Networks have sizes — but they also have owners. Fiji's Year 12 networks chapter closes with the organisational view: the networks an organisation builds for itself, opens carefully to its partners, and meets in the public internet. This lesson draws those three circles.

## Sub lesson 1 — The intranet — the organisation's own network

An **intranet** is a network **inside one organisation**, using internet ways — web pages, browsers, email — but **private**: only the organisation's own people may enter. A school's intranet carries its notices, its shared files and its internal mail, reachable in the building and invisible outside it. Fiji's point is the boundary: the intranet's value is exactly its wall — the same tools as the public web, with the public left outside.

## Sub lesson 2 — The extranet and the internet — circles opening outward

An **extranet** opens **part** of the intranet to **chosen outsiders** — suppliers who check orders, parents who read reports — each let through the wall to their part only, by account and password. Beyond both lies the **internet itself**: the public network the organisation also uses, where everyone may enter and nothing is the organisation's own. Fiji's three circles — intranet (ours), extranet (ours, shared by invitation), internet (everyone's) — are the map on which every organisational network decision is drawn.
