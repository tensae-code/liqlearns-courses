# Lesson plan — Bluetooth — short-range wireless and pairing

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Networks · **Lesson:** Bluetooth — short-range wireless and pairing
**Place in the curriculum:** Title — Networks in the Computer Science and ICT ladder (TITLES.md); stocked from Ethiopia's Grade 10 book, Unit 2 — Computer Network. The ladder is the recommended order, never a forced one.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** **Bluetooth** replaces the cables between nearby devices: devices **pair** on the **2.4 GHz ISM band**, then communicate freely.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the wireless lessons — Bluetooth is mobile communication at its shortest range.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or in steps — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Mobile communication — no physical connection** — mobile communication defined; Bluetooth as its short-range example
2. **The cable replacer on 2.4 GHz** — cable replacement; the headset picture; the unlicensed 2.4 GHz ISM band
3. **Pairing, then free communication** — pairing joins devices; then free communication; the class practical
4. **Bluetooth against Wi-Fi (teacher guide — evidence)** — the guide's advantages and the Bluetooth/Wi-Fi line

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Radio waves — broadcasting in all directions. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define mobile communication.
2. Say what Bluetooth replaces and which band it uses, shared with what.
3. Describe pairing in order: request, acceptance, link.
4. Draw the guide's line between Bluetooth and Wi-Fi.

## What you need
Two nearby phones with Bluetooth, if available, for the practical; otherwise paper and a pencil.
