# Shielded twisted pair — when UTP is not enough

## Do it first

Stand a radio beside a motor and listen to the crackle. Some places are full of interference. This lesson is the cable built for those places.

## Sub lesson 1 — Crosstalk — wires troubling each other

**Crosstalk** is the **interference neighbouring copper wires cause each other**. **Twisting reduces it** — but where **interference sources are many** (motors, wireless devices, radio transmitters), more defence is needed.

[Picture 14506 — UTP and STP cut open side by side]

## Sub lesson 2 — STP — foil on each pair, braid over all

**Shielded twisted pair (STP)** wraps **each pair in metallic foil** and **all four pairs together in an overall braid or foil**. It combines **cancellation, twisting and shielding** to cut both **crosstalk from inside** and **EMI/RFI from outside**.

## Sub lesson 3 — Where STP serves

STP serves **fast Ethernet** and **telephone voice/data channels**.

## Sub lesson 4 — UTP against STP (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide keys the comparison: **UTP is cheap, easy to install and fit for high-speed LANs**, but **limited to shorter distances** by attenuation and interference; **STP's surrounding mesh gives higher capacity and transmission speed**. UTP is the commonest twisted pair — **four colour-coded pairs inside one jacket**, each pair **twisted at a different rate** to cut interference.
