# Wired and wireless media

## Sub lesson 1 — Wired media — copper and fibre

The **medium** carries the message **from sender to receiver**. Media split into two families: **wired (guided)** and **wireless (unguided)**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Wireless media — through air, water or vacuum

Wired media are **cables**. **Copper** — the commonest being **unshielded twisted pair (UTP)** — carries **electrical signals up to 100 metres**. **Fibre optic** uses **light**, and goes **faster and farther**. Watch the GIF: a pulse of light travelling down a fibre strand.

Wireless media carry through **air, water or vacuum**: **radio waves, microwaves, infrared and satellite communication**. Detail on both families is promised for Grade 10.

## Sub lesson 3 — Transmission media at Grade 10 depth — the pathway, encoding, and choosing a medium

For data to travel, a physical pathway must exist: **transmission media** — either **tangible cable media (twisted pair, coaxial, fibre optic)** or **wireless media sending signals through air or space (cellular radio, microwave, satellite, radio, infrared)**. Modern networks mainly use **copper cable, glass/plastic fibre, and wireless**.

How a bit is carried differs by medium: **electrical impulses in patterns on metal wire, pulses of light on fibre, patterns of electromagnetic waves in wireless**. Media **differ in features and benefits**, so they are **not interchangeable for every purpose**. All media split into the two great families: **guided (wired)** and **unguided (wireless)**.

Choosing a medium (teacher guide — evidence): a medium is chosen by **the distance it can carry a signal, the environment it will be installed in, the amount and speed of data, and the cost of medium plus installation** — and the guide keys the same encoding per medium: **electrical impulses on metallic wires, pulses of light on fibre, patterns of electromagnetic waves in wireless**.
