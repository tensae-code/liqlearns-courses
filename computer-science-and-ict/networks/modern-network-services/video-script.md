# Video script — Modern Network Services

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

A **network service** is a **job the network does for its users** through its servers and programs: the connection is the road; the service is the shop at its end. The modern set the chapter surveys is the one daily life now assumes: **electronic mail and messaging**, the **World Wide Web** of published content, **file transfer and sharing**, **remote access** to distant machines, and **voice and video** carried as data — each with the client that asks and the server that answers.
The chapter's modern edge: whole **computing itself** is becoming a service — programs, storage and even platforms **rented over the network** (the cloud services the new-technologies lessons open), so the user's device grows thinner as the network's services grow thicker. Judging a network then changes: not 'is it connected?' but '**which services stand on it, and how well do they serve?**' — the question this book's applied unit asks of every network it meets from here on.
