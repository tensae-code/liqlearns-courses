**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the book's own order — it follows the book's lessons in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Services, not just connections** — A **network service** is a **job the network does for its users** through its servers and programs: the connection is the road; the service is the shop at its end. The modern set the chapter surveys is the one daily life now assumes: **electronic mail and messaging**, the **World Wide Web** of published content, **file transfer and sharing**, **remote access** to distant machines, and **voice and video** carried as data — each with the client that asks and the server that answers.
2. **The service turn — computing itself as a service** — The chapter's modern edge: whole **computing itself** is becoming a service — programs, storage and even platforms **rented over the network** (the cloud services the new-technologies lessons open), so the user's device grows thinner as the network's services grow thicker. Judging a network then changes: not 'is it connected?' but '**which services stand on it, and how well do they serve?**' — the question this book's applied unit asks of every network it meets from here on.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
