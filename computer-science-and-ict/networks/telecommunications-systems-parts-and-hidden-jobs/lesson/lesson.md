# Telecommunications systems — parts and hidden jobs

## Do it first

Make a phone call. You did almost nothing — yet the message found its way, arrived whole, at the right speed. Who did all that work? This lesson lists the system and its hidden jobs.

## Sub lesson 1 — Telecommunications and its subset

**Telecommunications** is all **long-distance communication over common carriers** — telephone, radio, television — exchanging information in **any form (voice, data, text, images, audio, video)** over networks. **Data communications is its computer subset**.

[Picture 14519 — a sender-to-receiver telecommunications system]

## Sub lesson 2 — The eight components

A telecommunications system is the **compatible hardware and software** arranged to move information between locations, with **eight components**: **hardware, communications media, communications networks, communications processors, communications software, data communications providers, communications protocols, and communications applications** — among the applications: **electronic data interchange, tele/videoconferencing, email, fax and electronic funds transfer**.

[GIF 14520 — several signals merging into one line (multiplexing)]

## Sub lesson 3 — The hidden jobs

Behind the user's back the system must **transmit, connect sender to receiver, route by the most efficient path, process and error-check, convert speeds and formats, control flow, and keep information secure at all times**.

## Sub lesson 4 — Five components keyed, and multiplexing (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide's Activity key lists a system as **message, sender, receiver, medium and protocol**, with the **modem** keyed as the device converting digital data into a form fit for an analogue medium (and back). The guide also defines **multiplexing** — **combining multiple signals into one signal over a shared medium** — and keys the payoff of modern systems: **voice, video and data on one network** let people work productively **from the office, on the road or at home**.
