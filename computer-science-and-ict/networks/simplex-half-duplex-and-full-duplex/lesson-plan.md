# Lesson plan — Simplex, half-duplex and full-duplex

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Networks · **Lesson:** Simplex, half-duplex and full-duplex
**Place in the curriculum:** Title — Networks in the Computer Science and ICT ladder (TITLES.md); stocked from Ethiopia's Grade 10 book, Unit 2 — Computer Network. The ladder is the recommended order, never a forced one.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Flow comes in three modes: **simplex** one way only, **half-duplex** both ways **in turns**, **full-duplex** both ways **at once**.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Data communication — the modes name the directions that lesson's flow can take.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or in steps — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Simplex — the one-way street** — one way only; keyboard and monitor; whole channel one way
2. **Half-duplex — turns on a one-lane road** — both ways in turns, never at once; whole channel to whoever transmits
3. **Full-duplex — the two-way street** — both ways at once; channel split or divided; telephone conversation

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Data communication — delivery, accuracy, timeliness, jitter. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Tell the three modes apart by direction and timing.
2. Give each mode's road picture and device example.
3. Say how full-duplex divides its channel, both ways.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil.

## If the learner is stuck
- Ask one question: can both ends talk at the same moment? No, and only one ever can — simplex. No, they take turns — half-duplex. Yes — full-duplex.
