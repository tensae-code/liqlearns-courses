# Keeping a Message Secret — Packets, TCP and Encryption Keys — short answer

Answer in a short sentence of your own.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What does TCP stand for?**
**Answer: Transmission Control Protocol**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What does TCP number?**
**Answer: The packets**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What does the receiver send back?**
**Answer: Acknowledgements of what arrived**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What happens to missing pieces under TCP?**
**Answer: They are sent again**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) What is TCP, next to IP?**
**Answer: IP is the addressing; TCP is the promise-keeping**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What does encryption lock?**
**Answer: The contents of the packets, with a key**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What does an interceptor see without the key?**
**Answer: Only scrambled data**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What if the pirate catches the key?**
**Answer: The lock opens for them too — secrecy dies with the key's safety**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) Name two key-keeping rules.**
**Answer: Guard keys, change them, never send them alongside the message**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) What does TCP alone not give?**
**Answer: Secrecy — packets can arrive whole and still be read**
