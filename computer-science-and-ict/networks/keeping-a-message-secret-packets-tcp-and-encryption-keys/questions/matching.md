# Keeping a Message Secret — Packets, TCP and Encryption Keys — matching

Match each item on the left with the one it belongs to on the right.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What does TCP stand for?** → **Transmission Control Protocol**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) What does TCP number?** → **The packets**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What does the receiver send back?** → **Acknowledgements of what arrived**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What happens to missing pieces under TCP?** → **They are sent again**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) What is TCP, next to IP?** → **IP is the addressing; TCP is the promise-keeping**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What does encryption lock?** → **The contents of the packets, with a key**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) What does an interceptor see without the key?** → **Only scrambled data**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) What if the pirate catches the key?** → **The lock opens for them too — secrecy dies with the key's safety**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) Name two key-keeping rules.** → **Guard keys, change them, never send them alongside the message**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) What does TCP alone not give?** → **Secrecy — packets can arrive whole and still be read**
