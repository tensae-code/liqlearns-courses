## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Performance — the measurable side** — A network's **performance** is measured, not felt: **bandwidth** (how much data the road can carry), **speed / throughput** (how fast data actually arrives), **delay (latency)** (how long a message takes to cross), and **reliability** (how often it works, whole and unharmed). Jordan's Grade 7 book teaches the measures beside the networks themselves, so a pupil can say 'the delay is high at noon' instead of 'the internet is bad'.
2. **Effectiveness — performance for a purpose** — **Effectiveness** asks the next question: does the network serve its **users' actual work** — lessons load, files arrive, calls hold — at a cost the school can bear? A network can perform beautifully on a measure nobody needs (huge bandwidth at 3 a.m.) and fail effectively where it matters (thirty pupils at 9 a.m.). Improving performance follows the measures: find the narrow place — an old device, a busy hour, a long cable — and widen that, not everything.
