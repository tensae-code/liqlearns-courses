## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **CAN — the campus area network** — A **Campus Area Network (CAN)** interconnects the **LANs of one institution** spread over a **campus** — a university, a school, a company site, a hospital — across distances of a few kilometres at most, on ground the institution itself controls. It is owned and run by the one institution, so its roads can be fast and private: the library's catalogue, the laboratory's instruments and the office's records live on one network, though they sleep in different buildings.
2. **CAN among its neighbours — LAN, MAN, WAN** — The ladder of sizes now reads whole: **PAN** one person, **LAN** one building, **CAN** one campus of buildings, **MAN** one city, **WAN** countries and the world. CAN and MAN can cover similar ground; the difference is **ownership and purpose** — a CAN is one institution's private linking of its own LANs, while a MAN serves many organisations across a city, usually on a provider's roads. Bangladesh's book keeps CAN in the ladder because its students live on campuses: the network they use daily deserved its own rung.
