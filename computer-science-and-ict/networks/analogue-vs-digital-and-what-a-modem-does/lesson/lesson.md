# Analogue vs digital — and what a modem does

## Do it first

Hum a sliding note — up, down, smooth. Now clap single claps: on, off, on, off. You have just made both kinds of signal. Which one is a computer's own?

## Sub lesson 1 — Analogue — the continuous wave

Media carry two signal kinds. **Analogue** signals are **continuous waves** whose **amplitude (loudness)** and **frequency (pitch)** carry the information — as in **sound, radio and the historic telephone system**.

[Picture 14521 — a smooth wave and a square pulse train, with a modem]

## Sub lesson 2 — Digital — discrete pulses of 1 and 0

**Digital** signals are **discrete on/off pulses of 1s and 0s**, like a computer's own working. They are **harder for noise to corrupt**, can be **repeatedly strengthened over long distances**, and need **no conversion between computers**.

[GIF 14522 — pulses moulded into a wave, travelling, restored to pulses]

## Sub lesson 3 — The modem — modulate, demodulate

Because the **public telephone system (POTS)** was built **analogue**, a **modem** (from **modulate/demodulate**) converts a computer's **digital signals to analogue** for the line and **back again** at the other end. **Modems always work in pairs.** **Communications processors** — modems, multiplexers, front-end processors, concentrators — are the hardware supporting this transmission and reception.
