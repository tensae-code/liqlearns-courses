# Lesson plan — Analogue vs digital — and what a modem does

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Networks · **Lesson:** Analogue vs digital — and what a modem does
**Place in the curriculum:** Title — Networks in the Computer Science and ICT ladder (TITLES.md); stocked from Ethiopia's Grade 10 book, Unit 2 — Computer Network. The ladder is the recommended order, never a forced one.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** **Analogue** signals are **continuous waves**; **digital** signals are **discrete 1s and 0s** — and a **modem** converts between them for the telephone line.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Telecommunications systems — the modem is one of its communications processors, now in depth.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or in steps — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Analogue — the continuous wave** — continuous waves; amplitude and frequency carry the information
2. **Digital — discrete pulses of 1 and 0** — discrete pulses; harder to corrupt; strengthenable; no conversion between computers
3. **The modem — modulate, demodulate** — modem converts both ways on the analogue POTS; modems work in pairs; communications processors

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Telecommunications systems — parts and hidden jobs. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Tell analogue from digital by shape and by what carries the information.
2. Give digital's three advantages.
3. Explain what a modem does, why the telephone line needs one, and why modems work in pairs.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — Hum a sliding note — up, down, smooth. Now clap single claps: on, off, on, off. You have just made both kinds of signal. Which one is a computer's own? (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Wave = analogue (smooth, continuous). Steps = digital (on/off, 1/0). The modem stands where the computer's steps must ride the telephone's wave — and its twin undoes the change at the far end.
