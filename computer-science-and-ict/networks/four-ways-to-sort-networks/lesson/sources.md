**Acceptance count: taught in 9 countries (Ethiopia, Fiji, Libya, Laos, Greece, Jordan, Armenia, Indonesia, South Sudan).**

The Grade 9 teacher guide's closing answer keys (guide placement item 27; Unit 2 keys at guide printed pp. 36–37) were used as the verified-answer check for this lesson's question files.

Fiji's own way (Year 12 Computer Studies student book): Written locally by the same TEST/USP panel for the trialled Year 12 syllabus; the Internet chapter is grounded in Fiji (fiji.gov.fj URL anatomy with the .fj country code, and Fiji ISPs Connect Fiji, Unwired Fiji, Vodafone, Digicel and Kidanet by Fintel), programming is a full Visual Basic course with hands-on examples, and data management is taught as a move to cloud storage (Google Drive, Dropbox, SpiderOak).

Libya's own way (Grade 9, Computer student book): the whole year is networks and the internet — a full networks unit (types, cables, topology, components), then Internet Explorer and Google search taught as named skills, then email and a closing tour of social sites (Facebook, YouTube, Twitter, Instagram, Yahoo) with practical activities.

Laos's own way (Grade 8 ICT student book): a full PC-technician year in full colour — learners take a computer apart and rebuild it (CPU, RAM, board, cards, drives), install Windows XP and drivers, fix install faults by BIOS beep codes, then finish with networks and the internet.

Greece's own way (Grade 10, Efarmoges Pliroforikis A' Lykeiou): the older Lykeio applications book in four thematic units — hardware/software and applications, programming environments and creating applications (life cycle, modern IDEs, mobile-app and 3D object-oriented programming), communication and the internet (HTML5/CSS, learning online), and collaboration and safety online (cloud applications, telework, social networks, privacy).

Greece's own way (Grade 11, Eisagogi stis Arches tis Epistimis ton H/Y B' Lykeiou): a theory-first introduction to computer science in three units — basic concepts (theoretical vs applied CS), theoretical CS (problem, algorithms in depth including complexity/computability, data structures operations, debugging logic errors and documentation, programming paradigms and structured programming, software life cycle), and applied CS (operating systems in depth, information systems with databases and SQL/XML, networks, AI).

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 7 G7T1 Student): Grade 7 starts the whole subject from zero — what a computer is and how it works, hardware inside and out, software, operating systems and human-computer interaction — then networks from definition through topologies to secure communication and network performance.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 7 G7T1 Teacher): Same guide model as the other Jordan teacher guides — project-based three-stage lesson plans for every student-book lesson, with teaching strategies and assessment tools in the front matter (student-book page references below are the guide's own contents references).

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 7 G7 student): A new author team (Topchyan & Melkonyan, 2025) rebuilds the course around the "virtual world": data and encoding, networks, e-security and e-communication, then a full Python chapter — decisions, lists, loops, functions, objects and classes — taught as making the real world virtual.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 7 G7 teacher): SERIES MISMATCH NOTED — this guide (Velijanyan & Aslanyan) is the teacher guide for the OTHER Grade 7 series, whose student book (§1 Electronic security … §23 Computer hardware maintenance, Python-based) is NOT among the records; it does not match the Topchyan & Melkonyan Grade 7 student book filed as Book 12. The guide is complete in itself: 20 themes, a thematic plan, the student book's questions/tasks with answers for §1–§23, worked Python solutions, and assessment rubrics.

Indonesia's own way (Indonesia Grade 12 G12 Informatika student): Grade 12 (original Kurikulum Merdeka edition) is built around making things: single-board computers/controllers and a full Arduino C course (IDE, UnoArduSim simulator, sensors, S.O.S. LED and melody projects), then networks with cyber security and data governance, social impact with the UU ITE law, and a cross-field group project (smart trash bin / waste-bank apps) with journals, reports and peer assessment.

South Sudan's own way (Grade 10 S2 Student): Year 2 moves from single-computer skills to connected and analytical work — networks taught through intranet file-sharing and a full Microsoft Outlook unit (contacts, calendar, meetings, task lists), then a deep Excel unit that goes well beyond basics into lookup/financial/text functions, inventories, correlation and regression, what-if analysis with Solver, and workbook protection; social media is taught platform by platform with its ethical and legal issues.

South Sudan's own way (Grade 10 S2 Teacher): The guide mirrors the student book's three units with teaching notes, exercise answers and research tasks (e.g. learners research hybrid topologies themselves); it keeps the same emphasis on resource sharing in an intranet and on Excel as an analysis tool (what-if, Goal Seek, Solver).
