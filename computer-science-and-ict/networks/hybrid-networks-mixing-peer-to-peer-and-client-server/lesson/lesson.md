# Hybrid Networks — Mixing Peer-to-Peer and Client-Server

## Do it first

Must every file pass through a server, or may computers share directly like neighbours? Real networks refuse to choose once for everything: the accounts live centrally while the printers and files flow peer to peer down the corridor. That mixture has a name, and most networks you will ever use are it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The two pure structures — recalled

In a **peer-to-peer** network, computers are equals: each shares its own files and devices, none is in charge — simple and cheap, hard to secure and to back up as it grows. In a **client-server** network, central **servers** hold the shared data and services and **clients** ask them: controlled, securable, backed up — at the price of the server and its keeper. The comparison lesson weighs the pure forms; this lesson is about the world declining to be pure.

In your mind's eye: the two pure structures — recalled.

## Sub lesson 2 — The hybrid — each job to its fitting structure

A **hybrid network** mixes the two: **client-server where centrality earns its cost** — accounts, records, backups, the databases of the companion lessons — and **peer-to-peer where directness serves better** — a workgroup sharing its own files and printer without troubling the centre. Most real school, office and home networks are hybrid, whether or not anyone named them so. The designer's question is never 'which structure is best?' but 'which structure fits *this* service?' — asked service by service, which is what makes the hybrid a design and not a muddle.

In your mind's eye: the hybrid — each job to its fitting structure.
