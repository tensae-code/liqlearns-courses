# Radio waves — broadcasting in all directions

## Do it first

Drop a stone in a pond. The rings go everywhere at once — no aiming. A radio antenna does the same with waves. Who can catch them?

## Sub lesson 1 — The radio band

Waves from **3 kHz to 1 GHz** are **radio waves**; **1–300 GHz are microwaves** — **behaviour matters more than the boundary**.

[Picture 14514 — one antenna radiating to many receivers]

## Sub lesson 2 — Omnidirectional — no alignment needed

Radio waves are **omnidirectional** — **sent in all directions** from antennas that **need no alignment**. That suits **multicasting**, one sender to many receivers: **AM/FM radio, television, maritime radio, cordless phones and paging**. The cost: **interference from any other antenna on the same frequency**.

## Sub lesson 3 — Sky-mode radio travels far

**Sky-mode radio travels long distances**, as in **AM broadcasting** — the sky path from the propagation lesson, put to work.

## Sub lesson 4 — Radio keyed (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide keys **radio waves to broadcasting and satellite transmission**, and keys wireless against wired on four counts: **mobility and collaboration, accessibility in hard-to-cable areas, expandability without running cables, and secure guest access**.
