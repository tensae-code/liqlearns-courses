## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Sharing a folder — opening the door** — To **share a folder** is to offer it to the local network: the folder stays on its own computer, but other computers on the LAN can open it as if it were theirs. The sharing is switched on for a chosen folder, never for the whole disk by habit — share the folder the work needs. Once shared, the folder is reached by the **network path** through its owner's computer, and appears in the network places of the other machines in the room.
2. **Permissions and your address — governing the door** — Sharing without governing is giving the room your desk. **Sharing permissions** decide who may only **read** the folder and who may **change** it — the class reads the materials folder; only its owner writes there. To be reached, a computer is known by its **IP address**, and Mongolia teaches the finding of your own: the **ipconfig** command, typed at the command line, prints the machine's addresses. Permissions and address together are the lesson's pair: the door is opened, the door is governed, and the house has a number the visitors can use.
