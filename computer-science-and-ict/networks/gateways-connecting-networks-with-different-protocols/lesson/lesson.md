# Gateways — Connecting Networks With Different Protocols

## Do it first

A router joins networks that share a language and need directions. But what joins two networks that do not even speak alike — different protocols, different rules of address and form? At that border stands a translator, and this lesson is its portrait.

## Sub lesson 1 — The gateway — translator at the border

A **gateway** connects networks that use **different protocols** and **translates** between them: it receives a message in one protocol's form, understands it, and re-expresses it in the other's — form, addressing and rules converted, meaning preserved. It is the deepest of the joining devices of this title: a hub repeats, a switch directs by address, a router chooses roads between networks of one protocol family — a gateway changes the language itself.

## Sub lesson 2 — Where gateways stand

Gateways stand wherever unlike systems must cooperate: between a local network and a provider's differently-built network, between old and new generations of a system that cannot be replaced at once, and at the doors of services reached through the internet from networks of every kind. The home 'router' box is quietly several devices in one coat — switch, router, and often gateway among them. The lesson's test for naming any device is its work: if it translates protocols, gateway is its name, whatever the box is called in the shop.
