# Lesson plan — Cable Connectors — RJ45, BNC and Fibre

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Networks · **Lesson:** Cable Connectors — RJ45, BNC and Fibre
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Nepal (2026-11-14) — in the title Networks on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Nepal's *Computer Science, Class 10*, because no existing lesson teaches the three cable connectors together as one lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a connector is the plug at the end of a cable that joins the cable to a device or to another cable — and each cable family has its own connector: RJ45 for twisted-pair cable, BNC for coaxial cable, and fibre connectors for fibre-optic cable.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on knowing that networks are joined by cables (wired media); what a connector is, and which connector belongs to which cable, are built inside the lesson from Unit 1 §1.3 of Nepal's book.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **RJ45 — the plug on twisted-pair cable** — The clear plastic plug with eight pins at the end of a twisted-pair network cable; it pushes into the network port until its small clip clicks, and the clip is pressed to pull it out again.
2. **BNC — the connector on coaxial cable** — The metal connector that joins coaxial cable with a bayonet lock: push it on and twist it, and it stays locked; twist it back to free it.
3. **Fibre connectors — joining a glass thread of light** — The small, carefully polished ends that join fibre-optic cables so light passes straight from one glass thread into the next; the ends must be clean and exactly lined up.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a connector is and what job it does at the end of a cable.
2. Name the connector for each cable family: RJ45 for twisted pair, BNC for coaxial, fibre connectors for fibre-optic cable.
3. Say how an RJ45 plug locks and is released, and how a BNC connector locks.
4. Say why a fibre connector's end must be clean and exactly lined up, and pick the right connector in a new example.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. If your school has them, one short network cable of each kind to look at — looking is enough; nothing has to be plugged in.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — A cable by itself joins nothing: a network is made when the end of a cable meets a device. Look at the end of any cable you know — a charger, a network cable, an aerial cable — and ask: what shape is its end, and what does that shape fit into? Keep the question; this lesson answers it for the three network cable families. (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
Mixes the connectors up → sort by cable, not by shape alone: twisted pair always ends in RJ45, coaxial in BNC, glass fibre in a fibre connector. Pushes an RJ45 in and cannot free it → the clip must be pressed flat first; pulling against the clip is what breaks plugs. Thinks a fibre join is like any other join → light is the signal here: a dirty or crooked end does not weaken the join, it blocks the message.
