# Cable Connectors — RJ45, BNC and Fibre — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two, in your own words.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) A network cable will not stay in its port — it keeps sliding out. What has most likely broken, and what should have been done when removing it?**
**Answer:** The RJ45's plastic clip has most likely snapped off, so nothing locks the plug in. When removing it, the clip should have been pressed flat before pulling; pulling against the clip is what breaks it.

**2. (Sub lesson 2) Why does a BNC join stay firm even when the cable is tugged, while a plain push-on join might work loose?**
**Answer:** The bayonet lock's studs sit in the locked ends of the slots after the quarter-turn twist, so the connector cannot be pulled straight off; it must be twisted back first.

**3. (Sub lesson 3) A fibre link worked yesterday and fails today after a connector was unplugged and left lying on a desk. Give the likely cause.**
**Answer:** Dust or dirt on the unprotected fibre end (or the ends no longer lined up cleanly); the light is blocked at the join. Fibre ends are capped when not joined and kept clean.
