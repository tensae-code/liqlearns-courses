## The bridge between wireless and wired

A **Wireless Access Point (WAP)** is the **intermediary device that connects wireless communication devices to a wireless network**. It works through **Bluetooth and Wi-Fi**, and it stands as the **intermediary between a network's wireless and wired devices**: waves on one side, cables on the other, the WAP in the middle joining them.

[Picture 12912 — a WAP joining waves to wires]

## A wireless LAN — a LAN without wires (Grade 10 depth)

A **wireless LAN (WLAN)** carries a **wired LAN's service over radio frequency**, as an **extension of a wired LAN or an alternative to one**. WLANs give **mobility inside a local coverage area**. They are **standardised by the IEEE 802.11 committee** and sold as **Wi-Fi**, with the book's current rate given as **up to 11 Mbps and faster speeds coming**. IEEE is the **Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers**.

## Access points bridge and repeat — and overlap by 10–15% (Grade 10 depth)

An **access point (AP)** **bridges wireless traffic into the wired network** and can also **repeat for wireless nodes, doubling their reach** — so **larger areas are served by several APs overlapping by 10–15%**.

## How a wireless LAN is built (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide keys the **access point as the portal joining devices to the LAN**, **extending coverage and user count**, with **overlapping access points** as the way to keep **strong, fast coverage through solid walls and across a whole property**.
