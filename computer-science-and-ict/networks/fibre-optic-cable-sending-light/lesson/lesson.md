# Fibre-optic cable — sending light

## Do it first

Shine a torch down a dark corridor. The light travels and arrives. Now shrink that corridor to a hair of glass. That is this lesson's cable.

## Sub lesson 1 — Light instead of electricity

**Fibre-optic cable** guides **light impulses** along **glass or plastic fibres** — a **central glass core** in protective layers — with **bits encoded as light**. So **electrical interference simply does not arise**. This makes fibre the **standard for joining networks between buildings**, **immune to moisture and lightning effects**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Farther and faster

Fibre carries signals **much farther and far faster than coax or twisted pair**, opening services such as **video conferencing**.

## Sub lesson 3 — Multimode and single-mode

Its two types: **multimode** (wider core) and **single-mode** (longer reach, higher cost) — both **high-bandwidth**. The practical visits a school installation where a **fibre backbone feeds Cat 6 twisted pair** out to workstations.

## Sub lesson 4 — Ethiopia's network and fibre's advantages (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide keys that Ethiopia has a **single Internet Service Provider, Ethio Telecom**, and that joining an **Addis Ababa office to one in Bahir Dar calls for fibre-optic cable** over the telecom network. Its five keyed advantages of fibre over copper: **greater bandwidth, faster light-borne speed, longer distances, better reliability** (immune to the temperature changes that disturb copper) and a **thinner, sturdier cable** that withstands more pull. School network cables must be **protected, organised and managed**, because damage interrupts the internet and the school's application services with it.
