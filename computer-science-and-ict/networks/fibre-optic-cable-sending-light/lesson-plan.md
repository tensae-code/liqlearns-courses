# Lesson plan — Fibre-optic cable — sending light

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Networks · **Lesson:** Fibre-optic cable — sending light
**Place in the curriculum:** Title — Networks in the Computer Science and ICT ladder (TITLES.md); stocked from Ethiopia's Grade 10 book, Unit 2 — Computer Network. The ladder is the recommended order, never a forced one.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** **Fibre** guides **light impulses** along glass or plastic fibres, so **electrical interference does not arise** — faster and farther than copper.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the guided-media lessons — the learner knows copper's troubles; fibre is the medium that escapes them.

GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or in steps — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Light instead of electricity** — bits encoded as light; no electrical interference; between buildings
2. **Farther and faster** — much farther and faster than coax or twisted pair
3. **Multimode and single-mode** — multimode wider core; single-mode longer reach, higher cost
4. **Ethiopia's network and fibre's advantages (teacher guide — evidence)** — Ethio Telecom; Addis Ababa–Bahir Dar; the five keyed advantages

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Twisted-pair cable — twisting cancels interference. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say how fibre carries bits and why electrical interference does not arise.
2. Compare fibre's reach and speed with coax and twisted pair.
3. Tell multimode from single-mode.
4. Give the guide's five advantages of fibre over copper and name Ethiopia's single ISP.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil.
