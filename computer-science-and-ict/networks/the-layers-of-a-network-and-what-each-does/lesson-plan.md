## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Layers — the work divided** — A network divides communication into **layers**: levels of work, each doing one part of the journey and serving the layer above it. The idea is the engineer's oldest trick — a hard journey split into stages, each stage simple, each stage trusting the one below. Your message is prepared at the top layers (what is said, in what form), carried by the middle (addressed, routed), and moved by the bottom layers as signals on wires and air. Change one layer's technology — cable to wireless — and the layers above need never know.
2. **The postal analogy — layer by layer** — The book maps the layers onto the **postal system**: you write and address the letter (the message prepared, its destination named); the post office accepts and sorts it (the route chosen); trucks and planes carry sacks between cities (the physical journey); the far office sorts again and the postman delivers to the house; the receiver opens and reads — the message arriving as if no layers intervened. Each postal worker knows only their stage, yet the letter crosses the country: that is what layers buy a network — specialists at every stage, and a journey no single part must understand whole.

A network's message travels the same way: through layers of specialised work that the sender never sees whole. Grade 9 teaches those layers through the post itself. (lesson/lesson.md).
