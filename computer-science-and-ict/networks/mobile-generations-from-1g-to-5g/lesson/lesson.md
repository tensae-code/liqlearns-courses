# Mobile generations — from 1G to 5G

## Do it first

Ask an elder what the first mobile phone they saw could do. Then look at yours. Five generations of climbing stand between them — this lesson climbs the stairs.

## Sub lesson 1 — Numbered generations, present at 4G

**Mobile generations are numbered**: the book places the **present at 4G, with 5G emerging**.

[Picture 14530 — a five-step staircase, 1G to 5G]

## Sub lesson 2 — 4G — mobile broadband everywhere

**Fourth generation** aims high: **high capacity, 100 Mbit/s in a moving car and 1 Gbit/s standing still, at least 100 Mbit/s between any two points on earth, smooth handoff across different networks, seamless global roaming, high quality for multimedia, and working with existing standards** — earning its other name, **mobile broadband everywhere**. **4G LTE** is its type, promising **100 Mbps** transfer.

## Sub lesson 3 — 5G — everyone and everything

**5G** goes beyond faster downloads to a **flexible, responsive network connecting everyone and everything — machines, objects and devices** — with **multi-Gbps peak speeds, ultra-low latency, greater reliability and massive capacity**.

## Sub lesson 4 — Generations mapped to technologies (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide keys the five generations to their technologies: **1G now obsolete; GSM enables 2G, UMTS 3G, LTE 4G and NR 5G** — where the student book names GSM alone.
