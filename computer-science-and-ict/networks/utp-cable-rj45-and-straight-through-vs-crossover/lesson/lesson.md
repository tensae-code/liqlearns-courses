# UTP cable, RJ45 and straight-through vs crossover

## Do it first

Look at the plug on a network cable. It only fits one way up. Count its pins. This lesson names that plug, grades the cable, and shows the two ways its wires can be ordered.

## Sub lesson 1 — UTP — the commonest network cable

**Unshielded twisted pair (UTP)** is the **commonest network cable**. It follows the **IEEE 802.3 Ethernet** wiring standards, is graded in **categories from Cat 1 upward**, and does **without a physical shield**. IEEE is the **Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers**.

## Sub lesson 2 — The RJ45 connector

Its connector is the **keyed RJ45 (registered jack)**, which **fits only one way**. It joins **computers to routers and switches**.

## Sub lesson 3 — Straight-through and crossover — the two wirings

How the wires are **ordered in the connector** gives two cable types. **Straight-through** is used **between different devices** — **computer to hub or switch** — and is the cable found in **school labs**. **Crossover** is used **between similar devices** — **computer to computer**. In the laboratory practical you rehearse **both colour-code orders**, **make UTP cables**, and **link two computers** with **Cat 5/6/7** cable.

## Sub lesson 4 — The cabling kit and its tools (teacher guide — evidence)

The practical's kit is **UTP cable, RJ-45 connectors, a crimper and a network cable tester**. A **cable tester verifies the electrical connections** in a cable and the **correct wiring of its connectors** — basic testers check **continuity** between the ends. A **crimper joins cable and connector** into a reliable connection. A straight-through cable carries RJ-45 connectors wired to the **same pinout at both ends**, under either the **T568A or T568B** standard. The guide also names the **RJ-11 telephone connector** — twisted pairs in a modular jack of **two, four or six contacts**.
