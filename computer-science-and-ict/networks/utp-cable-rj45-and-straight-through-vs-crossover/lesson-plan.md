# Lesson plan — UTP cable, RJ45 and straight-through vs crossover

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Networks · **Lesson:** UTP cable, RJ45 and straight-through vs crossover
**Place in the curriculum:** Title — Networks in the Computer Science and ICT ladder (TITLES.md); stocked from Ethiopia's Grade 10 book, Unit 2 — Computer Network. The ladder is the recommended order, never a forced one.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** **UTP** joins devices through the keyed **RJ45** plug; **straight-through** links **different** devices, **crossover** links **similar** ones.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Twisted-pair cable — the learner already knows what a twisted pair is and why it is twisted.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **UTP — the commonest network cable** — UTP, IEEE 802.3 Ethernet, categories from Cat 1, no shield
2. **The RJ45 connector** — the keyed RJ45 fits only one way and joins computers to routers and switches
3. **Straight-through and crossover — the two wirings** — straight-through for different devices, crossover for similar ones
4. **The cabling kit and its tools (teacher guide — evidence)** — tester, crimper, T568A/T568B, RJ-11

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Twisted-pair cable — twisting cancels interference. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what UTP is, which standard it follows, and how it is graded.
2. Say what the RJ45 is and why it fits only one way.
3. Wire the right cable for the job: straight-through between different devices, crossover between similar ones.
4. Name the lab kit and say what the tester and crimper each do.

## What you need
For the practical: UTP cable, RJ-45 connectors, a crimper and a cable tester, if the school lab has them; otherwise paper and a pencil.

## If the learner is stuck
- If the learner mixes the two cables, use the pair rule: different devices — straight; same kind — cross. Then check: which two devices are being joined?
