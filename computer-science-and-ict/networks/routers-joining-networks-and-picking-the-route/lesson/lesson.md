# Routers — joining networks and picking the route

## Do it first

A taxi driver in a city with many streets chooses by distance and traffic. Now imagine a message that must leave your network and reach a different one. Something must choose its streets. That is this lesson.

## Routing — finding the best path

A **router** is likened to a **taxi driver choosing among streets by distance and traffic**. Its job is **routing — finding the best path for messages** — and **connecting dissimilar, different networks together**.

## When a router is — and is not — needed

If sender and receiver sit on the **same switch**, the router is **not needed**. When a message must **cross to another network**, the router picks the **best route** using its **routing table** — a table of **destination networks and interface numbers**.
