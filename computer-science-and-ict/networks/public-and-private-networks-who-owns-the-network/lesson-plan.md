## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Public networks — the provider's roads** — A **public network** is built by a **provider** and offered to the **public** — anyone may subscribe and use it, as with the telephone network and the internet access a carrier sells. The provider owns the roads, maintains them, and charges for passage; the users own nothing but their subscription. Public networks win by reach and shared cost: no school could build a national network, but every school can rent a road on one.
2. **Private networks — one owner's ground** — A **private network** is owned by **one organisation** for **its own** communication — a bank's branches, a university's CAN, a company's lines between offices. Admission is the owner's gift only; the organisation controls security, speed and rules because it controls the roads themselves, and pays the whole cost for that control. The two kinds interlock in practice: a private network's distant parts often travel *over* public roads, fenced by encryption — the VPN idea — which is the ownership lesson's closing picture: public ground, privately fenced.
