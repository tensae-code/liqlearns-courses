# IP addresses — IPv4 and IPv6

## Do it first

Every house on a street has one address, and no other house has it. Devices on a network live the same way. Read one device's address aloud — this lesson takes it apart.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Internet Protocol and its address

The **Internet Protocol** is the **principal protocol of internetworking** — the **rules governing the format of data** sent over the internet or a local network. An **IP address** is the **unique address identifying a device** on that network, carrying **location information** so information can be **routed between computers, routers and websites**.

## Sub lesson 2 — IPv4 — dotted decimal, 32 bits

Two versions stand: **IPv4**, the common current one — **32-bit addresses** written most often as **dotted decimal, four numbers of 0–255 separated by dots** — and **IPv6**. Each of the four numbers is an **octet**. IPv4 notation in **binary, dotted decimal and hexadecimal** is pictured in the book.
