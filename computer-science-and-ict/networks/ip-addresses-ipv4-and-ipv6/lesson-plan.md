# Lesson plan — IP addresses — IPv4 and IPv6

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Networks · **Lesson:** IP addresses — IPv4 and IPv6
**Place in the curriculum:** Title — Networks in the Computer Science and ICT ladder (TITLES.md); stocked from Ethiopia's Grade 10 book, Unit 2 — Computer Network. The ladder is the recommended order, never a forced one.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** An **IP address** is a device's **unique address** on a network; **IPv4** writes it as **four numbers of 0–255** separated by dots — **32 bits** in all.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Protocols — the learner knows IP addresses devices and forwards data; this lesson gives the address itself.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what the Internet Protocol governs and what an IP address is.
2. Describe IPv4's shape: bits, numbers, range, separators.
3. Read a dotted-decimal address into its four octets.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil.

## If the learner is stuck
- Four numbers, dots between, none above 255, 32 bits in all. If a 'number' reads 300, it is not an IPv4 octet — the address is wrong somewhere.
