Placement choice: this lesson was built new for the ICT expansion (Egypt, 2026-11-14) because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson — the live tree was checked for the slug and for near matches before building. The placements file named this slug directly (NEW LESSON NEEDED — networks/network-transfer-speed-and-bandwidth). It sits in the Networks title (networks/), following the book's own order. Provenance scope note: every placement stocked here is from an Egypt student book (all counted placements in this expansion are student books); parallel Arabic/English/French editions of the same term book are cited once, by the primary edition read, with the parallels named in the book entry. Media slots in lesson/lesson.md are described slots only ([Picture — …]); no picture numbers are invented.

Egypt's own way (Grade 10, Term 1 Programming and Artificial Intelligence student book): A full Japanese-style "Information Study" course adopted for Egypt's new secondary track: 13 chapters running from information theory, ethics and security through number systems, data analysis (frequency, variance, correlation, regression in spreadsheets), simulation and queues, Python, and a final AI-programming project chapter building web pages, a quiz app and a block-breaker game with generative AI.

Greece's own way (Grade 8, Pliroforiki B' Gymnasiou, 2024): Grade 8 moves from bits/bytes and data rates into networks, then data analysis in spreadsheets and presentations, then modular Scratch programming (own blocks with parameters, realistic character motion), list data structures, a full AI unit training a neural network, and ICT's changes to the economy (e-commerce, Industry 4.0, new jobs, sustainability).

Bangladesh's own way (Grade 8, Digital Technology): Grade 8 deepens the same project pattern — a public information-verification campaign, a family phone-safety investigation, a planned civic/e-commerce service, Python with binary logic first, and an online fair for the Asia-Pacific experience. The NCTB Digital Technology line runs in parallel English and Bangla editions; placements are cited from the English Version with the Bangla Version parallel spot-checked.

Bangladesh's own way (HSC ICT, Classes XI–XII and Alim): one compulsory ICT book for every HSC and Alim student in the country, saturated with Bangladesh itself: Bangabandhu Satellite-1 and its ground stations, bdjobs and chakri.com job portals, Daraz and Bikroy e-commerce, the Walton factory's automated production line, the National ID card used to teach primary keys and its database used to teach RDBMS at work, a full Bangla Unicode table and a worked example of putting Bangla text on a web page with meta charset, the national letter-grade scale (A+ from 80) as the else-if programme, "I Love Bangladesh" as the loop example text, an algorithm for reaching college by bicycle or rickshaw, and the Digital Bangladesh programme — National Data Centre, hi-tech parks, Startup Bangladesh and Bangladesh Bank's QR-based Cashless Bangladesh. C is the HSC programming language, taught with scanf/printf in Code::Blocks.


Kazakhstan's own way (Ataeva & Vyshkova G8 Informatics, NIS): the NIS Grade 8 course is the most distinctive of the wave: vector graphics flows into geographic information systems (planning an excursion, making maps, visualising data in GIS), spreadsheets are used to solve logical problems, loops are taught through mathematics, physics, business and multimedia problems, and the year ends with a full project cycle — six stages, presentation, documentation, defence — plus a mini-project on artificial intelligence.

Kazakhstan's own way (Zhunusov G8 Informatics, bilingual edition): the bilingual Grade 8 book follows the updated national frame (measurement and coding, processor, networks and bandwidth; health; Excel statistics; Python while/for with break/continue/else and tracing) and ends its practical-programming part with a four-part worked game project, "Akkala".

Kazakhstan's own way (Zhunusov G8 Informatics, English-medium): the English-medium twin of the bilingual Grade 8 book (Book 40) — same five chapters; the practical-programming game project is the "Melting Snowman" game in four parts.

Kazakhstan's own way (Shaniev G8 «INFORMATICS»): an English-medium course in four chapters that runs spreadsheets and Python programming side by side, with «Check yourself» after every chapter and three full game projects at the end (Guess the Number, a quiz game, Rock–Scissors–Paper).

Kazakhstan's own way (Atayeva/Vyushkova/Zhurbenko G8 «Информатика» NIS): the NIS Grade 8 book is the widest of the set: a whole section on vector graphics and geographic information systems (planning an excursion, making maps, visualising data in GIS), octal and hexadecimal coding, loops applied separately to mathematics, physics, business and animation, strings and subroutines, and a nine-step project method ending in a mini-project on artificial intelligence.

Kazakhstan's own way (Zhunusov G8 «INFORMATICS»): the Astana-kitap English-medium Grade 8 book (2nd edition) opens with measuring information by Hartley's formula worked on the Kazakh alphabet in Cyrillic (42) and Latin (32) scripts, and its practical-programming chapter builds one game — the "Melting Snowman" — across four parts from algorithm design to testing.
