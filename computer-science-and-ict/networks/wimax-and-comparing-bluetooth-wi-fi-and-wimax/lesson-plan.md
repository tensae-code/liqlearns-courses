## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **WiMAX — the metropolitan wireless** — **WiMAX** (Worldwide Interoperability for Microwave Access) is broadband wireless at the **metropolitan** scale: one base station serves an area **kilometres** wide, carrying internet to homes and offices without a cable to each — a wireless answer where laying lines is slow or dear. It sits in the network-kinds ladder where MAN sits among wired kinds: bigger than a LAN, smaller than a WAN, and wireless all through.
2. **The comparison — three scales side by side** — The book's table compares the three by **standard, speed and range**: **Bluetooth** — personal, metres of range, modest speed, tiny power; **Wi-Fi** — the LAN, tens of metres, fast; **WiMAX** — the metropolitan network, kilometres, broadband speed. Read the table as one law in three lines: **range, speed and power trade against each other** — no technology wins all three, which is why all three exist. The citizen's skill is matching the errand to the scale: earphones to Bluetooth, the classroom to Wi-Fi, the unwired town to WiMAX.
