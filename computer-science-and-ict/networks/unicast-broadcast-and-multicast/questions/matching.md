# Unicast, Broadcast and Multicast — matching

Match each item on the left with the one it belongs to on the right.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) What is unicast?** → **One sender to one receiver**

**2. (Sub lesson 1) Name a unicast example.** → **A message to one friend (or a page to one browser)**

**3. (Sub lesson 1) What is unicast's cost problem?** → **A thousand viewers need a thousand streams**

**4. (Sub lesson 1) What is broadcast?** → **One sender to every receiver on the network**

**5. (Sub lesson 1) Why is broadcast used sparingly?** → **It burdens everyone, wanted or not**

**6. (Sub lesson 2) What is multicast?** → **One sender to a chosen group of receivers**

**7. (Sub lesson 2) Who receives a multicast?** → **Only those who joined the group**

**8. (Sub lesson 2) How does multicast save the road?** → **One stream branches only where members wait**

**9. (Sub lesson 2) Name a multicast example.** → **A live lesson streamed to enrolled classes**

**10. (Sub lesson 2) What decides the mode?** → **The audience — one, all, or the group between**
