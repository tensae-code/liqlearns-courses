# Video script — Unicast, Broadcast and Multicast

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

**Unicast** sends from **one sender to one receiver**: your message to one friend, a page delivered to your browser. It is private and exact, and its cost grows with the audience — a thousand viewers need a thousand streams. **Broadcast** sends from **one sender to every receiver** on the network: an announcement all must hear, like address announcements machines make. Broadcast reaches everyone with one sending — and burdens everyone with it, wanted or not, so it is kept inside local networks and used sparingly.
**Multicast** is the middle wisdom: **one sender to a group** — the receivers who joined that group, and no others. A live lesson streamed to the classes that enrolled, a match carried to its subscribers: one stream travels the shared road and branches only where group members wait, saving the sender's thousand copies of unicast and sparing the uninterested the broadcast's flood. The three modes are a designer's daily choice — audience first: one, all, or the group between.
