**Acceptance count: taught in 4 countries (Ethiopia, Mozambique, Afghanistan, Yemen).**

Yemen's own way (Grade 11): the book pivots from operating the machine to understanding information itself — data kinds, number systems and coding get full units before the applications (presentations, Excel) and a networks/Internet finale; every unit closes with a formal تقويم (assessment).

Afghanistan's own way (Grade 10): a Windows XP-era office apprenticeship — one full chapter per application, taught menu by menu (File, Edit, Insert, Format), with the Dari keyboard language added as its own lesson; networks arrive only in the final chapter.

The student book prints the word once as “netwok” (printed p. 37) — a misprint. This lesson teaches “network” and treats the slip as nothing. The four-shifts part is teacher-guide evidence (guide placement item 7, printed p. 22), labelled as such in the table; it supports, and does not replace, the student book's definition.

The Grade 9 teacher guide's closing answer keys (guide placement item 27; Unit 2 keys at guide printed pp. 36–37) were used as the verified-answer check for this lesson's question files.
