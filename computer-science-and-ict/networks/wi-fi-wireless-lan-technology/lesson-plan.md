## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Wi-Fi — the LAN without cables** — **Wi-Fi** is the technology of **wireless local area networks**: devices connect to an **access point** by radio, and the access point joins them to the wired network and the internet. It works in the **2.4 GHz and 5 GHz bands** — the lower band travels farther and through walls better; the higher band carries more speed over shorter reach. Within its building-sized range, Wi-Fi gives the LAN's services — shared files, printers, internet — with the freedom to move, which is why homes, schools and cafés run on it.
2. **Range and security — the open air's price** — Radio respects no walls, so Wi-Fi's **range** is also its **risk**: the network extends into the street, where a stranger's device can listen, guess at the door, or set up a false access point with the same name. The book's defences are the citizen's checklist: a **strong password** on the network, modern **encryption (WPA2/WPA3)** switched on, the router's own admin password changed, and care with open networks in public places — on an unencrypted café network, other listeners are not a possibility but a population. The convenience is real; so is the air it travels.
