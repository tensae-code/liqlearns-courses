# Wi-Fi — Wireless LAN Technology

## Do it first

The network with no visible network: laptops and phones join it through the air of one building, and the cable is a radio conversation. Wi-Fi is the wireless LAN nearly everyone alive has used — this lesson names what it is, how far it reaches, and what can go wrong inside it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Wi-Fi — the LAN without cables

**Wi-Fi** is the technology of **wireless local area networks**: devices connect to an **access point** by radio, and the access point joins them to the wired network and the internet. It works in the **2.4 GHz and 5 GHz bands** — the lower band travels farther and through walls better; the higher band carries more speed over shorter reach. Within its building-sized range, Wi-Fi gives the LAN's services — shared files, printers, internet — with the freedom to move, which is why homes, schools and cafés run on it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Range and security — the open air's price

Radio respects no walls, so Wi-Fi's **range** is also its **risk**: the network extends into the street, where a stranger's device can listen, guess at the door, or set up a false access point with the same name. The book's defences are the citizen's checklist: a **strong password** on the network, modern **encryption (WPA2/WPA3)** switched on, the router's own admin password changed, and care with open networks in public places — on an unencrypted café network, other listeners are not a possibility but a population. The convenience is real; so is the air it travels.
