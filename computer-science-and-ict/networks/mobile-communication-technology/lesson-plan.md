**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Mongolia (2026-11-14) — in the title Networks on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). ## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Generations and families — 1G to 4G, GSM and CDMA** — Mobile technology grew in **generations**: **1G**, the first analogue voice networks; **2G**, digital voice — and with it **SMS**, the short written message that outlived its engineers' expectations; **3G**, carrying data and the mobile internet; **4G**, fast data as the default. The digital world divided into families, above all **GSM** — the global system whose SIM card carries the subscriber's identity between phones — and **CDMA**, the code-division family spreading calls by codes. A phone's generation and family together say what it can do and where it can roam.
2. **Base stations, the connected call, and Mongolia's map** — A call travels by **base stations**: the phone speaks by radio to the nearest station, the network routes the call through its switches to the station nearest the other phone, and the last radio hop completes it — a **cell** being each station's served ground, which is why the technology is called cellular. Mongolia's own map is the lesson's ground truth: coverage spread from Ulaanbaatar across the aimags along roads and settlements, over steppe distances few networks on earth must cross. The book closes where its ethics always closes: **safe and polite phone use** — the technology in the pocket governed by the citizen who carries it.
