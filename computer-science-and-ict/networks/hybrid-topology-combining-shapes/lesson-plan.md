## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Hybrid — shapes combined on purpose** — A **hybrid topology** combines **two or more basic topologies** in one network: a star-bus (stars joined by a backbone bus), a star-ring, a tree with a mesh core. Each part keeps its own character and its own strengths; the combination is chosen because a real network's parts have different needs — the examination office wants a controlled star, the laboratories a resilient mesh, and both must be one network. The pure topologies of the earlier lessons are the alphabet; the hybrid is the sentence real builders write with it.
2. **The internet — the hybrid of hybrids** — The book's closing example is the largest: **the internet is a hybrid topology** — every shape ever named lives somewhere inside it, joined by every joining device of this title. The hybrid's gifts are the fit and the growth: parts can be added, replaced and re-shaped without redesigning the whole. Its costs are honest too — design and management grow complex where shapes meet, and the joining points are where faults love to hide. The lesson ends the topologies where practice begins: choose per part, join with care, and document the whole.
