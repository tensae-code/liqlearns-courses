# Hybrid Topology — Combining Shapes

## Do it first

No real city is built on one street pattern — the old town winds, the new town grids, the ring road circles them both. Real networks are the same: grown in pieces, each piece the shape that fitted its place. This lesson names that grown-together form and teaches when mixing is wisdom rather than mess.

## Sub lesson 1 — Hybrid — shapes combined on purpose

A **hybrid topology** combines **two or more basic topologies** in one network: a star-bus (stars joined by a backbone bus), a star-ring, a tree with a mesh core. Each part keeps its own character and its own strengths; the combination is chosen because a real network's parts have different needs — the examination office wants a controlled star, the laboratories a resilient mesh, and both must be one network. The pure topologies of the earlier lessons are the alphabet; the hybrid is the sentence real builders write with it.

In your mind's eye: hybrid — shapes combined on purpose.

## Sub lesson 2 — The internet — the hybrid of hybrids

The book's closing example is the largest: **the internet is a hybrid topology** — every shape ever named lives somewhere inside it, joined by every joining device of this title. The hybrid's gifts are the fit and the growth: parts can be added, replaced and re-shaped without redesigning the whole. Its costs are honest too — design and management grow complex where shapes meet, and the joining points are where faults love to hide. The lesson ends the topologies where practice begins: choose per part, join with care, and document the whole.

In your mind's eye: the internet — the hybrid of hybrids.
