## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The five grounds of comparison** — Jordan compares the three on five grounds. **Speed**: Wi-Fi is fastest by far; Bluetooth is modest; Zigbee is slowest. **Range**: Wi-Fi and meshed Zigbee reach farthest; Bluetooth is a room's reach. **Power**: Zigbee sips, Bluetooth is frugal, Wi-Fi is thirsty. **Cost**: Bluetooth and Zigbee chips are cheap; Wi-Fi costs more in money and battery. **Reliability**: each is reliable inside its design — the mesh makes Zigbee stubborn, Wi-Fi's speed makes it steady for streams, Bluetooth's pairing makes it personal.
2. **Choosing — the job picks the standard** — The comparison's lesson is that there is no winner, only fits. **Streaming video or files** — Wi-Fi. **A headset, a watch, a keyboard** — Bluetooth, paired and personal. **Sensors and controls by the hundred, living on batteries** — Zigbee. An IoT designer reads the job's five numbers — how much data, how far, how long on battery, how many devices, what budget — and the standard chooses itself.
