# Satellite networks — uplink, downlink, transponder

## Do it first

Shout a message up to a friend on a high roof, who shouts it down to someone far on the other side. Up, relay, down — you have just run this lesson's network.

## Sub lesson 1 — Nodes that reach anywhere

A **satellite network's nodes — satellites, earth stations, end-user terminals, telephones** — carry communication **from any point on earth to any other**. Like cellular networks it **divides the planet into cells**, but its special strength is **reach**: transmission to **any location however remote**, bringing quality communication to **undeveloped areas without huge ground infrastructure**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Uplink, transponder, downlink

The system's parts are **earth stations, the uplink, the downlink and the transponder** — the satellite's **combined receiver-transmitter** that **takes signals up and sends them back**.

## Sub lesson 3 — Ethiopia in space (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide keys Ethiopia-specific satellites the student book never names: **EDUSAT**, a satellite **dedicated to education**, can carry **e-learning to schools, colleges and non-formal institutions across remote areas**; and **ETRSS-1 is Ethiopia's first low-Earth-orbit satellite, an Earth-observation satellite**. A satellite's parts are keyed as the **communications system (antennas and transponders), the power system (solar panels) and the propulsion system (rockets)**, and satellite coverage is keyed as serving **Ethiopia's rural and pastoral communities best**. The guide also keys **GPS** as a satellite system giving **land and sea navigation and clock synchronisation**.
