# Lesson plan — Microwaves — aimed beams, dish and horn antennas

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Networks · **Lesson:** Microwaves — aimed beams, dish and horn antennas
**Place in the curriculum:** Title — Networks in the Computer Science and ICT ladder (TITLES.md); stocked from Ethiopia's Grade 10 book, Unit 2 — Computer Network. The ladder is the recommended order, never a forced one.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** **Microwaves** travel **line-of-sight** as **narrow, unidirectional beams** — antennas must be **aligned** — gathered by a **dish** or thrown by a **horn**.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Radio waves — microwaves are the aimed opposite of radio's all-directions spread.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or in steps — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Unidirectional beams that must be aligned** — narrow line-of-sight beams; aligned pairs do not interfere; unicast uses
2. **The parabolic dish — a funnel for waves** — the dish gathers wide-area waves into one focus point
3. **The horn — a scoop that throws and collects** — the horn throws parallel beams and collects down its stem
4. **Microwaves keyed (teacher guide — evidence)** — the guide's keyed range, uses and advantages

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Radio waves — broadcasting in all directions. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say how microwaves travel and what alignment buys.
2. Explain the dish's focusing and the horn's two directions of work.
3. Name three services microwaves' unicast character serves.
4. Give the guide's keyed advantages of microwaves.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil; a torch and a curved bowl if you have them.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
