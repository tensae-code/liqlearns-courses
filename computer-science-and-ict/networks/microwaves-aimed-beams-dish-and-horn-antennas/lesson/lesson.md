# Microwaves — aimed beams, dish and horn antennas

## Do it first

Shine a torch in a thin beam at one friend, not the whole room. Microwaves are the torch of the wireless family — narrow and aimed. What must the two ends do?

## Sub lesson 1 — Unidirectional beams that must be aligned

**Microwaves** travel **line-of-sight** as **narrow, unidirectional beams**, so **sending and receiving antennas must be aligned**. The advantage: **one aligned pair does not interfere with another**. Microwave links **sit high**, and their **unicast** (one-to-one) character serves **cellular phones, satellite networks and wireless LANs**.

## Sub lesson 2 — The parabolic dish — a funnel for waves

The **parabolic dish**'s curve **gathers waves from a wide area into one focus point**, like a funnel.

[GIF 14517 — parallel rays converging on a dish's focus]

## Sub lesson 3 — The horn — a scoop that throws and collects

The **horn** is a **scoop shape** that **throws outgoing waves out in narrow parallel beams** and **collects incoming ones down its stem**.

## Sub lesson 4 — Microwaves keyed (teacher guide — evidence)

The guide keys microwaves as **line-of-sight beams in the 1–300 GHz range, unidirectional**, suited to **one-to-one (unicast)** links — used in **mobile phones, satellite networks and wireless LANs** — with advantages of **larger bandwidth, more antenna gain, higher data rates, smaller antennas and low power use**.
