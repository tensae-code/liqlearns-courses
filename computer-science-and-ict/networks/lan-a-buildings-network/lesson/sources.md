**Acceptance count: taught in 12 countries (Ethiopia, Mozambique, Nepal, China, North Macedonia, Sudan, Fiji, Rwanda, Greece, Bangladesh, South Sudan, Pakistan).**

Book error taught corrected: the student book's text calls the LAN picture “Figure 2.3” when the picture is Figure 2.13. This lesson teaches the LAN from the pictured network itself and names the figure correctly here; the wrong in-text number is flagged, not taught.

The Grade 9 teacher guide's closing answer keys (guide placement item 27; Unit 2 keys at guide printed pp. 36–37) were used as the verified-answer check for this lesson's question files.

North Macedonia's own way (Year 1): five themes move from modern digital technology and online living into a full structured-programming course taught in C++ (with Scratch/C++/Java/Python/PHP/Lisp compared), then advanced Word work (styles, contents, templates) and spreadsheets up to pivot tables.

Mauritania's own way (Grade 9): The final secondary year is five practical blocks in 30-minute fiches — a full PowerPoint sequence, internet ethics, a first formal algorithms unit (variables, conditions, loops, writing and testing algorithms, no named programming language), simple networks, and email with attachments — each block closed by evaluation fiches.

Sudan's own way (Grade 6): the final primary book is built around real Sudanese systems — networks and cloud first, then a full security unit taught through named children's everyday internet dilemmas, and a worked example of the ipSchools school-management system (with a real Bakht Al-Ruda school results table) before closing with computers in banks and police work.

Fiji's own way (Years 7–8 Computer Education student book): A practical, exercise-driven primary book built around a class "students folder" of set files (work is saved as U<unit>-Ex<n>-<FirstName>); local Fijian names and schools throughout (e.g. Babasiga Secondary School), Internet searching is practised on the "Map of Fiji" with kids' search engines, and a full unit covers computers in Fijian society including Braille keyboards for blind learners. PARTIAL: the PDF as received has no cover, title page, contents or imprint — the PARTIAL label travels with every citation of this book.

Rwanda's own way (S1 ICT Student Book, KLB): competency-based units each opening with a Key Unit Competency and keywords; a full GIS/ArcGIS unit and a Scratch music-making unit sit inside the first secondary year, and ICT use is grounded in Rwandan daily life.

Greece's own way (Grade 8, Pliroforiki B' Gymnasiou, 2024): Grade 8 moves from bits/bytes and data rates into networks, then data analysis in spreadsheets and presentations, then modular Scratch programming (own blocks with parameters, realistic character motion), list data structures, a full AI unit training a neural network, and ICT's changes to the economy (e-commerce, Industry 4.0, new jobs, sustainability).

Bangladesh's own way (HSC ICT, Classes XI–XII and Alim): one compulsory ICT book for every HSC and Alim student in the country, saturated with Bangladesh itself: Bangabandhu Satellite-1 and its ground stations, bdjobs and chakri.com job portals, Daraz and Bikroy e-commerce, the Walton factory's automated production line, the National ID card used to teach primary keys and its database used to teach RDBMS at work, a full Bangla Unicode table and a worked example of putting Bangla text on a web page with meta charset, the national letter-grade scale (A+ from 80) as the else-if programme, "I Love Bangladesh" as the loop example text, an algorithm for reaching college by bicycle or rickshaw, and the Digital Bangladesh programme — National Data Centre, hi-tech parks, Startup Bangladesh and Bangladesh Bank's QR-based Cashless Bangladesh. C is the HSC programming language, taught with scanf/printf in Code::Blocks.

South Sudan's own way (Grade 10 S2 Student): Year 2 moves from single-computer skills to connected and analytical work — networks taught through intranet file-sharing and a full Microsoft Outlook unit (contacts, calendar, meetings, task lists), then a deep Excel unit that goes well beyond basics into lookup/financial/text functions, inventories, correlation and regression, what-if analysis with Solver, and workbook protection; social media is taught platform by platform with its ethical and legal issues.


Pakistan's own way (Grade 8 Computer Education 8): the Grade 8 book is the older National-Curriculum-2006 Punjab book (not SNC): it teaches networks through physical media and cutting-edge wireless (cellular, satellite, GPS, Bluetooth), a whole unit of hands-on antivirus management (definitions, updates, an AVG whole-computer scan), Excel 2007 taught through three full worked reports (monthly expenses, salaries, school results), and programming in GW-BASIC with line numbers — the pre-SNC Punjab way into code.
