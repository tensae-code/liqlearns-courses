**Acceptance count: taught in 10 countries (Ethiopia, Afghanistan, Iraq, Sudan, Egypt, Bhutan, Libya, Sri Lanka, Kuwait, Laos).**

Afghanistan's own way (Grade 11): grade 11 is the health-and-PowerPoint year — a full chapter on computer health and strain-relief exercises, then Word finished off (Format, Insert, Edit) before a five-lesson PowerPoint block and a first step onto the Web.

No book error touches this part.

Mauritania's own way (Grade 9): The final secondary year is five practical blocks in 30-minute fiches — a full PowerPoint sequence, internet ethics, a first formal algorithms unit (variables, conditions, loops, writing and testing algorithms, no named programming language), simple networks, and email with attachments — each block closed by evaluation fiches.

Iraq's own way (Grade 10): the preparatory stage turns practical and examinable — a whole unit on computer maintenance (preventive vs corrective, hardware and software, hard-disk management with defragmentation, ScanDisk, partitions and FAT32 vs NTFS), then PowerPoint and Microsoft Publisher taught tab by tab, looping control structures in Scratch, and a final unit pairing HTML web-page building with information security.

Sudan's own way (Grade 6): the final primary book is built around real Sudanese systems — networks and cloud first, then a full security unit taught through named children's everyday internet dilemmas, and a worked example of the ipSchools school-management system (with a real Bakht Al-Ruda school results table) before closing with computers in banks and police work.

Egypt's own way (Grade 5, Term 2 student book (Arabic)): Primary ICT is organised as themed units — "The Digital Citizen in the Internet World" then "My Digital Project" — with every lesson phrased as a journey or a how-to question.

Bhutan's own way (Class VII, Literacy with ICT student book): Social media is taught hands-on — learners create a Facebook account, post with audience settings, and follow netiquette and an Acceptable Use Policy; backup is taught to Google Drive, and the end-of-year experience is shared on Facebook.

Libya's own way (Grade 12 Humanities, Information Technology student book): the Humanities final year pairs the social/legal side of ICT (data security, e-government, computer law and software licences) with a long multimedia-and-PowerPoint practical; the closing projects are Libyan — a deck about Libyan dishes and a deck about Libya's archaeological cities and landmarks.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 12 Science for Technology, Tamil): the A/L technology-stream ICT volume teaches every office skill twice — once in Microsoft Office 2010 and once in LibreOffice (Writer/Calc/Impress 4.1) — and closes with creating a real Gmail account, mailbox folders, attachments and password safety; hardware goes down to cache levels L1/L2/L3, ROM types and file systems (FAT/NTFS/ext4).

Kuwait's own way (Grade 4 Digital Explorer Term 2, printed title — records name it 'Our Digital World'): the Digital World unit mixes identity (pride in Arabic language and identity online), cloud storage on the national platform (OneDrive inside Teams), and a three-lesson AI arc (data → training a machine → AI decisions) before the tool unit; PowerPoint is taught as a five-step 'Exciting Presentation' journey ending in presentations about Kuwait itself.

Laos's own way (Grade 10 ICT teacher guide): the guide quietly carries the year's only practical work as a supplementary appendix (ແນະນໍາເພີ່ມ, printed pp. 82–118) the student book does not contain — Google searching with Internet Explorer/Firefox, hand-coding a web page in Notepad (with w3schools named as the reference), a full PowerPoint 2007 course, and a DOS command-prompt practical. Laos's own way (Grade 12 ICT student book): the final year is a modern, applied course — problem-solving method, a long English-titled internet-safety sequence (Internet Safety, Email Tips for Scams and Spam, Smart Social Networking, Cyber harassment/Stalking/Addiction), data summaries and PivotTables for decision-making, copyright and Creative Commons licensing, a full PowerPoint course, and choosing technology for your future occupation.
