# Collaborative Presentations Online

## Do it first

Grade 9's collaboration unit reaches the presentation: no passing a file around by memory stick, but one deck living online, where the group's hands meet on the same slides.

## Sub lesson 1 — One deck, many hands

A **collaborative presentation** is built in an **online presentation environment** where the deck lives **once, shared**: every member opens the **same slides** from their own machine, **adds and edits** their part, and sees the others' work **appear as it happens** — the service keeps one current deck instead of five merging-pain copies. Greece teaches it inside its own collaboration service's unit (the +γραφίς document world), because the deck is a document like the unit's texts: shared, versioned by the service, and answerable by all.

In your mind's eye: one deck, many hands.

## Sub lesson 2 — Dividing the work — and presenting as one

Collaboration's craft is division with a seam: the group **assigns slides by member**, agrees the **shared style** (one master look, so twenty slides read as one deck), **comments** instead of overwriting where they disagree, and **rehearses the handovers** so the presented deck speaks in one voice although five hands built it. The unit's rule for all its collaborative documents holds here with slides: the tool shares the file; only the group's agreements share the work.

In your mind's eye: dividing the work — and presenting as one.
