## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **One deck, many hands** — A **collaborative presentation** is built in an **online presentation environment** where the deck lives **once, shared**: every member opens the **same slides** from their own machine, **adds and edits** their part, and sees the others' work **appear as it happens** — the service keeps one current deck instead of five merging-pain copies. Greece teaches it inside its own collaboration service's unit (the +γραφίς document world), because the deck is a document like the unit's texts: shared, versioned by the service, and answerable by all.
2. **Dividing the work — and presenting as one** — Collaboration's craft is division with a seam: the group **assigns slides by member**, agrees the **shared style** (one master look, so twenty slides read as one deck), **comments** instead of overwriting where they disagree, and **rehearses the handovers** so the presented deck speaks in one voice although five hands built it. The unit's rule for all its collaborative documents holds here with slides: the tool shares the file; only the group's agreements share the work.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
