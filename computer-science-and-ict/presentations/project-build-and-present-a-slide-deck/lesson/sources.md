**Acceptance count: taught in 7 countries (Ethiopia, Sudan, Cuba, Bhutan, Libya, Kuwait, Moldova).**

No book error touches this part.

Mauritania's own way (Grade 9): The final secondary year is five practical blocks in 30-minute fiches — a full PowerPoint sequence, internet ethics, a first formal algorithms unit (variables, conditions, loops, writing and testing algorithms, no named programming language), simple networks, and email with attachments — each block closed by evaluation fiches.

Sudan's own way (Grade 6): the final primary book is built around real Sudanese systems — networks and cloud first, then a full security unit taught through named children's everyday internet dilemmas, and a worked example of the ipSchools school-management system (with a real Bakht Al-Ruda school results table) before closing with computers in banks and police work.

Cuba's own way (Grade 6): Grade 6 adds the three productivity firsts in one year — digital presentations, Scratch animation with event/control blocks, and spreadsheets with formulas and charts — closing with a TIC chapter on searching and processing digital information; computer generations are taught inside Chapter 1.

Bhutan's own way (Class VII, Literacy with ICT student book): Social media is taught hands-on — learners create a Facebook account, post with audience settings, and follow netiquette and an Acceptable Use Policy; backup is taught to Google Drive, and the end-of-year experience is shared on Facebook.

Bhutan's own way (Class VIII, Literacy with ICT student book): The final primary/middle year is project-led — a full Audacity audio unit (record, edit, mix, share), information ethics taught through Copyright, Creative Commons and software piracy, and Scratch taught to game level with lists, custom blocks (functions) and logical operators.

Libya's own way (Grade 12 Humanities, Information Technology student book): the Humanities final year pairs the social/legal side of ICT (data security, e-government, computer law and software licences) with a long multimedia-and-PowerPoint practical; the closing projects are Libyan — a deck about Libyan dishes and a deck about Libya's archaeological cities and landmarks.

Kuwait's own way (Grade 4 Digital Explorer Term 2, printed title — records name it 'Our Digital World'): the Digital World unit mixes identity (pride in Arabic language and identity online), cloud storage on the national platform (OneDrive inside Teams), and a three-lesson AI arc (data → training a machine → AI decisions) before the tool unit; PowerPoint is taught as a five-step 'Exciting Presentation' journey ending in presentations about Kuwait itself.

Laos's own way (Grade 10 ICT teacher guide): the guide quietly carries the year's only practical work as a supplementary appendix (ແນະນໍາເພີ່ມ, printed pp. 82–118) the student book does not contain — Google searching with Internet Explorer/Firefox, hand-coding a web page in Notepad (with w3schools named as the reference), a full PowerPoint 2007 course, and a DOS command-prompt practical. (teacher guide — evidence)

Moldova's own way (Grade 7, Informatică): lower-secondary Informatica is a real textbook discipline, not computer-skills practice — number systems, text/image/sound encoding and data types in Pascal sit alongside GUI literacy, and two full chapters are given to behaviour, law and information culture in virtual spaces.
