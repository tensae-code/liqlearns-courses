# Linking with Hyperlinks

## Do it first

A presentation normally walks in a straight line. Laos's Grade 12 PowerPoint course breaks the line on purpose: hyperlinks that let a talk jump — to a detail slide, a document, a page on the web. This lesson builds the jumps.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a hyperlink on a slide does

A **hyperlink** on a slide is a **piece of text or a picture that, when clicked during the show, jumps** — to **another slide** in the same presentation, to **another file** on the computer, or to a **web page**. It turns the deck from a single road into a small network: the presenter follows the audience's question, not only the plan. The link is recognised the way web links are — coloured, underlined text, or a picture the presenter names as a door — and it works **only while the show is running**, not while editing.

In your mind's eye: what a hyperlink on a slide does.

## Sub lesson 2 — Making links — and keeping them honest

A link is made by **selecting the text or picture, giving the Insert Hyperlink command, and choosing the target** — place in this document, existing file, or web address. Laos teaches the making beside its discipline: a link is a **promise** — its words must say where it goes, its target must be **tested in the show before the audience arrives**, and a link to a file or the web is only as reliable as that file's place and that connection. A broken promise mid-talk costs more than no link at all, so the book's presenters test every jump twice.

In your mind's eye: making links — and keeping them honest.
