# Lesson plan — Animating slide content — entrance, emphasis, the Animation Pane

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Presentations · **Lesson:** Animating slide content — entrance, emphasis, the Animation Pane
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Application Software, Grade 10 depth, in the Presentations title of the Computer Science and ICT ladder (TITLES.md). The ladder is the recommended order, never a forced one.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** animations reveal a slide's items one at a time — picked on the Animations tab for selected bullets, then ordered and timed in the Animation Pane: entrance first, emphasis second, the picture following.
**This lesson's own level:** it is a doing-lesson. It stands on Slide transitions; transitions move between slides, this lesson moves the items inside one slide.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or in steps — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Animating the bullets — the worked route** — pass mark: animate selected bullets with a previewed, picked effect (Fade).
2. **The Animation Pane — ordering the sequence** — pass mark: order entrance, emphasis and picture in the Animation Pane and preview the sequence.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Slide transitions — and using one throughout. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what an animation does that a transition does not.
2. Apply an animation to selected bullets, previewing by hover and picking from the full list.
3. Order a sequence in the Animation Pane: entrance, emphasis, picture.
4. Preview a sequence and state the restraint rule.

## What you need
A computer with PowerPoint and a bulleted slide with a title and a picture.

## If the learner is stuck
- The whole slide appears at once → the animation was applied to the slide, not to the selected bullets; select the bullet points first, then pick the effect.
- Effects run in the wrong order → open the Animation Pane and move the title's entrance before the list with the timing controls.
