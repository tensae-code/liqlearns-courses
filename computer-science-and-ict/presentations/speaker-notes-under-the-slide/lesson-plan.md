# Lesson plan — Speaker Notes Under the Slide

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Presentations · **Lesson:** Speaker Notes Under the Slide
**Place in the curriculum:** ICT expansion — Afghanistan (2026-11-14) — in the title Presentations on the ICT course ladder (TITLES.md). Built new from Afghanistan's *Computer* Grade 12, because no existing lesson in the course covers this thing as its own lesson.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Speaker notes are the presenter's private text kept under each slide: the audience sees the slide, the presenter sees the slide plus the notes — the words to say that do not belong on the screen.
**This lesson's own level:** It stands on the earlier lessons of its title and on the Afghanistan book's own order — it follows the book's sections in sequence, so its neighbours in the book are its natural companions. It stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **What speaker notes are** — Under every PowerPoint slide is a **notes area** — a place for text the **audience never sees on the show screen**. In the Normal view it sits below the slide, waiting: click and type what you, the speaker, need — the fuller explanation, the figures you must quote exactly, the story that goes with this picture, the reminder to ask a question here. Afghanistan's Grade 12 book teaches notes as part of finishing the PowerPoint course for a reason: a slide is a poster, not a script. The words the audience reads and the words the speaker says are two different texts, and notes are where the second text lives.
2. **Using notes while presenting** — When the show runs, the audience sees the clean slide; the presenter can see the notes on their own screen or on a printed **notes page** — one slide small at the top, its notes beneath. Writing good notes is its own skill: write **what you will say, in the order you will say it**, short enough to glance at, complete enough that a lost place is one look away from being found. Notes also save a presentation when someone else must give it: slides plus notes hand over not just the pictures but the talk. The lesson's test: cover the slides and read only the notes — if the presentation still makes sense, the notes are doing their job.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.
