# Video script — Speaker Notes Under the Slide

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Under every PowerPoint slide is a **notes area** — a place for text the **audience never sees on the show screen**.
In the Normal view it sits below the slide, waiting: click and type what you, the speaker, need — the fuller explanation, the figures you must quote exactly, the story that goes with this picture, the reminder to ask a question here.
Afghanistan's Grade 12 book teaches notes as part of finishing the PowerPoint course for a reason: a slide is a poster, not a script.
The words the audience reads and the words the speaker says are two different texts, and notes are where the second text lives.
When the show runs, the audience sees the clean slide; the presenter can see the notes on their own screen or on a printed **notes page** — one slide small at the top, its notes beneath.
Writing good notes is its own skill: write **what you will say, in the order you will say it**, short enough to glance at, complete enough that a lost place is one look away from being found.
Notes also save a presentation when someone else must give it: slides plus notes hand over not just the pictures but the talk.
The lesson's test: cover the slides and read only the notes — if the presentation still makes sense, the notes are doing their job.
