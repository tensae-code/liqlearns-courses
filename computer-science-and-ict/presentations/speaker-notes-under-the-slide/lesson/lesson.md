# Speaker Notes Under the Slide

## Do it first

The slide shows six words; the presenter speaks for two minutes about it. The difference is written underneath. In this lesson you learn speaker notes in PowerPoint.

## Sub lesson 1 — What speaker notes are

Under every PowerPoint slide is a **notes area** — a place for text the **audience never sees on the show screen**. In the Normal view it sits below the slide, waiting: click and type what you, the speaker, need — the fuller explanation, the figures you must quote exactly, the story that goes with this picture, the reminder to ask a question here. Afghanistan's Grade 12 book teaches notes as part of finishing the PowerPoint course for a reason: a slide is a poster, not a script. The words the audience reads and the words the speaker says are two different texts, and notes are where the second text lives.

[Picture — PowerPoint's Normal view — a slide with its notes area underneath, holding the speaker's text]

## Sub lesson 2 — Using notes while presenting

When the show runs, the audience sees the clean slide; the presenter can see the notes on their own screen or on a printed **notes page** — one slide small at the top, its notes beneath. Writing good notes is its own skill: write **what you will say, in the order you will say it**, short enough to glance at, complete enough that a lost place is one look away from being found. Notes also save a presentation when someone else must give it: slides plus notes hand over not just the pictures but the talk. The lesson's test: cover the slides and read only the notes — if the presentation still makes sense, the notes are doing their job.

[Picture — a printed notes page — the slide small at the top, the speaker's notes below it]

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: Speaker notes are the presenter's private text kept under each slide: the audience sees the slide, the presenter sees the slide plus the notes — the words to say that do not belong on the screen. Then let your companion ask you one question about it — and answer from the lesson, not from memory of the book's exact words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Show it, don't just say it: The presenter's private text kept under each slide. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
2. Show it, don't just say it: Below the slide. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
3. Show it, don't just say it: A notes page — slide small at the top, its notes beneath. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
4. Show it, don't just say it: Not just the pictures, but the talk. Do this with a real example of your own and write or say how you know it is right.
