# PowerPoint — making a slideshow; inserting slides

## Do it first

You must present a tale to your class. One idea at a time, shown big, with words and pictures together — that is a slideshow. The program that makes it is this lesson's subject. Open it and look: one slide is waiting.

## Sub lesson 1 — What PowerPoint is

**PowerPoint** creates a **slideshow addressing a topic** — sometimes simply called a **presentation**, or a **slide**. It has three major functions:

1. An **editor** for inserting and formatting text.
2. A method for **inserting and manipulating graphic images**.
3. A **slide-show system** to display the content.

Words, pictures, showing: every presentation is built from those three.

## Sub lesson 2 — Inserting slides

A presentation **begins with one slide**. One slide is rarely enough — so, **as many slides as needed can be inserted**, and each new slide can be taken from a **variety of layouts**: a layout decides how the slide's text and pictures are arranged. Insert slides until the topic is fully addressed, one idea per slide.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion PowerPoint's three functions, and how a one-slide presentation grows.
