# Lesson plan — Adding content to a slide

**Course:** Computer Science and ICT · **Title:** Presentations · **Lesson:** Adding content to a slide
**Place in the curriculum:** Title — Presentations in the Computer Science and ICT ladder (TITLES.md); stocked from Ethiopia's Grade 9 book, Unit 3 — Application Software. The ladder is the recommended order, never a forced one.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** text is the default content — click and type; the other content comes from the six icons in the content placeholder: Table, Chart, SmartArt, Pictures, Online Pictures, Video.
**This lesson's own level:** it is a doing-lesson. It stands on PowerPoint bullets — size, colour, shape.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or in steps — a GIF here is 2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: PowerPoint bullets — size, colour, shape. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Add text the default way: click in the placeholder and type the bulleted list.
2. Name the six standard graphical elements: Table, Chart, SmartArt, Pictures, Online Pictures, Video.
3. Add content by clicking an icon in the content placeholder — each icon opens its dialog or the Drawing Tools contextual tab.
4. Find the same options on the Insert tab.
## What you need
A computer with PowerPoint; a slide with a content placeholder.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner attempts the thing before any explanation (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the parts below, in order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE [Picture 13720]: a picture of a content placeholder with its six icons — Table, Chart, SmartArt, Pictures, Online Pictures, Video — each labelled — the student studies it before doing the steps on a real computer.
   - ADD AUDIO: a voice reading the lesson's key words and steps aloud — the student listens and says, or does, each one after the voice.
3. **Say it to your companion** — explain the lesson back in your own words (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the lesson retold in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, true-or-false, fill-in-the-blank, and the type files this lesson holds — served at the learner's level (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the doing block at the end of lesson/lesson.md.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the order of the steps → go back to the numbered steps in lesson/lesson.md and do them one at a time, saying each step aloud before doing it; the steps are never reordered.
- Works from memory instead of the screen → every result in this lesson can be checked on screen (a formula in its bar, a view by its name); check, do not guess.
