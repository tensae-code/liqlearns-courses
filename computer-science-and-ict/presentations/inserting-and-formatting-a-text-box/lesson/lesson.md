# Inserting and Formatting a Text Box

## Do it first

Slides come with places for text — but a designer places their own. Kuwait's second 'Exciting Presentation' lesson gives the slide its free text: the text box, drawn where the maker wants, and dressed as the maker chooses. This lesson is the box and its dressing.

## Sub lesson 1 — Inserting a text box — text where you put it

A **text box** is a box the maker **inserts and draws** on the slide — at the chosen place, of the chosen size — and types into: a caption under a picture, a label on a diagram, a title where no title placeholder stands. Unlike the slide's built-in placeholders, the text box belongs entirely to its maker: it goes where the design needs words, and it can be moved, resized and removed as one object.

## Sub lesson 2 — Formatting the box and its text — and WordArt

The box is formatted as an **object** (its fill, its outline, its position) and its **text is formatted inside it** (the font, size, colour and alignment of the words) — two dressings, kept distinct. The lesson's flourish is **WordArt**: text given a ready **style** — shaped, coloured and shadowed display lettering — for the words that must be seen before they are read. The judgement taught with the tool: WordArt for the few display words; plain, readable text for everything the audience must actually read.
