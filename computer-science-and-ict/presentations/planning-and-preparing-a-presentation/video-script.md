# Video script — Planning and Preparing a Presentation

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

A presentation is prepared in **stages**. First the **aim**: what must the listeners leave able to do or ready to decide? Then the **topic** is chosen and bounded inside the aim — the Grade 8 project carries the air-pollution leaflet's subject to the screen, its topic cut to what a talk can hold. Then **material is gathered**: facts, pictures, charts — more than the talk needs, so the plan can choose. Aim before topic, topic before material: each stage narrows the next, and a presentation that skips the narrowing arrives at the slides with everything and says nothing.
The **slide plan** assigns the material: one idea per slide, in the talk's order, each slide sketched — title, its picture or chart, its few words — on paper before the program opens. Last comes **rehearsal**: the talk spoken aloud, timed, its stumbles repaired in the plan and not on the day; the presenter learns where the audience will ask, because the rehearsal's small audience already did. Mongolia's five stages close with the book's standing judgement: a presentation is a prepared act, not a file — the file is merely its evidence.
