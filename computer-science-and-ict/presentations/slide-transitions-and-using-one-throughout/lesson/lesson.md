# Slide transitions — and using one throughout

## Do it first

Your deck jumps harshly from slide to slide. Give the move a gentle effect — then run the show and feel the difference.

## Sub lesson 1 — Adding a transition — in order

PowerPoint organises information into **slides** that may hold **text, graphics, sound and movies**. **Transitions are the animated effects between slides** — as opposed to **animations, which act on items within a slide**.

In the book's order, in **Normal view** with the **first slide selected**:

1. **Transitions tab → choose an effect** — a **star appears beside the slide miniature**.
2. **Effect Options** sets its **direction**.
3. Lengthen **Duration** to slow it.
4. The **Sound dropdown** can add a sound, such as a **camera shutter**.
5. **Advance Slide** can move on **automatically after set seconds**.
6. Repeat per slide — then **Ctrl+S** to save, **F5** to run, **Esc** to stop.

## Sub lesson 2 — The craft rule — one transition throughout

Mixed transitions **distract and harm accessibility**. The book's craft rule:

1. Remove all transitions: **None → Apply to All**.
2. **Choose one transition**.
3. **Apply to All**.

One calm move, the same between every pair of slides, lets the audience watch the content instead of the joins.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the difference between a transition and an animation, the setting order, and the craft rule with its reason.

## Do It Yourself

1. Add a transition to a first slide; set its direction and lengthen its Duration.
2. Add a camera-shutter sound, then set Advance Slide to move on automatically.
3. Run the show with F5 and stop it with Esc.
4. Remove all transitions (None → Apply to All), choose one, and Apply to All.
