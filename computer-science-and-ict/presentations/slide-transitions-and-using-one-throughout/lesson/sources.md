**Acceptance count: taught in 10 countries (Ethiopia, Iraq, Sudan, Bhutan, Libya, Laos, Greece, Moldova, Vietnam, Kazakhstan).**

Mauritania's own way (Grade 9): The final secondary year is five practical blocks in 30-minute fiches — a full PowerPoint sequence, internet ethics, a first formal algorithms unit (variables, conditions, loops, writing and testing algorithms, no named programming language), simple networks, and email with attachments — each block closed by evaluation fiches.

Iraq's own way (Grade 10): the preparatory stage turns practical and examinable — a whole unit on computer maintenance (preventive vs corrective, hardware and software, hard-disk management with defragmentation, ScanDisk, partitions and FAT32 vs NTFS), then PowerPoint and Microsoft Publisher taught tab by tab, looping control structures in Scratch, and a final unit pairing HTML web-page building with information security.

Sudan's own way (Grade 6): the final primary book is built around real Sudanese systems — networks and cloud first, then a full security unit taught through named children's everyday internet dilemmas, and a worked example of the ipSchools school-management system (with a real Bakht Al-Ruda school results table) before closing with computers in banks and police work.

Bhutan's own way (Class VIII, Literacy with ICT student book): The final primary/middle year is project-led — a full Audacity audio unit (record, edit, mix, share), information ethics taught through Copyright, Creative Commons and software piracy, and Scratch taught to game level with lists, custom blocks (functions) and logical operators.

Libya's own way (Grade 12 Humanities, Information Technology student book): the Humanities final year pairs the social/legal side of ICT (data security, e-government, computer law and software licences) with a long multimedia-and-PowerPoint practical; the closing projects are Libyan — a deck about Libyan dishes and a deck about Libya's archaeological cities and landmarks.

Laos's own way (Grade 12 ICT student book): the final year is a modern, applied course — problem-solving method, a long English-titled internet-safety sequence (Internet Safety, Email Tips for Scams and Spam, Smart Social Networking, Cyber harassment/Stalking/Addiction), data summaries and PivotTables for decision-making, copyright and Creative Commons licensing, a full PowerPoint course, and choosing technology for your future occupation.

Greece's own way (Grade 8, Pliroforiki B' Gymnasiou, 2024): Grade 8 moves from bits/bytes and data rates into networks, then data analysis in spreadsheets and presentations, then modular Scratch programming (own blocks with parameters, realistic character motion), list data structures, a full AI unit training a neural network, and ICT's changes to the economy (e-commerce, Industry 4.0, new jobs, sustainability).

Moldova's own way (Grade 7, Informatică): lower-secondary Informatica is a real textbook discipline, not computer-skills practice — number systems, text/image/sound encoding and data types in Pascal sit alongside GUI literacy, and two full chapters are given to behaviour, law and information culture in virtual spaces.

Armenia's own way (Armenia Grade 8 G8 teacher): Matches Book 14 exactly: its mapping table ties the student book's 19 §s to 14 standard themes (8-1 to 8-14), each theme with outcomes, competencies and practical work; the guide adds a thematic plan, the book's questions/tasks with answers, and slide-presentation assessment criteria.

Vietnam's own way (Grade 4 Tin học 4): Grade 4 is where the visual programming language first appears (its preface announces it), software legality is taught as a child's rule — only use software when you have permission — and the applied theme offers alternative Bài 12A (multimedia tools) or 12B (typing-trainer software).

South Sudan's own way (Grade 9 S1 Teacher): The guide teaches the same four units as the student book through co-operative group investigation, with answer keys and assessment guidelines; distinctive content includes using the internet to promote South Sudanese culture and creating user accounts from the Windows command line (net user). (teacher guide — evidence)


Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G3 Digital Literacy): Grade 3 is built around making a whole Scratch game (scenario, scenes, characters, costumes) and an EV3 robotics project, with Word and PowerPoint taught as the project's tools; "Check yourself! Creative tasks" lessons close each section (printed pp. 29, 59, 95, 126 — assessment pages, not placed).

Kazakhstan's own way (Kopeeva G3 ICT): the older Grade 3 "ICT" subject (not Digital Literacy): no programming or robotics at all — the year is computer basics, WordPad-style text work, sound recording, a long Paint strand (tools, shapes, repeating patterns, palette, printing, mixed text-and-picture documents), Internet safety and search, and a full presentation strand ending with sound inside slides.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kopeeva G3 «Ақпараттық-коммуникациялық технологиялар»): Grade 3 ICT is a tools year organised by cross-cutting themes («Менің мектебім», «Сәулет», «Өнер»…): text, then sound, then computer pictures in a graphics editor, then a mixed document that combines them, then internet safety/search and presentations — with a practicum closing almost every section.

Kazakhstan's own way (Kadirkulov G3 «Цифровая грамотность»): the Almatykitap Grade 3 digital literacy book is built as projects: a Scratch game built scene by scene, then a robotics project that oddly starts in Word (the project document is written and illustrated first) before the robot arm moves, and a cleaner robot is built; presentations and a photo collection close the year.
