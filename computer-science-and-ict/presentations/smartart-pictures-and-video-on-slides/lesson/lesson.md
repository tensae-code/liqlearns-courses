# SmartArt, pictures and video on slides

## Do it first

A school's staff as a list of names is hard to hold in mind. Drawn as a tree — director at the top, teachers branching below — it is understood in seconds. That drawing-with-meaning is SmartArt. And when words pile up, a picture can carry them instead.

## SmartArt

**SmartArt graphics are shapes showing relationships between things or people** — an **organisation chart** or a **timeline**. They are formatted in **SmartArt Tools**.

## Pictures — and where the extra words go

Pictures are advised **instead of too much text** — put the extra text in the **Notes section** and speak from it while the audience looks at the picture.

## Online pictures

**Online Pictures** (which replaced Clip Art) open a **search box**: **type a word or phrase**, or **browse categories via Bing Image Search**.

## Video — embedded or linked

Video can be **embedded in the file**, or **linked** — to a **local file** or a **site such as YouTube** — **to limit file size**. All video and audio options sit on **Insert, in the Media group**.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: SmartArt draws relationships — organisation charts, timelines; pictures carry what words would overload, with the extra words kept in Notes; video is embedded or linked. Use the lesson's own words for the key names.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do the lesson's central doing again from the start, without looking — then check each step against the lesson.
2. Teach the doing to someone else, steps in order, while they follow on a real computer or phone.
3. Write the key steps from memory, numbered, in order.
