# Printing Handouts of Your Slides

## Do it first

Slides are made to be shown — but the audience goes home with paper. Fiji's Years 7–8 book closes its presentation unit by printing the handouts: the small copies of your slides that listeners keep, write on and remember you by. This lesson teaches printing them well.

## Sub lesson 1 — Handouts — slides on paper, several to a page

A **handout** is the printed form of a presentation: the slides set **several to a page** — one, two, three, four, six or nine — so a whole talk fits on a sheet or two. The **three-slides-per-page** layout is the classroom favourite because it prints **lines beside each slide** for the listener's own notes. Fiji teaches handouts as their own step because a handout is designed, not just printed: the layout is chosen for the audience's job — following, noting, keeping.

## Sub lesson 2 — Printing the handout — settings and preview

The working order: open **Print**, choose **handouts** instead of full-page slides, pick the **number of slides per page** and their order (across, then down — or down, then across), check the **preview** — are the slides big enough to read, is the header and footer right — and only then print. Printing one test page before a class set is the book's economy: paper and ink are spent on the preview's evidence, not on a pile of mistakes.
