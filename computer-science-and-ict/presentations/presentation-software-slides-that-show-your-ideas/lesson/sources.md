**Acceptance count: taught in 14 countries (Ethiopia, Yemen, Sudan, Cuba, Egypt, Fiji, Bhutan, Mongolia, Sri Lanka, Rwanda, Greece, Moldova, Bangladesh, Jordan).**

Yemen's own way (Grade 11): the book pivots from operating the machine to understanding information itself — data kinds, number systems and coding get full units before the applications (presentations, Excel) and a networks/Internet finale; every unit closes with a formal تقويم (assessment).

No book error touches this part.

Mauritania's own way (Grade 9): The final secondary year is five practical blocks in 30-minute fiches — a full PowerPoint sequence, internet ethics, a first formal algorithms unit (variables, conditions, loops, writing and testing algorithms, no named programming language), simple networks, and email with attachments — each block closed by evaluation fiches.

Papua New Guinea's own way (Grade 9): one TIA teacher guide carries two ICT subjects side by side — Communication Technology (networks, ergonomics, safety, societal impact) and Computer Technology (architecture, software) — organised by content standards and numbered benchmarks (9.4.x / 9.5.x), with the design brief used as the problem-solving method for both a product and a simple network, and typing assessed at 20 wpm with 80% accuracy. (teacher guide — evidence, PARTIAL)

Sudan's own way (Grade 6): the final primary book is built around real Sudanese systems — networks and cloud first, then a full security unit taught through named children's everyday internet dilemmas, and a worked example of the ipSchools school-management system (with a real Bakht Al-Ruda school results table) before closing with computers in banks and police work.

Cuba's own way (Grade 6): Grade 6 adds the three productivity firsts in one year — digital presentations, Scratch animation with event/control blocks, and spreadsheets with formulas and charts — closing with a TIC chapter on searching and processing digital information; computer generations are taught inside Chapter 1.

Cuba's own way (Grade 8): Taught entirely in free software — LibreOffice Impress for presentations and LibreOffice Calc for spreadsheets; web work means designing and editing HTML pages with a plain text editor, and Scratch continues with arithmetic and logic operator blocks.

Egypt's own way (Grade 5, Term 2 student book (Arabic)): Primary ICT is organised as themed units — "The Digital Citizen in the Internet World" then "My Digital Project" — with every lesson phrased as a journey or a how-to question.

Fiji's own way (Years 7–8 Computer Education student book): A practical, exercise-driven primary book built around a class "students folder" of set files (work is saved as U<unit>-Ex<n>-<FirstName>); local Fijian names and schools throughout (e.g. Babasiga Secondary School), Internet searching is practised on the "Map of Fiji" with kids' search engines, and a full unit covers computers in Fijian society including Braille keyboards for blind learners. PARTIAL: the PDF as received has no cover, title page, contents or imprint — the PARTIAL label travels with every citation of this book.

Fiji's own way (Year 11 Computer Education student book): The first locally written text of its kind (the book's Preface): written by a panel of Computer Studies officers and University of the South Pacific lecturers for the new Year 11 Computer Education syllabus trialled in 2014; each chapter uses "Discovery Learning" prompts, and the database chapter is a full Access 2007 practical building a Student database in a "Lab Chapter 3" folder.

Bhutan's own way (Class VII, Literacy with ICT student book): Social media is taught hands-on — learners create a Facebook account, post with audience settings, and follow netiquette and an Acceptable Use Policy; backup is taught to Google Drive, and the end-of-year experience is shared on Facebook.

Mongolia's own way (Grade 7): the whole applications half is one long project — research and produce a printed booklet "Усаа хайрлая" (Cherish the Water) in Word, a Paint.NET layered picture, and a class PowerPoint; image work uses the free Paint.NET (downloaded from getpaint.net) rather than MS Paint; the e-communication chapter teaches Yahoo mail and Yahoo Messenger, Mongolian history of the örtöö relay post and the @ sign, and makes correct Latin transliteration of Mongolian Cyrillic (standard MNS 5217:2003) an ethics topic in its own right.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 7 Reading Book): the CPU is taught through a hotel-kitchen/chef analogy; programme development (decomposition, flowcharts, variables, bugs) precedes presentation software; HTML page-building is already in Grade 7.

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 7 Workbook): a companion workbook mirroring the Reading Book chapter-for-chapter (CPU, OS, security, word processing, program development, presentations, internet).

Sri Lanka's own way (Grade 12 Science for Technology, Tamil): the A/L technology-stream ICT volume teaches every office skill twice — once in Microsoft Office 2010 and once in LibreOffice (Writer/Calc/Impress 4.1) — and closes with creating a real Gmail account, mailbox folders, attachments and password safety; hardware goes down to cache levels L1/L2/L3, ROM types and file systems (FAT/NTFS/ext4).

Rwanda's own way (S3 ICT Learner's Book, Fountain): the same ten units as the S3 guide taught as long screenshot-by-screenshot practicals rooted in Rwandan school life (a GS Bumbogo parents'-meeting mail merge, an administration map of Rwanda built in ArcGIS, an environmental-protection presentation, 'Rwanda Nziza' hyperlink decks), closing with a complete Scratch platform game, Hard Journey.

Rwanda's own way (S3 ICT Teacher Guide, Fountain): the S3 guide (a different publisher from the KLB S1/S2 set) organises the year into four study areas — application software, GIS/spreadsheets/presentations, graphics and multimedia, networks and programming — ending in platform game programming in Scratch. (teacher guide — evidence)

Laos's own way (Grade 7 ICT teacher guide): uniquely, this guide — not the student book — carries the real software syllabus: separate activities for DOS, Windows/Linux, Unix/Mac, translators, BASIC and C, Logo, Java, and one activity per application family — a teacher-only expanded software syllabus. (teacher guide — evidence)

Greece's own way (Grade 8, Pliroforiki B' Gymnasiou, 2024): Grade 8 moves from bits/bytes and data rates into networks, then data analysis in spreadsheets and presentations, then modular Scratch programming (own blocks with parameters, realistic character motion), list data structures, a full AI unit training a neural network, and ICT's changes to the economy (e-commerce, Industry 4.0, new jobs, sustainability).

Moldova's own way (Grade 7, Informatică): lower-secondary Informatica is a real textbook discipline, not computer-skills practice — number systems, text/image/sound encoding and data types in Pascal sit alongside GUI literacy, and two full chapters are given to behaviour, law and information culture in virtual spaces.

Bangladesh's own way (Grade 7, Digital Technology): the Grade 7 book continues the Learning-Experience project format — intellectual property, virtual identity, cybercrime, robotics-through-pseudocode and e-services are each learned by doing a class project with the recurring robot character Nano. The NCTB Digital Technology line runs in parallel English and Bangla editions; placements are cited from the English Version with the Bangla Version parallel spot-checked.

Jordan's own way (Jordan Grade 2 G2 Workbook): At Grade 2 the digital tool widens from Paint to Office: every block's learning product is a PowerPoint presentation, Word is used for written work, and keyboard finger placement and saving-after-every-edit are taught as habits.
