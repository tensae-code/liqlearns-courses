# Short Answer — Literature, Background and Culture

(Every item is marked with the sub lesson it belongs to: (Sub lesson N). The gate pool for a sub lesson is all of that sub lesson's questions — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the whole set is the pool.)

1. Why must a work not be read as the writer's diary? (Sub lesson 1)
   — Because background shapes a work without exhausting it: the writer invents, combines and argues — the work is more than the life it came from.
2. Name three places a work's culture shows itself. (Sub lesson 2)
   — Any three of: food, festivals, speech rhythms, proverbs, landscape (sea/desert), rules of respect.
3. What three comparisons does this lesson practise? (Sub lesson 3)
   — Works by the same writer; studied works against other literary works; works against other media forms (film, song).
4. What makes a life connection a real literary response and not just a feeling? (Sub lesson 3)
   — Precision: the connection is stated with evidence — the page in one hand (what the work says) and the reader's life in the other (where it touches).
