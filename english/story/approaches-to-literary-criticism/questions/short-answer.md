# Short Answer — Approaches to Literary Criticism

(Every item is marked with the sub lesson it belongs to: (Sub lesson N). The gate pool for a sub lesson is all of that sub lesson's questions — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the whole set is the pool.)

1. What does a lens decide, before any reading begins? (Sub lesson 1)
   — What counts as evidence — which features of the work the reading will attend to and build on.

2. Give a formalist and a moralist sentence about the same story. (Sub lesson 1)
   — E.g. formalist: 'The story withholds Keesh's secret until the spies report, so the reveal lands as proof.' Moralist: 'The story praises fairness and humility rewarded, and mocks proud power.'

3. Apply the Marxist lens to the giant grain tale in two sentences. (Sub lesson 2)
   — The grandfather's world had no buying and selling of grain — men lived by their own labour. The tale blames the present order, where men live on others' labour, for the shrunken grain: the struggle is over work and its fruits.

4. Does using the Marxist lens require agreeing with it? (Sub lesson 2)
   — No — a lens is a discipline of attention: you find what it looks for in the text and report it; agreement is a separate, later judgement.
