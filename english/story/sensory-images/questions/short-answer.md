# Short Answer — Sensory Images (Imagery)

(Every item is marked with the sub lesson it belongs to: (Sub lesson N). The gate pool for a sub lesson is all of that sub lesson's questions — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the whole set is the pool.)

1. Why is "the soup was delicious" weaker than "the soup steamed, sour and hot, against his tongue"? (Sub lesson 1)
   — The first judges without showing; the second lands on senses — sight, smell, taste, touch — so the reader senses the soup instead of trusting the writer's word.

2. Name the five senses imagery can speak to. (Sub lesson 1)
   — Sight, hearing, smell, taste, and touch (feeling).

3. How do images help compare two settings in a story? (Sub lesson 2)
   — Each setting carries its own set of images; laying the two sets side by side shows how the places differ in what a person would sense there.

4. What is the point of the "Fellow Feeling" activity? (Sub lesson 2)
   — To use a story's sensory images to stand where the characters stand and feel what they feel — entering the story through the senses.
