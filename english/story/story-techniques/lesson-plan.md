# Lesson plan — Story techniques

**Course:** English · **Title:** Story · **Lesson:** Story techniques
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 41, "Stories and Poems in
Detail" — the first lesson of the level.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the story's CRAFT —
the moves a writer makes on top of the basic shape:
flashback and foreshadowing (playing with time), suspense
(playing with the reader's worry), the mystery story's
clue-craft, characterisation by STEAL, and the coda and
the choice of endings.
**This lesson's own level:** The story taught the furniture
(people, place, happenings); this lesson assumes it and
teaches only the craft — the basics are not retaught here.
**What the learner can do at the end:** plant a flashback
and a foreshadowing hint, build suspense, lay a mystery's
clues and red herring, show a character by STEAL, and close
a story with a chosen ending and a coda.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The story (the furniture this craft
  decorates). Recommended, never forced — every move here
  is defined as it arrives.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a story road with a loop arrow
   jumping BACK along it (flashback) and a faint sign far
   ahead showing through the mist (foreshadowing).** What
   the student does: the student traces both moves with a
   finger and says which way each one points in time.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of a detective's board: three real
   clue cards pinned in a row, one red card pinned to the
   side.** What the student does: the student says which
   cards the ending must answer, and which one is the trick.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first**: one dull telling, one crafted telling —
   what did the writer DO?
2. **Playing with time** (Ghana Basic 9: backstory,
   flashback, flash forward, foreshadowing).
3. **Suspense and its neighbours** (South Sudan Primary 8's
   named styles: suspense, coincidence, flashback, stories
   told through letters).
4. **The mystery built** (Egypt's Tip Box: mystery,
   detective, 2–3 real clues, 1 red herring, a solution
   that fits the clues).
5. **Characterisation by STEAL** (Indonesia Grade 11:
   Speech, Thoughts, Effect, Actions, Looks) beside Ghana's
   four channels.
6. **Endings and the coda** (Indonesia's four-stage frame;
   the ending chosen, not stumbled into).
7. **Do it yourself**: hands-on mystery scene with one
   flashback, one hint, one STEAL portrait.

## If the learner is stuck
- Flashback losing the reader: Ghana files it among the
  time moves — signal the jump (a time word, a memory
  trigger) and signal the return.
- Mystery ending feels cheated: Egypt's law — the answer
  must FIT the clues; count the real clues back.
- Next in this level: Poetry writing.
