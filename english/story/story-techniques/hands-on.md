# Hands-on — Story techniques

A real writing task — one crafted scene, every move used once.

## The scene (frame; your story inside)
Invent: a character who has lost something on the day of a
festival. Write the scene in this order:
1. **Open in suspense** — the character discovers the loss;
   something (a bell, a boat, a closing gate) is counting
   the time down. Three or four lines, no answer yet.
2. **One flashback** — a sight or smell jumps the story back
   to the day the thing was given. Signal the jump ("Two
   summers ago…") and the return.
3. **One foreshadowing hint planted earlier** — go back to
   part 1 and slip in one small strange detail (a loose
   pocket, a stranger watching) that part 4 will use.
4. **The mystery solved fairly** — the character follows
   two real clues; one tempting wrong idea (your red
   herring) is met and set aside; the ending FITS the clues.
5. **A STEAL line-check** — somewhere in the scene, the
   character must be shown by Speech, by a Thought, by
   their Effect on someone, by an Action, by their Looks.
   One line each is enough.
6. **Coda** — two closing lines after the ending: how life
   went on, or what the day meant.

## Check
- Can a reader point at the flashback's signal and return?
- Does the solution use BOTH real clues — and neither the
  red herring nor luck?
- Count the STEAL windows: five, or rewrite the missing one in.
