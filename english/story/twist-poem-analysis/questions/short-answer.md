# Short Answer — TWIST Poem Analysis

(Every item is marked with the sub lesson it belongs to: (Sub lesson N). The gate pool for a sub lesson is all of that sub lesson's questions — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the whole set is the pool.)

1. Why does TWIST begin with tone and word choice? (Sub lesson 1)
   — Because they are closest to the words themselves: every later claim (imagery, style, theme) must be earned by evidence the first passes gather from the lines.

2. Turn the topic 'autumn' into a theme for "Song of Autumn". (Sub lesson 2)
   — E.g. 'Light and warmth are brief and loss is certain, so love must shelter us while the light lasts' — a truth the poem argues, not a topic it mentions.

3. What is the difference between a topic and a theme? (Sub lesson 2)
   — A topic is what the poem is about in one word (autumn, war, love); a theme is the truth the poem argues about it, in a full statement.

4. A learner writes: 'The poem is sad.' Improve it as TWIST analysis. (Sub lesson 1)
   — Name the tone precisely (dread mixed with tenderness), then stand it on quoted word choice and imagery — the scaffold and coffin-nail images against the hearth and the beloved's lap.
