# TWIST Poem Analysis

## Do it first

"Analyse the poem" is a frightening instruction if you do not know where to start. The Philippines' Grade 10 book gives the start — and the whole road — as five letters: **TWIST**. Tone, Word choice, Imagery, Style, Theme. Work the five in order and you have analysed the poem. This lesson teaches the method on the book's own worked poem, Baudelaire's "Song of Autumn". (Imagery on its own is taught in *Sensory Images (Imagery)*.)

## Sub lesson 1 — T, W, I — tone, word choice, imagery

*Difficulty: first step — the ground of the lesson.*

**T — Tone:** the speaker's attitude, heard in the words — in "Song of Autumn", dread and tenderness together: winter is coming like a scaffold being nailed, yet the beloved is asked for a mother's comfort. **W — Word choice:** the loaded words that carry the tone: *plunge, vivid, dismal, clatter* (the book's own word list prints "clutter" — its poem reads "clatter", taught corrected), *atremble, battering, lulled, haste, boudoir, hearth* — each chosen word is evidence, and the analysis quotes it. **I — Imagery:** the sense-pictures the words build — falling wood, echoing courtyards, autumn's last yellow rays. Three passes, three kinds of evidence, all from the lines.

Try it now: Take one stanza of any poem. Write one line of analysis for each of T, W and I, quoting the words you stand on. If a line has no quote in it, it is not analysis yet.

## Sub lesson 2 — S, T — style and theme

*Difficulty: builds on Sub lesson 1.*

**S — Style:** the poem's making — its form and stanza shape, its sounds and rhythm, its repetitions and turns; in "Song of Autumn" the falling, echoing sounds are built to be heard, coffin-nails and scaffold in rhythm. **T — Theme:** what the whole poem says about life — here, that light and warmth are brief, loss is coming, and love is asked to shelter us against it: the theme must be a truth the poem argues, not a topic it mentions ("autumn" is a topic; "cherish the light before the dark" is a theme). The TWIST order is a ladder: tone and words first, the whole meaning last, because the theme is earned by the evidence of the first four.

Try it now: Finish the poem from Sub lesson 1: write its S line (one making-feature and what it does) and its T line (a theme stated as a truth, not a topic). Check the theme against the T-W-I evidence: does it hold?

## Do it yourself

Choose a poem of at least eight lines (from this course or your own reading). Write its full TWIST analysis — five headed parts, every part standing on quoted words, the theme stated as a truth. Under it, write one line: which letter was hardest to evidence, and why.
