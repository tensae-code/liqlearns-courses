# Riddles

## Do it first

Listen: *I have walls and a roof, a door and windows, but I am not a person. People live inside me. What am I?* … A house! You just answered a **riddle** — a guessing game made of clues. This lesson teaches you to answer riddles, and then to make your own.

## Sub lesson 1 — Answering riddles

A riddle gives you clues and hides the answer. Work the clues one by one:

- *I am tall when I stand still. I eat leaves from the top of the tree. My neck is very long.* — The animal with the long neck: a **giraffe**.
- *I can sing sweetly. I am small and I fly.* — A **bird**.
- *I wear black and white stripes, and I look like a horse.* — A **zebra**.
- *I am big and grey, and my nose is a long trunk.* — An **elephant**.
- *I have one horn on my nose, and my skin is thick and grey.* — A **rhinoceros**.

The trick is to listen for the clue that only ONE thing fits. Stripes fit many animals — but stripes on a horse shape fit only the zebra.

*Difficulty: Many clues, one answer — find the clue that fits only one.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Making your own riddle

To make a riddle, turn the game around:

1. Choose your answer in secret — an animal, a thing, a place.
2. Say what it does, what it looks like, where it lives — clue by clue.
3. Do NOT say its name. End with: *What am I?*

The best riddles start with clues that fit many things and end with the one clue that fits only yours. A riddle is a tiny test of describing — every clue is a describing sentence at work.

*Difficulty: Your own clues, and the answer kept secret to the end.*

## Say it to your companion

Ask your companion your riddle. If they cannot answer, give one more clue. Then swap: answer theirs.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write a riddle about an animal. Three clues, then "What am I?"
2. Write a riddle about a thing in your home.
3. Read both riddles to someone at home and let them guess.
