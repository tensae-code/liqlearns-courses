# Lesson plan — Poetry writing

**Course:** English · **Title:** Story · **Lesson:** Poetry writing
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 41, "Stories and Poems in
Detail" — the second lesson of the level.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** writing poems to
named shapes — the bio poem, the colour poem and the
five-line limerick — with the writer's working terms
(line, stanza, rhyme, rhythm, refrain, persona, imagery)
held ready as tools, not as a reading list.
**This lesson's own level:** it follows Story techniques;
where that lesson crafted prose, this one crafts verse.
**What the learner can do at the end:** build a bio poem
and a colour poem to their frames, write a limerick whose
five lines scan and rhyme AABBA, and choose rhyme or free
verse on purpose, knowing the terms for what they did.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The story (poems are its kin in this
  title). Recommended, never forced — every term and shape
  is defined as it arrives.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of three poem shapes side by side:
   a person-shaped poem (bio), a rainbow-edged poem
   (colour), a small five-line poem with its long and short
   lines showing (limerick).** What the student does: the
   student points at each shape and says whose it is.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of a poem as building bricks: single
   lines stacked into a stanza, a repeated line glowing
   (the refrain).** What the student does: the student
   counts lines, then stanzas, in a poem in front of them.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first**: three tiny poems — which shape is which?
2. **The writer's terms** (South Sudan Primary 7's glossary,
   cut to the tools a writer uses daily).
3. **How a poem gets written** (South Sudan's steps: goal,
   theme, imagery, rhyme/rhythm, revise; Egypt's steps
   beside them).
4. **Three named shapes** (Barbados's Scope and Sequence:
   bio poems, colour poems, limericks begin at Class 3) —
   each built to its frame; Ghana's growing line counts
   (4 → 6 → 8) and poem types (sonnet, acrostic, haiku) as
   the wider family.
5. **Rhyme or free verse — a choice** (South Sudan: poems
   written in both forms).
6. **Do it yourself**: hands-on trio, one of each shape.

## If the learner is stuck
- Limerick will not scan: count the beats aloud — long,
  long, short, short, long — and let the rhyme words be
  chosen first, the lines built backwards from them.
- Poem reads like chopped prose: Egypt's steps — feelings
  and images first, short lines, one comparison; cut every
  word the image does not need.
