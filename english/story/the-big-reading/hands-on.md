# Hands-on — The big reading

The lesson lived with a real book in the hands.

## The page test, performed
At a library shelf or a schoolbook's Readers: each learner
opens three candidate books at a middle page, counts
unknown words aloud with a partner, and sorts the books —
too high / my step / lazy Sunday. The sort, defended by
counts, is the level skill in one gesture. Choose ONE true-
step book before leaving the shelf.

## The island outline
For Crusoe readers (and any journey story): a big outline
map of the place, drawn as the book is read — wreck site,
house, footprint, the far shore. Each chapter adds only
what it earned. The finished map is the book's furniture
made visible — and a retelling's best friend.

## Two-line notebook club
A shared notebook (class or family): every reader's chapter
gets its two lines, signed and dated. Readers browse each
other's lines before choosing their next book — the
notebook becomes a living catalogue whose reviews are
exactly two sentences long and always honest.

## The five-minute finish
When a learner finishes a book, the room gives them five
minutes: the whole story, told back standing, listeners
holding the two-line notebook open as the score. Finishers
earn the room's choosing-rights for the next shared book —
the climb celebrated as the school's sport.
