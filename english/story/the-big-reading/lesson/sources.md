**Acceptance count: taught in 4 countries (Libya, Pakistan, Sudan, Kuwait)** — the
countries whose schoolbooks carry a complete book-length
text: Libya binds complete GRADED readers into its book;
Pakistan binds one complete novella in the author's own
words into its book; Sudan carries one complete graded
reader as its Grade 8 set's supplementary reader volume. That is why the curriculum names these books as the
road-markers. The habits taught (ladder at book
scale, two-line chapter law) are this course's own earlier
lessons grown, cross-referenced in the lesson plan rather
than re-sourced. Kuwait binds a complete graded retelling into
its Student's Book 10B: Treasure Island, simplified and
abridged, in five episodes reaching the story's end — a
graded retelling, not the original text, with no questions
on the story printed in the book. Other closed countries join as their
books are read (see READING-QUEUE.md).

**Sudan added 2026-11-04** — 1 book stocks this lesson: **Book:** *Anna and the Fighter* (literature reader) — Sudan, Macmillan Heinemann ELT, this edition first published 2005, ISBN 978-1-4050-7610-4. Filed plainly, as a judgment call on the Libya precedent: Sudan's Grade 8 set carries a complete graded reader — Elizabeth Laird's *Anna and the Fighter*, Macmillan Readers Beginner level (about 600 headwords, printed p.2) — whose whole story is printed on printed pp.3–30 and reaches its end on printed p.30, with a final illustration on printed p.31; the held copy is complete, with no printed page missing. It stocks this lesson the Libya way, not the Pakistan way: like Libya's readers it is a complete GRADED retelling-scale reader, not an original text, and its physical scale (a 28-page story) is the scale of Libya's bound-in *Robinson Crusoe* segment. Two differences from both precedents are stated exactly: the reader is a separate supplementary volume of the Grade 8 set, not bound into the pupil's book (the pupil's book's introduction names reading supplementary readers among the course's outcomes), and it sits at Beginner level, far below Libya's secondary readers and Pakistan's novella. The short stories and adapted extracts inside Sudan's textbooks (Oliver Twist, *Little Women*, "The Wedding of Zein") do NOT stock this lesson and are not claimed here.
