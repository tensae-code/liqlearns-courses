# The big reading — true or false

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — What "Graded" Means (item 1) and Sub lesson 2 — Libya's Proof, Bound In (item 2) · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 4 — Reading at Book Scale · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 5 — Stepping Up (item 6), except item 5, the zero-new-words page -> Sub lesson 3 — Finding Your Level.

Say true or false. If it is false, say why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** A graded reader is a children's summary of
a classic.
**False** — it is the WHOLE story retold at a level;
Libya binds two into its Grade 11 book, not its nursery
books.

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** The Scientific Grade 11 set also carries
the Readers.
**False** — the Science set drops them; the Readers belong
to the Literary/language path, as filed.

**3. (Sub lesson 4)** Reading a graded book with the dictionary
open at every word is the careful way.
**False** — the ladder runs the road (infer first); the
dictionary waits for twice-blocking words. Story speed is
the skill.

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** If you cannot tell a chapter back in two
sentences, you have still read it fully.
**False** — the two-line law: an untellable chapter was
passed over, not read. Re-read it; it is short.

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** A page with zero unknown words is the
perfect growth book.
**False** — nothing new means no growth; that is the lazy-
Sunday step. Growth lives at a handful of new words a page.

**6. (Sub lesson 5)** When the graded steps run out, reading
stops.
**False** — the road thins into the real library: originals
unretold, the Novel title. The steps were always aimed
there.
