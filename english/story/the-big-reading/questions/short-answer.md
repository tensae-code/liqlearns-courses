# The big reading — short answer

Answer in a word or a short line.

**1. (Level 1)** Why is Libya's Literary book 218 pages
where its sibling runs to 141?
**Answer:** the Readers bound into its back — two complete
graded readers from p.150 onward. The Scientific set drops
them.

**2. (Level 1)** Name the two readers, their authors, and
their reteller.
**Answer:** Robinson Crusoe — Daniel Defoe; Treasure
Island — Robert Louis Stevenson; both retold by Peter
Viney (Garnet, 2019).

**3. (Level 2)** Mid-chapter you meet a hard word twice, and
the story limps both times. The ladder's ruling?
**Answer:** now it has earned the dictionary — look it up,
swap-test it in its sentence, note it in the word log, read
on. Twice-blocking is the book-scale threshold.

**4. (Level 2)** Tell back this chapter-opening in two
sentences: "Crusoe saw the footprint in the sand. He ran
to his house and locked the door. For two days he did not
go out."
**Answer:** e.g. "Crusoe found a footprint that was not his
and fled home in fear. He hid inside for two days." — any
two whole sentences, furniture kept, dress yours.

**5. (Level 3)** A learner finishes Treasure Island and
demands Defoe's original Crusoe "because graded is for
children." Answer them in the lesson's terms.
**Answer:** graded is for READERS at a step — Libya binds it
into Grade 11, not the nursery; the road to the original
runs THROUGH the graded steps, and the page test, not
pride, chooses the next book.

**6. (Level 3)** Where does the graded road end?
**Answer:** it thins into the real library — the originals
unretold (the ladder's Novel title). Steps up are chosen by
evidence until no retelling stands between reader and book.
