# Lesson plan — The big reading

**Course:** English · **Title:** Story · **Lesson:** The big reading
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 17 — Stories — the
third lesson of the unit (CURRICULUM.md). Building it
completes Unit 17 and with it the built English path —
the graded readers bound into Libya's Grade 11 books mark
where this road leads.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** reading WHOLE story
books at your level — the graded reader (a real classic
retold in English sized to the learner) — and the habits
that carry a reader through a book and up the levels
toward the ungraded real thing.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the whole of the
reading and story levels before it — The story (what you
are reading), Words you don't
know (the ladder, now at book scale), Answering from the
text (a reader's self-check) — and it is the English plan's
last built lesson: beyond it stands the Novel title, added
to the plan when the reading reaches those books.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or museum-held art, with source); story-teaching images may be
simple drawings where a photograph cannot teach. GIFs come
first where a thing must be shown changing — a GIF here is
1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **What "Graded" Means** — a whole real story retold in
   English sized to the learner: the dress is graded, not
   the tale. (Was the first part of depth Level 1.)
2. **Libya's Proof, Bound In** — two complete graded
   readers bound into the back of Libya's Grade 11
   Literary book, from its Readers section at p.150.
   (Was the second part of depth Level 1.)
3. **Finding Your Level** — the page test: a handful of
   unknown words and the story still runs — that is your
   honest step. (Was the third part of depth Level 1.)
4. **Reading at Book Scale** — the word ladder at book
   scale, the furniture compass, and the two-line chapter
   law: no new chapter until the last is told back in two
   sentences. (Was depth Level 2.)
5. **Stepping Up** — when a level reads easy twice, climb;
   beyond the last graded step the originals wait,
   unretold. (Was depth Level 3.)
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The story; Words you don't know.
  Recommended, never forced.
- Copyright honesty, stated in the lesson: this course
  teaches the ROAD and the habits; the readers themselves
  are the learner's own books (school copies, libraries) —
  the platform's books are never handed out (his standing
  rule for the whole library).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a graded reader is and find their own level: a
   book they can read at story speed, inferring as they go,
   opening the dictionary rarely.
2. Name Libya's proof of the road: the two complete readers
   bound into the Grade 11 Literary course book — Robinson
   Crusoe (from p.150) and Treasure Island (from p.178),
   both retold by Peter Viney (Garnet, 2019).
3. Read a book the reader's way: whole chapters without
   stopping, the ladder for hard words, a two-line telling-
   back after each chapter (Level 17's own skill as the
   chapter check).
4. Step up on purpose: when a level reads easy twice, take
   the next — the road ends at the real, unretold books.

## What you need
One graded reader at the learner's level (a school or
library copy). A bookmark; a notebook for the chapter
tellings-back.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner reads the same storm at
   sea twice: once in the original's hard dress, once in a
   graded retelling; they feel both tell ONE story — and
   only one of them can be RUN through (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — what graded means; Libya's bound
   readers as the country's own proof; finding your level;
   reading at book scale (the ladder grown; chapter
   tellings-back); stepping up (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: an animated-map film of Crusoe's island
     story, armies-on-the-move style — the wreck, the
     shore, the footprint — told in the super-simple voice
     from video-script.md (made later, when videos are
     made) → the student marks the three furniture pieces
     on a printed island outline as the film runs.
   - ADD IMAGE under the level part: a picture of a staircase
     of books — small thick-storied readers on the low
     steps, the real Crusoe at the top — a reader figure on
     a middle step → the student places their own current
     book on the staircase honestly.
   - ADD AUDIO in the book-scale part: a voice reading a
     graded chapter opening at story speed, NOT pausing at
     the one hard word — the student notes the word, infers,
     and keeps the story's thread in one sentence.
3. **Say it to your companion** — the two-line law: after
   any chapter, each tells the other the chapter back in two
   sentences; a chapter that cannot be told back in two was
   not yet read (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the stepping-up part: a picture of the book-
     staircase figure climbing one step and setting a flag,
     1–2 seconds — the student names their flag-book for
     this term.
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — begin a real graded reader this
   week: level chosen by the test in the lesson, one chapter
   a day, two lines of telling-back per chapter in the
   notebook (lesson/lesson.md).

- VIDEO — an animated-map film of Crusoe's island → the

- IMAGE — a picture of a staircase of books → the student
  places their current book honestly.
- AUDIO — a graded chapter read at story speed → the student

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the
plan is the build sheet, so nothing the lesson needs gets forgotten.

## If the learner is stuck
- Chooses books far above level (pride) or far below
  (comfort) → the level test is mechanical, not moral: a
  page with a handful of unknown words and a story you can
  retell is your step; count, don't blush.
- Reads word-by-word, dictionary open → the ladder at book
  scale: inference runs the road; the dictionary waits for
  words that BLOCK the story twice. Chapter deadlines
  (one a day) restore speed kindly.
- Finishes books but keeps nothing → the two-line law was
  skipped. No new chapter until the last one is told back
  in two sentences — Telling a story back is this lesson's
  memory engine.
- With this lesson, **Level 17 — and the built English
  path — is complete**. The plan's
  next country is the deeper sentence work and longer
  writing the later books carry — and the Novel at the
  ladder's top.
