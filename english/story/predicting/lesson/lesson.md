# Predicting What Happens Next

## Sub lesson 1 — Reading the clues the story has planted

Writers plant clues. A prediction is a guess built on them.

- **Title and pictures** — what do they promise?
- **What you know about the people** — a brave character acts bravely; a secret will come out.
- **Things shown early** — a key, a storm cloud, a locked door: stories show them because they will matter.
- **The feeling** — if the page feels tense, something is coming.

A prediction is not a wish and not a random guess: it is the clues, joined up.

Do it: a story opens with a girl finding a small key under her door. Say two clues and what each one points to.

## Sub lesson 2 — Saying your prediction and why

Say it in the prediction shape: **I think … will … because …**

- *I think the girl will open a hidden door, because the key was left for her to find.*
- Weaker and stronger: *maybe*, *probably*, *I'm sure* — pick the word that matches your clues.
- Then read on and CHECK: a good reader tests the prediction against the next page and changes it when the story says otherwise.

Do it: finish this prediction with two becauses: "I think the boy will run home because… and because…" Then say which word — maybe, probably, or I'm sure — fits your clues.

## Say it to your companion

Read the key sentences aloud and explain them to your companion in your own words — what the thing is, and one example of your own.

## Do It Yourself

Close the lesson and use the thing once in real life today — say it, write it, or notice it — then say how it went in one sentence.
