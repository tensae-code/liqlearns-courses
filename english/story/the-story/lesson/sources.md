**Acceptance count: taught in 11 countries (Bangladesh,
Egypt, Ghana, China, Indonesia, Sierra Leone, Jordan, Greece, Kazakhstan, Iraq, Ethiopia).** Ghana names the narrative mode; Bangladesh
teaches stories from its earliest classes with the fables;
Egypt reads whole stories through the secondary years;
China names the story's three parts outright (setting,
characters, plot) and binds complete play scripts to
perform. Indonesia tables the narrative's own stages
(Orientation, Complication, Resolution — and a Coda at
Grade 9) on the stories themselves and re-teaches the
definition whole; Sierra Leone names characters, setting
and plot from Primary 4–5 and restates the shared elements
(plot, theme, characters, setting, conflict, point of
view) in the SSS3 creative-writing lesson; Jordan reads
a model detective story and writes one at Grade 7 under
its "How to write… a good story" reference, sets a short
story as a Grade 10 writing outcome with its linkers
grouped, and writes a story inside Grade 11's
narrative-tenses unit; Greece gives a whole primary unit
to stories — a fairy tale reconstructed and dramatised,
then the pupil's own short story — names a Grade 7 lesson
"Tell me a story!" over a prize-winning model, and teaches
narrative craft outright at Grades 11–12, where the story
is charted and enriched with background, flashback and
monologue; Kazakhstan gives whole modules to stories —
its Grade 6 book glosses the story's own elements (plot,
character, setting, conflict) beside Oliver Twist and
sets a story as the module's writing outcome, its
Grade 7 book closes each story text with a theory box
telling narrative, legends and folk tales apart, and
its other Grade 6 course prints story-writing advice
beside its model story; Iraq prints stories throughout
its primary course — six two-page picture stories bound
as their own section at Grade 1, a story closing every
main unit at Grade 2 (The toy maker, Goldilocks and the
three bears, The rabbit and the tortoise among them),
and six stories with printed text at Grade 3, the later
ones in full past-tense narration;
Ethiopia stocks the story in part — narrative writing
is taught as the narrative essay at Grades 11 and 12,
and stories are read whole from primary school up
(the Grade 3 fable "The Running Race", the Grade 2
story "Birra and Bari", the Grade 9 "Spooky Story"
carrying its past-tense work) — but no read Ethiopia
book names the story's furniture (setting, characters,
plot) as such, so that naming rests on the other
countries. Other closed countries join as their books are read (see
READING-QUEUE.md).
