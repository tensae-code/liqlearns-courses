# Hands-on — The story

The lesson told aloud and built with the body in the room.

## Furniture tag, live
One teller, one anecdote. Listeners hold three cards —
WHO / WHERE / THEN WHAT — and raise a card silently when
the telling starves that piece. A story that never raises
a card stands furnished. Rotate tellers; the cards make
audiences honest without a word of criticism.

## Picture-string stories
Five pictures in a row (cards, magazine cuts, drawings):
the class strings them into one story — person chosen,
place named, happenings hung on the pictures in order.
Then shuffle two pictures: the story must be re-strung, and
the class hears how much of the meaning was ORDER.

## The map telling
For a journey story: draw the place as a map on the floor
(chalk, paper, stones for houses). The teller walks the
map as they tell — standing IN the place for each
happening. This is the seed of the animated-map kind of
telling the platform loves, done with feet.

## Fable repair shop
Take a deliberately broken fable (place removed / order
shuffled / want deleted — three broken versions). Pairs
diagnose which furniture piece is missing and repair ONLY
that piece. Broken stories are the fastest teachers of what
the pieces do.
