# The story — true or false

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — The Story's Furniture · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 1 (item 2, the happenings' order), Sub lesson 2 — The Story's Coat — the Past (item 6) and Sub lesson 3 — Story or Essay — the Border (item 3) · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 4 — The Trouble Seed (item 4). Items are marked by what each item actually asks.

Say true or false. If it is false, say why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** A story can have animals as its people.
**True** — person-shaped is enough: the fables of every
country's books run on crows, foxes, and hares who want
and work.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** If the happenings are exciting, their order
does not matter.
**False** — order is meaning (First/Then/Finally grown
tall): stones after drinking is no story.

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** A story and an essay are the same shape
with different topics.
**False** — different spines: happenings happening TO
someone vs points SERVING an idea. The argue/happen test
separates them.

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** A story with no trouble or want can still
stand, but flatly.
**True** — stated as flavour, not law: furniture complete,
fire missing. The books' stories nearly always carry the
seed.

**5. (Sub lesson 1)** The place must be described for pages
before the story may start.
**False** — the place can be set in a line ("a jar in a dry
land"). It must EXIST, not sprawl.

**6. (Sub lesson 2)** Live tellers may use the present tense for
drama, so the past rule is fake.
**False** — the flare is a licence ON the rule: books'
stories and this course's tellings wear the past; the
present flare works BECAUSE the home is past.
