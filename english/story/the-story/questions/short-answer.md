# The story — short answer

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — The Story's Furniture · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — The Story's Coat — the Past (item 3) and Sub lesson 3 — Story or Essay — the Border (item 4) · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 4 — The Trouble Seed (item 5), except item 6, what the place does -> Sub lesson 1.

Answer in a word or a short line.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Tag the crow fable's furniture in three
lines.
**Answer:** PEOPLE — a thirsty crow. PLACE — a dry land,
by a jar. HAPPENINGS — she found the jar, dropped stones,
the water rose, she drank.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** Why can a crow be a story's "people"?
**Answer:** the who must be someone happenings can land
on — she wants, works, and wins. Person-SHAPED is enough;
fables run on it.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** A classmate's story reads: "He will go to
the market. He will buy rice. He will come home." One fault,
one fix.
**Answer:** the coat — a told story wears the past: "He
went… he bought… he came home." (A plan for tomorrow would
lawfully wear the future; a telling does not.)

**4. (Sub lesson 3)** "Reasons our school is best: teachers,
yard, library." Story or essay — and the test?
**Answer:** essay — the test is ARGUE or HAPPEN: it argues
(points serving an idea); nothing happens to anyone.

**5. (Sub lesson 4)** Add the trouble seed to this skeleton: "A
boy. A river. He fished."
**Answer:** e.g. the boy must catch a fish before dark or
his family does not eat / the river is rising and his
sister is across it — a want or a wrong that makes the
happenings worth stringing (any true trouble earns it).

**6. (Sub lesson 1)** What dies when a story's place is removed?
**Answer:** its standing — happenings float unanchored;
the listener cannot picture where anyone is, and journey
happenings ("then he crossed…") lose their meaning.
