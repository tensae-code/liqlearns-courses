# The story — multiple choice

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — The Story's Furniture · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — The Story's Coat — the Past · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 4 — The Trouble Seed (item 5), except item 6, the spine test -> Sub lesson 3 — Story or Essay — the Border.

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** A story is built of —
a) people, a place, and things happening, in order
b) points, reasons, and a conclusion
c) questions and answers
**Answer: a)** — b) is the essay's furniture.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** "The bus leaves at six and arrives at
seven" is NOT a story because —
a) it is too short  b) nothing happens TO anyone — there
are no people for the happenings to land on  c) buses
cannot be in stories
**Answer: b)** — a timetable: happenings with the people
left out.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** A story's tense-home is —
a) the future  b) the past — happened, finished, tellable
c) no tense at all
**Answer: b)** — Level 3's recount law at story scale.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** Ghana names the story's mode among the four
modes of writing as —
a) expository  b) argument  c) narrative
**Answer: c)** — narrative, beside expository, description,
argument.

**5. (Sub lesson 4)** The crow's stones become a story (not just
events) when —
a) there are many stones  b) a wanting person works them
against a trouble — she is thirsty and the water is low
c) the jar is pretty
**Answer: b)** — the trouble seed: a who, a want, resistance.

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** An essay that opens with a small story —
a) becomes a story  b) stays an essay: the spine decides
the name — points serving an idea, with a story borrowed
for its opening  c) breaks the rules
**Answer: b)** — borrowed clothes change nothing structural.
