# Lesson plan — The story

**Course:** English · **Title:** Story · **Lesson:** The story
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 17 — Stories — the
first lesson of the unit (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** what a story IS —
people (someone it happens to), a place (where it stands),
and things happening, in an order, told mostly in the
past: the narrative shape every story in every country's
book is built from.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Past actions (the
story's tense is the past — Level 12's own rule that a
narrative wears the past) and on First/Then/Finally (the
happenings' order rides on those signposts). It opens the
Story title; the Novel beyond it is the plan's far shore.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule: for stories,
real photographs or museum-held art from the time, with
source, for anything the world holds; simple drawings are
allowed for teaching pictures a photograph cannot teach.
GIFs come first where a thing must be shown changing — a
GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The Story's Furniture** — people, a place, and
   things happening in an order: the three things every
   story is built of. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **The Story's Coat — the Past** — narrative is the
   story's mode, and its tense-home is the past: happened,
   finished, tellable. (Was the coat half of depth
   Level 2.)
3. **Story or Essay — the Border** — the essay is points
   serving an idea; the story is happenings happening to
   someone: does it ARGUE or does it HAPPEN? (Was the
   border half of depth Level 2.)
4. **The Trouble Seed** — something wanted or wrong makes
   the happenings worth stringing: a who that wants, a
   place that holds them, happenings that resist and
   yield. (Was depth Level 3.)
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Past actions; First, then, finally.
  Recommended, never forced.
- The story's difference from the essay is taught inside
  the lesson (points vs happenings); no earlier lesson is
  needed for it.
- Story-like subjects across the platform are taught as
  stories (his standing design) — this lesson is where
  that instinct becomes a named shape the learner can use
  everywhere.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name any story's three furniture pieces — the people,
   the place, the happenings — and its tense-home (the
   past).
2. Tell why a list of happenings with no person is not yet
   a story, and why points in order are an essay, not a
   story.
3. Follow a story's happening-order (the sequence is the
   meaning — First/Then/Finally at story scale).
4. Begin shaping a story of their own: choose the person,
   set the place, string the happenings.

## What you need
Any short story — a fable from a schoolbook serves best
(Aesop's crow lives in Bangladesh's books). Paper and a
pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner gets three texts: a bus
   timetable (happenings, no person), an essay paragraph
   (points, no happenings), and a four-line fable — and
   sorts them by "which one is a story?" with their gut,
   before the furniture is named (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the three pieces; the order; the
   past coat; story vs essay; the trouble seed (a story's
   happenings usually turn on something going wrong or
   wanted — stated plainly, as flavour, not law)
   (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO (this lesson's first add-on is a video, by
     the media rule — story-like things are taught as
     stories): an animated-map/short-film telling of a tiny
     fable, armies-on-the-move style at story scale — the
     crow's journey flown over, stone by stone — the student
     pauses it at three stones and names: person, place,
     happening.
   - ADD IMAGE under the furniture part: a picture of a
     stage set with three labelled stands — a figure (the
     PEOPLE), a painted backdrop (the PLACE), a path of
     footprints (the HAPPENINGS) → the student furniture-
     tags a story from their own book.
   - ADD AUDIO in the tense part: a voice telling the same
     four happenings first in the present, then in the past
     — the student calls which telling "sounds like a
     story" and why.
3. **Say it to your companion** — furniture-tag each
   other's told anecdotes live: interrupting (kindly) only
   to ask "who? where? then what?" (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the order part: a picture of footprints
     appearing one by one along a path, each past-tense
     verb stamping as its print lands, 1–2 seconds — the
     student reads the path aloud in order.
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching, ordering — served at
   the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — take a story you know by heart:
   write its furniture in three lines, then tell it in ten
   past-tense sentences under the signposts (lesson/lesson.md).

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- VIDEO — an animated telling of a tiny fable → the student
  names person, place, happening at three pauses.
- IMAGE — a picture of a stage set with three labelled
  stands → the student furniture-tags a story.
- AUDIO — the same happenings in present, then past → the
  student calls which sounds like a story.
- GIF — a picture of footprints stamping past-tense verbs
  along a path → the student reads the path in order.

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the
plan is the build sheet, so nothing the lesson needs gets forgotten.

## If the learner is stuck
- Retells essays when asked for stories → the sorter from
  the Do-it-first, replayed with their own texts: does it
  HAPPEN, or does it ARGUE? Happenings belong to people.
- Happenings without a spine (and then, and then…) → the
  signpost set is the spine's first form (Level 14); a story
  is a sequence paragraph that grew people.
- Present tense throughout a telling → not an error of the
  story-shape but of its coat: Past actions is the repair
  lesson; live tellers may use the present for drama, and
  the plan says so plainly — books' stories wear the past.
- Next in this level: Telling a story back — the retelling
  craft; then The big reading.
