# Lesson Plan — Point of View

**Unit 41 — Stories and Poems in Detail.**

**One thing:** point of view — who tells the story and how much they can see: first person, second person, or third person, limited or omniscient — and why writers choose one.

## Sub lessons

1. **Who is telling the story.** *Can-do: Name the narrator: first person (the narrator is inside the story, saying I), second person (the narrator speaks to you), third person (the narrator stands outside, saying he/she/they).*
2. **Limited and omniscient.** *Can-do: Judge how much the narrator knows: limited — one character's eyes and thoughts; omniscient — the all-knowing narrator who sees every heart — and defend the identification with evidence.*

(The gate pool for each sub lesson is every question marked with that sub lesson's number in this lesson's `questions/` files — the whole set, because no hardness ranking is written in questions/.)

## Teaching points

- Point of view studied across model texts (item 414): first, second and third person identified; limited compared with omniscient; learners state the point of view of their own novel with evidence, then rewrite a scene from a new point of view. Model texts named in the placements: The Buddha in the Attic, The Outsiders, Frankenstein, Harrison Bergeron, The Lottery, The Hunger Games, To Kill a Mockingbird, The Glass Castle.
