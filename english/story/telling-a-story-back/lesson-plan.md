# Lesson plan — Telling a story back

**Course:** English · **Title:** Story · **Lesson:** Telling a story back
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 17, "Stories" — the
second lesson of the level (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** retelling a story —
the story again, whole, in its true order, in YOUR words
and the past's coat, shorter than the original by dropping
small stones but never a load-bearing one; including the
books' own variations (the word box, the chain, the
first-person retelling).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The story
(retelling keeps its furniture standing) and Ghana's
summarising law from Finding the idea (own words, or it is
not understood) — applied to a tale instead of a text.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source); for stories, simple drawings are
allowed for teaching pictures a photograph cannot teach. GIFs
come first where a thing must be shown changing — a GIF here
is 1–2 seconds.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The story. Recommended, never forced.
- The word box and the chain are Bangladesh's and Ghana's
  classroom shapes, named inside the lesson — a learner
  meeting them here can use them in any class tomorrow.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Retell any short story whole: who, where, happenings in
   order, ending standing — in their own words, past coat
   on.
2. Shorten lawfully: drop small stones (decorative details)
   while never dropping a load-bearing one (removing it
   breaks what follows).
3. Retell with helps: from a word box (Bangladesh), in a
   chain (Ghana), on a frame (Ghana's Herring Bone), and AS
   a character (Bangladesh's first-person retelling of the
   crow).
4. Check a retelling against the original: same furniture,
   same order, new words — and catch imports (things the
   teller added that the story never had).

## What you need
A short story everyone can hear or read first — a fable
serves best. Paper and a pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner hears the crow fable, the
   book closes, and they retell it to the room; the room
   then hears the original again and lists what the
   retelling kept, dropped, and invented (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — whole/in-order/own-words; the two
   kinds of stones; the helps (word box, chain, frame,
   first person); the import law (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD AUDIO at the whole part: a voice telling a fable,
     then retelling it shorter — the student ticks each
     load-bearing stone on a printed list as the retelling
     passes it, and shouts "missing!" if one is skipped.
   - ADD GIF at the stones part: a picture of a jar filling
     with stones — big stones first settling the water's
     rise; then small pebbles shuffled in as decoration,
     1–2 seconds — the student calls which stones the rise
     NEEDED.
   - ADD IMAGE under the helps part: a picture of a herring-
     bone frame pinned on a wall — spine labelled ORDER,
     ribs labelled WHO/WHERE/WHAT HAPPENED — the student
     pins a story they know onto the frame in writing.
3. **Say it to your companion** — chain retelling: each
   teller adds the next happening and hands it on; the last
   teller owes the whole (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — a
     story passed around a fire, in the super-simple voice
     (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served at
   the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — retell one story three ways: full,
   short (half the sentences), and as its main character
   (lesson/lesson.md).

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- AUDIO — a fable told, then retold shorter → the student
  ticks load-bearing stones and catches a skip.
- GIF — a picture of a jar filling with big stones, then
  pebbles → the student calls the stones the rise needed.
- IMAGE — a picture of a herring-bone frame on a wall → the
  student pins a story onto it.
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later) → the student
  chains along with it.

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the
plan is the build sheet, so nothing the lesson needs gets forgotten.

## If the learner is stuck
- Retells by memorised sentences, cracks at any change →
  that is reciting, not retelling. The word box is the
  bridge (the books' own): words given, sentences the
  learner's own. Then box away.
- Drops the ending (the last happening) → endings are load-
  bearing by definition here; the chain's last teller shows
  it: a story without its landing has not been told.
- Invents freely (new characters, new events) → imports are
  lawful in MAKING stories, not in telling one BACK. The
  lesson's word is fidelity: same furniture, own words.
- Next, and last in Level 4: The big reading — whole story
  books, graded to your level.
