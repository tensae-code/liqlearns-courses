# Telling a story back — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Level 1)** A retelling's three laws are —
a) fast, funny, loud  b) whole, in order, in your own words
c) copied, complete, memorised
**Answer: b).**

**2. (Level 1)** In the crow fable, a LOAD-BEARING stone is —
a) the jar's colour  b) the water rising (without it, the
drinking cannot stand)  c) the third stone's splash
**Answer: b)** — small stones decorate; this one carries.

**3. (Level 2)** A retelling that stops before the last
happening has —
a) shortened lawfully  b) broken wholeness — the ending is
load-bearing by definition  c) improved the story
**Answer: b).**

**4. (Level 2)** Bangladesh's word box gives the reteller —
a) the whole story's sentences  b) the story's load-bearing
words; the sentences stay the learner's own  c) the answers
**Answer: b)** — help for the furniture, not a recitation.

**5. (Level 3)** In a first-person retelling, the teller —
a) changes the story's happenings to their own life  b)
tells the same story as its character: "I was thirsty. I
found a jar…"  c) skips the order
**Answer: b)** — same furniture, new teller; then compare
with the original.

**6. (Level 3)** An added bright detail (a red scarf the
story never had) in a retelling is —
a) an import — a fault in telling BACK, lawful only in
making your own stories  b) always good  c) a small stone
**Answer: a)** — fidelity is this title's border.
