# Telling a story back — short answer

Answer in a word or a short line.

**1. (Level 1)** Retell the crow fable in exactly three
sentences.
**Answer:** e.g. "A thirsty crow found a jar with a little
water at its bottom. She dropped stones into it, one by
one, until the water rose. Then she drank." — whole, in
order, every stone load-bearing.

**2. (Level 2)** A retelling drops "she dropped stones" and
keeps "the water rose." Why does it limp?
**Answer:** a load-bearing stone is gone: the rising has no
cause; the next happening cannot stand. Shortening may
only spend small stones.

**3. (Level 2)** What is Ghana's chain, in one line?
**Answer:** the story told in a circle — each teller
carries the next happening and hands it on, the last
teller landing the ending.

**4. (Level 3)** Retell the crow's first happening as the
crow.
**Answer:** "I was thirsty, and I found a jar with a little
water at the bottom — too low to reach." — the I-form,
facts unchanged.

**5. (Level 1)** How does a retelling differ from reciting?
**Answer:** reciting repeats the author's sentences from
memory; retelling rebuilds the story from its furniture in
your own words — the closed-book test separates them.

**6. (Level 3)** After a first-person retelling, Bangladesh
ends the lesson by —
**Answer:** comparing it with the original fable — item by
item: same furniture, same order, new teller and words.
