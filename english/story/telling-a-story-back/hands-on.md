# Hands-on — Telling a story back

The lesson told around tables, stones in hand (near enough).

## Stones on the table
Literally: for a known fable, write its happenings on
stones (or cards weighted like them) — big printed stones
for load-bearing happenings, small for decoration. A teller
retells while a partner lifts each stone as it is told.
At the end the table shows the untold stones: dropped —
and the room judges each: small (lawful) or load-bearing
(the story limps).

## The chain, standing
Circle, standing. Chain retelling as in the lesson, but
the teller of each happening takes one step toward the
centre; the final landing is reached together. Bodies
remember sequences that mouths drop — the chain in the
feet first, then in words alone.

## Word-box relay
Bangladesh's form as a race against the story, not each
other: a word box on the board; pairs retell against a
gentle clock, striking each box word as they spend it.
Unspent load-bearing words at the end name what the
retelling owes. Then the box is erased and the retelling
goes again, unaided.

## First-person masks
Paper masks or simple signs (CROW / FOX / FARMER). Retell
wearing your character's sign, in the I-form, to a listener
who holds the original and may tap the table at any
import. The tap — kind, immediate — is the comparison
lesson in a gesture.
