**Acceptance count: taught in 10 countries (Bangladesh,
Ghana, China, Mongolia, Indonesia, Sierra Leone, Jordan, Greece, Kazakhstan, Iraq).** Bangladesh equips the retelling (word box,
first-person, comparison); Ghana frames it (chain, Herring
Bone, sequence order); China equips it again (the fill-in
retell, the phone retell, the sorted diary recount);
Mongolia's first-year book carries a wordless farm story
made to be told back from pictures alone. Indonesia names
retell-and-rewrite in its chapter objectives and performs
the telling-back (a song retelling, a performed sequel
narration, group retelling from a printed script); Sierra
Leone drills retelling with a focus on main points, then
the plot back, then whole passages back; Jordan names
retelling the story as a Grade 4 unit's function and
sets rewriting a familiar story as a Grade 6 project;
Greece stocks the retelling in part — a picture-gap
retelling of the whole Pinocchio tale at Grade 2, a chat
retelling as a model and an assessment retelling of the
Golden Fish tale at Grade 5 — but no Greece book names an
own-words retelling lesson of its own;
Kazakhstan stocks the retelling in part — a folk tale
retold as a chain story and again from the rich man's
point of view at Grade 6, the Canterville Ghost retold
from the ghost's point of view at Grade 9, and the
giant-squid extract retold from four points of view in
its other Grade 9 book — but no Kazakhstan book sets an
own-words retelling lesson with a comparison against
the original; Iraq stocks the retelling in full — its
Grade 1 Teacher's Book sets the retelling of Sami's
Rocket in the revision unit, and its Grade 5 Teacher's
Book names the own-words retelling as a set aim,
"Retell a story in your own words", the point where the
course first asks pupils to retell stories in their own
words.
Other closed countries join as
their books are read (see READING-QUEUE.md).
