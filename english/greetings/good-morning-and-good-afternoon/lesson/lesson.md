# Good morning and good afternoon — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Greetings · Lesson: Good morning and
good afternoon
Built from: Libya, English for Libya Primary 1 — and Egypt, English
KG1 and KG2 Term 1.
This lesson is ONE thing only: the greeting changes with the time
of day. Plain hello, names, and goodbye live in their own lessons.

## Do it first
Look at the time right now. Is it before midday, or after it? Say
the greeting that fits this moment, out loud, to someone near you —
or to your explain companion.

You just did the whole lesson. Now let's see how it works.

## Then the explanation
So "Hello" works all day. But English also has greetings that know
what time it is — Libya's Grade 1 book teaches them in its second
lesson, right inside the alphabet unit:

- In the **morning** — from when you wake up until midday — you say:
  **"Good morning."**
- In the **afternoon** — after midday, while the day is still
  bright — you say: **"Good afternoon."**

Egypt's youngest book shows the morning one in a picture story: a
child arriving at school in the morning says "Good morning," and
the same page pairs it with leaving at sunset — that closing word,
"Goodbye," is a different lesson, next door. Egypt's KG2 book then
puts "Hello / Good morning" side by side as the two ways to open
the day.

Same door, two greetings — the time of day chooses which one.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: it is 9 in the morning. What do you say? Now it
is 3 in the afternoon. What do you say? Explain why the greeting
changed and the door did not. (The greeting follows the time of
day; opening a talk is still opening a talk.)

## Do It Yourself
1. Tomorrow, greet the first person you meet with the right
   time-of-day greeting for that moment.
2. Later the same day, after midday, greet someone with
   "Good afternoon."
3. Write the two greetings down with the time next to each one:
   the time is the proof you used the right one.
