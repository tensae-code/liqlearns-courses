**Series verdicts.** Two overlapping generations
of the ministry's *English for Ethiopia* course.
Primary (G1–G6) is oral- and word-study-led at
first: G1–G2 teach no tense system at all (past
forms surface unexplained inside stories), writing
never passes sentence level, and the alphabet and
letter sounds are the year's real work. Explicit
grammar starts at G3 — and G3's guide already
assumes a simple past that neither book ever
teaches, the primary set's largest structural gap.
The ladder then climbs fast: articles and the
present continuous (G4), the passive in three
tenses and the past continuous (G5), and by G6
the past perfect, conditionals Types 1–3 (never
Type 0) and an introduction to reported speech —
but writing still caps at the single paragraph,
the present perfect continuous never arrives, and
listening texts exist only inside the guides.
Secondary (G7–G12) rebuilds more slowly than the
primary ladder suggests: G7 passivises only the
present simple and teaches no conditionals or
reported speech; G8 adds neither reported speech,
past perfect nor present perfect continuous; G9
(2021-Framework course) runs tenses, conditionals,
modals of obligation, relative clauses and
connectors — still no reported speech, no passive
as a taught topic, and no literature anywhere.
The 2023-curriculum band (G10–G12) is the fullest:
G10 gives reported speech and conditionals one
unit each; G11 is the grammar peak (the future
perfect taught, a passive paradigm table running
to forms like "will have been being painted");
G12 is a revision year whose new-structure
teaching stops after Unit 3. **Across G10–G12 the
present perfect continuous is never taught as a
form anywhere, the future continuous and future
perfect continuous appear only as rows in G11's
passive table, and mixed conditionals are never
named as a category. And in the national-exam
year itself, neither the G12 student's book nor
its guide contains any exam-preparation apparatus
at all** — no model exams, no past papers, no test
strategy. A second series-wide fact: **no primary
book prints an ISBN** (the ISBN/Price labels on
the covers are blank in every copy); ISBNs appear
only from G9 up, and two of those are
production-stage numbers (see corrections 2 and
4).

**Pair verdicts.** All twelve student's
book ↔ teacher's guide pairs are genuine pairs —
same course, same units, same order; the guides
print the listening texts the student's books
withhold. The mismatch feared for Grade 9 (guide
recorded "new curriculum" via Telegram, student's
book from the Amhara bureau server) does not
exist: both are the same 2021-Framework course in
different printings (guide 2022, student's book
August 2023). Two pair-level defects are real,

### Unit-by-unit summary

- **Unit 1 — My Family (printed pp. 1–26).** Oral Language: the small and capital alphabets (listen-and-repeat charts, two alphabet songs), dotted-line and letter tracing, classroom greetings (a "Good morning" song; a teacher–class greeting exchange), letters Aa Bb Ff Mm Ss taught through Brother/Father/Mother/Sister, the sounds /a/ /b/ /m/ /f/ /s/ with picture words (apple, ant, axe; bag, book, bat; fish, fox, foot; moon, mouse, match; sandal, sun, snake), family words (father, mother, sister, brother, then grandfather, grandmother, uncle, aunt, son, daughter) and the "My Family" song. Word Study: classroom objects (bag, book, chair, desk, duster, eraser, pen, pencil, ruler, table) and more family words. Reading: initial sounds s a t i p n m f b in words, letter-ordering to make words (ant, arm, name, bag, pen), missing-letter family words. Writing: tracing dotted letters and family words, colouring letter pairs.
- **Unit 2 — Times of The Day (pp. 27–42)** (unit opener headed "Times of a Day"). Morning/afternoon/evening/night with the matching greetings; numbers 0–12 with counting song; letters Cc Hh Ll Mm Oo and the sounds of l, c, m, h, o; asking and telling the time ("What time is it? — It is 1 o'clock", clock-face reading to the hour only); greetings and self-introduction dialogues giving name, age and home town (Gambella, Jimma, Somali region, Assosa, Mekele, Dire Dawa). Writing is copying letters and the words minute, clock, hour, late, o'clock.
- **Unit 3 — Colours (pp. 43–52).** Eight colours (green, blue, red, yellow, purple, black, brown, pink — capitalisation inconsistent as printed) with a colours song; initial-sound matching for g, j, r, y and for b, c, f, h, l, m, s; talking about oneself and others ("My father's name is …", "Let me introduce …", "He is my father / She is my mother"); completing sentences with is/am/are; the pattern "What colour is this? — It is green colour" (as printed). Writing: copying colour words, unscrambling letters, reordering jumbled sentences.
- **Unit 4 — Plants (pp. 53–68).** Parts of a plant (fruit, leaf, stem, root) with a parts-of-plant song; letters Aa Pp Ss Ww and blending (arm, van, pen, pin, sun, sock, worm, web); a named grammar lesson on 'have' and 'has' ("I have a pineapple / She has a pineapple"; "What do you have? / What does she have?"); saying goodbye (song and dialogues). Word Study: plant, fruit, air, soil, tree, sun, flower, water, vegetables. Writing: copying letters and have/has sentences, reordering words.
- **Unit 5 — Domestic Animals (pp. 69–88).** The animal set donkey, horse, camel, goat, sheep, dog, ox, cat, hen, cow; sounds of c, d, g, h, o and blending animal names; likes and favourites ("I like … the most", "My favourite domestic animal is …"); a lesson on Wh-question words (what, where, why, how, who, which, when) with a dialogue that uses bought/did past forms; counting animals with "There is … / There are …"; word study matching; reading by blending; writing missing initial letters and unscrambling (tca = cat, ykdeno = donkey).
- **Unit 6 — Hot and Cold Weather (pp. 89–98).** Weather words (cold, cloud, "Fogy" as printed, hot, rain, sun, wind; hot, cold, rainy, windy, sunny, foggy) and the frames "It is … / Today is …"; letters Cc Ff Hh Rr Ss Ww; reading sentences with is, am, are, was, were ("Yesterday was cold"); writing by blending syllables (wea + ther = weather), spelling out words, and a short dictation with missing initial letters.
- **Unit 7 — My Role Model (pp. 99–108).** Sounds of c, f, h, r, s, w with hero vocabulary; role-model dialogues ("Who is your role model? — My mother is my role model. — Why? — She always works hard."); who/what/how/why questions; a listening text on Kenenisa Bekele with a fill-in word bank (see the error note below — the student book's date disagrees with the guide's); word study honest, brave, hero, great, strong, winner, fear, kind; reading sounds b, h, d, w, g, m, r; writing missing initial letters and copying from a substitution table ("My dad is my role model").
- **Unit 8 — Caring for Body Parts (pp. 109–132).** Body-part words (head, hair, eye, ear, nose, mouth, tongue, face, neck, hand, finger, leg, knee, foot, toe, arm, lip, chest, stomach, teeth) with the "Head, shoulder, knees and toes" song (singular "shoulder" as printed); caring for the body ("We wash our face", "We brush our teeth"); matching parts to their uses (eye — to see, ear — to hear, nose — to smell, tongue — to taste, finger — to touch); "This is a … / This is an …"; greetings and parting dialogues; self- and family-introduction models; classroom imperatives (sit down, stand up, come here, shut the door, open the window, open your book); personal pronouns I, we, you, he, she, they, it with am/is/are; reading the remaining letters (e, i, j, k, n, q, u, v, x, z) with elephant, iron, jet, kangaroo, nose, queen, umbrella, village, xylophone, zebra; final writing lesson builds am/is/are sentences from a table and writes has/have sentences about classroom materials. The book ends at printed p. 132 with no back matter.

### Grammar spine

No grammar is named as grammar except two lessons: 'have'/'has' (Unit 4) and "am, is and are" (Unit 8). Everything else is pattern absorption: greetings and self-introduction frames, "What time is it? — It is … o'clock", "This is a/an …", "There is/are …", Wh-question words modelled in dialogue, personal pronouns with be, classroom imperatives, and was/were met once in weather sentences. Phonics is initial-sound work only — letter sounds in word-initial position, blending of short words, no vowel-sound system.

### Honest negatives

- No past-tense teaching (was/were appear in one reading set; bought/did appear unexplained inside a Unit 5 dialogue).
- No plurals as a topic, no articles beyond the a/an contrast inside "This is a/an", no possessives beyond father's/mother's name frames, no questions with do/does except "What do/does … have?" in Unit 4.
- No connected reading text longer than a dialogue or song until the short Kenenisa listening text (Unit 7); "Reading" sections are word- and sound-level throughout.
- No free writing anywhere: writing is tracing, copying, unscrambling, reordering and gap-filling from first page to last.
- No days of the week, months, or numbers above 12.

### Contents and unit-by-unit account

**Front matter.** Title page, image imprint, Foreword, and a Contents (printed pp. I–III) that lists, for each unit, its A–F sections **plus a separate per-unit "Answer key(s)" entry** — the guide is organised as teaching notes followed by a key block per unit.

**Unit notes (printed pp. 1–218).** Each unit opens with the student book's Objectives, the Minimum Learning Competencies, and a **Language Focus** summary, then gives A–F teaching notes with the **full listening scripts**, and closes with the unit's Answer Key.

- **Unit 1: Sustainable Development (pp. 1–c. 22).** Language Focus — Listening: "Growth Vs. Surrounding" / "Development Observed in My Surrounding"; Reading: "Sustainable Development Goal" / "Sustainable Development in Ethiopia"; Grammar: transitional words, comparison and contrast, sentence fragments; Writing: arranging sentences, model paragraphs, interpreting charts. The listening scripts are printed in full: Text A "Multi-Benefit Projects" (an *Ethiopian Herald* extract on Addis Ababa development projects) and Text B "Innovation for Business and Development" (incremental, disruptive, architectural and radical innovation).
- **Unit 2: Time Management.** Language Focus includes listening "What is Time Management" / "Procrastination", and vocabulary "Word game; Analogy Exercise" (p. 24).
- **Unit 3 (contents title: "Evidence on Road Traffic Accidents").** The body covers 3E in full — the Language Focus line (p. 45) reads "Grammar: Tense balance in Conditionals; Participles and participle phrases; Misplaced participles / Misplaced modifiers" — although the Contents omits 3E from this unit's listing (see Slips). Writing includes arranging jumbled sentences and the narrative essay.
- **Unit 4: Natural Resource Management.** Grammar focus: "Tense balance in sentences with adverbial times; adverbial words/phrases" — tracking the student book's 4E.
- **Unit 5 (contents title: "Mechanized Farming").** Grammar focus: "verbals; Common Variations in Conditionals" — tracking 5E. The guide's reading titles here ("Benefit of Mechanize Farming" / "Role of Mechanization in Agriculture") differ in wording from the student book's printed reading title.
- **Unit 6: Green Economies.** Advice-giving functions and the green-economy texts; relative clauses tracked at 6E.
- **Unit 7: National Pride.** Reading focus lists "Mega Events" (the student book's reading is "Mega Projects of Countries") and "Longevity of National Pride"; vocabulary includes phrasal verbs; reported speech tracked at 7E.
- **Unit 8: Telemedicine.** Speaking focus: running meetings (agenda, minutes, decisions, reporting) and the structure of oral presentations; passive revision tracked at 8E.
- **Unit 9: Conflict Management.** Grammar focus: the verb *wish*; faulty subject-verb agreement; tense change in reported speech — exactly the student book's 9E trio. Reading includes a drama script on traditional conflict management.
- **Unit 10: Robotics (keys to p. 218).** Listening "Robots" / "Robotics in Ethiopia"; reading "Learning to Manipulate Robotics" / "Socially Assistive Robots". The Answer Key (printed pp. c. 205–218) works the student book's analogy set with explanations (e.g. the Indolence/Work and Ophthalmia/Eye items, p. 212) and the error-identification set in an imported US test-prep voice — "making Choice E the correct response" (p. 216), "You have a 50-50 chance on this one" (p. 217).

### Grammar spine

As a teaching companion the guide mirrors the student book's revision spine (fragments and cohesion in U1 through the common-mistakes clinic in U10) and contributes the apparatus the student book lacks: full listening scripts, minimum learning competencies per unit, and worked answer keys with explanations — the keys' explanations are the guide's most substantial original writing.

### Honest negatives

- **No exam-preparation support either:** the guide adds no model exams, marking schemes or test strategy for the national-exam year — it mirrors the student book's gap exactly.
- The guide never treats the band's untaught forms (present perfect continuous, future continuous, future perfect continuous as active forms): there is nothing in the student book to key.
- No student-book page references anywhere; matching is by section/activity numbers.
- The answer keys' explanatory voice is lifted from US SAT-prep material ("Choice E", "50-50 chance") without adaptation — teachers get no guidance on how those conventions map to the Ethiopian exam.

### Slips and errors as printed

- **Contents title variants against the student book:** Unit 3 listed as "Evidence on Road Traffic **Accidents**" (student book: "…Traffic Accident") and Unit 5 as "**Mechanized Farming**" (student book: "Mechanized Agriculture") — contents, PDF pp. 5–6.
- **Contents omits 3E Grammar from Unit 3's listing** (it jumps 3D Vocabulary → 3F Writing) although the guide's body covers 3E fully — a listing slip, not missing content.
- **Guide reading titles drift from the student book's** in the Language Focus lines (U5 "Benefit of Mechanize Farming" — also ungrammatical as printed; U7 "Mega Events" for "Mega Projects of Countries").
- **Answer-key typo:** "ceiing" for *ceiling* in the Unit 10 key's dictation answers (printed p. 211).
- Unit 1's objectives are printed with the formula "learners will able to" (p. 1) — the missing "be" is in the guide's text as printed.
- Unit 9's grammar section is listed in the Contents only as "Grammar: Revision", vaguer than the body's actual *wish*/agreement/reported-speech content.
