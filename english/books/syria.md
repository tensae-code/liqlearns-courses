### Grammar spine
Greetings and formulas (Hello / Good morning / How are you? — I'm fine) → "I'm + name" and "What's your name?" → "What's this? It's a + noun" → imperatives (stand up, sit down, open/close, touch) → numbers 1–5, then 6–10 → he/she with "He's/She's a…" and "Who's he/she?" → yes/no questions with "Is it…? Yes, it is / No, it isn't" → colours as predicates → "How many…?" → "How old…?" → prepositions in/on → "has" for possession ("Jack has a red hat") → "I like…" → "Where's…? He's in the…" with negative "isn't" → "There is/There are" with "Is there…? Are there…?" → this/that → jobs with "Is she a…?" → next to/under → possessive 's → can/can't → like/don't like → plural "they are / Are they…?". Phonics runs alongside: one or two letters per unit (L, O, T, C, A, D, F, P+H, I+K, N+G, B+S, M+J, R+V, E+Q, Z, W+X, U+Y) with four Phonics Corners on initial sounds (C), final sounds, and rhyming (-ox, -en).

### Honest negatives
- No past or future forms are taught anywhere; the only extended reading (the two closing stories) is nevertheless narrated in the past tense, so learners meet "lived / met / ran / put / held" as sight text with no teaching support.
- No present continuous, no comparative/superlative adjectives as a topic (only paired opposites: big/small, fast/slow, tall/short, hot/cold), no question words beyond What/Who/Where/How many/How old, no "do/does" questions except the fixed frame "What do you like?".
- Writing in the Pupil's Book is limited to drawing, circling and a crossword; all letter formation is left to the Workbook. Reading is word-and-caption level plus the two picture stories; there are no passages, no dialogues longer than four exchanges, and no Syrian content beyond the imprint — names, settings and stories are generic international (Tom, Mary, Peter; Little Red Riding Hood).
- No contents page, no wordlist beyond the closing picture dictionary, no answer keys or teacher notes in the book.
