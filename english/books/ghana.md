Ghana's set is different in kind: there is NO government
pupil textbook for English. The library holds the
official standards every approved book must teach —
the NaCCA English Language curricula (Ministry of
Education, Ghana) — plus the COVID-era Distance
Learning teacher guides and pupil workbooks. Rows in
the record: the curricula for Basic 1–3, Basic 4–6 and
JHS 1–3 (Common Core), and the Distance Learning
guides/workbooks for Lower and Upper Primary, filed
per grade (28 rows in all).

## English Language Curriculum for Basic 1–3 (NaCCA, 2019)

125 pp., standards-based curriculum document: content
standards, core competencies, indicators and exemplars
for Basic 1, 2 and 3, each year in SIX strands —
1 Oral Language · 2 Reading · 4 Writing · 5 Using
Writing Convention/Grammar Usage · 6 Extensive Reading
(3 in the numbering is Grammar Usage territory; the
B1 TOC runs 1, 2, 4–6).

(Read in full by the earlier Ghana front, 2026-10-25,
from the ministry portal copy; its detailed Basic 1
map is kept in the front notes. Basics:)

- Ghana's bedrock is STROKE PATTERNS: vertical,
  horizontal and oblique strokes for muscular control
  and hand-eye coordination (air-writing, then books;
  tracing/joining dots), with a teacher note to ensure
  correct sitting posture and correct grip of the
  writing tool — mentioned, not pictured (B1, Writing
  Strand, Pre-Writing).
- Book handling comes before reading proper: hold a
  book, turn pages, cover/title/author/illustrator
  (B1, Reading, Pre-Reading).
- Sound play is ORAL first: blend 2–3 sounds into
  one-syllable words (a-sh = ash, b-i-n = bin), segment,
  delete/add/substitute sounds (brat → rat; cat → bat →
  bet → bell) — all before letters.
- Phonics: the alphabet is identified IN ORDER aA–zZ by
  song/chart/cards, but the SOUND-teaching order is the
  teacher's choice, two letters at a time — Ghana sets
  NO fixed letter sequence (unlike Egypt's fixed start
  and Libya's groups). First blends: am, at; fan, bag,
  bed, pen; word families pat/mat/fat.
- Greetings are a standard, not a script: Good morning
  / Good afternoon / Good EVENING by time of day, with
  responses and body language (B1, Oral Language,
  Conversation).
- First sight words (chair, table, child; "the" in a
  sentence) and first sentences ("This is a mango";
  written descriptive sentences "My classroom is big").

## English Language Curriculum for Basic 4–6 (NaCCA, September 2019)

246 pp., same portal. BASIC 4 (p.2), BASIC 5 (p.71),
BASIC 6 (p.152) — each year the same SIX strands:
1 Oral Language · 2 Reading · 3 GRAMMAR USAGE AT WORD
AND PHRASE LEVELS · 4 Writing · 5 Using Writing
Conventions/Grammar Usage · 6 Extensive Reading.

Basic 4 detail (the frame the other two years follow):
- Oral Language sub-strands: Songs, Poems, Story
  Telling, Dramatisation and Role Play, Conversation,
  Listening Comprehension, Asking and Answering
  Questions, Giving/Following Commands and Directions,
  Presentation.
- Reading keeps phonics alive at upper primary: Word
  Families, Rhyming Endings and Common Digraphs ·
  Diphthongs · Blends and Consonant Clusters · then
  Vocabulary, Comprehension, Silent Reading, Fluency,
  Summarising.
- GRAMMAR USAGE is its own strand, word classes one by
  one: Nouns · Determiners · Pronouns · Adjectives ·
  Verbs · Adverbs (Basic 4's list, in order).

## English Language Curriculum for JHS 1–3 — Common Core Programme (NaCCA, 2021)

139 pp., from the ministry portal's JHS library. Covers
Basic 7 (p.1), Basic 8 (p.37), Basic 9 (p.69); each year
in FIVE strands:

- Strand 1 Oral Language (Listening and Speaking):
  Conversation/Everyday Discourse · Listening
  Comprehension · English Sounds
- Strand 2 Reading (incl. Summarising)
- Strand 3 Grammar Usage: Grammar · Punctuation and
  Capitalisation · Vocabulary
- Strand 4 Writing: Production and Distribution of
  Writing · Text Types and Purposes · Building and
  Presenting Knowledge
- Strand 5 Literature: Narrative, Drama and Poetry —
  literature becomes its own strand at JHS.
