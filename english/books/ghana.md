## English Language Curriculum for Basic 1–3 (NaCCA, September 2019)

Ghana Education Service, Ministry of Education,
Republic of Ghana. "English Language Curriculum for
Primary Schools (Basic 1–3)," September 2019, © NaCCA
2019; 125 PDF pp. (prelims iii–xxi + body pp.1–104).
Enquiries to the Executive Secretary, NaCCA, P.O. Box
CT PM 77, Cantonments, Accra. Foreword by Dr. Matthew
Opoku Prempeh (MP), Minister of Education: a
standards-based curriculum built on the 4Rs — Reading,
wRiting, aRithmetic and cReativity — with core values
of teamwork, respect, resilience and excellence, and
inclusive, gender-responsive, learning-centred teaching
with ICT as a tool. Writing panel led by Dr. Rev.
Philip Gborsong (Department of English, UCC); expert
reviewers Simon Tsadidey and Daniel Hammond; the
printed panel list runs 1–10, then 12–18 (no 11).

Frame (pp.vi–xxi). Rationale: English is Ghana's
official language and the language across the
curriculum; children first need a strong foundation in
their first language. Philosophy: developmental theory
+ social constructivism ("meaning making"). General
aim: use English effectively, purposefully,
imaginatively, creatively and critically. SIX core
competencies run through every table: Critical
Thinking and Problem-Solving (CP), Creativity and
Innovation (CI), Communication and Collaboration
(CC), Cultural Identity and Global Citizenship (CG),
Personal Development and Leadership (PL), Digital
Literacy (DL). Three learning domains: Knowledge,
Understanding and Application (a Bloom-style ladder —
knowing, understanding, applying, analysing,
synthesising, evaluating, creating); Language Skills
(listening and reading = receptive; speaking and
writing = productive); Attitudes and Values
(curiosity, perseverance, flexibility, respect for
evidence, reflection; values: respect, diversity,
equity, excellence, teamwork, truth and integrity).
Assessment: formative (as learning / for learning) and
summative (of learning); criterion-referenced — each
indicator is a criterion. TIME: ten 30-minute periods
a week, two a day. Organisation: strands →
sub-strands → content standards → indicators →
exemplars, with reference numbers of the form
B1.2.3.1.1 (year.strand.sub-strand.standard.indicator).
Note to the Teacher: skills are taught INTEGRATED;
at primary level grammar is "basically internalised"
— learners must NOT be burdened with grammatical
terminology, definitions or lengthy explanations;
every learner reads FIVE books a term (fifteen a year).
Printed reading-topic lists for Primary 1–3, 4–5 and 6
(malaria, healthy living, safety, forests, values,
energy, computers, climate change in the lower years;
drug abuse, disasters, HIV/AIDS, teenage pregnancy,
entrepreneurship added higher up).

Each year is built in FIVE printed strands — 1 Oral
Language · 2 Reading · 4 Writing · 5 Using Writing
Conventions/Grammar Usage · 6 Extensive Reading.
There is NO Strand 3 in the body: grammar at word and
phrase level exists only inside Strand 5 at this
phase (the Scope and Sequence grid, pp.1–4, does print
a "Strand 3 Grammar Usage at Word and Phrase Levels"
row set — nouns, determiners, pronouns, adjectives,
verbs, adverbs, idiomatic expressions, conjunctions,
modals, prepositions, adjective phrase, adverb phrase,
direct and reported speech — checkmarked across
B1–B6). Sub-strand numbers are the strand-wide master
numbers, so a year shows only the sub-strands it
teaches (gaps in the numbering are normal here).

### Basic 1 (body pp.6–37)

STRAND 1 ORAL LANGUAGE — ten sub-strands.
- Songs (B1.1.1.1): sing familiar songs with expression,
  clap/tap/dance to the rhythm, answer questions on
  them, discuss the moral lesson.
- Rhymes (B1.1.2.1): recite rhymes and tongue-twisters
  with actions; echo-read; identify rhyming words;
  recycle rhymes over days to memorise.
- Story Telling (standard printed B1.1.4.1, indicators
  numbered B1.1.3.1.x): identify characters and their
  roles from story pictures; retell in a chain;
  personal response ("I like the story because…").
- Dramatisation and Role-Play (B1.1.5.1): act stories
  heard, take character roles, talk about the roles.
- Conversation (B1.1.6): GREETINGS BY TIME OF DAY —
  Morning "Good morning," Afternoon "Good afternoon,"
  Evening "Good evening," with correct responses and
  body language, and the importance of greetings;
  describe home, people, places; talk about the school
  and its places (library, computer lab, headmaster's
  office, playing field); describe situations and
  events; collaborative conversation; likes and
  dislikes (food, animals, toys).
- Listening Comprehension (B1.1.7.1): before/during/
  after routine on narrative texts; sequence of events
  (beginning, middle, ending); characters; describe
  pictures and objects.
- Asking and Answering Questions (B1.1.8.1): Yes/No
  questions ("Is this a book?" — "Yes, it is / No, it
  isn't") and Wh- questions ("What is this?", "What is
  your name?", "How old are you?", "How are you?")
  with the printed intonation note: Yes/No questions
  use RISING intonation, Wh- questions FALLING, and
  answers to both fall.
- Commands/Instructions/Requests (B1.1.9.1): obey and
  give commands ("Keep quiet," "Hands up," "Sit
  down"); follow drawing instructions (a triangle in a
  circle, a square in the triangle); polite requests
  with "Please" and "May I use your pen?"
- Presentation (B1.1.10.1): show-and-tell (introduce a
  friend — "Hello, meet my Dad"); choose ideas on a
  topic ("My friend"); speak with confidence to small
  groups and the class (draw and talk about the
  nuclear family).

STRAND 2 READING.
- Pre-Reading (B1.2.1): BOOK HANDLING first — hold a
  book, turn and open pages properly, keep books neat;
  the cover carries the TITLE, AUTHOR, ILLUSTRATIONS
  and ILLUSTRATORS. Then ORAL sound play before any
  letters: blend two or three sounds into one-syllable
  words (a-sh = ash, b-i-n = bin, a-t = at, c-o-t =
  cot); segment spoken words into sounds (stop =
  s-t-o-p); phoneme deletion (brat → rat), addition
  (all → ball) and substitution (cat → bat → bet →
  bell).
- Phonics (B1.2.2.1): identify the alphabet IN ORDER
  aA–zZ by songs, charts and cards; recognise letter
  names and sounds at random; the teacher SELECTS the
  letters whose sounds to teach — "e.g. two letters at
  a time" — Ghana sets NO fixed sound order; sounds
  taught first in context, then in isolation (ants-a),
  with words carrying the sound at initial, medial
  and final positions (bag, table, bulb); blend vowel
  + consonant (am, at); blend sounds into words with
  letter cards (f-a-n = fan, b-a-g = bag, b-e-d = bed,
  p-e-n = pen); blend syllables (sis-ter = sister);
  segment two-syllable words (pencil = pen-cil, baby =
  ba-by, mother = mo-ther); decode known words in
  sentences ("This is a mango").
- Word Families, Rhyming Endings and Common Digraphs
  (B1.2.3.1): change the initial, medial or final
  sound on a phonic slide — pat, mat, fat — build on
  the endings, use the words in sentences.
- Vocabulary (B1.2.6.1): first SIGHT WORDS beginning
  with the commonest (chair, table, child; "the" as a
  determiner: "The boy is my friend"); ask questions
  to find the meanings of new words; use words from
  talk and reading in conversation.
- Comprehension (B1.2.7): use illustrations, title and
  headings to predict and infer; ask and answer
  factual, inferential and applicative questions;
  retell in own words with the Herring Bone frame
  (who? what? when? how? why?); the purpose and
  features of narrative texts; describe characters and
  their actions; connect stories to own experience;
  KWL (what learners Know / Want to know) opened at
  B1; identify story structure — beginning, middle,
  end.
- Fluency (B1.2.9.1): read short simple sentences at a
  good pace; recognise ONE HUNDRED AND TWENTY sight
  words including content words; echo-reading and
  vocabulary games (Lucky Dip, Pick and Say, Word
  Hunt).

STRAND 4 WRITING.
- Pre-Writing (B1.4.1.1): STROKE PATTERNS for muscular
  control and hand-eye coordination — VERTICAL,
  HORIZONTAL and OBLIQUE strokes, made in the air,
  then in books; tracing and joining dots if need be;
  the printed teacher note: "Ensure correct sitting
  posture and correct grip of the writing tool"
  (mentioned, never pictured); model objects of
  interest from paper.
- Penmanship (B1.4.2.1): copy letters of the alphabet
  clearly — the lines and spaces to write in, the
  letter shown on the board and in the air, practice
  in the air, on paper and in jotters.
- Writing Letters — Small and Capital (B1.4.3.1):
  match lower- and upper-case letters; copy names of
  labelled classroom objects in both cases; write
  words using capitals and small letters with proper
  spacing.
- Labelling Items (B1.4.4.1): draw and label three
  objects; draw a peer and write a line or two about
  them; draw oneself and write a sentence or two —
  invented spelling accepted at this stage.
- Writing Simple Words and Sentences (B1.4.5.1): list
  objects in parts of the school; write familiar words
  (the ingredients of a favourite dish, e.g. Jollof);
  use basic descriptive words in simple sentences —
  "My classroom is big," "The board is long and
  white," "The doors and windows are big" (two
  sentences each; exceptional learners four).
- Controlled Writing (B1.4.7.1): read, form and copy
  sentences from 3–4 column substitution tables; copy
  a short composition set in a table; match jumbled
  parts of sentences into meaningful texts.
- Descriptive Writing (B1.4.12.1): describe oneself in
  2–3 simple sentences ("What is your name? — My name
  is…; How old are you? — I am…").

STRAND 5 USING WRITING CONVENTIONS/GRAMMAR USAGE.
- Capitalisation (B1.5.1.1): write capital letters
  correctly; capitals begin the names of people (Kofi,
  Ama, Samuel, Sule) and their titles (Mr Badu, Mrs
  Darko), and other proper nouns — towns and
  particular places (Adukrom Primary School).
- Action Words (B1.5.4.1): present tense for habitual
  actions ("I wash my face every morning"); present
  tense for the present state of things ("My bag is
  red"); the present continuous form is opened (its
  exemplar text runs off this page in the printing).
- Qualifying Words — Adjectives (B1.5.5.1): identify
  adjectives in texts describing people; write
  sentences describing oneself and friends.
- Simple Prepositions (B1.5.7.1): position words in,
  on ("Mary is in the room," "Aba is sitting on the
  chair"); write two sentences on the position of
  classroom objects.
- Spelling (B1.5.10.1): spell two-letter words (go, so,
  in, on) and three-letter words (see, low, bow) from
  phonics; fill blanks in simple words.

STRAND 6 EXTENSIVE READING (B1.6.1.1): book talk /
book tease opens library time; picture and wordless
books, pop-up and flip-the-page texts; read
individually and in pairs.

### Basic 2 (body pp.39–68)

STRAND 1 ORAL LANGUAGE: Songs — INTERPRET familiar
songs in own words, with the moral values drawn out.
Rhymes — interpret rhymes and tongue-twisters in own
words. Story Telling (B2.1.4.1): identify characters
and relate them to real life; retell sequentially by
beginning, middle and ending; tell stories from the
learners' own cultural background. Dramatisation —
role-play stories heard OR read, with roles assigned.
Conversation: greetings for SPECIAL OCCASIONS
(birthdays, anniversaries, festivals) alongside the
daily time-of-day set; talk about events and things
at home and places in the home; talk about manners;
TURN-TAKING in conversation (speak after the last
speaker stops, or when invited); ask and answer
questions for clarification. Listening Comprehension:
interact actively with DRAMA (before/during/after);
sequence of events; characters, settings and moral;
share FACTS from information texts with the KWL
strategy in full (K before, W questions, share after).
Asking and Answering: Wh- questions with pronunciation
and intonation ("Who took my pen?" "Where is the
book?") and learners write their own simple Wh-
questions. Commands/Requests: give and respond to
commands ("stand up," "close the door"); polite
requests widened — "Please, could you…, do you
mind…, May I…"; respond to classroom instructions
(draw a circle in a square, write "A" in the circle).
Presentation: speak with confidence (share a friend's
holiday plans with the class); POSTURE, EYE CONTACT
and verbal/non-verbal cues (crossed arms and an
unblinking gaze signal disapproval).

STRAND 2 READING: Phonics climbs — blend syllables
into words; decode unknown words from beginning and
ending consonants, consonant blends and digraphs,
vowel sounds and digraphs and word patterns;
STRUCTURAL ANALYSIS opens: prefix un- + root clean =
unclean, root slow + suffix -ly = slowly, compound
words (class + room = classroom). Word Families:
rhyming endings by changing the beginning letter
(pan, man, fan); DIGRAPHS named and used — ch
(church), ph (photo), sh (shirt) — build words and
sentences. Vocabulary: sight words in sentences;
CONTEXT CLUES to infer meanings; HOMONYMS — "right"
(you are right / raise your right hand) and "bat"
(the animal / a tennis bat); picture dictionaries and
glossaries with guide words. Comprehension:
predictions DURING reading; factual and inferential
questions; retell with the herring-bone; purpose and
features of INFORMATION texts; describe events in a
story in sequence; connect ideas within a text; read
with LITTLE support; VISUALISATION strategy (form
mental images) named. Fluency: read with pace,
accuracy and expression; recognise 120 sight words;
use context to confirm or self-correct pronunciation
aloud.

STRAND 4 WRITING: Penmanship — copy SENTENCES
clearly, watching inter- and intra-word spacing and
letter alignment. Letters: copy words in lower and
upper case; use both appropriately in words and
simple sentences (the class composes "My Family" on
the board, then rewrites sentences with wrong
capitalisation correctly); space words appropriately.
Labelling: draw and label objects from a chosen area
(home, market, shops), edit and post for peer review.
Simple Words and Sentences: write a sentence to
express a FEELING ("I am happy"); write meaningful
sentences on objects seen on a field trip ("There is
a blue house near the school," "Taxi cabs are on the
road"); draw a picture and write at least a sentence
sharing an OPINION (smiley faces — the learner's
interpretation "must be accepted"). Controlled
Writing: sentences from substitution tables; match
sentence halves ("My house — is big"); RE-ARRANGE
JUMBLED SENTENCES logically. Guided Composition
(opens at B2): develop TWO COHERENT PARAGRAPHS on
one idea through leading questions (topic "Myself").
Narrative Writing (opens at B2): write about real or
imagined experiences by the process approach ("A
visit to the market"), a paragraph or two, posted on
the notice board. Descriptive Writing: describe
feelings; describe an object in 3–5 sentences.

STRAND 5: Capitalisation — capitals for particular
places and DAYS OF THE WEEK, and to begin the first
word of a sentence. PUNCTUATION OPENS (B2.5.2.1):
FULL STOPS at the end of sentences and QUESTION
MARKS at the end of questions. Action Words: the
SIMPLE PRESENT for habitual actions, simple truths
("Lemons are sour"), interest, feelings and opinions.
Adjectives: describe the weather ("It is cold"), show
quantity ("I have two pens") and position or order
("She is on the first row"). Prepositions: in and on
for days, dates and places ("I saw a bird on
Monday," "I have a test on 1st May, 2018," "They are
in the classroom"); position and time sentences.
SIMPLE SENTENCES OPEN (B2.5.9.1): the structure named
as (Name) + (Doing word) — "Yaa came," "Musa ate" —
and learners identify NOUNS and VERBS inside simple
sentences ("Ofori danced — Who danced? What did
Ofori do?"). Spelling: phonetically irregular words
by the pick-and-spell game (the, face).

STRAND 6 EXTENSIVE READING: the Author's Chair opens
library time; narratives and pop-up/flip texts;
E-BOOKS introduced if available; learners choose
their own books each session.

### Basic 3 (body pp.70–102)

STRAND 1 ORAL LANGUAGE: Songs — talk about the
BENEFITS of songs (patriotic songs, "The National
Anthem"). Rhymes — COMPOSE short rhymes and
tongue-twisters in groups and recite them.
Story Telling: respond to and ask questions on
stories (Ananse stories as the printed examples —
"Ananse and the family," "Ananse and the wisdom
pot"); tell PARALLEL stories; create own events to
EXTEND a story. Dramatisation: act stories heard,
read or created, then comment on the performances.
Conversation: special-occasion greetings in groups;
talk about events in the COMMUNITY (durbar, festival,
naming ceremony, birthday) and important community
places (community centre, lorry station, mission
house, market, fire service station); wider words
for CUSTOMS AND CULTURAL VALUES; turn-taking on
topics like sanitation, bush fires, flooding,
pollution; clarification questions (climate change as
the example); communicate with more varied and
accurate language — APOLOGISING, AGREEING AND
DISAGREEING. Listening Comprehension: information
texts by the full KWL routine; sequence of events;
share facts heard. Asking and Answering:
clarification questions on balloted topics; INVERSION
questions with pronunciation and intonation ("He is
going. — Is he going?"), and learners write answers
to inversion questions. Commands/Instructions/
Directions: SEQUENCED instructions (First… Next…
Then… — a square, a rectangle inside it, an oval
inside that); DIRECTIONS with flash cards (go
straight, turn left/right) to locate objects and
places; polite requests from pictures, accepting or
declining ("Yes, of course," "No problem," "I'm
sorry, I can't"). Presentation: present ideas
LOGICALLY (what learners would like to be important
for, and why); verbal and non-verbal cues — nodding
and smiling show approval.

STRAND 2 READING: Phonics — spelling–sound
relationship in dictation; decode with consonant
blends and digraphs, vowel sounds, vowel digraphs
and word patterns (the note lists bl, br, ch, ck, cl,
cr, dr, fl, fr, gh, gl and the vowel digraphs ai, ee,
ea, ie, ei, oo, oe, ue, oi, au as printed); structural
analysis (syllables, prefixes, suffixes, root words).
Word Families: rhyming endings (pin, sin, fin);
digraphs sh, ch, gh (Ghana), ph in sentences.
DIPHTHONGS OPEN as their own sub-strand (B3.2.4.1):
ou in out, loud, cloud; generate diphthongs and build
words. BLENDS AND CONSONANT CLUSTERS open
(B3.2.5.1): bl (black), br (brush), st (stone), tr
(tree). Vocabulary: sight words in meaningful
COMPLEX sentences; use prefixes and suffixes to
interpret unfamiliar words (its indicator carries no
exemplar bullets in the printing); content words and
function words; dictionaries, glossaries and the
internet; learners build their own word albums and
glossaries. Comprehension: SELF-CORRECTION
strategies; factual, inferential and VOCABULARY
questions; retell, explain and illustrate with the
herring bone or story map; purpose and features of
POEMS, RHYMES AND FABLES; respond with own OPINION
AND VALUE JUDGMENT; visualisation (Bingo and
matching games); read INDEPENDENTLY. Fluency: read
LONG texts with pace, accuracy and expression, each
learner at their own pace; recognise MORE THAN TWO
HUNDRED high-frequency words with automaticity;
self-correction while reading aloud.

STRAND 4 WRITING: Penmanship — copy short PARAGRAPHS
clearly. Letters: letter-sound relationships;
DICTATION of words and simple sentences. Labelling:
list objects in a thematic area (The Bus Stop),
invented spelling allowed before editing. Simple
Words and Sentences: opinions on favourite
characters, with peer editing checked against
spelling, capitals, small letters and spacing;
feelings and opinions about peers and things; draw
and write at least THREE sentences sharing an
opinion (the fruit example: "Eat fruits every day;
Fruits have vitamin C; Fruits protect your body").
Guided Composition: two coherent paragraphs by
leading questions ("My Home"). WRITING AS A PROCESS
opens (B3.4.9): choose a topic ("Myself," "My
School"), brainstorm and organise (PRE-WRITING);
write the first draft in groups without stopping for
corrections (WRITING); REVISE against printed
questions (Is my topic interesting? Does the
beginning catch the reader's attention? Are all the
sentences about the topic?); PROOFREAD with CUPS —
C Capitalisation, U language Usage, P Punctuation, S
Spelling — peer edit, write the neat final copy, and
PUBLISH by reading aloud or posting on the class or
school notice board. Narrative Writing: story
structure filled out — SETTING, CHARACTERS, PROBLEM
AND RESOLUTION (the printed model is a road-crossing
narrative: "On my way to school, I crossed the road.
I looked left, right and left again…"); engage and
orient the reader with a context and a narrator.
Descriptive Writing: describe PLACES in a coherent
paragraph, built from happy or sad experiences.
PERSUASIVE/ARGUMENTATIVE WRITING opens (B3.4.13.1):
support an opinion with clear reasons in a short
paragraph — the class splits in two to argue which
food is better, rice or fufu. INFORMATIVE/ACADEMIC
WRITING opens (B3.4.14.1): write the events of the
day in simple sentences beside pictures; rehearse
and make RADIO/TV presentations in groups.

STRAND 5: Capitalisation — INITIALS AND ABBREVIATIONS
(WHO – World Health Organisation; K. S. Manu – Kofi
Sakyi Manu) and capitalising the TITLES of texts
("Ananse and the Wisdom Pot," capitals at the start
of content words). Punctuation: full stops in initials
and abbreviations (Mr. Badu; A. O. Boateng); COMMAS
to list items ("My friend has a pen, pencil, eraser
and a book"); the APOSTROPHE for possession ("Kofi's
mother has travelled"). Action Words: identify the
PRESENT CONTINUOUS in speech and writing ("Adongo is
running with the ball"; "Aba is singing," "They are
jumping"); the SIMPLE PAST AND THE PAST CONTINUOUS
open ("use simple past and the past continuous forms
of verbs to express past actions," taught one form at
a time by examples and drills). Adjectives: describe
height and length ("I saw a tall tree"). ADVERBS open
(B3.5.6.1): identify qualifying words ("He walked
quickly to the church," "She danced beautifully");
adverbs of TIME AND FREQUENCY. Prepositions:
directions and means ("We are going home by bus,"
"The monkey is climbing up the tree," "Mr. Badu is
travelling ___ train"). COMPOUND SENTENCES open
(B3.5.9.1): join simple sentences with the
coordinating conjunction AND — "The boy woke up" +
"The boy prayed" → "The boy woke up and prayed" —
then AND and BUT in groups. Spelling: phonically
irregular words by Pick and Spell (enough, answer,
because, bought) with fill-in sentences ("I drink
___ water," "My father ___ chocolate").

STRAND 6 EXTENSIVE READING (B3.6.1.1): Author's
Chair; narratives, EXPOSITORY and PROCEDURAL texts;
read a variety of books and SUMMARISE them.

## English Language Curriculum for Basic 4–6 (NaCCA, September 2019)

"English Language Curriculum for Primary Schools
(Basic 4–6)," NaCCA, September 2019, © NaCCA 2019;
246 PDF pp. (prelims + body printed pp.1–222).
Same frame as B1–3: standards-based, the 4Rs, the
SIX core competencies in every table, three learning
domains, formative + summative criterion-referenced
assessment, ten 30-minute periods a week, strands →
sub-strands → content standards → indicators →
exemplars, reference numbers of the form B4.2.3.1.1.
The Note to the Teacher repeats the B1–3 note:
skills INTEGRATED, primary grammar "basically
internalised" without burdening learners with
terminology, every learner reads FIVE books a term.
The big structural change at upper primary: each
year now has SIX strands — 1 Oral Language ·
2 Reading · 3 GRAMMAR USAGE AT WORD AND PHRASE
LEVELS (its own strand from B4 on; the B4–6 TOC
also calls it "Grammar and Usage") · 4 Writing ·
5 Using Writing Conventions/Grammar Usage (much of
Strand 3 repeated in use-form) · 6 Extensive
Reading. Sub-strand numbers remain strand-wide
master numbers, so gaps are structural.
Basic 4 body printed pp.1–69; Basic 5 pp.71–150;
Basic 6 pp.151–220; panel list p.221.

### Basic 4 (body pp.1–69)

STRAND 1 ORAL LANGUAGE.
- Songs: relate messages to personal experience.
  Poems become their own sub-strand: recite, discuss
  central messages and values, COMPOSE FOUR-LINE
  poems; a new poem is not memorised the same day.
- Story Telling: connections to personal experience,
  tell stories around themes. Dramatisation: whole
  or parts of stories, moral lessons discussed,
  write plays out of stories.
- Conversation: describe objects, people, events;
  dates of birth in two formats; TELLING THE TIME —
  "half past 8, quarter past 8, quarter to 8"
  (p.6); names of regions; collaborative conversation
  with unfamiliar audiences; turn-taking rules.
- Listening Comprehension: stress, rhythm and
  intonation; main idea/gist; sequence of events;
  background knowledge (KWL's K and W); opinions;
  problems and solutions; compare and contrast.
- Asking and Answering Questions: Yes/No and Wh-
  forms consolidated; QUESTION TAGS OPEN — positive
  and negative tags with auxiliaries, e.g. "You
  have a pen, don't you?" (p.12).
- Commands/Instructions/Directions: instructions
  and manuals for games; polite requests to
  unfamiliar people; directions with landmarks.
- Presentation: spoken-language features named —
  contractions, ellipsis; formal vs informal;
  meaning through pace, stress and tone (news
  reporting); speak and read aloud at a good pace
  with expression; plan speeches (purpose, audience,
  structure, register, supporting examples).

STRAND 2 READING.
- Phonics kept alive: ending sounds, multisyllabic
  words; digraphs sh (ship), ch (rich), ck (lock);
  structural analysis widens — prefixes mis- (misuse),
  en- (enjoy), anti- (anticlockwise), de- (decompose),
  poly- (polyclinic), and the notes ante-, bi-, ex-.
- Word Families: -at, -all, -it, -ot families in
  context. Diphthongs: CLOSING DIPHTHONGS /ei/ /ai/
  /oi/ with the printed examples "ei — take, oi —
  boy, ai — why/try." Blends taught through the
  "Tapping Out" finger game (p.21) — tap each sound
  on the fingers to decode.
- Vocabulary: content words vs function words;
  the terms list is now explicit — compound word,
  idiom, simile, synonym, antonym, prefix, suffix,
  phrasal verb; homonym pair flour/flower (p.24);
  meaning from context and word relationships;
  word formation by compounding; jokes, riddles and
  puns (the coal-pot riddle is the printed example).
- Comprehension: construct meaning, note main ideas
  in sequence, SQ3R named in full (Survey, Question,
  Read, Recite, Review), skim/scan in 3–4 minutes,
  silent close reading by Directed Reading Activity,
  critical reading — factual, implied, judgment,
  evaluation, personal response; compare/contrast
  events, characters, settings; text structure
  (introduction, body, conclusion).
- Silent Reading gets its own sub-strand; Fluency:
  three-stage approach (Before, While, After
  Reading), read aloud reflecting the author's
  purpose. Summarising: oral first, then written,
  main ideas in sequence order.

STRAND 3 GRAMMAR USAGE — the word classes in order.
- Nouns: common, proper, collective, abstract;
  collective nouns take a singular verb.
- Determiners: a/an/the; ZERO ARTICLE for words
  like music, soup, soap; quantifiers; possessives
  filed here; demonstratives; interrogative
  determiners which/whose.
- Pronouns: personal and interrogative.
- Adjectives: comparison of regular adjectives;
  SLIP recorded as printed (p.39): "Regular
  adjectives form their imperative by adding er"
  (for comparative).
- Verbs: main vs auxiliary; subject-verb agreement
  including collective nouns singular; SIMPLE
  PRESENT's five uses; regular past -d/-ed plus the
  irregular note; imperative.
- Adverbs: time and place (its time indicator's
  exemplar wrongly repeats "adverbs of place,"
  recorded as printed).
- Idiomatic Expressions; Conjunctions: and, but,
  or, nor — the combination drill joins sentences
  about fufu or kenkey.
- Modals: the full meaning list — can (ability),
  may (permission, politeness, possibility), must
  (obligation), shall/will (prediction, intention,
  determination), could, would, might, should,
  used to, have to, ought to, need to.
- Prepositions: direction, time, purpose,
  possession, comparison, support/opposition.

STRAND 4 WRITING.
- Penmanship: JOINED HANDWRITING in four stages —
  flick letters → join pairs → join all the letters
  in words → copy sentences and passages.
- Paragraph Development: the four modes named —
  expository, narrative, description, argument;
  main idea vs supporting details; cohesive devices.
- Writing as a Process at one-paragraph scale:
  pre-writing (brainstorm, mind map, outline),
  drafting, revising against printed questions,
  editing/proofreading (capitalisation, punctuation,
  spelling), publishing (class magazine, notice
  board, The Junior Graphic).
- Narrative Writing: first person, then rewritten
  in third person; settings, characters, plot,
  direct speech. Creative/Free Writing: four- or
  five-line poems, imaginative stories. Descriptive
  Writing: sensory details, simile and metaphor.
- Argumentative Writing: the motion "A doctor is
  more important than a farmer" (p.55); claim,
  reasons, evidence, formal style, conclusion;
  debate features. Expository/Informative: write
  the school's history; TV news written from
  pictures. Letter Writing OPENS: informal/friendly
  letters only.

STRAND 5 largely repeats Strand 3 in use-form
(nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs, prepositions,
conjunctions) and adds: capitalisation in direct
speech; comma rules (before and after Yes/No;
after addressing a person — "Kofi, can you help
me?"); subject and predicate; invented spelling
tolerated for fluency; the "Spelling Champion"
game built from word clues.

STRAND 6 EXTENSIVE READING: read widely and present
a TWO-PARAGRAPH SUMMARY of each book read.

### Basic 5 (body pp.71–150)

STRAND 1 ORAL LANGUAGE.
- Songs: explain central messages, compose songs
  around values. Poems: compose SIX-LINE poems
  (the line count grows by year: 4 at B4, 6 at B5,
  8 at B6). Story Telling: the story-element grid —
  main character, setting, events; connections and
  themes. Dramatisation: COSTUME added; learners
  develop sketches from stories and justify the
  central messages.
- Conversation: conversation WITH ADULTS on social
  issues, modelled on a talk show; the printed
  example topic is "Children should not WhatsApp"
  (p.78); sustained listening with eye contact;
  turn-taking; questions for clarification.
- Listening Comprehension: THINK-ALOUD strategy
  named; visualisation; cause and effect; draw
  conclusions; generalisations from main ideas.
- Asking and Answering: do/be/have question forms
  drilled — "Do you like lots of pepper?"; question
  tags consolidated — "The bad boys aren't in
  school today, are they?"
- Directions: direct a stranger using landmarks —
  to the chief's palace, to the Central Mosque.
- Presentation: DISCOURSE MARKERS (well, also,
  finally; "To sum up"); formal vs informal register
  ("I have not gone there" written vs "I haven't"
  spoken); speech structure named — Heading,
  Vocative Address, Introduction, Body; speeches
  of about five minutes; research speeches from
  print and non-print sources.

STRAND 2 READING.
- Phonics: HARD AND SOFT c and g (country/cell,
  gain/age); R-CONTROLLED vowels (or — lord,
  er — her, ar — arm, car, ur — burn); MINIMAL
  PAIRS (sash/wash, watch/catch); two-syllable
  blends (dr — hundred, bl — problem, nd —
  handcuff).
- Vocabulary: ACRONYMS (WHO) and CLIPPING
  (telephone → phone); the "Build My Vocab App"
  named as a tool; function words as "glue pieces
  of sentences together"; the table riddle — "I
  have four legs and a top… a table."
- Comprehension, silent reading, fluency and
  summarising continue in the B4 pattern (SQ3R,
  DRA, three-stage reading).

STRAND 3 GRAMMAR USAGE.
- Nouns: quantities and units — "a piece of paper,
  a pair of trousers"; proper nouns for FESTIVALS
  (Christmas, Homowo); count/non-count; regular
  plurals -s/-es against irregular child–children,
  ox–oxen, man–men, goose–geese; plurals without
  markers (sheep, people); collective adds audience,
  congregation; abstract adds ghost, holiness.
- Determiners: zero article named as such;
  quantifiers add ordinals, both/each/every,
  another/other, fewer/less; possessives as
  pronouns — mine, yours, his, hers, theirs.
- Pronouns: INDEFINITE (someone, anyone, everything)
  and possessive added.
- Adjectives: irregular comparison — good–better,
  bad–worse.
- Verbs: simple present adds needs/preferences and
  thoughts/ideas; simple past for past needs and
  feelings; irregular past build–built; PAST
  CONTINUOUS for an action going on when a second
  action took place; imperative for instructions
  and directions.
- Adverbs: manner opens — "Mercy ate slowly"
  (p.116); then Idiomatic Expressions (take after =
  to resemble; to pull down = to destroy someone's
  reputation; "Kofi takes after his father").
- Conjunctions widen: because, since, so, although —
  "I'm late because I missed the bus. Although Afi
  is has eye problems, she doesn't wear glasses.
  He eats well, so he is healthy" (p.117; "is has"
  recorded as printed).
- Modals: the same full meaning list as B4, taught
  one or two at a time (p.118). Prepositions: the
  seven meanings with a worked sentence for each —
  position (by), direction (towards), time (at),
  purpose (for), possession (for), comparison
  (taller than), support/opposition (for you,
  against you) (p.119).

STRAND 4 WRITING.
- Penmanship: copy sentences in joint script; write
  COMPOUND sentences clearly (at least two subjects
  and predicates joined by a coordinating
  conjunction).
- Paragraph Development: the modes list grows to
  seven — process, narration, description,
  comparison, problem solution, classification,
  argument; the printed model paragraph is the
  children of Boti who build their own school from
  mud bricks and grass (p.123), its first sentence
  underlined as the Topic Sentence; elaborate a
  topic sentence ("Kofi is a boy") with details;
  cohesive devices — pronouns, repetition,
  connectors.
- Writing as a Process: topic from community
  issues; the printed organiser is a line diagram
  for "Poor sanitation" (causes/effects, solution);
  the drafting indicator says a TWO-paragraph
  draft in its heading and a THREE-paragraph draft
  in its body (recorded as printed, p.126); revise,
  edit with the CUPS-style checklist, publish in
  the class magazine or The Junior Graphic.
- Narrative Writing: PERSONAL NARRATIVE defined —
  "a true story told from the writer's point of
  view"; planning by Time Line organiser and the
  5 Ws Chart (who, what, when, where, why — "How
  could be added"); write about what was seen,
  heard, smelled, tasted and touched; the printed
  revising checklist (beginning/middle/ending,
  descriptive words, order, natural sound,
  feelings).
- Creative/Free: stories built from title,
  characters, setting, plot, conflict, dialogue;
  poems and plays. Descriptive: sensory details,
  simile–metaphor.
- Persuasive/Argumentative: the motion "A teacher
  is more important than a doctor" (p.134);
  claims with reasons and evidence from credible
  sources; formal style; debate.
- Informative/Expository: describe a process from
  picture sequences; expository pieces of BETWEEN
  90 AND 120 WORDS; write the event of the day and
  broadcast it as radio/TV news; publish on the
  internet.
- Letter Writing: informal letters to friends about
  events, in the correct format.

STRAND 5 (use-form repeat of Strand 3, with new
items): capitalisation to open direct speech;
comma to write an ADDRESS ("I live at Adu Street,
Adukrom") and apostrophe in CONTRACTION ("They
can't do the work") (p.139); nouns, plurals and
collectives as in Strand 3 (pp.140–142); verbs —
the Strand 5 line goes further than Strand 3:
SIMPLE PAST vs PRESENT PERFECT contrasted, and
PAST PERFECT taught (p.144); adjectives in three
forms; adverbs of manner ("Mercy ate slowly");
prepositions in the seven meanings; conjunctions
because/since/so/although; subjects and predicates
in compound sentences; spelling by phonics clues
(the Christmas cake game: "It is eaten at
Christmas. It is made of flour, eggs, sugar… It
is baked").

STRAND 6 EXTENSIVE READING: read widely and present
AT LEAST A THREE-PARAGRAPH SUMMARY of each book
(the summary length grows: two paragraphs at B4,
three at B5, a one-page commentary at B6).

### Basic 6 (body pp.151–220)

STRAND 1 ORAL LANGUAGE.
- Songs: relate central messages to personal
  experiences; compose songs around values (love,
  honesty). Poems: relate messages and values to
  daily life; COMPOSE EIGHT-LINE poems (p.153).
- Story Telling: connections, themes (love, honesty,
  patriotism, hard work), retelling by story map /
  herringbone. Dramatisation: whole stories and
  scenes; express personal opinions on moral
  lessons; WRITE PLAYS out of stories and perform
  them; justify central messages in sketches.
- Conversation: talk about objects, personalities
  and events in the country and the world (role
  models, MPs, festivals); sustained listening with
  eye contact; collaborative conversation with an
  UNFAMILIAR audience — the talk-show model again,
  example topic "Children should not do WhatsApp"
  (p.158); turn-taking; questions on key details
  ("How I spend my holidays; My future career").
- Listening Comprehension: Think-Aloud and
  visualisation; sequence of events; questions on
  moral lessons; KWL's K and W used with
  informational texts (p.161); opinions; problems
  and solutions; generalisations.
- Asking and Answering: Yes/No questions answered
  with FUTURE forms — "Will you come to school
  tomorrow? Yes, I will / No, I won't"; question
  tags with positive tags, negative tags and
  auxiliaries — "It is cold, isn't it? / It isn't
  cold, is it?" (p.162).
- Commands/Instructions: create and present a
  manual on how to play a game; polite requests
  for help from unfamiliar people.
- Presentation: modal expressions for tentativeness
  (probably, not too sure); spoken vs written forms
  ("I can't" vs "I cannot"); news presentation with
  pace, stress, tone; research and deliver speeches;
  draw on prior knowledge of speech structure and
  roles (participants, facilitators, advisors,
  time-keepers); gather facts from print and
  non-print sources; register for formal and
  informal contexts; speak with confidence using
  verbal and non-verbal cues.

STRAND 2 READING.
- Phonics: ending sounds -sure (measure), -ture
  (creature), -tch (stretch); CCVCC and CCCVC words
  (trust, striped; scrap, sprite) and multisyllabic
  words (communication, immediate) (p.168).
- Word Families/Minimal Pairs/Digraphs: minimal
  pairs by one-sound and two-sound difference (road/
  loan; goat/coat/boat, wall/ball, train/chain,
  bridge/fridge); digraphs ch, ck, ph, ng, sh, qu,
  wh in sentences.
- Diphthongs: CENTERING DIPHTHONGS /iə/ (fear, ear,
  near, clear), /eə/ (prepare, there, stairs),
  /ʊə/ (pure, secure) (p.170).
- Blends and Clusters: three-syllable words; digraph
  spelling rules (ch — church, sh — shirt, gh —
  tough).
- Vocabulary: content vs function words in use; the
  terms list again; build vocabulary by substituting
  synonyms in texts and by vocabulary games (Word
  Hunt, Lucky Dip, I Know It) with a portfolio;
  meaning from word relationships — HYPONYMS
  (fruit – apple) and MERONYMS (hand – finger)
  named (p.173); compounding refreshed (breakfast =
  break + fast); playful words (jokes, riddles,
  puns); words fitted to purpose, audience, context
  and culture.
- Comprehension: the B4–B5 pattern consolidated —
  SQ3R in full (p.175), skim/scan, DRA silent
  reading, critical response, compare/contrast,
  text structure, contextual meanings. Silent
  Reading and Fluency continue; Summarising: oral
  summaries, then written — the printed example
  reduces "This is the boy who killed the snake"
  to "The boy killed the snake."

STRAND 3 GRAMMAR USAGE.
- Nouns: nouns and noun phrases for CONDITIONS;
  proper nouns for organisations/events; the full
  B1–B5 revision with drills — the pair drill
  (mango → mangoes, "I love mangoes") and the Lucky
  Dip of collective nouns ("a team of horses, a
  bevy of ladies, a flock of sheep," pp.180–181);
  "-ING" NOUNS for activities ("I enjoy singing.
  I like swimming"); abstract nouns (intelligence,
  knowledge, idea, patience — "I admire Ama because
  of her patience").
- Determiners: articles; quantifiers (ordinals,
  some, few/little, no/both/all, each/every, another/
  other, fewer/less); possessives; demonstratives
  for concepts and ideas.
- Pronouns: three NEW kinds — REFLEXIVE (myself,
  yourself), RELATIVE (which, where, whose) and
  RECIPROCAL (each other, one another) (p.184).
- Adjectives: comparative and superlative, regular
  and irregular (good–better–best), and the more/
  most forms (much–more–most; more intelligent).
- Verbs: main vs helping verbs (primary auxiliary
  and modal auxiliary); subject-verb agreement with
  indefinite pronouns, singular/plural and
  collective nouns (a collective subject takes a
  singular OR plural verb — the rule widens from
  B4's singular-only); irregular verbs; the
  INFINITIVE for personal activities ("I asked my
  friend to read the story"); SIMPLE PRESENT for
  SCHEDULED FUTURE actions ("The bus leaves early
  tomorrow") and future possibilities ("If he
  doesn't come early, we shall go without him")
  (p.187; the indicator is printed twice, as
  B6.3.5.1.4 twice, recorded as printed); past
  continuous ("We were playing football when the
  fire broke out"); imperative for warnings and
  prohibitions ("Break the glass, and you will pay
  for it").
- Adverbs: degree and reason added — "The shirt is
  too small for me. You are making so much noise.
  I sing because I like singing" (p.189).
- Idiomatic Expressions: the same two anchors as
  B5 (take after; to pull down), now also drawn
  from learners' own culture and first language,
  written on manila cards for the classroom wall.
- Conjunctions: so that, when, while, if — purpose,
  time, condition ("Take a hot bath so that you can
  sleep well. I drank the soup while my mother was
  in the kitchen").
- Modals: the full B4/B5 meaning list revised.
- Prepositions: the meaning set (time, purpose,
  possession, comparison, support/opposition).
- NEW at B6: ADJECTIVE PHRASES ("a very beautiful
  flower"; "a strong African oven") and ADVERB
  PHRASES ("He comes to the house every day").
- NEW at B6: DIRECT AND REPORTED SPEECH — the
  three printed direct examples ("We saw an eagle,"
  said Aba; "Esi is tired," said Mum; "Joe has
  become rich," said Atongo) converted by the
  printed steps: add the reporting clause, introduce
  "that," remove the quotation marks — "Aba said
  that they had seen an eagle. Mum said that Esi
  was tired" (p.195).

STRAND 4 WRITING.
- Penmanship: a legible, fluent and PERSONAL
  handwriting style; write COMPLEX sentences —
  built by combining simple pairs: "You will come.
  We will go. → When you come, we will go."
- Paragraph Development: modes (process, narrative,
  descriptive, persuasive, argumentative, letter);
  introduce the main idea with key words, phrases
  or clauses (topic sentence "Kofi is a good
  child"); cohesive devices for contrast, result
  and purpose (first, second, third, finally, again,
  also, besides, further, likewise, next, then,
  too).
- Writing as a Process: the topic is now a NATIONAL
  issue; the printed organiser is "Bush fires"
  (causes, effects, solutions); a THREE-paragraph
  draft with a wider range of cohesive devices (as
  a result, on the other hand, however); revise,
  edit, publish — The Junior Graphic or The Daily
  Graphic now named (p.200–201).
- Narrative Writing becomes REFLECTIVE WRITING:
  who you are now against who you were before, by
  the Then/Now chart with reasons for the change.
- Creative/Free: national issues and other learning
  areas; stories and poems/plays as before.
  Descriptive: personification and sound devices
  added to simile/metaphor. Argumentative: the
  motion "A lawyer is more important than a
  doctor"; claims in order of importance; the
  conclusion poses a RHETORICAL QUESTION; debate
  features add vocatives and taking a stand.
- Expository: short paragraphs on incidents
  (accidents, fire outbreak); write ARTICLES on
  varied topics. Letter Writing: informal letters
  expressing views, contrasted with formal letters.

STRAND 5 (use-form repeat, with new items):
punctuation grows — comma around a modifying
phrase ("Kofi, my friend, will help me do my
homework"), quotation marks for direct speech,
apostrophe for PLURAL POSSESSION ("This is girls'
work") (p.210); nouns add GENDER; verbs — simple
past vs present perfect, the participle forms, and
PAST PERFECT with its anchor sentence "When you
came I had already finished the work" (p.213);
essays mixing simple present, present perfect and
past perfect; adjectives in superlative;
adverbs of degree and reason; conjunctions so
that/when/while/if/unless; subjects and verbs in
COMPLEX sentences, and changing compound sentences
into complex ones; spelling checked inside
learners' own story continuations (the printed
seed: "Cinderella wore the shoes and she was taken
away to marry the prince," p.220).

STRAND 6 EXTENSIVE READING: read widely, visit the
local library, and present a ONE-PAGE CRITICAL
COMMENTARY on each book, judged against a set of
criteria (p.220).
