## Grade 12 — Study English, 6th Year Secondary School — FULLY READ + CITATION CHECK PASSED 2026-10-26

"The Islamic Republic of Mauritania — Honor · Fraternity · Justice," Ministry of National Education and Educational System Reform, National Pedagogic Institute (IPN), 2025 printing (Adobe InDesign 20.5 creation stamp Aug 26, 2025). Reviewed and updated by Cheikh ould Mohamed Lemine (IGESRE), Mohamed Yeslem Babah, and Mohamed Lemine Khabaz (English Department coordinator, IPN); proofread by Dr. Abdellahi Cheikh Khlil, Aminetou Deyoun, Fatimetou Sidi Abdallah. Foreword by the IPN Director General Dr. Cheikh Moadh Sidi Abdalla: English entered junior high with the 1999 reform; the secondary curricula were rewritten Aug–Sept 2020 on the holistic, learner-centred approach. SEVEN units, EIGHTEEN lessons. Every lesson runs the same seven-part shape: **I read → II I learn my vocabulary → III I explore the text → IV I learn how to use (the lesson's grammar/function) → V I can try it → VI I can interact → VII I can do it** — a group discussion with notes, then a written piece built from those notes. The year's spine is FUNCTIONS: possibility/prediction → cause and effect → argument → duration → reported speech → checking/confirming → congratulating.

- **Unit One — possibility, probability, prediction.** L1 The homework (p.7): Omar phones Brahim about the climate-change essay; **CAN/COULD box** — ability, probability, suggestion, surprise ("You can't be serious"). L2 Mauritanians and Superstitions (p.10): the regional-superstitions text (east — cemeteries and abattoirs; centre — dirt and ash; north-west — never call a name as someone leaves); **MAY/MIGHT box** — possibility, permission, wishes ("May your wounds heal!"). L3 A Picnic at the Beach (p.13): the three friends, the father's car, the weather forecast (rain in Nouakchott, fog in Nouadhibou), the postponed picnic; **WILL for prediction + MUST/SHOULD/OUGHT TO box** — must's four uses (obligation, probability, prediction, advice) and ought to's mild obligation. L4 What's Our World Will Be Like? (p.16): the future-world text (warmer world, fossil fuels, computers "million times faster," population exploding by the end of the 21st century); **WILL vs BE GOING TO box**; classify nine sentences by plan/decision/prediction.
- **Unit Two — cause and effect.** L5 An Invitation to the River (p.18): Sidi and Moussa; **because/so/thus/therefore connectors box**; write a refusal dialogue with reasons. L6 The Pandemic of COVID-19 (p.20): a full factual article — Wuhan, December 2019; WHO emergency Jan 30, 2020, pandemic Mar 11, 2020; "more than 676 million cases and 6.88 million confirmed deaths" as of Mar 10, 2023; symptoms, transmission, prevention; summarise each paragraph in a word or phrase; the full cause-effect connector set (because, since, as, that's why, thus, hence, therefore, consequently).
- **Unit Three — making an argument.** L7 Should Students Bring Their Cell Phones to School? (p.23): a one-sided pro essay (95% of teens have smartphone access, 45% "almost constantly" online; contact, safety/location, learning); **the argument frame box: claim (In my view / I think…) → 2–3 reasons (First… Second… Finally…) → conclusion (p.24)**; the writing task argues the OTHER side (why phones should NOT be brought). L8 Noura and Her Grandmother (p.26): Noura argues girls should do sport; grandmother Aicha refuses ("Sport is only for boys"); the opinion/agreement phrase bank (I agree / I disagree / you're right / you're wrong / Nonsense!).
- **Unit Four — duration.** L9 Hawa and Salma (p.28): the nurse and the new teacher meet again in Aioun; **PRESENT PERFECT box — form (have + past participle), all three forms, use, and the time adverbs since/for/recently/yet/already/ever/never (p.29)**. L10 The Interview (p.31): Abidine's job interview at BNM Bank (training in computer science, no house yet, "call you in two weeks"); present perfect re-taught through its adverbs.
- **Unit Five — reported speech, in four steps.** L11 A Friend in Need Is a Friend Indeed! (p.33): **the hare-and-her-friends fable told in full** (horse, bull, goat, ram, calf each refuse; she escapes alone) with the moral question (the keyed answer: in a time of crisis you discover who your true friends are); **REPORTED STATEMENTS in full** — pronoun/possessive changes one by one, then the backshift chart (present→past, past→past perfect, present perfect→past perfect, continuous→past continuous, will→would, shall→should, can→could, may→might, must→had to; would/could/should/might/ought to unchanged, p.35); the practice text is a sermon of Caliph Umar Ibn al-Khattab to report. L12 Nasreddin and the Three Wise Men (p.37): the tale told in full (the centre of the world under his right heel; stars = hairs on the donkey; tail hairs = beard hairs); **REPORTED WH- QUESTIONS** with their own backshift chart (p.38–39). L13 At the Restaurant (p.40): the Baccalaureate celebration lunch at the Prince restaurant, Tevragh-Zeina; **REPORTED COMMANDS — imperative → to-infinitive, negative → not to (p.41)**. L14 Looking for a Job (p.42): Abdou's tourist-guide interview (born in Madrid, grew up in Nouakchott, worked in Atar and Dakar); **REPORTED YES/NO QUESTIONS with if + the tense-correspondence chart (p.43)**.
- **Unit Six — checking and confirming.** L15 It's Unbelievable, Isn't It? (p.45): the journalist Moulay visits the class (grew up in Nema, left a medicine scholarship for journalism); **QUESTION TAGS in full — polarity rule, auxiliary inversion, do-support, and the special points: aren't I, Let's → shall we, commands → will/won't you (p.46–47)**. L16 Play Preferences (p.48): the child-psychology interview (boys — outside, large hierarchical groups, winners and losers, boasting; girls — small groups and pairs, best friend at the centre, jump rope and hopscotch, no losers); **question tags with main verbs + the nobody/somebody/everybody → they rule and the negative-words take a positive tag rule (seldom, rarely, never, few…; p.50)**.
- **Unit Seven — congratulating and wishing.** L17 I Have Got It! (p.52): Omar's exam-preparation call to Ali (drop the phone, understand don't memorise, solve former papers) and the pass with distinction; the congratulation forms (Congratulations! / Congratulations on… / I congratulate you on…). L18 Happy Birthday to You! (p.55): Fatou's birthday (January 10th, flowers, and she has passed her exam too); the congratulations-and-wishes bank (Great! Lucky you! Marvelous! Wonderful! Incredible! Well done! Good job! Best wishes! Happy…! Good luck!).
