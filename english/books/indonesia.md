## Grammar / vocabulary spine (Grade 3)
Greetings + name/age questions with be (Ch.1A) → I like/love +
activity verb phrases (Ch.1B) → family nouns, he/she/they and
Who-questions (Ch.2A) → be from + Indonesian place names
(Ch.2B) → present continuous for current activities, all
persons (Ch.3A) → in + room (Ch.3B) → there is/are + Is there…?
(Ch.4A) → prepositions of place + place adjectives (Ch.4B) →
this/that, in/on/under, How many…?, numbers to 50 (Ch.5A) →
whose, possessive 's, his/her, colours (Ch.5B) → job nouns and
What is he like? / What does he look like? + adjectives
(Ch.6). No past tense, no future form, no alphabet/phonics
strand anywhere in the book — English starts here as whole
spoken phrases backed by audio; writing is copying and
gap-filling only.

## Honest negatives (Grade 3 student book)
- No letter names, phonics or spelling work of any kind;
  reading is whole-phrase recognition of taught expressions.
- Numbers stop at 50, and 31–39 are never printed (the ladder
  jumps 30 → 40 → 50 on p.121).
- The alphabet song and days/months are absent (contrast the
  China G3 book, which teaches letters from Unit 1).
- Two internal inconsistencies: the front matter (p.vii) says
  Bara's family is from Bima, but Ch.2's teaching and story
  put Bara from Sumba throughout (pp.50, 52); and the mother
  is from Bandung in the chant and story (pp.49, 52) but from
  Cianjur in the front matter (p.vii) — the teacher guide's
  answer key sides with Cianjur (see the guide's section).
- Chapter title drift: Ch.3 is "My Family's Activities at
  Home" in the Contents but "My Family Time" in the running
  heads; Ch.6 is "My Teacher" in the Contents, "My Teachers"
  in the body.

## Grammar / vocabulary spine (Grade 5)
How many / how much + kilos, measures (a bag of, a
bunch of, a spoonful of) and countable vs uncountable
nouns (Ch.1) → utensil nouns + position phrases and
Can you…? requests, then recipe imperatives in sequence
(Ch.2) → direction imperatives and street positions,
then in front of / beside / behind / between (Ch.3) →
How much is it? with prices to 100,000 rupiah, totals
and change, and cheap/expensive, thick/thin, big/small
description (Ch.4) → can/can't for rules, I'd like to…
for wants, Can I…? for polite requests, and
because-reasons for book choices (Ch.5) → the past
tense as a system: irregular pairs (see/saw, run/ran,
go/went), regular -ed forms, was/were and felt/became,
inside retelling and rewriting (Ch.6). Against
Grade 4: prices and quantities replace clock time as
the number work; writing grows from guided paragraphs
to procedures, reviews and a full story rewrite; and
the phrase-only approach gets its first explicit form
notes (I'd like = I would like, p.124; circle the past
tense verb, p.157).

## Honest negatives (Grade 5 student book)
- Still no alphabet, phonics or spelling strand, and
  no future form taught — will appears only inside
  instructions and questions, never as material.
- The past tense is used in print long before it is
  taught: the hide-and-seek story (Ch.3B), the birthday
  story (Ch.4B) and the book-return discussion (Ch.5B)
  are all past-tense prose, three chapters before Ch.6
  presents a single past form.
- Comparatives never appear: cheap/expensive and
  thick/thin are taught as word pairs (p.108) with no
  cheaper / more expensive / thicker forms anywhere.
- Present continuous, a Grade 3–4 staple, is not a
  target anywhere in this book; it survives only in
  divider questions and story captions.
- Map and text misprints worth recording: the
  neighbourhood map prints "Chruch" for Church on every
  appearance (pp.63, 67, 73, 78); the Ch.6 objectives
  page prints the keyword "storie" (p.152); the Ch.4
  worksheet asks what Gita bought "for his sister"
  where the story says her sister (p.98, against p.96);
  the Indonesian rendering of the Ch.4 example price
  drops a digit ("12.00 rupiah", p.103); the wolf story
  is credited as "reltold" by Hennessy (p.169) and the
  mouse-deer source author is spelled "Baverley
  Randell" (p.159) against "Beverley Randell" in the

  introduction and background (pp.2–3); aims and
