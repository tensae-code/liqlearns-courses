Honest series verdicts:

- **The grammar ceiling rises slowly, and unevenly.** Grade 1 teaches no past tense at all and no systematic phonics. ### 4. Honest negatives

- **No past tense of any kind** — no *was/were*, no *-ed* forms; every verb is present simple of *be* or *have got*. The Book Map's grammar column confirms this ceiling.
- **No present continuous, no future forms, no questions with do/does, no comparatives, no possessives beyond *'s* names and *my/his/her/our*.**
- **No systematic phonics/decoding strand:** letters are taught as names plus one or two exemplars ("a is in apple"), and three-letter words are read whole in the boxes; there is no blending, no vowel-sound teaching, and no letter-sound chart. The only digraphs met are *ch* and *th*, in Unit 5, as reading items.
- **No answer key** (the Activities half has no answers; the Listening Scripts are the only back matter besides the Book Map).
- **No Arabic support, no glossary, no word list** beyond the Book Map's per-lesson new-language column.
- Writing never reaches free composition: the ceiling is copying, gap-spelling and reordering given words.

### 4. Honest negatives

- **No past tense of any kind — the Grade 1 ceiling holds unchanged.** No *was/were*, no *-ed* forms, no *could*; every verb in the Book Map's grammar column is present simple (first person, then *we*, then third-person singular of *be* and *have got*), *have got*, *can/can't*, imperatives, or *would like*. The two story pairs and the chick and Waheed stories are all narrated in the present. Starter 2 therefore sits beside Grade 1, not above it, in the filing's grammar-ceiling account: the past still enters the series only in Grade 4, capped there at *was/were* + *could*.
- **What Starter 2 does add above Grade 1:** *can/can't* for ability (absent from Grade 1 entirely), negative imperatives with *don't*, *there is/are* with *some* for countable and uncountable food, *like/don't like* + noun, *would like/'d like*, possessive *our*, negative *hasn't got*, subject pronouns named as a set, and third-person singular *has got / is* in descriptions. No present continuous, no future form, no *do/does* questions (questions are *be*, *have got*, *can* and *How many…?*), no comparatives, no numbers past 20.
- **Phonics is still incidental, not a strand:** ten single sound-spelling boxes scattered through the Lessons (listed in §2), one pattern each with three or four exemplars; there is no blending work, no progression and no decodable text. The systematic writing strand of this book is handwriting, not phonics: the Activities complete the joined alphabet (*i–z*) that Grade 1 began.
- **No answer key** (the Activities have no answers; the Listening Scripts and Book Map are the only back matter).
- **No Arabic support, no glossary, no word list** beyond the Book Map's per-lesson new-language column — the Grade 1 pattern again.
- Writing never reaches free composition: the ceiling is joined-letter formation, copying, reordering given words, gap-spelling, matching-and-writing, and drawing with a frame sentence — one step above Grade 1 only in that the hand is now joined.

The Dangeil treasure text — the book's one fully past narrative in the main lessons: in 2014 treasure was found in tombs about 2,000 years old under the village near the River Nile; one tomb held a beautiful box with large eyes and a silver ring for a woman; the people were very excited — taught entirely through *there was/were* and *was/were* questions. Helping in class closes the unit (present continuous with *or*: "Is the girl opening the window in A1 or A2?"). Writing outcomes: how often I help at home, and about the Dangeil treasure.

**Unit 9 — Here to There (pp.65–72).** Transport chant with *by* + vehicle and *on foot* (bike, bus, boat, taxi, train, rickshaw, plane, van, car); "How do you go to school? I go by bus / on foot." Ordinals to 10th applied to realia: two tickets are read in detail (bus, Khartoum to Atbara, Friday 8th March, 10:15, 125 SDG; train, Wadi Halfa, Sunday 6th May, 6:30, 400 SDG — fields as printed). The two-part Ali and Hamoor story: Ali and his dad ride their small, slow, young, strong donkey to an eight o'clock football match, leaving at two; villagers, a taxi and a bus laugh at them ("Your donkey is very slow. Don't miss the match!"), but the traffic jams, the donkey gets through, and when Ali laughs back his dad rebukes him ("Don't laugh at people and their problems, Ali!") before they watch the match together (plot summary only) — conjunctions *and/but/or* and size/speed adjectives are the language point. Bike safety with *should/shouldn't* (wear a helmet, keep traffic rules, stop at traffic lights, look after your bike; not: ride without a light, take someone on your bike, ride between cars). The traffic policeman's likes with *like/enjoy + -ing* (working for the traffic police, helping people cross, checking licences, controlling traffic; doesn't like giving fines). Hassan's "Schools Together" email (from Khartoum, Nile Basic School) models the unit writing outcome: write an email about yourself and ask for a penfriend. Writing outcomes: what I can see outside the window, and the Ali story retold.

**Unit 10 — Machines (pp.73–80).** Machines chant and naming (washing machine, phone, fan, air conditioner, bike, van, mixer, clock, car) with *What's this in English?*; riddles ("I eat dirty clothes for my breakfast. What am I?"). Kitchen machines with impersonal *you* and object pronouns ("How do you cut tomatoes? Use a knife to cut them"; cooker, kettle, fridge). The Daedalus and Icarus story in two lessons, read by Dalia with her grandma: the inventor Daedalus and his son are held by a king in a high tower on an island; Daedalus makes four big wings of feathers and wax; he warns Icarus not to fly near the sun because it can melt the wax; Icarus flies up — and the lesson ends on the moral spoken to the reader, "You should always listen to your parents" (plot summary only; the fall itself is left to the pictures and the listening script). Grammar is *was/were* and *could/couldn't* in sentences and questions. A Pedal Mill advertisement contrasts old and new flour-making (a kilogram of sorghum flour in two hours by hand, "It's slow… It's hard", vs ten minutes by pedal mill, "It's fast… It's easy") and teaches *Would you like…?* offers. Solar cookers are explained in present simple (women in Sudan cooking with them; mirrors shine sunlight onto a black surface; cheap, clean, no smoke). Inventions and dates: saying years (2010, 1930, 1850, 2018, 1995) and inventor sentences corrected from the listening script (Alexander Bell — phone, 1876; Karl Benz — car, 1886; Wright Brothers — plane, 1903; Konrad Zuse — computer, 1939), then cars in 1920 vs cars today (slow/fast, large/small wheels, noisy/quiet engine, hard/comfortable seats). Writing outcomes: cars in 1920 and today, and the Daedalus story retold.

**Unit 12 — A Second Look (pp.89–96).** Planned revision of the whole book. ### 4. Honest negatives

- **The past tense never goes beyond *be* and *can*:** the Book Map's own grammar column lists only "past simple of *be*: was/were" and *could/couldn't*. There is no past simple of ordinary verbs anywhere — no *went, saw, played, visited* — even inside the stories, which are narrated in present tenses or with *was/were*.
- **No present perfect** (only possessive *have got*, carried over from the Starter books), **no future forms** (no *will*, no *going to*), **no passive, no conditionals, no reported speech, no comparative or superlative forms** (size adjectives are used, but *-er/more … than* is never taught).
- **No continuous past, no *used to***; time contrast is carried by *now / last year / yesterday / today* with *can/could* and *is/was* only.
- **Phonics is incidental, not a strand:** six sound-spelling boxes in scattered lessons (see Architecture); there is no systematic phonics progression, no blending work and no decodable reader — the stories are whole-language picture texts.
- **No answer key** for any exercise; the only back matter is the Book Map and the Listening Scripts.
- **No glossary or Arabic support**; new language is listed per lesson only in the Book Map.

### 2. Architecture

The Activity Book shadows the Pupil's Book unit for unit and lesson for lesson: the same 12 units in the same order with the same titles, each unit containing Lessons 1–7 plus a Revision Lesson 8 keyed to the Pupil's Book lesson of the same number. The difference is density: where the Pupil's Book gives one page per lesson, the Activity Book gives each lesson about two pages (Unit 1 starts p.1, Unit 2 p.16, Unit 3 p.32, Unit 4 p.47, Unit 5 p.63, Unit 6 p.79, Unit 7 p.95, Unit 8 p.111, Unit 9 p.126, Unit 10 p.142, Unit 11 p.158, Unit 12 p.174; the book runs to p.189).

Almost every lesson is built from the same three block types, in varying order:

- **A/B written exercises** on the Pupil's Book lesson: look-and-write answers, complete-the-words/sentences from a box, write the words in the correct order, change-the-sentences transformations, match-and-number, tick/circle the correct word, draw-and-write-four-sentences, and personal writing lines.
- **"Read, say and spell"** — the book's systematic phonics strand (which the Pupil's Book lacks): each lesson carries one sound-spelling pattern with four pictured example words to read aloud and copy, e.g. *en* (pen, hen, ten), *oo* (book, cook, look, foot), *ch*, *v*, *st*, *y* for /i/, long and short vowel patterns with the silent-e notation written as "i_e" (five, drive, smile, crocodile), digraphs *sh, ch, nk, ct, mb, tch, ight, oa, oi, ou, ow, ai,* and initial blends (*br, dr, cl, fr, pl, pr, sk, sm/sn, sp, st*). Roughly two patterns per unit accumulate across all 96 lessons.
- **"Read and write"** — four short model sentences using the lesson's pattern, to be read and copied.

Revision Lesson 8 of each unit is also standardised: "A. Read, number and say" (six unit words against pictures), "B. Write the words in the correct order" (five jumbled unit sentences), "C. Read, say and spell" (one last pattern). The interior is printed in black and white (line art and grey tones), unlike the full-colour Pupil's Book. There is no Book Map and no Listening Scripts section in this book; some A-exercises are listening tasks ("Listen, write the letters and say") whose audio and answers live only in the Pupil's Book/teacher materials.

### 2. Architecture

Standard SMILE basic-level pattern, stated in the Introduction and followed exactly: units of eight lessons; Lesson 8 of every unit is a Revision lesson built on five fixed slots (re-sing the unit chant; a guided writing task; retell the unit story; "Add words" vocabulary columns; "Say it. Spell it!"). Most units carry two audio recordings (dialogues, chants, stories) and most include a story. Unit 1 consolidates the previous year's book. Every unit opens with a chant/rhyme (Lesson 1) that returns in the Revision lesson. Recurring lesson furniture: picture-prompted speaking, "Listen, read and chant/say", gap-fill readings, pair ask-and-answer with printed model exchanges, and a story in Lessons 5–6 of most units. Back matter: Listening Scripts (printed pp.49–52) and a Book Map tabulating, per lesson, Grammar / New language / Functions for Units 1–6.

Unit titles as printed in the body: U1 Back to School; U2 The Earth, the Sun and Space; U3 Clothes; U4 Animals Partners [sic — see Slips]; U5 Helping Hands; U6 Travel and Holidays.

### 4. Honest negatives

- **The past simple is finally complete — and that is the book's ceiling.** Book 6 is the first held basic-level book to teach the past simple of ordinary verbs systematically: regular verbs in Unit 2's timeline and the crow story, and irregular verbs in full (the diary's ten, the Machu Picchu gap-fill, the Book Map's "past simple of common (irregular) verbs" for Units 2–7). It is the step up from Grade 5's introduction of the past simple, and the step after Grade 4's *was/were* + *could* cap — but the tense system stops there.
- **No present perfect, no past continuous, no future form.** *Will* and *going to* never appear; plans are made with *would like to* (Unit 5 Lesson 2) and instructions with *must / mustn't*. The past is simple only; *used to* never appears; the present continuous is used for pictured and pictured-past moments, never named as a past continuous.
- **The passive exists only as two fixed phrases.** The Book Map (printed p.122) states it twice over: Unit 3 Lesson 4, "fixed passive form: is/are called"; Unit 4 Lesson 5, "fixed passive form: is/are made of"; Unit 7 Lesson 1, "passives in very simple fixed forms: is/are called, is/are made of". Exactly the Grade 5 ceiling, repeated, not extended.
- **No conditionals, no reported speech.** The chant's "If you go and visit them, can you also pay for me?" (printed p.25) is a song line, not a conditional lesson; no *if*-clause is ever taught. No speech is ever reported — stories are told in direct dialogue or plain narration.
- **What Book 6 does add above Grade 5:** comparatives and superlatives as a system across three units, relative clauses with *who* (first taught in the basic level here), *must/mustn't* and *should/shouldn't* as a pair, possessive pronouns *his / hers / theirs*, numbers to 100,000, dates said in full with ordinals, and the first model speech (Unit 7 Lesson 7). It is also the first held book to give a written history text per unit (numbers, writing, Meroë, the Taj Mahal) as reading-for-answers, not only as chant and story.
- **No answer key** — the Activity Section has no answers; the Audio Scripts and Book Map are the only back matter. Several Activity exercises are listening tasks ("Listen, write the letters and say") whose audio lives only in the scripts, and the Revision "Add words" columns have no key.
- **No Arabic support, no glossary, no word list** beyond the Book Map's per-lesson new-language column — the Grade 1 pattern again, now at a book whose vocabulary is far wider.
- Writing reaches guided paragraphs for the first time in the basic level (the diary, the invitation, the speech, the recycling procedure) — still framed and modelled, never free composition; and there is no examination preparation of any kind, not even a revision test.

### 2. Architecture

Twelve thematic chapters, each built on the same five-section frame:

1. **Section 1: Reading I** — a main passage (often with a boxed "read quickly and answer this question" prompt), comprehension, vocabulary-in-context and discussion questions.
2. **Section 2: Reading II** — a second, related text (advertisement, letter, dialogue, report, story).
3. **Section 3: Exploring Language** — (A) Working with Words (word formation, spelling, pronunciation), (B) Grammar Corner, (C) Time for Tenses.
4. **Section 4: Writing** — one taught genre per chapter (Read this → Step by Step → Over to You, with word counts).
5. **Section 5: Spine Challenge** — Fact File, Brain Teasers, Word Challenge, poems/songs ("Read and enjoy"), Words of Wisdom, and the recurring problem-page letters to "Aunt Layla".

Listening/speaking is woven in as pair work, role-play and teacher-read dictation-style items; there is no separate listening section and no answer key.
