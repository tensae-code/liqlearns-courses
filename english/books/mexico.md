# Mexico — English books

## Grade 7 (1° de Secundaria) — *Uérakua anapu uantakua. Projects and Readings. First Grade. Secondary*

**FULLY READ 2026-10-26 + CITATION CHECK PASSED (see closing block).**

- **What it is:** Secretaría de Educación Pública (SEP), Dirección General de Materiales Educativos. Primera edición 2024 (school year 2024–2025). ISBN **978-607-579-568-3** (printed p.2). Free distribution, sale prohibited. This copy is the **accessible format** ("formato accesible") edition from SEP's Conoce tus Libros platform — written for screen readers, with image descriptions spelled out and **the answers printed inline after each exercise**. Working copy: 1,818,864 bytes, 198 PDF pages; printed folios run 1–184 (the adapter's pages add the extra leaves). Printed page = the "Page N" folio in the text.
- **The shape is new to the library:** this is NOT a unit-grammar textbook. It is the Nueva Escuela Mexicana (NEM) project book: **twenty projects**, each owned by a formative field (Languages · Of the Human and Community · Ethics, Nature and Societies · Knowledge and Scientific Thought), each running the same project cycle (Identification / Retrieval / Approach / Planning / Comprehension and Production / Exploration / Integration / Broadcasting / Consideration — the human-community projects run Awareness / Inquiry / Building / Sharing instead). **The English teaching lives inside the projects**: most projects carry one Grammar box, some a Vocabulary or Reading section, all of it in English, with the project introductions bilingual (English + Spanish).
- **Front matter (pp.1–9):** title in Purépecha ("Uérakua anapu uantakua"); a long Spanish institutional introduction built on Paulo Freire (dialogue vs anti-dialogue) and Bakhtin, framing English learning as emancipation, not submission; "Know Your Book" (pp.5–6) explains the project parts incl. the English Sections (Grammar, Spelling, Vocabulary, Reading, Listening, Speaking, Writing); pp.7–9 print the INVEDECORG2.0 twelve-step NEM strategy in Spanish. Contents pp.9 + repeated.

### The twenty projects and their English

1. **Art and Healthy Living** (Languages, p.10) — art forms of the community (son jarocho, danza de los viejitos, jarabe mixteco) and health. **Grammar: SIMPLE PRESENT** box (p.12) — affirmative subject + base verb (+ -s/-es for he/she/it), negative with do/does not; 10-item verb-form exercise with answers printed; a Marco/David dialogue completion; a week-planner of daily habits.
2. **My Individual and Collective Identity** (Human & Community, p.20) — the four-circles identity exercise and a class mural. **Vocabulary** (p.23): school subjects (Spanish, History, Mathematics, Biology, Geography, Physical Education, Arts, Technology, Civics and Ethics, English) and hobbies (playing football/basketball, reading, drawing, listening to music, playing an instrument, watching movies/series, playing video games, using social media). **Grammar** (pp.24–26): YES/NO QUESTIONS — be-questions (am/is/are + subject) vs do/does questions, with the full short-answer sets; **SUBJECT PRONOUNS** (I you he she it we you they) and **OBJECT PRONOUNS** (me you him her it us you them) tables with definitions; question-formation and pronoun-replacement exercises, answers printed. Speaking: likes/dislikes dialogue (Alan/Pedro).
3. **The Amazing World of Coral Reefs** (reading project, pp.28–35) — a story by **Leonor Díaz** (pp.28–29, summary only): Marina and her cousin Delfín fall asleep on the beach and dream-swim a reef where Crab introduces corals, anemones, sponges, sea urchins, starfish, clams, turtles, jellyfish and sea horses ("Did you know that those little animals are fish?"); a shark approaches — and Marina wakes: it was a dream, and the real reef trip is starting. Speaking: favourite free-time activities. Writing: like / love / dislike / can't stand frames (p.30). A five-day itinerary speaking project. **Grammar: ADVERBS OF FREQUENCY** (pp.33–35) — position rules with be and with other verbs, affirmative/negative/interrogative; 11 word-order sentences with answers.
4. **A World of Words, Stories, and Readings** (Languages, p.36) — literary genres and subgenres; a literary festival project. **Grammar: WH-QUESTIONS IN SIMPLE PRESENT** (p.37) — wh-word + do/does + subject + verb, and wh-word + be + subject; a 10-set "find the correct question" exercise with answers. **Reading: "The Kindness Princess"** (p.39, summary only) — Princess Axanxy of Kourdom, the scepter of kindness, the sorcerer Waka's curse (unite the four kingdoms in three days or sleep forever), undone by the three guardians of the jungle; kingdoms of Water, Fire, Earth and Air reunited. Writing: wh-question interviews about favourite reading; write a tale of your own.
5. **The Relationship Between Food, Culture, and Identity** (Languages, p.46) — food as identity (atole, tejate); a Culture Fair on English-speaking countries. **Grammar: QUESTION WORDS** table (pp.49–50) — who (person), where (place), when (time), why (reason), what (objects/ideas), how (manner), what time (hour), how often (frequency), how old (age), each with a dialogue example; 12-item completion, answers printed; extra-practice QR bit.ly/3W1kiMG. **Listening** (p.52): the video "Anansi Goes to Lunch" (bit.ly/4fty5nb) — Anansi's three invitations (hippo/Africa, elephant/America, zebra/Italy) with the menus as answers.
6. **Technical Solutions to the Problems and Needs of the Community** (Human & Community, p.56) — physical inactivity as public-health problem (INSP figures printed: 84.6% of 10–14s and 46.3% of 15–19s under 60 minutes a day). **Grammar: PRESENT PROGRESSIVE** (p.58) — be + verb-ing, "right now / at this moment / at present"; the full singular/plural paradigm table; learners derive the negative and interrogative formulas; three progressive sentences found in the project's own text. **Vocabulary** (p.60): sports — running, cycling, swimming, skateboarding, volleyball, football, walking, yoga, dancing.
7. **Is That Reading?!** (reading project, pp.64–69) — an essay by **María de Jesús Prado Vite** (pp.64–65, summary only): Genoveva, who thinks she doesn't "really read" — everyone reads in their own way and pace; dictionary use is reading. Writing: daily routines by weekday; a dictionary discussion set. **Grammar: SIMPLE PRESENT vs PRESENT PROGRESSIVE contrast** (pp.68–69) — full affirmative/negative/interrogative structure table for both tenses side by side; two exercise sets with answers.
8. **Think Before You Act: Games and Decision-Making** (Human & Community, p.70) — a Modified Games Factory. **Grammar: MODALS OF OBLIGATION** (p.74) — must (strong obligation, rules/laws), have to (necessity, external factors), should (advice); the note that have to is more frequent in conversation, must more formal, "must only exists in the simple present and present perfect forms"; 5-item modal choice exercise (no key printed here).
9. **Let's Play With Words!** (Languages, p.78) — a poetry-reading project; no grammar box, four readings: "The Versatility of Hands" (pp.78–79, prose, MCQ); **"The Dagger" by José Juan Tablada** (p.79, four lines quoted in the book) — a shape poem (a dagger) about heartache, with shape/feeling MCQs; a **Shakespeare** biography paragraph (p.82, MCQ: plays and sonnets; wordplay; emotions/relationships/societal issues); a **Robert Frost** biography (p.84, MCQ: rural life vs civilization; clear and simple style; "The Road Not Taken"; four Pulitzer Prizes). Slang note (p.81): Mexican chido/chamba beside English "ain't", "let's bounce".
10. **Technology in the Development of Societies** (Human & Community, p.88) — the ball across history and a ball gallery. **Grammar: TIME EXPRESSIONS** (pp.93–95) — the three-tense table: present (nowadays, today, right now, at the moment, on Mondays, always/usually/never), past (yesterday, a week ago, last night…), future (next week, later, tomorrow); three exercise sets with answers (incl. the Yucatán asteroid 65,000,000 years ago).
11. **We Are Words, We Are Valuable** (Languages, p.96) — an Identity Handbook; opening story (summary only): grandparents selling tlacoyos in Huehuetoca who serve Haitian customers in French, classmates with Maya — languages as identity. **Vocabulary** (p.99): body-language gestures — kiss, bow, hug, point, raise one's hand, wink, nod, shrug one's shoulders, wave — matched to nine drawings, key printed. Three short language conversations to read (Italian/French, Thai/Chinese, K-pop). **Grammar: COMPARATIVE AND SUPERLATIVE ADJECTIVES** (pp.101–102) — the full rule table: -er/-est, -r/-st after -e, double-consonant (bigger, hottest), -y → -ier/-iest, more/most, good→better/best, bad→worse/worst; 9-item choice exercise, key printed (the key itself prints "funnier tan" and "louder tan" — see Slips).
12. **Trash** (project, pp.108–115; the book marks it "only indicative, not mandatory") — waste as a "glocal" issue. **Vocabulary** (p.109): a word-search (wood, trash, recycle, batteries, organic garden — also hiding metal, solution, clean, sand in the grid). **Grammar: MODALS OF PROHIBITION AND PERMISSION** (p.112) — can (allowed/possible), can't, may not, must not, each with a trash example; 6-item exercise, key printed. Printed data: waste in Mexico 14% recycled / 86% not (UNAM 2021), and UN figures (p.115): a ton of recycled paper = 17 trees; the richest 1% pollute more than the poorest 50%.
13. **Teenagers and The Social Networks** (project, pp.116–121) — a research project on teens and social media (use, socializing, pros/cons, security — cyberbullying and sexting named). **Grammar: COUNTABLE AND UNCOUNTABLE NOUNS** (pp.117–118) — countable (posts, comments, likes, shares, followers) vs uncountable (information, time, fun, popularity, content, engagement, feedback, entertainment); how many / how much; some, much, many; a/an only with singular countables; **IS THERE / ARE THERE**; two exercise sets, keys printed.
14. **Feelings and Emotions as Part of Culture** (Human & Community, p.122) — an Emotional Dictionary project. **Vocabulary** (p.124): a ten-word cloze (inclusive, appreciate, equitable, recognize, dictionary, importance, emotions, moods, mediate, feelings), key printed. **Grammar: WAS / WERE — the verb to be in the past** (p.126) — was for I/he/she/it, were for you/we/they, taught through emotions ("I was happy with my friends"); 4-item exercise with key; emotion list for the dictionary cards (joy, sadness, fear, surprise, frustration, guilt, jealousy, anxiety, melancholy, shame, pride).
15. **Reconstructing the Unknown Past of Women** (Ethics, Nature and Societies, p.128) — women's hidden history (the Candaces of Kush, Hypatia, Theano, Sojourner Truth 1851, Lorena Cabnal; CEDAW 1979; Mexico figures: 66% of women have experienced violence, INPI 2017; women's pay 22% lower, Oxfam). **Vocabulary** (pp.131–132): ten terms matched to definitions — suffrage, empowerment, activists, privilege, feminism, social movement, gender, sexism, patriarchy, equity — key printed. **Grammar: SIMPLE PAST with REGULAR VERBS** (p.132) — add -ed; negative didn't + base; questions Did…?; examples are all women (Curie, Sor Juana, Kahlo, Earhart, Austen, Thatcher); a 6-sentence transformation exercise, key printed. (Mexico note printed p.132: women have voted since 1955.)
16. **Indigenous and Afro-Mexican Communities: History and Contributions to the National Identity** (Ethics, Nature and Societies, p.138) — a timeline project; the Cristina Masferrer fragment (Cuajinicuilapa, Guerrero — Rosa's story, quoted in the book) and the resistance line (Gaspar Yanga, Jacinto Canek, Micaela Bastidas, Túpac Amaru II, Bartolina Sisa, Morelos, Vicente Guerrero, Juana Azurduy, Toussaint Louverture). **Grammar: SIMPLE PAST with IRREGULAR VERBS** (pp.147–149) — past forms that follow no pattern (went, bought, ate, heard, came); did/didn't frames; three exercise sets, keys printed (broke, flew, forgot, spent, began…).
17. **Where Does Our Food Come From?** (Knowledge and Scientific Thought, p.150) — crops and traditional production. **Grammar: PAST SIMPLE WH-QUESTIONS** (pp.153–154) — wh-word + did + subject + base verb, six worked Q/A pairs; **AGO** explained (counts back from the present, comes after the time expression); a 7-item "write the question" exercise, key printed. **Vocabulary** (p.155): pick the natural products (seeds, radish, asparagus, zucchini, artichoke, eggplant, cauliflower) against the processed ones. **Reading: "Agricultural Techniques of Ancient Mexico"** (p.156) — the milpa (corn + beans + zucchini together, 2,600 years ago), the chinampas, sun-drying; a true/false set, key printed.
18. **Individual Goals That Encourage the Collective Well-Being** (Human & Community, p.160) — a community health/environment manual. **Vocabulary** (p.162): environment set (pollution, acid rain, climate change, biodegradable, fuel, renewable energy, drought, litter, flood), cloze with key. **Grammar: MODALS in full** (pp.163–165) — obligation (must; have to, incl. don't have to = not required), permission (may formal, can informal), prohibition (mustn't) — every form affirmative/negative/interrogative with structures; a 10-item best-modal exercise, key printed.
19. **Collaboration, Communication, and the Satisfaction of Achieving a Common Goal** (Human & Community, p.168) — a Movement Fair. **Grammar: WILL and GOING TO** (p.169) — will for predictions not based on evidence, beliefs, and decisions made at the moment of speaking; going to for predictions based on current evidence and planned actions; the project then requires will/going to throughout the planning and reflection writing.
20. **English-Speaking Cultural and Artistic Manifestations Album** (Languages, p.174) — an album of art/culture from the USA, Canada, the United Kingdom, Jamaica and Australia. **Grammar: PRESENT CONTINUOUS** (pp.177–180) — am/is/are + verb-ing, affirmative/negative/interrogative, incl. the book's own extension: the continuous for work in progress toward a coming presentation ("We are setting up our display boards"); a 5-item completion about the album itself, key printed.

- **Back matter (pp.182–184):** the accessible edition replaces the bibliography and photo credits with a note to consult an ink copy; the colophon (p.184) prints the print-run details as XXXXX placeholders — this accessible file was issued with the placeholders unfilled.
- **The English spine of the book, in order:** simple present → yes/no questions + pronouns → frequency adverbs → wh-questions → question words → present progressive → present simple vs progressive → obligation modals (must/have to/should) → time expressions → comparatives/superlatives → prohibition/permission modals → countable/uncountable + there is/are → was/were → past simple regular → past simple irregular → past wh-questions + ago → modals in full → will/going to → present continuous (again, at the end). No alphabet, phonics or first-words material — Mexico's English in this record starts at secondary, exactly as the library record says ("no early English").
