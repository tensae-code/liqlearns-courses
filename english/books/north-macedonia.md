## Slips and quirks, recorded as printed

- Acknowledgements (PDF p.2): "If any have been **inadvertedly**
  overlooked" — the publisher's own misspelling of "inadvertently",
  printed in the North Macedonia edition's imprint.
- Contents' last entry is the Certificate at p.113, yet Checkpoint
  1 (printed p.10) sends pupils to stickers "on page 115" — the
  printed book runs at least two pages past its own Contents'
  final folio.
- The module's formal word set (Lesson 1) is father / mother /
  grandfather / grandmother / brother / sister, but the song
  (p.5), the project captions (p.8) and the Checkpoint sentences
  (p.11) all run on the informal pair **mummy / daddy** — both
  registers are taught, side by side, with no note marking the
  difference.
- Lesson 3's named presents are six, but Lesson 7's colouring
  page (p.9) quietly adds a seventh pictured toy (a plane) that
  is never named anywhere in the copy.
- Nothing else in the copy is misprinted that the read could
  catch; the book is a clean commercial production.

## New for the course

Most of this copy's ground — family words, "This is my …", "I'm …
years old" — is already stocked in the built English lessons from
other countries' books; North Macedonia now stands as a further
witness for those parts, and its sources entries belong in those
lessons' files when this filing is merged. What it genuinely adds:

- **Level 5, Conversation — `hello-and-your-name` / introducing
  family**: the self-introduction frame **"I'm Tracy and this is
  my family."** (Checkpoint 1, printed p.10) — a child introducing
  herself *and her whole family in one frame*, a shape the built
  introductions (name, friend) do not carry.
- **Level 5, Conversation — a present-giving exchange as a taught
  dialogue**: "Happy Birthday! This is for you!" → "A CD! Thank
  you!" (Lesson 3, printed p.6), backed by the Lesson 4 listening
  story (printed p.7) and the project frame "This doll is for my
  sister." (Lesson 6, printed p.8) — giving, thanking, and saying
  who a gift is for, as one small conversation block.
- **Level 5, Conversation — `whats-his-and-her-name`**: the named
  family portraits (Lesson 1, printed p.4) work the frame "This is
  my grandfather, Bill." through all six family words with names —
  naming third people inside the family set.
- **Level 5, Conversation — `how-old-are-you`**: Checkpoint 1
  matches children to birthday cakes by candle count (Ex.3,
  printed p.11) and closes on "I'm ______ years old." (Ex.4,
  printed p.11) — age practised through birthdays, with the answer
  carried by listening.
- **Level 7, Words for the World — `the-home` (family words)**:
  the six-word family set with its informal pair — father, mother,
  grandfather, grandmother, brother, sister (Lesson 1, printed
  p.4) and mummy, daddy (printed pp.5, 8, 11) — filed here because
  the built path carries family vocabulary with the home set; the
  formal/informal pair taught side by side is this book's own
  contribution to that stock.
- **Hands-on / culture stock**: the Craftwork heart card (printed
  p.5) and Activity Time colouring (printed p.9) as hands-on
  shapes for the early levels; the **Welcome to Britain** spot
  (Lesson 5, printed p.8) — Mother's Day / Father's Day gift
  customs — as culture material of the kind the no-level country
  courses hold.
- **Not stocked, honestly**: Modules 2–8 (Shapes, school day,
  clothes, pets, food likes, body, weather, the festival pages and
  the Picture Dictionary) are named in the Contents but are **not
  in this copy**, so nothing from them is filed or claimed. If a
  fuller copy of the printed book is ever secured, this filing is
  where their reads append.
