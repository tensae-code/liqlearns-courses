## Basics found, and where they are filed
1. **Greetings are the opening basic** — Week 1 (p. 4), before any unit:
   speak *Hi / Goodbye, What's your name? / My name's*; color the two
   hands — left hand = Goodbye (red), right hand = Hello (green) — and
   trace the words Hello / Goodbye; "My name is ___" (p. 5). Unit 1 is
   "Greeting and Numbers" (p. 14). → Filed: **Greetings →
   hello-and-your-name** (3rd country; spot-checked by Muse on p. 4,
   2026-10-25 — page matches exactly).
2. **No alphabet, no letter sounds anywhere** (full-text check). Letter
   work is only "Complete with vowels" inside whole words (H_ll_, p. 14;
   Sk__rt, p. 36). → Letter title gains nothing from Nicaragua.
3. **Pre-writing = cursive tracing**, from Week 1 — every model word and
   sentence in cursive, whole sentences by Unit 6. No pencil-grip page
   (Egypt KG1 p. 1 stays the only grip source so far). → Noted for
   Pencil and Lines: a different writing model (cursive-first) exists;
   merges when its turn comes.
4. Sentence patterns arrive as tracing only ("I have a pink skirt,"
   p. 37; "I like / I don't like ice cream," Unit 8) — traced, never
   composed. → Sentence title: patterns recorded, not yet built from.

## Activity Book 3 (primary English programme)

Ministerio de Educación (MINED), Dirección de
Educación Primaria Regular y Extra-Edad. "Activity
Book 3 — Let's Practice English Together!"
Compilation: Jamyn Efraín García Romero; technical
revision: Melkis Barrios, Margarita Soza, Anielka
Ofelia Ortiz Orozco, Orlando Alexander Rayo
(MINED); design and covers Martha García Pérez;
funded from Recursos del Tesoro. 64 pp.; printed
page = PDF page. The Presentación (p.3) anchors the
book to the Estrategia Nacional de Educación
"Bendiciones y Victorias 2024–2026," Eje 1, Lineamiento
8 (strengthening English as a second language) and
the Plan de Educación 2022–2026, with CEFR named as
the standards frame. SLIP recorded as printed: the
Presentación says the activities are "dirigidas a
niñas y niños de PRIMER GRADO" — first grade — in
the third-grade book (the sentence is carried over
from Book 1). Sources named in the references
(p.64): MINED's unpublished 2018 Mallas de Inglés
for Grades 3–4 and the unpublished 2020 Bounce Now
third-grade document, plus stock-image sites and
Cambridge's Little Steps pages. NINE units; each
unit is a run of numbered Activities (10–18 per
unit), with GRAMMAR TIP boxes in Units 2, 4 and a
story text in most units. Audio is assumed
throughout ("Listen to the audio") but no script is
printed.

- U1 The Alphabet (pp.4–10). The Alphabet arrives
  as a NAMED unit here, in the third book — after
  two books of talk and tracing (AB1 had no alphabet
  at all). Its content is the alphabet AT WORK, not
  an A–Z list: spell your name ("How do you spell
  your name?"), put words in ALPHABETICAL ORDER
  (Activities 6 and 12), and complete words with
  missing letters. The frame text is personal
  information: read about ANGELY — "My name is
  Angely, I am in third grade, I am eight years old,
  my phone number is 8264-9645, I am in classroom B2,
  in Teacher Claudia's Class, my mom's name is
  María, she is 28 years old; my dad's name is
  Elias, and he is 29 years old. I have two brothers
  and one sister Angel, Daniel and Mariana. I have
  two pets, a cat named Moni and a dog named Tarzan.
  I like to eat apples and I don't like to eat
  carrots. My favorite color is pink." — then write
  your own card the same way, and ask classmates'
  phone numbers ("What's your mom's / dad's /
  teacher's / classmate's number?"). The unit's
  second half is BODY PARTS: word search, a
  dice-building activity, SINGULAR AND PLURAL nouns
  with THIS IS / THESE ARE ("This is my crayon /
  These are my crayons… This is my glue," Activity
  11), labelling a body picture and listing the
  parts in alphabetical order, and the Momo the
  monster reading (a full descriptive text — three
  blue eyes, small pink nose, two pointy ears, wide
  mouth with big white teeth, four long arms, five
  fingers on each hand, two short legs, feet with
  three toes each) with draw-him, true/false and
  answer-the-questions exercises.
- U2 Let's do it (pp.11–14). IMPERATIVES. Word set:
  jump, run, walk, touch + floor, wall, door, window.
  The story "Alissa and Jefferson's Butterfly
  Adventure" is built entirely from the verbs
  (walk to the room, run to the butterfly, touch the
  wall, jump on the floor, "Goodbye, butterfly!").
  GRAMMAR TIP (p.13): imperatives give direct
  orders, invitations or advice; use the infinitive;
  negative with "do not / don't" before the
  infinitive; PLEASE makes it polite ("Sit down,
  please! / Don't climb the tree, please!"); no
  subject pronoun is used. Writing work: complete
  imperatives from pictures, a crossword, and the
  Miss Lily story with the blanks to fill from
  context (open your books to page 5, look at the
  big picture, read the first paragraph, close your
  books and look at me, stand up).
- U3 I want to be a/an… (pp.15–20). PROFESSIONS with
  the verb BE: the matching table of long and short
  forms (He is → He's, She is not → She isn't,
  I am not → I'm not), affirmative and negative
  sentences from pictures ("She isn't a teacher.
  She's a police officer"), professions matched to
  WORKPLACES with sentences ("A doctor and a nurse
  work at the hospital. A teacher works at school.
  A dentist works in the dental clinic. A chef
  works in the restaurant. A waiter serves food in
  the restaurant. A police officer works in the
  police station. A firefighter works in the fire
  station"). The reading "A happy morning in El
  Crucero" runs seven workers through the town
  (Mr. Lopez the chef, Miss Clara the teacher,
  Mr. Brown the firefighter, Dr. Green, Miss Lily
  the nurse, Joe the waiter, Miss Emily the dentist)
  with true/false; a job-responsibilities grid;
  six riddle sentences to order ("He isn't a
  dancer. He flies planes."); a 14-word profession
  word search; "What do you want to be?" listening
  and a Find Someone Who. SLIPS recorded as
  printed: the listening options offer "He wants
  to be a FIRE DENTIST" (Activity 11), and the
  name labels (Alyssa, Damian, Javiera, then Isaac,
  Andrea) do not line up in order.
- U4 At School (pp.21–27). School supplies with
  HAVE/HAS. The reading "Tom and Lucy's School
  Supplies" is a colour-and-count text (Tom's big
  blue backpack, red pencil case, two blue felt
  pens, yellow pencil sharpener; Lucy's pink
  backpack, green glue stick, purple scissors,
  orange pencil case, three brown pencils).
  GRAMMAR TIP (p.22): HAVE for I, you, we, they and
  plural nouns; HAS for he, she, it, singular nouns
  and a person's name ("They have backpacks. She
  has a lunch box."). Second GRAMMAR TIP (p.24):
  DON'T / DOESN'T for things we don't possess, with
  four worked contrasts ("They don't have a lunch
  box, they have a pencil case… She doesn't have a
  pencil case; he has a backpack" — the pronoun
  switches mid-example as printed). The long story
  "Tom's Busy School Day" (sharing scissors, art
  time, lunch boxes, "Sharing is caring!") with
  true/false, and a Find Someone Who ("Do you have
  a lunch box?") whose model sentence is "Anna has
  scissors, but she doesn't have a felt pen!"
- U5 My toys (pp.28–35). DO/DOES questions with
  have ("Does she have a skateboard? Do you have a
  video game?"), Show and Tell ("I have a (toy).
  It's (color). I like my (toy)."), toys sorted
  individual vs group play. Two stories: "Tom's
  Birthday Gifts" (videogame, puzzle, basketball —
  but no roller blades) with matching and a
  7-question multiple-choice set, and "Emma and
  Liam's Surprise Box" with multiple choice and
  sentence completion. PLURALS return as candy
  words (lollipop, candy bar, cookie, popsicle,
  caramel, jelly bean — Activity 12 prints "jelly
  VEAN" for jelly bean, recorded as printed) with
  "They have / They don't have" sentences.
- U6 Feelings and appearance (pp.36–42). Adjective
  set one: clean, dirty, happy, sad, fat, thin, big,
  small — with I AM / I AM NOT / HE IS / HE ISN'T /
  SHE IS / SHE ISN'T sentence completion. Reading
  "A Day at the Park" (Tom big and happy, Anna thin
  and clean) with 6 multiple-choice questions, and a
  scaffolded Perfect Day writing frame.
  Adjective set two: tall, short, beautiful, ugly,
  young, old — with an opposites chart (tall,
  beautiful, young, happy, big, clean, thin are
  given; learners supply the opposites). A second
  word search carries a wider descriptive set
  (blond, weak, busy, careful, strong, clever,
  different, boring, hot, double, easy, funny,
  difficult, terrible, thirsty, famous, surprised,
  tired — and "QUIT" in the list for quiet, as
  printed). The unit project is MY SUPERHERO
  CHARACTER, with a full model text ("My superhero's
  name is Lightning Girl. She is blond, tall, and
  young. She is very strong and can lift a car with
  one hand!…").
- U7 Animals (pp.43–49). Eight animals by riddle
  description: bear (hibernates), flamingo (stands
  on one leg, pink feathers), camel (survives
  without water "in the DESSERT," as printed),
  zebra, hippopotamus, parrot (mimics sounds),
  snake, gorilla — the answer list prints "Came"
  for Camel, as printed. THERE IS / THERE ARE opens
  here, through the "Welcome to the Happy Zoo!"
  paragraph (a big gorilla, two pink flamingos, a
  talking parrot, three zebras, a long snake) with
  completion and a mistake-correction exercise
  ("There are a big bear" → "There is a big bear").
  HE HAS / SHE HAS for owned animals, adjective
  riddles ("I am brown and very big. I love to eat
  honey."), wrong-adjective correction, and the
  long reading "A Day at the Ocean Zoo" (Arctic
  Zone polar bear and penguins, Ocean Zone whale
  and dolphins, Tropical Zone octopus and fish)
  with a true/false-and-correct set. The project
  is a ZOO MAP in four zones (Arctic, Ocean, Jungle,
  Savanna) with one There is/He has sentence per
  animal. SLIP: Activity 11f reads "I in the desert.
  I am pink." (the verb is missing, as printed).
- U8 School Subject (pp.50–56). Subjects: Math,
  Art, Science, English, History, Physical
  Education, Reading, Geography — with LIKE / DON'T
  LIKE in the dialogue text "Our Favorite Subjects!"
  (Carlos, Sofia, David, Emma, Luis, Ana; the text
  drops the opening quotation marks on Carlos's and
  Emma's lines, as printed) and yes/no checks, a
  Find Someone Who over likes and dislikes, and
  the DAYS OF THE WEEK (word search, then the
  week's work): Activity 8 writes the days in a
  NUMBER CODE — 20-8-21-18-19-4-1-25 = THURSDAY,
  and so on — the alphabet used as an A=1…Z=26 key,
  and four coded sentences to decode ("I DO NOT
  LIKE ENGLISH / I LIKE MATH / I LIKE GEOGRAPHY /
  I DO NOT LIKE ART"). Emily's CLASS SCHEDULE
  (Mon–Fri 7:00–12:00, break 9:15–9:45, Saturday
  NO CLASSES) is read for ten gap-fill questions
  and a sentence-building table, then learners
  build and present their own schedule. SLIP:
  question (c) asks about Science "from 10:30 to
  11:00 AM" while the schedule itself prints
  10:30–11:15.
- U9 Places (pp.57–63). Places by definition:
  hospital, movie theater, firehouse (fire station),
  toy store, school, supermarket, candy store,
  restaurant — "In a toy store, I can buy a doll"
  with items per place (bandages, fire truck,
  pizza, popcorn, books, apples, candy bar).
  Listening multiple choice and a true/false set
  built from swapped places ("You can buy toys…
  at the hospital — False"). PREPOSITIONS OF PLACE
  arrive by listening (ten sentences: the ball is
  ___ the basket, the dog ___ the tree…) and then
  in the reading "A Walk Around the Neighborhood,"
  which chains NEXT TO / BEHIND / IN FRONT OF
  through twelve places (toy store next to the
  bakery, candy store behind it, hospital in front
  of the firehouse, restaurant next to the
  supermarket, movie theater in front of the
  bookstore, drugstore next to the beauty parlor,
  hotel behind the bank, school in front of the
  library) with a where-is quiz. A map Speaking
  Time asks where the hospital, supermarket and
  school are, and where you buy shoes and a pet.

## Activity Book 4 (primary English programme)

MINED, Dirección de Educación Primaria Regular y
Extra-Edad. "Activity Book for 4th Grade — Let's
Practice English Together!" Compilation: Margarita
Soza; technical revision: Melkis Barrios, Margarita
Soza, Anielka Ofelia Ortiz Orozco, Orlando Alexander
Rayo; covers Martha García Pérez; layout Wilder
Alexander Mercado Salmerón; Recursos del Tesoro.
72 pp.; printed page = PDF page. The Presentación
(p.3) is Book 3's text with the grade changed in the
title line only — SLIP recorded as printed: it still
says the activities are "dirigidas a niñas y niños
de PRIMER GRADO." References (p.72): the same 2018
MINED Mallas for Grades 3–4, ESL Kids Games &
Activities (eslkidstuff.com), and Kniveton & Llanas,
Bounce Now Teacher's Edition fourth grade (Macmillan
Education, 2020). NINE units, each framed
"Vocabulary and Grammar" and built in the same
five-part shape: a vocabulary opening, Listening,
Reading (a text plus exercises), Writing, Speaking,
and MY PROJECT TIME — a four-step project that
closes every unit. The grammar spine of the whole
book is the PRESENT CONTINUOUS, opened in Unit 2
and carried through every unit to Unit 9, with
months/ordinals in Unit 1 and clock time in Unit 9.
(Correction to the earlier MAPPED filing: Unit 5's
printed header repeats Unit 4's title — see below.)

- U1 Special Days (pp.4–10). Celebrations: Christmas,
  Easter, Halloween, Mother's Day, Independence Day,
  Valentine's Day, Father's Day, Birthday — taught
  with MONTHS AND ORDINAL DATES ("Valentine's Day
  is on February 14th. February is the second month
  of the year… Mother's Day is in May, the fifth
  month… Halloween is on October 31st… Christmas is
  on December 25th"). Listening fixes the NICARAGUAN
  dates: Independence Day September 15th (parades
  and dances), Mother's Day MAY 30TH (gifts and
  special food), Christmas (church, and both beach
  options as printed), and Ana's list — La Purísima,
  Independence Day, Christmas, Mother's Day. Reading
  "Special Celebrations in Nicaragua" (Sofía):
  Easter processions and beach, Mother's Day cards
  and songs, presents opened AT MIDNIGHT on December
  24th; true/false and a put-in-order set (the
  instructions say "writing 1-5" for SIX events, as
  printed). Writing: a favourite-celebration frame
  and a birthday invitation email (Lucas to Mark:
  March 10th, pizza and cake). Project: a poster or
  mini-book of celebrations by SEASON (Winter,
  Spring, Summer, Fall).
- U2 The Weather (pp.11–17). Weather and clothes
  word search (sunny, sweater, scarf, rain, skirt,
  trousers, windy, jeans, weather, cloudy, wind,
  clothes, glasses, coat, handbag…). Reading "A
  Windy Day": "Emma is wearing a red coat, a scarf,
  and black boots. Her brother, Tom, is wearing a
  blue sweater and jeans" — the PRESENT CONTINUOUS
  for clothes, with true/false and multiple choice.
  Writing: unscramble six present-continuous
  sentences (item 3 is numbered "3. 3." as printed)
  and complete a WEATHER REPORT IN NICARAGUA by
  region (Pacific hot — t-shirts and shorts; Central
  cloudy and cool — a sweater; Caribbean sunny and
  windy — sandals; "dress acording to the weather,"
  as printed). Speaking is a FASHION SHOW narrated
  with ORDINALS ("this is the first student: Mario.
  He is wearing a green jacket, and the second
  student is: Martha…"). Project: research a
  department's weather (Matagalpa, Managua,
  Bluefields) and present a full-day weather report.
- U3 Leisure time (pp.18–25). Action verbs drawn
  and pasted: play soccer, skate, sing, talk, laugh,
  cry, eat, drink, paste, count, cut, write (+ draw,
  colour, paint, ride in the activities). Present
  continuous affirmative and negative from pictures
  ("He is painting / He isn't painting"). Reading
  "Tom's Afternoon at the Park" (playing with his
  ball, talking with Alex, counting to ten while Tom
  hides, eating snacks, laughing at the dog) with
  true/false, ordering and gap-fill. Writing prompts
  list the grammar explicitly: "What is he/she
  doing? He/she is coloring. He/she isn't skating,"
  with connectors so, but, and. Project: a five-action
  "My Day" storybook in the present continuous.
- U4 On vacation time (pp.26–32). Beach and nature
  vocabulary in two lists (swim, ocean, sandcastle,

  postcard (present continuous, connectors so, but,
  because). Project: a storybook trip to a named

- U5 (pp.33–40) — printed header repeats "On

- U6 Taking Care (pp.41–49). Health and helping:

  "Be a Hero!" — a rescue story in at least five

- U7 At The Restaurant (pp.50–56). Food riddles

  the true/false set names her "Sara" in item 4, as

- U8 THE CONCERT (pp.57–64). Party vocabulary:
