**Series arc:** Grade 4 teaches the alphabet and the first sentence frames but **no numbers, colours, days or time at all**. The past tense is never taught in Grades 4–6, though past forms saturate the Grade 5–6 readings. Past simple is first taught as a system in Grade 8. Grade 9 is the grammar hinge: first AND second conditionals (Unit 3), present perfect, past continuous, and a full reported-speech system (Units 14–15). Present perfect continuous is first taught as a named system in Grade 10 (Unit 11). **The passive is systematised in Grade 11** (Unit 3, plus the present perfect passive in Unit 8) — it is never taught as a system in Grades 4–10; Grade 12 adds extensions to the Grade 11 system rather than a new system of its own: the past perfect passive (Unit 2, printed pp.17–18) and the simple present passive for scientific statements (Unit 5, printed pp.56–57), with the present perfect passive revised in Unit 6. The third conditional appears only in Grade 12's Unit 10 Revision, and no conditional is named or numbered anywhere in Grades 11–12. Non-defining relative clauses arrive only in Grade 12. *(Header corrected 2026-11-04, after the Afghanistan merge checked this header against the Grade 12 book sections: the earlier wording said the passive is never taught as a system in Grade 12, but Grade 12's own sections teach the past perfect passive and the scientific present passive as new forms; the sections govern.)*

### How the book is built

Units One–Four and Six–Nine follow one repeating pattern: an objectives page; a short teacher–student greeting dialogue; picture-naming of a set of 6–7 new words; word-reading and word/picture discrimination drills; copying of a block of letters (lowercase first in Units 1–4, capitals in Units 6–9); spelling-out of the new words; and writing the words in notebooks. Units Five, Ten and Fifteen are review units. There are no unit titles beyond numbers — topics below are identified from content.

### Honest negatives — what this book does NOT teach

Checked across the whole book, not a sample:
- **No past tense** of any kind; no future forms; **no present continuous** (no be + verb-ing models anywhere — "I am fine" is the only *am* use); **no passive**; **no conditionals**; **no comparatives/superlatives**.
- **No numbers**: no number words, counting, or numerals lesson appears anywhere (numerals occur only as exercise item numbers and page folios).
- **No colours, no days/months, no time-telling, no weather, no animals topic beyond single picture words** (cat, dog, goat, hen, fox, eagle, wolf, yak, zebra occur as alphabet/vocabulary items only).
- **Question range is narrow**: only "What is this/that?", "Is this/that…?", "How are you?", "What is your name?", "What do you do?" and "Do you like…?" No other wh- questions (no where/when/who/why/how many).
- **No phonics programme**: no letter sounds, no blending, no pronunciation guide; spelling means naming letters aloud (e.g. f-i-s-h).
- **No reading texts or stories**: the longest connected text is the 8-sentence family description in Unit Thirteen (printed p.120); everything else is dialogues of 2–6 lines and single model sentences.
- **No songs, rhymes or games**; the only non-language activity is "draw one of the pictures and colour it" (Units 6, 10, 11).
- **No answer key, no tests or exam preparation** (Units 5, 10, 15 are practice reviews, not tests), **no glossary or word list** at the end, **no Dari/Pashto translation** of any English content (the only non-English pages are the Pashto national anthem, PDF p.2, and the copyright notice on the specifications page).

### Honest negatives — what this book does NOT teach

Checked across the whole book:
- **No past tense teaching**: past forms (was, went, visited, bought, turned, celebrated, roared…) are used freely in readings and dialogues from Unit 4 onward, and Unit 14 even drills past model sentences, but no past-tense paradigm, rule or formation lesson exists anywhere in the book.
- **No future teaching**: "I'm going to go…", "I'm planning on…" (Unit 7) occur as dialogue lines only.
- **No passive, no conditionals as grammar** (a conditional sentence occurs once inside the Unit 14 story's reported speech — "If you paint me a good face, I will reward you" — as story content, not teaching), **no comparatives/superlatives, no reported speech**.
- **No phonics or pronunciation programme**: the only pronunciation-adjacent work is spelling words letter by letter and unscrambling; no sounds, stress or intonation are taught.
- **Numbers stop at 100** and are taught as word lists (Unit 4); no arithmetic language, no numbers beyond hundred, no prices/money.
- **No answer key, no formal tests or exam preparation** (Units 8 and 15 are practice reviews), **no end-of-book glossary/word list**, **no grammar reference section** — the only paradigms given are the present-continuous table (Unit 5) and the do/be summary (Unit 11).
- **No listening material beyond "listen to your teacher"** dictation/repetition; no audio scripts are printed.
- Days of the week are never presented as a taught list — they appear only inside exercises (Units 4–5, 8); months are taught as a list (Unit 4) but their native-language equivalents are left for pupils to supply.

### Honest negatives — what this book does NOT teach

Checked across the whole book:
- **No past tense teaching** — the single largest gap. Past forms saturate the readings (Units 5, 7, 9, 10) and *did*-questions are drilled in Units 10 and 13, but no paradigm, formation rule (no -ed presentation) or tense explanation appears anywhere.
- **No future teaching**: "going to" appears only inside a contractions exercise (Unit 6) and plans talk; no will/going-to lesson.
- **No passive, no conditionals, no reported speech, no comparatives/superlatives** (comparative meaning appears once, untaught, in "Is life in a village… better than city life?", Unit 7).
- **Almost no pronunciation teaching**: only three short dialogues touch it — -ache/EA (Unit 5), th and south (Units 6–7), and final -ing (Unit 8). There is no phonics programme, no stress/intonation work.
- **No numbers teaching** (assumed from Grade 5; numbers occur only inside times, dates and the football/Olympic content), **no new alphabet work** beyond the Unit 1 self-check.
- **No answer key, no tests or exam preparation** (Units 9 and 15 are practice reviews), **no glossary, word list or grammar summary** at the end, **no songs** (poems occur in Units 11 and 12 only), **no translation** of English content into Dari/Pashto anywhere.

### 3. Honest negatives — what this book does NOT teach

Checked across the whole book, not a sample:

- **Tenses named and taught as systems:** verb "be" (present), simple present (affirmative statements only as a paradigm; its questions/negatives appear in use but no do/does negative system is set out as a grammar box), present continuous (affirmative/negative statements only, Unit 9 — no present-continuous question paradigm is taught), and there is/are. That is the ceiling.
- **No past tense of any kind is taught as a system** — no simple past, no past continuous, no present perfect, no past perfect. Past-tense verb forms occur inside reading texts and dialogues (e.g. the twin-brother story, the house paragraph "He bought us this house last year", "Cheese was first made…") but are never presented, named or drilled.
- **No future form is taught as a system** — no will / going to grammar section. "Will" appears inside texts and examples only.
- **No passive voice. No conditionals** — no conditional type is named or taught; an "if" sentence appears in exercises (e.g. in the Unit 16 map questions) but there is no conditional grammar. **No reported speech. No present perfect continuous** (and no present perfect at all, above).
- **No modals system** — can appears in classroom/ability contexts in texts and the earlier-level sense, and should/may appear inside dialogues (advice, permission), but no modal is presented as a grammar system in this book.
- **No comparative or superlative adjectives** as a system (comparative words occur in readings only). **No relative clauses** as a system (a relative "who" appears inside example sentences for "the"). No gerund/infinitive system, no question-tag system (tag questions occur in one dialogue only, untaught), no direct/indirect objects, no phrasal-verb system.
- **Writing ceiling:** single paragraphs only, governed by the repeated three "rules of writing paragraph" (capital letter, full stop, no new line per sentence). No letter format, no essay structure, no summary writing is taught.
- **No exam preparation, no tests section, no answer key, no bilingual glossary.** The unit "Vocabulary" lists are English-only word lists sorted by word class; listening work throughout depends on the teacher/classmates reading aloud — no transcripts are printed in the book.
- Phonics ceiling: short a/e/i/o/u and long a/e/i/o are taught with two stated rules; there is no long "u" lesson and no consonant-blend/digraph system beyond soft/hard c and g.

### 3. Honest negatives — what this book does NOT teach

Checked across the whole book:

- **Taught as named systems:** can/can't for ability; like + -ing; present continuous (full, including questions); past simple (regular/irregular, negatives, did and wh- questions); past tense of be; simple-present do/does questions (affirmative paradigm from Grade 7 carried into questions here); imperatives; direct and indirect objects; comparatives (short, long, irregular) and equality as…as; superlatives; modals for rules/advice (have to, must/mustn't, should/shouldn't, can/can't, had better) in Unit 15; adjectives vs adverbs of manner. That is the ceiling.
- **No present perfect** in any form — no present perfect simple and **no present perfect continuous** — as a taught system. **No past continuous, no past perfect, no future perfect.**
- **No passive voice** anywhere as a system. **No conditionals** — no conditional type is named or taught; an "if" clause occurs inside a reading ("if this trend continues", Unit 12) and in directions, untaught. **No reported speech** — the Unit 10 "report what your friends say" activity is retelling from notes, with no reported-speech grammar, no backshift, no reporting verbs system.
- **Future forms are used but never taught as a system:** will / going to / might / may appear in the Unit 12 future-speculation listening texts and in dialogues, with no grammar presentation. **Used to** appears in the Unit 10 album conversation for past habits but is never presented as a system. Question tags, relative clauses as a system, gerund-vs-infinitive contrasts (beyond like + -ing), and phrasal verbs as a system are likewise absent.
- **No exam-preparation section** (Unit 15's test-taking tips are study advice, not exam practice), **no tests, no answer key, no bilingual glossary.** Unit word lists are English only. Listening tasks depend on the teacher/classmates reading aloud; no transcripts are printed (one listening explicitly tells the teacher to invent the names, p.83).

### 3. Honest negatives — what this book does NOT teach

Checked across the whole book:

- **Taught as named systems:** future with present continuous, be going to and will; suggestions; first conditional and second conditional; verb + infinitive and verb + object + infinitive; stative verbs; present perfect (ever/never, for/since, yet/already adverbials, contrasted with past simple); relative clauses (who/that, which/that, when, where — defining clauses only); by + gerund; sequence adverbs; past continuous including when/while/as; reported speech in unusual depth for the level — statements, yes/no and wh- questions, commands and requests, with backshift and changed time expressions; requests/invitations/complaints as function systems. That is the ceiling.
- **No third conditional and no mixed conditionals** — conditional teaching stops at types 1 and 2 (Units 3 and revision references agree).
- **No passive voice as a taught system** — yet passives are frequent in the readings (the Eiffel Tower "was built… is now loved", the ozone layer "is being reduced by chemicals", women "were not allowed to patent", notes "were invented"). A learner is expected to read a present-continuous passive (Unit 8) that no unit teaches.
- **No present perfect continuous as a system.** It is never presented or named; the only trace is one Unit 7 completion item ("I'm at the airport waiting for him. I ___ (be/wait) for ___ hours", p.73) whose expected answer would be a present-perfect-continuous form the book has not taught. **No past perfect as a system** (past-perfect forms occur inside reported-speech examples — "she had big dreams" as a backshift — and in stories, untaught), **no future perfect, no used-to system, no modal perfects.**
- **No non-defining relative clauses**, no reduced relatives, no relative-clause punctuation teaching; no reported questions with whether beyond the if/whether line in Unit 14, no reporting of modals as a topic.
- **No exam preparation, no test papers, no answer key.** Unit 1 teaches study skills (dictionary use, listening, summarizing, proofreading) but there is no examination practice anywhere.
- **No glossary.** The book ends with the Unit 16 word list (p.176); the word "glossary" itself appears only as a vocabulary item in Unit 1's list (p.10) and inside a Unit 1 true/false statement. Listening work depends on teacher/classmate reading; printed listening texts are given only where the text itself is the exercise (e.g. Units 8, 14), with no answer transcripts.

### Honest negatives — what this book does NOT teach

- **Passive voice: NOT taught as a system.** The word "passive"
  appears once in the whole book, inside the label "causative passive
  vice" [sic] for *have something done* (p.82). Passive sentences
  occur unexplained inside texts and drills — e.g. "The floor was
  covered with grass" (p.43), "He is being treated for his skin
  disease" (p.120) — but no *be* + past-participle rule, no agent
  *by*-phrase and no active↔passive conversion is ever presented.
- **Conditionals: NOT taught as a system.** No conditional type is
  named anywhere (the word "conditional" never occurs). Conditional
  sentences appear only incidentally: one question in the Unit 4
  listening set ("If you were a man of thousands years ago, what
  would you do…?", p.42), and *if*-clauses inside readings and word-
  study examples. There is no *if* + present → *will* lesson, no
  *would* conditional lesson, nothing on unreal past conditions.
- **Reported speech: taught, in two units, for statements, commands
  and questions** (Units 8–9, recycled in Unit 10) — with pronoun and
  tense changes and the present-reporting-verb exception. Reported
  speech with modal verbs is not covered.
- **Present perfect continuous: TAUGHT as a named system** (Unit 11,
  formula and *since/for* contrast).
- **Past perfect: NOT taught as a system.** Past-perfect forms occur
  only inside reported-speech examples (pp.95, 102: "had worked",
  "had had a party") and one Review sentence ("the airplane had
  already taken off", p.102), never explained or drilled as a tense.
- **Future forms:** only *will* and *going to* (Unit 2). No future
  continuous, no future perfect.
- **Relative clauses: taught** (Unit 12: *who/whom/which/that*, and
  *where* added in the Review). Defining/non-defining distinction and
  comma rules are not taught; relative omission is not taught.
- **Other systems present:** simple present, simple past, present
  perfect; comparatives and superlatives; *had better*; tag questions;
  possessive *'s/s'*; the gerund; basic conjunctions; articles *a/the*
  at first-mention level only.
- **Examination preparation: NONE.** There is no Kankor (university-
  entrance) material, no final-exam apparatus, no past papers, model
  answers, marking schemes or test-strategy section anywhere in the
  book. The word "examination" occurs only incidentally (school
  examinations in drills; "participate in the annual examination" in
  the Unit 12 model application letter, p.148).
- **Writing ceiling:** paragraphs throughout; letters (friendly letter
  anatomy in Unit 9, application letter in Unit 12) and one memo
  (Unit 2). No essay writing is taught (the word "essay" never
  occurs).

### Honest negatives — what this book does NOT teach

- **Passive voice: TAUGHT as a full system** (Unit 3: form, *by*-
  phrase, tense range incl. modal passive, and the rule that
  intransitive verbs cannot be passivised), and extended to the
  **present perfect passive** in Unit 8. The past perfect passive and
  future perfect passive are not separately taught.
- **Conditionals: two patterns taught, one more present, none named
  as numbered types in the grammar sections.** Taught formulas:
  *If + present … future* and *If + present … instruction* (Unit 1),
  *If + present … present* for always-true results (Unit 4 Revision),
  *If + past … would* with the "If I were you" special form
  (Unit 6). The Unit 6 opener objectives use the phrase "the first
  conditional", and that is the only occurrence of the word
  "conditional" as a teaching label in the book; the backshift table
  in Unit 9 uses the words "conditional" / "conditional continuous"
  only as names of reported forms. **No third conditional**
  (*if + past perfect … would have*) is taught anywhere, and no
  mixed conditionals.
- **Reported speech: TAUGHT as a full system** (Unit 9) — backshift
  tables reaching past perfect continuous and conditional continuous
  targets, modal shifts, pronoun/time/place changes, embedded
  questions; recycled for reported questions in Unit 10.
- **Present perfect continuous: NOT taught as a tense in this
  book.** A form occurs once in a revision prompt ("has been
  waiting", Unit 2 area) without explanation; the reading of the
  three-book band is that the tense is taught in Grade 10, not here.
- **Past perfect: TAUGHT** (Unit 5 Revision, with timelines) and
  presupposed by the Unit 9 backshift tables.
- **Past perfect continuous: NOT taught as a standalone tense** —
  it appears only as a reported-speech target form in the Unit 9
  tables.
- **Future forms:** *will / going to* assumed from Grade 10; future
  passives appear inside Unit 3 examples. No future continuous or
  future perfect taught as a system.
- **Relative clauses:** defining relatives from Grade 10 are
  extended by **clause shortening/omission** (Unit 4) and passive-
  participle shortening (Unit 8). The defining/non-defining
  distinction is not named in this book.
- **Examination preparation: NONE.** No Kankor syllabus, no past
  papers, no model answers, no marking schemes, no entrance-exam
  guidance and no test sections. The single Kankor trace in the
  whole three-book band is one ordinary exercise sentence in
  Unit 4's Revision (p.59): "If Waleed passes university entrance
  kankor exam, his father will buy him a car." "Examination"
  otherwise occurs only incidentally (school examinations in drills;
  the name of a US health survey in the Unit 12 reading).
- **Writing ceiling:** formal letter (Unit 1), paragraphs throughout
  with an explicit ten-rule method (Unit 3), process paragraphs
  (Units 7, 9), descriptions from tables/notes (Units 10–12). The
  word "essay" occurs once, in a vocabulary list, not as a taught
  form.

### Course shape

Twelve units. The Grade 12 sections differ from Grades 10–11: opener
objectives ("At the end of this unit, students will be able to…") →
Conversation / Discussion → Grammar → Reading → Word study →
**Revision** sections (headed "REVISION", misspelled "REVESION" twice

vocabulary lists anywhere in this book**, and no closing grammar
summary or appendix: much of the year's genuinely new grammar is
carried inside the Revision sections, and the book is largely a
consolidation/revision year over Grades 10–11. The front matter
(folios d–e) repeats the series rationale and methods and states the
book "consist of (12) units".

### Unit-by-unit account

### Unit 1 — Water (opens printed p.1)

dialogue. Reading (pp.6–8): "Water" — where the Earth's fresh water

### Unit 2 — Conservation (opens printed p.15)

vocabulary. Grammar (pp.17–18): **the past perfect passive taught**

### Honest negatives — what this book does NOT teach

- **Conditionals:** the unreal-present pattern (*if* + past …
  *would*) is taught as an unnamed note (Unit 7), and the **third
  conditional is taught as a system** (Unit 10 Revision: *if* + past
  perfect … *would have* + participle). No conditional is ever named
  or numbered as a "type" in this book (the word "conditional" never
  occurs). **No mixed conditionals.** First/zero patterns are assumed
  from Grade 11, not re-taught.
- **Passive:** past perfect passive is new (Unit 2); simple present
  passive for science (Unit 5) and the present perfect passive
  (Unit 6 Revision) are consolidation of the Grade 11 system.
  Transitive/intransitive explains the passive's limits (Unit 8).
  No future perfect passive, no modal-perfect passive system.
- **Reported speech:** NOT taught as a system in this book — it
  appears only as past-perfect forms inside reported sentences in
  Unit 7's Revision. The full system lives in Grades 10–11.
- **Present perfect continuous:** revision/contrast only (Units 2
  and 6); the system teaching is Grade 10's.
- **Past perfect:** presented in Unit 7's Revision and presupposed
  by Units 2 and 10; the first teaching is Grade 11's.
- **Past perfect continuous: NOT taught as a standalone tense**
  anywhere in the band's Grade 12 book.
- **Future forms:** NO new future system at all — no future
  continuous, no future perfect; *will / going to* are assumed.
- **Relative clauses:** the defining vs non-defining distinction
  WITH the comma rule is new here (Unit 8); shortening/omission was
  Grade 11.
- **Writing:** this book teaches **no extended writing** — no essay,
  no letter, no paragraph-writing section. All production is
  sentence-level exercise answers. (The word "essay" never occurs.)
- **Grammar ceiling for the band:** third conditional, past perfect
  passive, defining/non-defining relative clauses and participial
  phrases are the top of the Grade 12 ladder; the genuinely new
  systems in this volume are few — past perfect passive, third
  conditional, non-defining relatives, transitive/intransitive —
  the rest is revision of Grades 10–11.
