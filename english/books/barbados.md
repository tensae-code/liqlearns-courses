# Barbados — Language Arts Syllabi, Classes 1–4 (Ministry of Education) — FULLY READ + CITATION CHECK PASSED 2026-10-27

Barbados's set is different in kind: there is NO government
student textbook for Language Arts (the English subject).
What the library holds — and what this filing reads — is the
ministry's own syllabus for each primary class: the document
that sets what is taught, in what order, to what standard.
The same case as Ghana's curricula. Four documents, one per
class, all held locally in phase3-staging under
grade-0N/gov/barbados/language-arts/curriculum/syllabus.pdf.

## The four documents

All four are titled "PRIMARY LANGUAGE ARTS SYLLABUS" with
the class named on the title page, issued by the **Ministry
of Education and Human Resource Development, Barbados**
(title pages checked on renders — Class 1 PDF p.1 reads
exactly that, over "CLASS ONE"). No year or edition is
printed on any title page or elsewhere in the documents;
the PDFs' internal producer is Microsoft Office Word 2007
in all four. Verified against the library record before
reading — every figure an exact match:

- **Class 1** — 518,824 bytes, 61 PDF pp. Printed folios:
  front matter i–vii, body 1–54; printed = PDF − 7.
- **Class 2** — 503,504 bytes, 58 PDF pp. Printed folios:
  front matter ii–v, body 1–53; printed = PDF − 5.
- **Class 3** — 349,933 bytes, 40 PDF pp. Printed folios:
  front matter i–iii (its contents page is anomalously
  foliated "1"), body 1–35; printed = PDF − 5.
- **Class 4** — 782,971 bytes, 92 PDF pp. Printed folios:
  front matter ii–vii, body 1–9 then 21–85 (see the folio
  jump under Class 4 below); printed = PDF − 7.

How the four relate: each document is one class's complete
syllabus, built on one shared frame. Every document carries
(1) a Rationale, (2) the shared General Objectives, (3) a
Format note, (4) the **same Scope and Sequence grid for all
four classes together** — every topic marked ■ "Begin
teaching skill/concept" in the class where it starts and ✓
"Maintain and develop" in each class after, (5) **Attainment
Targets** for its own class, and (6) its own detailed
**Syllabus body**: tables of Topic × Objectives (Skills,
Knowledge, Attitude) × Suggested Activities × Assessment ×
Suggested Resources, with cross-subject integration marked
by abbreviation (HFLE, SC, VA, SS…) in the activities.

The honest structural finding: the Attainment Targets are a
**shared list, near word-for-word identical in all four
classes** — the same "The pupil should be able to…" items
strand by strand. The differences are tiny and are named
where they occur below (sentence-structure count two →
three from Class 2 on; the plural item's wording). The real
class-by-class step-up lives in the detailed body tables and
in the Scope and Sequence grid's begin/marks — not in the
targets. Class 4's document is also visibly a later working
of the set: its Rationale is rewritten (below), and it alone
adds appendices of recommended literature texts.

**The frame all four share.** Rationale (Class 1, printed
p.iv): the programme's ultimate aim is students
"communicatively competent in Standard English," becoming
"critical and independent thinkers and learners"; language
is learned by using it, not by knowing its elements; the
four language arts (listening, speaking, reading, writing)
are taught as one interrelating programme. The Caribbean
framing is explicit and is the documents' own: in "our
Caribbean language context," oral language comes first —
the early years lean on the oral aspect so pupils build "a
bridge, which allows for a smooth transition from oral
proficiency in the native language, to full communicative
competence in Standard English" (printed pp.iv–v). General
Objectives (7 in Classes 2–4's printing): spoken-word
understanding and expression; audience and purpose; reading
and responding to all types of writing; information
retrieval including the Internet; constructing meaning in
writing, style matched to audience and purpose; correct
spelling, redrafting, fluent legible writing, clear
presentation; critical thinking (Classes 2–4).

**Strands.** Speaking and Listening (Speaking: oral
communication, questions, recounting, reporting, role play,
explanations, intonation/rate/expression, "Standard English
rules and language appropriate to context"; Listening:
attentive — directions, announcements, details/sequence/main
idea, reproductions/summaries; responsive/analytical;
appreciative) · Reading (Word Attack; Oral Reading; Silent
Reading; Comprehension/Interpretation; Functional Reading;
Recreational Reading; Organisational) · Writing (Process;
Communication; Expository; Conventions; Creative/
Composition; Penmanship and Presentation). The Scope and
Sequence grid (Class 1 printing, printed pp.2–9) starts
nearly every row in Class 1 (■ at 1, ✓ after) — the four
years mostly deepen the same stock; the rows that begin
later are named in the class sections below.

## Class 1 (printed pp.1–54)

Front matter: Acknowledgements (printed iii — three senior
teachers and the Education Officer (Language Arts) as
facilitator), Rationale iv–v, General Objectives vi, Format
vii, Key to Abbreviations Used p.1, Scope and Sequence
pp.2–9, Attainment Targets pp.10–20, Syllabus body pp.21–51,
resources pp.52–54.

**Attainment Targets (pp.10–20)** — the shared list in its
first printing: Speaking and Listening (speak clearly and
audibly; language appropriate to context; recount
experiences fluently; follow oral directions with three or
four steps; listen to build memory…) · Reading: Skills and
Strategies (sight vocabulary; decoding systems "phonetic,
syntactic and semantic"; unknown words by context,
glossaries, dictionaries; read aloud at independent level;
self-correct after a miscue; rhythm, pace and intonation
"associated with Standard English") · Reading: Literature
(characters, summaries, lessons learnt, favourite authors
and genres) · Reading: Comprehension (details, main idea,
significant vs minor details, predictions made/confirmed/
revised, inferences, conclusions) · Writing: Process
(brainstorm → organise → self edit → peer edit → proof read
→ revise) · Writing: Communication (four modes; skits and
plays "using Standard English dialogue"; beginning, middle
and end; letters — invitation, praise, request, sympathy,
apology; directions in prose, home to school, how to make a
cake) · Writing: Conventions (the grammar list filed below)
· Penmanship (form and size letters, spacing, headings,
margins, "the four basic handwriting joins").

**Syllabus body (pp.21–51)**, in teaching order: Speaking
and Listening pp.21–27 (objectives numbered per block:
speaks clearly and audibly with "use of habitual present";
initiates discussion using sensory data; asks appropriate
questions; listens to and appreciates others' opinions —
activities run show and tell, story building, news, using
the telephone, "Author's Chair") · Oral Reading + Reading:
Skills and Strategies pp.28–35 — the phonics/word-attack
core of the whole set: sight vocabulary from the **Dolch,
Fry, Ekwall** lists; decoding by initial letters; **common
digraphs th, sh, wh, ch, ph, ck**; initial consonant blends
(bl, pl, gr, br, sp, str) and **three-letter blends (spr,
str, spl)**; **silent letters**; variant consonant sounds —
c (cat/cent), g (get/gender), q (kw), s (sat/as); like
sounds in different digraphs (ph, gh); **vowel sounds —
long a (cake), e (be), u (mule); short a (hat), e (get), u
(cup); vowel digraphs ea, ai, oa, ee**; spelling by doubling
the final consonant (shop – shopping); syntactic work
(statements and questions, the negative form); suffixes s,
es, ies, ed, ied, ing; prefixes (un-fair → unfair, dis-
honest); roots; compound words (sea-side, to-day, cup-
board, as printed); syllabication; synonyms, antonyms,
homophones; context clues · Functional + Recreational
Reading p.36 (table of contents, telephone directories,
alphabetising by 1st and 2nd letter — "Alphabetiises" as
printed; pictograms; glossary/encyclopaedia/junior
dictionary; book corners, book logs, journals) · Reading:
Literature pp.37–38 · Reading: Comprehension pp.39–42 ·
Writing: Process pp.43–44 · Writing: Communication
pp.45–46 · Writing: Conventions pp.47–50 (nouns naming
people/places/things; proper nouns replaced by subject and
object pronouns; capitals for proper nouns and sentence
starts; verbs; end punctuation; commas in lists, in letter
greetings/closures, in dates; question marks; complete
sentences; adjectives and adverbs, and extending sentences
with them; simple link words; at least two sentence
structures; negative forms; the three simple tenses; the
present continuous; spelling; synonyms/antonyms/homonyms;
plurals regular and irregular) · Penmanship and
Presentation p.51.

**Resources (pp.52–54)**: a "Selected Resources Related to
Education" web list (period matter — AskEric at Syracuse,
eskimo.com, schnet.edu.au — recorded under slips), then
**Fry's 300 Instant Sight Words** as the sight-word
appendix: the First Hundred printed in full; the next list
is headed "Third Hundred" (see slips).

## Class 2 (printed pp.1–53)

Front matter has **no Acknowledgements and no Key to
Abbreviations** (contents jumps Format → Scope and
Sequence). Contents (printed ii) · Rationale iii (the shared
text) · General Objectives iv (7 objectives) · Format v ·
Scope and Sequence pp.1–7 · Attainment Targets pp.8–18 ·
Syllabus body pp.19–52 · resources p.53 (the same Selected
Resources web list as Class 1; no sight-word appendix).

**Attainment Targets (pp.8–18)**: the shared list; in
Conventions, "use at least **three** types of sentence
structures" (Class 1: two) and the plural item reworded —
"use 's' and 'es' to form the plural." Everything else
matches Class 1 strand for strand.

**Syllabus body (pp.19–52)**: Speaking and Listening
pp.19–25 · Reading: Skills and Strategies pp.27–32 —
word-shape and picture clues first, then sight words by
drill and meaningful use, the **Dolch, Fry or similar word
list** named again; sounds of simple consonants in initial,
medial and final positions; consonant clusters; **digraphs
ai, au and ee**; **vowel diphthongs oi and oy** (printed
"dipthongs"); the root word as "'meaning' unit"; compound
words split into their parts; prefixes and suffixes ·
Reading: Literature pp.33–34 · Comprehension pp.36–39 ·
Writing: Process pp.40–42 · Writing: Communication
pp.43–45 · Writing: Conventions pp.46–51 — a spelling-first
run (consonant clusters in initial and final sounds, short
vs long vowel sounds rag/rage, consonant digraphs ch, sh,
th, wh, "t sounding like sh" as in portion), then
punctuation step-ups: **full stops in abbreviations; commas
in a series, in addresses, after greetings and at closings;
the apostrophe for ownership and in contractions**;
capitals for first words of sentences, lines of poetry,
titles of books and stories, the words I and Oh, titles of
persons (Mr., Miss., Mrs., His Excellency), proper nouns —
persons, countries, places, holidays, organisations; basic
sentence types with printed model sentences; and the
strand closes with objective 25 (printed p.51):
**"Discriminate between dialect and standard usage
according to place and circumstance."** · Penmanship and
Presentation p.52.

## Class 4 (printed pp.1–9, 21–85)

A fuller, later-style document — 92 pages, the only one
with literature appendices. Contents ii · Acknowledgements
iii (five contributors incl. a Peripatetic Teacher –
Language Arts and the Senior Education Officer
(Curriculum)) · Rationale iv · General Objectives v ·
Format vi · Key to Abbreviations vii · Scope and Sequence
pp.1–9 · Attainment Targets **pp.21–31 as foliated** (the
contents says 10 — see slips) · Syllabus body **pp.32–72 as
foliated** (contents says 21) · Appendices – Recommended
Literature Texts pp.73–84 · Useful Websites p.85.

**Rationale (p.iv) — rewritten, and the set's fullest
dialect statement.** The aim is now pupils "competent in
Standard English" (the word "communicatively" dropped);
language is presented "in authentic contexts which facilitate
meaning and transfer"; and a passage the other three
documents do not carry: **"the role of dialect must be
clearly defined, as students maintain their sense of
national pride and identity in their language while
acquiring Standard structures. The role of dialect and the
ability to switch codes must be clearly articulated."** The
bridge passage follows as in the shared text. Assessment is
also named here: formative and summative, portfolios and
journals (the page continues past the rationale proper).

**Scope and Sequence (pp.1–9)**: the shared four-class grid
in its fourth typesetting — the speaking block renumbered
1.1–1.8 without sub-headings. The later-beginning rows of
the grid (from the Class 1 printing, which is the fullest):
silent reading's "elimination of vocalization" begins at
Class 2; alphabetising reaches the 3rd letter at Class 2
and the 4th at Class 3; comprehension's "moods/feelings,
tones of text and author's point of view" (3.4.10) begins
only at Class 4; organisational "classification of facts"
begins at Class 3 and "paragraph structure" (3.7.6) is a
**Class 4-only** row (begins and ends at 4, no ✓ after);
poetry forms (bio poems, colour poems, limericks, post
cards) begin at Class 3; "writing from different
perspectives" begins at Class 4; complex sentences begin at
Class 3 (simple and compound begin at Class 1).

**Attainment Targets (pp.21–31)**: the shared list
(Speaking and Listening pp.21–22; Reading: Skills and
Strategies 23; Literature 24; Comprehension 25–26; Writing:
Process 27; Communication 28; Conventions 29–30 —
three sentence structures; Penmanship 31).

**Syllabus body (pp.32–72)**: Listening and Speaking
pp.32–39 (heading order reversed against Classes 1–3) ·
Reading: Skills and Strategies pp.40–41 — strategies named
as a working set: use context clues (descriptions,
definitions, synonyms, surrounding sentences), employ
re-reading strategies · Reading: Literature pp.42–43 ·
Reading: Comprehension pp.44–49 · Writing: Process
pp.50–53 · Writing: Communication pp.54–56 · Writing:
Conventions pp.57–72 — the longest conventions run in the
set (spelling of homophones and their use, full stops, and
the whole grammar stock re-worked at length), closing at
objective 24 (p.72) with the dialect objective in its
fullest form: **"Discriminate between dialect and standard
usage according to place and circumstance and create
scenarios between formal and informal usage,"** practised
through scenarios and taped presentations. No separate
Penmanship block stands in the Class 4 body (the attainment
targets still carry the strand).

**Appendices (pp.73–84)**: Recommended Literature Texts for
Primary Schools, a class-by-class author/title/publisher
list "from classics to contemporary and from prose to
poetry" — the Class Four list (p.78) includes Julia
Donaldson's Hamlet and Midsummer Dream retellings and
David Orme's Macbeth, Warlord of Space (Longman), Jack
London's The Call of the Wild (Penguin), Scott O'Dell's
Island of the Blue Dolphins (Puffin), Robert Louis
Stevenson's Treasure Island (Baronet Books), Walter Dean
Myers's At Her Majesty's Request (Scholastic). The lists
are headed **"MINISTRY OF EDUCATION, YOUTH AFFAIRS AND
SPORTS"** — a different ministry name from the syllabus's
own title page (see slips). **Useful Websites (p.85)**: a
short period list (yahooligans, funbrain, enchanted
learning, gigglepoetry…), with two URLs misprinted as
printed (see slips).

## What is genuinely new for the LiqLearns course

- **Level 3, Sounds Together / Phonics stock** — Barbados
  is a fully specified phonics programme in ministry print:
  the digraph order (th, sh, wh, ch, ph, ck — Class 1 body,
  printed pp.28–35), three-letter blends (spr, str, spl),
  silent letters, variant c (cat/cent) and g (get/gender),
  long/short vowel sets with printed examples (cake/be/mule
  against hat/get/cup), vowel digraphs ea, ai, oa, ee
  (Class 1) extended by ai, au, ee and the diphthongs oi, oy
  (Class 2 body, printed p.30), and final-consonant doubling
  for spelling (shop – shopping). Sight words are stocked
  from named lists — **Dolch, Fry, Ekwall** (Class 1 body)
  — with Fry's 300 printed as an appendix (Class 1,
  pp.52–54).
- **Level 5, Conversation / register** — the set's spine is
  a register doctrine no other filed country states this
  plainly: oral work first as a **bridge from the native
  language to Standard English** (Rationale, all four);
  "Standard English rules and language appropriate to
  context" as a scope row from Class 1; and the dialect
  objective itself — **"Discriminate between dialect and
  standard usage according to place and circumstance"**
  (Class 2 body, printed p.51, objective 25), fullest in
  Class 4 (body, printed p.72, objective 24: "…and create
  scenarios between formal and informal usage"), under
  Class 4's rationale: dialect's role defined, **code-
  switching** named, national pride and identity kept while
  acquiring Standard structures (Class 4, printed p.iv).
  Barbados stocks the course's dialect/register lesson
  directly — Bajan at home, Standard English for school and
  formal use, chosen by place and circumstance.
- **Level 2/4, word work** — roots as the "'meaning' unit,"
  prefixes un-/dis-, suffixes -s/-es/-ies/-ed/-ied/-ing,
  compound words split and built (Class 1–2 bodies);
  syllabication (Class 1); alphabetising by 1st–2nd letter
  rising to the 4th letter by Class 3 (Scope and Sequence
  3.5.1).
- **Levels 10–13, sentence stock** — sentence types counted
  up across the years (attainment: two types in Class 1,
  three from Class 2); negatives, link words, the three
  simple tenses and the present continuous in the shared
  conventions list; Class 2's body adds the **apostrophe
  (ownership and contractions)**, commas in addresses and
  series, full stops in abbreviations, capitals for titles
  of persons and of books/stories (printed pp.46–51).
- **Level 14, The Paragraph** — the grid's organisational
  strand is a paragraph ladder: one-idea paragraphs and
  topic sentences from Class 1, transitional devices,
  outlines, and "paragraph structure" as a Class 4-only row
  (Scope and Sequence 3.7); Class 3's body makes it craft:
  paragraph development and text structure used "to hold
  the reader's attention" (printed p.31).
- **Level 15, Reading Whole Texts** — comprehension targets
  shared across all four classes (main idea, significant vs
  minor details, predictions, inference, conclusions), with
  Class 3's strategy set (context clues, rereading, self-
  correcting, predicting, questioning, clarifying,
  summarizing — printed p.22) and Class 4's context-clue
  kinds (definitions, synonyms, surrounding sentences —
  body, printed pp.40–41); Class 4's appendix is a real
  class reading list (printed pp.73–84).
- **Pencil and Lines / Penmanship** — the four basic
  handwriting joins are specified by type: diagonal and
  horizontal joins, to letters with and without ascenders,
  with printed examples (ai, ar, un; ou, vi, wi; ab, ul, it;
  ol, wh, ot — Scope and Sequence 4.6.5, Class 1 printing);
  manuscript or cursive named as the Class 3 choice
  (printed p.32).
