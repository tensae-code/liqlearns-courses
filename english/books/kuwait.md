**The primary spine (Skyline, held B halves).** A rigid, predictable architecture — four units per term-book, a fixed lesson cycle (listening / grammar / phonics-or-reading / writing), a progress test, a project, a Minimum Words list and QR-audio songs per unit — carries a sound graded grammar line: Grade 1 word-family phonics (-an/-ap … -ug/-ed), *can*, prepositions, present-continuous questions and numbers to 30; Grade 2 adds the past simple in full (with *Did …?*), digraph phonics (ph, oa, wh, ee, ch, th, oo, ue, ai), *have/has got* and a first *will*; Grade 3 adds negatives, *was/were*, comparatives, possessive adjectives, *going to* and past-simple statements; Grade 4 adds superlatives, present continuous, *should/shouldn't*, past continuous with *when* (4A only) and past-simple questions; Grade 5 reaches the present perfect (with *just* only), relative clauses with *who/which*, past continuous with *while*, the first conditional, and the set's only pronunciation strand (silent letters *wr-, -mb, -lk, -gh*). Writing never passes the guided paragraph, and every Skyline imprint carries production slips — most visibly, the 1B and 2B imprints thank their authors for the *English Pearls* series, the previous course's name, inside Skyline covers.

**The intermediate spine (Pearls).** Four learning units of seven lessons each (two readings, two grammar points, listening, speaking, and a writing lesson), a full Scope and Sequence, a glossary and an irregular-verbs table in every Student's Book. 6A works the past system (past simple, *used to*, past continuous with *when/while*) plus a first conditional, and caps writing at one paragraph; 7A is a present-system book (present simple, *have got*, *have/has to*, four modals, purpose connectors, present continuous, countable/uncountable nouns, and the present/modal passive) whose writing outcome steps up to the two-paragraph report; 9B is the strongest grammar book in the set (past perfect, third conditional, contrast linkers, passive in the continuous and present-perfect forms, reported questions only, tag questions, *so … that / such … that*) and writes compare-contrast, argumentative and expository reports. **Workbook 7A is a true companion** — same units in order, vocabulary sections keyed to the SB's two reading lessons, the SB's missing pronunciation strand supplied as micro-skill pages, both of the SB's delegated report topics present (only the first is marked "(Work Book)" in the SB's scope — corrected at the citation check), and an answer key whose every keyed answer the checker re-solved and confirmed. The Pearls books state no CEFR level for any volume, and 9B's imprint names the series the "2026 edition" against its own 2025 copyright — recorded as printed in both places.

**The secondary books (Over to You).** Student's Book 10B (Modules 3–4) is a full coursebook half: conditionals (general/first/second, third held to Unit 12), *wish*, reported statements, the passive in mixed tenses, relative clauses, a five-episode graded *Treasure Island*, and 150–170-word compositions (article, advertisement, opinion essay, informal letter, notes/messages, news story) — but its internal cross-references were never re-pointed from the one-volume edition's pagination, and the second promised Literature Time story (*Three Men in a Boat*) is not in the volume. The two Grade 12 workbooks are exam-style practice for Student's Books that are **not held**: substantial reading texts, grammar recycled at full secondary range (wish, causatives, inversion, modal passives, reported speech both directions), 200–220-word essays, progress tests and question-only Literature Time workbooks for *Henry V* (12A) and *Great Expectations* (12B). Only four exercises in each workbook are keyed (the tabbed Self-assessment items; Units 8 and 12 of 12B have none), and 12B's pages carry a second folio (+44, breaking to +46 on printed p.49) inherited from the combined workbook it was split from.

**What Kuwait's held books never teach.** No book in the held set teaches: reported commands or (outside 10B) reported statements; the second conditional as a system before 10B, or any conditional contrasted as a set before 10B/9B are read together; the present perfect contrasted with the past simple (5B teaches it with *just* only, and it is otherwise only recycled in the Grade 12 workbooks); a phonics course beyond Grade 2's digraphs and Grade 5's silent letters (Pearls has no phonics at all, and pronunciation everywhere else is a micro-skill page or a tip); listening that can be verified from the page (every listening lesson in every held book depends on QR or CD audio, and no script is printed anywhere); or free writing beyond the two-paragraph report until Over to You's word-counted compositions. There are no answer keys except Workbook 7A's assessment key and the Grade 12 workbooks' four keyed exercises each, no examination paper or marking scheme anywhere, and no Arabic support in any held book.

### 4. Honest negatives

- **No past tense is taught.** The past forms that do occur are *ran*, *got*, *went* and *fed*, and they appear strictly as phonics word-family items — *ran* in the -an family (printed p.10), *got* in the -ot cumulative text ("The hot pot got one red dot", printed p.51), and *went* alongside *fed* in the -ed cumulative text ("Ted the farmer fed the red hen and went to his bed", printed p.69); no lesson presents them as past tense, and every reading text is in the present. (anchor corrected at the independent citation check, 2026-11-03)
- **No future forms, no comparatives/superlatives, no possessives beyond the names in the character pages, no articles system** (a/the are used in models but never taught), **no plural system** beyond counting and the (s) notation in the word lists.
- **Question forms are narrow:** Wh-questions with *be* (Unit 5), *Can I have …?*, *Can a …?*, *How many … are there?*, present-continuous Yes/No questions (Unit 7) and *What do you / does he/she like …?* (Unit 6). There are no *do/does* questions outside the likes pattern, and no *have got* questions — the declarative phrase *has got* does occur once, untaught, in a completion sentence in Unit 7, Lesson 10 (printed p.53): "The pot has got red …". (anchor corrected at the independent citation check, 2026-11-03)
- **Phonics is word-family (rime) work only:** -an, -ap, -it, -am, -op, -ot, -ug, -ed. There are no consonant blends, no digraphs, no long-vowel patterns, and no letter-name/letter-sound chart in this book.
- **Writing never reaches free composition:** the ceiling is tracing/copying model words and sentences, completing sentences from initial letters, grouping rhyming words, and (once, Unit 8 Lesson 10) reordering two short sentences.
- **No listening scripts, no answer key, no glossary** beyond the per-unit Minimum Words lists; all listening and songs depend on QR-linked audio that is not in the file. No Arabic support.

### 2. Architecture

The book is built as **4 Learning Units × 7 lessons**, and every unit follows the identical Pearls lesson spine:

- **Lesson 1 — Reading** (2 printed pages): a passage with before-you-read discussion, comprehension questions, true/false-with-correction and vocabulary-matching work.
- **Lesson 2 — Grammar** (1 page): first grammar point of the unit, presented in a rule box with time-expression side notes, then controlled practice.
- **Lesson 3 — Listening** (1 page): an audio text (tracks numbered 1.1, 2.1, 3.1, 4.1 in the unit sequence, reached by QR codes printed on the page) with match/complete/answer tasks, plus a short pronunciation slot.
- **Lesson 4 — Speaking** (1 page): structured pair/group talk — think-pair-share, guessing games, role-play — with sentence starters and a "Linkers Bank".
- **Lesson 5 — Reading** (2 printed pages): a second, longer reading (blog, chat exchange, story) with group jigsaw work, Venn/T-F-NG tasks and vocabulary in context.
- **Lesson 6 — Grammar** (1 page): second grammar point.
- **Lesson 7 — Writing** (2 printed pages): one paragraph genre per unit, taught through a model-paragraph deconstruction, a topic–detail–conclusion graphic organiser, and a writing checklist that repeats in every unit (topic sentence? three details? target tense? punctuation and capitals? "Did I use AI tools to help me proofread my writing?", with the standing note to use AI wisely and not let it do the work).

Front matter carries the series Introduction, the four general educational standards for the end of Intermediate Education (Listening and Viewing; Speaking and Representing; Reading and Viewing; Writing and Representing), the Table of Contents, and a **four-page Scope and Sequence (printed pp.13–16, one page per unit)** tabulating, per unit: grammar focus, both readings with their full vocabulary lists, listening, speaking, writing, pronunciation, and the 21st-century skills and values claimed. Each unit opens with a full-page illustrated divider, a blank verso, and a Learning Outcomes page listing can-do outcomes under the same four standards. Every lesson head carries its own "I can…" statement and a Linkers Bank.

Recurring features across all four units: pronunciation is always the same micro-skill — **breaking multi-syllable words into syllables** (plus the /θ/–/ð/ "th" contrast in Unit 1 Lesson 3); a "Pronunciation Tip" box in each Reading 1; vocabulary is recycled into the end-of-book **Glossary (printed pp.74–77)** with phonemic transcription and a short definition per word, sourced from Oxford/Cambridge/Longman learner's dictionaries; and an **Irregular Verbs table (printed pp.78–79)** closes the book, followed by a References page (Canva and ChatGPT/DALL·E for images, ElevenLabs for audio, KUNA news items behind the Unit 1 listening). A one-page **Group Project** template (printed p.73) — topic and task left blank "to be provided by the teacher", with fixed project steps, presentation advice, evaluation criteria and a self/peer reflection — stands between Unit 4 and the Glossary. **There is no answer key and there are no listening scripts anywhere in the book.**

The values/21st-century-skills strand is printed, not incidental: the Scope and Sequence names skills (communication, creativity, collaboration, critical thinking, digital literacy, civic and environmental responsibility) and values (pride in national identity, respect, cooperation, empathy, honesty, kindness, bravery) per unit, and the lessons work them as content (heritage crafts, respectful tourist behaviour, environmental action, moral tales).

### 4. Honest negatives

- **The grammar ceiling is the past system plus three single points.** The book teaches past simple, *used to*, past continuous, present simple (review-level, with preference verbs), present continuous for now, future simple *will/won't*, and the first conditional. It does **not** teach: the present perfect in any form, the past simple passive or any passive at all, the second or third conditional, reported speech, comparatives or superlatives as grammar (size/quality adjectives appear as vocabulary only), question formation as a system beyond the examples inside each tense lesson, modal verbs (they are used inside texts — *should* advice appears in Unit 3 speaking, *can/could* inside readings — but never presented or practised as grammar), or articles/quantifiers as a system.
- **No phonics or decoding strand.** Pronunciation is one repeated micro-skill — syllable-breaking of long words — plus a single /θ/–/ð/ sorting exercise. There is no letter-sound teaching, no stress/intonation work beyond the Introduction's general claim, and no graded reader.
- **Writing never leaves the single paragraph.** All four writing outcomes are one paragraph (descriptive ×2, expository, narrative-favourite-story), always through the same topic–detail–conclusion organiser and the same checklist. There is no email, letter, dialogue, story-writing beyond the paragraph, note-taking for the pupil's own use, or multi-paragraph text.
- **Listening is unverifiable from the book alone:** the audio sits behind QR codes (ElevenLabs voices per the References), there are no listening scripts and no answer key, so nothing in the listening lessons can be checked from the printed page.
- **No assessment or revision apparatus:** no unit tests, no review lessons, no exam-format practice, no self-assessment beyond the writing checklists and the project reflection.
- **Reading texts are short and undemanding:** the longest (the two-part readings) run to roughly 250–300 words; there is no extended text, no poem as a set text (poetry appears only as a vocabulary word and a past-life activity), and no non-Kuwaiti literary text apart from the *Alice in Wonderland* model paragraph and the universal woodcutter tale.
- The cultural lens is almost entirely Kuwaiti and celebratory in Units 1–2; the "global perspectives" the Introduction promises arrive only in Unit 2's three-city tour, Unit 3's four-country 3Rs passage and Unit 4's general story talk.

### 2. Architecture

The same Pearls architecture as 6A, one step up: **4 Learning Units × 7 lessons**, every unit running Reading – Grammar – Listening – Speaking – Reading – Grammar – Writing, with a full-page illustrated divider, a Learning Outcomes page (the four Intermediate standards: Listening and Viewing; Speaking and Representing; Reading and Viewing; Writing and Representing), a four-page Scope and Sequence (printed pp.13–16, one page per unit, giving grammar focus, both readings with their vocabulary lists, listening, speaking, the writing outcome(s), and the 21st-century skills and values per unit), lesson-level "I can…" statements, and a Linkers Bank per speaking/writing strand (in 7A the bank is one fixed set per unit, repeated across its lessons: Unit 1 — because, also, first, finally, therefore, in addition, for example, next; Unit 3 adds the purpose connectors; Unit 4 adds to / in order to / so that).

### 4. Honest negatives

- **The grammar ceiling is mid-elementary, and one taught point is mislabelled.** The year's grammar is: present simple, *have got*, *have/has to*, four modal verbs in one lesson, purpose connectors, present continuous (now and future plans), countable/uncountable nouns with *a/an/some/any*, and the present-simple and modal passive. ### 3. Unit by unit

**Module 1 — World Issues**

**Unit 1 — The Law (printed pp.4–9).** Key words: break into, fake, invisible, techno-criminal, worthless. *Reading:* "New crimes" — an article on computer crime: identity theft and fraud, why computer crimes are hard to solve and prosecute (invisible criminals, nothing physically stolen), techno-criminals ordering without paying, breaking into business systems, spreading viruses, and using the Internet to pass confidential information and run fake charities. **Unit 4 — The Earth at Risk (printed pp.26–31).** Key words: at the expense of, devastating, logger, vital. *Reading:* "Feeding chickens is destroying the climate" — Amazon rainforest destruction: the forest's role in world climate (absorbing carbon dioxide), 70,000 square kilometres destroyed (six football pitches a minute), land cleared for soya grown to feed chickens in Western Europe, ranching, logging and oil, and the cost to the roughly 180 tribes and 220,000 people who live there.
