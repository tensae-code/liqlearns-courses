### Phonics / letters progression
- Only 16 letters are taught, one per unit, in this non-alphabetical order: B (U1), C (U2), A (U3), D (U4), I (U5), E (U6), G (U7), H (U8), O (U9), M (U10), U (U11), L (U12), N (U13), T (U14), F (U15), W (U16).
- Each letter is taught as a letter name/shape (upper- and lower-case tracing) tied to four words that begin with it; the target letter is printed in red inside the words. There is no blending instruction, no CVC word-building, and no IPA in the units themselves — IPA appears only in the Glossary.
- Vowels taught are only a, e, i, o, u, each in short-word contexts (apple/bag/can/hat; bell/pen/pencil/red; fish/chips/milk/chicken; clocks/pots; bus/sun/truck). No long-vowel sounds, digraphs, or final-letter patterns are taught.
- From Unit 9 on, Activity 5 changes from "Look and trace" to "Look, trace and write", adding missing-letter completion (m__ps, ___ango-type items).
- Letters never taught in this book: J, K, P, Q, R, S, V, X, Y, Z (10 letters).

### Grammar / sentence-pattern progression
- One model pattern per unit, always practised as listen-and-repeat, then a substitution "Let's talk", then a song. Sequence: greetings (Hi, I'm… / Bye…) → I have a… → This is my… → This is a… → I like… → It's a red… → There's a… → imperative Touch your… → How many…? + number answer, with There are… in the chant → That's a/my… → He's/She's running → imperative Look at… → He's/She's having… → I can see a… → imperative Point to your… → How many… can you see? / I can see [number].
- Questions are limited to the two "How many…?" patterns; there are no yes/no questions, no wh- questions other than How many, and no question-answer dialogues beyond pattern drills.
- Structures that appear only inside chants, songs or review stories and are never taught as patterns: There are + number + plural; It's on/near…; name possessives (Hoa's, Nam's, Lucy's, Bill's, Father's); he/she has (song/chant, U6 and U14); The boy is running; We're-type forms do not appear at all in this book.
- No grammar terminology, rules, or tables anywhere; no past tense, no future forms, no negatives (no "no/not/can't/don't" sentences in the units), no plurals rule — plurals simply appear from Unit 9.

### Vocabulary and skills progression
- Four core words per unit (64 unit words), almost all concrete nouns tied to a place theme; exceptions are the adjective red (U6), the -ing forms running (U11) and washing (U16 scene label), and personal names used as letter carriers (Bill, Lucy, Nick, Wendy). Numbers arrive late and incidentally: 1–5 in U9, extended to 10 in U16.
- The Glossary is larger than the taught set: it lists singular forms the units teach as plurals (banana, nut, lock, clock, mop, pot, lemon) and the base forms run and wash where the units label running/washing.
- Skills: listening dominates (every unit has three audio activities plus chant and song); speaking is pattern repetition and substitution; reading is limited to single words, the model sentences, and review "Read and tick" matching — there are no reading passages or stories to read independently (the Phil and Sue stories are "Listen and repeat" in this book); writing is letter tracing at first, growing to missing-letter word completion and, in Reviews/Fun times, writing whole missing initial letters.
- Vietnamese is used in the preface and Glossary only; all activity instructions in the units are in English with picture icons.

### Honest negatives — what this book never teaches
- It never teaches 10 letters of the alphabet (J, K, P, Q, R, S, V, X, Y, Z) and never presents the alphabet as a sequence or song.
- It never teaches: colours other than red; numbers before Unit 9 or above ten; days, months, time, weather (beyond "sunny" in one song); family words beyond mother and father; school subjects; body parts beyond head, hair, hand, face, foot; animals beyond cat, dog, duck, goat, horse, monkey, mouse, tiger, turtle; food beyond the U2/U5/U13 items.
- It never teaches negatives, yes/no questions, "What's this?", "Where…?", "How are you?", please/thank you routines (no "please" or "thank you" in any unit pattern), or any past/future tense.
- It never teaches letter-sound blending, spelling rules, capitalisation or punctuation rules, or handwriting of whole words from memory (writing is tracing and gap-filling only).
- It contains no extended reading text, no writing composition, and no Vietnamese explanation of grammar for learners.

### Phonics / letters progression
- One sound per unit, in this order: p (U1), k (U2), s (U3), r (U4), q (U5), x (U6), j (U7), v (U8), y (U9), z (U10), i in -ing words (U11), a in cake/grapes/table (U12), n final (U13), er final (U14), sh (U15), t final (U16).

- The position of the sound changes deliberately: initial for U1–U5, U7–U10 and sh (U15); final for x (U6), n (U13), er (U14), t (U16); medial in U11 (the i of -ing words) and U12 (the a of cake/grapes/table). The target sound is printed in red in every word.

### Grammar / sentence-pattern progression
- The big step from Grade 1 is questions with spoken answers. Sequence: statement The ___ is yummy → Is he/she …ing? Yes, he is / No, she isn't → Let's look at…! → What can you see? I can see… → What's he/she doing? He's/She's …ing / doing a quiz → Is there a/an…? Yes, there is / No, there isn't → polite request Pass me…, please / Here you are → Can you draw…? Yes, I can / No, I can't → What do you want? I want some… → Do you like…? Yes, I do / No, I don't → They're …ing (plural subject) → The ___ is / The ___ are on the table → What number is it? It's… → How old is your…? He's… → Where are the…? Over there → Is the ___ near…? No, it isn't. It's in/on….
- Present continuous is the workhorse tense (flying, riding, playing, colouring, answering, doing, having in Grade 1, sliding/driving) but is never named or explained; the same -ing forms serve as vocabulary, chant material and question frames.
- Negatives appear for the first time (isn't, don't, can't, there isn't), always inside short answers. Articles a/an are contrasted only inside "Is there a fox / an ox?". Prepositions taught in patterns: on, in, near (over there as a chunk). "some" appears only with want.
- Still no past or future tense, no "I am/you are/we are" paradigms (we're occurs in songs only), no possessive adjectives beyond my/your in single patterns, no comparatives, no grammar terminology or rules anywhere.

### Vocabulary and skills progression
- Three core words per unit (48 unit words), mostly concrete nouns, but now also action forms (sliding, riding, driving), number words (eleven–fifteen, with sixteen–twenty in use), family words (brother, sister, grandmother), and one adjective-free set overall — the book teaches almost no new adjectives (yummy comes from Grade 1 stock; near is a preposition).
- Several unit words recycle Grade 1 items in new roles: bike (G1 U1) returns as a U2 word; cake (G1 U2) returns in U12; table was used but not taught in Grade 1 songs.
- Skills: listening remains heaviest; speaking moves from pure repetition to genuine question-answer exchanges in Let's talk; reading grows — review stories change from "Listen and repeat" (Grade 1) to "Listen and read", and Self-checks add "Read and tick" over short printed dialogues, though there are still no independent reading passages; writing is missing-letter and missing-word completion throughout, plus sentence completion in Fun times — still no free writing.
- Vietnamese appears only in the Glossary; instructions stay in English with icons, and the Icons page is now a word list (Listen, Repeat, Track, Point, Say/Answer, Chant, Tick, Circle, Look, Write/Draw, Let's talk/Ask, Let's sing, Find, Play, Read, Guess, Rearrange, Match, Cross).

### Honest negatives — what this book never teaches
- It never teaches numbers 1–10 (assumed from Grade 1) or above twenty; twelve is never presented as a Lesson 1 word at all (see Slips), and sixteen–twenty are never presented as Lesson 1 words either — they surface only in chants, picture tasks and the Glossary.
- It never teaches: colours; days of the week (Saturday/Sunday occur in one song only); months; telling the time; weather words; school subjects beyond maths/quiz/question; family beyond brother, sister, grandmother (no mother/father words — Grade 1 had them); body parts; animals beyond kitten, fox, ox, zebra, zebu.
- It never teaches the past or future, "going to", comparatives, adverbs, or question words beyond What, Where, How old, Can, Do, Is.
- It never explains any grammar point, gives no spelling/blending rules, and contains no extended reading text and no guided or free writing — writing never goes beyond completing given words and frames.
- It never re-teaches or lists the Grade 1 letters/sounds; a learner starting at this book gets no alphabet summary (no alphabet chart or song in either book).

### Phonics progression

The volume's work is mostly vowel spellings and their differing sounds inside identical spellings (oo, ea, o, a), closing with the final -ng and two more blends. Where the same spelling carries two different sounds (oo in *room* vs *door*; ea in *bread* vs *meat*), the book prints the same letter label for both and offers no explanation of the difference.

### Grammar / sentence-pattern progression
Again nothing is named as grammar; the pattern ladder continues from Volume 1: identification of family members with *who* (U11) → third-person *he/she* statements and yes/no questions about jobs (U12) → *where* questions, singular and plural, with *in/on* (U13) → *there is / there are* and adjective descriptions with *is/are* (U14) → polite offers and requests with *would like* and *some* (U15) → *any* in questions and *how many* with plural nouns (U16) → *has* for he/she and *have* for they (U17) → present continuous, first for *I/you* (U18), then for *he/she* (U19), then for *it* with animals, alongside the first ability *can* in "What can you see?" (U20). By the end of Grade 3 the learner has present simple and present continuous in the persons I/you/he/she/it/they, but no past tense of any verb.

### Vocabulary and skills progression

Reading tasks in Lesson 3 grow slightly (short photo-description and family texts with completion questions, a True/False zoo text in U20). ### Honest negatives — what this book never teaches
- No past tense at all: no *was/were*, no past verbs; Grade 3 as a whole (both volumes) contains no past-time grammar. "Last" time words (last week, yesterday) never appear in this volume.
- No days of the week and no months: a Grade 3 pupil finishing both volumes still has no English for Monday or January (they arrive in Grade 4).
- No clock time, no prices or money, no directions or road language, no weather words.
- No comparatives or superlatives (*bigger, biggest*); *big/small* are used as plain adjectives only.
- No future forms; *would like* (U15) is the only modal-like structure beyond Volume 1's *can/can't*.
- No questions for *they*: "What are they doing?" and "Do they have …?" never appear; *they* is used only in statements (U17, and *they're* from U13).
- No negative present continuous ("He isn't running") and no continuous questions for *you/they* beyond "What are you doing?".
- Family vocabulary stops at *mother, father, brother, sister*: no *grandfather/grandmother, aunt, uncle, baby*, although grandparents appear in pictures and in the U13 reading answers.
- Zoo vocabulary is five animals; no farm animals are taught anywhere in Grade 3.
- As in Volume 1: no grammar explanations, no answer keys, no handwriting instruction; Vietnamese only in preface and Wordlist.

Every unit follows the same seven-part architecture: Getting Started (a “Listen and read” dialogue) → A Closer Look 1 (Vocabulary + Pronunciation) → A Closer Look 2 (Grammar) → Communication (Everyday English dialogue + a topic section) → Skills 1 (Reading + Speaking) → Skills 2 (Listening + Writing, usually a ~70-word paragraph) → Looking Back (vocabulary + grammar revision) → Project (group poster/presentation) with a “Now I can …” self-check grid. Grammar is presented in short English-only “Remember!” boxes; there are no Vietnamese explanations of grammar anywhere.

### Grammar progression
In the book’s own naming and order: present simple (U1) → simple sentences, i.e. subject–verb (–object/–adverb) structure (U2) → past simple (U3) → comparisons with like, different from, (not) as … as (U4) → some, a lot of / lots of for quantity, with How many / How much practised alongside (U5) → prepositions of time and place at / in / on (U6) → It for distance and should / shouldn’t (U7) → connectors although / though and however (U8) → Yes / No questions (U9) → present continuous (U10) → future simple (will) and possessive pronouns (U11) → articles a / an / the (U12). The Reviews recycle these cumulatively. Tenses are introduced one at a time with form-and-use “Remember!” boxes and controlled drills; there is no consolidated grammar reference or irregular-verb table in the back matter — the only back matter is the Glossary.

### Pronunciation progression
U1 /ə/ and /ɜː/ → U2 /f/ and /v/ → U3 /t/, /d/, /ɪd/ (-ed endings) → U4 /ʃ/ and /ʒ/ → U5 /ɒ/ and /ɔː/ → U6 /tʃ/ and /dʒ/ → U7 /aɪ/ and /eɪ/ → U8 /ɪə/ and /eə/ → U9 stress in two-syllable words → U10 stress in three-syllable words → U11 sentence stress → U12 rising and falling intonation for questions. Reviews test the same contrasts in odd-one-out format. Sounds are taught by listen-and-repeat sorting and sentence practice; no phonemic chart is printed.

### Vocabulary and skills progression
Vocabulary is strictly topical and cumulative by theme: hobbies and liking verbs → health → volunteering → arts → food, drink and measurement → school → transport and signs → film → festivals → energy → future transport → the English-speaking world. Each unit’s new words reappear in Looking Back and in the Reviews, and all are collected in the Glossary (printed 136–139), arranged by unit, each entry giving part of speech, a phonemic transcription and a Vietnamese meaning. Skills work is uniform and scaffolded: every unit has a dialogue to listen to and read, one substantial reading text with comprehension tasks, one listening text, pair/group speaking built on a model, and one guided ~70-word writing product (paragraph, email, informal invitation letter, or diary entry in U12); each unit closes with a group Project (poster, campaign, exhibition or presentation) and a three-level “Now I can …” self-assessment.

### Honest negatives — what this book never teaches
- No present perfect, past continuous, passive voice, conditionals of any kind, reported speech, or relative clauses anywhere; the future is will only — going to is never presented as a grammar point.
- No systematic treatment of comparatives/superlatives of adjectives (only the like / different from / (not) as … as patterns in U4); no modal verbs are taught as a set — should/shouldn’t is the only modal given a grammar box (can / must / could appear incidentally, e.g. as distractors in U7 Looking Back multiple-choice, but are not taught).
- Countable vs uncountable nouns are never named as a grammar topic; quantity is taught only through some / a lot of / lots of and How many / How much practice (any appears in exercises without a rule box).
- Wh-questions are used constantly but never taught as a system; only Yes / No questions get a grammar box (U9). Word order, question formation with do/does in the present and past is practised inside the tense units, not isolated.
- No irregular-verb list or verb tables, no grammar summary appendix, and no answer keys in the student’s book; grammar explanations are brief English-only boxes with no Vietnamese support.
- No explicit teaching of the phonemic alphabet, spelling rules, punctuation or paragraph structure beyond one-line “Writing tip” boxes (mind map in U1; invitation formulas in U4); writing length never exceeds about 70 words.

### Grammar progression
In the book’s own naming and order: verbs of liking/disliking + gerunds and + to-infinitives (U1) → comparative forms of adverbs (U2) → simple and compound sentences (U3) → Yes/No and Wh-questions, and countable and uncountable nouns (U4) → zero article (U5) → future simple (recycled) and the first conditional, including unless (U6) → complex sentences with adverb clauses of time (U7) → adverbs of frequency and present simple for future events (U8) → past continuous, contrasted with past simple (U9) → prepositions of place and time (in / for / by) and possessive pronouns after of (U10) → reported speech (statements) (U11) → reported speech (questions) (U12). The year thus moves from clause combining (compound → complex) to a first full reported-speech system; articles are completed by adding the zero article to Grade 7’s a / an / the. As in Grade 7 there is no consolidated grammar reference or verb table in the back matter — the only back matter is the Glossary.

### Pronunciation progression
U1 /ʊ/ and /uː/ → U2 /ə/ and /ɪ/ → U3 /ʊə/ and /ɔɪ/ → U4 /k/ and /g/ → U5 /n/ and /ŋ/ → U6 /br/ and /pr/ → U7 /bl/ and /kl/ → U8 /sp/ and /st/ → U9 stress in words ending in -al and -ous → U10 stress in words ending in -ese and -ee → U11 sentence stress → U12 intonation for making lists. The pattern mirrors Grade 7 (eight consonant/vowel contrast units, then stress and intonation), and the Reviews test the same material in odd-one-out and stress-pattern formats.

### Vocabulary and skills progression
Vocabulary themes: leisure and liking expressions → countryside life → teen clubs, social media and stress → ethnic-group lifestyles → customs and traditions → lifestyles → environmental protection → shopping → natural disasters → communication technology → science and technology in the future → outer space. All unit vocabulary is collected in the Glossary (printed 136–139), arranged by unit with part of speech, phonemic transcription and Vietnamese meaning. Skills demands step up from Grade 7: writing is 80–100 words throughout and adds new genres — a formal notice (U7), safety instructions (U9) and an opinion paragraph (U11) — alongside emails, descriptive and advantages/disadvantages paragraphs; reading adds advertisements (U11), news articles (U9) and notices (Review 4); speaking adds information-gap card work (U3 clubs, U8 shopping centre) and fact-panel presentations (U7 Vu Quang, U12 planets). Projects remain group poster/presentation work with the same “Now I can …” self-check.

### Honest negatives — what this book never teaches
- No present perfect (simple or continuous), no passive voice, no second or third conditional (only the first conditional, U6), and no relative clauses anywhere.
- Reported speech is limited to statements (U11) and Wh-questions (U12): reported Yes/No questions with if / whether are never taught, and imperatives/requests in reported speech are not covered; backshift is presented only for present simple, present continuous and will.
- Superlative forms are used (e.g. in facts and quizzes) but never taught as a grammar point; comparative adjectives from Grade 7 are not re-systematised — only comparative adverbs are new (U2).
- The past simple is never re-taught as a full paradigm; it appears only as the interrupting tense beside the past continuous (U9) and inside reported-speech backshift. Present continuous likewise appears only as input to backshift, not as a revised topic.
- Articles are never given as one complete system in this book: only the zero article is new (U5); a / an / the rules are assumed from Grade 7.
- No verb tables, irregular-verb list, grammar appendix or answer keys in the student’s book (the single printed key is the Unit 9 questionnaire key on printed p. 101); all grammar explanation is in brief English-only “Remember!” boxes, with no Vietnamese-language grammar support.

### Grammar progression
In the order taught: question words before to-infinitives and phrasal verbs (1) (U1) → double comparatives and phrasal verbs (2) (U2) → modal verbs in first conditional sentences (U3) → past continuous, and wish + past simple (U4) → present perfect (U5) → verbs + to-infinitive and verbs + V-ing (U6) → reported speech for Yes/No questions only (U7) → relative pronouns who/which/whose (U8) → defining relative clauses, with omission of an object pronoun (U9) → non-defining relative clauses (U10) → suggest/advise/recommend + V-ing or a clause with should (U11) → adverbial clauses of concession, result and reason (U12). The arc is: infinitive patterns and phrasal verbs first, one tense contrast in the middle (past continuous, present perfect), then a three-unit clause-building run (reported questions → relative clauses, defining then non-defining) and a final unit of adverbial clauses. Rules are given only inside each unit's "Remember!" boxes; there is no consolidated grammar reference at the back.

### Pronunciation progression
U1 vowel revision /æ/, /ɑː/, /e/ → U2 diphthong revision /aʊ/, /əʊ/, /eə/ → U3 /h/ and /r/ → U4 /m/ and /l/ (with light vs dark /l/) → U5 /j/ and /w/ → U6 consonant clusters /fl/ and /fr/ → U7 clusters /sl/ and /sn/ → U8 word stress in -ic and -ious words → U9 word stress in -ion and -ity words → U10 rhythm in sentences → U11 stress on all words in (short) sentences → U12 intonation in statements used as questions. The sequence moves from single sounds, through clusters, to suffix-driven word stress, then to sentence-level rhythm, stress and intonation.

### Vocabulary and skills progression
Vocabulary is strictly thematic, one topic set per unit (community → city → health/time → heritage → experiences → lifestyle change → nature → tourism → language itself → the planet → devices → jobs), recycled in Looking Back and the Reviews, and collected in the Glossary with IPA and Vietnamese glosses. Skills work is highly uniform: every unit has one reading text (guessing words in context, matching headings/definitions, true/false and multiple choice, and reference questions — "the word 'them'/'that'/'it' refers to" recur), one speaking task growing from pair exchanges to group presentations, one listening task (specific information, true/false, gap-fill of one or two words), and one guided writing task. Writing grows in length and genre across the year: paragraphs of about 100 words in Units 1–3, 100–120 words from Unit 4 on, an email in Unit 6 and Unit 12, and the only summary in Unit 10. Every unit ends with a Project (poster, survey, interview, leaflet or plan) and a "Now I can …" self-assessment tick table. Reviews 1–4 (two printed pages each) consolidate language and skills after every three units.

### Honest negatives — what this book never teaches
- **Examination preparation: none.** Grade 9 is the final year of Vietnamese lower secondary school, yet the book contains no examination-preparation apparatus of any kind: no past papers, no model/mock tests, no timed test practice, no exam strategy or technique section, no entrance-examination (grade 10 entry) material, and no answer key. The four Reviews are short two-page language-and-skills consolidations, not mock examinations; nothing in the book is presented as test practice for any named examination.
- No grammar reference appendix, no irregular-verb table and no verb lists at the back: the only back matter is the Glossary (pp.136–139). The present perfect (U5) is supported only by a five-verb past simple / past participle table inside one exercise.
- Reported speech is taught only for Yes/No questions (U7): reported statements, reported Wh-questions as a grammar topic, reported commands/requests and reporting verbs beyond ask / want to know are not taught as grammar (Wh-questions appear only inside exercises there).
- The passive voice is never taught as a grammar point in this book (passive forms occur inside reading texts but are never presented or drilled), and there is no second or third conditional (only modal verbs inside first conditionals, U3), no future-tense unit, and no systematic revision of the present simple/continuous or past simple, which are assumed from earlier grades.
- No listening transcripts / audioscripts and no tapescript appendix are printed in the student's book, although almost every unit has two or three audio-tracked listening tasks.
- No extended literary reading: there are no poems, stories or drama extracts; all reading texts are dialogues, brochures, announcements, articles and short informational passages written for the book.

### Grammar progression
In the order taught: present simple vs. present continuous (U1) → the future with will and be going to, and the passive voice (U2) → compound sentences, and to-infinitives and bare infinitives (U3) → past simple vs. past continuous with when and while (U4) → present perfect, and gerunds and to-infinitives (U5) → passive voice with modals (U6) → comparative and superlative adjectives (U7) → relative clauses, defining and non-defining, with who, that, which, whose (U8) → reported speech — statements and both question types (U9) → conditional sentences Type 1 and Type 2 (U10). The book revisits lower-secondary material at a higher level and builds it in layers: the plain passive (U2) returns with modals (U6); infinitive forms are split across two units (to-/bare infinitives in U3, gerunds vs to-infinitives in U5); relative clauses are done in one combined unit here rather than split across two units as in the Grade 9 book; and reported speech is done whole in U9, where the Grade 9 book had taught only reported Yes/No questions. Rules live only in each unit's Remember!/Tips boxes; there is no consolidated grammar reference at the back.

### Pronunciation progression
Consonant blends /br/, /kr/, /tr/ (U1) → blends /kl/, /pl/, /gr/, /pr/ (U2) → stress in two-syllable words (U3) → stress in two-syllable words with the same spelling, noun vs verb (U4) → stress in three-syllable nouns (U5) → stress in three-syllable adjectives and verbs (U6) → stress in words with more than three syllables, including primary and secondary stress (U7) → sentence stress (U8) → rhythm (U9) → intonation (U10). A steady climb from sounds, through word stress of increasing word length, to sentence stress, rhythm and finally intonation; every unit's Looking Back recycles its own pronunciation point.

### Vocabulary and skills progression
Vocabulary is one topic set per unit (family → environment → music → community → inventions → gender → international organisations → learning → environment protection → ecotourism), recycled in Looking Back, in the Reviews and in the Glossary (IPA + Vietnamese meaning). Two environment units (U2 and U9) and the ecotourism unit (U10) form a deliberate green thread that the Reviews also reuse. Skills are taught with explicit strategy support that the Grade 9 book does not give to the same degree: Tips boxes on choosing a reading title (U2), labelling a diagram in listening (U5), primary/secondary stress marks (U7), and presentation structure for speaking (U9). Writing is the skill that visibly steps up from the Grade 9 book: the standard length is 120–150 words (blog in U3 at about 120 words; application letter in U4; benefits-of-an-invention paragraph in U5; paragraph in U6, U7, U8, U9; website advertisement in U10), with outlines, diagrams and model texts supplied. Each unit also carries a Culture/CLIL text (British family values, carbon footprint, chau van singing, Save the Children, computer hardware, women's football, FAO, modern schools, Earth Hour, tourism impact) — a section the Grade 9 book does not have — and ends with a research/presentation Project and a "Now I can …" self-assessment.

### Honest negatives — what this book never teaches
- **Examination preparation: none.** Grade 10 is the first year of upper secondary school, not a final/examination year (that is Grade 12 in Viet Nam), and the book makes no move towards examinations anyway: there are no past papers, no model or mock tests, no timed practice, no exam-strategy or test-technique sections, and no answer key anywhere in the student's book. The four Reviews (four printed pages each) are language-and-skills consolidations, not tests, and nothing is labelled as preparation for the national upper-secondary graduation examination or any university entrance examination.
- No grammar reference appendix, no irregular-verb table and no tense chart at the back: the only back matter is the Glossary (printed pp.124–131). The present perfect (U5) is taught without any printed list of irregular past participles.
- Conditional sentences stop at Type 1 and Type 2: Type 3 conditionals and mixed conditionals are never taught. The passive is taught only in its simple forms and with modals — no passive continuous or perfect passives as grammar points. Reported speech (U9) does not cover reported commands, requests or offers as a separate pattern (reporting verbs such as advise appear in a dialogue but are not presented as a rule).
- No listening transcripts / audioscripts are printed in the student's book, although every unit and review has numbered audio tasks (the track numbers run to 81 by Review 4).
- No literature component: there are no poems, short stories, novel extracts or plays; all reading texts are informational, functional or written-for-the-book passages, the longest being a single page.
- No phonemic chart and no pronunciation reference appendix, despite a ten-unit pronunciation strand that assumes learners can read IPA stress marks and transcriptions (the Glossary supplies transcriptions word by word only).

### Grammar progression
In the book's own naming and order: Past simple vs. Present perfect (U1) → Modal verbs: must, have to, and should (U2) → Stative verbs in the continuous form + Linking verbs (U3) → Gerunds as subjects and objects (U4) → Present participle and past participle clauses (U5) → To-infinitive clauses (U6) → Perfect gerunds and perfect participle clauses (U7) → Cleft sentences with It is/was … that/who … (U8) → Linking words and phrases (U9) → Compound nouns (U10). The arc moves from tense contrast and modality through non-finite clause types (-ing, to-infinitive, perfect forms) to sentence-level emphasis and cohesion; each point is taught in a single "Remember!" box inside the unit, with no consolidated grammar appendix. Reviews 1–4 recycle the points in the same order.

### Pronunciation progression
Entirely connected-speech and intonation work — no individual consonant or vowel sounds are taught: strong and weak forms of auxiliary verbs (U1) → contracted forms (U2) → linking final consonants to initial vowels (U3) → elision of vowels (U4) → sentence stress and rhythm (U5) → intonation in statements, commands, and lists (U6) → intonation in Wh- and Yes/No questions (U7) → intonation in invitations, suggestions, and polite requests (fall-rise) (U8) → intonation in choice questions (U9) → intonation in question tags (U10). Review 3 explicitly consolidates the four intonation patterns of Units 6–8.

### Vocabulary and skills progression
Vocabulary is strictly topic-bound, five or so head items introduced per unit with matching/definition tasks, then recycled in Looking Back crosswords and the Glossary: health and fitness → generational differences → cities and smart living → ASEAN → global warming → heritage → education after school → teen independence → social issues → ecosystems. Reading texts progress from short advice articles and news items to magazine features, always for "main ideas and specific information" with heading-matching, true/false, and short-answer tasks. Listening is always for main/specific information (talks, interviews, conversations). Speaking moves from guided pair exchanges with "Tips" boxes to group discussions and project presentations; the Everyday English strand cycles through functional language (offering help, permission, certainty/uncertainty, compliments, warnings, directions, appointments, best wishes, disappointment/sympathy, likes/dislikes). Writing is the most visibly graded strand: short message (35–45 words) → opinion essay, article, proposal, and leaflet (120–150 words) in Units 2–6 → request letter (140–170 words) → articles/proposals/essays at 150–180 words in Units 8–10; Reviews repeat the same genres at the same lengths.

### Honest negatives — what this book never teaches
- **Examination preparation: none.** There is no apparatus for the national graduation examination anywhere in this book — no past papers, no model/mock test, no exam-strategy section, no marking scheme, and no answer key. The four Reviews are ordinary coursebook revision (mixed open exercises, crosswords, some multiple choice), not exam-format tests. The word "exam" occurs only inside lesson content (e.g. the university entrance exam mentioned in Unit 7 texts and a listening about studying abroad in Review 3), never as a test the student is being prepared to sit.
- No reported speech, no passive voice as a grammar topic, no conditional sentences as a grammar topic, no relative clauses as a grammar topic (to-infinitive clauses modifying nouns in U6 are the nearest approach), no articles, no future-forms unit (the future is used in texts but never taught as a system), and no question-tag formation (U10 teaches only the intonation of tags).
- No grammar reference section, irregular-verb list, or phonemic chart anywhere; the Glossary (words + IPA + Vietnamese meanings) is the sole reference back matter.
- No listening transcripts or tapescripts are printed in the student's book.
- Pronunciation never covers single sounds (vowels/consonants in isolation); it is suprasegmental only, as listed above.

### Grammar progression
Past simple vs. Past continuous (U1) → Articles, review and extension (U2) → Verbs with prepositions + Relative clauses referring to a whole sentence (U3) → Present perfect, review and extension + Double comparatives (U4) → Simple, compound, and complex sentences, review and extension (U5) → Active and passive causatives (U6) → Adverbial clauses of manner and result (U7) → Adverbial clauses of condition and comparison (U8) → Three-word phrasal verbs (U9) → Reported speech: reporting orders, requests, offers, and advice (U10). The design is explicit about consolidation: Units 2, 4, and 5 are labelled "review and extension" of earlier-course grammar, and the genuinely new points (causatives, adverbial-clause families, three-word phrasal verbs, to-infinitive reported speech) are clause-combining devices of exactly the kind the exam-format sentence-combination and transformation items in the Reviews test. Grammar is again taught only in "Remember!" boxes — there is no grammar appendix.

### Pronunciation progression
The first three units finish the vowel system with diphthongs — /eɪ, əʊ/ (U1), /ɔɪ, aɪ, aʊ/ (U2), /ɪə, eə, ʊə/ (U3) — the only units in either Grade 11 or Grade 12 devoted to individual sounds. Then: unstressed words in connected speech (U4) → stressing auxiliary and modal verbs (U5) → homophones (U6) → linking /r/ between two vowels (U7) → assimilation (U8) → sentence stress and rhythm (U9, repeating the Grade 11 Unit 5 topic) → intonation in questions (revision) (U10, a summary of the Grade 11 Units 6–10 intonation work). Reviews additionally test pronunciation in the exam's two formats: underlined-part-differs and main-stress-differs MCQs.

### Vocabulary and skills progression
Vocabulary stays topic-bound and is recycled through the Glossary and the exam-style closest/opposite-meaning items: life stories → cultural diversity → green living → urbanisation → work → science and technology → mass media → wildlife conservation → career paths → lifelong learning. Reading lengthens and hardens across the year (paired opposing opinion pieces in U7, text-structure and writer's-purpose questions in U9 and the Reviews) and is repeatedly set in the exam's MCQ formats. Writing lengths rise to a 180–200-word ceiling (biography, opinion essay, reports, home-robot essay, tiger report, lifelong-learning article) alongside practical documents (application letter, line-graph and pie-chart descriptions, a CV). Speaking and listening remain classroom-format (discussions, information gaps, projects; talks and conversations for main/specific information), capped by Appendix 1's conversation strategies. Two school-leaving themes bookend the year: careers (U9) and learning after school (U10).

### Honest negatives — what this book never teaches
- **Examination preparation: present, but limited — checked explicitly because this is the final year.** What the book DOES contain: (1) Appendix 2, "Exam Strategies" (printed pp. 145–146), a strategy table for each multiple-choice question type in the national graduation exam format; (2) "A+ EXAM TIPS" badges beside exercises throughout the book pointing to pp. 145–146; (3) all four Reviews written largely in exam format ("Mark the letter A, B, C, or D to indicate …"), including cloze passages, error identification, sentence combination/transformation, and exam-style reading questions; (4) many unit exercises (especially Reading and Looking Back) in the same A–D format. What it does NOT contain: **no past papers, no complete model or mock examination, no timed test, no marking scheme or answer key, and no score or grade guidance** — the strategies are generic technique advice (e.g. eliminate grammatically incorrect options, watch for distractors), not practice on real papers, and the book never names the graduation examination it is clearly shadowing. Speaking and writing are never presented as exam tasks.
- No reported statements or reported questions: Unit 10's reported speech covers only orders, requests, offers, and advice reported with to-infinitive clauses; tense backshift for statements is not taught.
- No passive voice as a topic (only active/passive causatives in U6), no conditional-sentence system (only adverbial clauses of condition inside U8), no inversion or emphatic structures beyond Grade 11's cleft sentences, no future perfect or past perfect unit.
- No grammar reference appendix, irregular-verb list, or answer key; no listening transcripts printed in the student's book.
