### Unit by unit

**Unit 1 — We learn ABC (printed pp. 7–8).** The alphabet A–Z, each letter with
its name in transcription and a pictured animal word (Aa for snake through Zz
for zebra), letter recognition, choral and pair reading of the alphabet,
copying the letters in a handwriting grid, spelling your own name aloud.
Skills: listening, speaking, letter formation. No sentences are taught yet.

**Unit 2 — All about me (printed pp. 9–19).** Lessons: Hello. What's your
name? (p. 9); Where are you from? (p. 10); How old are you? (p. 11); I've got
two sisters (p. 12); This is my family (p. 13); What's your address? (p. 14);
pronunciation, grammar and classwork/homework (pp. 15–19). Grammar, in the
book's own presentation: greetings and introductions with the verb "be"
contractions (I'm, my name's, what's); where-from questions with he/she and
his/her; numbers 0 (zero)/oh to 12; the articles a/an in single examples
(a baby — an apple, p. 11); family words and plurals (brother — brothers);
have got for family possession (I've got two sisters; Have you got …? Yes, I
have / No, I haven't, p. 14); there are for family size (p. 13); addresses and
telephone numbers. Vocabulary: greetings by time of day, countries (Uzbekistan,
England, America, Kazakhstan), family members. Skills: chain drills, pair
interviews, bingo, copying and completing written homework.

**Unit 3 — At home and at work (printed pp. 20–30).** Lessons: I live in a …
(p. 20); What number is your house? (p. 21); What's your job? (p. 22); Where do

### Honest negatives — what this book never teaches

Checked across all 14 units, the three reviews and the Grammar Reference,
which is the book's own complete grammar inventory (printed pp. 168–177):

- Present perfect: never taught and never used as a tense. The only "have"
  forms are have got (possession) and have for routine activities.
- Present perfect continuous: absent.
- Past perfect: absent.
- Past tenses of any kind: absent. There is no past simple anywhere in the
  book; every text, including the stories, is narrated in the present.
- Future forms: absent. There is no will, no going to; tomorrow appears only
  as a vocabulary word with the present simple.
- Passive voice: absent.
- Conditionals: absent — no type 0, 1, 2 or 3, and no if-sentences are taught
  at all. The nearest approach is when for time habits (printed p. 115).

### What "1st year" means here — the book's full course identity

"1st YEAR" on the cover does not mean the first year of a multi-year
advanced course, and it does not mean Grade 1. The title page settles it:
this is the textbook for the 5th class (the fifth year of school, pupils
about 11 years old) of the Russian secondary school. In the Russian system
this book was written for, English began in the 5th class, so the 5th-class

third named author of this edition.

### Does any of it duplicate Fly High 5?

No text, exercise, picture or page is shared with Fly High 5; the two books
were written independently, in different languages of instruction, for
different school systems. They cover the same beginner territory, so the
topic lists overlap in the way any two first-year courses overlap: both teach
the alphabet, numbers (Fly High to 12 early and 100 later; Starkov to 20
orally and 100 in Unit 10), family, the home, school, colours, the body, days
and months, seasons and weather, animals, telling the time, and the same
small grammar core — the verb "be", have (Fly High: have got; Starkov: plain
have/has), there is/are, can and must, the present simple and the present
continuous. The treatment is wholly different: Fly High is a colour
communicative course built on games, songs and pair work in English with
Uzbek support, while Starkov is a black-and-white reading-first course in
which Russian explains everything and English is built up word by word under
a syllable-reading system. Nothing in the Starkov book can stand in for, or
be merged into, the Fly High 5 record; it belongs to a Russian Grade 5
record, not to Uzbekistan.

### Honest negatives — what this book never teaches

Checked across all 25 units and against the Reference section (printed
pp. 217–229), which is the book's own complete statement of its grammar:

- Present perfect: absent. There is no have + participle form anywhere.
- Present perfect continuous: absent.
- Past perfect: absent.
- Past tenses of any kind: absent. The book teaches no past simple; every
  text, including the stories in Units 23 and 24, is told in the present.
  (The word "already" appears once, glossed as a vocabulary item inside a
  joke on printed p. 200; it is not a grammar point and no perfect tense
  accompanies it.)
- Future forms: absent — no will and no going to.
- Passive voice: absent.
- Conditionals: absent — no types and no if-clause teaching at all.
- Reported speech: absent. Speech is always direct and quoted; the nearest
  approach is "he says / he thinks" inside the William story (printed
  pp. 209–212), which introduces direct speech, never reported speech.
- Relative clauses: not taught. Who, which and whose are taught as question
  words only. A single relative clause occurs untaught inside one reading
  text ("her small daughter, who is reading a book", printed p. 202); it is
  never named, explained or practised.
- Question tags: absent — none taught and none noticed anywhere in the book.
- Exam preparation: none. The starred units (7, 18, 25) are revision units
  with self-check lists, not examination practice; there are no test papers
  or exam-format tasks.
- Answer keys: none printed. Self-check is by wordlists of words the pupil
  "must know and be able to read" (printed pp. 73, 162, 216), not by answers
  to exercises.
- Listening scripts and audio: there is no cassette, disc or audio reference
  anywhere in the book. Listening exists only as live teacher speech in
  Раздел I (for example "listen to and correct the statements", printed
  p. 3); no script for it is printed.
- Dictionary skills: the opposite of a negative — Memo 8 (printed p. 231)
  is a full, explicit lesson in using a dictionary, and the book ends with
  its own English–Russian dictionary (printed pp. 232–239).
- Phonics and alphabet work: likewise a strength, not a gap — the copybook
  (printed pp. 14–16), the syllable-type reading system that structures
  Units 1–13, Reference sections I–V (printed pp. 217–219), the

#### Honest negatives — what this book never teaches

Checked across the whole classbook, workbook, extensive reading and all three language versions of the Grammar Reference:

- **Present Perfect:** not taught. There is no lesson, Remember box or reference section on have + past participle, and the form is not presented anywhere as a pattern. "Have you got ...?" (for example Unit 11 Lesson 2, p. 67) is the possessive have got construction, not the Present Perfect.
- **Present Perfect Continuous:** not taught; no trace as a named or modelled pattern.
- **Past Perfect:** not taught. Past narration is done entirely with the Past Simple, supplemented late in the book by the Past Continuous.
- **Passive voice:** not taught. No lesson or reference section presents be + past participle, and the book's texts and models are written in the active voice throughout.
- **Conditionals:** not taught as grammar — no conditional types, no if-clause lesson, no reference section. A few ordinary sentences containing "if" occur incidentally in reading texts and examples (for example the some/any note in Grammar Reference section 3, and pupils' model sentences about taking a tent "if it's hot" on p. 51), but the book never names, classifies or practises conditional sentences.
- **Reported speech:** not taught. Stories and dialogues use direct speech abundantly, and Unit 7 teaches "want someone to do something", but there is no reporting of statements, questions or commands and no backshift.
- **Relative clauses:** not taught or named, and there is no reference section on relative pronouns. Isolated relative clauses occur unremarked inside reading texts, but pupils are never asked to form or analyse one.
- **Question tags:** not taught; no tag questions appear in the lessons or the reference.
- **Future forms:** not taught. There is no "will" or "going to" lesson or reference section; future time is expressed through present forms, "want to" and timetable-style statements.
- **Exam preparation:** none. The book contains no examination papers, no test-format practice and no timed tests. Its only assessment-like material is the three Learning Review units (5, 11 and 15), which consist of games, projects and "I can ..." self-assessment quizzes checked with the teacher. (The back cover's phrase "regular revision and tests" refers to this review material; no actual tests are printed.)
- **Answer keys:** none printed. Workbook and classbook exercises have no key anywhere in the volume; the recurring instruction is to check answers in the Wordlist, in the Grammar Reference, or with the teacher (for example Unit 11 Lesson 5, p. 70: "Now check your answers with your teacher").
- **Listening scripts:** none printed. Listening work is pervasive and is built on a separate Class Cassette, whose existence is stated in the "Dear Pupil" letter (PDF p. 4) and on the back cover, but no tapescript of any listening text appears in this book.
- **Dictionary skills:** present, and worth recording as a positive against expectation. Beyond constant "Find the words in the Wordlist" work, the book twice shows dictionary-style entries and asks what a dictionary tells you — Unit 13 Lesson 2 Activity 3b–3c (p. 79, entries for "rainfall" and "average") and Unit 13 Lesson 5 Activity 2b (p. 82, entries for "cut down", "destroy" and "oxygen") — and Unit 11 Lesson 5 (p. 70) tests Wordlist and Grammar Table use as named learning skills. What the book does not contain is training in using a standalone monolingual learner's dictionary; its dictionary work is all done with its own trilingual Wordlist and these sample entries.

### Honest negatives — Grade 8 book

Checked item by item against the whole book, contents and Grammar Section:

- **Present perfect (active): never taught.** No lesson and no Grammar Section entry presents the active Present perfect simple. Its forms are used inside Unit 5 news texts and gap-fills (printed 50–51, 60) and learners must supply them, but the tense is never named or explained. Only the **Present perfect passive** is explained, in one short Grammar Section entry (p. 153).
- **Present perfect continuous: never taught.** One form occurs once, inside a reading text in the Hobbies lesson (p. 4), where a writer says he has been playing football for more than seven years. It is not pointed out, named or practised.
- **Past perfect: taught, but thinly.** It is the named focus of the Shakespeare lessons (pp. 44–45), practised by sentence matching, and explained in the Grammar Section (p. 153). There is no Past perfect continuous anywhere.
- **Passive voice: taught.** Present and past passive in Lessons (pp. 40–41, 88–89), active-to-passive rewriting in reviews, and a Grammar Section entry (p. 153) covering Present simple, Past simple and Present perfect passive. No future passive, no modal passive, no passive with two objects.
- **Conditionals: first and second only, and only in use.** Unit 6, Lessons 1–2 (pp. 62–63) is built on first-conditional superstitions and second-conditional advice ("If I were you…"), and the contents table names "first and second conditional"; the forms are never explained and there is no Grammar Section entry. **Third conditional: absent** — no form, example or exercise anywhere. No mixed conditionals.
- **Reported speech: practised, never explained.** Unit 5, Lessons 5–6 (pp. 54–55) requires rewriting direct speech as reported speech, with one worked example, and Test 3 tests it; but no rules (backshift, reporting verbs beyond "said", time-word changes, reported questions or commands) are stated anywhere, and the Grammar Section has no reported-speech entry. Reported questions are only met as a listening-completion task (the disaster interview, p. 55).
- **Relative clauses: one practice exercise only.** The Edison gap-fill with who/whose/where/which/when (p. 31) is the whole treatment; relative pronouns are named in the contents for that block, but no rule is given, defining and non-defining clauses are not distinguished, and there is no Grammar Section entry.
- **Question tags: never taught.** A tag appears incidentally in a Review dialogue gap-fill (p. 84) and in a tapescript (p. 132, "don't you?"); there is no presentation, rule or practice set for tags.
- **Modal perfects (would have / could have / should have + participle): absent.** None is presented or required anywhere in the book.
- **Exam preparation: absent.** The book contains its own four course tests (see above) but no external-exam formats, techniques, timed practice or exam strategy. (Unit 6's superstitions mention a "lucky pen when writing an exam" only as content, p. 63.)
- **Answer keys: none in this book.** No exercise answers are printed anywhere in the Student's Book. The back cover states that the accompanying Teacher's Guide contains "keys to all exercises" (see Cross-references).
- **Listening scripts / audio references: scripts present, audio itself external.** Every listening task refers to a tape/cassette (cassette icons in the margins), and full tapescripts for all listening activities and for the tests' listening exercises are printed at pp. 120–140. The recordings themselves are not part of this book.

### Honest negatives — Grade 9 book

Checked item by item against the whole book, contents and Grammar Section:

- **Present perfect (active): never taught as a tense.** There is no lesson and no Grammar Section entry for the active Present perfect simple. As in Book 8, only the **Present perfect passive** is explained, inside the Passive entry (p. 144). Present perfect forms occur in texts and in the third-conditional formula (see Slips), but the tense itself is never presented.
- **Present perfect continuous: absent entirely.** No presentation, no Grammar Section entry, and no exercise requires it.
- **Past perfect: no standalone treatment.** Unlike Book 8, this book has no Past perfect lesson or Grammar Section entry. The form is named only inside the third-conditional formula in the Grammar Section (p. 145) and is used in that conditional's examples; learners meet past perfect forms in stories (for example the complaint letter, p. 81) without any explanation.
- **Passive voice: taught, at the same ceiling as Book 8.** Present simple and past simple passive in the Money unit (pp. 38–41) and in reviews, plus Present perfect passive and passive questions in the Grammar Section (p. 144). No future passive, modal passive or other passive forms are presented.
- **Conditionals: all three types taught — the clearest advance on Book 8.** First, second and third conditionals are practised in Unit 6, Lessons 3–4 (pp. 64–65), tested in Test 3 (p. 115), and explained with formulas and examples in the Grammar Section (p. 145). No mixed conditionals and no conditional forms beyond these three.
- **Reported speech: absent.** No lesson, exercise or Grammar Section entry teaches or practises reported/indirect speech anywhere in this book — a step back from Book 8, which at least practised it. (Direct speech appears inside stories and dialogues only.)
- **Relative clauses: never taught.** No lesson or Grammar Section entry presents relative pronouns or relative clauses. Relative clauses occur untaught inside reading texts (for example a product "which is grown in the hills", p. 41).
- **Question tags: never taught.** Tags appear incidentally — in the bus-stop story's reported small talk (p. 89), in a tapescript ("We remember the shape of Italy because it looks like a boot, don't we?", p. 118), and as a conversation item to respond to in Test 4 (p. 117) — but there is no presentation, rule or practice set for forming tags.
- **Modal perfects: only as part of the third conditional.** The third conditional's "would have + participle" is taught (pp. 64–65, 145). No other modal perfect (could have, should have, might have) is presented as a form or taught anywhere.
- **Future forms beyond the syllabus named above: the future progressive/perfect are taught** (Unit 2, pp. 20–21; Grammar Section, p. 143, where the progressive is called "Future continuous" — see Slips), but there is no future perfect continuous and no "going to" future presentation in this book.
- **Exam preparation: absent.** "A testing time" (pp. 32–33) is about feelings towards tests and writing advice; the end-of-ninth-form test is mentioned as a fact inside the education text (p. 27, and its correct tapescript version, p. 122). There are no external-exam formats, techniques, timed practice or exam strategies. The four printed tests are the course's own.
- **Answer keys: none in this book.** No exercise answers are printed anywhere in the Student's Book. The back cover states that the accompanying Teacher's Guide contains "keys to all exercises" (see Cross-references).
- **Listening scripts / audio references: scripts present, audio itself external.** Listening tasks refer to a tape (cassette icons in the margins), and full tapescripts for all listening activities, plus the Test 2 listening script, are printed at pp. 118–133. The recordings themselves are not part of this book. One task (Unit 6, Exercise 7, p. 65) depends on instructions spoken by the teacher, which are not printed anywhere.

### Slips and misprints noticed — Grade 9 book

- Grammar Section, third conditional formula (p. 145): the pattern is written as "if + past perfect + would/wouldn't + present perfect". The form in the book's own example ("If I had studied computers, I'd have found a better job") is would + have + past participle, which is not the present perfect; the label in the formula is wrong, or at best misleading.
- Naming inconsistency for the same form: the contents (printed iii) calls the future progressive "Future progressive", while the Grammar Section (p. 143) heads the same form "Future continuous". A learner looking the contents term up in the Grammar Section will not find it under that name.
- Grammar Section, "Making predictions" (p. 143): the model sentence about China's population is garbled compared with the version used in the lessons and tapescript — the grammar page says the population "will be the same as the rest of the world", where the Unit 2 tapescript (p. 121) says it will be the same as the whole population of the planet today.
- Deliberate errors that are **not** misprints, noted so they are not filed as defects: the Uzbek education text (pp. 26–27) contains six planted factual mistakes (school starting "at the age of sixty-seven", school "every day except for Monday", swapped shirt/trouser colours, and so on) which learners must find; the tapescript (p. 122) prints the corrected text. Likewise the "Modern communication" article (p. 93) contains one planted spelling mistake per line for learners to correct, and the Review complaint letter (p. 85) deliberately mixes formal and informal style for correction.
- Explorers project (p. 59): the fourteen mixed sentences are numbered as a single list running into two columns; in this copy the numbering in the second column is hard to follow at scan size. No definite numbering error is claimed — the point could not be verified at the resolution used, and the sentences themselves are all present.
