# South Africa — English books

**SOUTH AFRICA OPENED 2026-11-03 under the user's
all-closed-countries ruling.** South Africa is CLOSED
in the library. Its English set as recorded is 24
files — the Department of Basic Education's Rainbow
Workbooks for English Home Language (HL) and English
First Additional Language (FAL), Grades 1–6, Workbook 1
(Terms 1–2) and Workbook 2 (Terms 3–4) per grade per
strand, all student workbooks, no teacher books — all
24 in hand, no link-only books, and all 24 were pulled
from the secured GitHub library (repo tree + the
mid-size-books-6 / mid-size-books-7 /
full-quality-oversized-books releases), 2.10 GB, 3,120
PDF pages. Every book below was read whole, and every
filing was then citation-checked as a separate pass by
a checker who had not read the book for the filing:
**493 citation anchors in total (18–22 per book)** —
485 passed exactly as cited; 7 did not and are
corrected inside their sections (the Grade 4 HL
Workbook 2 dictionary anchor sat on printed p. 100

(all hashes distinct). One copy is image-only: the
Grade 6 FAL Workbook 2 has no text layer and was read

**Series verdicts.** The set is workbook-only: the
Rainbow Workbooks are the whole held English provision
for Grades 1–6 — no separate pupil textbook and no
teacher guide exists in the record, and the record
holds no English book past Grade 6 (the DBE publishes
no language workbooks for Grades 7–9, and no
government English book for Grades 10–12).
Foundation Phase HL is a phonics arc: Grade 1
Workbook 1 teaches all 26 single letters in the order
s a t p i n m d o g | c k e u r b f l h j v w y z q x
with no reading text longer than one sentence; the
digraphs and long vowels arrive in Workbook 2, where
writing still tops out at three sentences and nothing
is yet called a noun, verb or adjective. Grade 2 adds
spelling patterns, contractions with the apostrophe
rule (Workbook 2) and present→past rewriting frames,
but no possessive apostrophe and no future system
beyond "Tomorrow I will…" frames; Grade 3 completes
the punctuation set (possessive apostrophe singular
then plural, commas in lists, the a/an rule) with
homophones and similes — and still no passive voice,
no reported speech, and no comparatives as a taught
system. Foundation Phase FAL starts lower and stays
oral: Grade 1 Workbook 1 has no letter–sound sequence
at all (phonics arrives in Workbook 2) and teaches
memorised frames around pictures; the past tense
arrives as one narrative and one -ed worksheet in
Grade 2 Workbook 1 and is consolidated in Workbook 2,
where the future appears inside a single reading text
and is never drilled; by Grade 3 the strand teaches
there is/are as a system, subject–verb agreement
drilled directly, and (Workbook 2) degrees of
comparison — but no passive, no reported speech, no
conditionals and no relative clauses.
Intermediate HL is where the grammar lives, and its
ceiling is uneven. Modals and auxiliaries are taught
in Grade 4; direct-speech punctuation is first taught
in Grade 5 Workbook 1, which also carries the set's
only passive-voice teaching — a single worksheet page
of definition-plus-conversion (printed p. 95) — and
practises reported speech as rewriting, without a
backshift rule being stated there or anywhere else in
the HL strand. Grade 5 Workbook 2 is the strand's
peak and its only conditional book: relative pronouns
joining sentences (the set's first relative-clause
work), conditional sentences, sentence mood including
the subjunctive, noun clauses and metonymy — peaks
Grade 6 never revisits. The present perfect is taught
exactly once in the whole HL strand, in the final
theme of Grade 6 Workbook 1; the past perfect and the
future perfect are exercised only in Grade 6
Workbook 2's revision themes, the present perfect is
assumed there and never re-taught, and **no perfect
continuous form is taught in any of the 24 books**.
Intermediate FAL climbs a different ladder: reported
speech is practised as direct↔indirect conversion
from Grade 4 Workbook 2 onward and taught twice in
Grade 6 Workbook 2 — more conversion practice than
the HL strand ever gives — while conditionals appear
only as if…then frames (Grade 5), the passive appears
only as short conversion exercises (Grade 5
Workbook 2), and no perfect tense is taught or named
in any FAL book. FAL texts stay short and heavily
scaffolded to the end of Grade 6, and across all
twelve FAL books there is effectively no home-language
support in print: word boxes gloss English in simpler
English, the "My dictionary" pages are blank ruled
pages in every book of both strands, and the single
exception in 24 books is one Grade 6 FAL Workbook 1
instruction (printed p. 118) telling learners to
translate vocabulary into their first language
themselves.

### Structure and content summary
The book runs on a four-page weekly cycle (2 pages per activity, 64 numbered activities). Weeks 1–3 are pre-reading readiness; from Week 4 each week has a theme opener (Let's speak picture talk, Let's read one model sentence, a Sounds find-and-circle grid), a word-work page (word list, word-card matching from the cut-outs at the back, a "Fun" task), a "The letter X" formation page, and a letter-practice page. Term 1 (Themes 1–2: School; Playing together). ### Grammar / language spine
Deliberately pre-grammar. The only sentence models are the one-sentence weekly readings, all present-tense subject–verb(–object) lines, matched word-by-word with cut-out word cards. No tense contrast, no plurals, no questions, and no punctuation is taught (full stops exist only inside the model sentences). The real spine is phonics: all 26 single letters in the order s a t p i n m d o g | c k e u r b f l h j v w y z q x, each with formation tracing, initial-sound picture circling, and missing-letter CVC completion.

### Honest negatives
No reading text longer than one sentence anywhere; no comprehension questions; no digraphs or blends (sh, ch, th never appear as taught units); no past or future forms; no capital-letter rules (capitals appear only in the letter-formation work); letter q is taught through qu- words without the u being explained, and x only as a final sound.

### Structure and content summary

### Grammar / language spine
Contractions; modal verbs (will; must/must not); an advanced set of connecting words (however, nevertheless, furthermore, therefore, consequently, although, whereas); plural rules; cause-and-effect sentence joining; commas in lists; dictionary strategies (synonyms/antonyms, prefixes and suffixes, context clues); figures of speech — simile, metaphor, personification, alliteration, onomatopoeia, and identifying hyperbole/oxymoron; comparative adjectives; possessive pronouns; adverbs of place; the possessive apostrophe; adjective classes; the full paradigm of the verb to be; countable/uncountable nouns; idiomatic expressions; commands; demonstrative pronouns; the past progressive; direct and indirect/reported speech (taught twice, in Themes 6 and 7); turning statements into questions; rewriting into the future tense; either…or.

### Honest negatives
- Other languages appear only as facts inside English texts — a Sesotho name meaning (Lira), the Afrikaans origin of "white" rhino, moon customs of Muslims, Christians and the San — never as printed text, glosses or translation support. The dictionary pages are blank, as in all six books.
- The language ceiling by the end of Grade 6 is genuinely intermediate on the reading side — adapted Twain, a real newspaper report, a press release, idiom and figurative language — but production is still heavily scaffolded: every extended piece is planned on a printed organiser, mind map or frame, and the only unscripted writing is short.
- The printed play is deliberately incomplete (Scene 1 and the start of Scene 2 only); the drama learning is in writing the rest, so the book contains no complete play.
