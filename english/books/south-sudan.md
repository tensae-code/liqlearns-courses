### Unit-by-unit summary
- Unit 1 Greetings (pp.1–7): time-of-day greetings and farewells as fixed exchanges — good morning (to mother, teacher, children), good afternoon, goodbye, good evening and goodnight (a short song about the sun going), "How are you? — I am fine, thank you", a hello song, and a closing picture-matching exercise in which the learner says the greeting that fits each picture (p.7, pictures only).
- Unit 2 Myself (pp.8–12): introducing yourself by name ("My name is Miss Alam"; "My name is ___"), boy/girl ("I am a girl / I am a boy" — Mercy and John), asking a name ("What is your name?"), introducing a third person ("This is Mary. She is a girl. / This is Eric. He is a boy."), and friends — a recitation naming friends with he/she, plus describing what you like in a friend.
- Unit 3 Our home (pp.13–18): naming a pictured home and its people (house, father, mother, baby), things at home — farm animals (cow, goat, hen, dog) and kitchen items (plate, pot, cup, spoon, glass, jug, knife) — with a counting recitation ("We have one dog / two houses / three cows"), people at home (brother, sister, grandmother; Akot introduces her family), roles and chores at home (sweep, wash, graze, cook; a morning/afternoon grazing rhyme), and a closing story, "Kenyi's home", in which family members share roles in present-continuous captions (mother cooking, father cutting grass, Kenyi washing cups and plates).
- Unit 4 Our school (pp.19–24): a pictured school (flag, classroom, cleaner, ball, play), things in school (desk, table, book, pencil; a "Who am I?" set — bag, duster, pen, rubber), a counting song ("On my table I have three books…"), people in school (teacher, pupils, cleaner, secretary), what we do in school (assembly, learn, clean, play; "I learn at school"), and the story "My school" (Alex, Primary One, Stars Primary School; head teacher; read, write and play).
- Unit 5 Our environment (pp.25–29): what is around us (hill, river, trees, farm, hospital, church; birds, forest, grass, flowers), a recitation ("I see the River Nile. What do you see?"), destroying the environment (cutting trees, littering, wasting water; a song whose refrain says these "destroy our world"), protecting it (plant a tree, water plants, clean our school, burn litter; a "We care about our world" recitation), and a closing text of must/need sentences about not cutting many trees and taking care of the environment.
- Unit 6 Transport and travel (pp.30–34): types of transport pictured and named (aeroplane, ship, car, train; then camel, bus, bicycle, motorcycle in "This is a…" sentences), a recite-and-act poem in which each vehicle speaks ("I am an aeroplane. High high I fly."), and "How far?" — how children get to school (bus, walk, bicycle), a recitation ("We are going to school. John runs… Mary catches the bus… Kenyi walks"), and telling how you and your family travel.
- Unit 7 Accidents and safety (pp.35–40): common accidents at home (fall, fire, injury, cut), at school (including nose bleed; "Have you had an accident at school?"), and in the community (road, danger, animal, bicycle, tree), helping during accidents (ambulance; what an ambulance does), warnings in "Do not play with…" form (hot water, medicine, on the road — with a reason clause each), and the story "Bees in class" (bees enter; the class lies down; Mark runs and is hurt).
- Unit 8 Health and hygiene (pp.41–47): parts of the body named on a figure (head, hair, ear, nose, eye, mouth, neck, shoulder, elbow, arm, hand, finger, knee, leg, toe), the "Head, shoulder, knees and toes" song, this/these naming ("This is my head… These are my eyes…"), uses of body parts ("I use it for breathing / them for seeing / hearing"; completion sentences "I walk with ___"), keeping clean (bathe, brush, wash hands, cut nails, comb), and the story "Kenyi is clean" (a morning hygiene routine in short present-tense sentences).
- Unit 9 Nutrition (pp.48–52): foods we eat (a meal scene; "Tell your partner what you ate last night"), naming foods (cassava, kisra, potatoes, beans, vegetables, rice), types of food — a recitation that good food makes us grow, makes us strong and "protects us from diseases", keeping food safe (cover food, dry maize in the sun, put milk in the fridge — each captioned "We keep it safe."), and the story "My home" (Maria's farm grows wheat, maize and vegetables and keeps cows, goats and hens for milk and meat).
- Unit 10 Weather (pp.53–56): elements of weather (sun, rain, cloud, wind; draw today's weather), a sun/rain recitation ("You make us warm / wet"), activities of the year (plant, plough, weed, harvest — pictured, then mimed), and the story "John and the rain" (John runs home in the rain, feels cold, sits near the fire and feels warm; present-continuous forms throughout).
- Unit 11 Occupations (pp.57–61): different occupations pictured (doctor, teacher, pilot, farmer), role-play guessing, "I am a…" job sentences (a weaver who makes and sells mats; a doctor who treats sick people; a farmer who keeps cows and goats), occupations in the community (grocer, hair dresser, carpenter, tailor, fish monger, teacher; a guess-my-occupation game), the story "Mr Wole's Farm", and a closing game text ("I am an engineer. I make cars. I build roads. Who are you?").
- Unit 12 Games and sports (pp.62–66): different games shown in six uncaptioned pictures (the guide names racing, skipping, tyre-rolling and hide-and-seek among them) for role-play, common play materials (toys, balls, rope, rocks, doll, balloon; draw your toy), the game I like ("I like football / running / riding / swimming"; a completion exercise contrasting "They like…" with "He likes…"), short texts about a runner and a basketball player ("He runs very fast"; "She always wins"), and the story "Playtime" (Ochol and Riya make balls from leaves and papers and play football, hide and seek and skipping).
- Unit 13 Telling time (pp.67–72): different times of day in four uncaptioned pictures, telling a partner when you wake and sleep, ways of telling time (sun, shadow, sunset, moon, clock; draw a clock), units of time — reading clock faces ("It is eight o'clock", "It is noon", "It is 10.30 am", "It is 2.30 pm"; make a clock), a clock poem through the day, photographs of watches and clocks (p.71, uncaptioned), and the story "My day" (Mary's routine from waking to lunch, in the present tense).
- Unit 14 Peace (pp.73–76): peace and security at home — Judith ("They keep me safe and happy") against Mark, who lives with his uncle and sometimes is sad because he misses his father and mother; peace in school (Nancy: "We work together. We do not take each other's things… We are kind to each other."), peace in the community (a borehole picture; a "game of peace" with the words Peace and Love), and the story "Tony's big day" — the book's one past-tense story (visitors came, ate, sang, danced and gave gifts for his brother's special day).
- Unit 15 Technology (pp.77–79): communication devices pictured and named (mobile phone, radio, computer, television, laptop), using them (a phone-call exchange, "Hello Mother. I miss you."; a mime recitation "I type / I read / I watch / All on my computer."), and the closing text "The mobile phone" (what a phone is and does — make and receive calls, write messages, talk to those who are far).

### Grammar spine
No grammar is ever named or explained; the book is a sequence of spoken frames absorbed by repetition, in this order: greeting/farewell formulas → "My name is…" / "What is your name?" → I am a boy/girl → he is/she is with "This is…" → have in counts ("We have two houses") and possession of family ("I have one brother") → naming with a/an implied in labels → present-continuous captions for actions in progress (Unit 3 and 10 stories) → "This is a (vehicle)" → "We are going to school" with third-person -s (runs, catches, walks) → imperative warnings "Do not play with…" + can ("It can burn you", "You can get hurt") → this/these for body parts → "I use it/them for…" → simple present routines (Units 8–9 stories) → likes ("I like…", "He likes… / They like…", Unit 12) → clock time with o'clock, noon, am/pm (Unit 13) → a single past-tense narrative (Unit 14 story) → can for ability ("You can move around with it", "It can also write messages", Unit 15). Third-person -s, the present continuous and the past appear only inside model texts, never as topics.

### Honest negatives
- No alphabet, letter names, phonics or letter formation anywhere: the book assumes oral-first teaching and contains no reading-readiness apparatus at all — reading is whole-phrase recognition modelled by the teacher.
- No grammar terminology, no rules, no tables, no question-formation teaching beyond the memorised frames "What is your name?", "What do you see?", "How are you?"; no negatives except "Do not…" warnings and "We do not take each other's things"; no plurals as a topic (plural forms occur untaught in texts); no past tense except the Unit 14 story and "what you ate last night" as a talk prompt; no future form at all.
- Writing is almost absent: learners draw, name, match and complete single frames ("My name is ___"); there is no copying, dictation or sentence-writing exercise in the pupil's book itself, no workbook section, no tests, no revision units, no word lists or picture dictionary — vocabulary review is delegated to the Teacher's Guide's end-of-unit games and the learner's own "word bank" in later grades (not present here).
- Numbers appear only incidentally (counting recitations to three, song counts, clock times); colours, days, months and classroom-object English beyond Unit 4's set are not taught.

### Grammar spine
The P3 book runs an unnamed but deliberate tense programme inside its themes, in this order: imperatives and the command/instruction distinction (Unit 1) → present-tense routines and pronoun sets (myself/your/my/his) (Unit 2) → present descriptions and where-questions (Unit 3) → shall/will in quoted futures (Unit 4) → present description and reporting (Unit 5) → weather sentences and a past-tense remembered narrative (Unit 6) → like + gerund-style frames carried from P2 (Unit 7) → the singular/plural contrast this is/these are, the band's only morphology lesson (Unit 8) → an explicit present/past/future table and "while" (Unit 9) → past-tense verse and prose contrasted with present and a will-future (Unit 10) → past-tense sentence-making and a past-to-present-continuous rewrite (Unit 11) → past-tense model sentences about yesterday's food (Unit 12) → past/present/future sentence writing about jobs, with was and irregular "weaved" as printed (Unit 13) → persuasive phrases (please, I ask you, it is important — supplied by the guide) (Unit 14) → reported instruction and present-tense explanation (Unit 15). Capital letters and full stops are the only punctuation rules ever stated (via the guide); question forms remain memorised frames.

### Honest negatives
- Grammar is never named in the pupil's book: no word "tense" is explained (it appears only in task rubrics from Unit 11 — "in the past, present and future tense"), no verb tables, no rules for -s, -ed or plurals beyond the single this/these spread; a learner gets models and transformations, not a system.
- No phonics, spelling programme or handwriting work (one three-word spelling item in Unit 15); no listening material or audio reference; no tests, revision units or self-check apparatus — assessment lives in the Teacher's Guide.
- Comprehension is thin in places: several "stories" are poems or texts in disguise (Unit 4's future poem; Unit 14's "poem" is prose sentences), and Units 5 and 8's matching exercises are printed with definitions pre-scrambled (see Cross-check), so the pupil cannot complete them by meaning alone without the guide's answer key.
- Nothing on letters, email or extended writing beyond a paragraph; no dictionary or glossary — the word bank is a copying routine with the unit title printed, not a word list.

### What the book teaches, unit by unit

Every unit follows the same rhythm: picture talk, new vocabulary (with a "word bank" habit — new words are collected in a personal word-bank book), a reading passage or dialogue, comprehension, a "Word attack" exercise, poetry work, writing, a "Further learning" research task and an "In summary" closing activity. Group modes are labelled on every activity (In groups / In pairs / Individually / As a class).

**Unit 1 — Games and Sports (pp. 1–15; 14 activities).** Sports vocabulary (referee, stadium, athlete, professional, assistant referee, trophy, podium, kick-off, friendly match) built through picture naming, word–picture matching and oral quizzes. ### Grammar spine

The book's explicit grammar is thin and mostly carried in NOTE boxes and "Word attack" slots rather than in a graded sequence:

- **Punctuation I (Unit 1, p. 7):** commas and full stops — a NOTE explains the comma as a break in a sentence, with punctuation practice on story sentences.
- **Punctuation II (Unit 1, p. 12):** semicolons ("used to join two related sentences or ideas"), exclamation marks and capital letters, spotted in the story and then used in the pupils' own sentences.
- **Question marks (Unit 2, p. 24):** forming and punctuating questions, following the meteorologist interview.
- **Nouns and verbs (Unit 2, p. 31):** a NOTE defines both ("A noun is name of anything…"; "A verb tell us what is being done" — both as printed, see Slips), with noun/verb hunting in a passage and a sentence-joining game.
- **Tense awareness (Unit 1, p. 9–10):** the three speeches are sorted by tense — Lalu speaks in the present, Eunice in the past, Riya in the future (the guide states this explicitly; the pupil book asks the questions).
- **Comparatives (Unit 5, p. 69):** a short NOTE — good → better, bad → worse, long → longer, nice → nicer, in the model sentence "Being a doctor is better than being a pilot".
- **Was / were (Unit 6, p. 78):** a NOTE states was is used with singular subjects and were with plurals, with gap-fill sentences.
- **Persuasive writing structure (Unit 6, pp. 80–81):** the book's one extended writing-skills lesson — parts of a persuasive piece, then Revise, Edit and Publishing steps.
- **Similes (Unit 7, p. 95):** comparing using "as", "like" or "than" — "as white as snow", "whiter than snow" — then completing simile frames ("As mischievous as a…", "As happy as a…"); similes recur in the Unit 10 funeral story, where "like" comparisons are practised again (p. 148).
- **Past tense (Unit 8, p. 120–121):** a NOTE ("Past tense refers to things that happen in the past") with worked examples (eat → ate, jump → jumped), sentence conversion, and a matching table (is–was, come–came, steal–stole, change–changed, preach–preached).
- **Like / dislike / prefer and the -ing form (Unit 9, p. 127):** a NOTE explains prefer + to for comparing two likes, dis- + like for dislikes, and that adding -ing to a verb makes the "continuous tense" (the book's term), with conversion practice (watch → watching, paint, cook, draw, dance, weave, sing).
- **"Let us" and "Shall we" (Unit 9, pp. 133–134):** making suggestions and requests, statement form and question form, with conversion exercises both ways.
- **Adverbs of time and "going to" (Unit 9, pp. 135–136):** tomorrow, next week, today at 7 o'clock, in three short self-introductions ("I am going to sing my latest album tomorrow").
- **"Unless" (Unit 10, p. 153):** defined as "except on condition that", with model sentences and five completions ("I cannot mend this puncture unless…").
- **Comparing adverbs (Unit 10, p. 157):** earlier/faster identified as adverbs; two actions compared by adding -er + than (early → earlier than, fast → faster than), with rewriting practice.

### Honest negatives — what this book does not teach

- **No phonics, spelling system or letter work** of any kind; spelling appears only as word lists, word-searches and one correct-the-misspellings exercise. Pupils are assumed already literate.
- **No systematic tense course:** present/past/future are only pointed at inside the Unit 1 speeches; the past tense gets one lesson (Unit 8) and there is no present-tense paradigm, no questions/negatives formation teaching beyond the question-mark punctuation point, and no modal verbs beyond the "unless" and "shall we" formulas.
- **No articles, plurals, possessives or pronouns teaching** — all are used in texts but never explained.
- **No listening material** beyond teacher-read poems and dictation-style reading; no pronunciation guidance.
- **No answer material in the book itself** and no revision or test sections; assessment is entirely activity-based (observation of presentations, voting for the best poster/poem/story).
- **No South Sudan Sign Language, braille or differentiation guidance** in the pupil book; "slow learners" are mentioned only in the guide.
- The Harry Potter extract (p. 88) is the book's only text from outside East Africa, and its British wizarding setting is left wholly unexplained except for the discussion question "Do you think the story is true?"

### Grammar spine
Nouns (Unit 1: definition; person/place/thing) → past tense as the diary register with is/was/were gap-fill, contrasted with present tense for weekly routines (Unit 2) → adjectives (Unit 4: describing nouns — number, colour, size, feel, sound, behaviour) → conjunctions and/but/or/because/so joining clauses (Unit 6) → prepositions of place and direction phrases for giving directions (Unit 9). Carried inside the thematic work, not as lessons: imperatives and commands (Unit 1 mechanic dialogue; Unit 8 planting instructions), comparatives (Unit 1 sentence table "bigger than / more comfortable than", used but never taught), letter formulas and layout (Unit 7), story elements and summary structure (Unit 10). Tenses in the reading texts run ahead of the teaching: past narration in Units 1, 4 and 10, and future "will" in speeches and letters, are met as text long before any tense system is taught — the only tense lesson in the book is the Unit 2 diary past.

### Honest negatives
- Only five explicit language lessons in 156 pages (nouns, is/was/were + diary past, adjectives, conjunctions, prepositions). There is no teaching of articles, pronouns as a topic, question formation, the present perfect, any future form, conditionals, reported speech, or spelling/phonics as a system — pronunciation work is left to "read after the teacher" throughout.
- Comparatives are required by exercises (Unit 1, p.18) and used in texts, but never taught anywhere in the book.
- No revision units, no end-of-unit or end-of-book tests in the pupil's book, no glossary, wordlist or answer section; the "word bank book" each pupil is told to keep is the only vocabulary record, and it is the pupil's own notebook, not part of this book.
- Two activities depend on the internet in class (Unit 5's Maasai video link; Unit 6's volleyball/sports video link, p.89), and Unit 6's commentary activity presupposes a recording the book does not supply.
- Local grounding is strong on place (Payam, Juba, Torit, Bor, Kapoeta; SSP prices; kisra named in a Unit 6 exercise) but the diary reading is a British First World War text and the health passage is adapted from Wikipedia; neither is re-contextualised beyond comprehension questions.

### Grammar spine

The guide follows the Student's Book spine and adds the keys: gerunds (Unit 1) → articles, definite/indefinite/zero (Unit 2) → will/shall futures and general modal review (Unit 3) → subject-verb agreement across five rule sets (Unit 4) → adjectives and their order, plus make/help + object + adjective patterns (Unit 5) → connectors, "in order to" phrases, and time clauses with when/while/as (Unit 6) → conjunctions and when-clauses again (Unit 7).

### Honest negatives
- This is the final year of the series (Secondary 4 = Grade 12), yet the guide contains no examination preparation at all: nothing on the South Sudan Certificate of Secondary Education examinations, no past or specimen papers, no marking schemes, no revision programme — Part A's assessment chapter stays generic (formative/summative, Bloom).
- Answer keys are uneven: Units 2, 3 and 4 have full printed keys, but Units 1, 5, 6 and 7 mostly have assessment rubrics instead of answers, and several keys that do exist contain errors (see Slips).
- No tapescripts for the listening activities (Units 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7 all have one), and the anti-corruption lesson depends on a web link the teacher must fetch and print in advance.
- The guide never teaches reported speech, passive voice, conditionals as a system, relative clauses or question tags — it assumes the earlier years' guides, and a teacher who only has this book cannot fill those gaps from it.
