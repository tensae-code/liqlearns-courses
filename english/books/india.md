government's own publisher) — Marigold for Classes 1–5,

## Class 1 — Marigold (Textbook in English for Class I)

NCERT, first edition February 2006; this copy the
2020-21 printing; ISBN 81-7450-478-8; price ₹65 as
printed; printed at Amit Printing Press, Mathura;
cover, layout and illustrations Henu Mehtani. Built on
the National Curriculum Framework 2005. Chairperson,
advisory committee at primary level: Anita Rampal;
chief advisor R. Lalitha Eapen (EFLU, Hyderabad).
Read in full, page by page, 2026-10-26, from the
Internet Archive's copy of NCERT's own chapter files
(prelims + Units 1–10; NCERT's site will not serve this
network). 122 printed pages in all.

SHAPE: TEN units (pp.1–120) plus a closing PICTURE
DICTIONARY (pp.121–122). Every unit is the same
machine: one POEM, one STORY, then the apparatus —
"New words," "Let's read" (two model sentences), and
activity strands (Reading is fun / Let's talk / Let's
share / Let's act / Let's draw / Let's do / Let's
write / Say aloud / Sing) — and every unit ENDS WITH
ITS OWN TEACHER'S PAGES inside the same chapter file:
listening, pronunciation (rhyming sets), exposure to
language (sight words), speaking (role play in four
colour groups), writing/fine motor, raising awareness,
and a materials list. No formal grammar anywhere: the
teacher pages state the method — mother tongue allowed
at the start, DO NOT correct mistakes, concept order
"from near to far," writing is the LAST milestone.

The letters are taught as a slow strand across the
whole book, never as one alphabet lesson: a–c written
in U1, a–f said aloud in U2, CVC word-building from U3
(bag/hen/tap/cap/tin/cot/ant/pen/fox/axe with the
missing vowel filled), join a–z in U4, writing in
groups a–r by U5, s–z by U6; CAPITALS introduced in
U7 and finished W–Z in U10. Numbers: 1–10 in U1,
11–20 in U3's teacher pages. The Picture Dictionary
(p.121–122) pairs each letter with one word and a
picture, letter first, no "for": A apple, B balloon,
C cap, D dog, E elephant, F fish, G grapes,
H helicopter, I igloo, J jug, K kite, L lamp, M moon,
N nest, O orange, P peacock, Q quilt, R rose, S sheep,
T tomato, U umbrella, V violin, W whale, X Xmas tree,
Y yak, Z zebra.

- U1 (pp.1–15) A Happy Child (poem: red house, green
  tree, hardly ever cry) · Three Little Pigs (story:
  Sonu, Monu and Gonu; houses of straw, sticks,
  bricks; the wolf huffs and puffs the first two down).
  New words in two sets (cry/day/red/sun; and/bad/big/
  but/not/one/pig/the/was). Feelings work: happy/sad
  faces; house kinds (hut, flat, igloo, bungalow);
  "Say aloud" numbers 1–6; join-the-dots 1–10; animal
  homes (farm/water/jungle). Teacher's Pages: rhyming
  sets blow/flow/glow, brick/kick/stick,
  huff/puff/stuff; sight words bad, bed, big, cot, bun,
  sad, red, dig, hot, sun; two-letter words am, he, in,
  is, it, me, my, so in talk; save-water awareness.
- U2 (pp.16–28) After a Bath (poem, Aileen Fisher —
  the poem page is picture-led; the unit's work on it
  runs through the Teacher's Pages: cry/dry/try) ·
  The Bubble, the Straw and the Shoe (story, Alexei
  Tolstoy: the Straw bridges the river, the Shoe's
  jump breaks it, the Bubble laughs till it bursts).
  Doing-words filled into the story's own sentences
  (jumped, shook, stretched, burst); float-or-sink
  "Think time" (ribbon, stick, feather, paper, marble,
  straw, leaf, stone, shoes, spoon); number-word
  matching (seven, eight, nine, ten); letters a–f.
  Teacher's Pages: hygiene talk, vowels a e i o u with
  numbers 1–10, buttons/zips/shoelaces, switch off
  lights and fans.
- U3 (pp.29–41) One Little Kitten (poem, Carolyn
  Graham — a counting poem, one kitten up to "Fifteen
  donkeys / With fifteen tails," every creature with a
  feeling or size word) · Lalu and Peelu (story,
  Vineeta Krishna: red Lalu eats a red chilli, yellow
  Peelu brings a yellow laddu) · a third piece, the
  mini-play Mother Hen and the Chicks (adapted from
  E.H. Grassam, The Beacon Readers). "Say aloud"
  banks: all two-letter words, then three-letter words
  (all, and, bad, big, bit, but, can, cat, cry, day,
  dog, fat, let, met, not, one, pig, ran, red, sad,
  saw, sit, sun, ten, the, try, two, was, wet, you);
  CVC vowel-fills; the colour-mixing riddle (red +
  yellow is orange…); vowels traced on eggs.
  Teacher's Pages: numbers 11–20, healthy food, wash
  hands before and after meals.
- U4 (pp.42–52) Once I Saw a Little Bird (poem —
  picture-led page; the unit's bird theme runs through
  the Teacher's Pages) · Mittu and the Yellow Mango
  (story, Chitra Narendran: Mittu the green parrot
  pecks a red balloon behind the tree; the pop scares
  the crow off the mango tree for good). Join the
  letters a to z; house-parts colour ticking (roof,
  door, windows, walls, floor, plants, dustbin);
  fruit-to-tree matching; CVC sets (bat, bed, bit,
  dot, bun / cat, fed, hit, hot, fun / mat, red, pit,
  pot, run) and vowel-fills (s_t, p_g, b_g, d_t, s_n,
  f_n, l_g, l_t, b_n). Teacher's Pages: tastes
  activity (making nimbu pani), the peacock as
  national bird, "birds are our friends."
- U5 (pp.53–63) Merry-Go-Round (poem, Dorothy W.
  Baruch — round and round, up and down, on a big
  brown horse) · Circle (story, Manorama Jafa:
  Grandmother draws a circle, three lines make a
  ball, a zig-zag makes a balloon; Mohini draws a
  wheel, a moon, a sun, a rabbit and her own face).
  Letters written in groups a–f, g–l, m–r; picture
  word-blanks (dog, bull, elephant, owl, horse, cat;
  mango, orange, nut, peach). Teacher's Pages: shape
  sight words (circle, line, oval, square, triangle,
  zig-zag), the counting game (5 or 10 says "chup"),
  cover food from flies.
- U6 (pp.64–76) If I Were an Apple (poem: the apple
  would drop at once and say "Eat me, my boy!") ·
  Our Tree (story-poem, Pranab and Smita Chakravarti:
  a berry seed falls, rains come, the plant is born,
  the tree grows) · a third piece, Murali's Mango
  Tree (short story, Shyamala Kumaradas: the thrown
  seed becomes the tree his children eat from).
  "If I were a bird, I would ___ (fly, cry)" play;
  fruit crossword; seed-growth picture ordering (dig,
  plant, water, grows); "Yes, I can climb / pluck /
  catch / jump"; letters s–v (p.65) and w–z (p.71)
  complete the small-letter strand. Teacher's Pages:
  the class handprint tree; tree, bee, flea; don't
  throw things into the river.
- U7 (pp.77–87) A Kite (poem: "I often sit and wish
  that I / Could be a kite up in the sky") · Sundari
  (story, adapted from 'Cleo' by Ruth Dixon: Bobby's
  red, white and blue kite; a little dog holds her
  tail on the first try; on the second she tugs free
  and flies as high as she pleases). Sky word-fills
  (stars, sun, cloud, rainbow, moon); kite-making
  objects ticked; the picture story The Umbrella and
  the Crow retold, then six sentences put in order.
  Teacher's Pages: CAPITALS A–Z introduced here;
  sound words (blow, whoosh, puff, whiff, dizzy, shoo,
  woof); the 1–20 counting rhyme as a group play.
- U8 (pp.88–97) A Little Turtle (poem: "I carry my
  house / Wherever I go") · The Tiger and the Mosquito
  (story, Mrinalini Srivastava: the tiger's own paws
  scrape his cheek; he walks away; "Don't be so
  proud, my friend. Everyone is great in his own
  way!"). Five story fill-ins with traced words; Say
  aloud sets by first sound (circle/fire, cat/kick,
  quack/queen…; bank/bin/biscuit/bun, goose/gown/grey/
  gun, fan/few/field/fish, lamp/lick/lip/loose);
  capitals A–F (p.90) and G–L (p.95); The Giant Mouse
  picture talk. Teacher's Pages: SYLLABLES explained
  (mug, truck — one; never, lazy — two; September,
  October — three); brush teeth after meals.
- U9 (pp.98–109) Clouds (poem: "It is hot. / The sky
  is blue. / A little cloud comes looking for you") ·
  Anandi's Rainbow (story, Anup Ray: Anandi paints
  the garden flowers with the rainbow's colours —
  violet, indigo, blue, green, orange, red — and
  leaves the yellow "so that the Sun may look bright
  and gay"). Personal writing on p.102: "I can read
  my name… I am ___ years old… I can count till ___…
  My teacher's name is…"; the am/have riddle (a
  sheep); Aa-to-Zz join-the-dots; capitals M–R (p.101)
  and S–V (p.107). Teacher's Pages: the months song
  ("Thirty days hath September…"), a class birthday
  calendar, weather sentences (It's cold / It's
  raining today / It's hot).
- U10 (pp.110–120) Flying-Man (poem: "Over the
  mountains, / And over the sea!") · The Tailor and
  his Friend (story: Kalu the tailor pricks Appu the
  elephant's trunk with his needle; Appu fills his
  trunk at the river and soaks every new cloth; "An
  elephant never forgets"; sorry said, friends
  again). Occupations matching (sailor, astronaut,
  artist, dentist, doctor, postman, farmer, tailor,
  pilot, teacher); days/months odd-one-out;
  make-a-friendship-band craft; capitals W–Z (p.113)
  close the alphabet strand. Teacher's Pages: by now
  the child should know basic punctuation (capital
  letters, full stops, question marks) and singular
  and plural forms.
