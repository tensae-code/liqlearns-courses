### Grammar progression

be (am/is/are) statements and yes/no questions → numbers 1–20 → a/an → singular/plural nouns → There is/There are → prepositions of place and time (first exposure) → imperatives (classroom rules) → have/has and don't have → possessive 's (in vocabulary/contents listing) → can/can't (rules in Unit 2, abilities in Unit 3.8). No past tense, no present simple paradigm teaching, no future forms — see Negatives.

### Phonics progression

Letters are taught in a non-alphabetical, story-driven order: S, A (1.4) → T, P (1.6) → I (1.7) → N, M (1.8) → D (2.3) → G, O with oo (2.5) → C (2.6) → K with ck (2.7) → H with ch, sh (2.8) → E, U with ee (3.2) → R, V (3.4) → B, J (3.6) → L, F (3.7). By the end of Part 1 the letters covered are: A B C D E F G H I J K L M N O P R S T U V — 21 letters. Letters Q, W, X, Y, Z never appear in Part 1.

### Vocabulary / skills progression

Vocabulary is thematic (greetings/colours/numbers → friends and feelings → school → family and jobs) and is consolidated in a printed bilingual "My Vocabulary" list at the end of each unit, with IPA and Armenian translations. Skills: listening is the dominant skill (numbered audio in almost every lesson); speaking is pair-dialogue based; reading is short texts/dialogues and word-level reading in phonics lessons; writing is almost entirely copybook work (copying words, completing words, crosswords, drawing and labelling) — there is no free composition beyond a birthday card and short patterned sentences.

### Honest negatives — what Part 1 never teaches

- No past tense of any kind; no future (no going to, no will); no present continuous; present simple is not taught as a paradigm (only fixed phrases and have/has).
- No question formation beyond be-questions, can-questions, and the set question words practised in the Progress Check.
- Letters Q, W, X, Y, Z are never introduced; digraphs th, wh, ph never appear (only ch, sh, ck, ee, oo).
- No days/months/seasons beyond the days of the week; no telling the time beyond o'clock phrases in listening.
- No Armenian-language explanation of grammar in the student book; grammar is shown by pattern tables ("REMEMBER"-style modelling in the activities) and the end-of-book contents matrix.

### Grammar progression (whole book)

Perfect tenses, all six (U1.2) → passive voice, present simple/present perfect/modals (U1.4) → defining and non-defining relative clauses (U2.2) → reported speech and reporting verbs (U2.4) → passive voice, present/past/future simple and modal passives (U3.2) → passive progressives (U3.4) → modals of deduction and speculation about the past (U4.2) → prefixes and compound nouns as word-formation grammar (U4.4) → participle clauses for reason (U5.2) → gerund and infinitive (U5.4) → mixed conditionals with wish/if only (U6.2) → causative have (U6.4). The design is a revision-and-extension spiral for the final school years: every point is presented as already partly known and is immediately applied to the unit theme. Basic conditionals (zero/first/second/third as a system), articles, comparatives and elementary tense formation are **not** taught here — they are assumed from earlier grades.

### Vocabulary and skills progression

Vocabulary moves from the personal future (jobs) through medicine, environment and politics to history and space, with a heavy Armenian-interest strand (Armenian physicians, Armenia's government system, Hellenistic Armenia, Urartu, Metsamor/Karahunj, Hambardzumyan, Bagian, Shushanik Kurghinian). Each unit's Word List gives IPA transcriptions. Writing genres progress: short paragraph → CV → opinion essay → informative essay → blog → speech → descriptive essay, each with a model text and a "Useful Expressions" bank. Speaking progresses from pair discussion to interview role-play, debate language (U4.8, U5.8) and speculation (U6.8). Listening is audio-only (numbered tracks) with no printed transcripts.

**None as such.** There is no mock test, no university-entrance or final-exam format practice, no timed test and no answer key anywhere in the book. ### Grammar progression (whole book)

The grammar strand is explicitly **revision** ("Grammar Review" / "Brush up your knowledge"): past tenses with would/used to (Topic 1) → reported speech and reporting verbs (Topic 2) → present tenses including present perfect and present perfect continuous, and state/action verbs (Topic 3) → no tense review in Topic 4, where phrasal-verb systems (get, put) take the grammar slot → conditionals 0–3 with wish/if only (Topic 5) → future forms including future continuous and future perfect (Topic 6). Passive voice is **not** given a review section anywhere in this book, and relative clauses, participle clauses, gerund/infinitive contrasts and causative have — all taught in the Grade 11 book — are not revisited.

### Vocabulary and skills progression

Vocabulary is topical and abstract (civilisation, translation, religion, volunteering, success, future planning), recycled in a back-of-book glossary with Armenian equivalents but **no IPA**. Every topic trains all four skills; listening is video-based (the References page keys YouTube and one Google Drive link to printed page numbers). Writing progresses: academic paragraph → translation and a motivating letter → scaffolded essay → informal letter and interview script → goals essay → CV and "My Way to Success" essay. Each topic ends with a creative project.

### Exam-preparation apparatus

**None as such.** Despite this being the final school year, the book contains no mock examination, no university-entrance-style test, no timed writing paper and no answer key. The Grammar Review exercises and multiple-choice reading/listening checks are the only test-like material, and nothing in the book presents them as exam preparation.

### Honest negatives (what the book never teaches)

- No answer key; no listening transcripts (listening is reached via QR codes in class and the video links listed on printed p. 142).
- No pronunciation work and no IPA anywhere, including the glossary.
- No self-assessment or can-do grids (unlike the Grade 11 book).
- No passive-voice revision; no revision of Grade 11's relative clauses, participle clauses, gerund/infinitive or causative have.
- No explicit CEFR level is stated in the English front matter; the Armenian introduction describes alignment with the new state standard and programme only.
- Contents is printed at the back of the book (p. 143), not the front.
