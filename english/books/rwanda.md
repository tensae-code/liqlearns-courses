# Rwanda — English

**Read in full + citation-checked 3 November 2026 (244 anchors tested across the 11 books: 224 passed as cited, 20 corrected in place; 1,605 PDF pages).** Rwanda's English record is primary only: the Rwanda Education Board's Competence-Based Curriculum course for Primary 1–6 — six pupil's books and the teacher's guides for Primary 2–6 (no P1 guide is held). Every book is the Revised Edition 2019 except the P6 Teacher's Guide, whose imprint is © 2018 but whose content keys page-by-page to the held 2019 Pupil's Book — a printing-date gap, not an edition mismatch (see its section). No ISBN is printed in any of the 11 books, and most name no authors — REB is the only publisher named. All 11 files were pulled from the secured library and byte-verified (MD5s in the sections), and every cover was checked against its record slot before reading.

**Cover-proved record corrections: none.** Unusually for this library, every one of the 11 slots holds exactly the book its record names — no duplicates, no swaps, no wrong-course guides, no part-books. Three record refinements only: (1) the P5 Teacher's Guide has no separate title page — its "Revised Edition 2019 / © 2019 REB" sits on the cover leaf itself; (2) the P4 Teacher's Guide file opens on its combined title/imprint page, not a picture cover; (3) the P6 guide's recorded 2018 year is genuinely printed (verified on a render of its copyright page) and it is a true companion to the 2019 pupil's book. The serious errors in this set are inside the books, not in the shelving: the P1 pupil's book's Contents omits a whole unit ("Unit 7: Home") and mis-numbers the last three; the P4 guide's Unit 4 is foliated 40–52, duplicating Unit 3's folios, so no printed folios 53–64 exist; the P3 pupil's book's sample papers test material the book never teaches; and two guides' answer keys are unreliable in places (P3's Unit 9 arithmetic key; P5's key, which includes five flatly wrong answers and one block of "expected answers" about Chinese airports that belongs to another book entirely).

**The pupil's-book spine.** One architecture throughout: ten thematic units per book, and teacher-read listening — there is no audio, CD or QR code anywhere in the set; every listening text is the teacher's voice, and the texts themselves live in the guides. The grammar line climbs slowly. P1 is present-tense frames, the alphabet and numbers to 50 — no past, no future and no comparatives taught. P2 adds *can/can't* and *may*, but still no present perfect, passive, conditionals or comparatives as taught structures. P3 uses the past simple (Unit 4) without ever stating a paradigm, adds *going to*, and is the first book with test papers — two Sample Papers whose keys sit in its guide. P4 is the first book to NAME its tenses — simple past (Units 2 and 8), *going to* (Unit 2), *will* (Unit 5), and the present perfect named only in Unit 9 (printed p.107) — plus *must/mustn't* and comparison through word-ending work, closing with a Glossary. P5 teaches the first conditional (Unit 7) and the passive (Unit 8 only — its own Unit 4 summary credits the passive to a unit that never teaches it) and carries per-unit tests, but the present perfect is never taught (one untaught dialogue occurrence aside). P6 repeats the first conditional (Units 3, 4 and 8) as the set's only conditional, adds reported commands and direct-speech punctuation, and closes with a selective A–V Glossary. Writing ceilings stay low throughout: frames and guided paragraphs, plan–draft–revise story work in P5, and no essay teaching anywhere.

**The teacher's guides — all five are TRUE COMPANIONS** to the held pupil's books: same units in the same order, procedures keyed to the pupil's own activity numbers and pages, and they print the listening texts and answers the pupil's books withhold; none reproduces pupil pages, the P2 guide's one read-aloud story (printed pp.78–79) excepted. Their reliability differs sharply. P2's guide is sound in its answers, but its content map mis-states three unit page-ranges and claims question tags the pupil's book never teaches. P3's guide supplies keys, unit assessments and solved crosswords — but its Unit 9 key marks 32+28 as 50, 18×7 as 105 and 96−12 as 18. P4's guide prints all three listening scripts in full and keys most closed activities, but it carries the foliation error above and spells "Mississipi" throughout (the pupil's book spells Mississippi correctly). P5's guide is the set's weakest key: at least five answers are flatly wrong against the Learner's Book's own pages ("120 ÷ 2 … equal to six" among them), one expected-answers block belongs to another book, the listening story it supplies is about Keza's plane trip while the Learner's Book titles it "Ishema's Birthday", and four of the pupil's test groups are left unkeyed. P6's guide is the most thorough — 415 pupil's-book page references landing in the right unit ranges — but its answer headings lag the pupil's exercises by one in places, and most of its assessment "answers" are marking guidance, not keys.

**What Rwanda's held books never teach.** No book in the set has a phonics programme — no letter-sound teaching, blending or sound chart in any of the 11 (spelling is jumbled-letter, missing-letter and dictation work). No second or third conditional anywhere (the first conditional, P5–P6, is the only one taught); no past perfect anywhere; no reported statements or questions (P6 teaches reported commands only); the passive is taught once, in P5 Unit 8; the present perfect is never taught as a system (named once in P4 Unit 9, used untaught in P5/P6 dialogue). There is no verifiable listening (no audio, and in the pupil's books almost no printed scripts), no Kinyarwanda support in any book, no extended reader or chapter-length text, and no examination apparatus beyond P3's two sample papers — which test untaught material.

**Still owed by the library side.** The P1 English Teacher's Guide: REB's own training notices confirm a P1 guide exists, and a 2025 revised P1–P3 teacher's-guide set now sits on REB eLearning (the P2 and P3 revised guides were located as exact links on 2026-10-23) — none of these is held. All secondary English: no S1–S6 English student's book is held at all, and the S1/S2/S3/S5 teacher's guides exist only as located links in the download list (the S4 book the library holds is Literature in English, a separate subject, and REB's catalogue has no S4 English teacher's guide).

### 4. Honest negatives
Verified across the whole book:
- No phonics system: there is no letter-sound teaching, no blending, no sound chart anywhere. The recurring "Sounds and spelling" blocks are a poem, a riddle and a dictation only; spelling is practised through jumbled-letter and missing-letter puzzles, never through sound–letter rules.
- No past-tense paradigm is ever stated: the past simple is used in Unit 4 (and irregular forms such as went, saw, ate appear), but no verb table, no -ed rule and no list of irregular forms is printed.
- No present continuous teaching as a structure: "wearing" sentences occur in Unit 7 and its assessment tests "(wear, wearing)" and "(is, are)", but the form is never explained or drilled as a tense anywhere in the book.
- No future form beyond "going to" and the "will" that appears untaught inside the Unit 4 assessment table and Sample Paper items; "will" is never taught in any lesson.
- No comparatives or superlatives are taught (cheap/expensive are presented as opposites only; "cheapest / most expensive" appear only inside the Unit 10 assessment questions, printed 140).
- No articles lesson (a/the are used but never taught), no possessive adjectives lesson, no plurals lesson — yet Sample Paper 2 (printed 145) tests singular forms of lorries/leaves/knives, and Sample Paper 2 (printed 146) tests sentence joining with which/where/because and Both…and…, none of which is taught anywhere in the pupil's book.
- No question-word teaching beyond what/where/when/how many/how much in context; no "why" structure lesson (why-questions occur only inside activities).
- No handwriting or letter-formation instruction; all writing is composition, copying, gap-fill and dictation.
- No audio component, no songs with notation beyond the anthem verse (printed 108) and chants, and no Kinyarwanda support or bilingual glossary — the glossary is English-only.
- Reading texts are all very short: the longest continuous prose is about half a page (the Unit 10 stories); there is no extended reader, no chapter story, and no non-fiction text beyond the graph data passages in Unit 9.
