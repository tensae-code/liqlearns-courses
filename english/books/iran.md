## Grammar and vocabulary spine for the grade

There is **no named grammar in this book at all** — no grammar box, no rule, no reference section. The spine below is the order in which forms are actually drilled, taken from the Map's Key Language column and confirmed in the Practices. In order: **be** in greetings and introductions ("What's your name? / My name is / I'm …", Lesson 1) → **this is** + person, **who is that …? — He/She's …**, nice-to-meet-you formulas (Lesson 2) → **how old …?** with be, and **when's …? — It's in + month**, numbers to 31 (Lesson 3) → **how old is your …?**, possessive **'s in family and job questions** ("What's your father's job? — He's a mechanic"), numbers to 100 (Lesson 4) → identifying people by description: **"Who/Which one is …? — He's the tall man. He's wearing + clothes/colour"** — present continuous used only inside the wearing formula here (Lesson 5) → **where** questions with be, possessive his/her with rooms, yes/no be questions with short answers, and the **full present continuous for current actions** ("What are you/they doing? — I'm/They're cooking"; "What is he/she doing? — He/She is studying", Lesson 6) → address/telephone formulae and **time** ("What time is it / are you going? — at/around 5 o'clock, a.m./p.m.", Lesson 7) → **favourite / like / would like** ("What's your favorite food?; What do you like to eat/drink?; I'd like some …"), **feel + hungry/thirsty**, and suggestions with **Let's … / How about …?** (Lesson 8). Vocabulary is entirely everyday and local: names, family and jobs, Persian-calendar months, clothes including chador, manteau and scarf, house rooms, Iranian street addresses and phone numbers, and Iranian food (kebab, dates, quince) alongside general food words. The alphabet is taught distributed, three or four letters per lesson in the Sounds and Letters pages — Aa Kk Mm · Ee Bb Pp · Ii Tt Nn · Uu Ss Gg · Dd Ll Rr · Ff Vv Ww · Oo Hh Xx Yy · Cc Jj Qq Zz — and is only complete at Review 4.

## What the workbook adds — and what it does not do

**Adds:** (1) the entire handwriting programme — stroke order for every letter and two example words per letter; (2) the only extended English reading texts in the Grade 7 package — nine short workbook-only texts (the two sisters at Mahan School; Matin Bateni; the eight-person family; Miss Samimi; dressing in Iran; Mr. Danesh's appearance; the big house; Mansoor Mohammadi; Iranian eating habits) plus two Persian–English parallel texts (Hassan Omidi; healthy eating) and one airport e-mail; (3) real-world document work found nowhere in the student book: a school ID card, an ELA membership application, a conference poster, a postal address, an architect's plan, a shop invoice list; (4) the recurring **International Words** strand (Lessons 5–8) — English words also used in Persian, built from a cumulative letter set that grows lesson by lesson, which is also the workbook's spelling discipline alongside the build-six-words tasks in Lessons 2–4; (5) explicitly Iranian content the student book only implies: named martyrs to identify, Iranian calendar occasions to date in English, and the Friday-holiday table. **Does not do:** no listening or speaking of any kind; no repetition of the student book's dialogues or substitution tables; no grammar exercises as such (forms are practised only inside the reading/writing tasks); no answer key. Instructions are in Persian throughout the lesson units, so the workbook presupposes a Persian-reading pupil or teacher mediation in a way the student book does not.

## Filing note — the Prospect → Vision transition across both pairs

Reading the four books whole, the join between Grade 9 and Grade 10 is a change of book architecture, not just of level. Prospect 3 (student book 136 pp, workbook 96 pp) teaches in six short, drill-centred lessons with photo-taught vocabulary, an intonation strand, a Photo Dictionary, and a bilingual workbook of ten exercise-types per lesson whose only new texts are one short reading per lesson.
