## 2. Structure of the guide

- **Front matter is only 3 printed pages**: title page (printed p. 1 (PDF 3)), credits (printed p. 2 (PDF 4)), and a welcome/how-to letter headed "Using My victory" (printed p. 3 (PDF 5)), addressed to teachers in Spanish. There is **no table of contents, no index and no bibliography**.
- The "Using My victory" letter (printed p. 3 (PDF 5)) states the organising system, in the guide's own terms:
  - All class steps are in Spanish; each lesson of the guide is divided into **four parts**, grouping the student book's lettered sections (its Lesson 1 example: Part 1 = sections A–D, Part 2 = E–F, Part 3 = G–H–I, Part 4 = J–K–L and "More Fun!"). In practice the letter groupings vary a lot from lesson to lesson (see §3 and §6).
  - **Each part is a 40-minute session**: Introduction 10 min + Presentation 20 min + Consolidation 10 min — i.e. normally 160 minutes of teaching per lesson. One part breaks this pattern (see §6).
  - Each lesson opens with a one-page **"Plan de estudios"** (study plan): the student-page range, teaching materials to make in advance (dialogue posters, audios, wordcards, flashcards, charts), the lesson's vocabulary, its grammar, what students will learn, and notes. The Plan is followed by the **"Pasos de enseñanza"** (teaching steps) for the four parts, with a right-hand margin column, "Lenguaje del estudiante", listing the English the students are expected to produce.
  - The plans repeatedly instruct the teacher to **make the resources him/herself** — posters of the student dialogues, audios recorded with students' voices using the scripts ("guiones") in the student book, wordcards and charts, very often "con material de reciclaje" (recycled card/board). The recurring instruction "Encontrar en resources" ("find in resources") points to a resources set that is **not included in this book**.
- **Units and lessons — verified from this book, not assumed.** The course is **24 lessons in 8 units, but NOT 3 lessons per unit**: Unit 1 = Lessons 1–3, Unit 2 = Lessons 4–6, Unit 3 = Lessons 7–9, **Unit 4 = Lessons 10–13 (four lessons)**, **Unit 5 = Lessons 14–15 (only two lessons)**, Unit 6 = Lessons 16–18, Unit 7 = Lessons 19–21, Unit 8 = Lessons 22–24.
- Only two unit/lesson titles are printed anywhere: Unit 3 is titled **"Time in our lives"** (misspelled in two of its three plans, see §6), and Lesson 14's plan carries the side heading "I want fried eggs for breakfast". All other lessons are identified only by unit and lesson number.
- Recurring student-book section types the guide keys, lesson after lesson: lettered dialogues (A, C, F, L…), "Let's read and learn", "Reading and reflecting / Read and reflect", "All ears!" (listening), "Building blocks" (grammar charts), "Now, let's practice!", "Let's reflect and create!", "Did you know?" and a closing game section usually headed "More Fun!" (in Lessons 9 and 24 replaced by a section titled "Either we invent or we err!").
- **Appendices / answer keys / transcripts: there are none.** The book ends with Lesson 24, Part 4 (guide printed p. 175 (PDF 177)) — no appendix, no collected answer key, no audio-script section, no back matter of any kind. Answers, where given at all, are scattered inside the plans and teaching steps (§4). Audio scripts are never printed in the guide: the teacher is told to record the audios using scripts in the student book, cited variously as student pp. 206–208 (Lesson 10 plan, guide printed p. 62 (PDF 64)), as the single student p. 208 (Lessons 20, 22, 23, 24 plans), as student p. 206 (Lesson 21, section I), or as the lesson's own student pages (e.g. Lessons 13, 14).

## 3. Unit-by-unit account of what is taught

For each lesson: guide pages (Plan + teaching steps), the student pages keyed, then the content **as the guide evidences it**. "Plan prints" flags student material actually reproduced in the guide (§4); everything else is material the guide only points to in the student book / resources.

### Unit 1 — Lessons 1–3 — student pp. 10–33

- **Lesson 1** — guide printed pp. 4–9 (PDF 6–11); student pp. 10–17. Greetings and introductions: *hi, hello, good morning, good afternoon, pleased/nice to meet you*, titles Miss/Ms./Mrs./Mr., leave-taking. Grammar: verb **be**, personal pronouns (I, you, he, she), **what**-questions. The alphabet is taught and names are spelled (Part 3, sections G–I, student pp. 13–14). A song is keyed to student p. 17 (audio only in the guide). Outcomes as printed: introduce yourself and others; greet and say goodbye. *Plan prints:* the wordcard answers for dialogue D (student p. 11) and for the be/practice activity K (student p. 16).
- **Lesson 2** — guide printed pp. 10–15 (PDF 12–17); student pp. 18–25. Same greeting/introduction field, extended with *this*, *goodbye, bye bye, see you later*. Grammar: verb **be** continued (including negatives in the wordcard answers, e.g. *'m not*), **what**-questions, and yes/no ("confirmation") questions. Outcomes: introduce self/others, greet and take leave. *Plan prints:* wordcard answers for K1 (student p. 24), word-order answers for K2, and the capital-letter set for More Fun!.
- **Lesson 3** — guide printed pp. 16–21 (PDF 18–23); student pp. 26–33. Classroom objects and polite formulas: *excuse me, may I…?, can you…?, I'm sorry, I'm really sorry, thank you*; classroom nouns with plurals (*door(s), eraser(s), box(es), watch(es)*). Grammar: the **imperative**, indefinite article **a/an**, **regular plurals**, demonstratives **this/that/these/those**. Outcomes: introduce people, greet, name people and objects, follow instructions. This is the first appearance of *can* — only inside the formula *can you…?*, not as a taught modal system.

### Unit 2 — Lessons 4–6 — student pp. 34–57

- **Lesson 4** — guide printed pp. 22–27 (PDF 24–29); student pp. 34–41. People around you: *classmates, neighbours, friends, husband and wife*, jobs and occupations, **cardinal numbers 1–10**, possessive adjectives *my, your, his, her, its, our, their*. Grammar: **a/an** again, possessive adjectives, nouns for personal relations and occupations. Outcomes: ask/answer about people, jobs and telephone numbers.
- **Lesson 5** — guide printed pp. 28–33 (PDF 30–35); student pp. 42–49. Countries and nationalities, occupations again. Grammar: **what- and where-questions**, definite article **the**, nouns (occupations, countries) and nationality adjectives. Outcomes: ask/answer about people — job, country of origin, nationality; count people and objects. *Plan prints:* the article answers (*the, a, a, the, an…*) for activity K1 (student p. 47).
- **Lesson 6** — guide printed pp. 34–40 (PDF 36–42); student pp. 50–57. The family: *mother, father, grandma/grandpa, sister, brother, son, daughter, aunt, cousin, husband, wife*; family tree work; **ordinal numbers** are named in the vocabulary (no list printed); wordcards for numbers 1–100 in words are to be made by the teacher. Grammar: demonstrative frames *This is… / These are…*, **irregular plurals** (family words), short questions/answers with **have**, *How many … do you have?*, possessives (the possessive **'s** is explained in the teaching steps, Part 1). Outcomes as printed: identify family vocabulary; write correct sentences with verb be. The Plan notes this lesson "tiene anexo un cuadro de imágenes" (an annexed picture chart in the student materials) and, like Lessons 7–9, warns that if the institution/students do not have *My First Victory* the teacher must make all the resources from recycled materials.

### Unit 3 — "Time in our lives" — Lessons 7–9 — student pp. 58–81

- **Lesson 7** — guide printed pp. 41–46 (PDF 43–48); student pp. 58–65. Months of the year, days of the week, time expressions (*in the morning, at noon, in the afternoon, in the evening*), ordinal numbers. Grammar: **when- and what-questions**, prepositions of time/place **in, on, at**, **there is / there are**. Outcomes: refer to day, month and date; talk about actions using time prepositions. Part 4 consists only of "More Fun!" (student p. 65).
- **Lesson 8** — guide printed pp. 47–55 (PDF 49–57); student pp. 66–73. Telling the time: numbers from 100 upwards, 3–5-digit numbers, numbers 1–60 for minutes, *time, o'clock*, parts of the day, days of the week, school subjects (Physical Education, Biology, Natural Science…), leisure activities (*a concert, a play, a baseball game, a movie*). Grammar: *What is…? / When…?* questions, prepositions **in, on, at**, nouns and adjectives. Outcomes: tell the time, use part-of-day expressions, identify time prepositions. Teaching steps are noticeably fuller here on clock mechanics (o'clock, quarter, minutes/seconds).
- **Lesson 9** — guide printed pp. 56–61 (PDF 58–63); student pp. 74–81. Age, body and colours: *How old are you?* with be; the human body (*hair, head, nose, mouth, hand, back, foot/feet, leg, arm, tooth/teeth, face, eyes, ear*); colours (yellow, blue, red, green, orange, purple, pink, brown, white, black); adjectives describing people (*old, young, thin, fat, tall, short*). Grammar: *how old?* and *what colour?* questions, yes/no questions with be. The "Head, Shoulders, Knees and Toes" video/song is used (Part 2, student pp. 76–77). Part 4 ("Either we invent or we eer!", student p. 81) is a class birthday-calendar craft project, and the *Happy Birthday* song lines are printed in the teaching steps.

## 5. Honest negatives — what this level never teaches

Verified against the whole guide (all 24 Plans and teaching steps):

- **No future forms.** Neither *will* nor *going to* is presented as a grammar point in any lesson. *Going to* occurs only incidentally, in two comprehension questions in Lesson 14 ("What is the mother going to do?", "What are they going to cook?") and in teacher talk.
- **No present perfect** (simple or continuous), **no past continuous**, **no conditionals**, **no passive**, **no reported speech** — none is named or presented in any unit or plan.
- **Past tense is narrow.** It consists of the past of *be* (was/were, Lessons 19–20), *there was/were* (Lesson 21), recycling in Lessons 22–23, and — only in the final review, Lesson 24 — affirmative sentences with a small set of lexical past forms (*went, made, visited, learned, spoke, arrived, loved, won, came*). There are **no *did*-questions, no past negatives, no presented -ed spelling rule and no irregular-verb list** anywhere.
- **No comparatives or superlatives** of adjectives (descriptive adjectives in Lesson 9 are never compared).
- **No modal system.** *Can* appears only in formulas (*Can you…?* in Lesson 3, *Can I help you?* in Lesson 12). *Must, should, would, could* are never taught (*should* never appears as content at all).
- **No object pronouns and no possessive pronouns** (mine/yours…): possession is possessive adjectives (Lesson 4) plus the possessive *'s*.
- **No quantifier grammar**: *how many* (Lesson 6) and *how much* for prices (Lesson 12) are taught as question frames, but much/many/some/any are never presented as a system.
- **No examination preparation of any kind**: no exam sections, model tests, test practice or assessment rubrics appear anywhere in the guide. (The words "English test" in Lesson 18 are dialogue content — a student studying for a test — not test preparation.)
- **No extended writing**: no paragraph or essay writing is taught at any point. Writing is copying, gap-filling, sentence writing, recipe ingredient lists, and one self-made dialogue in the final lesson.
- **No long reading**: every reading text is a short text or single paragraph, and all of them live in the student book, not in the guide.
- **No phonetics system**: pronunciation is handled by repetition and one initial-sound game (Lesson 10); spelling is taught only with the alphabet in Lesson 1. No phonetic transcription appears.
- **No answer-key or transcript apparatus** (see §2/§4): a teacher using only this guide cannot check most Lesson 1–9 exercises from the guide alone.

## 6. Honest negatives — what this level never teaches

Verified against the whole guide (overviews and steps; text-layer searches for the relevant terms return nothing):

- **No present perfect** in any form — no *have/has* + participle presentation, no *ever/never/just/already/yet* work. The past system taught is simple past + past continuous only.
- **No *will* future.** The only future form taught is *going to* (Lessons 19–21, reviewed in 23–24). *Will* occurs only inside the teacher's own scripted phrases ("we will start Lesson 2", "Now we will work in pairs").
- **No conditionals** — no *if*-sentence of any type is presented anywhere.
- **No passive voice.**
- **No reported speech.**
- **No superlatives as grammar.** Comparatives appear only in Lesson 18's overview (and, per §7, are not actually taught in that lesson's steps). The single word *biggest* in the book is inside a teacher's example sentence in Lesson 24.
- **No present simple as a new teaching point.** It is recycled from the previous year (heroes, oil, occupations lessons) and only one sub-point is ever presented: third-person -s pronunciation and *do/does*, in Lesson 23.
- **No examination preparation of any kind.** There are no exam-format exercises, no mock tests, no test-taking sections. School tests and grades appear only as *topics* (Lessons 17, 19, 20 talk about tests and marks in dialogues).
- **Writing is minimal throughout:** copying and completing dialogues, writing sentences, charts, posters and wordcards in notebooks. No paragraph writing, no letters, no essays, no guided composition is taught at this level (the word "essay" occurs only inside an example question about a history essay, Lesson 16).
- **No consolidated answer key** (see §4) and **no transcripts in the guide**; several printed exercise sets carry no answers at all (e.g. the Teresa Carreño true/false statements, Lesson 13).
- **No lesson or unit titles** are printed anywhere; the guide never names its own themes.
- The guide **nowhere identifies the student edition** it keys (no year, edition, or ISBN for the student book is printed in it), and never mentions pp. 201–205 of that edition.

## 2. Structure

**Front matter (printed 1–3 / PDF 3–5):** title page (printed 1); imprint/credits (printed 2); a one-page welcome and method note, "Using My Victory" (printed 3), addressed to teachers. There is **no table of contents, no index, and no bibliography** anywhere in the book.

**Method as stated in "Using My Victory" (printed 3 / PDF 5):** the guide contains ready-to-use lesson plans matching the student book; all teaching steps are written in Spanish. Each lesson is divided into **four parts**; a typical part covers student sections A–D (Part 1), E–G (Part 2), H–J (Part 3), and K, L plus "Let's have fun!" (Part 4). Each part is planned as a **40-minute** class: Inicio 10 min, Presentación 20 min, Consolidación 10 min. Each lesson opens with a one-page curriculum plan (*Plan de estudios*) listing teaching materials to make (posters, charts, wordcards, flashcards, audios), the lesson's vocabulary, what students will learn, the grammar, and notes. The steps pages carry a right-hand column, *Lenguaje del estudiante*, giving the English students are expected to produce. Materials are repeatedly flagged "Encontrar en resources" (on the order of 180 times in the text layer): the posters, charts and audios live in a **separate resources pack that is not bound into this PDF** — the guide describes the materials and tells the teacher to make them, it does not print them.

**Recurring student-book section types** (as named in the guide): Let's explore, Activating your mind, Now let's practice, Grammar emphasis, Vocabulary, Reading and reflecting, Discuss and reflect, Did you know?, Let's apply, All ears (listening/dictation), Keep in mind, Let's create, Ask me…, Let's have fun!, and a closing project section, "Either we invent or we err!" (a Simón Rodríguez motto), present in most lessons. Most lessons open with a proverb/saying (*dicho*).

**Organisation — verified from this book:** **8 units × 3 lessons = 24 lessons**, numbered continuously Lesson 1–24. Unit assignment follows the *Plan de estudios* pages: Unit 1 = Lessons 1–3, Unit 2 = Lessons 4–6, Unit 3 = Lessons 7–9, Unit 4 = Lessons 10–12, Unit 5 = Lessons 13–15, Unit 6 = Lessons 16–18, Unit 7 = Lessons 19–21, Unit 8 = Lessons 22–24. (The teaching-steps headers frequently contradict this — see §6.) Only two lessons carry a printed title in their plans: Lesson 1, "I'll be a great…", and Lesson 3, "My holidays". All other lessons are identified by number and theme only.

**Back matter:** **none.** The book ends with the last teaching steps of Lesson 24 (printed 161 / PDF 163). There are **no appendices, no answer-key section, no audio transcripts, no glossary, and no word list** in the guide. Twice (Lesson 6 steps) the guide sends students to "the glossary at the end of the book" — that is the *student* book's glossary, reported by the guide, not something this guide contains or that was verified.

**Keying range:** the 24 lessons key student pages 8–199 in blocks of mostly 8 pages per lesson (Lesson 11: 6 pages, 88–93; Lesson 21: 7 pages, 168–174), with student pages 94–95, 175 and 199 never keyed by any step (see §6).

## 4. Student-content accounting (honest grades)

Grades: **(a)** full reproduction of student pages · **(b)** keyed excerpts / partial student text · **(c)** answer keys + teaching notes only (student content referred to by page but not printed) · **(d)** methodology only.

This guide cannot substitute for the student's book, and no lesson content should be sourced to the student edition on the strength of this guide alone beyond what §3 marks as printed.

## 5. Honest negatives (verified against the whole book)
