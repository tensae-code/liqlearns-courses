## The book itself

"ENGLISH FOR PRIMARY SCHOOLS — TEXTBOOK — GRADE 3," Ministry of Education and Sports, Research Institute for Educational Sciences (RIES), 2021. The Lao foreword (unnumbered, PDF p.4) calls this the **revised edition** (ສະບັບປັບປຸງ): a communicative course — listening and speaking first as the base for reading and writing, vocabulary and grammar sized for beginners, topics tied to the children's daily lives, pair and group work, learner-centred, with songs, stories, games, role play and creative activities; it names the **phonics ("sounds") method as new in this revised curriculum** — children learn how sounds relate and use that to read and write words. A QR code on the foreword page points to extra teaching media. Writers: Manoly Dongvan, Vongmani Sitha, Viengkham Singvongsa, Soutta Inthavong, Phonesavanh Hongsomboun; Editor in Chief Khamphanh Phimsipasom; Managing Editor Manoly Dongvan; Illustrator Chongkham Phonkeo; design and layout Chaleunsak Khamsakpasit. **Structure.** An Introduction plus **7 units, 29 lessons**. Each unit is three content lessons and a "Let's practice!" review lesson (Unit 7 adds the Revision lesson). A content lesson runs one fixed shape of numbered activities across about six pages: an opener (Talk about the picture / Chant / Listen and chant / Listen and sing) → a second speaking activity (Listen and point / Ask and answer / Point and say / Speak) → some lessons a headed **Grammar** box on the first page → the phonics block: **Say the sound** (one letter-sound, taught through a two-to-three-line mini-text packed with the sound) → **Trace the letter** (stroke arrows) → **Copy the letter** (four ruled lines) → **Find the letter** (early lessons: hunt the letter in a grid, with three picture words) or **Listen/Read and point** with letter tiles (later lessons: blend CVC words) → **Listen to the story** (a six-panel picture story with speech bubbles) or a listening activity → a closer (role play, match, game, speak, order the pictures). Practice lessons run: Talk about the picture → Draw → a reading/matching activity → a blending or word-building activity → a listening activity → a game or spot-the-difference.

**The phonics spine** (one sound per content lesson, in teaching order): **s** (L1) → **a** (L2) → **t** (L3) → **i** (L5) → **p** (L6) → **n** (L7) → **c/k, written "ck"** (L9) → **e** (L10) → **h** (L11) → **d** (L13) → **m** (L14) → **r** (L15) → **o** (L17) → **g** (L18) → **u** (L19) → **l** (L21) → **f** (L22) → **b** (L23) → **j** (L25) → **z** (L26) → **w** (L27). Twenty-one sounds; all five short vowels; blending with letter tiles starts at L9 and CVC reading runs to the end. Letters q, v, x, y get no sound lesson; the whole alphabet appears once, as a song, in L16. Recurring children: Sounthone, Houmphanh, Champa, Payia (and Kham, Phone, Noy, Hong, Soumaly, Moneng in name activities).

## What looks new for the LiqLearns English ladder (from this book)

- **A complete sound-first teaching order** (Level 2–3 material): s-a-t-i-p-n first, then ck, e, h, d, m, r, o, g, u, l, f, b, j, z, w — the child can read real CVC words from the third lesson on; the alphabet song arrives only at L16 (p.73), as a review after sixteen sounds are already working. A full worked example of "sounds before alphabet names."
- **Blending by letter tiles** from L9 (c-a-p / c-a-t / k-i-ck, p.43) and the **-at family built by swapping the first letter** (hat/rat/cat/mat, p.74) plus "What is the first sound?" (p.74) — first-sound and word-family steps for the Sounds Together / First Words levels.
- **Feelings taught with no printed words** (L11, p.51): the six-picture song is the feelings set; the words around it in the book are "It's itchy!" (p.24), hot (L11's sound text p.52; L17 p.78) and cold (p.78) — a feelings lesson whose Laos evidence is picture-and-audio first, matching the platform's pictures-and-sound first stage.
- **Position words inside a song**: in/on/under taught as six sung farm lines with animal sounds (p.69) — a ready-made media-slot shape for the in/on/under lesson.
- **Numbers 6–10 through fruit and first sums**: counting six-to-ten fruits, then five picture addition sums on the same page (p.87) — numbers and adding meet in one lesson.
- **Do you like…? with Yes, I do / No, I don't** (p.86) and **Can you…?** (p.104): yes/no questions with short answers, each grown out of the statement lesson before it — Laos's Grade 3 evidence for the Questions level.
- **A wordless story for retelling** (L21, pp.98–99) and **story-ordering as the check** (L27, p.126, re-ordering L25's story): two story-level shapes not in the built lessons yet.
- **Write the word** (Revision, p.131): the single step in the whole book where the child writes a word freely — everything before it is tracing and copying; a clean marker of where this system puts first writing.
- Laos-flavoured stock for examples: Hmong New Year dancing and a drum (p.60), a gecko on the wall (pp.84, 117), papaya/papayas and mango trees, a farm of pond, mud and tractor (p.69), and the name set Sounthone, Houmphanh, Champa, Payia, Kham, Phone, Noy, Hong, Soumaly, Moneng.

Back page (printed p.202): ISBN **978-9932-00320-4** (the student book is 978-9932-00317-4); printed by Theppanya Printing Company Sole Co., Ltd., print registration 006ວພຈ02032021, run of **13,000 copies**, 21×29.7 cm (A4 — the student book is a smaller 18×25 cm); supported by the Australian Government and the state budget, curriculum development additionally supported by the European Union; made into an e-book by the educational-media development centre and distributed free. Title page (PDF p.2): writers Vongmani Sitha, Viengkham Singvongsa, Soutta Inthavong, Phonesavanh Hongsomboun, Manoly Dongvan, Chongkham Phonkeo; Managing Editor Khamphanh Phimsipasom; Editor in Chief Viengkham Singvongsa; design and layout Chaleunsak Khamsakpasit — the same team as the student book, with the two editor roles swapped between the two books as printed. **What kind of book it is.** A bilingual (Lao–English) script for teaching the student book: every student-book lesson gets one full lesson plan, and the guide prints everything the student book leaves to the audio — every song, chant, mini-text, story script and listening script, with answer keys. Lesson numbering, activity numbering and page order line up with the student book one-to-one, so anything cited here by lesson matches the student-book filing's lesson map.

## The method pages (Introduction, printed pp.5–7) — what the guide adds before any lesson

- **Materials the teacher makes (p.5).** **Phonics cards** for every sound taught in Grade 3, the letters drawn exactly as printed in the student book (a fan of the s-a-t-i-p-n cards is pictured). **Word cards** with simple CV/CVC words, used from Part 4 (Unit 4) onward and posted around the classroom; the pictured example set is bag, book, pencil, pen, desk, chair.
- **Assessment (section 5, pp.5–6).** Each unit's four lessons each assess a different skill: **Lesson 1 — vocabulary and listening; Lesson 2 — grammar and speaking** (speaking assessed in the pair activity, hour 3, practice stage); **Lesson 3 — writing** (copying the unit's three new letters, assessed in hour 2 or from notebooks collected after the hour; letter writing is also watched across hours 1–3); **Lesson 4 — reading** (sound links from Units 1–4 first, later reading words from Units 5–7; assessed in hour 2, practice stage). Every lesson plan carries a 0–3 rubric for its assessed skill, and rubric marks convert to /10 (worked table on p.6: rubric 3 → 10, 2 → 7, 1 → 3, 0 → 0). So no child is marked on everything every week, the class is split into **Group 1 and Group 2 (10–15 pupils each)** and the groups alternate which lessons they are assessed on, by a printed 7-unit rotation table (p.6).
- **The week and the unit (section 6, pp.6–7).** English runs **one lesson per week, each lesson 3 hours**; a unit is 4 lessons = 12 hours, and every unit has the same shape (table, p.7): Lesson 1 — hour 1 vocabulary set 1, hour 2 sounds & letters (sound 1), hour 3 the story, part 1; Lesson 2 — hour 1 grammar part 1, hour 2 sounds & letters (sound 2), hour 3 grammar part 2; Lesson 3 — hour 1 vocabulary set 2, hour 2 sounds & letters (sound 3), hour 3 the story, part 2; Lesson 4 — hour 1 creative activity, hour 2 review of sounds & letters, hour 3 review lesson.
- **The lesson-plan shape** (used for all 29 lessons + the Introduction lesson): I. Objectives · II. Assessment (the 0–3 rubric) · III. Main content · IV. Teaching media (student book, the named Audio Tracks, real objects where used) · V. Teaching activities by hour, each hour in stages — lead-in ≈5 min, main activity ≈15–25 min, practice & use ≈15–25 min, summary ≈5 min — with an "Assessment" check note after activities. Every plan is headed "time: 3 hours."

## The Introduction lesson — "Let's learn English!" (printed pp.8–11)

The guide prints the three things the student book carries only in pictures and audio:

- **The seven classroom commands** (p.8): **Stand up, Sit down, Open your book, Point to…, Look, Listen, Say** — each with its Lao gloss. No assessment in the first week.
- **The hello song, Audio Track 0.1 — full words (p.8)**: a Hello / How are you? call-and-response whose answer lines are "I'm good!" and "I'm great!" (each sung twice). Actions (p.9): Hello = wave; How are you? = point at the pupils; I'm good! = thumbs up; I'm great! = raise both arms high. Taught line by line, the class split in halves singing alternate lines, then in groups.
- **The game "Soutta Says"** (pp.9–11): the Simon-Says game under this printed name — act only when the command is prefaced with "Soutta says"; act without it and you are out and sit down; play to a winner. The game returns in Lesson 21 (p.147) and in the Revision lesson (p.198).
- **Audio Track 0.2 (Listen and point) — script and answer key (p.11)**: heard order 1. stand up, 2. look, 3. point to, 4. say, 5. sit down, 6. open your book, 7. listen; the printed key gives the student book's picture numbers in heard order: **3, 4, 1, 6, 5, 7, 2**.

## What the guide adds, lesson by lesson

For every lesson: the assessed skill and rubric, the full audio scripts, the answer keys, and the guide-only games. The phonics additions (action + formation rhyme + mini-text for every sound) are collected in one list after the units.

## The book itself

"ENGLISH FOR LAO PRIMARY SCHOOLS — STUDENT'S BOOK 2 — GRADE 4," Ministry of Education and Sports, Research Institute for Educational Sciences (RIES), **2012**. The English Introduction (PDF p.4) dates and frames the series: this Grade 4 book is "the second in a series of three textbooks" (Grades 3–5) built for the reform of general education from 11 years to 12; under that reform English entered the primary curriculum from Grade 3, starting in academic year 2009–2010; the books were written by the Foreign Language Research and Resource Center at RIES with the Faculty of Letters, National University of Laos, and this book's development was financed by **AusAID and Save the Children Norway (SCN)**. The Introduction states the method in one line — contents "mainly focus on the illustration base learning," activities stressing the four skills (listening, speaking, reading, writing) to build basic knowledge for further study — and admits the book "is produced under the time pressure; therefore, some minor errors could not be avoided" (the slips list below bears that out). Credits (PDF p.2): Advisor Phouangkham Somsanith; Editor in Chief Assoc. Prof. Dr. Bouasavanh Keovilay; Managing Editors Khamphanh Phimsipasom and Viengkham Phonpraseuth; Writers Manivone Phaxayavong, Bouakeo Vilaylath, Thongsouk Keomany, Bounthapany Bounxouay; design and layout Khonesavanh Chanthavixay. PDF p.3 is a Lao preface; PDF p.5 is the acknowledgements ("ACKNOWLEGEMENTS" as printed). **No ISBN is printed anywhere in the book** — checked on the cover spread, the credits page and the back pages; the back cover is a blank pink panel with one short Lao printer line left empty. PDF p.1 is the whole cover spread (back cover, spine, front cover on one landscape sheet): the front cover shows two foxes, one asking "What's your favourite colour?" and the other answering "Green" beside a thought-bubble tree. Every interior page carries the footer "Ministry of Education and Sports — Research Institute for Educational Sciences," a printed folio, and a faint slanted Lao "RIES" watermark.

**Standing against the Grade 3 book filed before it.** The filed Grade 3 student book is the **2021 revised edition** of Book 1 of this same series; this Grade 4 is the **original 2012 Book 2**, written a decade before that revision. So Grade 4 continues the series the older way, and several Grade 3 (2021) features do **not** carry over: there is **no phonics spine at all** — no Say the sound, no Trace/Copy/Find the letter, no sound mini-texts; letter work shrinks to one capital↔lowercase matching page (p.3) and the alphabet shown as a song page (p.9). The six-panel picture stories are gone too. What replaces them is a fixed dialogue-lesson machine (below), photographs of real schoolchildren in red-scarf uniform carrying many speaking pages where Grade 3 used drawings, and a bilingual back matter (Vocabulary + Expressions, English–Lao) that Grade 3 does not have. The name stock overlaps only in Kham and Noy; the Grade 4 cast is Pha, Noy, Kham, Aloun, Sack, Naly, Boun, Keo, Vanh, Sone, Thong, Vieng, Nanthida (and Jenny in the birthday song).

**Structure.** One running head, "Every day English," over **18 lessons**: fourteen content lessons, three short Revision lessons (after Lessons 4, 9 and 14) and a final Revision 1–17. Every content lesson is two sections (A and B), and every section runs the same numbered activity shape: **1 Look, listen and repeat** (a speech-bubble dialogue) → **2 Look and say** (the vocabulary set or pattern over pictures/photos) → **3 Read and match** (sentences/words to pictures, or English to Lao) → **4 Listen and tick ✓** (a/b pairs, pictures or printed sentences) → **5 Look and write** (copy the model sentences, then gapped copies with missing letters) → in many sections **6 Let's write** (gapped words/sentences, word-order tiles, or arithmetic answered in words) → **Let's talk** (speech-bubble frame to speak) → some sections close with **Let's sing / Let's chant / Let's play**. The Contents (front section, printed pp.1–6) prints each lesson's target dialogues beside a **Competencies** table — what the child can do, with the lesson's page range; the ranges match the body's printed pages exactly (L1 pp.1–9 … L18 pp.135–139).

## What is new against Grade 3

- **The series' older design, not the revised one.** This 2012 Book 2 predates the phonics revision that reshaped the 2021 Grade 3 book: no sound lessons, no letter tracing, no blending — after Grade 3's 21-sound spine, Grade 4's only letter work is matching capitals to lowercase (p.3) and the alphabet as a song page (p.9). Reading here means matching whole sentences — to pictures and, in nearly every section, **to Lao translations**, a bilingual shape Grade 3 never uses.
- **Writing jumps a full step.** Grade 3's writing was tracing and copying letters, with one free "Write the word" at the very end. Grade 4 writes in every section: copy the model sentences, then the same sentences gapped, missing-letter words, **word-order tiles rebuilt into sentences** (pp.61, 111, 136), a **word-bank dialogue to complete** (p.135), and **arithmetic answered in written number words** (pp.26, 73).
- **Plurals become a system**: What are these? / They are…, Are these…?, How many…?, Where are the…? / They're…, animal names in plurals — where Grade 3 had only the plural -s inside counting ("1 pig / 2 pigs").
- **New grammar Grade 3 does not teach**: Who's he/she? + describing people (tall/short/old/young, opposites marked ≠); positive and negative **commands** (please / Don't…! / I'm sorry); **Let's…** suggestions; **Can you…? as a help request** with Yes, of course / I'm sorry. I'm busy — a different job from Grade 3's ability Can you…?, which itself returns in third person (Can he/she…?, Can the bird…?); **How old are you?**; **weather** (How's the weather today?); the **seven days**; **next to** added to in/on/under, with plural locations; **wants** (I'm hungry/thirsty, What do you want?, Do you want…?) beside Grade 3's likes; **shapes** and the first adjective-before-noun descriptions (a red cross); **favourite colour** with What about you?; animal abilities (hop, crawl, bark).
- **Numbers run 11→50 in three steps** (11–20, 21–30, 31–50), always word + numeral together, and meet arithmetic (+, −, ×, ÷) whose answers are written as English words; the final revision drills the teen/ty sound pairs (15/50, 13/30…) by ear and plays Bingo to 50.
- **Contractions are taught as printed boxes** — I'm/What's (p.1), Who's/He's/She's (p.41), don't/we're (p.54), They're (p.91) — where Grade 3 used contractions without ever naming them.
- **The book carries its own dictionary**: the back-matter Vocabulary (per lesson, English–Lao) and Expressions (per lesson key sentences) — the child's first reference pages, and the Contents' Competencies tables tell the child what they can now do, lesson by lesson.

## What is new against Grades 3–4

- **The same older machine, one full load heavier.** Grade 5
  keeps Grade 4's 2012 design exactly — 18 lessons, two sections,
  the eight-step activity shape, Read and match to Lao, the
  bilingual back matter — and spends it on finishing every line
  Grade 4 opened: numbers 11–50 become **50–100 plus telephone
  numbers**; the days of Grade 4 are joined by **months,
  ordinals and the date**; Grade 4's in/on/under/next to is
  completed by **in front of / behind / between**; Grade 4's
  colour+shape descriptions grow into **adjectives before any
  household noun, singular and plural**; Grade 3's "There is a…"
  returns as a full **There is / Is there / Are there** system;
  Grade 3's ability Can you…? and Grade 4's Can he/she…? settle
  into **What can he/she do?** with two verbs joined by and/but.
- **First reading passages in the series.** Grades 3 and 4 have
  no reading text at all — reading meant matching sentences.
  Grade 5's Revision 15 prints a short text about a house with
  three written comprehension answers (p.117), and Lesson 18
  prints a longer first-person text and then says **"Now write
  about yourself"** (p.139) — the series' first free writing,
  three years after Grade 3's single "Write the word."
- **First test shapes.** "Choose the right answers" (p.138) is
  the first multiple-choice exercise in the three books, and the
  friend survey (p.137) the first activity that sends the child
  to ask classmates and record their answers.
- **New grammar neither earlier book teaches**: this/**that**,
  these/**those** (Grade 4 had only this/these); telephone
  numbers read digit by digit with "o" for zero; the clock —
  o'clock plus "seven fifteen / nine thirty"; "What time do
  you…? — At…"; months and the date with ordinals to 31st;
  professions as their own lesson, with **third-person job
  descriptions** — "They are teachers. They teach English." —
  the series' first plain present-tense verbs about other
  people; daily routines across four day-parts ("What do you do
  in the morning?"); the body with its plurals printed, and
  **"What's the matter? — I have a…"**; **"What's wrong? — I
  can't…"** for classroom troubles; **wearing** — the only
  present-continuous form in the three books, kept in one lesson
  (pp.118–123); and likes joined into long answers — "I like
  sausage but I don't like fish soup" (p.132).
- **Contraction notes shrink to one**: only "Who's = who is"
  (p.49) gets a printed box, where Grade 4 boxed four sets; the
  book otherwise uses there's/isn't/aren't/can't without naming
  them (the Contents itself heads Lesson 12 "There's… / Is
  there…?").
- **Writing steps up again**: letter-unscrambles (spell the word
  from its jumbled letters — months, ordinals, clothes), word
  tiles that build **questions** as well as statements (p.109),
  written answers to comprehension questions (pp.117, 138–139),
  and the free self-description (p.139).

admission as the primary books ("the textbook is produced under
the time pressure; therefore, some minor errors could not be
avoided"); signed "Vientiane Capital, 01 February, 2010." PDF

lessons each**, a Revision after Units 1–4 (printed p.54), after
Units 5–6 (p.77), after Units 7–9 (p.118) and after Units 10–11

disabled, RC4); its English text layer is clean but **doubled**

visible one, which is why the credits extract with every line
repeated); its Lao layer extracts as mojibake under the same
legacy font encoding as Grades 4–5 — the Lao was read on the
English-facing structure where it mattered, and the several
Grammar notes whose rules are stated only in Lao are filed here
by their English tables and examples. Every interior page
carries the running unit head, a printed folio, and the faint
slanted Lao "RIES" watermark.

**Standing against the primary series — what the secondary book
changes.** (1) **It starts over.** Secondary year 1 assumes
nothing: greetings, the alphabet, numbers 1–12, classroom words,
can/can't and the family are all taught again from zero — then
carried much further than the primary books ever took them. (2)
**Grammar becomes a system.** The primary books almost never
named a rule (Grade 5's plurals-in-the-word-list was as far as
it went); this book prints a headed **Grammar note** in nearly
every lesson — be, can, this/that/these/those, there is/are,
have/has, articles, the present simple, do/does, the clock,
frequency adverbs, at/on/in, have got, the present continuous,
the past simple — each a table of statements / questions /
short answers. No other Laos book filed so far does this.
(3) **Pronunciation arrives**: two sound systems the primary
books never printed — the three sounds of verb -s (/s/ /z/
/iz/, p.77) and the three sounds of -ed (/t/ /d/ /id/, p.138).
(4) **Reading is in every lesson**: a short passage with
questions (or true/false) closes nearly every lesson from
Unit 1 Lesson 3 on — where Grade 5 managed two passages in the
whole book. (5) **Writing steps up**: sentence-building from
word tiles and prompts throughout, and the first named
**paragraph** ("Write a paragraph about one of your family
member," p.117, with a model paragraph). (6) The lesson shape
changes: the primary books' fixed eight steps (Look, listen and
repeat → … → Let's …) give way to numbered activities under
named heads (Vocabulary with Lao glosses, Listen and repeat,
Grammar note, Read and answer, Write, Games, Translation), and
**Lao→English translation exercises** — absent from the
primary books — recur from Unit 1 on. (7) The series is openly
built on outside grammars: the Bibliography (p.147) names
Gammidge's Grammar Works 1 (Cambridge, 1998), Headway Beginner
(Oxford, 2008), Murphy's English Grammar in Use, 2nd ed.
(1994), New English File (Oxford, 1997) — and a 1998 predecessor,
"English for Lao Secondary Schools (book 1)" (Phandamnong et
al., Ministry of Education, 1998), so this 2010 Book One is a
revision of a 1998 series opener.

## The map, unit by unit

**Unit 1 — Greeting and Introduction (printed pp.1–13).**
*Lesson 1, Hello. How are you? (pp.1–4):* greeting vocabulary
with Lao glosses (hello/hi, good morning/afternoon/evening,
fine, thank you, bye/goodbye, How are you?); the greeting
dialogue ("Hello. How are you? — Hello. I am fine. Thank you.
And you?", p.1); pair practice; gapped-dialogue completion;
**the English alphabet** — capitals and small letters, then
**vowels (A E I O U) and consonants named as sets** (p.3);
listen-and-circle the letter heard; write capitals under small
letters; **"How do you spell your name?" — S-O-U-M-A-L-Y**
(p.3); copying names sorted into people / roads / cities /
countries, capitals at work (p.4); the **Alphabet Song**,
printed in full (p.4). *Lesson 2, Meeting different people
(pp.5–10):* eight jobs (teacher, student, farmer, nurse,
doctor, policeman, soldier, driver); "I am a teacher. And you?"
and the **Are you a…? / Is he a…?** exchanges with Yes, I am /
No, he is not (p.6); the book's first **Grammar note** (p.8):
the be table — statements (I am / you, we, they are / he, she,
it is) across the job words, questions, negatives and short
answers; **contractions table** (I am = I'm … they are =
they're, p.9); ask-and-answer and gapped mini-dialogues; the
guessing game "What's my job?"; a Translation exercise
(Lao→English, p.10). *Lesson 3, Pleased to meet you (pp.11–13):*
introducing a third person — "Siboun, this is Bouakeo. —
Hello, Bouakeo. Pleased to meet you. — Pleased to meet you

+ job prompts ("Siboun is a student"); the book's **first
reading passage** — three short be-paragraphs with a
true/false check (p.13).

**Unit 2 — Classroom Language (pp.14–25).** *Lesson 1, In the

late-for-class dialogue — "I'm sorry. I'm late. — That's all

this in English? (pp.17–20):* thirteen classroom objects (bag,
bench, book, chair, desk, door, paper, pen, pencil, rubber,
ruler, table, window); **What's this? — It's a… / Is it a pen?
— No, it isn't** exchanges built over the set (pp.19–20);
jumbled-letter spelling (gab, lcipne, rrule…, p.18); word–
picture matching; letter-completion spelling drills (p.20).
*Lesson 3, Can you do this? (pp.21–25):* action words (cook,
dance, eat, jump, read a book, read the sign, ride a bicycle,
sing, swim, touch the fan) with can/can't in the word list
itself; a **can Grammar note** (p.22) — statements, Can you /
he / she…? questions, short answers Yes, I can / No, he can't,
and the notice that the verb does not change; pair dialogues;
a tick/cross can-table read into sentences ("Bounmy can read
the sign. He can't sing"); a class **Can you…? survey** and six
written sentences about yourself and friends (p.25).

**Unit 3 — Countries and Nationalities (pp.26–39).** *Lesson 1,
Where are you from? (pp.26–29):* the eleven ASEAN countries
(Laos, Vietnam, Cambodia, Thailand, Myanmar, Malaysia,
Indonesia, Singapore, the Philippines, China, Brunei) named on
a map of the region (p.26); "I'm from Laos. Where are you

**Countries / Nationalities / Languages** — the languages being

and Brunei), Khmer, Vietnamese, Chinese, Myanmese, Tagalog
(p.33); a reading passage (Thongdy and his Chinese friend
Chong, "He can speak a little Lao") with questions; a word
puzzle. *Lesson 3, I'm from Laos. And you? (pp.35–39):*
**and/but joining** — two same-country statements fold into
"Thongdy is from Laos, and Soutchay is from Laos. They are
from Laos," and different countries take but (pp.35–36), with
a Note box (Singular is / Plural are); joining practice
("Join the following sentences with and or but," p.37);
speaks-language sentences ("Lien speaks Vietnamese and Lao,"
p.38); can/can't joined with but; a true/false passage
(pp.38–39).

**Unit 4 — Numbers (pp.40–54).** *Lesson 1, Numbers 1–12
(pp.40–43):* the twelve number words re-taught with plus,

— "Eight plus four equals twelve" (p.42) and five written sums

(p.43) — the rule stated in Lao, the English example being
three sentences about Vikanda. *Lesson 2, This is a pen. These
are pens (pp.45–49):* **this/that/these/those** — "What is
this? — This is a pen. / What are these? — These are pens"
(p.45), that/those for far things (p.47); a Grammar note and a
**Grammar summary** (p.49) that turns into the book's **plural
rule**: one bag → two bags, with the note (in Lao) and the
English examples box–boxes, dish–dishes for the **-es** ending
after s, x, ch, sh; write-the-plural drill over ten nouns.
*Lesson 3, How many…? (pp.50–53):* **there is / there are** and
**How many…?** — "There are six books in the picture"; a
true/false classroom-counting game; "How many houses are there
in this village? — There are six houses"; listen-and-draw; a
**there is/are Grammar note** (p.52) with Is there…? / Are
there…? and short answers; a passage with five questions; a
crossword. **Revision (Units 1–4)** (p.54): am/is/are gap-fill
and short-answer completion across Units 1–4, closing with two
How many…? questions answered in words.

**Unit 5 — The Family (pp.55–66).** *Lesson 1, My family
(pp.55–58):* the family set (father, mother, parents, brother,

true/false (p.62). *Lesson 3, Articles (pp.63–66):* **a/an and

**Unit 6 — Every Day Life (pp.67–78).** *Lesson 1, What do you

*Lesson 3, Getting up early (pp.75–77):* house-and-chore words

note is two clock faces** (p.88, checked on the render): the

**Unit 8 — Days and Months (pp.93–103).** *Lesson 1, What day

Important dates (pp.99–103):* dates read in full — "1st January

**Unit 9 — Parts of the Body (pp.104–120).** *Lesson 1, Parts

*Lesson 2, Parts of body (pp.110–112):* seventeen body words
