# Greetings — the lesson

Course: English (skill course) · Title: Welcome to My School · Lesson: Greetings
Built from: Egypt, English Language Primary 1 Term 1 — Unit 1, Lesson 1.
This is the level-1 layer: the first thing Egypt's Grade 1 book teaches.

## Do it first
Stand up. Walk to someone near you — or to your explain companion — and
say: "Hello!" Then ask them: "What's your name?" Listen to their answer.
Now say your own name like this: "My name is ___." Shake hands, and say:
"Nice to meet you."

You just did the whole lesson. Now let's see how it works.

## Then the explanation
So a greeting is how we open the door between two people. Before the
greeting, you are strangers standing next to each other. After it, you
are two people talking.

It goes in small steps, like this — this is the talk in your book, between
Maya and Hana:

- One person starts: "Hello!"
- Then the question: "What's your name?"
- Then the answer, as a full sentence: "My name is Maya."
- The other one answers the same way: "I'm Hana!" — "I'm" is the short
  way of saying "I am."
- Then the warm part: "Nice to meet you." And the answer to that is the
  same words back, with one small word added: "Nice to meet you, too."
- You can shake hands — hold out your hand, take theirs, one small shake.
- And when it is time to go, the closing word is: "Goodbye!"

See the shape of it? Hello opens it. The names go in the middle.
Goodbye closes it. Open, middle, close — every greeting you will ever
make has that same shape.

One thing to notice, because it matters for all your English: "My name
is Maya" is a complete sentence. The book teaches you to introduce
yourself using complete sentences — not just "Maya," but the whole
sentence, with its ending. One idea, one whole sentence.

## Say it to your companion
Read the Maya and Hana talk out loud, both parts. Then do it again with
your own name in it. Explain to your companion: what are the three parts
of a greeting? (Open — names — close.)

## Do It Yourself
1. Greet someone for real today — at work, at church, anywhere. Use the
   whole shape: Hello → What's your name? → My name is ___ → Nice to
   meet you → Goodbye.
2. Do it a second time, and this time shake hands when you say "Nice to
   meet you."
3. Write the five sentences down, one idea on each line:
   Hello! / What's your name? / My name is ___. / Nice to meet you. /
   Goodbye!
