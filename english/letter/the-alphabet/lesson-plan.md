# Lesson plan — The alphabet

**Course:** English · **Title:** Letter · **Lesson:** The alphabet
**Place in the curriculum:** Year 1, Term 1, Unit 1 "The Alphabet" —
the first lesson of the unit (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the 26 letters — each with a
name, two shapes (capital and small), and a sound.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the Pencil and Lines course (the hand before the
  letters). Recommended, never forced.
- This is the first lesson of the English course proper.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say or sing all 26 letters in order, A to Z.
2. Point at a letter and give its name.
3. Match each capital to its small letter: A–a, B–b, …
4. Say the sound a letter makes, with its book word: "A — apple."
5. Tell the two ways books teach the letters: the order way (the
   whole train A to Z) and the sounds way (a few at a time, grouped
   by sound).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil for the writing steps. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner says the letters they already know,
   out loud, in order, and stops where they stop; that stopping place
   is where the lesson starts working (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE at the top of the lesson: a picture of a train with
     26 cars, the engine marked A and the last car marked Z — the
     student counts the cars and finds where their own alphabet
     stopped.
2. **The explanation** — the alphabet as the toolbox of reading; each
   letter's three things (name, two shapes, sound); Libya's A-to-Z
   word list; Egypt's sounds way (T, I, then S, A, N) (lesson/
   lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE under the three things: a picture of one letter in
     its two coats — a big A beside a small a — the student points
     and says "same letter, two shapes."
   - ADD AUDIO: the 26 letters read aloud in order, with each
     letter's word ("A — apple") — the student listens once, then
     says each letter after it.
3. **Say it to your companion** — read the 26 aloud; then cover the
   list and explain the three things every letter has.
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in questions/video-script.md, filmed —
     the alphabet sung and pointed to, letter by letter, in the
     super-simple voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (questions/levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — questions/hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — sing the alphabet three times today and mark
   the stumbling letter; write the capitals with their small letters
   under them; pick five letters and say each one's word.

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- IMAGE — a picture of a 26-car train, A on the engine, Z on the last
  car → the student counts the cars and finds their stopping letter.
- IMAGE — a picture of a capital and small letter side by side →
  the student says "same letter, two shapes."
- AUDIO — the alphabet read aloud with each letter's word → the
  student listens, then repeats each letter.
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later) → the student sings
  along, pointing at each letter.

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the
plan is the build sheet, so nothing the lesson needs gets forgotten.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the order in the middle → go back to the train: sing from A
  and feel where the gap is; the missing car is the work.
- Mixes a name with a sound → the name is for saying the alphabet;
  the sound is what the letter does inside a word ("A — apple").
- This lesson's next door: Blending sounds — pushing the sounds
  together into words (a different thing, its own lesson).
