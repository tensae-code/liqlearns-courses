# Before the letters: hold a pencil — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Letter · Lesson: Before the letters
This is the bottom of the English ladder — the lesson before Lesson 1.
Built from: Egypt, English KG1 Term 1, book p.1.

## Do it first
Get a pencil. Hold it up. Now look at your fingers: where are they on
the pencil? Take a photo in your head. Then put the pencil down — we
are going to hold it the book's way, in five small steps.

## Then the explanation
So before a letter, there is a hand. A letter is a shape your hand makes
with a pencil — if the hand is wrong, every shape fights you.

The book shows it in five pictures, like this:

1. The pencil lies on the table. Your hand comes down open.
2. Two fingers — your thumb and your first finger — take the pencil,
   like a bird taking something small with its beak.
3. The pencil rests on your middle finger, underneath.
4. Now the pencil tilts back, into the soft place between your thumb
   and fingers. It is resting, not being squeezed.
5. And you write. The pencil stands at an angle, like a small tree
   leaning. Your hand moves; the pencil follows.

Not squeezed. If your fingers turn white, you are squeezing. Resting,
like the pencil is sitting in a small chair made of your fingers.

Then, before any letter, the book gives you lines to trace: wavy lines,
like small hills, and zigzag lines, like mountains. Why? Because every
letter is made of just these moves — curves and straight lines, up and
down. Trace the hills until your hand is smooth. Trace the mountains
until your corners are clean. Then your hand is ready, and the letters
can come.

## Say it to your companion
Explain to your companion: why does the course start here, and not
with A? (Because a letter is a shape a hand makes — the hand comes
first.)

## Do It Yourself
1. Hold your pencil the five-step way. Check: thumb and first finger
   on top, pencil resting on the middle finger, no white fingers.
2. On paper, draw a line of wavy hills, slow. Then a line of zigzag
   mountains, slow. Then again, a little faster, still smooth.
3. Do it once more tomorrow before any letter work. This is the
   warm-up, every time.
