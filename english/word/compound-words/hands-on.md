# Hands-on — Compound words

The lesson with words in your hands, not only on the page.

## Slide and lock
Write the lesson's small words on cards: FOOT, BALL, SUN,
DIAL, HOUR, GLASS, BLACK, BOARD, TOOTH, BRUSH, CUP, WATER,
FALL. On the table, slide pairs together, read the compound
aloud, and act or draw what it names. Every true join stays
locked in a row; count your row at the end.

## Compound hunt at home
Walk your home and hunt compounds hiding in plain sight:
bedroom, bathroom, cupboard, notebook, toothbrush,
doorbell… Name each thing, then split it aloud. The home
lessons' rooms are compounds too — bed + room.

## Split to read
Take a book page. Find three long words. For each one ask:
does it split into two whole words? If yes, write the
split and guess the meaning from the parts before you read
on. If no part stands alone, it is a built word, not a
compound — different toolbox.

## Build a new one
Invent a compound for a thing that needs a name: a box for
rain clothes? A rainbox, a wetbox? Build it, define it from
its parts, and teach it to a partner — compounds were all
invented by somebody, once.
