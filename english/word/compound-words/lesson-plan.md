# Lesson plan — Compound words

**Course:** English · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** Compound words
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Words for the World —
the second building way: whole word + whole word
(CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** compound words —
two whole words joined into one new word (foot + ball =
football; sun + dial = sundial; cup + board = cupboard) —
how to read them by splitting, how to build them, and the
three spellings: joined, hyphenated, and open.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Word building
(same level, the lesson before it in the build order: parts
that are NOT words alone). A compound's parts ARE words
alone — that difference is this lesson's spine. It needs
nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown changing — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons

1. **Splitting Compound Words** — the books' compounds split into
   their two whole words, each part passing the alone-test. (Was

2. **The Books' Sets and Formation** — words joined into true
   compounds, the books' joined and hyphenated spellings, and the

3. **Reading by Parts** — a compound never seen before read by its
   parts, and compound plurals formed by Vietnam's rules. (Was
   depth Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking
is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set
is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Word building (same level). Recommended,
  never forced — this lesson stands alone: the alone-test
  below teaches the difference inside the lesson itself.
- Prefixes and suffixes are NOT this lesson's parts. If a
  part cannot stand alone (un-, -ness), it belongs to Word
  building. Here every part can stand alone.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Split a compound into its two whole words: football,
   hourglass, sundial (Sudan's own examples), cupboard,
   seaside (Barbados's, as printed).
2. Build compounds by joining words that belong together —
   Vietnam's formation types — and form their plurals the
   book's way.
3. Read a new compound by its parts: a blackboard is a
   board that is black; a waterfall is water that falls.
4. Sort spellings: joined (football), hyphenated (sea-side,
   as Barbados prints it), and know both are one word with
   one meaning.

## What you need
Word cards — small words on separate cards for joining.
Paper and a pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner writes FOOT and BALL on
   two cards, slides them together, and reads the new word
   that walks in. The lesson then gives the rule
   (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the joining rule and the
   alone-test; Sudan's time compounds; Barbados's printed
   set; Vietnam's formation types and plural rules
   (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the rule part: a picture of two word blocks,
     FOOT and BALL, sliding together and locking into
     FOOTBALL, 1–2 seconds — the student splits it back
     aloud: foot, ball.
   - ADD IMAGE under the sets: a picture of a real cupboard
     with its doors open and cups inside on a board — the
     student says the two words hiding inside cupboard.
   - ADD AUDIO in the splitting part: a voice reading
     compounds slowly with a clap between the parts —
     hour | glass, sun | dial, black | board — the student
     splits each one before the clap.
3. **Say it to your companion** — the joining game: one
   holds first-word cards, the other second-word cards;
   make true compounds and defend them (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the
     joining workshop in the super-simple voice (made
     later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — split six compounds, build four new
   ones, and write each part's meaning (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Calls un- a compound part → the alone-test, out loud: can
  it stand alone as a word? Foot can. Un- cannot. un- goes
  back to Word building's toolbox.
- Reads cupboard as "cup-board" with a hard p-b → the parts
  tell the HISTORY, not always the sound: the word is said
  as one, "cubberd." Split for meaning, say it whole.
- Builds word-salads (table + sun = tablesun) → Vietnam's
  rule in plain form: the second word is the THING, the
  first word tells what kind: a sundial is a kind of dial.
  If the pair names no real thing, it is not a compound.
- This lesson's next door: Word relationships (same level)
  — when two whole words meet as partners instead of
  joining into one.
