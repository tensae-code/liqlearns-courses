# School and Classroom Things

## Do it first

Empty your school bag on the table. Look at the things in front of
you — you use them every day. Can you name every one in English?
Try. Say the names out loud, even the guesses. Trying comes first
here; then we learn every name together — the things in your bag,
the things in your classroom, and the rooms of your whole school.

## Sub lesson 1 — School things you carry

Here are the eight things you carry to school.

A **ruler** — the flat stick you draw straight lines with, and
measure with.
A **pencil** — you write and draw with it, and you can rub it out.
An **eraser** — it rubs out pencil lines. (Say **an** eraser — the
word starts with a vowel sound.)
A **crayon** — a coloured stick for drawing and colouring.
A **bag** — everything travels in it.
A **pen** — you write with it in ink.
A **pencil box** — your pencils and pens live in it.
A **book** — you read it and learn from it.

Now hold each thing up and say what you have: "**I have a**
ruler." "**I have a** pencil." "**I have an** eraser." One starts
with **an** — eraser. All the others take **a**. Say all eight
sentences with the real things in your hand.

## Sub lesson 2 — In the classroom

Now look around the room you learn in. Its name is the
**classroom** — and here are its things.

The **window** lets the light in. The **blackboard** is the big
board the teacher writes on. The **light** shines when the day is
dark. A **picture** hangs for you to look at. The **door** is
where you come in. The **teacher's desk** is the teacher's table.
The **computer** is the machine that thinks and shows. The
**fan** moves the air when the room is hot. The **wall** stands
around you, and the **floor** is under your feet.

Walk around your classroom. Touch each thing and say its name:
"Window." "Blackboard." "Door." "Floor." Eleven names — the room
itself and the ten things in it.

## Sub lesson 3 — Rooms in the school

Last, the whole school. A school is not one room — it is many
rooms, each with its own name.

The **teachers' office** is where the teachers work. The
**library** is the room of books — you read there and borrow
books there. The **playground** is the open place outside where
you play. The **garden** is where plants grow. Downstairs is the
**first floor**; up the stairs is the **second floor**. The
**computer room** is full of computers. The **music room** is
where you sing and play music. The **art room** is where you draw
and paint. The **gym** is the big room for sport and exercise.

Walk your school in your head, from the gate to your classroom,
and name every room you pass. Then name the rooms again out loud
with a partner — ten names, no stopping.

## Say it to your companion

Explain to your companion, in your own words, the three groups of
this lesson: the things in your bag, the things in your classroom,
and the rooms of your school. Then test each other: your companion
points at a thing — in the bag, in the room, or in a picture of
the school — and you must name it before they count to three.

## Do It Yourself

1. Put five things from your bag in a row. Say a whole sentence
   for each one: "I have a…" / "I have an…"
2. Stand in your classroom door. Name all eleven classroom things
   you can see from where you stand.
3. Draw a quick map of your school. Write the name of every room
   on your map: library, gym, music room, art room, computer room,
   teachers' office — and mark the playground and the garden
   outside.
