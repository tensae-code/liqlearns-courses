# Lesson plan — School and Classroom Things

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** School and Classroom Things
**Lesson:** school-and-classroom-things — the words for the things of
school: the supplies you carry, the things in a classroom, and the
rooms of a school, with "I have a/an…" for saying what is yours
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Words for the World (with the
other word sets, beside school-subjects — the subjects are taught
there; the things are taught here)
**The one thing:** The English names of the school things — ruler,
pencil, eraser, crayon, bag, pen, pencil box, book; classroom,
window, blackboard, light, picture, door, teacher's desk, computer,
fan, wall, floor; teachers' office, library, playground, garden,
first floor, second floor, computer room, music room, art room, gym —
and the sentence that carries them: "I have a/an…"
**This lesson's own level:** Word-set lesson: know the three sets by
sight and by ear, and say what you have with "I have a/an…"

## Why this lesson exists

No lesson in the course taught the school's own things.
school-subjects teaches the subjects (maths, English, art);
the-town teaches the rooms of a town. China's primary books teach
the things themselves, in three sets across three books: the
supplies in Grade 3 Volume 1's opening unit "Hello!" (printed
pp.4–8), the classroom's things in Grade 4 Volume 1's "My
classroom" (printed pp.5 and 8), and the school's rooms in Grade 4
Volume 2's "My school" (printed pp.5 and 8). The 2026-11-08
re-verification sweep re-read the books page by page, rebuilt the
placements from the PDFs themselves (462 teaching items; 224
already in the course, 237 missing) and marked these five parts
NEW LESSON NEEDED. Under the everything-goes-in ruling
(2026-11-06), a teaching item with no lesson gets a new lesson
built; this is that lesson. Its only sources are the China books
named in lesson/sources.md.

## Sub lessons

1. School things you carry
2. In the classroom
3. Rooms in the school

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is needed before this lesson. It sits beside
school-subjects and the home and its rooms; a learner who has met
Hello and your name will find the "I have a/an…" frame easy, but no
lesson is forced first — this lesson stands alone.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- Name the eight school things you carry, and say "I have a…" /
  "I have an…" for each one.
- Name the eleven things of a classroom and point to each one in a
  real classroom.
- Name the ten rooms and places of a school and say where each one
  is — the library, the gym, the playground, the second floor.

## What you need
Your own school bag with its real things in it, a real classroom to
look around, and a walk around the school if you can. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson
1. **Do it first** — empty your bag on the table and try to name
   every thing in English before anyone tells you (lesson/lesson.md).
2. ADD IMAGE: the eight school things in a row, each one big and
   clear, no words under them. The learner points and guesses the
   names first — trying comes before telling.
3. Work through sub lesson 1: name each thing, hold it up, and say
   "I have a…" / "I have an…" with it in your hand.
4. ADD IMAGE: a classroom picture with its eleven things — window,
   blackboard, light, picture, door, teacher's desk, computer, fan,
   wall, floor, and the classroom itself. Work through sub lesson 2
   the same way: point, name, say where it is.
5. ADD VIDEO (short): a walk through a school — teachers' office,
   library, playground, garden, up the stairs to the second floor,
   computer room, music room, art room, gym — each room named as the
   camera arrives. Then the learner names the rooms of their own
   school for sub lesson 3.

## Add-ons
- Bag game: one learner closes their eyes; the other takes one
  thing out of the bag. "What is missing?" — the first learner
  names it: "I have a ruler. Where is my ruler?"
- Classroom hunt: the teacher names a thing — "blackboard!" — and
  the learner runs to touch it and names it back.
- School walk: walk the real school and name every room you pass.

## If the learner is stuck
Cut back to the bag: the eight carried things are the ones the
learner can hold, and holding teaches fastest. Name two at a time
(pencil and pen first), say "I have a…" with each in the hand, and
only move to the classroom's things when the bag is sure. The rooms
come last — they are the biggest things and the easiest to point
at on a real walk.

## Teacher note
China's books teach the three sets in this order — the things you
carry (Grade 3), the things in the classroom (Grade 4 Volume 1),
the rooms of the school (Grade 4 Volume 2) — and this lesson keeps
that order: from your hand, to your room, to your school. The
books' own unit names for the parts ("Hello!", "My classroom",
"My school") are kept as searchable tags in lesson/sources.md.
