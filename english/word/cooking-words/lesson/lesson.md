# Cooking Words

## Do it first

Your favourite food — how is it made? Is it grilled? Baked? Fried in a pan? And what do the cook's hands do — slice, stir, pour? Bolivia's book for future cooks and hotel workers teaches the whole kitchen in English. Learn the words, and a recipe becomes a story you can follow.

## Sub lesson 1 — Cooking methods

A cooking **method** is HOW the food meets the heat. The book's set, grouped:

**Dry heat:** **grill** (over fire on a rack), **broil** (heat from above), **barbecue** (slow smoke over fire), **bake** (in the oven, for bread and cakes), **roast** (in the oven, for meat and vegetables), **toast** (brown bread by heat), **gratin** (a golden crust on top).

**Water:** **steam** (cook in the vapour of boiling water), **poach** (cook gently in hot water), **simmer** (cook just below boiling), **stew** (cook long and slow in liquid).

**Pan:** **stir-fry** (fast in a hot pan, moving all the time), **sauté** (jump in a little hot oil), **scramble** (eggs stirred as they set), **caramelize** (cook sugar — or food — to gold), **glaze** (paint with a shiny coat), **microwave** (cook in the microwave).

Which methods does your family's cooking use most?

## Sub lesson 2 — Cooking actions

A cooking **action** is what the cook's hands do before and during the method:

**add** (put in), **slice** (cut in flat pieces), **dice** (cut in little cubes), **chop** (cut roughly), **mince** (cut very, very small), **peel** (take the skin off), **drain** (pour the water away), **pour** (let liquid flow), **mix** (put together), **combine** (mix things into one), **beat** (hit fast with a fork or whisk), **whisk** (beat with a whisk), **stir** (move round with a spoon), **break** (open an egg; split), **grease** (rub oil on the pan), **carve** (cut meat to serve), **knead** (work dough with your hands), **measure** (find the amount), **dissolve** (melt into liquid), **crush** (break into powder or paste), **weigh** (find how heavy).

Careful: the book prints “Weight” for the verb — the verb is **weigh** (weight is the noun, how heavy a thing is).

Mime them: slice, stir, whisk, knead. Your hands already know these words.

## Sub lesson 3 — In the kitchen — methods and actions together

Now a recipe in the book's own words, end to end:

“**Peel** the potatoes and **slice** them. **Steam** them for twenty minutes. **Add** salt. **Pour** a little oil over them and **serve**.”

Methods say how it cooks; actions say what you do. Every recipe in English is these two sets dancing together.

The teacher's book adds picture grids of foods, drinks and cooking verbs — the same words with faces. Learn to read a recipe aloud, and you can cook from any English cookbook in the world.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion how to make one simple dish from your home, using at least three action words and one method word: “First peel… then slice… then steam…” They must repeat the steps back in order.

## Do It Yourself

Write the recipe for a food you can really make, in English, with at least five cooking words from this lesson. Cook it this week if you can — and say each word aloud as your hands do it.
