# Lesson plan — Cooking Words

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** Cooking Words
**Lesson:** cooking-words — the words for ways of cooking and the actions of the kitchen: grill, bake, slice, stir
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Words for the World (with the other word sets)
**The one thing:** The cooking-method words (how food is cooked) and the cooking-action words (what the cook's hands do).
**This lesson's own level:** Word-set lesson: seventeen cooking methods and twenty-one cooking actions, from Bolivia's culinary (BTH) spreads and its gastronomy grids.

## Why this lesson exists

Bolivia teaches cooking words in two places and neither had a lesson: the Grade 11 book's Culinary Terms spread for the technology branch (BTH), and the compendium's Year 5 gastronomy picture grids. This lesson is built from those placements under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06).

## Sub lessons

1. Cooking methods
2. Cooking actions
3. In the kitchen — methods and actions together

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is needed before this lesson. Food and Fruit names what is cooked; Process Writing uses recipe steps; this lesson stands alone as the kitchen word set.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- Name the cooking methods: grill, broil, barbecue, gratin, stir-fry, bake, roast, stew, caramelize, steam, sauté, toast, poach, microwave, simmer, scramble, glaze.
- Name the cooking actions: add, slice, drain, dice, pour, mix, beat, break, grease, carve, combine, knead, chop, measure, stir, mince, peel, dissolve, crush, whisk, weigh.
- Follow and give simple kitchen instructions using both sets.

## What you need
A real kitchen if one is near; pictures of dishes if not. Real smells teach fastest.

## The run of the lesson
1. ADD IMAGE: three dishes — grilled meat, a baked cake, a steaming soup. The learner guesses how each was cooked before the method words appear.
2. Method round (sub lesson 1): one dish picture per method; heat is the story — dry heat (grill, bake, roast, toast), water (steam, poach, simmer, stew), pan (stir-fry, sauté, scramble).
3. ADD VIDEO (short): hands at work — slicing, peeling, stirring, pouring. Sub lesson 2 mimes each action as it is named.
4. Sub lesson 3 puts them together: a simple recipe told in full — “Peel the potatoes. Slice them. Steam them. Add salt.”

## Add-ons
- Action charades: mime a cooking action (whisk! knead!) — partners name it in English.
- Method match: “Bread in the oven?” “Bake!” “Eggs in hot water?” “Poach!”

## If the learner is stuck
Methods by heat-group (dry, water, pan) and actions in the order of a real recipe; never all thirty-eight at once.

## Teacher note
The Grade 11 book lists “Weight” as a cooking verb where it means weigh (printed p. 36) — this lesson teaches weigh, and the slip is recorded in lesson/sources.md.
