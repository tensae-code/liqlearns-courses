# Leaving School and Illiteracy

## Do it first

Not every child finishes school. Tunisia's Perform Year 1 book ends with a text and a class poll about leaving school early and illiteracy. This lesson teaches those words — why pupils leave, what illiterate people face, and how learning can start again.

## Sub lesson 1 — Why people leave school

- **leave school early** — to stop going to school before finishing.
- **truant** — a pupil who stays away from school without permission.
- **course** — a set of lessons you join to learn something (an illiterate adult can join a reading course).

The book's poll asks pupils for the reasons: work and money, family needs, distance, finding school hard. Learn to name the reasons in English — a reason you can name is a reason people can work on.

*Difficulty: Three school words, and the reasons a whole class poll gives.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Illiteracy and learning again

- **illiterate** — not able to read or write.
- **illiteracy** — the state of not being able to read or write.

The book's text names the difficulties illiterate people face: forms they cannot fill, signs and labels they cannot read, jobs they cannot take. And its hope: courses exist where adults learn to read and write — illiteracy is a problem with a door out of it.

*Difficulty: Two hard words, and the honest difficulties — plus the way back.*

## Say it to your companion

Give one reason pupils leave school early. Your companion answers with one difficulty illiterate people face — or one way learning can start again.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the five words with a short meaning for each.
2. Write two reasons pupils leave school early, in English.
3. Write one sentence about how an illiterate adult can learn again.
