# Spelling Patterns

## Do it first

Write the word "necessary". Now "separate". If you hesitated, you met the truth of spelling: English spelling is not chaos — it is a system of patterns, and the patterns can be learned family by family. This lesson teaches the system.

## Sub lesson 1 — Spelling foundations

*Difficulty: first step — the entry into this lesson.*

Spelling starts with a **technique**: look at the word, say it, cover it, write it, check it. Words learned this way stay. And every word divides into **syllables** — beats you can clap: *but-ter-fly*, three beats, three chunks to spell.

The foundation patterns are sound patterns. Spell by ear first: the **initial, middle and final consonant sounds** and their letters; **short vowel sounds** (a in *cat*) against **long vowel sounds** (a in *cake*) and the **letter combinations** that spell the long sounds; **blend sounds** at the start and end of words; **digraph sounds** — two letters, one sound, like *sh* and *ch*. The words you meet every day — the **sight words** and high-frequency words — are learned whole, by heart.

Try it now: Take five new words. For each: look, say, cover, write, check. Then clap the syllables of each word and write the word chunk by chunk.

## Sub lesson 2 — Word parts and spelling patterns

*Difficulty: builds on Sub lesson 1 — the same skill, taken further.*

Words are built from parts, and the parts spell steadily: a **root word** (*help*), a **prefix** before it (*un-help*), a **suffix** after it (*help-ful*). Learn the parts and long words stop being long. **Inflectional endings** — *-s, -es, -ed, -ing* — sometimes just attach (*walk-ed*) and sometimes change the word first: double the consonant (*run → running*), drop the silent e (*make → making*), change y to i (*cry → cried*).

The middle patterns: **double consonants** (*letter, happy*); **r-controlled vowels**, where r bends the vowel (*ar, er, ir, or, ur*); **diphthongs** — gliding vowel sounds (*oi, oy, ou, ow*); **silent consonants** (*knight, lamb*); **silent e** making the vowel long (*hat → hate*); **compound words** spelled as their two words joined; **contractions** with the apostrophe standing where letters left; and **homophones** — same sound, different spelling, different meaning (*there/their/they're*) — the set that catches every speller. Add **alphabetising**: putting a list in letter order, first letter first, then the second.

Try it now: Write the pattern family of each word: running, making, cried, knight, hate, joyful, their. Name the pattern aloud before you write each one.

## Sub lesson 3 — Spelling longer words

*Difficulty: builds on Sub lesson 2 — the same skill, taken further.*

Long words are only short words in a row. **Break a polysyllabic word into manageable units** — its syllables and its parts — and spell unit by unit: *un-for-tu-nate-ly*. The **sound-symbol relationships** and phonological patterns you know still work inside long words; where they stop working, the word's **family, root and origin** explain the spelling — words from the same root keep the root's letters (*sign, signal*).

**Visual patterns** help too: some words are spelled by how they look, learned by eye as shapes. And the speller's last tool is **proofreading with dictionaries**: write, then check the doubtful words. Discrimination in spelling complexities — telling the pattern that fits from the pattern that almost fits — is the mark of the finished speller.

Try it now: Take three long words (ten letters or more). Break each into units, write it unit by unit, then check all three in a dictionary. Which units did the dictionary correct?

## Say it to your companion

Read this lesson aloud to your companion — a toy, a pet, a person. Then explain, in your own words: the pattern system of spelling — sounds to letters, word parts, endings and their changes, and breaking long words into units.

## Do It Yourself

1. Learn five words by the technique: look, say, cover, write, check.
2. Sort twelve words into pattern families: silent e, double consonant, r-controlled, diphthong, silent letter.
3. Take three long words and write each one broken into units before writing it whole.
4. Proofread one page of your own writing with a dictionary. List every word it corrected and its pattern.
