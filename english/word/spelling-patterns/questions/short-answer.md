# Short Answer — Spelling Patterns

(Every item is marked with the sub lesson it belongs to: (Sub lesson N). The gate pool for a sub lesson is all of that sub lesson's questions — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the whole set is the pool.)

1. How do you divide "butterfly" for spelling, and why does it help? (Sub lesson 1)
   — But-ter-fly — three syllables; spelling chunk by chunk makes a long word manageable.
2. Spell "cried" from "cry" and name the change. (Sub lesson 2)
   — Cry → cried: when adding -ed, the y changes to i.
3. What three changes can an inflectional ending force on a word? (Sub lesson 2)
   — Doubling the final consonant (run → running), dropping the silent e (make → making), and changing y to i (cry → cried).
4. How do you attack the spelling of "unfortunately"? (Sub lesson 3)
   — Break it into manageable units — un-for-tu-nate-ly (root fortune/fortunate with prefix un- and suffix -ly) — and spell unit by unit, then check.
5. Why do sign and signal share their spelling of the root? (Sub lesson 3)
   — They come from the same root; words in one family keep the root's letters even when the sound shifts.
