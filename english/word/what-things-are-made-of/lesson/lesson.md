# What Things Are Made Of

## Do it first

Put five things on the table in front of you: a book, a cup, a
spoon, a chair, a key. Look at each one. Touch it. Can you say
what it is **made of**? Try — say the material out loud for every
thing, even if you are only guessing. Trying comes first here.
Then we learn the material words together, and the sentence that
says what a thing is made of.

## Sub lesson 1 — Materials and their adjective forms

Everything around you is made of something. The somethings are
the **materials**. Here are the big building materials.

**Wood** — it comes from trees. Houses, tables and chairs are
made of it.
**Brick** — bricks are baked clay blocks. A wall takes many
bricks, one on top of another.
**Concrete** — a grey, hard material that starts soft and wet
and dries like stone. Big bridges and tall buildings are made
of it.
**Granite** — a very hard stone, often shiny. Big buildings and
monuments are made of it.

Now the clever part. A material word can stand in front of a
thing's name, like a describing word:

- a **wooden** house — wood becomes **wooden** in front of a name
- a **brick** house
- a **concrete** bridge
- a **granite** building

Only wood changes its shape: wood → **wooden**. The others keep
their own names: a brick house, a concrete bridge, a stone wall,
a glass window, a metal door.

Say the pairs out loud, with the real things or pictures in
front of you: "wood — a wooden chair." "brick — a brick wall."
"concrete — a concrete bridge." "granite — a granite building."

*Difficulty: first steps — name the material, then say it in front of the thing's name.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Saying what a thing is made of

Now the whole sentence. The frame is **be made of**:

- A wooden house **is made of wood**.
- A brick wall **is made of bricks**.
- A concrete bridge **is made of concrete**.
- A window pane **is made of glass**.
- A metal key **is made of metal**.

Watch how the two ways fit together: "a **wooden** house" says
it short, with the adjective. "The house **is made of wood**"
says it in a full sentence, with the material's own name. Notice
— in the full sentence the material goes back to its plain name:
wood, not wooden.

Ask about anything: "**What is it made of?**" — "It is made of
glass." "What is the bridge made of?" — "It is made of
concrete."

Walk around your room, then your street. Name ten things and
say what each one is made of: "The door is made of wood." "The
roof is made of metal." "The steps are made of stone."

*Difficulty: building — the frame is new; keep the thing in your hand or in your sight while you say it.*

## Say it to your companion

Explain to your companion, in your own words, the two ways to
name a thing's material: the short way (a wooden house) and the
sentence way (the house is made of wood). Then test each other:
your companion points at a thing — anything in the room — and
you must say what it is made of before they count to three.

## Do It Yourself

1. Find one thing made of wood, one made of glass, one made of
   metal and one made of stone in your home. Say a full sentence
   for each: "This … is made of …"
2. Look at your own house or school from outside. Say what the
   walls are made of, what the roof is made of, and what the
   windows are made of.
3. Draw a house and label its parts with their materials: "The
   walls are made of brick. The roof is made of wood. The window
   panes are made of glass."
