# Lesson plan — What Things Are Made Of

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** What Things Are Made Of
**Lesson:** what-things-are-made-of — the material words (wood,
brick, concrete, granite and their kin) and the sentence that says
what a thing is made of: "be made of"
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Words for the World (with the
other word sets, beside the-home — the home's parts are taught
there; what they are made of is taught here)
**The one thing:** naming a thing's material, two ways: with the
material adjective (a **wooden** house) and with the sentence frame
"**be made of**" (a wooden house is **made of wood**).
**This lesson's own level:** Word-set and frame lesson: know the
material words and their adjective forms, and say what common
things are made of.

## Why this lesson exists

No lesson in the course taught materials and "be made of" as its
own thing. the-passive carries "made of / made from" rows where
books teach them inside the passive, but no lesson housed the
material words themselves with the "be made of" frame.
Mongolia's English XI teaches exactly that as a named part of its
buildings unit: material adjectives and "be made of" — "a wooden
house — made of wood" (Unit 4, printed p.58), on the back of the
unit's building materials (bricks, concrete, granite, printed
p.57). The 2026-11-08 re-verification sweep re-read Mongolia's
books page by page, rebuilt the placements from the books
themselves (690 teaching items; 425 already in the course, 265
missing — all of Grades 11 and 12) and marked this part NEW
LESSON NEEDED. Under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06),
a teaching item with no lesson gets a new lesson built; this is
that lesson. Its only source is the Mongolia book named in
lesson/sources.md.

## Sub lessons

1. Materials and their adjective forms
2. Saying what a thing is made of — be made of

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is needed before this lesson. It sits beside the-home
and the-passive; a learner who has met the home's parts will find
the materials easy, but no lesson is forced first — this lesson
stands alone.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- Name the building materials — wood, brick, concrete, granite,
  stone, glass, metal — and use the material adjectives (wooden,
  brick, concrete, granite) in front of a thing's name.
- Say what a thing is made of: "The house is made of wood."
  "The bridge is made of concrete."
- Ask and answer: "What is it made of? — It is made of glass."

## What you need
Real things to touch: a piece of wood, a brick if you can find
one, something glass, something metal, something stone. Nothing
else.

## The run of the lesson
1. **Do it first** — put five things on the table and try to say
   what each one is made of before anyone tells you
   (lesson/lesson.md).
2. ADD IMAGE: a wooden house, a brick house, a concrete bridge
   and a granite building, side by side, each one big and clear.
   The learner points and guesses the materials first — trying
   comes before telling.
3. Work through sub lesson 1: the material words and the
   adjective forms, with the real things in hand.
4. Work through sub lesson 2: the "be made of" frame — walk
   around the room and the street and say what everything is
   made of.
5. ADD VIDEO (short): a builder's yard — wood, bricks, concrete
   and stone arriving, and a house rising wall by wall, each
   material named as it is used.

## Add-ons
- Materials hunt: name ten things in the room and say what each
  is made of. The winner is the one who finishes first with no
  mistakes.
- True or false game: "A window pane is made of wood." — false!
  Say the true sentence: "A window pane is made of glass."
- Describe and guess: describe a thing by its material only —
  "It is big. It is made of concrete and glass." Your partner
  guesses the thing.

## If the learner is stuck
Cut back to two materials — wood and glass — and two things the
learner can hold. Name the material, touch it, say the sentence
with the thing in the hand: "This cup is made of glass." Only
move to the full set when those two are sure.

## Teacher note
Mongolia's book teaches the material adjectives and "be made of"
inside its buildings unit, straight after the building materials
themselves — and this lesson keeps that order: the materials
first (the unit's own set on printed p.57, stocked in full in
the-home), then the frame (printed p.58). The book's example —
"a wooden house — made of wood" — is the lesson's model.
