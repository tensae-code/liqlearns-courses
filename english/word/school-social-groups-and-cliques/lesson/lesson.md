# School Social Groups and Cliques

## Do it first

Think of your school. Who sits with whom at lunch — and what does everyone call each group? This lesson teaches the words of the school's social map.

## Sub lesson 1 — School groups and their names

Every school has its social map. Grade 10 names the groups: **jocks** (the sporty ones), **geeks** and **nerds** (the ones deep in study and their interests), **stoners** — labels learners stick on each other, fair and unfair.

The set is taught beside its weighing words: **values, attitudes, background** — the things the labels hide.

*Difficulty: Names everyone at school knows and nobody teaches — here, taught.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Cliques, peers and reputation

A **clique** is a tight circle of friends that keeps others out. Your **peers** are the people of your own age around you. Your **reputation** is what the school thinks you are — built from the map, and not always fair.

The book sets **stereotypes** beside the map for a reason: a label about a whole group is a shortcut that often lies.

*Difficulty: The map's machinery — circle, peers, reputation — and the warning label on all of it.*

## Say it to your companion

Your companion names a group — geeks! You say what the name points at. Then swap, and finish by naming what the label hides.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the group names and what each points at.
2. Write what a clique is, and how it differs from a group of friends.
3. Write one label used at your school and one thing it hides.
