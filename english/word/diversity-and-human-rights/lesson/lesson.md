# Diversity and Human Rights

## Do it first

Look at your own street or school. How many different origins, languages, foods and beliefs can you count? That is diversity. And what does every one of those people need, without exception? This lesson names both.

## Sub lesson 1 — Many cultures, one country

One country holds many peoples. **Cultural diversity** means the many ways of life inside one place: different **origins** — where families come from; different **religions** and **beliefs** — what people hold true; different foods, music and festivals.

Diversity is not a problem to hide. In this lesson it is a country's richness, named with respect.

*Difficulty: Diversity named through origins, religions and beliefs.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Rights and basic needs

Every person has **human rights** — things no one may take away. The Grade 11 lesson names them through the **basic needs**: **food** to eat, **health** — a body cared for, a clinic when you are sick — and **shelter** — a safe place to live.

Food, health, shelter: when these are missing, a right is missing. The words of this sub lesson let a learner say so.

*Difficulty: Rights named through the three basic needs.*

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion one difference you value in your community, and one basic need you would protect first.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write three differences in your community — origins, religions, foods or beliefs.
2. Write the three basic needs and one sentence for each: why it is a right.
3. Ask someone at home what diversity means to them. Write their answer in English.
