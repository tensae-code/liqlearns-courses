# Daily Routines

## Do it first

Mime it: close your eyes and pretend to sleep. Now open them wide and stretch your arms up. You just did the two ends of the day — going to bed, and waking up. This lesson gives you the English phrases for both ends, and for saying when you do them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Waking up

*Difficulty: first steps. You only need everyday words.*

When sleep ends and your eyes open, you **wake up**.

Say it: "I wake up."

You can wake up early or late. **Early** means before the usual time. A pupil who has far to walk wakes up early, while it is still dark outside.

Say it: "I wake up early."

Ask a friend: "Do you wake up early?"

## Sub lesson 2 — Going to bed

*Difficulty: a step up. You join the two ends of the day together now.*

When the day ends and you go to sleep, you **go to bed**.

Say it: "I go to bed."

You can go to bed **early** — before the usual time. Or you can go to bed **at midnight**. Midnight is the middle of the night: 12 o'clock at night. Going to bed at midnight is very late.

Say both: "I go to bed early." "I go to bed at midnight."

And here is how the two ends join up: if you go to bed early, you can wake up early. If you go to bed at midnight, waking up early is hard. Your bedtime decides your morning.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion your two phrases: when you wake up and when you go to bed — "I wake up early. I go to bed early." Then listen to theirs. Ask each other: "Do you go to bed at midnight?"

## Do It Yourself

1. Say your day in two sentences: "I wake up …" and "I go to bed …" — finish each one with **early** or **at midnight**, whichever is true for you.
2. Tonight, try going to bed early. Tomorrow morning, notice: was waking up easier?
3. Ask three people at home when they wake up and when they go to bed. Answer for each one: "She wakes up early." "He goes to bed at midnight."
