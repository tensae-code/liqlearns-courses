# Staying Safe Online

## Do it first

You are playing a game online. A friendly message pops up: "You won! Tell me your address and I will send your prize." Do you type it? This lesson teaches the five things a website or an online friend may ask for — and what to do instead, so you stay safe.

## Sub lesson 1 — What they ask for

The questions can sound friendly. Learn to spot them. A website or an online friend may ask for:

- **Your address** — where your home is.
- **Your name** — your real, full name.
- **Your school** — where you go to school.
- **A photo** — a picture of you.
- **A meeting** — "Come and meet me" somewhere, without your family knowing.

These five things are yours. They are not for strangers on a screen — not even friendly ones, and not even when a game or a website asks nicely.

*Difficulty: Five friendly-looking questions, spotted on sight.*

## Sub lesson 2 — What to do instead

When any of the five asks comes, the safe answer is the same:

- **Do not give it.** Do not type your address, your name, or your school. Do not send a photo.
- **Do not go.** Never meet an online friend alone.
- **Tell a grown-up.** Show the message to a parent, a teacher, or another grown-up you trust. Telling is not getting in trouble — telling is how you stay safe.

A real friend does not need your address to play with you. A real prize does not need your school's name. When the question is one of the five, close it and tell.

*Difficulty: One safe answer that works for all five asks.*

## Say it to your companion

Your companion plays a website: they ask for one of the five things, in a friendly voice. You answer with the safe answer — "I don't tell that. I will tell a grown-up." Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the five asks on a card: address, name, school, photo, meeting.
2. Under each one, write the safe answer in your own words.
3. Show the card to a grown-up and tell them what you will do if a website asks you.
