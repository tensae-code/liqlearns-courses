# Languages and Peoples

## Do it first

Say the languages you hear in your own street, your own family, your own town. How many can you name? This lesson names the languages of the world — and the peoples who speak them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Languages of the world

Every people has a language. **Spanish** is the language of Venezuela and of many countries. **English** is spoken across the world. **Portuguese** is Brazil's language, **French** is France's — and each language names a people too: the Spanish, the English, the Portuguese, the French.

Say them as pairs: the country, the people, the language. A language is not only words — it is a people's voice.

*Difficulty: Language names tied to the peoples who speak them.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Peoples of Venezuela

Venezuela's first peoples were there before Spanish came. The book names the country's **indigenous peoples** — peoples like the Wayuu, the Warao, the Pemón and the Yanomami — each with its own language, its own land and its own stories.

To name a people and its language is to say: they are here, they speak, they count. This lesson carries those names in English, the way the book teaches them.

*Difficulty: Names that carry respect — peoples and their own languages.*

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: your language, your people, and one other language you would like to learn.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write three languages and the people who speak each one.
2. Write the names of two indigenous peoples of Venezuela (or of your own country) and one word you know about each.
3. Ask someone at home which languages they can speak or understand. Write the list in English.
