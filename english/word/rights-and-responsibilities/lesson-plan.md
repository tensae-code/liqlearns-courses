# Lesson Plan — Rights and Responsibilities

**Unit 7 — Words for the World.**

**One thing:** say what every person may have and may do — human-rights statements built with *have the right to*: "Children have the right to education." "Everyone has the right to be safe."

## Sub lessons

1. **Have the right to + verb.** *Can-do: Say what people may do, with the pattern have the right to + verb: children have the right to learn and to play, every person has the right to speak, to be heard and to live in safety.*
2. **Have the right to + noun.** *Can-do: Name the rights as things every person holds — the right to education, the right to health, the right to a name, the right to protection, the right to food and clean water — and say that rights come with responsibilities: mine end where yours begin.*

(The gate pool for each sub lesson is every question marked with that sub lesson's number in this lesson's `questions/` files — the whole set, because no hardness ranking is written in questions/.)

## Teaching points

- Human rights statements with *have the right to* + verb / noun (item 217), from Book 3's Module 2 Lesson 2 (printed pp.52–53).
- The same module reads three accounts of rights taken away — a kidnapped journalist, a child worker in Somalia, a homeless man (item 218, stocked in answering-from-the-text) — and writes a Bill of Rights for teenagers (item 219, stocked in article-writing); the responsibilities side is stocked from item 255 in working-words.
