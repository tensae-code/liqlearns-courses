# Rights and Responsibilities

## Do it first

Can you say, in English, what every child in the world may have — school, safety, a name? There is one strong pattern that says all of it: **have the right to**. After this lesson you will use it both ways: with a doing-word, and with the name of the right itself.

## Sub lesson 1 — Have the right to + verb

*Very simple. Can-do: Say what people may do, with the pattern have the right to + verb: children have the right to learn and to play, every person has the right to speak, to be heard and to live in safety.*

A right is something every person may have or do — nobody may take it away. English says it with one pattern:

**have the right to + verb**

- Children **have the right to learn**.
- Every child **has the right to play**.
- Everyone **has the right to speak** — and **to be heard**.
- Every person **has the right to live** in safety.

Watch the verb: after *to*, the verb stays plain — *to learn, to speak, to live* — it never takes -s or -ed. One person: *has* the right. Many people: *have* the right.

Try it now: say three rights with this pattern — one for a child, one for a worker, one for an old person.

## Sub lesson 2 — Have the right to + noun

*Simple. Can-do: Name the rights as things every person holds — the right to education, the right to health, the right to a name, the right to protection, the right to food and clean water — and say that rights come with responsibilities: mine end where yours begin.*

The same pattern names the right itself, as a thing a person holds:

**have the right to + noun**

- the right to **education**
- the right to **health**
- the right to **a name**
- the right to **protection**
- the right to **food and clean water**

And here is the other half of the lesson's name: **responsibilities**. Rights do not walk alone. My rights end where yours begin — I may speak, and I must listen; I must be protected, and I must not harm. A right in your hands is a duty in mine: families, schools and countries must protect the rights of the people in their care.

Your turn: take one right from the list and say it both ways — "Girls and boys have the right to education" (noun), "Girls and boys have the right to learn" (verb).

## Say it to your companion

Read this lesson aloud to your companion. Then each of you says two rights — one with a verb, one with a noun — and one responsibility that walks with them.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the pattern have the right to, and one sentence with a verb and one with a noun.
2. Write five rights every child holds, using the noun way.
3. For one right, write who must protect it.
4. Say the sentence aloud: "My rights end where yours begin." Explain it in your own words to someone at home.
