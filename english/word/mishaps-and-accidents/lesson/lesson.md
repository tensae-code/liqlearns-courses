# Mishaps and Accidents

## Do it first

Some days everything goes wrong. You burn the food. You spill your drink. You trip over the dog. In English, each of those little disasters has its own words — and telling the story of a bad day is one of the most fun talks there is. Learn the mishap words, then tell yours.

## Sub lesson 1 — The mishap words

*Very simple. Can-do: Name the mishaps: trip over, pass out, get a flat tire, run into someone, spill a drink, fall down in public, burn food, have your possessions stolen.*

Eight mishaps, each with its words:

**trip over** — catch your foot on something and nearly fall (Victor trips over his sleeping dog). **pass out** — faint; everything goes dark for a moment. **get a flat tire** — the tire loses its air; the car or bike can go no further. **run into someone** — meet someone by surprise, bump into them in the street. **spill a drink** — knock it over; the drink goes everywhere. **fall down in public** — fall where everyone can see. **burn food** — cook it too long until it is black. And the worst: **have your possessions stolen** — someone takes your things (Victor's cards and money are stolen while he waits for a taxi).

Try it now: act out three mishaps without words; let your partner name each one.

## Sub lesson 2 — A bad-luck story

*Simple. Can-do: Chain the mishaps into a story that keeps going wrong — Victor's day: burnt food, spilt coffee, a tripping dog, a flat tire, stolen cards and money — until a friend helps.*

Now chain them, the way the book's story does. Victor's day: he **burns the food**, then **spills his coffee**, then **trips over his sleeping dog**. On the way to the supermarket he **gets a flat tire** — and while he waits for a taxi, his cards and money **are stolen**. What saves the day? He **runs into his friend Omar**, who helps him.

The chain words hold the story together: *first… then… next… after that… finally*. A bad-luck story is a past-tense story — every mishap finished and gone: burned, spilt, tripped, got, was stolen.

Try it now: tell a three-mishap chain of your own (real or invented) with first, then, and finally.

## Say it to your companion

Read this lesson aloud to your companion. Then tell Victor's whole bad day from memory, mishap by mishap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write five mishap sentences about an invented bad day.
2. Draw Victor's day as four pictures and label each mishap.
3. Tell a partner about a real small mishap in your life — in English.
4. List what Omar did right: how do we help a friend on a bad-luck day?
