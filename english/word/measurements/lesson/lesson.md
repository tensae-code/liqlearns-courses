# Measurements

## Sub lesson 1 — The units — metre, litre, kilo and their family

Length and distance: **metre (m)**, **centimetre (cm)** — 100 cm in a metre; **kilometre (km)** — 1,000 metres.

Weight: **kilo / kilogram (kg)**, **gram (g)** — 1,000 g in a kilo.

Liquids: **litre (l)**, **millilitre (ml)** — 1,000 ml in a litre.

- A door is about two **metres** high. A pen is about fifteen **centimetres** long.
- A bag of potatoes can weigh five **kilos**. A bottle holds one **litre** of water.

Do it: point at three things near you and name the right unit for each — cm, m, kg or l.

## Sub lesson 2 — Measuring in sentences

Asking: **How long is it? How tall is he? How heavy is the bag? How far is the town?**

Answering: *It is two metres long. He is one metre seventy tall. The bag weighs five kilos. The town is three kilometres away.*

- Length answers end with **long** (things) or **tall/high** (people and buildings).
- Weight answers can use **weighs**: *It weighs two kilos.*

Do it: ask and answer about your book (length in cm), your bag (weight in kg), and the road to your home (distance in km).

## Say it to your companion

Read the key sentences aloud and explain them to your companion in your own words — what the thing is, and one example of your own.

## Do It Yourself

Close the lesson and use the thing once in real life today — say it, write it, or notice it — then say how it went in one sentence.
