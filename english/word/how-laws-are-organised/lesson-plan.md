# Lesson plan — How Laws Are Organised

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** How Laws Are Organised
**Lesson:** how-laws-are-organised — Kelsen's pyramid — how a country's rules stack from the Constitution down to everyday administrative acts
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Words for the World

**The one thing:** put a country's rules in their true order — Constitution, international treaties, national law, decrees, administrative acts — and know that a lower rule can never beat a higher one.

**This lesson's own level:** built from two sub lessons that open in order.

## Why this lesson exists
Uruguay's Grade 9 English book teaches the hierarchy of laws through Kelsen's pyramid inside its Human Rights unit. The course held rights words and debate, but nothing that shows how laws themselves are stacked and which one wins — so this lesson is built, from Uruguay's book alone.

## Sub lessons
1. **Sub lesson 1 — The top of the pyramid.** Uruguay's Grade 9 book draws Kelsen's pyramid: the Constitution at the top, then international treaties, then national law.
2. **Sub lesson 2 — The lower steps — and why the order matters.** Below national law come decrees and administrative acts; a rule from a lower step can never beat a rule from a higher one.

Sub lesson 2 opens only when the prove-it check on sub lesson 1 is passed.
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is needed first. Citizens' rights and the constitution sits nearby — it names what stands at the pyramid's top, and can be tried first as a soft hint, never a gate.

## What the learner can do when it ends
Draw the five-step pyramid from memory, place any rule on its right step, and say which rule wins when two rules disagree.

## What you need
Nothing to bring: the learner's own country has the same kind of stack to compare.
