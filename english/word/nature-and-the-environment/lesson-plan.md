# Lesson plan — Nature and the environment

**Course:** English · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** Nature and the environment
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Words for the World —
the words for the natural world and for looking after it
(CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the nature words —
forest, mountain, lake, river, sea, hill, stream, rock, sun,
moon — where animals live, and the environment words for
what harms the earth and what saves it: pollution, climate
change, recycle, wind power.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Animals (Level 7 —
the animal names come back here, in their homes) and on The
town (places made by people; this lesson is the places no
one made). It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown changing — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The Nature Places** — the place set — forest, hill, mountain,
   lake, river, stream, rock, sea, grass — named and pointed out.
   (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Habitats** — where animals live, in whole sentences: "Bears
   can live in the forest". (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **Problems and Solutions** — the environment set sorted into
   problems and solutions, with one sentence on each side. (Was
   depth Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking
is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set
is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Animals (Level 7). Recommended, never
  forced — this lesson stands alone: every animal named here
  is named whole, with its home.
- The weather words (sunny, rainy…) are NOT this lesson;
  they get their own. This lesson names the places and
  things, not the day's sky.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the nature set the books teach: forest, grass, hill,
   mountain, lake, river, stream, rock, sea, sun, moon,
   sunrise, sunset (Albania's list).
2. Say where animals live — their habitats: "Bears can live
   in the forest and in the mountains." (Albania)
3. Sort the environment words the way Mongolia's book does:
   problems (climate change, air pollution, water shortage)
   against solutions (wind power, organic farming, recycle).
4. Use Mexico's big set — pollution, acid rain, drought,
   flood, litter, renewable energy — in simple sentences.

## What you need
A window, or a short walk outside. Paper and a pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner looks out of the window (or
   remembers a walk) and names everything natural they can
   see, in their own language. The lesson then gives the
   English names (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the nature places with Albania's
   sentences; habitats; problems and solutions sorted the
   Mongolian way; Mexico's environment set (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the start of the explanation: a picture of a
     landscape waking up — sun rising over a hill, a stream
     running into a lake, 1–2 seconds — the word sunrise
     written big as the sun lifts — the student says sunrise
     and sunset as the light comes and goes.
   - ADD IMAGE under habitats: a picture of a real forest
     with a bear in it — the student says where the bear
     lives, in a whole sentence.
   - ADD AUDIO in the sorting part: a voice reading problem
     and solution words mixed up — pollution, wind power,
     drought, recycle — the student holds up a red hand for
     problem, a green hand for solution, before the voice
     sorts them.
3. **Say it to your companion** — describe the land around
   your home: what is there, what lives there, one problem
   and one solution (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — a
     walk through nature in the super-simple voice (made
     later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — draw your land, label five nature
   words, and write one problem and one solution sentence
   (lesson/lesson.md).

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- GIF — a picture of a sunrise over a hill and a lake → the
  student says sunrise and sunset as the light changes.
- IMAGE — a picture of a bear in a real forest → the student
  says where the bear lives, in a whole sentence.
- AUDIO — problem and solution words read mixed → the
  student sorts them by hand-sign before the voice does.
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later) → the student
  names each place along the walk.

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the
plan is the build sheet, so nothing the lesson needs gets forgotten.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes lake and river → Albania's movement sentence: "We're
  crossing the lake in a boat." A lake is crossed; a river
  runs. Still water you cross, running water you follow.
- Says sea for every big water → Saudi Arabia's first set:
  "Look! It's a snake. I love the mountains." — the books
  name each place on its own picture; point, don't translate.
- Sorts recycle as a problem → Mongolia's own sorting key:
  problems are what hurts; solutions are what people DO.
  Recycle is a doing word — a solution.
- This lesson's next door: Animals' homes return in the
  Animals lesson; weather words wait for their own lesson.
