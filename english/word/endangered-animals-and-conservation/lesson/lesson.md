# Endangered Animals and Conservation

## Do it first

Some animals are disappearing from the earth — hunted, homeless, their forests cut, their water poisoned. People fight back with a word of hope: conservation. This lesson gives you the danger words and the answer words, then sends you to research one animal and present its case — like a defender in court.

## Sub lesson 1 — Words for wild life at risk

*Very simple. Can-do: Name the danger set: endangered species, habitat loss, poaching, deforestation, pollution — and the answer set: conservation, organisation, protected area, fact sheet.*

The danger words first:

An **endangered species** — a kind of animal (or plant) that may soon disappear from the earth forever. **Habitat loss** — its home is destroyed; it has nowhere to live. **Poaching** — killing wild animals illegally, to sell their horns, skins or tusks. **Deforestation** — cutting down the forests. **Pollution** — poison and dirt in the air, water and land.

And the answer words: **conservation** — protecting wild life and wild places. A **protected area** — land or sea where animals are safe by law. An **organisation** — a group of people working together for a cause; conservation organisations run the protected areas and fight the poachers. A **fact sheet** — one page of the key facts about a thing.

Try it now: which danger word fits — a forest cut for farms (deforestation); a hunter killing an elephant for its tusks (poaching); a river full of plastic (pollution).

## Sub lesson 2 — Finding out and presenting

*Simple. Can-do: Run the research project: choose an endangered animal, find out about it (collaborative jigsaw reading), complete a fact sheet, and make a poster or visual presentation of the case.*

Now use the words the way the book's project does:

1. **Choose** an endangered animal — one you care about. **Find out** about it: the class reads in jigsaw teams, each team reading one part (its home, its dangers, its helpers) and teaching the others. **Complete a fact sheet**: name, habitat, why it is endangered, what is being done. **Present** it: a poster or visual presentation that argues the animal's case — its dangers named with the danger words, its hope named with the answer words.

Try it now: draft your fact sheet's first two lines — the animal's name and its habitat — before the lesson ends.

## Say it to your companion

Read this lesson aloud to your companion. Then present your chosen animal's case in five sentences: name, home, dangers, hope.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write a fact sheet for one endangered animal (real facts you can check).
2. Sort eight cause-cards into dangers (habitat loss, poaching, deforestation, pollution) and give an example of each.
3. Design the poster: headline, picture, three facts, one call to action.
4. Say what a protected area does, in two sentences.
