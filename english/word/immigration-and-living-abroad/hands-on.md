# Hands-On — Immigration and Living Abroad

Things you do with real people and real stories. No special materials needed.

1. **The interview.** Find someone who has lived in another country (or whose family has). Ask the three questions: What was hard at first? What did you hope for? What did you build there? Write their answers in three short paragraphs.
2. **Two columns.** On one page, draw two columns: Problems | Contributions. Fill each with three true entries from the lesson and from your interview.
3. **First week plan.** A family arrives in your town from another country, speaking no word of your language. Write a one-week plan to help them adjust: language, food, school, work, laws. Five lines, one per day is enough to start.
4. **Letter home.** Write the letter of someone one year abroad: one paragraph of experiences, one of expectations, one of contributions. Read it aloud to a partner.
