# Lesson Plan — Writing the Date

**One thing:** how the date is written — inside a sentence, and in the British and American formats, from fully written-out to short numeric.

## Sub lessons

1. Writing a date inside a sentence — set off by commas, written in full, ordinal and cardinal numbers, consistency
2. British and American formats — day-month-year vs month-day-year, six formats, formality levels, where each is used

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Teaching points

- In a formal letter, the date is written properly, not in figures alone: the month, the day and the year, written out.
- Inside a sentence, a date written with month/day/year is set off by commas: "She was born on May 3, 2010, in Paramaribo."
- A date can be written in full. Numbers in dates come in two shapes: ordinal numbers (1st, 2nd, 3rd, 4th — first, second, third, fourth) and cardinal numbers (1, 2, 3, 4 — one, two, three, four); the British style often writes the ordinal, the American style usually puts the cardinal number after the month.
- Keep one style through the whole document — consistency: do not mix formats in one letter.
- The British order is **day – month – year**: 3 May 2026. The American order is **month – day – year**: May 3, 2026.
- Each system has formats from fully written-out (most formal) down to short numeric forms (least formal); the short forms join the numbers with slashes, full stops or hyphens.
- Where each is used: fully written-out dates on invitations and formal letters; shorter forms in memos; numeric forms on invoices and official documents.
- The danger of the short numeric form: 03/05/2026 is 3 May in the British order but March 5 in the American order — in writing that matters, write the month in words.
