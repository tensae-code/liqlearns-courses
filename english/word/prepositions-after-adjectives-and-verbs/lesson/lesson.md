# Prepositions After Adjectives and Verbs

## Do it first

Try to finish these sentences about yourself. Say them out loud:

- "I am good ___ football."
- "My school is different ___ your school."
- "I listen ___ music every day."

Did the little words come easily — at, from, to? Some English
words carry a little word with them, and the pair must be
learned as one thing. That is this lesson: the pairs, learned
whole.

## Sub lesson 1 — After adjectives

Here are the pairs that follow describing words. Learn each
pair as one thing, with its sentence.

**good at** — for a skill: "She is **good at** drawing."
**good for** — for a benefit: "Milk is **good for** children."
**good to** — for kindness: "He is **good to** his little
sister."
**different from** — "My bag is **different from** yours."
**famous for** — "The town is **famous for** its lake."
**safe from** — "Stay **safe from** the sun at midday."

Look at "good": it takes three different partners — **at** for
a skill, **for** a benefit, **to** for kindness. You cannot
guess the partner; you learn the pair.

Say the six pairs with sentences about your own life: "I am
good at…" "… is good for me." "My friend is good to…" "My house
is different from…" "My town is famous for…" "We stay safe
from…"

*Difficulty: first steps — say each pair inside its own sentence, never alone.*

## Sub lesson 2 — After verbs and nouns

Verbs and nouns carry their own little words too.

**listen to** — "I **listen to** the teacher." "We **listen
to** music." Never "listen music" — the **to** belongs to the
pair.
**a decision on** — "They made **a decision on** the new
bridge." The noun "decision" carries **on**.
**aware of** — "She is **aware of** the danger." A describing
word again, this time with **of**: aware **of**.

The rule that runs through both sub lessons: when you learn a
new word, learn its little partner with it. Write the pair, say
the pair, use the pair. "Listen — to. Decision — on. Aware —
of."

Make three sentences of your own, one for each pair: something
you listen to, a decision on something at school, and something
you are aware of today.

*Difficulty: building — three new pairs, and the habit of learning every pair whole.*

## Say it to your companion

Explain to your companion, in your own words, why "good" needs
three different partners, and give one sentence for each. Then
test each other: your companion says the first word — "good!",
"different!", "listen!" — and you must answer with the partner
and a whole sentence before they count to three.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write five true sentences about yourself with the adjective
   pairs: good at, good for, good to, different from, famous
   for.
2. Ask three people what they are good at, and write their
   answers: "… is good at …"
3. Write three sentences with the verb and noun pairs: one thing
   you listen to, one decision on something, one thing you are
   aware of.
