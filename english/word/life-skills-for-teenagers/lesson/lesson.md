# Life Skills for Teenagers

## Do it first

Being a teenager means a life is slowly handed to you — money, time, hunger and all. Can you run it? A budget that holds. Needs bought before wants. A meal cooked by your own hands. Tasks in the right order. These are life skills, and they are learnable — starting now.

## Sub lesson 1 — Money skills

*Very simple. Can-do: Budget: list income and outgo, follow the budget once made; needs first (food, water, clothing, shelter) and wants second (jewellery, a car) — the needs-versus-wants line, drawn and defended.*

A **budget** is a plan for money before the money moves: what comes in, what must go out, what can wait. Making one is half the skill; **following** it is the other half.

Every budget stands on one line: **needs** versus **wants**. Needs keep you alive and safe: **food, water, clothing, shelter**. Wants make life sweeter but can wait: **jewellery, a car**, the newest shoes. The skill is not saying no to wants forever — it is feeding the needs first, every time, and letting wants queue behind them.

Try it now: sort this shopping list — rice, a gold chain, drinking water, a warm jacket, a bigger phone, a roof repair. Needs first: which three are bought today?

## Sub lesson 2 — Running your days

*Simple. Can-do: Assess the situation before acting, prioritise your commitments (what must be done first), and cook: the kitchen words of the book's recipes — teaspoon, pan, boil, cool, ingredients, preheat — and making a microwave cake.*

Two thinking skills run the rest of life. **Assess the situation**: before you act, look — what is happening, what matters most, what happens if I wait? **Prioritise your commitments**: list what you owe (homework, family duties, practice, rest) and put them in order — the urgent and important first.

And the kitchen skill, with its own words: a **teaspoon** (the small spoon), a **pan** (the flat cooking pot), **boil** (heat water until it bubbles hard), **cool** (let it lose its heat), **ingredients** (everything the recipe asks for), **preheat** (heat the oven before the food goes in). The book's celebration skill: a **microwave cake** — mixed, cooked in minutes, and eaten with pride.

Try it now: order this afternoon — football practice, a maths test tomorrow, lunch, a film, helping grandmother. What comes first, and why?

## Say it to your companion

Read this lesson aloud to your companion. Then build a one-week budget together for a small sum, needs first, and defend your order.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write your own one-week budget: income, needs, wants, savings.
2. List your commitments for tomorrow and prioritise them 1 to 5.
3. Write the six cooking words and what each means.
4. Explain needs versus wants to a younger child in three sentences.
