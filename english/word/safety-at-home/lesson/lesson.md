# Staying Safe at Home

## Do it first

Look at a picture of a kitchen in your mind: a pot boiling on the stove, a knife by the sink, water spilled on the floor, a small child reaching up. Which things could hurt someone? This lesson teaches you to spot danger at home — and to say why it is dangerous.

## Sub lesson 1 — Spot the dangers

Danger hides in plain sight at home. Learn to look for it:

- **Fire and heat** — a burning stove, a candle, matches, a pot of boiling water.
- **Sharp things** — knives and broken glass.
- **Poisons** — cleaning bottles and medicines that look like sweets or juice.
- **Water and floors** — spilled water to slip on, a full bath with nobody watching.
- **Electricity** — wires, plugs, and water near them.

Look at a room the way you look at an odd-one-out picture: what here does not belong in a safe place?

*Difficulty: A safe home, searched with careful eyes.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Say why it is dangerous

Spotting is half of it. The other half is words: "The knife is dangerous because it can cut." "The candle is dangerous because it can start a fire." "That bottle is dangerous because it is poison, not juice."

When you can say why, you can warn someone else — a little brother, a friend — before anyone gets hurt. And the rule for every danger: do not touch it; call a grown-up.

*Difficulty: Danger, explained in one clear sentence.*

## Say it to your companion

Describe a room with one danger hidden in it. Your companion must spot the danger and say why it is dangerous. Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Look at your own kitchen or yard. Find one thing that could be dangerous.
2. Say one line: "It is dangerous because ___."
3. Tell a grown-up what you found.
