# Languages and Language Learning

## Do it first
How many languages can you say “thank you” in? One? Two? Before you read on, try to list every language you have ever heard spoken — at home, at school, in songs, on screens. Uruguay's learners face the same list, and their country built a whole system to teach them more of it.

[Picture 0001]

## Sub lesson 1 — Languages in Uruguay — and learning them there
Uruguay's Grade 9 book opens a door most learners never see: the **CLE language centres** (Centros de Lenguas Extranjeras), run by ANEP, where school learners choose from a shelf of foreign languages and study them alongside their normal classes. The same pages note **Uruguayan Sign Language** — a full language of the hands, not a translation of Spanish — and introduce **Inglés Sin Límites** (“English Without Limits”), the programme that carries English teaching into small rural schools that have no English teacher of their own.

Three ideas to hold: languages can be *chosen*, languages can be *signed*, and a language programme can be *carried* to the learners who would otherwise miss it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Languages of the world — and learning a new one
The Grade 12 book begins with one word in sixteen languages: **thank you** — in Arabic, Chinese, French, German, Greek, Guaraní, Hebrew, Italian, Japanese, Portuguese, Quechua, Russian, Serbian, Spanish, Zulu and more — and asks the learner to match each form to its language. Guaraní and Quechua stand in the list beside the world languages: South America's own languages, spoken long before any ship arrived.

Then the book turns on the learner: a graphic organiser collects the **benefits of a second language**; a television commercial about an older man learning English is taken apart so its **strategies** can be listed and judged; practice activities are sorted by the skill they train; and a set of beliefs about language learning is marked true, false or not sure — because how you believe a language is learned changes how you learn it.

*Difficulty: The matching is easy; judging strategies and beliefs is the real work of this sub lesson.*

## Say it to your companion
Say “thank you” in three languages from the lesson. Then name the programme that carries English to Uruguay's rural schools: Inglés Sin Límites.
