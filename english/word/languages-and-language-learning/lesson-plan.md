# Lesson plan — Languages and Language Learning

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** Languages and Language Learning
**Lesson:** languages-and-language-learning — the languages of one country and of the world — and how a new language is actually learned
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Words for the World

**The one thing:** name the languages a country's learners can choose, say “thank you” across the world's languages, and judge which ways of learning a language really work.

**This lesson's own level:** built from two sub lessons that open in order.

## Why this lesson exists
Two of Uruguay's English books teach about languages themselves: the Grade 9 book walks through the languages taught at the national language centres and the programme that takes English to rural schools, and the Grade 12 book opens its language unit with “thank you” in sixteen languages and a hard look at how languages are learned. The course names languages and peoples (in Languages and peoples) but never taught language learning itself — so this lesson is built, from Uruguay's books alone.

## Sub lessons
1. **Sub lesson 1 — Languages in Uruguay — and learning them there.** Uruguay's Grade 9 book tours the languages on offer at ANEP's CLE language centres, notes Uruguayan Sign Language, and introduces the Inglés Sin Límites programme that carries English to rural schools.
2. **Sub lesson 2 — Languages of the world — and learning a new one.** The Grade 12 book says “thank you” in sixteen languages, weighs the benefits of a second language, and judges the strategies and beliefs of real language learners.

Sub lesson 2 opens only when the prove-it check on sub lesson 1 is passed.
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is needed first. Languages and peoples sits nearby and can be tried first for the naming of languages — a soft hint, never a gate.

## What the learner can do when it ends
Name the languages at a CLE centre, say what Inglés Sin Límites does, match “thank you” to its language, list the benefits of a second language, and sort learning activities by the skill they train.

## What you need
Nothing to bring: the learner's own languages — and the one they are learning now — are the lesson's material.
