# Lesson plan — Describing people

**Course:** English · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** Describing people
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Words for the World —
the words for what people look like and what they are like
(CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** describing a person
two ways — appearance (tall, slim, long hair, a beard) with
"What does she look like?", and personality (kind, shy,
generous, lazy) with "What is she like?" — and never mixing
the two questions.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Adjectives (Level
6 — every word here IS an adjective at work) and on The
body (the hair, eyes and build words stand on the body
names). It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown changing — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Appearance Words** — what a person looks like, described in
   whole sentences. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Personality Words** — what a person is like, sorted into good
   and bad the way Bolivia's book does. (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **The Two Questions** — "What does she look like?" and "What is
   he like?" kept apart, each answered with its own set. (Was
   depth Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking
is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set
is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Adjectives (Unit 6) and The body
  (Unit 7). Recommended, never forced — this lesson stands
  alone: both word sets are taught whole, with pictures in
  words.
- Comparing people (taller than, more confident than) is
  NOT this lesson; Rwanda's book teaches it inside the same
  unit, and it belongs to the comparisons lesson. Here:
  describing one person at a time.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Describe appearance from Rwanda's set: tall, short, fat,
   thin, slim, overweight, attractive, handsome, beautiful —
   plus hair, a beard, a moustache, bald (Cambodia).
2. Describe personality from the books' sets: kind, friendly,
   shy, patient, generous, honest, funny, lazy, selfish,
   brave, cheerful (Rwanda, Cambodia, Bolivia).
3. Ask and answer the right question for each set: "What
   does she look like?" for the eyes; "What is he like?"
   for the heart (Cambodia's unit title itself).
4. Sort Bolivia's describing adjectives into good and bad,
   the way its book sorts them.

## What you need
A photo of a person, or a friend in the room. Paper and a
pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner describes a person they
   love, in their own language, first by how they look, then
   by how they are. The lesson then gives the two English
   sets and the two English questions (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — appearance words; personality
   words; the two questions side by side, with Nepal's
   look / look like / be like teaching (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the questions part: a picture of two frames
     of the same girl — in one, glasses appear on her face
     (look like); in the other, a heart glows in her chest
     (be like) — 1–2 seconds each — the student asks the
     right question for each frame.
   - ADD IMAGE under appearance: a picture of four real
     people standing in a line, different heights and builds
     — the student describes each one with two appearance
     words.
   - ADD AUDIO in the personality part: a voice reading
     personality words — kind, shy, generous, lazy — the
     student mimes or points at the classmate each word fits
     best, kindly.
3. **Say it to your companion** — describe a famous person or
   a classmate without the name; your companion guesses who
   (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed —
     describing a friend in the super-simple voice (made
     later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — write two paragraphs about a friend,
   one per question, the way Rwanda's and Nepal's books ask
   (lesson/lesson.md).

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- GIF — a picture of glasses appearing (look like) and a
  heart glowing (be like) → the student asks the matching
  question for each frame.
- IMAGE — a picture of four people in a line, different
  builds → the student describes each with two words.
- AUDIO — personality words read aloud → the student fits
  each word to a person they know.
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later) → the student
  describes along with it.

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the
plan is the build sheet, so nothing the lesson needs gets forgotten.

## If the learner is stuck
- Answers "What does she look like?" with "She is kind" →
  Cambodia's title is the fix: look like asks the EYES;
  be like asks the HEART. Eyes first, heart second; kind
  belongs to the heart question.
- Says "She is like tall" → Nepal separates the three:
  look + adjective ("She looks tall"), look like + noun
  ("She looks like her mother"), be like for character.
  Drill the three frames apart.
- Calls a classmate fat or ugly in practice → the books
  carry the full sets because books must, but the class rule
  rides with them: describe kindly — slim, tall, beautiful,
  handsome are the words we practise on real people.
- This lesson's next door: Feelings (same level) — the words
  for what a person feels today; these are the words for
  what a person IS.
