# Describing people — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Word · Lesson: Describing people
Built from: Cambodia, ENGLISH Grade 10, Unit 22 "What does
she look like? What is he like?" — Rwanda, Primary Learner's
English P4, Unit 2 "My Friends and I," and Pupil's Book 6,
Units 4–5 — Nepal, English Grade 7, Unit 12 (appearances) —
Bolivia, Year 3, Topic 2 (adjectives that describe people) —
Mongolia, English X (Grade 10), Unit 4 "Personal Identity."

A soft hint before you start: if you know the Adjectives
and The body lessons, this one will be easier — every word
here is an adjective, and half of them stand on body words.
You do not need them first. Both sets below are taught
whole.

## Do it first
Picture a person you love. First, what do your EYES see?
Tall or short? Long hair? A smile? Now — what does your
HEART know about them? Kind? Funny? Shy? You just described
a person two ways, and English keeps those two ways apart
with two different questions. This lesson teaches both sets
of words, and the two questions that carry them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Appearance Words
**Appearance — what the eyes see.** Rwanda's Primary 4 book
gives the set: **tall, short, fat, thin, slim, overweight,
attractive, handsome, beautiful, ugly**. Cambodia's unit
adds the hair and face words: long or short **hair**, a
**beard**, a **moustache**, **bald** (no hair), and **build**
— the shape of the body. Say them in sentences: "She's
tall. Isn't she?" — Rwanda's own model, tag and all. "He
has brown eyes." "She's got short curly hair" — Cambodia's
first-year frame, still carrying the set.

> a picture of three friends standing together — one tall
> with long hair, one short with a beard, one bald and
> smiling — the student describes each person with two
> appearance words.

## Sub lesson 2 — Personality Words
**Personality — what the heart knows.** The books' sets
agree across four countries. Rwanda Primary 4: **confident,
humble, friendly, fearful, funny, generous, honest,
intelligent, obedient, kind**. Rwanda Primary 6 adds the
character sets — the good side: caring, charming, faithful,
pleasant, polite, sincere, thoughtful, hardworking; the
hard side, taught in a crossword: rude, bossy, grumpy,
lazy. Bolivia's book sorts its set the same way, good
against bad — good: quiet, reliable, kind, patient,
generous, cheerful, brave, sensible; bad: ruthless,
hesitant, selfish, greedy, lazy, jealous — and matches six
of them to their meanings: **reliable** = can be trusted,
**generous** = gives freely, **hesitant** = slow to act.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Two Questions
**The two questions.** Cambodia's unit title IS the
lesson: "**What does she look like?**" asks the eyes —
answer with appearance. "**What is he like?**" asks the
heart — answer with personality. Nepal's appearances unit
separates the three frames that carry them:

- **look** + adjective: "She **looks** tall."
- **look like** + a person or thing: "He **looks like** his
  father." (Mongolia's Grade 8 book drills who people look
  like — same appearance, different person.)
- **be like** for character: "What **is** she **like**?" —
  "She is kind and funny."

Mongolia's Grade 10 identity unit runs the same discovery —
look vs be — beside its personality texts. One warning from
every book: **like** in "What is she like?" is not the
liking word. It asks: tell me about her. Answer with the
heart set, never with food or games.

## Say it to your companion
Describe a person you both know — a teacher, a singer, a
footballer — but do not say the name. First the eyes: "She
is tall. She has long hair." Then the heart: "She is kind
and funny." Your companion guesses who it is, then asks you
the two questions in full — and you answer each with its
own set. Swap after two people each.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write a paragraph describing a friend's appearance —
   Rwanda's and Nepal's writing task — five sentences,
   eyes only.
2. Write a second paragraph about the same friend's
   personality — heart only.
3. Write the two questions at the top of each paragraph,
   as its title.
4. Sort Bolivia's twelve adjectives into good and bad in
   two columns: quiet, reliable, ruthless, kind, patient,
   generous, selfish, cheerful, brave, greedy, lazy,
   jealous.
