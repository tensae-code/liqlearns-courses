# Lesson plan — Feelings

**Course:** English · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** Feelings
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Words for the World (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the feeling words —
happy, sad, tired, hungry, afraid, excited and their
partners — each one carried by the books' frame: "I am /
feel…" and Mexico's past pair, "I was happy."
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Level 1 reading
and The body (feelings show on faces and bodies). It
needs nothing else. If you know the body words already,
this lesson will be easier for you — you might try The
body first. Recommended, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Naming the Feelings** — the feeling words named from faces
   and from Bolivia's list of seventeen. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Saying How You Feel** — "How are you feeling?" answered
   whole, feelings matched to faces, and the make-me chain. (Was
   depth Level 2.)
3. **Causes, Pairs and the Past** — a feeling's cause with
   because, Albania's -ed/-ing pair, and past feelings in Mexico's
   frame. (Was depth Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking
is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set
is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The body (this level) and Nouns /
  Adjectives (Level 6). Recommended, never forced.
- Albania's evidence is a teacher's guide for a digital
  platform course (Akelius), not a pupil's book; worded
  so in the sources. Its list misprints "don't warry"
  and "clam" (for calm); both are taught corrected and
  cited nowhere in their wrong shapes.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the feeling set: happy, sad, tired, hungry,
   thirsty, bored, excited, surprised, nervous, afraid,
   angry, worried, shy, brave, calm.
2. Say how you feel now: "I am tired." "I feel happy."
3. Match feelings to faces and to causes: a clown makes
   me happy; long days make us busy (Mongolia's frames).
4. Say how you felt, in Mexico's past frame: "I was
   happy with my friends" — and name its dictionary set:
   joy, sadness, fear, surprise, pride, shame.

## What you need
Your own face. A mirror helps; a companion's face is the
picture set.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner makes four faces —
   happy, sad, tired, surprised — and names them in their
   own language; the lesson then gives the English words
   (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — Bolivia's seventeen; Albania's
   emotion set with its -ed/-ing pairs kept light;
   Mongolia's face-matching and make-me frames; Mexico's
   dictionary feelings (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE at the start of the explanation: a picture
     of six faces — sleepy, sad, excited, happy, angry,
     surprised (Mongolia's matched set) — the student
     names each feeling before reading on.
   - ADD GIF in the causes part: a picture of a clown
     appearing, and a child's face turning happy, 1–2
     seconds — the student says "A clown makes me happy"
     as the face changes.
   - ADD AUDIO in the saying part: a voice asking "How
     are you feeling?" with three answers — the student
     answers for themselves before the voice does.
3. **Say it to your companion** — the chain game: one
   names a cause, the other answers with the feeling it
   makes (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed —
     the feelings faces in the super-simple voice (made
     later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — a feelings page for today: four
   feelings, each with its cause (lesson/lesson.md).

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- IMAGE — a picture of six feeling faces → the student
  names each feeling.
- GIF — a picture of a clown making a face turn happy →
  the student says the make-me sentence as it changes.
- AUDIO — "How are you feeling?" read aloud → the
  student answers for themselves first.
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later) → the
  student makes the faces along with it.

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the
plan is the build sheet, so nothing the lesson needs gets forgotten.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes hungry and angry → the books list them side by
  side (Bolivia's seventeen hold both); the bodies
  separate them: hungry points at the stomach, angry at
  the face. (Bolivia even notes only two English words
  end in -gry: angry, hungry.)
- Mixes bored and boring → Albania's pair: "I feel
  bored. This movie is boring." The person feels -ed;
  the thing is -ing.
- This lesson's next door: The body — the faces these
  feelings live on.
