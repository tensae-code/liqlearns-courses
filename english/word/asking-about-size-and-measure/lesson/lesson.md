# Asking About Size and Measure

## Do it first

Look around the room. Find the biggest thing and the smallest
thing. Now try to ask about them in English: "How big is…?" Try
the same for the longest thing you can see, and for the tallest.
Trying comes first here — even a guess. Then we learn the
questions together: the questions that ask how big a thing is,
and how much it weighs.

## Sub lesson 1 — How big? How long? How tall?

When you describe a thing, you can ask about its size. The
questions start with **How**:

- **How big** is the box? — It is very big. / It is small.
- **How long** is the river? — It is ten kilometres long.
- **How tall** is the tree? — It is six metres tall.

Watch which word goes with which thing. **Big** asks about size
in general. **Long** asks about length — a river, a road, a
rope, a table. **Tall** asks about height — a tree, a building,
a person.

Answer with a measure when you can: "The table is two metres
long." "The door is two metres tall." Walk around the room and
ask about five things: "How long is the desk?" "How tall is the
door?" "How big is the window?"

*Difficulty: first steps — three How questions, each with its own kind of thing: big for size, long for length, tall for height.*

## Sub lesson 2 — How much does it weigh?

Size is not the only measure. You can also ask about **weight**:

- **How much does it weigh?** — It weighs five kilos.
- How much does the bag weigh? — It weighs two kilos.
- How much does the baby weigh? — She weighs eight kilos.

Lift the thing first, then ask. A book is light — about one kilo.
A bag of rice is heavy — ten kilos, twenty kilos. Guess first,
then weigh if you can, and say the true answer in a full
sentence: "The bag weighs ten kilos."

Ask about five things in the room: a book, a chair, a bag, a
stone, yourself. "How much does it weigh?" — and answer each one.

*Difficulty: building — one new question, How much does it weigh?, answered with a number and kilos.*

## Say it to your companion

Explain to your companion, in your own words, when you ask "How
long?" and when you ask "How tall?" — and give one example of
each. Then test each other: your companion points at a thing —
anything in the room — and you must ask the right size or weight
question about it before they count to three.

## Do It Yourself

1. Measure three things at home — a table, a door, a bed. Write
   the question and the answer for each: "How long is the table?
   It is two metres long."
2. Weigh three things — a book, a bag, a pot. Write the answers:
   "The book weighs one kilo."
3. Ask someone at home: "How tall are you?" and write the answer
   in a full sentence.
