# Lesson plan — Asking About Size and Measure

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** Asking About Size and Measure
**Lesson:** asking-about-size-and-measure — the questions that ask
how big a thing is: **How big…? How long…? How tall…?** and **How
much does it weigh?**
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Words for the World (with the
other word sets, beside what-things-are-made-of — that lesson asks
what a thing is made of; this one asks how big it is and what it
weighs)
**The one thing:** asking about a thing's size and weight — the
How big / How long / How tall questions for size, and How much does
it weigh? for weight.
**This lesson's own level:** Word-set and frame lesson: know the
size questions and use them about real things; answer with a
measure ("It is two metres long", "It weighs five kilos").

## Why this lesson exists

No lesson in the course housed the size-and-measure questions as
their own thing. Nicaragua's Grade 8 Student's Book opens with a
describing lesson, "What does it look like?", whose language focus
is exactly these questions — How big / how long / how tall…? and
How much does it weigh? — beside the "what it is made of" question
that what-things-are-made-of teaches (Lesson 2, printed pp.5–7).
The 2026-11-08 re-verification sweep harvested Nicaragua's
secondary books (389 teaching items placed for the country; 5
already in the course, 382 missing) and marked this part NEW

## Sub lessons

1. How big? How long? How tall?
2. How much does it weigh?

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is needed before this lesson. It sits beside
what-things-are-made-of and describing-people; a learner who has
met those will find the questions easy, but no lesson is forced
first — this lesson stands alone.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- Ask how big a thing is: "How big is the box?"
- Ask about length and height: "How long is the river?" "How tall
  is the tree?"
- Ask about weight: "How much does it weigh?"
- Answer with a measure: "It is two metres long." "It weighs five
  kilos."

## What you need
Real things to measure: a book, a bag, a table; a tape or a ruler
if you have one, and any scale you can borrow. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson
1. **Do it first** — hold up the biggest and the smallest thing in
   the room and try to ask about their size in English before
   anyone tells you (lesson/lesson.md).
2. ADD IMAGE: a very big dog beside a very small dog, a long river
   and a tall tree, each one big and clear. The learner tries the
   questions first — trying comes before telling.
3. Work through sub lesson 1: How big? How long? How tall? — with
   real things measured in the room.
4. Work through sub lesson 2: How much does it weigh? — with real
   things lifted and weighed.
5. ADD VIDEO (short): a market — the seller measures a fish with
   a ruler and weighs fruit on a scale, answering the buyer's
   questions.

## Add-ons
- Measure the room: ask and answer about the door, the table and
  the longest wall. "How long is the wall?" — "It is five metres
  long."
- Heaviest and lightest: pick five things, guess their order by
  weight, then weigh them and say the true order.
- Describe and guess: "It is very long. It is made of metal. It
  weighs a lot." Your partner guesses the thing.

## If the learner is stuck
Cut back to one question — "How big is it?" — and two things, one
big and one small, that the learner can hold. Ask, point, answer
together. Add long, tall and weigh only when that one is sure.

## Teacher note
Nicaragua's book teaches these questions inside its opening
describing lesson, "What does it look like?" — size and weight
are part of describing a thing, beside what it is made of
(Lesson 2, printed pp.5–7). This lesson keeps the book's set of
questions whole and in the book's order: size first (big, long,
tall), then weight.
