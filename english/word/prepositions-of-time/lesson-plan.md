# Lesson plan — Prepositions of Time

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** Prepositions of Time
**Lesson:** prepositions-of-time — at, on and in for times: at 7 o'clock, on Monday, in June
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 6 — Word Kinds (with the other preposition lessons)
**The one thing:** Which of at, on, in goes with each kind of time — clock times, days, months and longer times.
**This lesson's own level:** Word lesson: choose at for clock times and holidays, on for days and dates, in for months, years, seasons and parts of the day.

## Why this lesson exists

Four Bolivia sources teach at/on/in for time and none had a lesson: the Grade 8 book's full usage table, the Grade 9 book's practice in past sentences, and two compendium reference charts (Year 1 marked with a star in the compendium; Year 3). This lesson is built from those placements under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06).

## Sub lessons

1. At — times and holidays
2. On — days and dates
3. In — months, years, seasons and parts of the day
4. Choosing at, on, in

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is needed before this lesson. Telling the Time and Days and Months give the words these little words sit with, but this lesson stands alone and teaches its own examples.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- Use at with clock times and holiday names.
- Use on with days and dates.
- Use in with months, years, seasons and parts of the day.
- Choose the right word in real sentences, present and past.

## What you need
A clock face (or a drawn one) and a calendar page.

## The run of the lesson
1. ADD IMAGE: a clock showing 7:00, a calendar page with MONDAY circled, and a calendar showing JUNE — the learner guesses which little word goes with each before the rule is told.
2. ADD GIF (1–2 seconds): the clock's hands spin to 7:00 and the word AT lands beside it; the calendar page turns to a day and ON lands; the calendar flips months and IN lands.
3. Work the sub lessons in order: at, then on, then in — one kind of time at a time, each with the learner's own day as the example.
4. Final practice (sub lesson 4): a week of the learner's real life, told in sentences — “I get up at 7. I play on Saturday. My birthday is in May.”

## Add-ons
- Calendar hunt: point at any time on a calendar or clock; the learner must say the full phrase with at, on or in.
- True/false trap: the teacher says “in Monday” — the learner shouts the fix: “on Monday!”

## If the learner is stuck
Keep the three pictures in view (clock, day, month) and let the learner ask “clock, day, or longer?” for every sentence until the choice is automatic.

## Teacher note
The Grade 8 book prints the full usage table this lesson follows; the Grade 9 book practises the same words inside past sentences, which is why sub lesson 4 mixes past sentences in. The two compendium charts are teacher-compilation evidence, cited as such in lesson/sources.md.
