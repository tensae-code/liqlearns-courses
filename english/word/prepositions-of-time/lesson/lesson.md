# Prepositions of Time

## Do it first

“I get up ___ 7 o'clock. My birthday is ___ May. I play football ___ Saturday.” Three gaps — and English needs a different little word in each: at, in, on. Which goes where? Guess first. Then learn the pattern — it is one of the neatest in English.

## Sub lesson 1 — At — times and holidays

**At** goes with clock times: “at 7 o'clock”, “at noon”, “at night”.

And with holiday names: “at Christmas”, “at Carnival”.

Think of at as a point — a point on the clock, a point in the year.

Say it: “I get up at 7 o'clock. We eat at noon. We sing at Carnival.”

## Sub lesson 2 — On — days and dates

**On** goes with days: “on Monday”, “on Saturday”, “on my birthday”.

And with dates: “on 5 May”, “on 25 December”.

Think of on as one square on the calendar — one day, all yours.

Say it: “I play football on Saturday. The party is on 5 May.”

## Sub lesson 3 — In — months, years, seasons and parts of the day

**In** goes with longer times: months “in June”, years “in 2024”, seasons “in winter”.

And with parts of the day: “in the morning”, “in the afternoon”, “in the evening”. (But careful: night takes at — “at night”. Night is the odd one.)

Think of in as a big box of time — the event sits inside it.

Say it: “My birthday is in May. School starts in February. I read in the evening.”

## Sub lesson 4 — Choosing at, on, in

Now mix them, the way Bolivia's Grade 9 book does inside past sentences.

“We travelled in June. We left on Monday at 6 o'clock.” Both in one sentence — each time gets its own word.

Ask three questions: Is it a clock time or holiday? → at. Is it one day or a date? → on. Is it a month, year, season or part of the day? → in.

Try: “The festival was ___ August, ___ Saturday, ___ night.” Answers: in August, on Saturday, at night. You have it.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion your day: “I get up at 7. I study in the morning. I play on Sunday. My birthday is in (month).” They listen and say one sentence back about their own day.

## Do It Yourself

Write five true sentences about your life: one with at (a clock time), one with on (a day), one with in (a month), one with a season, and one about a past day (“We travelled in…, on…, at…”). Check each with the three questions: clock? day? longer?
