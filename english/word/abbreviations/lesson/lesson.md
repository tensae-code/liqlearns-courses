# Abbreviations

## Do it first

Look at a road sign, a letter's address, or the top of a school note. Some words are not written whole — they are cut short: **Rd.**, **Dr.**, **Jan.** Those cut-short words are this lesson's one thing. You will learn what they are, what they stand for, and how to write them out in full.

## Sub lesson 1 — Shortened forms

*Difficulty: first steps. You only need words you already know.*

Writers do not always write a word in full. To save space and time, they write a **shortened form** — a few letters that stand for the whole word or name. A shortened form like that is called an **abbreviation**.

The short form always stands for a full form:

- **Mr.** stands for **Mister**
- **Dr.** stands for **Doctor**
- **Rd.** stands for **Road**
- **Jan.** stands for **January**
- **Mon.** stands for **Monday**

You meet abbreviations everywhere space is short: on road signs, in addresses, in notes, in lists and timetables.

One warning: the same short form can stand for two different full forms. **St.** can mean **Street** — "St. Mary's Road" — or **Saint** — "St. Mary". How do you know which one? The words around it tell you.

## Sub lesson 2 — Writing them in full

*Difficulty: a step up. Now you turn every short form back into its whole word.*

An abbreviation is only a stand-in. Whenever you need the real word, you write it **in full** — every letter of it, nothing cut away.

Two places where the full form matters:

- **Reading aloud.** You never read the letters of the short form. "Dr. Bello is here" is read "**Doctor** Bello is here". "Born in Jan." is read "Born in **January**".
- **Careful writing.** In writing that matters — a letter, an examination answer, an official form — write the word in full: *January*, not *Jan.*; *Road*, not *Rd.* The full form leaves nobody guessing. A reader who meets a short form has to stop and work out what it stands for; a reader who meets the full form just reads on.

So the whole skill is two moves: meet a short form and know its full form; need the word in your own writing and write it whole.

## Say it to your companion

Read these to your companion the proper way — full forms out loud: "Dr. Osei will come on Mon." — "Meet me at St. Mary's Rd." Your companion checks: did you say Doctor, Monday, Road? Did St. come out right for its sentence?

## Do It Yourself

1. Find three abbreviations in the world around you — a sign, an address, a note, a timetable. Write each one with its full form beside it.
2. Write this sentence out in full, with no short forms left: "Dr. Mensah lives on Palm Rd. and was born in Jan."
3. Write two sentences with St. — one where it means Street, one where it means Saint. Read both aloud to someone and let them tell you which is which from the words around it.
