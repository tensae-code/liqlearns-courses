# Eating Out

## Do it first

Think of the last time you ate food that was cooked outside your home. Where were you? Who brought the food to you? How did you know what food there was, and what it cost?

This lesson gives you the English words for eating out — the places, the people, and how the whole meal runs.

## Sub lesson 1 — Restaurants and cafés — the places and the people

*Difficulty: first steps. Everyday words only.*

Two places serve you food away from home:

- A **restaurant** serves full meals — you sit at a table and eat a whole lunch or dinner.
- A **café** is smaller and lighter — drinks, cakes and simple food.

The people have names too:

- The **customer** is the person who comes to eat.
- The **waiter** (a man) or **waitress** (a woman) takes your order and brings your food.
- The **cook** makes the food where you cannot see.

And the first thing you are given is the **menu** — the list of the food and drinks, with the prices. You read the menu to choose.

## Sub lesson 2 — Eating out — sitting down, ordering, paying

*Difficulty: a step up. The whole meal, step by step.*

A meal out runs in steps, and now you can name them all:

1. You come in and sit down at a **table**.
2. The waiter brings the **menu**, and you read it.
3. You **order** — you tell the waiter what you want: "I would like the soup, please."
4. The cook makes your food, the waiter brings it, and you eat.
5. At the end you ask for the **bill** — the paper that says what your meal costs.
6. You **pay** the bill, thank the waiter, and go.

Restaurant, café, customer, waiter, menu, order, bill, pay — you can now tell the whole story of a meal out in English.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion about a restaurant or café you know: where it is, who works there, and what food is on its menu. Then tell the steps of your last meal out, from sitting down to paying. Listen to theirs.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write a small menu for an imaginary café: three drinks and three foods, with prices.
2. Act the meal with a friend or family member: one is the waiter, one is the customer. Use menu, order, bill and pay in real sentences.
3. Write two sentences about eating out in your town: name one restaurant or café and say what people eat there.
