# Government and Politics Words

## Do it first

Every country is ruled in some way. In one country the people choose their rulers; in another, a king is born to rule; in another, one man takes power and keeps it. This lesson teaches the English words for those ways — the words of government and politics.

## Sub lesson 1 — Systems of government

The main systems, in plain words:

- A **democracy** is a system where the people choose their rulers in elections, and can change them.
- A **republic** is a country with an elected head — a president — and no king or queen.
- A **monarchy** is a country ruled by a king or queen; in some monarchies the king truly rules, in others he reigns while an elected parliament rules.
- A **dictatorship** is a system where one person holds all the power and the people cannot change him.
- The **government** is the group of people who rule a country.
- A **parliament** is the assembly of chosen people who make a country's laws.
- A country's system is written down in its **constitution** — its highest set of laws.

Most democracies are also republics or constitutional monarchies; the words name different parts of the same picture — who chooses, who reigns, who rules.

*Difficulty: Four systems, and the three bodies that run them.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Elections and the people who rule

How rulers get their power, in a democracy:

- An **election** is the choosing of rulers by votes.
- To **vote** is to mark your choice; a **voter** is a person who votes.
- A political **party** is a group that works together to win power and rule.
- The **president** is the elected head of a republic.
- A **minister** is a member of the government with one job — the minister of health, the minister of education.
- The **leader** is the person at the head of a party or a country.
- A **law** is a rule made by the rulers that everyone must follow.

Elections are the hinge of the system: they are what makes a ruler chosen, and what lets the people change him.

*Difficulty: The words at work — from the voter's mark to the minister's desk.*
