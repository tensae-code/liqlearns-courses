# Culture and Living-Abroad Words

## Do it first

A young man moves to a new country. Try to talk about his
first year. Try to say:

- "He speaks with an ___ — you can hear where he is from."
- "After a year, he is ___ in the new language — he speaks it
  well and easily."
- "He got ___ to the food — it was strange, then it was
  normal."

The words you were missing — accent, fluent, accustomed —
are this lesson: the words of living in a new place.

## Sub lesson 1 — Living in a new place

Your **accent** is the way you speak that shows where you are
from: "She speaks with an **accent** from her home town."

**Fluent** means speaking a language well and easily: "He is
**fluent** in English." "She speaks **fluently**" — without
stopping and hunting for words.

**Accustomed** means used to a thing — it was strange, then
it became normal: "He got **accustomed** to the cold." "She
is **accustomed** to the noise now."

An **expatriate** is a person who lives in a country that is
not their own: "He works in another country — he is an
**expatriate**."

Say it whole: "At first, his **accent** was strong and he was
not **fluent**. After a year, he was **accustomed** to the
new place, and this **expatriate** spoke the language
well."

*Difficulty: first steps — say each word inside the newcomer's year, never alone.*

## Sub lesson 2 — People words

Your **fellow countrymen** are the people from your own
country: "Abroad, he met his **fellow countrymen** — people
from home." The word works for women and men of the home
country alike, the way the book uses it.

**Stubborn** means not changing your mind or your way, even
when others try to move you: "She is **stubborn** — she will
not change her plan." A stubborn person can be hard — and
can also hold on when a thing is worth holding.

Write two sentences: one about fellow countrymen meeting
abroad, one about a stubborn person you know (kindly — no
names needed).

*Difficulty: building — two people words join the new-place words in one whole story.*

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the story of a person who moved to a new
country, using accent, fluent, accustomed and expatriate.
Your companion then tells it back with fellow countrymen or
stubborn added.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write four sentences about a newcomer to your town, using
   accent, fluent, accustomed and expatriate.
2. Write about a time you got accustomed to something new —
   three sentences.
3. Write one sentence about fellow countrymen, and one about
   a stubborn person holding on to a good thing.
