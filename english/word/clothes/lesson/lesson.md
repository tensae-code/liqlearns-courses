# Clothes — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Word · Lesson: Clothes
Built from: Bolivia, Year 1 Topic 6 "Typical clothes"
(student p.173) — Cambodia, English Grade 7, Unit 15 "What
in the world are you wearing?" (pp.92–97) — Mongolia,
English IX, Unit 4 "Clothes" (p.50) and English X, Unit 5
"Clothing Now and Then" (p.72).

*Speaker note: the explanation around the words is read in the
learner's own language; the English words themselves — the words
this lesson teaches — are never translated and are always read in
an English voice.*

## Do it first
Look down at yourself. You are wearing a lesson. Name
everything you have on, in your own language — top to
shoes. Now the English names, because clothes are the
words you will use every single morning of your life.

## Then the explanation
The set, the way the books build it. On top: **shirt,
T-shirt, sweater**. Below: **trousers, jeans, shorts,
skirt**. Whole-body: **dress**. On the feet: **shoes,
boots**. The extras that finish an outfit: **scarf, belt,
hat, tie, necklace**. Bolivia's Year 1 book teaches the
set inside **typical clothes** — the clothes of its own
regions, colourful in the cold highlands, white in the hot
valleys — because clothes words are learned best on real
clothes people really wear.

*[Slot: a picture of a child in a shirt, trousers and
shoes, with a scarf and a hat beside them. Point and name
five clothes before you read on.]*

The set's own pattern is **wearing**. Cambodia's Grade 7
unit — "What in the world are you wearing?" — is built on
one contrast: what you **usually wear** against what you
**are wearing today**. "Usually I wear a T-shirt. Today I
am wearing a shirt." The -ing shape belongs to today, to
now, to the clothes on a body in front of you: "He is
wearing…", "I am wearing…"

And clothes take colours, the way Palestine's first-year
books already taught inside the colours set: **"a red
skirt, blue jeans"** — colour first, then the cloth.
Mongolia's clothes unit adds the describing words that
ride with them: pretty, patterned, multicoloured, neat,
loose, casual, smart, striped, tight, plain.

At the top of the set, **materials** — what the clothes
are made of. Mongolia lists them in two books: **cotton,
linen, silk, nylon, satin, leather, lace, velvet**, and
its Grade 10 set adds **rayon, wool, brocade, polyester**.
The pattern: **"His tie is made of silk."** And Mongolia's
fine pair: **made of** when you can still see the material
(a tie of silk), **made from** when you cannot (ketchup
from tomatoes).

## Say it to your companion
Describe your companion, whole outfit, colours in: "You
are wearing a…". Then describe yourself twice — usually,
and today. Your companion listens for the difference:
usually takes the plain shape, today takes -ing.

## Do It Yourself
1. Draw yourself twice: a usual day, and today. Label
   every item with its English name and colour.
2. Write two sentences: "Usually I wear…" "Today I am
   wearing…"
3. Write three material sentences about real clothes:
   "This … is made of …"
4. Ask your companion Cambodia's question — "What are
   you wearing?" — and write their answer whole.
