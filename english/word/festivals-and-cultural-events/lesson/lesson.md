# Festivals and Cultural Events

## Do it first

Think of the biggest celebration in your town or your family. Say its name. Now say one thing people eat there, one thing people wear, and one thing people say to each other. You are already using festival words — this lesson names the year's festivals and the English that goes with them.

## Sub lesson 1 — The festivals of the year

The year turns, and its special days come with it. **Christmas** comes at the end of December, and **New Year** follows it a week later — the book teaches them together. Then comes **Carnival**, the loud, colourful festival of costumes and dancing. **Holy Week** follows — a quiet, serious week. In May comes **Mother's Day**, the day for mothers. And all year long, every region and every town has **its own festival** — a saint's day, a harvest day, a founding day.

Say the year with me: Christmas — New Year — Carnival — Holy Week — Mother's Day — and my town's own festival. Each name is a word of this lesson, and each festival has its own work in the next sub lesson.

*Difficulty: Starts with naming the festivals; the dates and the year's order carry it.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Talking at festivals

Each festival speaks its own English. At **Christmas and New Year** we give **wishes and compliments**: "Merry Christmas!" "Happy New Year!" "What a beautiful tree!" At **Carnival** we **compare**: "Her costume is more colourful than mine." "This year's parade is bigger." At **Holy Week** we tell **what happened** — accounts in the past: "We travelled to the village. We visited the church." On **Mother's Day** we **describe**: "My mother is kind. She is patient and strong." And for a **local festival** we **invite** and give **directions**: "Come to our festival on Saturday! The music is in the main square, next to the church."

Five festivals, five kinds of talk. Name the festival, and you know the English it carries.

*Difficulty: The festival words at work — wishes, comparisons, accounts and invitations.*

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion about a festival: its name, when it comes, and one wish you would say there. Then invite them to a festival in your place.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the name of a festival and one sentence for it: a wish, a compliment, or an invitation.
2. Ask someone at home: "What is your favourite festival?" Write their answer in English.
3. Draw a small calendar of the year and mark three festivals on it with their English names.
