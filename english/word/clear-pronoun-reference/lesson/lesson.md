# Clear Pronoun Reference

## Do it first

Agreement makes a pronoun match its antecedent; reference makes it *findable*. A pronoun fails when a reader cannot tell — at once, without guessing — which noun it stands for. The Philippines' Grade 10 book teaches this as its own skill: twenty practice sentences of vague and ambiguous reference to spot and fix. This lesson teaches the spotting and the fixing. (The matching itself — number and person — is *Pronoun–Antecedent Agreement*.)

## Sub lesson 1 — Spotting vague and ambiguous reference

*Difficulty: first step — the ground of the lesson.*

Two failures fill the book's practice set. **Ambiguous reference:** two nouns stand where one pronoun points — "Terenty told Danilka that *he* had been brave" (Terenty? Danilka?). **Vague reference:** the pronoun points at nothing stated at all — "In the village *they* say the grain once grew huge" (who are *they*?), or a broad *this/which* waved at a whole idea the sentence never names as a noun. The test is the reader's pause: if you must stop and ask "wait — who?", the reference has failed, even if the writer knew what they meant.

Try it now: Spot the failures: (1) Keesh met Klosh-Kwan and told him that he was wrong. (2) The bears swallowed the balls and they died in pain — clear or not? (3) In the camp they hunt by night. Name each failure: ambiguous or vague?

## Sub lesson 2 — Fixing faulty reference

*Difficulty: builds on Sub lesson 1.*

The book's fixes use three tools. **Repeat the noun:** "Terenty told Danilka that *Danilka* had been brave" — a repeated name is never an error when clarity is at stake. **Restructure:** move the pronoun beside its antecedent, or rebuild the sentence — "Walking home, *Danilka* heard Terenty say he had been brave." **Quote directly:** direct speech kills ambiguity — Terenty said, "*You* were brave." For vague reference, supply the missing subject: "The *villagers* say the grain once grew huge", or name the idea: "…and *this failure* angered the king." A fixed sentence is one a first-time reader cannot misread.

Try it now: Fix each: (1) Sue told Johnsy that she would live. (2) The man could not light the fire because they were wet. (3) In the story they travel at sixty below. Write the fix and name the tool you used.

## Do it yourself

Write a ten-sentence retelling of a story you know, rich in pronouns. Then audit it sentence by sentence: for every pronoun, draw an arrow to its antecedent. Any pronoun whose arrow you hesitate over, fix by one of the three tools, and note the tool used.
