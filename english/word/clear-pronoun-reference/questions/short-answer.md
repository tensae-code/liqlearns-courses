# Short Answer — Clear Pronoun Reference

(Every item is marked with the sub lesson it belongs to: (Sub lesson N). The gate pool for a sub lesson is all of that sub lesson's questions — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the whole set is the pool.)

1. What is the difference between ambiguous and vague reference? (Sub lesson 1)
   — Ambiguous: two or more stated nouns could claim the pronoun. Vague: the pronoun points at nothing stated at all — an unnamed 'they' or a whole unnamed idea.

2. Why is the writer's own knowledge not the test of clear reference? (Sub lesson 1)
   — Because the reader does not share it; reference is clear only when a first-time reader can find the antecedent at once, without guessing.

3. Give the three fix tools the book's practice uses. (Sub lesson 2)
   — Repeat the noun; restructure the sentence so pronoun and antecedent stand clearly together; quote the words directly.

4. Fix: 'The man dropped the matches because they were frozen.' Name your tool. (Sub lesson 2)
   — E.g. restructure/repeat: 'The man's frozen fingers dropped the matches' or 'The matches fell because his fingers were frozen' — the original leaves 'they' claiming both matches and fingers.
