# Religions and Festivals Words

## Do it first

All over the world, people gather on special days: they pray, they sing, they eat special food, they remember old stories. This lesson teaches the English words for those gatherings — the words of religions and their festivals.

## Sub lesson 1 — World religions — the words

The words of religion:

- A **religion** is a people's way of believing in and worshipping God, or gods.
- **Faith** is strong belief and trust.
- To **believe** is to hold something true in your heart and mind.
- **Holy** means special to a religion — set apart for God.
- A **prayer** is words spoken to God; to **pray** is to speak them.
- To **worship** is to show love and honour to God.
- People worship together in a **church**, a **mosque**, a **temple** or a **synagogue**, led in prayer by a **priest**, an **imam** or a **rabbi**.

The world's great religions have different names, stories and places — the words above are the ones they share.

*Difficulty: One human thing, carried by the words all faiths share.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Religious celebrations

Every religion marks its year with special days:

- A **festival** is a special time of celebration, often on the same days each year.
- To **celebrate** is to mark a special day with joy — with prayer, songs, food and gathering.
- A **holy day** is a day set apart by a religion — for rest, for prayer, or for remembering.
- A **ceremony** is the set of actions done in the right way for the special day.
- Many celebrations remember a story — a birth, an escape, a gift — and the remembering is the point of the day.

Christmas, Easter, Ramadan and Eid, Passover, Diwali: different faiths, different stories — the same English words hold them all.

*Difficulty: The words at work — the days that mark a year.*

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion about one religious celebration you know: what it marks, where people gather, and what they do — using the lesson's words.
