# Spelling

## Do it first

Say this word slowly: **elephant**. Now write it — without
looking anywhere — exactly as it sounds to you.

However you wrote it, you have just done the thing this
lesson teaches. Spelling is getting a word's sounds onto the
page in order. The first way every writer does it is by ear.
This lesson teaches that way proudly, then teaches the step
after it: checking the word and making it standard.

## Sub lesson 1 — Write it as it sounds

**Friendly opener.** Ghana's Basic 4 curriculum names this
step without embarrassment: Use invented spelling. Invented
spelling is not wrong spelling — it is spelling under
construction.

**The shape of it.**
- Say the word slowly, stretching it: *sss-uuu-nnn*.
- Listen for the sounds in order: first sound, middle
  sounds, last sound.
- Write a letter — or a letter pattern you know — for each
  sound: *sun* → s-u-n.
- Read back what you wrote. If it says the word when read
  aloud, it works as invented spelling.

Young writers write *sed* for *said* and *wos* for *was*.
Those spellings show sharp ears: they carry the sounds
truly. What they lack is the standard pattern — and that is
Sub lesson 2's work, not a shame to hide.

**Work it.**
- *fish* stretched: f-i-sh → **fish** — the last sound needs
  the two-letter pattern *sh* (the course's two-letter-sounds
  lesson teaches the patterns).
- *cat* stretched: c-a-t → **cat**.

**Watch out.** Do not freeze because you are unsure of a
letter. A word written by ear can be fixed; a word never
written is a sentence lost. Write it as it sounds, mark it
in your mind, and check it after.

*Difficulty: first steps.*

## The companion

Say a word to your companion. They write it as it sounds,
sound by sound, saying each sound aloud as they write. Then
read their spelling back together: does it say the word?
Change places.

## Sub lesson 2 — From invented spelling to standard spelling

**Friendly opener.** Standard spelling is the spelling
everyone agrees to use, so every reader can read every
writer. Moving from your by-ear spelling to the standard
one is a checking job, not a new talent.

**The shape of it.**
- Compare your word with its family: *sed* sits near
  *said* — the *ai* pattern (pain, gain) spells that middle
  sound in many words.
- Use the patterns the course teaches: the spelling
  patterns of two-letter-sounds and word-building (the *-ed*
  and *-ing* endings, plural endings like *army → armies*).
- Some words simply must be remembered: *was, said, one,
  two* break the sound rules, and every writer carries them
  as sight spellings.
- Ghana's own ladder shows the destination: by Basic 5 the
  curriculum asks learners to spell phonically irregular
  words correctly, and by Basic 6 to use phonics knowledge
  to spell while checking their written work.

**Work it.**
- *wos* → the word is **was**: the sound is honest; the
  standard spelling must be remembered.
- *jumpt* → the word is **jumped**: the *-ed* ending
  spells the /t/ sound at the end, by the pattern's rule.

**Watch out.** Do not "fix" by sound alone. *Said* checked
by sound looks right as *sed* — the check is against
patterns and remembered spellings, not against the ear
twice.

*Difficulty: the full move.*

## Do It Yourself

Write three sentences about your day. Circle every word you
spelled by ear and were unsure of. Check each one — against
a pattern, a family, or a remembered sight spelling — and
write the standard spelling above it. You have spelled the
way this lesson teaches.
