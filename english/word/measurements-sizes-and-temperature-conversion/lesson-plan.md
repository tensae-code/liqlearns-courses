# Lesson plan — Measurements, Sizes and Temperature Conversion

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** Measurements, Sizes and Temperature Conversion
**Lesson:** measurements-sizes-and-temperature-conversion — the imperial and metric systems side by side, clothing and shoe sizes across countries, and temperature changed between Celsius and Fahrenheit
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 9 — Numbers, Days, and Time
**The one thing:** the learner can read a measure, a size or a temperature in the other system — imperial against metric, one country's size against another's, Celsius against Fahrenheit — and change between them
**This lesson's own level:** Word level — it stands in Unit 9 beside numbers and measurements. The units themselves are taught in the measurements lesson; this lesson is about the two systems standing side by side and the changing between them.

## Why this lesson exists

No lesson taught the two measuring systems side by side, the size tables, or the temperature formulas. Iceland's *Spotlight 9* (Grade 9) carries an appendix, "Useful Numbers", that sets imperial and metric measures side by side (inch/foot/yard/mile; UK and US gallon/quart/pint; ounce/pound/stone), prints men's and women's clothing and shoe sizes for Iceland/UK/USA, and gives the formulas for converting temperature between Celsius and Fahrenheit. The 2026-11-10 expansion read marked it NEW LESSON NEEDED. Under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06) it gets its own lesson; its single source is named in lesson/sources.md.

## Sub lessons

1. **Two systems side by side** — imperial and metric for the same three jobs: length (inch, foot, yard, mile against centimetre, metre, kilometre), volume (pint, quart, gallon — the UK gallon and the US gallon differ — against the litre), and weight (ounce, pound, stone against gram and kilogram)
2. **Sizes that travel** — men's and women's clothing and shoe sizes for Iceland, the UK and the USA: the same person, three different numbers; the size table is always read for the country
3. **Temperature in two scales** — Celsius and Fahrenheit, and the two conversion formulas: °F = °C × 9/5 + 32, and °C = (°F − 32) × 5/9

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is forced first — this lesson stands alone. The measurements lesson (the units themselves) and the numbers lesson help but are not required first.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- Name the imperial and metric units for length, volume and weight, and say which system each belongs to.
- Say why a UK gallon and a US gallon are not the same.
- Explain why the same shoe carries different size numbers in Iceland, the UK and the USA, and use a size table.
- Change a temperature from Celsius to Fahrenheit and back with the formulas.

## What you need
Something to write with; a shoe or a clothing label with sizes on it helps for sub lesson 2.

## The run of the lesson
1. **Do it first** — the learner reads a weather line, "Today it is 77 degrees", and tries to say whether that is a hot day or a cold one before the two scales are taught (lesson/lesson.md).
2. Work through sub lesson 1: two systems side by side.
3. Work through sub lesson 2: sizes that travel.
4. Work through sub lesson 3: temperature in two scales, formulas worked step by step.
5. ADD AUDIO: a weather report read in both scales — "twenty-five degrees Celsius, seventy-seven Fahrenheit" — the learner hears the same warmth wearing two numbers.

## Add-ons
- Label hunt: learners find labels at home (shoes, clothes, bottles) and sort what they find into imperial and metric.
- Weather swap: each learner takes today's temperature in Celsius and presents it in Fahrenheit, showing the formula steps.

## If the learner is stuck
If the systems blur, go back to the three jobs — length, volume, weight — and sort every unit by job first, system second. If the formula slips, anchor it on the two fixed points the learner already knows: water freezes at 0°C = 32°F and boils at 100°C = 212°F; check every answer against them.

## Teacher note
The lesson stands on one Iceland appendix: "Useful Numbers" in *Spotlight 9* (Grade 9), printed pp.126–127. The worked conversions in the lesson text are the course's own, built on the formulas the appendix prints; the size teaching states the appendix's own fact — that the tables for Iceland, the UK and the USA give the same person different numbers — without inventing table values the placements do not carry.
