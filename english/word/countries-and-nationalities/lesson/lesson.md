# Countries and Nationalities

## Do it first

Point at the map. This is Bolivia. This is Japan. This is France. Every country has two English words: the name of the place, and the word for its people. A person from Japan is Japanese. A person from France is French. Learn the pairs, and you can introduce anyone on Earth.

## Sub lesson 1 — Countries

The compendium's six countries:

**Japan** — islands in Asia. **Peru** — Bolivia's neighbour in South America. **Mexico** — in North America. **France** — in Europe. **The USA** (the United States of America) — in North America. **Bolivia** — your country, in the heart of South America.

Find each on the map as you say it. A country's English name is the name the world uses for it — say them until they feel like your own words.

## Sub lesson 2 — Nationalities

Now the people-words, in pairs:

Japan → **Japanese**. Peru → **Peruvian**. Mexico → **Mexican**. France → **French**. The USA → **American**. Bolivia → **Bolivian**.

Watch the endings: -ese for Japanese; -ian for Peruvian and Bolivian; -an for Mexican and American; and French changes its shape completely — French is its own word, like the language.

Learn them as pairs, never separately: “Peru — Peruvian. Mexico — Mexican.”

## Sub lesson 3 — “Where are you from?”

The question these words live in:

“**Where are you from?**” “I'm from Bolivia. I'm Bolivian.” “She is from Japan. She is Japanese.” “They are from France. They are French.”

Both answers are good: the country (from + place) and the nationality (I am + people-word). Give both and you sound complete.

Ask me. Ask your friend. Ask about a famous singer or footballer: “Where is she from?”

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: “I'm from (country). I'm (nationality).” Then introduce a famous person: “He is from… He is…” Your companion must ask you one “Where is … from?” question back.

## Do It Yourself

Write the six pairs from memory in two columns — country, nationality — then check against the lesson. Under them, write two true sentences about people you know: “My teacher is from Peru. She is Peruvian.”
