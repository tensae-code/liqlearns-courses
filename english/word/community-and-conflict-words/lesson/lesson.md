# Community and Conflict Words

## Do it first

Think of a quarrel you have seen — at school, at home, in the street. What words would name it? This lesson gives you the English words for trouble between people — and the words for ending it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Naming the trouble

Trouble between people has names. **Violence** is hurting with the body. **Bullying** is hurting again and again — with hands, or with words, or by leaving someone out. A **conflict** is a fight or a deep disagreement between people or groups.

Naming trouble is the first step to ending it: you cannot solve what you cannot name.

*Difficulty: Plain names for hard things.*

## Sub lesson 2 — The way out

The book's way out has three words. **Dialogue** — talking and listening until both sides are heard. **Tolerance** — bearing people who are different from you without hate. **Resolution** — the solving of the conflict, the end where the problem is worked out.

Dialogue first, tolerance through it, resolution at the end: that is the road this lesson teaches.

*Difficulty: The three words of the way out, in their order.*

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion about a small conflict and how dialogue could end it. Use the words conflict, dialogue and resolution.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the three trouble words and the three way-out words, with one line each saying what they mean.
2. Write about a conflict you know of: what happened, and what a resolution would look like.
3. Make a poster: "Talk. Listen. Solve." with the three way-out words explained in your own words.
