# Lesson plan — Community and Conflict Words

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** Community and Conflict Words
**Lesson:** community-and-conflict-words — the words for trouble between people — violence, bullying, conflict — and the words that end it: dialogue, tolerance and resolution
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Words for the World

**The one thing:** the learner can name conflict and its parts, and name the way out: dialogue, tolerance and solving the problem.

**This lesson's own level:** word level — it stands in Unit 7 (Words for the World) with the other word sets.

## Why this lesson exists
Venezuela's Grade 10 book gives conflict its own lesson: violence and bullying named plainly, and dialogue, tolerance and resolution named as the way through. No lesson in the course housed this set, so under everything-goes-in it is built here.

## Sub lessons
1. **Naming the trouble**
2. **The way out**
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is needed before this lesson.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- after sub lesson "Naming the trouble": name violence, bullying and conflict, and tell them apart
- after sub lesson "The way out": use dialogue, tolerance and resolution — talking, bearing differences, and solving the conflict

## What you need
Nothing special.

## The run of the lesson
1. **Do it first** — the learner meets the words in a doing task before any explaining.
2. **ADD IMAGE** — a picture for the lesson's world, when the picture pass reaches this lesson.
3. **Sub lesson 1 — Naming the trouble.**
4. **Sub lesson 2 — The way out.**
5. **ADD VIDEO** — the filmed run of this lesson, when the video pass reaches it.

## Add-ons
- **Say it to your companion:** the spoken round at the end of lesson/lesson.md.
- **Do It Yourself:** the closing task in lesson/lesson.md — the learner uses the lesson's words about their own life.

## If the learner is stuck
Anchor each word in one small story the learner knows; abstract words stick to a face and a day.
