# Pronoun Case

## Do it first

A personal pronoun changes its shape with its job: *I* gives, *me* receives, *my* owns. Those shapes are the pronoun's **cases**, and the Philippines' Grade 10 book teaches them outright — the full case table, then choosing the correct case in sentences. This lesson teaches the three cases and the choosing. (The pronoun kinds themselves — personal, demonstrative, interrogative, indefinite, relative — are taught in *Kinds of Pronouns*; finding pronouns and antecedents in a poem is stocked in *Pronouns*.)

## Sub lesson 1 — The three cases

*Difficulty: first step — the ground of the lesson.*

Case is the shape a pronoun takes for its job. The **subjective case** does the action: I, you, he, she, it, we, they. The **objective case** receives the action or follows a preposition: me, you, him, her, it, us, them. The **possessive case** shows ownership: my/mine, your/yours, his, her/hers, its, our/ours, their/theirs. The book prints the full personal-pronoun table across the three cases — one honest warning from the read: its table (printed p.288) runs the subjective and possessive headers together in one combined heading, although the book's own text names three cases. Learn them as the three the text names.

Try it now: Say the table aloud in rows: for *we* — we (does), us (receives), our/ours (owns). Then do the same for *she* and *they* without looking.

## Sub lesson 2 — Choosing the correct case

*Difficulty: builds on Sub lesson 1.*

To choose, ask what job the pronoun does in this sentence. Doing the action (or standing as the subject) → subjective: *She* gave the book to Danilka. Receiving the action, or standing after a preposition → objective: Terenty freed *him*; the bread was left for *them*. Owning → possessive: *her* brother, *their* barn. Two traps the book's practice sentences work on: pairs ("Danilka and *I* went" — test by dropping the partner: *I* went, not *me* went) and after *between* ("between you and *me*" — a preposition takes the objective case).

Try it now: Correct these: (1) Me and Fyokla ran to the tree. (2) The cobbler spoke to Danilka and I. (3) Between you and I, the hand was truly stuck. Then say the rule that fixed each one.

## Do it yourself

Write a six-sentence story about two children and a dog. Underline every personal pronoun, and in brackets after each one write its case and its job (does / receives / owns). Then rewrite two of the sentences with the pronouns deliberately wrong, and fix them again, naming the rule.
