# Short Answer — Pronoun Case

(Every item is marked with the sub lesson it belongs to: (Sub lesson N). The gate pool for a sub lesson is all of that sub lesson's questions — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the whole set is the pool.)

1. Why does English change a pronoun's shape at all? (Sub lesson 1)
   — The shape (case) shows the pronoun's job in the sentence — who does the action, who receives it, who owns — so the sentence can be read without guessing.

2. Give the three cases of 'she'. (Sub lesson 1)
   — Subjective: she. Objective: her. Possessive: her / hers.

3. Fix and explain: 'Me and him freed the hand.' (Sub lesson 2)
   — 'He and I freed the hand' — both pronouns do the action, so both take the subjective case; test each alone: 'I freed', 'he freed'.

4. Fix and explain: 'The teacher gave the book to Fyokla and I.' (Sub lesson 2)
   — '…to Fyokla and me' — after the preposition 'to', the objective case is used.
