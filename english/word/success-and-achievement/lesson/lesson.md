# Success and Achievement

## Do it first

Name a person who succeeded at something hard. What did they do when it got difficult — stop, or keep going? This lesson teaches the words of success, from Costa Rica's Grade 11 book.

## Sub lesson 1 — Success words

Grade 11's success box gives the working words: **perseverance, determination, goals, objectives, tireless, untiring, humility, goal setting, planning, smart plan, reflective, determined, stubborn**.

Read them right: the book's own box misprints three — “Determaniation”, “Fighh” and “sturbbon”. The words are **determination**, **fight** and **stubborn**.

*Difficulty: A tight box of thirteen words — and three book misprints to step over.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Perseverance — not giving up

The book's text asks what **perseverance** means: keeping going when the thing is hard. It names people who showed it — **Edison**, who kept trying until the light worked; **Harriet Tubman**, who kept going back to lead people to freedom; **Helen Keller**, who learned to read and write without sight or hearing; **Stevie Wonder**, who became a great musician without sight.

Then it turns the question on the reader: how can *you* show perseverance?

*Difficulty: One big word, held up by four lives.*

## Sub lesson 3 — Eight secrets of success

The book sets a video task: Richard St. John's TED talk **“8 secrets of success”**. Asked what leads to success, he gives eight one-word answers: **passion, work, good, focus, push, serve, ideas, persist**.

Eight words — the whole lesson in a pocket. Passion starts it; persist ends it.

*Difficulty: Eight single words, each one a door.*

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the eight secrets from memory. They check your list against the lesson. Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the thirteen success words, spelled right.
2. Write one line about a person who showed perseverance — from the lesson or your own life.
3. Pick three of the eight secrets and write what each one asks you to do.
