# Rights, Freedom and Peace

## Do it first

Some of the biggest words in English arrive in songs. Tunisia's Perform to Learn book teaches them that way — in a lesson called "Songs of freedom". This lesson teaches the three words at its heart: rights, freedom and peace.

## Sub lesson 1 — Words for rights and freedom

- **rights** — the things every person is allowed to have and do (the right to learn, to speak, to be safe).
- **freedom** — being free; not being held, stopped or ruled unfairly.
- **free** — the adjective: a free person, a free country, free to choose.

"Human rights" joins the first two: the rights every human has. (The disability-and-human-rights lesson stocks the rights words around work and equality; this lesson is the song's own set.)

*Difficulty: Three words — small to say, big to mean.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Peace, and songs of freedom

- **peace** — no fighting, no war; people living without fear.

The book teaches the set through a song because songs are how these words travel: a freedom song is sung by people who want rights, freedom and peace, and singing it together is part of asking for them. Learn to hear the three words inside a song, and to say what the singers are asking for: "They are singing for freedom. They want peace."

*Difficulty: The three words together, heard inside music and said about it.*

## Say it to your companion

Your companion hums a tune and says: "It is a song of freedom." Ask them: "What are the singers asking for?" They answer with two lesson words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the three words with a short meaning for each.
2. Write one sentence for each word, about your own country or town.
3. Find or invent a song line with one of the words, and write what the singer is asking for.
