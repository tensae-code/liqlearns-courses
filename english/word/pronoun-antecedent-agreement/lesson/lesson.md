# Pronoun–Antecedent Agreement

## Do it first

A pronoun must match the word it stands for — its **antecedent** — in number and person: *the children… they*, *Fyokla… she*. Most of the time the match is easy; the hard cases are the ones where something stands between the pronoun and its antecedent, or the antecedent itself is tricky. The Philippines' Grade 10 book gives the definition and nine rules for exactly those cases. This lesson teaches the nine. (When the problem is not agreement but a pronoun pointing nowhere clear, that is *Clear Pronoun Reference*.)

## Sub lesson 1 — The first rules: what stands between, and compounds

*Difficulty: first step — the ground of the lesson.*

The definition: a pronoun agrees with its antecedent in number and person. **Rule 1 — intervening phrases do not change number:** words between the antecedent and the pronoun do not move the match ("The box *of matches* was in *its* place" — box, not matches). **Rule 2 — indefinite-pronoun antecedents:** antecedents like everyone, each, either take singular pronouns; the some/any/none/all/most family takes its number from what it refers to — countable reference, plural pronoun; uncountable, singular. **Rule 3 — and-compounds take plural:** two antecedents joined by *and* are plural ("Danilka and Fyokla lost *their* way"). **Rule 4 — or/nor proximity decides:** with *or/nor*, the pronoun agrees with the nearer antecedent ("Neither the children nor Terenty lost *his* way" — Terenty is nearer).

Try it now: Choose and defend: (1) The basket of eggs (was/were) in its place — and which noun rules the pronoun? (2) Danilka and Fyokla ate (his/their) bread. (3) Neither Fyokla nor the boys ate (her/their) share — who is nearer?

## Sub lesson 2 — The special cases: collectives, titles and number traps

*Difficulty: builds on Sub lesson 1.*

**Rule 5 — collective nouns:** a group acting as one takes a singular pronoun ("The team raised *its* banner"); acting as separate members, plural. **Rule 6 — titles are singular:** a title is one work, whatever its words ("*Twenty-Three Tales* holds *its* readers"). **Rule 7 — plural form, singular meaning:** words like news or measles take singular pronouns despite their shape. **Rule 8 — every and many a:** "Every child brought *his or her* leaf"; "Many a hunter has lost *his* way" — singular. **Rule 9 — the number of vs a number of:** "The number of bears *was* small, and *it* grew" (singular — the number itself) against "A number of bears *were* seen" (plural — the bears).

Try it now: Agree or fix: (1) The number of leaves were falling. (2) Every student must bring their — or his or her — notebook; which does the book's rule choose? (3) "If I Forget Thee, Oh Earth" tells its story in one telling — it/they?

## Do it yourself

Take a page of your own recent writing. List every pronoun and its antecedent in two columns. Check each pair against the nine rules and fix any mismatch, writing the rule number beside the fix. Count how many pairs were already right.
