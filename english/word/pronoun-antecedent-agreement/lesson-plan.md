# Lesson Plan — Pronoun–Antecedent Agreement

**Unit:** Unit 6 — Word Kinds

**One thing:** making a pronoun agree with its antecedent — the noun it stands for — under the book's nine rules: intervening phrases, indefinite antecedents, compounds, proximity, collectives, titles, and the special number cases.

## Sub lessons

1. The first rules: what stands between, and compounds
2. The special cases: collectives, titles and number traps

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Teaching points

- Pronoun–antecedent agreement defined, with nine rules (Lesson 3, grammar box, pp.324–328).
- Intervening phrases do not change number; indefinite antecedents follow the countable rule; and-compounds are plural; or/nor follows proximity (pp.324–326).
- Collectives, titles, plural-form/singular-meaning words, every/many a, the number of vs a number of (pp.326–328).
