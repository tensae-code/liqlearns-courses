# Lesson plan — Sounds and Sound Words

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** Sounds and Sound Words
**Lesson:** sounds-and-sound-words — the words for sounds: clap, boom, ring, bang, buzz
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Words for the World (with the other word sets)
**The one thing:** The English words for five everyday sounds, and which thing makes each sound — in the book's own frame: "My hands go clap! My feet go boom! My phone goes ring! My drum goes bang!"
**This lesson's own level:** Word-set lesson: know the five sound words and say which thing makes each sound.

## Why this lesson exists

No lesson in the course taught the sound words. North Macedonia's Grade 2 course teaches them in its third CLIL lesson (Music): clap, boom, ring, bang, buzz, each word matched to the thing that makes the sound in the book's own frame sentences — "My hands go clap! My feet go boom! My phone goes ring! My drum goes bang!" — with a bee buzzing among the lesson's sound sources. Under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06), a teaching item with no lesson gets a new lesson built; this is that lesson. Its only source is the North Macedonia book named in lesson/sources.md.

## Sub lessons

1. The five sound words
2. Who makes the sound?

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is needed before this lesson. It sits beside Music Words and Animals; a learner who knows the animal words will enjoy the bee more, but no lesson is forced first — this lesson stands alone.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- Say the five sound words: clap, boom, ring, bang, buzz.
- Match each sound word to the thing that makes it.
- Say a whole sentence: "A bee buzzes." "My phone goes ring."

## What you need
Your hands, your ears, and things that make sounds — a bell or a cup to tap, if you have one. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson
1. ADD SOUND: five short sounds, one by one — hands clapping, feet stamping, a phone ringing, a drum bang, a bee buzzing. The learner guesses what made each sound first — trying comes before telling.
2. ADD GIF (1–2 seconds): hands clapping, then the word CLAP under them. The learner claps along and says the word.
3. Work through sub lesson 1: make each sound (or mime it), say its word aloud, and match it to its maker.
4. ADD IMAGE: a bee, a phone, a drum, hands and feet in a row, no words. The learner points and says each sound word for sub lesson 2.
5. Finish with whole sentences: "A bee buzzes." "My feet go boom." The learner makes up one more.

## Add-ons
- Sound hunt: close your eyes; a partner makes a sound; guess it and say its word.
- Sound chain: one learner says a maker ("a drum!"), the next says its sound word ("bang!").

## If the learner is stuck
Cut back to two sounds at a time (clap and buzz first — one the learner can make, one they can hear), act each one out big, and only add the next sound when the two are sure.

## Teacher note
North Macedonia's book teaches the sound words inside a CLIL music lesson, keyed to matching each word with its sound in the Activity Book; this lesson keeps that order — hear the sound first, name it, then say who makes it. The book's own name for the part is kept as the searchable tag in lesson/sources.md.
