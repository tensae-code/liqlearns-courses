# Sounds and Sound Words

## Do it first

Close your eyes. Listen. What sounds can you hear right now? A voice? A bird? Your own hands if you rub them together? Every sound has a word in English. Today we learn five of them — and you will guess each one before I tell you.

## Sub lesson 1 — The five sound words

Here are five words for five sounds — the book says them like this: "My hands go clap! My feet go boom! My phone goes ring! My drum goes bang!"

**Clap** — the sound your hands make when they hit each other. Do it now: clap, clap!

**Boom** — the big sound your feet make when they stamp hard. Stamp your feet: boom, boom!

**Ring** — the clear sound a phone makes when someone calls. A bell rings too: ring, ring!

**Bang** — one loud, sudden hit. A drum goes bang!

**Buzz** — the humming sound a bee makes as it flies. Buzz, buzz!

Say them with me: clap, boom, ring, bang, buzz. Which two can you make with your own body? The clap with your hands, and the boom with your feet!

## Sub lesson 2 — Who makes the sound?

Now we put each sound with its maker, in whole sentences — the book's own way.

My hands go **clap**. — "Clap your hands!"

My feet go **boom**. — "Stamp! Boom, boom!"

My phone goes **ring**. — "My phone goes ring! Someone is calling."

A drum goes **bang**. — "Bang the drum! Bang, bang!"

A bee **buzzes**. — "A bee buzzes round the flowers."

Listen once more: buzz… ring… bang… clap… boom. Can you name every maker? A bee, a phone, a drum, hands, feet. You know all five.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: "My favourite sound word is buzz." Then make a sound — clap, stamp, or hum like a bee — and let them guess the word.

## Do It Yourself

Go on a sound hunt in your home. Find three sounds: one you make (clap! boom!), one from an animal (a bee buzzes — or a cat, a dog?), and one from a thing (a phone goes ring, a pot goes bang). Say each sound's word out loud, and tell someone: "That is a bang." "That is a buzz."
