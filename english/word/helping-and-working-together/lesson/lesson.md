# Helping and Working Together

## Do it first

Look at your class or your family. Today someone helped you. Who was it? What did they do? Now think: who did YOU help today? If you cannot think of anyone, that is what this lesson is for. By the end, you will know the words — and the habits — of helping, and you will use them today.

## Sub lesson 1 — Helping each other

*Difficulty: first step — for the youngest learners, and for anyone meeting these words for the first time.*

Some jobs are too big for one person. A big box. A heavy bucket. A long row of chairs to move. When a job is too big for one, we **help each other**: I hold one side, you hold the other side, and together the job gets done.

Helping starts with going to the other person. Do not wait far away. Walk up, smile, and ask: "Can I help?" Then **work together**: you do your part, I do my part, and we finish at the same time.

Try it now: find one job near you that is easier with two people. Ask someone to do it with you. Say the words as you work: "We are helping each other. We are working together."

## Sub lesson 2 — Different and the same

*Difficulty: builds on Sub lesson 1 — the same class, seen more closely.*

Look around your class. No two people are the same. One is **tall**, one is **short**. One runs fast, one draws well. People eat different foods, speak different languages at home, and live in different ways.

Different is not bad. Different is just different. The rule of this sub lesson is **care and respect**: we are kind to everyone, the same way we want them to be kind to us. We do not laugh at how someone looks or lives. We learn from them instead — ask them about their way, and tell them about yours.

Try it now: pick one person who is different from you in one way. Find one way you are the same. Tell them both, kindly: "You are taller than me. We both like stories."

## Sub lesson 3 — Honest, sorry, sharing

*Difficulty: builds on Sub lessons 1 and 2 — the habits that keep a class kind.*

Three habits hold a class together.

**Honest.** Honest means you tell the truth. The word is spelled H-O-N-E-S-T-Y — say the letters with me. The rule is short: don't tell a lie. If you broke it, say you broke it. People trust a person who tells the truth.

**Sorry.** When something goes wrong and it is your fault — you bumped someone, you broke a pencil, you said a sharp word — say: "I'm sorry." Say it like you mean it, then help fix what you can.

**Sharing.** Sharing means what is mine is also for my friends: I have crayons, you use some too. **Sharing is caring** — when we share, everyone has enough, and everyone feels they belong.

Try it now: think of one thing you can share today, one person you can say sorry to if you need to, and one truth you will tell even if it is hard. Do all three today.

## Say it to your companion

Read this lesson aloud to your companion — a toy, a pet, a person. Then explain, in your own words: what does a good classmate do? Use the words: help each other, work together, different, respect, honest, sorry, sharing.

## Do It Yourself

1. Help someone with a real job today — carrying, cleaning, or finishing schoolwork together.
2. Write or draw three ways you and a friend are different, and three ways you are the same.
3. Spell H-O-N-E-S-T-Y aloud three times, and say the rule: don't tell a lie.
4. Share one thing of yours with someone today, and watch what happens to their face.
