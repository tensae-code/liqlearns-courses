# Table Manners and Social Etiquette

## Do it first

You are eating at a friend's house. The food is on the other side of the table. Your mouth is full and someone asks you a question. Everybody is watching. What do you do — and what do you say? After this lesson, you will know both: the manners of the table, and the polite phrases that carry them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Manners at the table

*Very simple. Can-do: Show the manners of a shared meal: wait until everyone is served before you start, sit up at the table, keep your elbows off it, chew with your mouth closed, never talk with your mouth full, and never reach across another person's plate — ask for things instead.*

Manners are how we show respect when we eat together. They are small, and everybody sees them:

- **Wait to start.** Do not eat until everyone is served — and, at someone's home, until the host starts or tells you to start.
- **Sit up.** Sit straight at the table, and keep your elbows off it.
- **Chew quietly.** Chew with your mouth closed, and never talk with your mouth full. Finish the bite, then speak.
- **Never reach across.** Do not stretch over the table or across another person's plate. The salt is far away? Ask for it — that is what the next sub lesson is for.
- **Take your share.** Take a fair portion, offer food to others, and try a little of what you are served.

Try it now: which of these is good manners — starting before your friend is served, or waiting? Reaching across a plate, or asking?

## Sub lesson 2 — The phrases of the table

*Simple. Can-do: Use the etiquette phrases of a meal: ask politely ("Please pass the salt"), excuse yourself ("Excuse me"), thank the cook ("Thank you — it was delicious"), offer food to the others before yourself, and answer an offer politely, with a yes or a no and a thank-you.*

Good manners have their own small sentences. Learn them whole:

- **Asking:** "Please pass the salt." — "Could you pass the bread, please?" Ask; never reach.
- **Offering:** "Would you like some rice?" — "Help yourself." Offer to others before you take.
- **Answering an offer:** "Yes, please." — "No, thank you." Both answers are polite; both end in thanks.
- **Excusing yourself:** "Excuse me" — when you leave the table, when you cough, when you must interrupt.
- **Thanking:** "Thank you for the meal." — "It was delicious." The cook is thanked at the end, always.

Your turn: run a mini-meal with a partner. One offers, one answers; one asks for the salt, one passes it; and the guest thanks the cook at the end.

## Say it to your companion

Read this lesson aloud to your companion. Then let your companion be the host: they offer, you answer — politely, both ways, yes and no.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the six manners of Sub lesson 1 from memory.
2. Write the five phrase groups of Sub lesson 2, one sentence each.
3. At your next real meal, use three of the phrases — and notice what happens.
4. Teach one phrase to someone at home.
