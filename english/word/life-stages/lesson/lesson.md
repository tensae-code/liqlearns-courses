# Life Stages

## Do it first

Point to yourself and say your stage: are you a schoolchild, a teenager, an adult? A whole life runs in stages, and English has words for every one of them — from before a baby is born to the end of a life. This lesson walks the whole road, in order.

## Sub lesson 1 — Birth and babies

*Difficulty: first steps. Everyday words for the start of a life.*

Before a baby is born, its mother is **pregnant**; the months of waiting are her **pregnancy**. She visits the **antenatal clinic** (also called the prenatal clinic) for **checkups**, so the doctors can watch her health and the baby's.

When the baby is ready to come, the mother is **going into labour** — and then she **gives birth**. The baby in its first days is a **newborn**. A newborn drinks milk: it is **breast fed** by its mother, or **bottle fed**. It wears a **nappy**.

Every birth is written down: the birth is **registered**, and the baby receives a **birth certificate** — its first document. Many families hold a **baptism**, also called a **christening**, to name and bless the child. Out of doors, the baby rides lying down in a **pram**, or sitting in a **pushchair**.

And there is another way a child joins a family: a family can **adopt a child** — take a child as their own, with the law's blessing.

## Sub lesson 2 — Growing up and growing old

*Difficulty: a step up. A ladder of ages — learn it in order and it holds.*

Here is the ladder of growing up, with the ages the reader gives:

- **baby / infant** — 1 to 12 months
- **toddler** — 1 to 3 years
- **preschooler**, also called a **child** or a **kid** — 3 to 5
- **schoolchild** — a **schoolgirl** or a **schoolboy** — 5 to 10
- **preteen** — 11 to 12
- **teenager**, short **teen** — 13 to 19
- **adult** — a **man** or a **woman** from 18

Grown-up life has its own words. A student who finishes a degree **graduates** and becomes a **graduate**; one who studies further is a **postgraduate**. Adults go to **work** and hold a **job**. Two people who marry enter **marriage**; they are **married**, and each is the other's **partner**. With children they are a **family**, and each parent is a **parent**. A **housewife** or a **househusband** is the one who runs the home. At 65, a worker can **retire** — stop working for good — and live as a **pensioner**.

Say the ladder to yourself once: infant, toddler, preschooler, schoolchild, preteen, teenager, adult… graduate, work, marriage, family… retire, pensioner. That is a whole growing-up in one breath.

## Sub lesson 3 — Death and dying

*Difficulty: a step further. Serious words, said plainly and kindly.*

The end of a life has its words too. The verb is **die**: "Her grandfather died last year." The adjective is **dead**, and the noun is **death**. There is also a gentler way to say it: **pass away** — "He passed away in his sleep." The dead person is called **the deceased**.

The **undertaker** is the person whose work is to arrange what follows: the **funeral**, the ceremony where people say goodbye, and the **burial**. The body lies in a **coffin** and is **buried** — put into the ground — in a **grave**. A **mausoleum** is a building built to hold the dead. People bring a **wreath**, a ring of flowers, to honour the one who died.

What a person leaves behind is settled by their **will** — the paper that says who should receive their things. The **executor** is the person named to carry out the will. The **heirs** are those who receive, and what they receive is the **inheritance**. And a child whose mother and father have both died is an **orphan** — a word that asks us to be kind.

## Say it to your companion

Walk your companion down the ladder: tell them the stages of a life from newborn to pensioner, one word at a time, and let them say the stage you are pointing at in your own family — a newborn cousin, a retired grandmother. Then say one gentle sentence with **pass away** and one with **inheritance**.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw the ladder: seven boxes for the seven stages of growing up, with the ages under each. Write two true sentences about people you know on the ladder.
2. Write three sentences about the start of one baby's life — real or imagined — using **pregnant**, **gives birth** and **birth certificate**.
3. Write a short, kind paragraph about an old person who has died, using at least four of these words: **pass away**, **funeral**, **wreath**, **will**, **inheritance**, **orphan**.
