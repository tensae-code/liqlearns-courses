# Libraries and Reading

## Do it first

What is a library for? Tunisia's Perform to Learn book gives a whole lesson to the answer — "The importance of libraries". This lesson teaches its words: the library itself, what you do there, and the big words for why reading matters to a whole people.

## Sub lesson 1 — In the library

- **library** — a place that holds books for everyone to read.
- **borrow** — to take a book home for a time, and bring it back.
- **literacy** — being able to read and write; a library grows literacy. (Its opposite, illiteracy, is taught in leaving-school-and-illiteracy.)

Say it: "I go to the library. I borrow a book. I read it and bring it back."

*Difficulty: Three words for the place, the loan and the skill it grows.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Why libraries matter

The Perform lesson's own argument, in its own words:

- **civilisation** — the life of a people: its learning, arts and cities. Libraries stand inside it from the beginning.
- **enlighten** — to give light to the mind; books enlighten their readers.
- **pay tribute** — to say, with respect, what someone or something has given: the lesson pays tribute to libraries.

"Libraries enlighten people. We pay tribute to them: they carry civilisation from one generation to the next."

*Difficulty: Three formal words, each said about something real.*

## Say it to your companion

Your companion says: "Libraries are just rooms with books." Answer with two of the lesson's big words — enlighten, civilisation, pay tribute.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the five words with a short meaning for each.
2. Write one line paying tribute to libraries, in your own words.
3. Borrow or choose one book this week and write its title and one thing it taught you.
