# Lesson plan — Colours

**Course:** English · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** Colours
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Words for the World (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the colour words — red,
green, blue, yellow, black, white, and the rainbow colours that
grow out of them — each one asked and answered with the books'
own question: "What colour?"
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Level 1 reading and
the Nouns lesson (a colour word names what a thing looks like).
It needs nothing else. If you know the home words already, this
lesson will be easier for you — you might try The home first.
Recommended, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The First Six Colours** — red, green, white, blue, black,
   yellow, and the "What colour?" question, with colours first
   riding on things in the book's colouring line. (Was depth Level
   1.)
2. **The Rainbow Seven and Colours on Things** — the rainbow seven
   in order, and colour + thing the way the books say it, on
   clothes too. (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **Mixing Colours** — Mongolia's mixing chart, the primary
   colours, and describing real things by colour in whole answers.
   (Was depth Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking
is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set
is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Nouns (Level 6, Word Kinds). Recommended,
  never forced.
- Mixing colours and patterns (striped, checked, dotted) are
  NOT this lesson's core; the mixing chart rides in at Level 3
  as this set's own pattern. This lesson names the colours.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name Palestine's six first colours: red, green, white,
   blue, black, yellow.
2. Name the full rainbow set Mongolia's book unjumbles: red,
   orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet.
3. Ask and answer the books' question: "What colour?" —
   "Red and black." / "It's a blue car."
4. Mix two colours and name the new one from Mongolia's
   chart: red + yellow = orange; yellow + blue = green;
   blue + red = violet.

## What you need
Things around you with colours — clothes, cars, a rainbow
picture. Paper and colour pencils if you have them.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner looks around the room and
   names the colours they can see, in their own language;
   the lesson then gives the English names (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — Palestine's six colours with the
   "What colour?" pattern; the rainbow seven; the mixing
   chart (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE at the start of the explanation: a picture of
     six umbrellas, one in each of Palestine's colours — red,
     green, white, blue, black, yellow — the student points
     and names each colour before reading on.
   - ADD GIF in the rainbow part: a picture of a rainbow
     growing colour by colour — red, orange, yellow, green,
     blue, indigo, violet — the student says each colour as
     it appears.
   - ADD AUDIO in the question part: a voice asking "What
     colour?" about a car, a taxi, a van, a bus and a bike —
     the student answers aloud before the voice does.
3. **Say it to your companion** — point at things around you
   and ask "What colour?"; your companion answers with the
   colour and the thing (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the
     colour walk in the super-simple voice (made later, when
     videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — draw and colour six things, one in
   each first colour, and write each colour's name
   (lesson/lesson.md).

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes blue and green → go back to Palestine's colouring
  line: "blue car, green car" (p.21). Say the pair with two
  real things in front of you.
- Forgets orange → it is a mixed colour: red + yellow =
  orange (Mongolia's chart). Mixing is the memory.
- This lesson's next door: Clothes — the same colours worn
  (Palestine's next unit describes clothes by colour).
