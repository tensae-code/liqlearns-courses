# Colours — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Word · Lesson: Colours
Built from: Palestine, English for Palestine Grade 1 Part 2,
Unit 12 "Colours" (p.16) — Bolivia, Year 1 Topic 6 "Typical
clothes" (student p.173) — Mongolia, English X, Unit 5
"Colours and Patterns" (p.66).

*Speaker note: the explanation around the words is read in the
learner's own language; the English words themselves — the words
this lesson teaches — are never translated and are always read in
an English voice.*

## Do it first
Look around you. Everything you see wears a colour — your
shirt, the door, the sky, a car outside. Name the colours
you can see, in your own language. Now we learn the English
names, because colour is one of the first things you will
ever describe in English: "What colour?" is a question the
books ask from the very first year.

## Sub lesson 1 — The First Six Colours
Palestine's first-year book teaches **six colours**, with an
umbrella as the word that carries them: **red, green, white,
blue, black, yellow**.

*[Slot: a picture of six umbrellas, one in each colour — red,
green, white, blue, black, yellow. Point at each umbrella and
say its colour before you read on.]*

And the question the book drills: **"What colour?"** —
"What colour?" "Red and black." The colour then rides on
real things, in the book's own colouring line: "blue car,
yellow taxi, white van, red bus, black bike, green car"
(p.21). Say them that way — colour first, then the thing:
**a red bus, a blue car, a yellow taxi**.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Rainbow Seven and Colours on Things
Bolivia's Year 1 book meets the same words on clothes —
clothes and colours taught together, because a colour is
easiest to see on something you wear. Palestine's next unit
does the same: clothes described by colour — "a red skirt,
blue jeans."

Now the rainbow. Mongolia's book opens its colours unit by
unjumbling the **rainbow seven**: **red, orange, yellow,
green, blue, indigo, violet** — and a rainbow colour poem to
complete.

*[Slot: a picture of a rainbow growing colour by colour —
red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet. Say each
colour as it appears.]*

## Sub lesson 3 — Mixing Colours
And this set's own pattern — **mixing**. Mongolia prints a
colour mixing chart: **red + yellow = orange**,
**yellow + blue = green**, **blue + red = violet**. Under
the chart stand the **primary colours — red, yellow and
blue** — the three no mix can make; orange, green and
violet are the **secondary colours** the mixes make; and
black, white and gray are **neutral colours**.

## Say it to your companion
Point at things around you, one by one, and ask: "What
colour?" Your companion answers with the colour and the
thing — "A blue book." Then swap: your companion points,
you answer. Finish with three mixes: "Red and yellow make…?"
"Orange!"

## Do It Yourself
1. Draw six things — a car, a taxi, a van, a bus, a bike, an
   umbrella. Colour each one a different first colour.
2. Write each colour's name under its thing: "a red bus."
3. Write the rainbow seven in order, from memory.
4. Write the three mixes from Mongolia's chart, and name
   the three primary colours.
