# Hands-on — Colours

The lesson in your real world, not only on the page.

## The colour walk
Walk through your home or your street. At every thing with a
strong colour, stop, point, and say its colour in English:
"Red. A red door." Ten things minimum. The colour you cannot
name is your new word to find in the lesson's list.

## Colour labels
Write each of the six first colours on a small card — RED,
GREEN, WHITE, BLUE, BLACK, YELLOW — and put each card on a
thing of that colour for a day. Read the card every time you
pass. Tomorrow, collect the cards and name the colours from
memory as you walk again.

## Mix and name
With paints, pencils or two see-through coloured things:
mix red and yellow, yellow and blue, blue and red. Say the
new colour's name each time — orange, green, violet — the
way Mongolia's mixing chart lays them out.

## The guessing game
With a partner: one thinks of a thing in the room, the other
asks "What colour?" until the colour is named, then guesses
the thing. Five rounds, then swap roles.
