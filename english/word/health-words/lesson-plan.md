# Lesson plan — Health words

**Course:** English · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** Health words
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 7, "Words for the World" —
the words for feeling ill and getting better (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the symptom words —
headache, fever, cough, a cold, stomachache, sore throat,
toothache — how to tell a doctor what is wrong, and how to
give and take health advice with should / shouldn't.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The body (Level 7 —
the names of the parts). A symptom word is a body word plus
what is wrong with it, so the body names come back as the
places that hurt. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown changing — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The body (Level 7). Recommended, never
  forced — this lesson stands alone: every symptom is taught
  with the body part in the sentence itself.
- Naming the body parts is NOT this lesson; that is The body.
  This lesson starts where that one ends: a part that hurts.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the symptom set the books teach: headache, fever,
   cough, a cold, stomachache, sore throat, toothache, pain,
   tired, sick — and say "I have a headache." "I feel sick."
2. Play both sides of the doctor–patient talk Rwanda's book
   prints: the doctor asks what is wrong; the patient names
   the illness (food poisoning, a cough, malaria).
3. Give health advice with should / shouldn't, the way
   Albania's, Bolivia's and Saudi Arabia's books drill it:
   "You should go to the doctor." "You should rest." "You
   shouldn't eat too much junk food."
4. Hear the illness warnings the books build: "If you do not
   wash your hands, you will get diarrhoea" (Rwanda).

## What you need
Nothing but yourself — your own last illness is the material.
Paper and a pencil for the advice cards.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner remembers the last time they
   were ill and mimes it: hand on head, hand on stomach,
   a cough. The lesson then gives the English words for
   exactly those feelings (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the symptom words with their body
   places; the doctor–patient dialogue; should / shouldn't
   advice (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the start of the explanation: a picture of a
     child touching their head, then their throat, then their
     stomach, 1–2 seconds each, the symptom word written big
     under each touch: headache, sore throat, stomachache —
     the student says each word as the hand lands.
   - ADD IMAGE under the dialogue: a picture of a real
     doctor's room, a doctor listening to a patient — the
     student points and says who asks and who answers.
   - ADD AUDIO in the advice part: a voice giving the books'
     advice pairs — "You should rest." "You shouldn't stay
     up late." — the student says should or shouldn't for a
     new problem before the voice does.
3. **Say it to your companion** — one is the doctor, one is
   the patient: the patient mimes a symptom, the doctor asks,
   the patient answers, the doctor gives should advice. Then
   swap (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the
     visit to the doctor in the super-simple voice (made
     later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — write three symptom sentences about
   yourself and three advice sentences for a friend
   (lesson/lesson.md).

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- GIF — a picture of a child touching head, throat, stomach,
  the symptom words appearing → the student says each word
  as the hand lands.
- IMAGE — a picture of a real doctor's room with doctor and
  patient → the student says who asks and who answers.
- AUDIO — should / shouldn't advice pairs read aloud → the
  student advises first, then checks against the voice.
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later) → the student
  plays the patient along with it.

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the
plan is the build sheet, so nothing the lesson needs gets forgotten.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes headache and toothache → the hand finds the place:
  head in both hands is headache; one finger on one tooth is
  toothache. The -ache family is one word-shape on four
  places: head, stomach, tooth, ear.
- Says "I have headache" → the books' shape carries the a:
  "I have **a** headache / **a** cough / **a** cold / **a**
  fever." Drill the four with a as one chunk.
- Gives advice with must for everything → Bolivia's scale:
  should is advice ("You should drink a chamomile mate");
  the doctor's orders come later in the course. Here,
  should / shouldn't only.
- This lesson's next door: Food and fruit (same level) — the
  diet half of Rwanda's health unit lives there and here as
  advice, each in its own lesson.
