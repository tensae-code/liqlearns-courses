# Social Problems

## Do it first

Some problems belong to one person. Others belong to a whole town or country — and English has a name for them: social problems. Tunisia's Perform Year 1 book teaches them as a vocabulary set. This lesson teaches that set, so you can name the problems plainly and talk about them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Naming social problems

- **violence** — fighting and harming people.
- **anti-social behaviour** — behaviour that harms other people or breaks shared life (noise, damage, trouble).
- **youth problems** — the problems of young people as a group.
- **overpopulation** — too many people living in one place.
- **food shortage** — not enough food for everyone.
- **being overweight** — a health problem of carrying too much weight.
- **poverty** — being very poor (also stocked in volunteering-and-charity-work).

*Difficulty: Seven names for seven big problems — learn each with its picture.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Talking about problems and help

Use the set in sentences:

- "**Overpopulation** makes the city crowded."
- "A **food shortage** means there is not enough food."
- "**Violence** is a social problem; so is **anti-social behaviour**."

Then take the next step the Perform lesson points to: for each problem, say one thing that would help. Naming a problem clearly is the first half of solving it.

*Difficulty: The words at work — problem, then help.*

## Say it to your companion

Name one social problem from the lesson. Your companion must say what it means in their own words, then name one thing that would help.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the seven words with a short meaning for each.
2. Write two sentences: one naming a problem, one saying what would help.
3. Ask a grown-up which social problem they think is biggest in your town. Write their answer in one English sentence.
