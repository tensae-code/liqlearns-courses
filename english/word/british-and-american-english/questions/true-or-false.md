# British and American English — true or false

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — British and American Word Pairs · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — British and American Spelling · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Reading the Wardrobe.

Say true or false. If it is false, say why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** American English is wrong English. **Answer: False** — neither side is wrong; the skill is recognising the wardrobe (Vietnam's World Englishes unit)

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** 'Chips' names the same food in Britain and America. **Answer: False** — British chips = American French fries; American chips = British crisps — the same word, different food

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** Real books always keep the two spelling systems perfectly apart. **Answer: False** — Nepal's own Sports Day list mixes 'Meters' and 'Metres' on one page (printed p.80)
