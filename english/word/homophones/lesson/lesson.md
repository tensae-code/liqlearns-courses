# Homophones

## Do it first

Listen: "I can see the sea." Two words sound exactly the same — but one is your eyes doing their work, and the other is the whole ocean. If you wrote the wrong one, your sentence would sink. This lesson teaches homophones: the word pairs that share a sound and share nothing else.

## Sub lesson 1 — Words that sound the same

**Homophones** are words that sound alike but have different meanings and different spellings:

- see (with your eyes) / sea (the ocean)
- their (belonging to them) / there (in that place) / they're (they are)
- right (correct; a direction) / write (to put words down)
- hear (with your ears) / here (in this place)
- night (dark time) / knight (a soldier of long ago)

Your ear cannot tell them apart — only the meaning around them can. Fiji's Year 4 book teaches homophones by name beside its recount "Watching the Golf" (printed pp.123–128): the sound is one, the words are two (or three).

*Difficulty: Trusting the meaning, not the ear — the ear hears one word where the page holds two.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Choosing the right one in writing

When you write, the choice is yours, and the reader cannot hear your voice to help them. The working method:

1. Say the sentence's meaning first: what do I want this word to do here?
2. Pick the spelling that carries that meaning: "Their boat is there" — belonging to them, in that place.
3. Check the known traps in what you wrote: their/there/they're, your/you're, its/it's, to/too/two.

A spell-checker will not save you — every homophone is a real word, spelled rightly for its own meaning. Only the meaning chooses.

*Difficulty: Slowing down at the known trap pairs and choosing by meaning, every time.*

## Say it to your companion

Your companion says a sentence with a homophone in it — "Write it right." You say which spelling each sound needs, and why. Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write five homophone pairs you know, with a tiny meaning note for each word.
2. Write one sentence for each word of one pair — two sentences that sound the same read aloud and mean different things.
3. Read a page you wrote before and hunt the trap pairs: their/there/they're, your/you're, its/it's, to/too/two. Fix any wrong ones.
