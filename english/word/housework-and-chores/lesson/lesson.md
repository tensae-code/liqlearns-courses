# Housework and Chores

## Do it first

Look around the room you are in. It did not clean itself. Someone made the bed, someone washed the dishes, someone swept the floor. Today you will learn the English words for all those jobs — and by the end of the lesson you will have done one of them.

## Sub lesson 1 — The jobs in the house

*Very simple. Can-do: Name the housework jobs: make the bed, wash the dishes, sweep and mop the floor, take out the trash, clean the windows, water the plants, iron the clothes — and the care jobs: take care of someone, feed the dog.*

Every house runs on jobs. Learn their names:

**make the bed** — pull the covers straight in the morning. **wash the dishes** (or **do the dishes**) — clean the plates after eating. **sweep the floor** — push the dirt away with a broom; **mop the floor** — wash it with water afterwards. **take out the trash** — carry the rubbish out of the house. **clean the windows** — make the glass clear. **water the plants** — give the plants their drink. **iron the clothes** — press them flat and hot.

Some jobs are care jobs: **take care of someone** — look after a younger brother, a grandmother, a sick friend; and **feed the dog** — the animal eats because you remembered.

Try it now: point at five jobs in the room or house you are in and say each one's name aloud: "Sweep the floor. Wash the dishes."

## Sub lesson 2 — Helping at home

*Simple. Can-do: Talk about a family's Saturday of chores — cleaning, sweeping and cooking together before visitors come — with can/can't for what each person can do and must not do.*

In the Dominican books, Saturday morning is chore time: the whole family cleans the house together before the cousins arrive — one cleans, one sweeps, one cooks. Nobody does everything; everybody does something. That is the rule of this sub lesson: **housework is teamwork**.

Say who can do what with *can* and *can't*: "I can sweep the floor. I can't iron the clothes yet. Mum can cook. We can clean the windows together." *Can* also carries the house rules — the things that are allowed and not allowed at home: "You can play after the chores. You can't leave the trash inside."

Try it now: plan your home's next chore time. Say three sentences: one job you can do, one job someone else can do, and one job you will do together.

## Say it to your companion

Read this lesson aloud to your companion. Then name every housework job you can remember without looking, and say which one you will do today.

## Do It Yourself

1. Do one real chore today and say its English name while you do it.
2. Draw your house and label five jobs where they happen.
3. Ask someone at home, in English: "Can I help?" — then do the job they name.
4. Write your family's chore list for Saturday: who can do each job?
