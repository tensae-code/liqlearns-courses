# Lesson plan — Measurements and Quantities

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** Measurements and Quantities
**Lesson:** measurements-and-quantities — how much, how heavy, how long — weight in kilos, height and length in metres, capacity in litres — and the quantities of medicine: grams and milligrams, bottles and spoonfuls, once, twice or three times a day
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Words for the World

**The one thing:** the learner can measure and be measured — weight, height, length and capacity — and read the quantities on medicine.

**This lesson's own level:** word level — it stands in Unit 7 (Words for the World) with the other word sets.

## Why this lesson exists
Venezuela's Grade 11 book opens with a measurements lesson: the classes of measure worked as English, and then the same measuring turned on medicine at the pharmacy. The course's asking-about-size-and-measure lesson teaches the question forms, not the measure words themselves; this set had no home, so under everything-goes-in it is built here.

## Sub lessons
1. **Measuring things**
2. **Measuring medicine**
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Numbers first (numbers) helps; asking-about-size-and-measure is this lesson's question-form companion — neither is forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- after sub lesson "Measuring things": weigh in kilos, measure height and length in metres, and pour by capacity
- after sub lesson "Measuring medicine": read pharmacy quantities: grams and milligrams, bottles and spoonfuls, once, twice or three times a day

## What you need
A bottle and a spoon help the second sub lesson; a scale or a tape helps the first, if they are near.
