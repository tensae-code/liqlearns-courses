# Measurements and Quantities

## Do it first

Stand up. How tall are you? Guess your weight. How much water is in your bottle? You just used three classes of measure. This lesson teaches their English names — and then the measuring that medicine needs.

## Sub lesson 1 — Measuring things

Things are measured in classes. **Weight** — how heavy: kilos. **Height** — how tall: metres. **Length** — how long: metres again. **Capacity** — how much a bottle or a tank holds.

Four classes, four questions: How heavy? How tall? How long? How much does it hold? Learn the classes and every measuring word has its place.

*Difficulty: Four measure classes, each with its question.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Measuring medicine

At the pharmacy, measuring keeps you safe. Medicines are weighed in **grams** and **milligrams** — a milligram is a thousandth of a gram. They come in **bottles**, and you take them by **spoonfuls**. And the label tells the times: **once a day**, **twice a day**, **three times a day**.

Small words, big care: a wrong quantity is a wrong medicine.

*Difficulty: The pharmacy's small, exact quantities.*

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion your height and your weight, and how much water you drink in a day.

## Do It Yourself

1. Measure three things at home: one by weight, one by length, one by capacity. Write each with its number and unit.
2. Read a medicine label at home (with an adult). Write its quantity words in English.
3. Write your height in metres and your weight in kilos.
