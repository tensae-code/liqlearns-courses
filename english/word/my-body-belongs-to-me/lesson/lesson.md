# My Body Belongs to Me

## Do it first

Put your hand on your own shoulder. This body is yours. Nobody else owns it, and nobody has the right to touch it in ways you do not want. This lesson teaches the words that keep you safe — the same words, taught year after year, because they matter that much.

## Sub lesson 1 — Your body belongs to you

Your body belongs to **you** — not to any other person. That means:

- You have the right to say **no** when you do not want to be touched — a hug, a kiss, a tickle, anything.
- Your feelings about your own body count. If a touch feels wrong or makes you feel bad, it is wrong for you.
- Grown-ups must listen when a child says no about their own body.

Say it out loud, because saying it is part of the lesson: **My body belongs to me.**

*Difficulty: The strongest sentence a child owns.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Private parts are private — and telling

The parts of your body covered by your underwear are your **private parts**. They are called private because they belong to you alone:

- Nobody has a right to touch your private parts.
- Nobody should ask you to touch theirs, or show you theirs.
- If anybody tries — anybody at all — **tell someone**: a parent, a teacher, a grown-up you trust. Keep telling until someone listens.
- It is never the child's fault, and telling is never wrong.

*Difficulty: Private means yours — and a secret about touching is a secret you must not keep.*

## Say it to your companion

Say the sentence together: "My body belongs to me." Then tell your companion: who would you tell if someone touched your private parts? Name a real person.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the sentence: "My body belongs to me."
2. Name two grown-ups you trust — one at home, one at school.
3. Practise saying, out loud: "No. My body belongs to me."
