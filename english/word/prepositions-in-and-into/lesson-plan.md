# Lesson plan — Prepositions In and Into

**Course:** English · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** Prepositions In and Into
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 6 — Word Kinds (CURRICULUM.md) —
a sweep addition beside the unit's word-kind lessons.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** two small words for
"inside" — **in** for where a thing already is, **into** for a
thing moving to the inside — and how to choose between them.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Verbs (the choosing
test asks what the verb is doing) and on the sports words a
learner meets in the Sports lesson. It needs nothing else.
**Why this lesson exists:** Albania's Akelius A2 teacher's
guide names prepositions "in" and "into" as the grammar focus
of its sports chapter (Chapter 33, printed p.21), with the
chapter's own examples — throw/kick the ball into the
goal/hoop. The 2026-11-08 re-verification sweep re-read the
guide page by page and rebuilt its placements from the PDF
itself (41 teaching items; 26 already in the course); this
focus was one of the 15 missing items, and no lesson housed
it — the place words in giving-directions are a different
set. Under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06) the
focus gets its own lesson.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **In — already inside** — in tells where a thing is: inside, and staying there. The ball is in the goal.
2. **Into — moving inside** — into tells a movement whose end is the inside: throw the ball into the hoop; kick the ball into the goal.
3. **In or into — the choosing test** — ask the verb: is the thing moving to the inside, or is it there already? Moving → into; already there → in.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Verbs, and the Sports lesson for the
  words the examples play with (goal, hoop, kick, throw).
  Recommended, never forced — the lesson stands alone.
- See also: Prepositions of Movement — the movement set
  (around, into, out of, through) this lesson's into belongs
  to; and giving-directions, whose place words (in, on, next
  to, over, under) answer "where?", a different question.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Use **in** for a thing that is inside and staying there:
   "The ball is in the goal."
2. Use **into** for a movement that ends inside: "Throw the
   ball into the hoop."
3. Choose between them with the test: moving to the inside →
   into; already inside → in.
4. Hear and say the pair in the sports sentences the book
   itself uses.

## What you need
A ball (or a scrunched paper ball) and a box, basket or hoop
shape to throw it into. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner puts the ball in the box,
   says where it is; then throws it into the box and says what
   changed — same inside, two different words
   (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — in for position, into for movement,
   and the choosing test (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the start of the explanation: a picture of a
     ball flying into a hoop, 1–2 seconds, the word "into"
     under it — then the ball resting in the hoop with "in"
     under it — the student says each word as its picture
     plays.
   - ADD IMAGE under the choosing test: a picture of a
     football goal with one ball inside it and one ball flying
     towards it — the student says which ball takes "in" and
     which takes "into", and why.
3. **Say it to your companion** — explain the difference in
   your own words, then test each other with new ball
   sentences (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the
     ball game in the super-simple voice (made later, when
     videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice,
   short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching —
   served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md (the throw-and-say game).
6. **Do It Yourself** — five sentences of your own, three with
   in and two with into, said out loud (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Uses "in" for the throw ("throw the ball in the hoop") →
  run the test out loud: "Is the ball moving? Where does it
  end?" Movement ending inside takes into.
- Uses "into" for the resting ball → the ball is not going
  anywhere; it is already there. Already there takes in.
- This lesson's next door: Prepositions of Movement — into
  again, with its movement family: around, out of, through.
