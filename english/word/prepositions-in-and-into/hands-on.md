# Hands-on — Prepositions In and Into

The lesson with your body, not only your eyes.

## Throw and say (the lesson's own game)
You need a ball or a paper ball, and a box, basket or drawn
hoop on the floor. One person plays, the other judges.
- Rest the ball in the box. Say: "The ball is IN the box."
- Take it out, throw it so it lands in the box. Say: "I
  threw the ball INTO the box."
- Now mix them: the judge calls "rest!" or "throw!" and the
  player must do it and say the right sentence. Ten turns,
  then swap. A wrong word costs the turn — run the test out
  loud: moving, or already there?

## Goal mouth
Draw a goal on paper (or use a doorway as the goal). Roll a
small ball slowly towards it and talk it in, sentence by
sentence: "The ball rolls to the goal… the ball goes INTO
the goal… now the ball is IN the goal." The two words must
land exactly when the ball's story changes — moving, then
resting.

## Around the room
Walk to three things in the room — a bag, a drawer, a cup.
For each one, say a resting sentence with in ("My pencil
is in the bag") and a moving sentence with into ("I put
the pencil into the bag") as you do it with your hands.
