# True or false — Prepositions In and Into

Sub-lesson markers: every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to — Sub lesson 1 — In, already inside · Sub lesson 2 — Into, moving inside · Sub lesson 3 — In or into, the choosing test. This lesson was built new with its sub lessons (Albania re-verification sweep merge, 2026-11-08), so its markers are original — there are no older depth layers to map from.

Say true or false. If it is false, say why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** "In" tells where a thing already is.
**True** — in is the word for position inside.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** In "The ball is in the hoop," the ball is flying.
**False** — is + in tells position; the ball rests inside the hoop.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** "Throw the ball into the hoop" uses into because the ball moves inside.
**True** — the chapter's own example (Albania, Akelius A2 Guide, Chapter 33, printed p.21).

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** Into and in mean exactly the same, so either word always works.
**False** — the book names them as a pair with different uses: into carries the movement, in does not.

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** The choosing test asks: is the thing moving to the inside, or already there?
**True** — moving → into; already there → in.

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** "The ball flew into the goal and now sits into the goal" is right in both gaps.
**False** — the flight takes into, but the sitting takes in: it rests there now.
