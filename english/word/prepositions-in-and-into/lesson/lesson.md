# Prepositions In and Into — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Word · Lesson: Prepositions In and Into
Built from: Albania, Akelius A2 Teacher's Guide, Chapter 33
"Sports" — its grammar focus, prepositions "in" and "into"
(printed p.21), and the chapter's own sports examples
(printed pp.21–22). Teacher-guide evidence: the guide prints

## Do it first
Take a ball — or scrunch a page into a paper ball. Put it in
a box, gently, with your hand. Now say where it is: "The
ball is ___ the box." Take it out. Now THROW it so it lands
in the box, and say what you did: "I threw the ball ___ the
box." Same ball, same box, same inside — but the two
sentences do not take the same word. This lesson is about
those two words: **in** and **into**.

## Sub lesson 1 — In, already inside
**In** tells where a thing **is** — inside, and staying
there. The ball resting in the box is *in* the box. The
sports chapter plays on a field, so take its own ground:
"The ball is **in** the goal." The ball has arrived; it is
not going anywhere; it sits inside the goal, and the word
for a thing that sits inside is **in**. "The ball is **in**
the hoop." Nothing moves in these sentences — in is the
word for the place a thing already fills.

## Sub lesson 2 — Into, moving inside
Now move the ball. "**Throw** the ball **into** the hoop."
"**Kick** the ball **into** the goal." Those are the
chapter's own examples, and the word has changed: **into**
is in with the movement left in it — a thing goes from
outside to inside, and into walks it through the door. The
verbs give it away: throw, kick — actions that carry the
ball across the line and land it inside. Where the sentence
tells a journey that ends inside, the word is **into**.

## Sub lesson 3 — In or into, the choosing test
Two words, one inside — here is the test that chooses.
Ask the verb: **is the thing moving to the inside, or is
it there already?**
- Moving to the inside → **into**: "She throws the ball
  into the hoop."
- Already there → **in**: "The ball is in the hoop now."
Try it on the pair that traps everyone: "He kicks the ball
___ the goal" — the kick moves it, so: into. "After the
goal, the ball rests ___ the goal" — no movement, it is
there: in. Say the test out loud each time until the two
words sort themselves.

## Say it to your companion
Explain to your companion, in your own words, when English
uses in and when it uses into. Then play the test on each
other: one of you says a ball sentence with the word left
out — throwing, kicking, resting, sitting — and the other
must answer with the word AND the reason: "into, because
the ball is moving inside."

## Do It Yourself
1. Finish with in or into, and say why: "The ball is ___
   the goal." "Kick the ball ___ the goal." "She threw the
   ball ___ the hoop." "The hoop holds the ball ___ it."
2. Make two sentences of your own about a box in your room:
   one for a thing resting in it, one for you putting a
   thing into it. Say both out loud.
3. Run the test on the bag: "The book is ___ the bag"
   (resting — which word?) and "I drop the book ___ the
   bag" (moving — which word?). Say the reason each time:
   moving to the inside, or already there.
