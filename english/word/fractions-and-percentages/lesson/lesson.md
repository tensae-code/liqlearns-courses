# Fractions and Percentages

## Do it first

Take a piece of paper. Fold it in two equal parts and tear or cut along the fold. You are holding one part of two. Now take another piece, fold it into four equal parts, and take one part. You already know parts like these from maths — this lesson gives you their English names, and the names for percentages too.

## Sub lesson 1 — Fractions: half, quarter, and the -fifths / -eighths words

*Difficulty: first steps. You only need everyday words.*

When one whole thing is cut into equal parts, each part is a **fraction** of the whole.

Cut one whole into **2** equal parts. One part is a **half** — we write it 1/2. Say it: "a half." Two halves make the whole again.

Cut one whole into **4** equal parts. One part is a **quarter** — we write it 1/4. Say it: "a quarter." Four quarters make the whole.

Now the pattern for the other fractions. The top number is a counting word — one, two, three. The bottom number is an ordering word — fifths, eighths. When the top number is more than one, the ordering word ends with **-s**:

- 3/5 is **three-fifths** — three parts, each one a fifth of the whole.
- 5/8 is **five-eighths** — five parts, each one an eighth of the whole.

Say them: "three-fifths." "Five-eighths."

## Sub lesson 2 — Percentages: per cent, and saying 75%

*Difficulty: a step up. You meet a new sign and a new way of counting parts.*

A **percentage** is a fraction counted out of **100**. Instead of cutting the whole into 2, 4, 5 or 8 parts, we always count out of every hundred.

The sign **%** means **per cent** — "out of every hundred."

So **75%** is said: "seventy-five per cent." It means 75 out of every 100 — if a tank holds 100 litres and 75 are full of water, the tank is seventy-five per cent full.

Say it: "seventy-five per cent."

## Say it to your companion

Read these to your companion, and let them read them back to you: a half — a quarter — three-fifths — five-eighths — seventy-five per cent. Then ask: "Which is the biggest part: a half, or a quarter?" Answer together.

## Do It Yourself

1. Fold a paper into 2 equal parts and say: "This is a half." Fold another into 4 and say: "This is a quarter."
2. Draw a bar cut into 5 equal parts. Colour 3 parts and say: "Three-fifths are coloured."
3. Draw a bar cut into 8 equal parts. Colour 5 parts and say: "Five-eighths are coloured."
4. Imagine 100 pupils. If 75 of them are present, say: "Seventy-five per cent are present."
