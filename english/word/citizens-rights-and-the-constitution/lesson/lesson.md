# Citizens' Rights and the Constitution

## Do it first
A country's constitution is its most important book of rules. Uruguay's constitution promises every person five basic rights — but the promise below has been scrambled. Unscramble the five words, then guess: which written document promises them?

LFEI — FDEEROM — NOUHOR — PORPERTY — KROW

[Picture 0001]

## Sub lesson 1 — The rights the constitution gives you
Your unscrambled words are the five rights themselves: **life, freedom, honour, property, work**. In Uruguay they stand in Article 7 of the Constitution — the first article of its rights section, which runs from Article 7 to Article 72.

**Life** — your life is protected. **Freedom** — you may move, speak and choose. **Honour** — your good name is protected. **Property** — what is yours stays yours. **Work** — you may earn your living.

Uruguay's Grade 9 book hears five speakers, one for each right, and asks which right each speaker is talking about; then a gapped text about the rights checks that the five words have landed.

[Picture 0002]
*Difficulty: Names a fixed list of five; the listening makes each right concrete.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Rights in Uruguay's history and the world
Rights on paper need a history of people defending them. Uruguay's Grade 11 book researches the milestones: a national institute for human rights, the Constitution's Articles 7 to 72, and the country's early steps — divorce law and the women's vote came early in Uruguay. Human Rights Day, December 10, marks the day the world wrote its own list down.

The same book compares human rights across five countries, then debates: **“Justice delayed is justice denied.”** One team defends the motion, one attacks it — a right that arrives too late, the defenders say, is a right refused.

[Picture 0003]
*Difficulty: Research and argument; the learner must weigh both teams before choosing a side.*

## Say it to your companion
Say the five rights aloud, in order: life, freedom, honour, property, work. Then say the debate motion and which side you would take: “Justice delayed is justice denied.”

## Do It Yourself
1. Find the rights section of your own country's constitution (or ask an adult to help you find it). Write down the first five rights it names and compare them with Uruguay's five.
2. Ask someone at home: which of the five rights matters most to you, and why? Write their answer in one sentence.
