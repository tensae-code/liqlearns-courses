# Lesson plan — Citizens' Rights and the Constitution

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** Citizens' Rights and the Constitution
**Lesson:** citizens-rights-and-the-constitution — the five rights Article 7 of Uruguay's Constitution names, and the human-rights milestones behind them
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Words for the World

**The one thing:** name the five basic rights a constitution can promise its people — life, freedom, honour, property and work — and say where those rights are written down and defended.

**This lesson's own level:** built from two sub lessons that open in order.

## Why this lesson exists
Uruguay's secondary English books teach civics in English: the Grade 9 book (Book 3) builds a whole lesson on Article 7 of the Constitution, and the Grade 11 book (Book 5) debates human rights in Uruguay against the world. The course held the rights words (in Rights, freedom and peace, from Tunisia) but nothing that names a constitution's own list of rights — so this lesson is built, from Uruguay's books alone.

## Sub lessons
1. **Sub lesson 1 — The rights the constitution gives you.** Uruguay's Grade 9 book unscrambles the five basic rights named in Article 7 of the Constitution — life, freedom, honour, property and work — and hears speakers talk about each one.
2. **Sub lesson 2 — Rights in Uruguay's history and the world.** The Grade 11 book debates human rights in Uruguay: its milestones (the national human-rights institute, the Constitution's Articles 7–72, early divorce and the women's vote) and a five-country comparison.

Sub lesson 2 opens only when the prove-it check on sub lesson 1 is passed.
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is needed first. Rights, freedom and peace sits nearby and can be tried first if the learner wants the wider rights words — a soft hint, never a gate.

## What the learner can do when it ends
Name the five rights of Article 7, say what each one protects, name Uruguay's human-rights milestones, and take a side in the debate motion “Justice delayed is justice denied”.

## What you need
Nothing but the learner's own country in mind: every learner can ask which of the five rights their own constitution names.
