# Expanding a Definition into a Paragraph

## Do it first

A sentence definition can be exactly right and still leave a reader unsure. The Philippines' Grade 10 book teaches the next step: take the definition and expand it into a paragraph. It works one word — *speed* — all three ways, so you can watch the same definition grow by example, by comparison, and by process. This lesson teaches those three ways. (The sentence definition itself — term, class, distinguishing characteristics — is taught in *Expanded Definitions of Words*.)

## Sub lesson 1 — Expanding by example and by comparison

*Difficulty: first step — the ground of the lesson.*

The first way grows the definition **by example**: after the core definition, the paragraph shows the thing at work in a real case, so the reader sees the definition happening. The second way grows it **by comparing and contrasting**: the paragraph sets the thing beside its close neighbours and walks the likenesses and the differences, so the reader feels the definition's edges. The book works both ways on the word *speed*: defined first in a sentence, then opened up with examples of speeds in the world, and then set against the slower and faster things around it.

Try it now: Take the sentence definition of *drought* (a long dry period of weather with little or no rain). Expand it by example: add two sentences showing a drought at work — a river, a farm. Read the paragraph aloud: does the example make the definition easier to hold?

## Sub lesson 2 — Expanding by describing a process

*Difficulty: builds on Sub lesson 1.*

The third way grows the definition **by describing a process**: the paragraph follows the thing as it happens, step by step, so the reader understands the definition by watching it unfold. The book's third expansion of *speed* works this way — speed shown through the steps of a moving journey measured against time. A process expansion suits things that happen or are done: rain, erosion, baking, voting. The three ways are tools, not rules: a strong definition paragraph may use one way fully, or mix two.

Try it now: Expand the drought definition a third way: describe the process — week by week, what happens to the land, the river and the people as a drought takes hold. Compare your three paragraphs: which way taught you the word best?

## Do it yourself

Pick three words — one thing, one action, one quality — from any subject you study. For each, write the sentence definition, then expand it into a paragraph using the way that fits it best, and say in one line under the paragraph why that way fits.
