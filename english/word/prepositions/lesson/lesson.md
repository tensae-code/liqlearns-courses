# Prepositions

## Do it first

Look at these two sentences:

- "The cat is sleeping."
- "The cat is sleeping **under** the table."

The second sentence tells you more: where the cat is. One
small word did that work — **under**. Words like under are
called prepositions, and this lesson teaches what they do.

[Picture 7880 — the mascot's cat sleeping under a table, with a book on the table and a ball beside it]

## Sub lesson 1 — What prepositions do

**Friendly opener.** A preposition is a small word that ties
a word to the rest of the sentence. It stands before a noun
(or a pronoun) and tells how that noun joins the sentence —
most often where it is.

**The shape of it.** The first family is the position family:
- **in** — inside: "The books are **in** the box."
- **on** — on top of a surface: "The cup is **on** the table."
- **under** — below: "The shoes are **under** the bed."
- **between** — in the middle of two: "The school is
  **between** the market and the river."

Ghana's curriculum opens this work at Basic 1: identify and
use prepositions in oral and written language to indicate
position.

**Work it.**
- "The hen is **under** the tree." — under tells where the
  hen is.
- "Put the pen **in** the bag." — in tells where the pen goes.

**Watch out.** A preposition needs its noun after it. "The
cat is under" is not finished — under what? The preposition
ties the noun to the sentence; without the noun the tie is
missing.

*Difficulty: first steps.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Place and direction

**Friendly opener.** The same small words also tell
direction — which way something moves.

**The shape of it.**
- Place: "She is **at** home." "The village is **near** the
  river." "The ball rolled **behind** the house."
- Direction: "He walked **to** school." "The bird flew
  **over** the wall." "Come **into** the room." "They ran
  **across** the field."

Ghana's Basic 3 standard names this step: prepositions in
sentences to indicate directions. Two of the course's own
lessons go deeper into single families: prepositions-of-place
and prepositions-of-movement, and prepositions-in-and-into
for the in/into pair.

**Work it.**
- "The goat ran **into** the yard." — direction: it moved in.
- "The goat is **in** the yard." — place: no movement, it is
  simply there.

**Watch out.** **In** tells place; **into** tells movement
towards the inside. "He jumped **in** the water" is heard,
but the careful form for the movement is **into**.

*Difficulty: building up.*

## The companion

Explain to your companion where three things in the room
are, using a different preposition for each one — then let
your companion guess which thing you mean from the
preposition alone.

## Sub lesson 3 — Time: days and dates

**Friendly opener.** Prepositions work with time as well as
place. Ghana's Basic 2 standard sets this step by name:
prepositions to indicate days and dates.

**The shape of it.**
- **on** for days and dates: "**on** Monday", "**on** the
  fifth of May".
- **in** for months, years and parts of the day: "**in**
  June", "**in** 2026", "**in** the morning".
- **at** for clock times and a few fixed points: "**at**
  seven o'clock", "**at** noon", "**at** night".
- **before** and **after** for order: "Wash your hands
  **before** lunch." "We play **after** school."

**Work it.**
- "The market opens **on** Saturday." — a day, so on.
- "School closes **in** December." — a month, so in.
- "The lesson starts **at** nine." — a clock time, so at.

**Watch out.** Do not carry the place rules into time by
sound. "On Monday" but "in the morning" — the pattern is set
by the kind of time word, not by what feels right. The
course's prepositions-of-time lesson practises this family
on its own.

*Difficulty: building up.*

## Sub lesson 4 — Many meanings

**Friendly opener.** The busy prepositions carry more than
one meaning each. Ghana's curricula say it outright at
Basics 4, 5 and 6: use prepositions to convey a variety of
meanings — and the Upper Primary Level 3 guide teaches five
of them by name: by, for, about, between, against.

**The shape of it.**
- **by** — who did it: "a story **by** Ama"; how: "travel
  **by** bus"; beside: "sit **by** me".
- **for** — purpose or person: "a gift **for** you"; "tools
  **for** digging".
- **about** — the subject: "a book **about** farming"; near
  a number: "**about** twenty people".
- **between** — the middle of two, and sharing: "divide it
  **between** the two of you".
- **against** — touching and leaning: "the ladder **against**
  the wall"; opposite in a game or argument: "our school
  **against** theirs".

**Work it.**
- "This letter is **for** Grandmother." — for names the
  person it is meant for.
- "He leaned his bicycle **against** the fence." — against
  tells touching and leaning.

**Watch out.** One preposition, several meanings: the
sentence around it tells you which meaning is at work. Read
the whole sentence before you choose.

*Difficulty: the full move.*

## Do It Yourself

Write five sentences about your own home or school, one with
each of these: in, under, between, on (for a day), and for.
Read them aloud. You have used prepositions the way this
lesson teaches.
