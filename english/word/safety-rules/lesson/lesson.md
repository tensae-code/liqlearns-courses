# Safety Rules

## Do it first

A pot of boiling water stands at the edge of the table, and a small child is running towards it. You have one second and a few words. This lesson is those words: the danger words, the rules that keep people safe, and how to talk about an accident when one happens anyway.

## Sub lesson 1 — Danger words and safety rules

Eritrea's Grade 3 book gives safety a unit of its own (Unit 18, printed pp.155–159). Its words are **careful**, **danger** and **dangerous**, **high** and **low** — and its strongest word is **never**: safety rules are built with it. *Never play with fire. Never run with a knife. Be careful with hot water.* A safety rule names the dangerous thing and the safe action in one short line, so a child can hold it and use it at the moment it is needed. The same unit's reading is a set of short safety dialogues — a zoo bite, a cleaning accident, a night latrine, being ill — safety language at work in real moments, stocked in answering-from-the-text.

*Difficulty: A small set of words, but they must come out fast and exactly; the rules are short on purpose.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Accidents — what happened, and what happens next

The Grade 4 book returns to safety in Unit 11 (printed pp.227–245): danger and accidents, warnings and advice, and predicting outcomes — asking *What is going to happen?* and answering with **going to**: *The glass is at the edge — it is going to fall.* To talk about an accident you need the past (what happened), the danger word (what was dangerous), and the advice (what to do next time). That is the whole arc of this lesson: see the danger, say the rule, and if the accident happens, tell it straight.

*Difficulty: Putting the past, the danger words and a prediction together in one short account — each piece is simple; the joining is the work.*

## Say it to your companion

Your companion names a danger — fire, a high place, a busy road, deep water. You give the safety rule for it, with never or be careful. Then your companion asks: what is going to happen if nobody listens? You answer with going to. Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write five safety rules for your own home, each with never or be careful, each naming the dangerous thing.
2. Look at one room and list its dangers: what is high, what is hot, what can fall, what can cut.
3. Write a short account of a small accident (real or invented): what happened, what was dangerous, and the rule that would have stopped it.
