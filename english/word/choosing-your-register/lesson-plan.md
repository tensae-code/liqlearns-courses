# Lesson plan — Choosing your register

**Course:** English · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** Choosing your register
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 43 — Words About Words (CATCH-UP-PLAN.md,
Units 42–44 build list).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** saying the same thing the way the moment demands: formal vs informal wording, home dialect vs standard English by place and circumstance, and the softening tools — hedging and vague language.
**What the learner can do at the end:** re-dress one message formally and informally; say when home dialect and when standard English serves you, and switch; and hedge a claim or use vague language deliberately, for politeness or honesty.

## Sub lessons

   informal wording and their pair words, for the way of speaking a

2. **Register for Place and Circumstance** — place and circumstance

   marking how sure you are, and vague language, looseness used on
   purpose. (Was depth Level 3.)

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — one message wearing three outfits on a rail — work clothes, home clothes, celebration clothes.** Written slot: "a picture of
   the same message on a rail in three outfits: formal, everyday, home." What the student does: the student points at the outfit each named moment demands — a wedding invitation, a note to a friend, talk at home
2. **Slot 2 — a volume dial labelled CERTAIN at one end and SOFT at the other, a hand turning it down.** Written slot: "a picture of
   a dial from CERTAIN to SOFT, with a hand turning it toward SOFT." What the student does: the student says one hard sentence, then turns the dial and says it hedged

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first**: one request said three ways — to a friend, to a teacher, to an office — and the learner hears the words change while the meaning holds.
2. **Register named**: South Africa's HL G6 question — 'How would the register (or way of speaking)…' (Workbook 1, printed p.53).
3. **Formal vs informal**: Uzbekistan EM9 U7 — invitations judged for correctness, and formal/informal vocabulary pairs (pp.78–80).
4. **Dialect vs standard**: Barbados's ruling — discriminate between dialect and standard usage according to place and circumstance; code-switching (syllabus evidence).
5. **Hedging**: Jordan G12's Active Writing: hedging, in the letter to the editor (S2, U6, pp.24–25).
6. **Vague language**: Jordan G12's fashion lesson set (S2, U7, pp.16–17) — stuff, things, sort of — used on purpose.
7. **Do it yourself**: the closing tasks.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck dressing a message: ask three questions — WHO receives it, WHERE, and what happens if I get it wrong? Formal is the safe suit when the answers are big.
- Dialect is not broken English: Barbados's syllabus treats switching as the skill. The error is wearing one outfit everywhere.
- Hedging is not lying: it is marking exactly how sure you are. Over-claiming is the dishonest move.
