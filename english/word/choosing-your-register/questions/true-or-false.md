# Choosing your register — true or false

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Formal and Informal · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Register for Place and Circumstance · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Hedging and Vague Language.

Say true or false. If it is false, say why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Home dialect is broken English that school should remove. **Answer: False** — Barbados's syllabus defines dialect's role and builds switching; dialect is a code of its own, not an error (syllabus evidence)

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** A formal letter may freely mix 'guy' and 'inform' in one paragraph. **Answer: False** — Uzbekistan's Review plants exactly that mix as the error to spot and correct (p.85)

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** Hedging a claim you cannot fully prove is more honest than stating it flatly. **Answer: True** — a hedge marks your real certainty; the flat over-claim is the dishonest move (Jordan, pp.24–25)
