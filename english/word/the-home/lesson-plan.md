# Lesson plan — The home

**Course:** English · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** The home
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Words for the World — the first lesson of the unit (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the words for the rooms
of a home and the things in them — each room with the name of
what you do there.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Nouns lesson (the
room words ARE nouns — names of places) and Level 1 reading.
It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown changing — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds per room.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The Five Rooms** — the five rooms every book teaches, each
   with its doing sentence: "I sleep in the bedroom." (Was depth
   Level 1.)
2. **The Things in the Rooms** — the things that live in the
   rooms, and which room each one lives in. (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **Rooms in Sentences** — the books' drilled question "What do
   you do in the …?" answered whole for every room. (Was depth
   Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking
is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set
is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Nouns (Level 6, Word Kinds). Recommended,
  never forced.
- Where things are — in, on, under — is NOT this lesson; it is
  a different thing (position words), taught by the same books
  a step later, and it gets its own lesson. This lesson only
  names the rooms and their things.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the five rooms every book teaches: bedroom, living
   room, kitchen, dining room, bathroom (Libya adds the
   sitting room and the garden).
2. Name the things that live in the rooms: bed, table, chair,
   oven, fridge, couch, sink, toilet, closet, lamp, shelf.
3. Say what you do in each room, in the books' own sentences:
   "I sleep in the bedroom." "I brush my teeth in the
   bathroom." "My mother cooks in the kitchen."
4. Answer the books' own question: "What do you do in the
   living room?" — "I watch TV in the living room."

## What you need
Your own home, or a picture of one. Paper and a pencil for the
drawing step.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner walks (or pictures) a trip
   through their own home, room by room, naming each room in
   their own language; the lesson then gives the English names
   (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the five rooms with the books'
   sentences for each; the things in the rooms; the question
   and answer the books drill (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the start of the explanation: a picture of a
     house cut open so all its rooms show — one room lights up
     at a time, 1–2 seconds each, its name written big under
     it: bedroom, living room, kitchen, dining room, bathroom —
     the student says each room's name as it lights.
   - ADD IMAGE under the things: a picture of a real bedroom
     with a bed, a lamp, a shelf, and a wardrobe in it — the
     student points and names four things in the room.
   - ADD AUDIO in the question part: a voice asking "What do
     you do in the kitchen?" and answering "My mother cooks in
     the kitchen," then asking about the bathroom and the
     living room — the student answers aloud for their own
     home before the voice does.
3. **Say it to your companion** — take your companion on the
   tour: name each room of your home and say what you do
   there, in whole sentences (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the
     house tour in the super-simple voice (made later, when
     videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — draw your home, label every room, and
   write one sentence per room (lesson/lesson.md).

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- GIF — a picture of a cut-open house, rooms lighting up one
  by one with their names → the student says each room as it
  lights.
- IMAGE — a picture of a real bedroom (bed, lamp, shelf,
  wardrobe) → the student points and names four things.
- AUDIO — "What do you do in the kitchen?" questions and
  answers read aloud → the student answers for their own home
  first.
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later) → the student
  gives the tour along with it.

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the
plan is the build sheet, so nothing the lesson needs gets forgotten.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes kitchen and dining room → the doing words separate
  them: we COOK in the kitchen; we EAT in the dining room.
  Egypt's sentences carry the difference.
- Says "I sleep in the kitchen" and laughs → good: the laugh
  means the room word landed. Swap in the true room and say the
  sentence again.
- Forgets "living room" → Libya's name for the same room is
  sitting room; both names are in the books, and either is a
  right answer on this course.
- This lesson's next door: The town — the places outside the
  home (same level). Position words (in, on, under) wait for
  their own lesson.
