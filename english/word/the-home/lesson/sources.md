**Acceptance count: taught in 10 countries (Egypt, Libya,
Palestine, Laos, China, North Macedonia, Mongolia, Indonesia,
Iran, Jordan).** Egypt carries the five rooms with a sentence for
each and the drilled question; Libya carries the same rooms
twice (Grade 1 set, Grade 2 labelled house) with the sitting
room name and the furniture; Palestine carries the bedroom set
for the youngest learners; China carries the rooms paired with
their actions, the position-word finding game, the family
words, and the there is/are room system with the six
prepositions of place taught as one set; North Macedonia carries the family set with its formal
and informal names taught side by side; Mongolia carries the
rooms and furniture in real photographs; Indonesia carries
the parts of the house found room by room in Grade 3, and the
there is/are room description with eight prepositions as one
set in Grade 7; Iran carries the rooms with the where-question
and yes/no short answers, widened in its Photo Dictionary to
house types and furniture. Jordan carries the ten home words with the where-question in Grade 2, the furniture set with there is/are in Grade 3, and the whole house — rooms, furniture, chores — again in Grade 8; Other closed countries join as
their books are read (see READING-QUEUE.md).

**Laos Grade 5 added 2026-10-27** — *Student's Book 3, Grade 5*
(MoES/RIES, 2012; full read in english/books/laos.md). Laos
asks about the house whole: **"Is there a kitchen in the house?
— Yes, there is. / No, there isn't"** and its plural twin
**Are there…?** (pp.88–91), the position set completed —
**in front of, behind, between** join in/on/under/next to:
"The TV is on the table. / The sofa is in front of the TV. /
The lamp is behind the sofa" (p.98) — and the series' first
reading passage is a house text with written answers (p.117).
