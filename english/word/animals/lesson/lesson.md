# Animals — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Word · Lesson: Animals
Built from: Egypt, English Language Primary 1 Term 1, Unit 5
"On the Farm" — Palestine, English for Palestine Grade 1
Part 1, Unit 3 "Animals" — Bangladesh, English for Today
Class 2 (the animals unit: their homes, their food) — Libya,
English for Libya Grade 1 (the one-word-per-letter list).

## Do it first
Count the animals you have seen this week — a cat on the
street, a bird over the roof, a dog, a fly. Say their names
in your own language. Every one of them has an English name,
and the books teach them the same way everywhere: a small set
first, with one true thing said about each.

## Then the explanation
Egypt's farm teaches the first four, and it teaches them with
the first describing pair in the whole course — **big** and
**small**:

**cow** — "The cow is big." · **horse** — "The horse is big."
· **duck** — "The duck is small." · **chicken** — "The chicken
is small." Noor and Ziad say them on the farm: "Look at the
cow!" "Wow! The cow is big!" "I love farm animals!"

Around the farm four, the home set grows: **cat, dog, goat,
rabbit, bird, fish** — Palestine's animal unit gives **goat,
rabbit, cat, kitten** (a baby cat), and **dog**; Egypt's farm
circle-the-animal work sets **bird, cat, dog** just outside the
farm gate, which is where they live: at home.

Then the wild set. Palestine adds **lion** and **zebra**;
Libya's letter list carries **elephant, monkey, zebra, duck,
insect**; and Bangladesh's food lesson needs **tiger, deer,
parrot, crow** — because its animals arrive with two facts
each:

**Where it lives** — "The monkey lives in a tree." "The crow
lives in a **nest**." "The parrot lives in a tree."

**What it eats** — the animal-to-food strips: dog — **meat**;
cow — **grass**; monkey — **fruits, nuts**; parrot —
**fruits**; tiger — **meat**; crow — **grains, insects**;
deer — **grass, leaves**. Seven animals, seven foods, whole
sentences built from a table: "The cow eats grass." "The
tiger eats meat."

So an animal in this course is never just a name. It is a name
with its size (big/small), its home, and its food — three
little facts, every one of them from the books.

## Say it to your companion
Play the quiz the books build toward: you name an animal,
your companion answers with a whole true sentence — its size,
its home, or its food: "Cow!" — "The cow is big." "Crow!" —
"The crow lives in a nest." Five animals each, then swap.
Any sentence your companion cannot make is a word to go back
and plant again.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write the animals you know in three groups: farm and home
   animals · wild animals · small creatures (insect, bird,
   fish).
2. Write Egypt's four size sentences from memory: the cow,
   the horse, the duck, the chicken.
3. Write two eats-sentences and one lives-in sentence from
   Bangladesh's table, with animals of your choosing.
4. Name the animals in your own street or village: which of
   this lesson's words do you need there?
