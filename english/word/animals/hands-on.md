# Hands-on — Animals

The lesson with real animals and real eyes, not only pictures.

## The animal watch
Go where animals are — a street, a yard, a farm, a window.
Every animal you see, say its English name out loud and one
true thing: "A dog. The dog is big." "A bird. The bird is
small." Six animals minimum. The one you cannot name is your
new word.

## Sounds and moves
With a partner: one mimes an animal — walks like a duck,
stands big like a cow, hops like a rabbit. The other names it
and says its sentence: "The duck is small." Five each, then
swap. (The mime carries the word the way Bangladesh's lessons
carry food words — the body remembers first.)

## Who eats what
Draw or lay out seven animal cards and seven food cards from
Bangladesh's strips (grass, meat, fruits, nuts, grains,
insects, leaves). Match them, then read each pair as a whole
sentence: "The monkey eats fruits and nuts." Wrong matches
get argued about out loud — the argument is the learning.

## Homes
Draw three homes: a tree, a nest, a house. Put each animal
you know into its home, saying "The ___ lives in ___" as it
goes in. Animals with no drawn home get a new drawing — the
set grows with the learner's world.
