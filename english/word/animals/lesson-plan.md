# Lesson plan — Animals

**Course:** English · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** Animals
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2, Unit 2 "Words for the
World" — the fourth lesson of the unit (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the words for animals —
the farm and home animals first, then the wider set — each
with the little the books say about it: big or small, where it
lives, what it eats.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Nouns lesson
(animal names are nouns — in the books' three-way sort they
sit with things) and the Adjectives lesson (big/small is the
first describing pair the books teach, on animals). It needs
nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds, and animals move.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Food and fruit (Bangladesh's animal
  lesson is about what animals eat — the two lessons hold
  each other up). Recommended, never forced.
- What animals DO in full sentences — their verbs, their
  sounds as a set — grows in later books and later depth;
  this lesson keeps the books' own first three facts: size,
  home, food.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the farm and home set: cow, duck, horse, chicken,
   cat, dog, goat, rabbit, bird, fish.
2. Name the wider set: lion, zebra, elephant, monkey, tiger,
   deer, parrot, crow, insect, kitten.
3. Say the size sentences the Egypt book teaches: "The cow is
   big." "The duck is small."
4. Say where an animal lives and what it eats, in
   Bangladesh's shapes: "The crow lives in a nest." "The cow
   eats grass." "The tiger eats meat."

## What you need
Pictures of animals, or real ones to point at. Paper and a
pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner names every animal they saw
   this week — at home, on the street, in a book — in their
   own language; the lesson gives the English names
   (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the farm set with Egypt's size
   dialog; the wider set from Palestine and Libya; homes and
   foods from Bangladesh (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the start of the explanation: a picture of a
     big cow standing still, then a small duck waddling past
     it, 1–2 seconds — "The cow is big. The duck is small."
     written under each — the student says both sentences as
     the animals move.
   - ADD IMAGE under the wider set: a picture of a zoo or a
     wild place with a lion, a zebra, an elephant, and a
     monkey in it — the student points and names four wild
     animals.
   - ADD AUDIO in the homes-and-foods part: a voice reading
     Bangladesh's pairs — "The cow eats grass. The tiger eats
     meat. The crow lives in a nest." — the student says each
     pair's animal name before the voice finishes the
     sentence, then repeats the whole sentence.
3. **Say it to your companion** — quiz your companion: name an
   animal, they answer with its size, its home, or its food
   in a whole sentence (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the
     farm visit in the super-simple voice (made later, when
     videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — write your animal list in three
   groups (farm/home, wild, small creatures) with one true
   sentence for three of them (lesson/lesson.md).

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- GIF — a picture of a big cow and a small duck waddling past
  → the student says "The cow is big. The duck is small." as
  they move.
- IMAGE — a picture of a zoo/wild place (lion, zebra,
  elephant, monkey) → the student points and names four wild
  animals.
- AUDIO — animal homes-and-foods sentences read aloud → the
  student names each animal early, then repeats the sentence.
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later) → the student
  visits the farm along with it.

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the
plan is the build sheet, so nothing the lesson needs gets forgotten.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes horse and cow → go back to Egypt's pictures and their
  size sentences; then the food test from Bangladesh: the cow
  eats grass and gives milk; the horse is the one people ride
  (the town's world). One true fact per animal untangles a
  pair faster than the picture alone.
- Calls every animal "it" correctly but freezes on names →
  the farm four are the floor: cow, duck, horse, chicken.
  The wider set is depth, not the gate.
- Says "The duck is big" → that is the size pair working;
  laugh, swap in the book's sentence, say it again.
- This lesson's next door: The body — the last of the world
  sets (same unit).
