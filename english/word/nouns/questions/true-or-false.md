# Nouns — true or false

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Nouns that name people (the definition and what fails it sit there) · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Nouns that name places · Sub lesson 3 — Nouns that name things · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 4 — Special names and capital letters · (Level 4) -> Sub lesson 5 — The noun test, noun or verb.

Say true or false. If it is false, say why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** A noun is a word that names a person, a place,
or a thing.
**True** — that is the lesson's one thing (Egypt p.68;
Bangladesh pp.68–71).

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** "Run" is a noun.
**False** — run names nothing; it is what a person does. A doing
word is a verb.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** "School" names a place.
**True.**

**4. (Sub lesson 3)** A cat's name is not a noun, because a cat is
alive.
**False** — animals are living things, and their names sit with
things in the books' sort. Cat is a noun.

**5. (Sub lesson 4)** We write "egypt" with a small e, like "book."
**False** — Egypt is a special name, one country only; it starts
with a capital: Egypt.

**6. (Sub lesson 4)** "Teacher" and "Karim" are both nouns.
**True** — teacher names any person who teaches; Karim is one
person's own special name. Both name a person.

**7. (Sub lesson 5)** In "Ofori danced," the word "danced" is the
noun.
**False** — Who danced? Ofori — that is the noun. Danced tells
what he did (Ghana's test).
