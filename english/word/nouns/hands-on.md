# Hands-on — Nouns

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## The naming walk
Walk slowly around your home, or your classroom. Touch each thing
as you pass it and say its name out loud: "door — noun. chair —
noun. book — noun." Ten things minimum. The touching matters:
a noun is the name of something that is really there.

## Three piles
On paper, or with real objects and three boxes, make three piles:
- **People** — name cards or photos of the people in your home.
- **Places** — draw or write the places you go: school, home,
  the shop, the park.
- **Things** — small things from the room: a cup, a key, a stone.

Say each one's name as it goes into its pile. A word that fits no
pile is not a noun — leave it out and say why.

## Special names on the door
Write your own name, your town's name, and your school's name on
cards, each starting with its capital letter. Put the cards where
you will see them tomorrow. Those are the special names — each
one belongs to one only.

## The two questions game
With a partner: one says a short sentence — "Mum cooked," "The
cat slept," "Ali drove." The other asks the two questions out
loud: "Who?" for the noun, "What did they do?" for the doing
word. Five sentences each, then swap.
