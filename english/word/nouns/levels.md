# Nouns — questions by level

Practice by level, tied to the skill number. The questions live in
the type files; this file is the by-level view. The levels are the
depths inside this one thing — a naming word — set by the pattern
across the countries' books: Egypt and Bangladesh teach the three
sorts first, the capital for special names comes with them, and
the noun-or-verb test is the deepest work the early books ask.

## Level 1 — is it a naming word?
Level 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: given a mixed
list of words, pick out every word that names a person, a place,
or a thing — and leave the doing words and describing words out.

## Level 2 — the three sorts
Level 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: sort nouns into
person, place, and thing without a miss (animals sort with
things, as the books sort them).

## Level 3 — special names
Level 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: spot the name
that belongs to one only — one person, one place — and write it
with its capital letter: Karim, Egypt, Tripoli, the Nile.

## Level 4 — noun or verb?
Level 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: in a short
sentence, find the noun with the books' own test — "Who danced?
What did Ofori do?" — and say why the doing word is not a noun.
(Telling the word kinds apart inside sentences is also the door
to the Verbs lesson, next door; the verbs themselves are taught
there, not here.)
