# Lesson plan — Nouns

**Course:** English · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** Nouns
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2, Unit 1 "Word Kinds" —
the first lesson of the unit (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a noun is a word that names
a person, a place, or a thing.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Level 1 lessons —
the learner can already read whole little words (Alphabet, Phonics,
First Words). It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the Level 1 lessons — the learner reads whole
  little words (word-families is the nearest door). Recommended,
  never forced.
- The other word kinds (verbs, adjectives, adverbs, pronouns) are
  each their own lesson, next door in this same unit.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a noun is: a word that names a person, a place, or
   a thing.
2. Sort naming words into the three sorts: person, place, thing —
   the sort Egypt's and Bangladesh's books both teach.
3. Pick the noun out of a short list of mixed words.
4. Spot a special name — one person or one place's own name — and
   write it with its capital letter (Karim, Egypt, the Nile).
5. Tell a noun from a verb with the books' own test: "Who danced?
   — Ofori" (the name) against "What did Ofori do? — danced"
   (the doing word).

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner looks around the room and names
   ten things they can see or touch, out loud; the lesson then
   shows that every one of those naming words is a noun
   (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — what a noun is; the three sorts (person,
   place, thing), each with the books' own example words; animals
   sit with things in this sort; special names and their capital
   letters; the noun-or-verb test (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the start of the explanation: a picture of three
     quick pops, one after another — a man with the word "dad"
     under him, a school with the word "school" under it, a car
     with the word "car" under it — the student says each word as
     it pops, and says which sort it is: person, place, or thing.
   - ADD IMAGE under the three sorts: a picture of a busy street
     with people walking, a library and a post office on it, cars
     and bicycles passing — the student points and names one
     person, one place, and three things in the picture.
   - ADD AUDIO in the noun-or-verb part: a voice reading mixed
     words aloud — "teacher … run … book … happy … swim …
     school" — the student claps only when the word names a
     person, a place, or a thing.
3. **Say it to your companion** — explain what a noun is, give one
   of each sort, and run the "Who danced?" test on your companion
   (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the naming
     game in the super-simple voice (made later, when videos are
     made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — name and sort the nouns of your own street;
   write three special names with their capitals (lesson/
   lesson.md).

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- GIF — a picture of three quick pops: "dad" under a man, "school"

  and its sort as it pops.
- IMAGE — a picture of a busy street with a library and a post

- AUDIO — mixed words read aloud → the student claps only on the

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the
plan is the build sheet, so nothing the lesson needs gets forgotten.

## If the learner is stuck
- Calls every word a noun → go back to the test: does the word
  NAME a person, a place, or a thing? "Run" names nothing — it is
  what a person does. That is the next lesson's word (verbs).
- Mixes place and thing → a place is somewhere you can go and be
  inside or at: a school, a library, a park. A thing is what you
  can hold, ride, or point at where it stands: a car, a book.
- Forgets the capital on a special name → there is only ONE Nile
  and only one Karim in the story; a name that belongs to one
  only wears a capital.
- This lesson's next door: Verbs — the doing words (a different
  thing, its own lesson).
