# Lesson Plan — Symbolism and Allegory

**Unit 43 — Words About Words.**

**One thing:** symbolism — things that stand for ideas — and allegory, a story whose every part carries a second meaning, as in Animal Farm and The Time Machine.

## Sub lessons

1. **Symbols that stand for ideas.** *Can-do: Name what symbols stand for: in Kafka's The Metamorphosis the vermin form, the father's apple, the sister's violin and the three lodgers each carry a meaning past themselves; in the lesson's poem, connotation does the same work in single words.*
2. **Allegory — a story inside a story.** *Can-do: Read allegory whole: Orwell's Animal Farm, where the farm stands for a state and its revolution, and Wells's The Time Machine, where the future's two peoples stand for the classes of the author's own day.*

(The gate pool for each sub lesson is every question marked with that sub lesson's number in this lesson's `questions/` files — the whole set, because no hardness ranking is written in questions/.)

## Teaching points

- Symbolism and allegory seminar (item 407): Kafka's The Metamorphosis and its named symbols (the vermin form, the apple, the sister's violin, the three lodgers), a poem read for connotation, Orwell's Animal Farm and Wells's The Time Machine as allegories.
- Stories carried as summaries and symbols only; no text transcribed.
