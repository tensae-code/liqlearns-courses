# Word building — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Word · Lesson: Word building
Built from: Barbados, Primary Language Arts Syllabi,
Classes 1 and 2 — Cambodia, ENGLISH Grade 12, Unit 5 "A
special day" (word formation) — Malaysia, English Language
Scheme of Work, Primary Year 6 (mapped evidence: the year's
planned lessons, not a pupil's-book read) — Nepal, ENGLISH
Grade 8, Unit 5 (syllable counting) — South Africa, English
Home Language workbooks (prefixes and suffixes at work).

A soft hint before you start: if you know the Word Kinds
lessons, this one will be easier — you will watch a suffix
change a word's kind in front of you. You do not need them
first. Everything below is taught on words you already
know.

## Do it first
Write the word **happy**. Now make it mean the opposite —
without using a different word. You need to ADD something.
When you add **un-** you get **unhappy**. You just built a
word. English builds thousands of its words this way, from
parts — and once you can see the parts, long words stop
being scary: they are small words with something added.

## Then the explanation
**The three parts.** A built word can have three parts:

- the **root** — the middle, carrying the main meaning.
  Barbados's Class 2 syllabus calls it the "'meaning'
  unit," and that is exactly right: happy, care, teach.
- the **prefix** — a part added at the FRONT: **un**happy,
  **re**read, **dis**honest (Barbados's Class 1 syllabus
  prints the pair itself: un-fair → unfair).
- the **suffix** — a part added at the BACK: happi**ness**,
  care**ful**, teach**er**. Barbados's Class 1 list runs s,
  es, ies, ed, ied, ing — the small endings you already use
  every day are suffixes too.

> a picture of the word UNHAPPINESS taken apart into three
> coloured blocks — UN | HAPPY | NESS — the student names
> each part: prefix, root, suffix.

**The building sets.** Cambodia's Grade 12 book teaches
word formation as a build-a-word exercise, with two full
toolboxes:

- Prefixes: **re-** (again: reread), **un-** (not: unhappy),
  **dis-** (not / opposite: dishonest), **mis-** (wrongly:
  misunderstand), **pre-** (before: pre-read), **under-**
  (too little: undercooked).
- Suffixes: **-ful** (full of: careful), **-ment** (the
  act or result: agreement), **-ian** (a person of a place
  or art: musician), **-or** (a person who does: actor).

South Africa's workbooks circle the same tools on real
pages — prefixes and suffixes **un-, re-, -ful, -less,
-ness** — and Malaysia's Year 6 plan (its Scheme of Work,
mapping the year's lessons) works **-tion / -sion** in its
making-things unit and **im- / un- / dis-** in its music
unit. A suffix often changes the word's KIND: teach (verb)
+ -er = teacher (noun); happy (adjective) + -ness =
happiness (noun). A prefix usually changes the MEANING,
not the kind: happy and unhappy are both adjectives.

**Syllables — the beats.** Every word is also built of
sound beats. Nepal's Grade 8 pronunciation work counts
them: **tea-cher** — 2. **hap-py** — 2. **un-hap-pi-ness**
— 4. Clap once per beat; put a hand under your chin and it
drops once per beat too. Barbados's syllabi name the skill
**syllabication** — splitting a word into its beats — and
it is how long built words are read: find the parts, clap
the beats, say the word.

## Say it to your companion
Build-a-word duel. Your companion names a root — CARE.
You must build two true words on it in ten seconds:
careful! careless! Then they name one — READ: reread,
reader, reading. A build only counts if you can say what
the whole word means and what the added part did. Five
roots each, then swap.

## Do It Yourself
1. Take these apart — prefix, root, suffix: unhappy,
   reread, dishonest, careless, teacher, happiness.
2. Build: put un- or dis- in front of happy, honest, kind;
   put -ful or -less at the back of care, help, use.
3. Clap and count the syllables: happy, teacher, unhappy,
   happiness, understand.
4. Meet a new word and guess it from its parts: "careful"
   you know — now guess **fearless** and **hopeless**, and
   say what -less did.
