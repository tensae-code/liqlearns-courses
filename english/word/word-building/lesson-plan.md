# Lesson plan — Word building

**Course:** English · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** Word building
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Words for the World —
the building skill itself: how words are made from parts
(CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** building and taking
apart words — prefixes at the front (un-, re-, dis-),
suffixes at the back (-ful, -less, -ness), roots in the
middle carrying the meaning, and syllables as the beats a
word is built from.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Word Kinds
(Unit 6 — a suffix can change a word's kind: teach →
teacher) and on Sounds Together (syllables are clapped
sounds first). It needs nothing else. Compound words —
two whole words joined — are the next lesson, not this one.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown changing — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The Three Parts of a Word** — prefix, root and suffix named,
   and built words taken apart. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Building with the Sets** — true words built with Cambodia's
   prefixes and suffixes and South Africa's circled set. (Was
   depth Level 2.)
3. **Syllables and New Words** — syllables counted by clapping,
   and a new built word's meaning guessed from its parts alone.
   (Was depth Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking
is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set
is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Word Kinds (Unit 6) and the syllable
  work in Sounds Together (Unit 3). Recommended, never
  forced — this lesson stands alone: root, prefix and
  suffix are taught on the word happy, whole.
- Compound words (football = foot + ball) are NOT this
  lesson; they get the next one. Here a part is not a whole
  word: un- alone is not a word.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Take a built word apart: unhappy = un- + happy;
   teacher = teach + -er; careless = care + -less.
2. Build new words with Cambodia's sets: prefixes re-, un-,
   dis-, mis-, pre-, under-; suffixes -ful, -ment, -ian,
   -or.
3. Name the root — the "meaning unit," in Barbados's Class 2
   syllabus words — and say what a front or back part does
   to it.
4. Count syllables by clapping, the way Nepal's Grade 8
   pronunciation work counts them.

## What you need
Paper strips or cards for building words. A pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner writes a word they know —
   happy — and tries to make it mean the opposite by adding
   something. The lesson then names the parts
   (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the three-part word (prefix, root,
   suffix); Cambodia's two sets; the root as meaning unit;
   syllables as the beats (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the build part: a picture of three blocks —
     UN, HAPPY, NESS — sliding together into UNHAPPINESS,
     1–2 seconds, each part a different colour — the student
     names each part as it lands.
   - ADD IMAGE under the sets: a picture of a workbench with
     word parts laid out like tools — re-, un-, dis- on one
     side, -ful, -less, -ness on the other — the student
     picks one front tool and one back tool for the root
     CARE.
   - ADD AUDIO in the syllables part: a voice saying words
     slowly with claps — tea-cher (2), hap-pi-ness (3),
     un-hap-py (3) — the student claps with the voice and
     counts.
3. **Say it to your companion** — build-a-word duel: one
   names a root, the other must build two true words on it
   (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the
     word workshop in the super-simple voice (made later,
     when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — take apart five built words and
   build five new ones (lesson/lesson.md).

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- GIF — a picture of blocks UN + HAPPY + NESS joining → the
  student names each part as it lands.
- IMAGE — a picture of a workbench of word parts → the
  student chooses front and back tools for a root.
- AUDIO — words read with claps per syllable → the student
  claps and counts with the voice.
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later) → the student
  builds along with it.

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the
plan is the build sheet, so nothing the lesson needs gets forgotten.

## If the learner is stuck
- Calls every part a whole word → the standing test: can it
  stand alone? Happy can; un- and -ness cannot. Alone-words
  that join are compounds — the next lesson.
- Builds rehappy or disness → parts have homes: prefixes
  live at the FRONT, suffixes at the BACK. The GIF's colours
  keep the homes apart.
- Cannot hear syllables → Nepal's way: hand under the chin;
  the chin drops once per syllable. Clap and chin together
  until they agree.
- This lesson's next door: Compound words (same level) —
  the other building way, whole word + whole word.
