# Lesson plan — Sports

**Course:** English · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** Sports
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 7, "Words for the World" (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the sports words —
football, basketball, swimming, running and their partners —
with the set's own pattern, Mongolia's three-way split:
play with a ball, do for exercises and martial arts, go for
the -ing sports.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Level 1 reading
and the Verbs lesson (a sport word is a doing word with a
name). It needs nothing else. If you know the verbs
already, this lesson will be easier for you — you might
try Verbs first. Recommended, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the sports set: football, basketball, volleyball,
   baseball, swimming, cycling, running, skateboarding,
   walking, yoga, dancing.
2. Use the play/do/go split without a miss: play football,
   do judo, go swimming.
3. Name the sports action verbs: throw, kick, pass,
   catch, jump, swim, bounce, block.
4. Talk about a favourite sport: what you play, how often,
   and who plays it with you.

## What you need
A ball if you have one; a yard or street to move in. Your
own games are the material.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner names every sport and
   game they play or watch, in their own language; the
   lesson then gives the English names (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the sports set; the action verbs;
   the play/do/go pattern (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE at the start of the explanation: a picture
     of children playing football, basketball and
     volleyball — the student points and names each sport
     before reading on.
   - ADD GIF in the pattern part: a picture of three
     players — one kicking a ball (play), one doing
     squats (do), one swimming (go) — the student says
     each pair, "play football," "do squats," "go
     swimming," as each one moves.
   - ADD AUDIO in the talk part: a voice answering
     Mongolia's interview question — "What sport do you
     do to keep fit?" — the student answers for
     themselves before the voice does.
3. **Say it to your companion** — interview each other
   about your sports: what, how often, with whom
   (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed —
     the sports parade in the super-simple voice (made
     later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — a sports page: your three sports,
   each with its right partner (play/do/go)
   (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Says "play swimming" → the -ing shape is the signal:
  a sport that ends in -ing travels with GO. Point at
  the ending, not the person.
- Says "do football" → a ball you can kick, throw or hit
  travels with PLAY. Martial arts and exercises take DO.
- This lesson's next door: Feelings — winning, losing and
  the faces they make.
