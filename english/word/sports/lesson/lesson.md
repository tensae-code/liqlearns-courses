# Sports — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Word · Lesson: Sports
Built from: Mexico, Projects and Readings Grade 7,
Project 6 "Technical Solutions…" — Vocabulary (p.60) —
Mongolia, English IX, Unit 3 "Sports and fitness" (p.34) —
Cambodia, English Grade 5, Chapter 4, Unit 2 "The Sports
Shop" (pp.70–73) and English Grade 8, Unit 11 "We love to
play sports" (printed pp.74–79).

*Speaker note: the explanation around the words is read in the
learner's own language; the English words themselves — the words
this lesson teaches — are never translated and are always read in
an English voice.*

## Do it first
Think of every game and sport in your life — the ones you
play, the ones you watch, the one you are best at. Act
one of them silently, right now: the kick, the throw, the
swim. Your body already knows these words. Now their
English names, and the three little partners that carry
them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Naming the Sports
Mexico's book gives the working set inside its health
project: **running, cycling, swimming, skateboarding,
volleyball, football, walking, yoga, dancing**.
Mongolia's sports unit widens the ball family: **football,
baseball, basketball, handball** — its compound-nouns
discovery shows how ball builds the names — and Cambodia
meets the set in a sports shop: **football, racket,
badminton**, with "likes playing" riding on them.

*[Slot: a picture of children playing football,
basketball and volleyball. Point and name each sport
before you read on.]*

## Sub lesson 2 — Sports in Action
Every sport is a bundle of actions, and Mongolia names
them on pictured sports: **throw, carry, kick, pass,
dribble, tackle, pitch, bat, jump, swim, paddle, bounce,
block, catch**. A sportsperson is named from them too: a
**lifter** lifts weights in the gym; a **snowboarder**; a
**skier**.

## Sub lesson 3 — Play, Do and Go
Now the set's own pattern — the one this lesson exists
for. Mongolia's discovery box sorts every sport under
three partners:

- **play** — for team sports and games with a ball:
  **play basketball, play football**.
- **do** — for exercises, compound sports and martial
  arts: **do squats, do judo**.
- **go** — for sports in **-ing**: **go cycling, go
  swimming**.

And Mongolia's interview question runs the whole system
in one breath: **"What sport do you do to keep fit? —
I do squats and lunges and go running every day. And I
play basketball twice a week."**

*[Slot: a picture of three players — one kicking a ball,
one doing squats, one swimming. Say each pair — "play
football," "do squats," "go swimming" — as each one
moves.]*

## Say it to your companion
Interview each other, in Mongolia's shape. "What sport
do you do to keep fit?" Answer with all three partners if
you can: one play, one do, one go. Add how often — every
day, twice a week. Then name your favourite sportsperson
and their sport, the way Mongolia's newspaper task asks.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write the sports set and sort it into three columns:
   play / do / go.
2. Write your keep-fit answer whole, with how often.
3. Pick one sport and write its five action verbs.
4. Write two sentences about a sportsperson you admire:
   their sport, and what they do in it.
