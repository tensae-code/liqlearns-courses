# Culture Shock

## Do it first

Move to a new country and at first everything shines — new food! new words! new friends! Then one morning everything is simply hard, and you want to go home. That rise and fall has a name: culture shock. It has stages, it is normal, and it passes. Learn its words today, and the journey will never frighten you — your own, or a newcomer's beside you.

## Sub lesson 1 — The stages of culture shock

*Very simple. Can-do: Name the stages in the book's text: the honeymoon (everything new is exciting), the crisis (everything new is hard — discrimination named in the same reading), and the recovery that follows.*

The book's text walks the journey in stages:

**The honeymoon** — the first days and weeks in a new culture. Everything is interesting: the food tastes like adventure, the language sounds like music, even the buses are fun. You are a tourist in your own new life.

**The crisis** — the shine wears off. Now the differences bite: you misunderstand and are misunderstood; the food is strange, the rules are strange, and some people are unkind — the same reading names **discrimination**, being treated badly for being different. Homesickness lives here.

**The recovery** — slowly, the new culture becomes readable. You learn the rules, find your people, and build a life that stands on both cultures. The journey's end is a **stable** life — steady, settled, your own.

Try it now: which stage — "Every meal is an adventure" / "Nobody laughs at my jokes here, and I miss home" / "I have friends, work, and two languages now"?

## Sub lesson 2 — Living through it

*Simple. Can-do: Tell and hear the real journey: a migrant's listening account (Berkly, to the USA), the word set of the move — move, stable, willpower, realise, push oneself — and the judgements empathise, sympathy and critical thinking it asks for.*

The second half of the lesson is a true-shaped listening: Berkly tells his own move to the USA. Listen for the journey's engine words: **move** (the leaving and arriving), **willpower** (the strength that keeps you going when you want to quit), **realise** (the moment a truth lands — "I realised I had to learn the language myself"), **push oneself** (to force yourself forward, kindly, when it is hard).

And the listener's words: **empathise** — feel with someone, stand inside their story; **sympathy** — feel for them, care about their pain; and **critical thinking** — judge fairly what is custom, what is unkindness, and what is simply new.

Try it now: tell a two-minute culture-shock story — invented or remembered — using move, willpower and realise.

## Say it to your companion

Read this lesson aloud to your companion. Then empathise aloud: imagine your companion's first month in a new country, and say what their honeymoon and crisis might hold.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the three stages with one sentence each from an imaginary move.
2. Explain willpower and push oneself with an example from school life.
3. Say the difference between empathise and sympathy in one sentence each.
4. Write a welcome note to a new student from another country, using two lesson words.
