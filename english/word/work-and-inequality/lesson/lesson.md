# Work and Inequality

## Do it first

Two workers do the same job. One is paid twice the other. Is that fair? What word names the gap? This lesson teaches the words for unequal work and pay — and for the papers that open job doors.

## Sub lesson 1 — Words for unequal pay

Two people work; their pay is not the same. That gap has names. **Inequality** — things not equal: pay, chances, treatment. **Poverty** — having too little to live on. **Society** — all the people living together, the whole that pay is shared inside.

With these three words a learner can say what is wrong, plainly and in English: the salary is not fair; the inequality is too big; poverty grows.

*Difficulty: Three big words, each anchored in pay.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Jobs and qualifications

Some jobs ask for a **qualification** — a paper or a skill that proves you can do the work. The lesson's hard point: a person may be able, but without the qualification the door stays closed; another may hold the paper and earn more for the same hands.

Qualification is the hinge word between school and work — it is why the book puts this lesson late in the course, when learners are about to knock on doors themselves.

*Difficulty: The hinge word — qualification — and the door it opens.*

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: is pay in your town equal? Use the words inequality and qualification in your answer.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the three big words and one true sentence with each.
2. Write about a job you want: what qualification does it ask for?
3. Ask someone at home if pay for the same job is always equal. Write their answer in English.
