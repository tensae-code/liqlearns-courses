# Lesson plan — Verbs

**Course:** English · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** Verbs
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 6, "Word Kinds" —
the second lesson of the level (CURRICULUM.md), right after Nouns.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a verb is a doing word —
it tells what a person or a thing does.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Nouns lesson (the
learner can already spot a naming word) and on the Level 1
lessons below it. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds, and a doing word is the
most natural GIF in the course: the action itself, moving.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Nouns — the naming words. A verb is found by
  the test the Nouns lesson taught ("What did Ofori do?"), so the
  two lessons lean on each other. Recommended, never forced.
- The tenses — present, past, going to — are NOT this lesson.
  They are a different thing (when the doing happens) and live at
  the Sentence stage, in Level 3. This lesson only names the
  doing word itself, plus the one change the early books already
  make to it (he/she/it adds -s).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a verb is: a word that tells what a person or a thing
   does.
2. Pick the doing words out of a mixed list of words.
3. Find the verb in a short sentence with the books' own test:
   "What did Ofori do? — danced."
4. Use the simplest sentence shape the books name — (Name) +
   (Doing word): "Yaa came." "Musa ate."
5. Change the verb for he, she, or it the way Egypt's book states
   it: for he, she, or it, we add -s — "I ride. My friend rides."

## What you need
Paper and a pencil, and room to move for the hands-on game.
Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner acts out three things they did
   this morning (woke up, ate, ran…) without words, then says
   them; the lesson shows every one of those doing words is a
   verb (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — what a verb is; the test that finds it
   ("What did … do?"); the (Name) + (Doing word) shape; the one
   change — he/she/it adds -s (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the start of the explanation: a picture of a boy
     running, 1–2 seconds, the word "run" under him, then the
     same boy jumping with "jump" under him — the student does
     each action as it pops and says the word.
   - ADD IMAGE under the (Name) + (Doing word) part: a picture of
     a sports field with children playing football, swimming,
     and running — the student names three doing words they can
     see happening in the picture.
   - ADD AUDIO in the he/she/it part: a voice reading pairs —
     "I swim — he swims. I ride — she rides. I play — it plays" —
     the student repeats each pair and listens for the little
     -s that arrives with he, she, and it.
3. **Say it to your companion** — explain what a verb is, run the
   "What did … do?" test on your companion, and build three
   (Name) + (Doing word) sentences together (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the
     doing game in the super-simple voice (made later, when
     videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — write your morning as (Name) + (Doing
   word) sentences; change three of them for he or she
   (lesson/lesson.md).

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- GIF — a picture of a boy running ("run"), then jumping
  ("jump"), 1–2 seconds each → the student does each action and
  says the word as it pops.
- IMAGE — a picture of a sports field, children playing → the
  student names three doing words happening in it.
- AUDIO — "I swim — he swims" pairs read aloud → the student
  repeats and listens for the -s.
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later) → the student
  plays the doing game along with it.

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the
plan is the build sheet, so nothing the lesson needs gets forgotten.

## If the learner is stuck
- Calls a naming word a verb ("school") → go back to the test:
  what did the school DO? Nothing — school names a place. A verb
  must answer "what did they do?"
- Cannot find the verb in a sentence → cover the words one by
  one and ask the test question at each: the word that answers
  it is the verb.
- Forgets the -s with he/she/it → Egypt's rule in its own words:
  "for he, she, or it, we add -s to the verb." One doer, one -s.
- This lesson's next door: Adjectives — the describing words
  (a different thing, its own lesson). The tenses wait at the
  Sentence stage.
