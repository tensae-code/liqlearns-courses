# Verbs — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Word · Lesson: Verbs
Built from: Bangladesh, English for Today Class 3 (the first
formal definition) — Libya, English for Libya Grade 7 (the
word-kind boxes) — Ghana, NaCCA English Curriculum Basic 1–3
(the test and the simplest sentence shape) — Egypt, English
Language Primary 2 Term 2 (the review work and the he/she/it
rule).

## Do it first
Think of three things you did this morning. Do not say them —
show them. Mime them: the waking up, the eating, the walking
or running to where you are now. Now say the words for what
your body just did: woke, ate, walked, ran. Every word that
tells what you DID is the kind of word this lesson is about.
You were already using them. Now we give them their name.

## Sub lesson 1 — Doing words, what a verb is
A **verb** is a doing word. It tells what a person or a thing
does.

Bangladesh's Class 3 book says it in its formal words: "A verb
is a word that expresses an action." Libya's books say it
shortest of all: verbs tell you what things or people do —
"go, didn't go" are their own examples.

## Sub lesson 2 — The test, what did they do
Here is the test that finds a verb anywhere. Ghana's curriculum
runs it inside a real sentence: "Ofori **danced**." Ask:
**What did Ofori do?** — danced. The word that answers that
question is the verb. Ask the other question from the Nouns
lesson — **Who** danced? — Ofori, and that one is the noun.
Two questions, two word kinds, never mixed up again: the word
that answers "who?" names; the word that answers "what did
they do?" does.

## Sub lesson 3 — The smallest sentence, name plus doing word
With a name and a doing word you already have the smallest
whole sentence there is. Ghana's curriculum names its shape:
**(Name) + (Doing word)** — "Yaa came." "Musa ate." Two words,
and the thought is complete. Egypt's sports unit is full of
doing words to fill that shape with: kick, hit, run, swim,
jump. "Ali kicked." "Hana swam."

## Sub lesson 4 — He, she, it adds -s
One more thing, and it is the only change this lesson makes to
a verb. When the doer is **he, she, or it** — one person or one
thing, not you, not me, not a group — the verb puts on a little
**-s**. Egypt's book states it as a rule in its own box: for
he, she, or it, we add -s to the verb. Watch it work on the
book's own sentence pair: "I take pictures. My friend **rides**
the camel." I ride — he rides. I swim — she swims. I play —
it plays. The -s is small, and leaving it off is the mistake
every learner makes; now you know where it lives.

(When the doing happened — now, before, later — is a different
thing. That is the tenses, and they live later, at the
Sentence stage. This lesson only names the doing word itself.)

## Say it to your companion
Explain to your companion what a verb is, in your own words.
Then say a short sentence — "The cat slept" — and ask them the
two questions: Who? What did it do? Finish by building three
(Name) + (Doing word) sentences together, and change one of
them for she, with its -s.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write your morning as three (Name) + (Doing word) sentences:
   "I woke. I ate. I ran." (Longer doing words are welcome —
   the shape is what matters.)
2. Take these words: book, swim, happy, teacher, jump, school.
   Say which ones are verbs, and prove each one with the test:
   does it tell what someone does?
3. Change these for he: I ride. I take pictures. I play.
   (He rides. He takes pictures. He plays.)
4. Find the verb in each: "Musa ate." "The bird sang." "Hana
   kicked the ball." Prove each with "What did … do?"
