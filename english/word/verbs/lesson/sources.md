**Acceptance count: taught in 18 countries (Bangladesh, Egypt,
Ghana, Libya, China, Mongolia, Indonesia, Iran, Sierra Leone, Jordan, Greece, Kazakhstan, Iraq, Ethiopia, South Africa, Syria, South Sudan, Nepal).**
Bangladesh carries the formal definition;
Libya the shortest one; Ghana the test and the smallest
sentence shape; Egypt the review work and the he/she/it -s rule;
China carries the -s rule as a whole taught system; Mongolia
carries the actions as pure pictures, with no printed words;
Indonesia names action and feeling verbs inside its describing
pattern; Iran carries the verb as a named word kind — action
against state; Sierra Leone carries action verbs picked out of
a passage, and the main/helping split.
The tenses themselves are a different thing and are NOT sourced
here — they live at the Sentence stage (Level 3, Unit 3 "Now,
Before, Later"). Jordan carries the third-person plays/doesn't work in Grade 3, and the action against state split twice — state verbs named in Grade 6, and state and action verbs set against each other in Grade 10; Greece carries the action against state split twice — the distinction itself in Grade 7, and stative verbs with their meaning-change pairs in Grade 8; Kazakhstan carries the action-verb set named as vocabulary in its Grade 3 Express book, the present simple built up affirmative, negative and questions across Grade 5 English Plus, and the third-person -s worked inside Top Stars Grade 3; Iraq carries the action verbs as a named set twice — the Grade 3 Picture Dictionary's own "Action verbs" spread of twenty doing words, and the Grade 2 can-set of actions a year earlier; Ethiopia carries verb forms, not a verb definition in the rows used here — regular and irregular verb tables in its Grade 4 and Grade 8 appendices, a list of about 150 irregular verbs in Grade 5, the transitive/intransitive split in Grade 6, and state verbs as a list in the Grade 8 appendix; South Africa carries verbs as action words in Grade 2 HL, doing words with tense-switching, finite verbs and the stative against action split in Grade 4 HL, and the infinitive and finite verbs again in Grade 5 HL; Syria carries action verbs as a set in its first year — swim, fly, jump, run with can / can't in Grade 1 — the verb named as a word kind in its Grade 12 Workbook's Parts of Speech appendix, and state verbs named beside the present continuous in the same book's Grammar Bank; South Sudan carries the verb in one place only — defined beside the noun in its Primary 4 NOTE ("A verb tell us what is being done," printed p.31, as printed), with noun/verb hunting in a passage; its primary books otherwise name no grammar, and no more is claimed here; Other closed countries join as their books
are read (see READING-QUEUE.md).
Nepal joined 2026-11-03: it carries action verbs inside first-book doing frames (Grade 1, Unit 8), the verb inside a named word-classes opening in Grade 11 (Unit 1) with transitive, intransitive and linking verbs as that book's Unit 7 grammar, and transitive/intransitive verbs again as Grade 9's Unit 11 Extra bit.
