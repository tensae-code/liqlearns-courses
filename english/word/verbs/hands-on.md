# Hands-on — Verbs

The lesson with your body, not only your eyes. A verb is the
easiest word kind to learn with your feet: you do it.

## The mime game
One person mimes a doing word — running, eating, reading,
swimming, sleeping, kicking a ball. The other says the word,
out loud, full voice: "Swim!" Five each, then swap. A word you
cannot mime and cannot picture is probably not a doing word —
that feeling is the test working in your hands.

## Your morning, acted
Act your whole morning in order, one action at a time, and say
each verb as you do it: woke — washed — ate — walked — sat.
Then say it as sentences in the books' smallest shape:
"I woke. I ate. I ran."

## Verb hunt on the move
Walk around your home or street for two minutes. Every time
you see someone or something DOING something, name the verb out
loud: the bird flies — "fly!"; mum cooks — "cook!"; the bus
stops — "stop!" Ten verbs minimum.

## The -s swap
With a partner: one says "I…" with a verb — "I swim." The other
answers for a boy or a girl — "He swims." "She swims." Ten
swaps. Listen for the little -s arriving; if it does not arrive,
say the pair again until it does.
