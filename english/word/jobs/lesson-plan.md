# Lesson plan — Jobs

**Course:** English · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** Jobs
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Words for the World (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the job words —
teacher, doctor, farmer, baker and their partners — each
job with what the person does, in the books' own shape:
"A baker bakes…" and the questions "What do you do?"
"What does she do?"
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Level 1 reading
and the Nouns and Verbs lessons (a job word names a
person; its partner names their doing). It needs nothing
else. If you know the verbs already, this lesson will be
easier for you — you might try Verbs first. Recommended,
never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Naming the Jobs** — the job set named, with "What do you do?
   — I'm a pupil"; the third-person question shapes met in its
   text are Sub lesson 2's depth. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Asking About Other People** — "What does she do?" for he and
   she, and "What do your parents do?" (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **Doings and Workplaces** — each job's doing sentence and its
   workplace, and the future frame "What would you like to be?"
   (Was depth Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking
is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set
is the gate pool.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the job set: teacher, doctor, nurse, farmer,
   seller, policeman/police officer, driver, cook,
   carpenter, shopkeeper, mechanic, chef, secretary.
2. Ask and answer: "What do you do?" — "I'm a pupil";
   "What does she do?" — "She's a teacher."
3. Say what each job does, in the -er shape the books
   build: teach → teacher, farm → farmer, bake → baker.
4. Say where each job works at Level 3: a doctor works
   in a hospital, a farmer works on a farm.

## What you need
The working people around you — your street is the
picture set.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner names the jobs of the
   people in their own family and street, in their own
   language; the lesson then gives the English words
   (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the job set; the two questions;
   the doing sentences; workplaces (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE at the start of the explanation: a picture
     of a busy street with a teacher, a doctor, a farmer
     and a seller at work — the student points and names
     each job before reading on.
   - ADD GIF in the doing part: a picture of a baker at
     an oven, bread going in and coming out, 1–2 seconds
     — the student says "A baker bakes bread" as the
     bread comes out.
   - ADD AUDIO in the question part: a voice asking
     "What do you do?" and "What does she do?" about
     pictured people — the student answers before the
     voice does.
3. **Say it to your companion** — interview each other:
   your family's jobs, then the job you would like
   (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed —
     the jobs parade in the super-simple voice (made
     later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — a jobs page for your street: five
   jobs, each with its doing sentence (lesson/lesson.md).

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- IMAGE — a picture of a busy street of workers → the
  student points and names each job.
- GIF — a picture of a baker and an oven → the student
  says "A baker bakes bread" as the bread comes out.
- AUDIO — the What-do-you-do questions read aloud → the
  student answers first.
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later) → the
  student names the jobs along with it.

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the
plan is the build sheet, so nothing the lesson needs gets forgotten.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes cook and chef → both are in the books: a cook
  cooks anywhere; a chef is the head cook of a kitchen
  (Rwanda lists both). Either names a person who cooks.
- Forgets the -er ending → build it live from the doing
  word: teach → teacher, drive → driver, sell → seller.
  Rwanda's own word-family box teaches the same move.
- This lesson's next door: School subjects — the jobs
  begin in a classroom.
