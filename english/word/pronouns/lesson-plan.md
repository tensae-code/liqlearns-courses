# Lesson plan — Pronouns

**Course:** English · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** Pronouns
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 6, "Word Kinds" —
the fifth and last lesson of the level (CURRICULUM.md), after
Nouns, Verbs, Adjectives, and Adverbs.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a pronoun is a word
that stands in for a noun — it does the name's job so the name
does not have to be said again.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Nouns lesson (a
pronoun replaces a noun, so the learner must know what a noun
is) and on the Conversation lessons, where I, you, he, and she
have been in the learner's mouth since Level 1. It needs
nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown changing — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Nouns. Recommended, never forced.
- This lesson carries the depth the curriculum names for it:
  I/you/he/she/it/we/they **and their forms**. The whose-forms
  (my, your, his, her; mine, yours) are this lesson's deepest
  level. The object forms (me, him, her, us, them) and the
  later kinds (reflexive, relative, indefinite) are named in
  the later books and wait for the Sentence stage — recorded
  in sources.md, not taught here.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a pronoun is: a word we use in place of a noun —
   "words we use instead of names," in Egypt's own title.
2. Use the seven person words and say who each one stands
   for: I (yourself), you (the one you talk to), he (a boy or
   a man), she (a girl or a woman), it (an animal or a thing),
   we (yourself and others together), they (more than one).
3. Replace a repeated name in a sentence with the right
   pronoun — the exact work Libya's WorkBook sets.
4. Use the whose-forms: my, your, his, her — and standing
   alone: mine, yours, his, hers, theirs.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner says three sentences about a
   friend using the friend's name every time, and feels how
   heavy it gets; the lesson then swaps the name for one small
   word and the sentences turn light (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — what a pronoun is; the seven person
   words, each with Egypt's own plain definition; the swap —
   replacing repeated names; the whose-forms (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the start of the explanation: a picture of a
     boy with a name card over him reading "AHMED" — the card
     flips, 1–2 seconds, and now reads "he" — the student says
     who "he" stands for, then watches a girl's card flip from
     "MARIAM" to "she" and says who "she" stands for.
   - ADD IMAGE under the seven words: a picture of a classroom
     full of people and things — a girl, a boy, a teacher, a
     dog, a group of children — the student points at each and
     says its pronoun: she, he, she, it, they.
   - ADD AUDIO in the swap part: a voice reading the heavy
     version and the light version — "Mariam is happy. Mariam
     is happy because Mariam won. — Mariam is happy. She is
     happy because she won." — the student says the pronoun
     version along with the voice the second time.
3. **Say it to your companion** — explain what a pronoun is,
   run Egypt's definitions as a guessing game ("When you talk
   about a girl or a woman — which word?"), and swap names for
   pronouns in each other's sentences (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the
     name-swap game in the super-simple voice (made later, when
     videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — rewrite a heavy paragraph light; label
   your things mine and yours (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Picks "he" for a girl or a thing → go back to Egypt's plain
  definitions, one by one: he is for a boy or a man; she for a
  girl or a woman; it for an animal or a thing. The book made
  them that plain on purpose.
- Uses the name and the pronoun together ("Ahmed he went") →
  the pronoun REPLACES the name; that is its whole job. One
  worker per job: the name or its stand-in, never both.
- Mixes my and mine → my rides with its noun (my book); mine
  stands alone (the book is mine). Ghana's pair, in the deeper
  level.
- This lesson's next door: Level 7, Words for the
  World. The object forms (me, him, them) wait at the Sentence
  stage, where the books teach them.
