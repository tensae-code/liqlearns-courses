# Hands-on — Pronouns

The lesson with people and things around you, not only paper.

## Point and swap
Stand where you can see people and things — a classroom, a
home, a street. Point at each one and say ONLY its pronoun,
fast: the girl — "she!"; the man — "he!"; the dog — "it!";
the children together — "they!"; you and I — "we!". Twenty
points minimum. Speed matters: the right stand-in should come
before the name does.

## The heavy story, the light story
Tell a partner a three-sentence story about someone you know,
the heavy way — their name in every sentence. Your partner
holds up a hand at every repeated name. Then tell it again the
light way, swapping in the pronoun. Count how many names your
partner's hand caught the second time: the goal is zero
repeats after the first sentence.

## Mine and yours
Put five of your things and five of a partner's things in a
mixed pile. Take turns picking one up and claiming it right:
"This is my pen." "This is your book." Then hold one up alone
and say whose it is with the standing-alone form: "Mine!"
"Yours!" "His!" "Hers!"

## The definition game
One person reads a definition from Egypt's page — "When you
talk about yourself and others together" — the other shouts
the word: "We!" All seven definitions, then swap. Wrong
answers get read again until the set is clean.
