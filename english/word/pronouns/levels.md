# Pronouns — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its SUB
LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure — README, "Units, Lessons and Sub
Lessons"). Practice is tied to the learner's skill number, as
before. The questions live in the type files; this file is the
by-sub-lesson view. The sub lessons are the depths inside this one thing — a word that stands in for a noun — the seven person words first (Egypt teaches them in Primary 1), then the swap, then the whose-forms.

Mapping from the old depth layers: the three old layers map one to one — old Level 1 (the seven words) = Sub lesson 1; old Level 2 (the swap) = Sub lesson 2; old Level 3 (whose-forms) = Sub lesson 3.

## Sub lesson 1 — The seven person words
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: given who is meant, choose the right word every time — I, you, he, she, it, we, they — and say Egypt's plain definition for each ("he — when you talk about a boy or a man"). (Old Level 1's pass mark.)

## Sub lesson 2 — The swap, replacing a repeated name
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: rewrite a short text replacing every repeated name with its stand-in, the way Libya's WorkBook sets it — no name repeated, no wrong stand-in, and never the name and its pronoun doing the same job together. (Old Level 2's pass mark.)

## Sub lesson 3 — Whose-forms, my and mine
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: use my, your, his, her, our, their with their nouns, and mine, yours, his, hers, theirs standing alone — and say the difference: my rides with its noun; mine stands by itself. (The object forms — me, him, us, them — and the reflexive and relative kinds are the later books' sets; they are tested at the Sentence stage, not here.) (Old Level 3's pass mark.)
