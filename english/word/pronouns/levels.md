# Pronouns — questions by level

Practice by level, tied to the skill number. The questions live in
the type files; this file is the by-level view. The levels are the
depths inside this one thing — a word that stands in for a noun —
set by the pattern across the countries' books: the seven person
words first (Egypt teaches them in Primary 1), then the swap,
then the whose-forms this lesson carries as its own depth.

## Level 1 — the seven words
Level 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: given who is
meant, choose the right word every time — I, you, he, she, it,
we, they — and say Egypt's plain definition for each ("he —
when you talk about a boy or a man").

## Level 2 — the swap
Level 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: rewrite a
short text replacing every repeated name with its stand-in,
the way Libya's WorkBook sets it — no name repeated, no wrong
stand-in, and never the name and its pronoun doing the same
job together.

## Level 3 — whose-forms
Level 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: use my,
your, his, her, our, their with their nouns, and mine, yours,
his, hers, theirs standing alone — and say the difference:
my rides with its noun; mine stands by itself.
(The object forms — me, him, us, them — and the reflexive and
relative kinds are the later books' sets; they are tested at
the Sentence stage, not here.)
