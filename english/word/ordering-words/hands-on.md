# Hands-on — Ordering words

The lesson with real lists and a real book open in front
of you.

## The deep sort — for real
Write these ten words on ten slips: dark, deep, doctor,
dog, door, terrace, terrible, term, test, thank.
Shuffle. Stand them in dictionary order, saying the
deciding letter aloud at every tie: "d's tie — second
letters: a, e, o… o's tie — third letters decide."
Time yourself; race a partner; the depth is the game.

## The index hunt (India's and Rwanda's way)
Open any textbook at its index (or its contents page —
Rwanda's P.5 Unit 3 practises both on specimens,
printed p.69). Pick three topics. For each: say the
letter you start at, the tied entries you pass, and the
page the index sends you to. Then check one: does that
page really carry the topic? An index keeps its
promises or it is not an index.

## Take one entry apart
On any dictionary page — India's Class 6 book
reproduces a real one: term, test, terrace, terrible,
terribly, terrify, terror, territory, than, that, thank
— pick the entry for "term". Write out: its headword;
every meaning the entry gives; one word that may follow
it. Do the same for "terrible". Two entries, taken
apart whole — that is the entry skill.

## My class dictionary sheet
Like India's class dictionary work, build one sheet for
your own room: five words from this week, in deep
dictionary order, each with its meaning in your own
words and the dictionary's transcription where your
dictionary gives one (Rwanda's dictionary tasks work
with transcriptions, pp.18–19). Your sheet follows the
same rule as the big book's — because it is the same
machine.
