# Ordering words — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Word · Lesson: Ordering words
Built from: Barbados, Primary Language Arts Syllabi,
Classes 1–4 (alphabetising by 1st and 2nd letter, Class
1 body, printed p.36; reaching the 3rd letter at Class
2 and the 4th at Class 3, Scope and Sequence 3.5.1) —
syllabus evidence: Barbados has no government pupil
textbook for Language Arts — India, Honeysuckle (Class
6), Unit 5 (a real dictionary page reproduced and
worked) and Beehive (Class 9), Unit 9 ("How to look at
an index") — Rwanda, English Learner's Book for Rwandan
Primary Schools, P.5 (Revised Edition 2019), Unit 2
"Using Reference Materials" (printed p.43) and Unit 3
"Using Tables of Contents and Indexes" (printed p.69).

This lesson is ONE thing only: ordering words DEEP —
past the first letter, to the second, third and fourth
letter and beyond — and knowing the parts of the entry
you land on. Finding a word with guide words is Using
the dictionary, the lesson beside this one; the
first-letter rule is restated whole below, so this page
stands alone.

A note on voices: the explanations in this lesson can be
read to you in your own language with the speaker button.
The words being ordered — dark, deep, doctor and the
rest — are the TARGET: they are never translated, and
the reader says them in the English voice, then comes
back to yours.

If you have done Using the dictionary first, this lesson
will be easier for you — you might try it first. You do
not have to: everything you need is on this page.

## Do it first
Stand these five words in dictionary order: **doctor,
deep, dark, dog, door**. The first letter is easy — all
d. Now what? If you stalled, you have found this
lesson's whole problem: the first letter ties, and
something deeper must decide. Hold the five words; the
rule that settles them is the next thing on this page.

Media slot — a picture of the five word cards — dark,
deep, doctor, dog, door — shuffling into line while
their letters pop one by one. What you do: say which
letter settled each card as it lands.

## Sub lesson 1 — Ordering Words by the Second Letter

**The rule, whole.** Words stand by their first letter.
When the first letters tie, the **second** letter
decides. When the second letters tie, the **third**
decides. Then the fourth, the fifth — the rule never
stops; you simply walk along the word until one letter
differs, and that letter is the judge.

**Barbados's ladder.** Barbados's syllabi set this as
school work with named rungs (syllabus evidence):
alphabetising **by 1st and 2nd letter** at Class 1
(Class 1 body, printed p.36); the order reaches the
**3rd letter at Class 2** and the **4th letter at
Class 3** (Scope and Sequence 3.5.1, row for
alphabetising in the shared grid). Each year, one
letter deeper. Work the five d-words up the ladder:

- First letters: d, d, d, d, d — tie.
- Second letters: **a** (dark), **e** (deep), **o**
  (doctor, dog, door) — dark, then deep, stand out.
- Third letters inside the o's: **c** (doctor), **g**
  (dog), **o** (door) — and the line is settled:
  **dark, deep, doctor, dog, door**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ordering Words to the Fourth Letter, and the Index

**The index is the same machine.** An index orders
TOPICS the way a dictionary orders words, and two
countries teach it as its own skill. India's Class 9
book carries a section named **HOW TO LOOK AT AN
INDEX**: a real subject index (from a Living World
History book) is reproduced in the lesson, questions
are answered from it, and two more indexes are shown
beside it (Amartya Sen's, and French's *Index*).
Rwanda's P.5 book practises the pair together — **Using
Tables of Contents and Indexes** (Unit 3, printed
p.69), worked on specimen examples (printed pp.69–71):
the contents tells you where a part of THIS book
stands; the index tells you where a TOPIC is talked
about, anywhere in the book, in the same deep letter
order.

Media slot — a picture of an open book's index page,
its entries standing in deep letter order, one entry
circled. What you do: say the first three letters of
the circled entry and the two entries it stands
between, and why it stands exactly there.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Dictionary Entry, Taken Apart

**The entry — what you land on.** India's Class 6 book
reproduces a real dictionary page and works it (the
term family): **term, test, terrace, terrible,
terribly, terrify, terror, territory, than, that,
thank**. The skills the book works on that page are
the parts of an entry, one by one:

- **The headword** — the word itself, standing in its
  ordered place. (Order the page's terr- words by the
  rule and feel the depth: terrace, terrible, terrify,
  territory, terror — settled at the fifth and sixth
  letters.)
- **The meanings** — plural. The book asks: find the
  word meaning the same as **ghastly**, and write its
  **two meanings**. One headword can hold more than
  one meaning; read them all.
- **The exact word for the exact job** — find a word
  for a part of the school year (**term**) and one for
  an examination (**test**): the entry answers the
  question you actually brought.
- **The word family on the page** — list all the terr-
  words: the order groups a family together where you
  can see it whole.
- **What may follow** — write five words that may
  follow "that": entries teach how a word is USED, not
  only what it means.
- **Your meaning against the book's** — write your own
  meaning of **thank**, then the dictionary's: the
  entry is the judge you check yourself against.

**The entry also tells you how to SAY the word.**
Rwanda's P.5 book sets dictionary work through its
units — look-up tasks with a dictionary and thesaurus
in "Using Reference Materials" (Unit 2, printed p.43;
tasks at printed p.45) — and its dictionary tasks give
**IPA transcriptions** for the words in hand (the job
words of Unit 1, printed pp.18–19; transcription work
again at printed p.33 and in Test 2.2, printed p.50).
Pronunciation is a part of the entry, printed beside
the headword: when a dictionary is near, the saying of
a word is findable too.

## Say it to your companion
Take six words that all start with the same letter —
your companion picks them from any page. Order them
aloud, and at every tie say the deciding letter out
loud ("…'s tie at the second letter; the third letter
decides: c before g"). Then swap: their six, your ears
as the check.

## Do It Yourself
1. Put this list in deep dictionary order: hospital,
   home, horse, house, terrace, terrible, term, test,
   thank, that.
2. In any book with an index, find two topics and write
   down the entries they stood between — the neighbours
   the order gave them.
3. Copy one short entry whole and label its parts:
   headword, pronunciation (if your dictionary gives
   one), and every meaning it holds.
4. Write your own meaning of "thank" first — then the
   dictionary's, the way India's book sets it.
