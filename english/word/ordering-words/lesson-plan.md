# Lesson plan — Ordering words

**Course:** English · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** Ordering words
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 8, "Using the Dictionary" —
the second lesson of the level (CURRICULUM.md;
CATCH-UP-PLAN.md places ordering words inside Level 8).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** alphabetising
DEEP — when the first letters tie, the second letter
decides; when the second ties, the third; when the
third ties, the fourth — and what the parts of a
dictionary entry are, so the word you fought to find
pays you when you land on it.
**This lesson's own level:** Using the dictionary
(Level 8's first lesson) teaches the finding machine —
letter order, guide words, the entry. This lesson is
the deep drill of that machine: ordering to the fourth
letter, the index as the same machine in another book,
and the entry taken apart piece by piece. A learner
can start HERE: the first-letter rule is restated whole
on this page.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown changing — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The alphabet (the order itself) and
  Using the dictionary (the finding machine this lesson
  drills deep). Recommended, never forced.
- Guide words and the fence belong to Using the
  dictionary; here they appear only as the place the
  ordered list lands you.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Alphabetise to the second letter (Barbados, Class 1),
   the third letter (Class 2) and the fourth letter
   (Class 3) — Barbados's own ladder, Scope and
   Sequence 3.5.1.
2. Use the same order in an index: India's Class 9 book
   teaches HOW TO LOOK AT AN INDEX from a real subject
   index, and Rwanda's P.5 practises tables of contents
   and indexes on specimens (Unit 3, printed p.69).
3. Take an entry apart on a real dictionary page —
   India's Class 6 reproduction (term, test, terrace,
   terrible, terribly, terrify, terror, territory, than,
   that, thank): headword, the word's meanings (one word
   can hold two — ghastly's pair), and the words that
   may follow a word.
4. Use a dictionary's pronunciation: Rwanda's P.5 book
   sets dictionary tasks giving IPA transcriptions for
   its job words (printed pp.18–19).

## What you need
Any dictionary or index — a book's back pages will do.
Paper and a pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner orders five d-words
   (doctor, deep, dark, dog, door) by the first letter
   alone, stalls, and discovers the tie (lesson/
   lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the top of the lesson: a picture of five
     word cards — dark, deep, doctor, dog, door —
     shuffling into line while their second letters pop,
     then their third, 1–2 seconds — the student says
     which letter settled each card as it lands.
2. **The explanation** — the ladder (Barbados: 1st–2nd,
   3rd, 4th letter); the index (India Class 9; Rwanda
   P.5 Unit 3); the entry's parts on India's real page;
   dictionary pronunciation tasks (Rwanda) (lesson/
   lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE under the ladder: a picture of the words
     term and test standing side by side with their
     third letters underlined — r against s — the student
     says what decided term before test (the third
     letter), then orders terrace and terrible, which
     tie until the fifth letter (a before i).
   - ADD AUDIO in the entry part: a voice reading the
     entry for "term" off India's reproduced page — the
     word, its meanings — the student names each part
     as the voice gives it.
3. **Say it to your companion** — order a six-word list
   aloud, letter by letter where it ties; the companion
   checks (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed —
     the deep ordering race in the super-simple voice
     (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served
   at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — order a ten-word list to the
   third letter, find one word in a real index, and take
   one entry apart by hand (lesson/lesson.md).

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- GIF — a picture of word cards shuffling into deep
  order → the student names the deciding letter.
- IMAGE — a picture of India's dictionary-page words in
  a row → the student says what decided each place.
- AUDIO — the "term" entry read aloud → the student
  names each part as it comes.
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later) → the student
  orders each list along with it.

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the

## If the learner is stuck
- Orders by the first letter and stops → the ladder is
  the fix: read the tied words letter by letter, aloud,
  until one letter differs. That letter is the judge.
- Thinks an index is a different machine → it is the
  same order wearing another coat (India's Class 9
  lesson and Rwanda's Unit 3 both treat contents and
  index as one finding skill).
- Copies the first meaning and stops → India's page is
  the warning: ghastly holds TWO meanings on the page;
  an entry is a small list, read whole.
- This lesson completes the deep drill of Level 8.
  Next: Level 9, Numbers, Days, and Time.
