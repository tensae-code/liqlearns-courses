# Prepositions of Movement — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Word · Lesson: Prepositions of Movement
Built from: Albania, Akelius A2 Teacher's Guide, Chapter 38
"Nature" — its grammar focus, prepositions of movement and
place: around, into, out of, through (printed p.48), and
the chapter's own example, "walking through the forest."
Teacher-guide evidence: the guide prints the platform
course's focus and example sets; no pupil's book is held.

## Do it first
Stand up. Walk **through** the doorway — in one side, out
the other. Now walk **around** a chair — a full circle,
back where you started. Four steps of your body just did
what this lesson names. Some small words do not tell where
a thing *is* — they tell **how it moves**. The nature
chapter of Albania's book names four of them as its grammar
focus: **around, into, out of, through**. This lesson takes
them one pair at a time.

## Sub lesson 1 — Through and around
**Through** is the crossing word. "Walking **through** the
forest" — the chapter's own example. You go in among the
trees, you travel inside the forest, and you come out the
far side. Through always passes *inside* a thing and out
again: through the door, through the trees, through the
water as a swimmer crosses a river.
**Around** never enters. Walk **around** the lake and you
stay on its edge, in a circle, and finish where you began.
The path **around** a mountain keeps the mountain beside
you the whole way. Test the pair with your feet: through
cuts across the middle; around keeps the outside.

## Sub lesson 2 — Into and out of
**Into** you met in its own lesson: movement whose end is
the inside — step **into** the forest, dive **into** the
water. Its facing partner is **out of**: movement whose
start is the inside and whose end is the outside — climb
**out of** the water, walk **out of** the forest into the
sun. Say the pair as one chunk each: *into the water — out
of the water*. Mind the small piece: out of is one word
wearing two parts, and the "of" may not be dropped —
"out the water" is the half-word learners say, and this
lesson does not let it pass.

## Sub lesson 3 — The movement words on a walk
Now walk the chapter's own country with all four. "We hike
**through** the forest. The path climbs **around** the
lake. At the far shore we run **into** the water, swim, and
climb **out of** it again." Four movements, four words,
none of them swappable: through crossed the forest's
inside, around circled the lake, into entered the water,
out of left it. When you choose, ask what the moving thing
does: Does it cross an inside? Through. Does it circle?
Around. Does it enter? Into. Does it leave an inside?
Out of. That is the whole choosing test, and it works on
any walk you will ever tell.

## Say it to your companion
Explain the four words to your companion in your own
words — no book language, your own. Then take turns: one
walks a small course in the room — through the doorway,
around the chair, into a marked square on the floor and
out of it — while the other narrates every move with its
word, out loud, as it happens.

## Do It Yourself
1. Finish with the right movement word: "We walk ___
   the forest and come out the far side." "The road goes
   ___ the mountain, never over it." "She dives ___ the
   sea." "The fish jumps ___ the water and falls back."
2. Tell a true walk of yours — to school, to the shop, to
   a friend's house — in four sentences, using through,
   around, into and out of once each. Say it out loud.
3. The trap pair: say why "walking around the forest" and
   "walking through the forest" tell two different walks.
