# True or false — Prepositions of Movement

Sub-lesson markers: every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to — Sub lesson 1 — Through and around · Sub lesson 2 — Into and out of · Sub lesson 3 — The movement words on a walk. This lesson was built new with its sub lessons (Albania re-verification sweep merge, 2026-11-08), so its markers are original — there are no older depth layers to map from.

Say true or false. If it is false, say why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** "Walking through the forest" means walking in among the trees and out the far side.
**True** — the chapter's own example of through (Albania, Akelius A2 Guide, Chapter 38, printed p.48).

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** Around and through tell the same path.
**False** — through cuts across the inside; around stays outside and circles back to the start.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Into and out of are a facing pair.
**True** — into enters an inside; out of leaves one.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** "He climbed out the water" is the full, correct form.
**False** — the of may not be dropped: "out of the water."

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** One walk can need all four movement words.
**True** — through the forest, around the lake, into the water, out of it again.

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** To choose a movement word, ask where the thing is resting.
**False** — movement words are chosen by what the moving thing does: crossing, circling, entering or leaving. Resting place is the other lesson's question.
