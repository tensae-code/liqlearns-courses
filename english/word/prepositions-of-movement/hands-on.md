# Hands-on — Prepositions of Movement

The lesson with your body, not only your eyes.

## The four-walks course
Set a small course in the room or yard: a doorway (or two
chairs close together), a chair standing alone, and a
chalked or taped square on the floor. Walk it in this
order, saying each word as your body does it:
1. Walk **through** the doorway — in one side, out the
   other.
2. Walk **around** the chair — a full circle, back to the
   start.
3. Step **into** the square.
4. Step **out of** the square.
Then your partner walks it while you narrate. Five rounds
each; the narrator's word must land at the exact step it
names.

## Draw the two paths
On paper, draw a forest (a blob of trees) and a lake (a
circle). Draw one walker's path cutting through the forest
and out the far side — label it "through the forest." Draw
another path circling the lake — label it "around the
lake." Under each, write the sentence the picture tells.

## Narrate the room
One person closes their eyes (or turns away). The other
walks one quiet move — through the doorway, around the
table, into the corner and out of it. The watcher (eyes
open on the repeat) must say the move with its word before
it ends. Swap after each move.
