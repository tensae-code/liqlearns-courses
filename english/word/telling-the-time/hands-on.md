# Hands-on — Telling the time

The lesson on a clock you can touch.

## Make a clock (Mongolia's game)
Cut a circle from card or a paper plate. Write the 12 hour
numbers around it. Pin two hands at the centre — a short one
and a long one, different colours the way Bangladesh prints
them (short red hour hand, long blue minute hand). This clock
is the lesson's tool for a week.

## Set and say
A partner calls a time — "Seven o'clock!" "Half past four!"
"Quarter to nine!" — you set your clock's hands, hold it up,
and say the time whole. Ten calls, then swap roles. Miss one,
set it twice.

## The day line
On a long strip of paper, draw your day as a line of clocks:
waking, eating, leaving, working, coming home, sleeping. Six
clocks, hands set, the time written under each in words. Read
the strip aloud as one story: "I wake up at…"

## Real-clock checks
Three times a day for three days, catch a real clock (wall,
phone, watch). Before you look, guess the time in English.
Then look, and say the true time whole, with a.m. or p.m.
The guessing is what builds the eye.
