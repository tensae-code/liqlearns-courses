# Lesson plan — Telling the time

**Course:** English · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** Telling the time
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 9 — Numbers, Days, and Time — the third and last lesson of the unit
(CURRICULUM.md). Building it completes Unit 9.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** reading a clock and
saying the time — o'clock first, then the halves and quarters
— with the question "What's the time?" and the day's routine
told in times.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Numbers (a clock is
counting by fives wearing a circle) and Days and months (a
time belongs to a day). It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds, and clock hands move:
this lesson is GIF country.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **O'Clock Times** — the two hands named, "What's the time?" asked, and any o'clock time said whole. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **The Day in Times** — a.m. split from p.m. by the book's rule, midday and midnight placed right, and a day's routine told in at-times. (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **Minutes, Past and To** — minutes counted by fives, and half past, quarter past, ten past and quarter to said for any clock shown. (Was depth Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Numbers, then Days and months.
  Recommended, never forced.
- The routine sentences themselves ("I wake up at 7") are
  present-simple work for the Sentence level; here the times
  ride inside them as Egypt and Bangladesh print them, but
  the lesson's one thing is the TIME, not the tense.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the clock's two hands and say any o'clock time:
   "What's the time?" — "It's six o'clock."
2. Split the day right: a.m. from midnight to midday, p.m.
   from midday to midnight — 12 o'clock is midday AND
   midnight (Bangladesh's language focus).
3. Tell a day in times, with at: "I wake up at 7 a.m. School
   begins at 7 a.m. Mita gets up at six o'clock."
4. Count minutes by fives around the clock and say the
   halves and quarters: half past seven, quarter past,
   quarter to, ten past ten (Mongolia's depth).

## What you need
A clock with hands — real, drawn, or the make-a-clock game
from Mongolia's book. Paper, a pencil, two sticks for hands.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner tells their day in their own
   language as a line of times: woke at…, ate at…, left at…;
   the lesson gives the English clock (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the two hands; o'clock; the a.m./p.m.
   rule; at with times; minutes by fives, past and to
   (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the clock part: a picture of a clock whose
     long hand sweeps one full turn in 1–2 seconds while the
     short hand creeps from 6 to 7, the hour numbers popping
     as the long hand passes — the student calls each o'clock
     as the hands land.
   - ADD IMAGE under a.m./p.m.: a picture of one day as a
     strip — midnight moon, morning sun rising, midday sun
     high, evening sun low, midnight moon again — with a.m.
     written under the first half and p.m. under the second —
     the student points and says which half three pictured
     times live in.
   - ADD AUDIO in the past/to part: a voice saying times
     slowly — "half past seven… quarter past eight… ten past
     ten… quarter to nine" — the student sets a paper clock's
     hands to match each one before the next is spoken.
3. **Say it to your companion** — ask and answer "What's the
   time?" on clocks your companion sets, then tell your whole
   day in times (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — one
     day told in times, in the super-simple voice (made later,
     when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — draw four clocks for four moments of
   your day and write each time in words (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Reads the hour hand as minutes → Bangladesh names the
  hands by their look: the SHORT hand is the hour, the LONG
  hand is the minutes. Short tells the big thing slowly;
  long runs.
- Says "It's six hour" → o'clock is one fixed shape: number
  + o'clock, nothing between. The books say and complete
  o'clock times until the shape is automatic.
- a.m./p.m. flips → anchor at the two 12s: midnight starts
  a.m., midday starts p.m. Meals help: breakfast is a.m.,
  dinner is p.m., in every book's day.
- Past/to confusion → Mongolia's clock is cut in two halves:
  minutes climb the PAST side (after the hour) and fall down
  the TO side (before the next hour); half past stands at
  the bottom between them.
- Building this lesson completes Level 9. The Word
  title's work stands whole: kinds, world,
  dictionary, numbers-days-time.
