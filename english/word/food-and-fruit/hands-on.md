# Hands-on — Food and fruit

The lesson at a real table and a real market, not only on the
page.

## Name the meal
At every meal today, before you eat, name the meal in English
and name everything on your plate: "Breakfast. Egg, bread,
milk." Three meals, three namings. The food you cannot name is
your new word to look for in the lesson's lists.

## The fruit stall game
At a market, a shop, or with fruit at home: one person points
at a fruit and asks "What's this?" The other answers whole:
"It's a banana." "It's an orange." Ten fruits. Then the
harder round — point at a bunch: "What are these?" "They're
grapes."

## Like, don't like
Lay out (or picture) six foods. Sort them into two piles with
your hands — like, don't like — saying the book's exchange for
each one: "I like mango." "I don't like fish." Then a partner
asks you properly: "Do you like guava?" Answer in the book's
whole shapes, never one word alone.

## Draw the pyramid
On paper, draw Bangladesh's pyramid: a wide base, floors
going up to a small top. Put the grains at the base, fruits
and vegetables on their floor above it, and two foods of yours
on their right floors. Say why as you place them: "Fruits
have vitamins."
