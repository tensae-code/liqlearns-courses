# Food and fruit — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Word · Lesson: Food and fruit
Built from: Egypt, English Language Primary 1 Term 2, Unit 1
"My Week" (the meals and the breakfast plate) — Bangladesh,
English for Today Class 2 (the like-exchange over the food
boxes) and Class 5 (the Food Pyramid) — Palestine, English
for Palestine Grade 1 Part 2, Unit 16 "In my country" (the
fruits that grow there).

## Do it first
Think of everything you ate today. The morning food. The
middle-of-the-day food. The evening food. Say the names in
your own language. English sorts a day's food the same way —
into three meals — and every food you named has an English
name waiting in this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Meals and First Foods
The three meals, from Egypt's book: **breakfast** (the
morning meal), **lunch** (the middle meal), **dinner** (the
evening meal) — with the two doing words that go with them,
**eat** and **drink**.

Egypt draws the breakfast plate for you: **egg, cheese,
beans, and a glass of milk**. And its Ali answers the book's
big food question: "What do you eat for breakfast?" — "I eat
**bread** and **eggs**." The wider plate from the other books
adds **honey, fish, chicken, rice** — Bangladesh's food boxes
hold fruits, vegetables, and egg / honey / fish / chicken /
milk side by side.

## Sub lesson 2 — Fruits and Likes
Now the fruits. Palestine's book teaches the ones that grow
in its own country: **orange, lemon, grape, apple, banana,
fig, olive** — each with its question and answer: "What's
this?" "It's a **fig**." "It's an **olive**." Hear the two
shapes: **a** fig, **an** olive — an before a vowel sound.
And many grapes get the plural question: "What are these?"
"They're **grapes**." Bangladesh's fruit labels add **a
guava, a banana, an orange, a papaya** — and the fruit its
children name first: **mango**.

With foods named, the books ask their second question —
about YOU. Bangladesh's exchange, over the picture boxes:
"Do you like guava?" "Yes, I do." Or the honest answer: "No,
I don't. I like mango." Short, whole, and yours: like + the
food word is all it takes.

## Sub lesson 3 — Food in Whole Answers
One step deeper, from Bangladesh's Class 5: the **Food
Pyramid** sorts food into floors — grains at the wide base,
then **fruits and vegetables** (which "have vitamins and help
eyes"), then the rest, up to the small top. Its fruit and
vegetable set widens this lesson's: **pineapples, bananas,
cucumbers, lettuce, carrots, tomatoes**. Food words are not
just names — the books already sort them into the foods that
build you and the foods that visit.

## Say it to your companion
Ask your companion the two questions about real life: "What
do you eat for breakfast?" and "Do you like mango?" — then
trade. Finish at the fruit stall: hold up (or picture) a
fruit and ask "What's this?" until your companion can answer
"It's a…" and "It's an…" without stopping.

## Do It Yourself
1. Draw your three plates of today — breakfast, lunch,
   dinner — and label every food on them in English.
2. Write your fruit list: the fruits you like, in the book's
   shape: "I like mango. I like grapes."
3. Answer in whole sentences: What do you eat for breakfast?
   Do you like guava? Do you like fish?
4. Ask "What's this?" about five foods in your kitchen, and
   answer: "It's a…" / "It's an…" / "They're…"
