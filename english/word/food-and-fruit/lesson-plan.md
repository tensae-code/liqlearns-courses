# Lesson plan — Food and fruit

**Course:** English · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** Food and fruit
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 7, "Words for the World" — the third lesson of the level (CURRICULUM.md), after
The home and The town.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the words for food —
the three meals, the foods on the plate, and the fruits — with
the two questions the books ask about them: what do you eat,
and what do you like.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Nouns lesson
(food words are nouns) and the world-set lessons before it.
It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown changing — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The home (the kitchen and dining room are
  where this lesson's words live). Recommended, never forced.
- Counting food (some/any, much/many) is NOT this lesson — a
  different thing, taught by the later books with the nouns'
  countable depth. This lesson only names the foods and asks
  the books' two questions.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the three meals — breakfast, lunch, dinner — and the
   first foods: egg, bread, cheese, beans, milk, honey, fish,
   chicken, rice.
2. Name the fruits the books teach: banana, orange, apple,
   mango, grapes, lemon, guava, papaya, fig, olive.
3. Answer Egypt's question: "What do you eat for breakfast?"
   — "I eat bread and eggs."
4. Run Bangladesh's exchange: "Do you like guava?" — "Yes, I
   do." / "No, I don't. I like mango."
5. Ask "What's this?" about a fruit and answer "It's a fig."
   "It's an olive." — and for many: "They're grapes."
   (Palestine's own shapes.)

## What you need
Real food or pictures of it. Paper and a pencil for the plate
step.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner names everything they ate
   today, meal by meal, in their own language; the lesson then
   gives the English names (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the three meals with Egypt's
   breakfast plate; the food words; the fruit set from
   Palestine and Bangladesh; the two questions (what do you
   eat / do you like) (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the start of the explanation: a picture of an
     empty plate that fills, 1–2 seconds — egg, bread, cheese,
     and a glass of milk land on it one by one, each name
     popping as its food lands — the student says each food as
     it lands.
   - ADD IMAGE under the fruits: a picture of a market fruit
     stall — bananas, oranges, apples, mangoes, grapes, lemons
     in piles — the student points and names six fruits.
   - ADD AUDIO in the like-exchange part: a voice asking "Do
     you like guava?" and answering both ways — "Yes, I do." /
     "No, I don't. I like mango." — the student answers aloud
     for themselves before the voice does, three fruits in a
     row.
3. **Say it to your companion** — ask your companion the two
   questions about real food: what they eat for breakfast, and
   which fruits they like (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the
     market game in the super-simple voice (made later, when
     videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — draw your three plates of today and
   label every food; write your like-list (lesson/lesson.md).

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- GIF — a picture of a plate filling up (egg, bread, cheese,
  milk landing one by one with their names) → the student says
  each food as it lands.
- IMAGE — a picture of a market fruit stall → the student
  points and names six fruits.
- AUDIO — "Do you like guava?" asked and answered both ways →
  the student answers for themselves first, three fruits.
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later) → the student
  plays the market game along with it.

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the
plan is the build sheet, so nothing the lesson needs gets forgotten.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes lunch and dinner → the day sorts them: breakfast in
  the morning, lunch in the middle of the day, dinner at the
  end. Egypt teaches them inside a week, with the days around
  them.
- Says "a orange" → Palestine's pair teaches the fix inside
  the fruit set itself: "It's a fig. It's an olive." — an
  before a vowel sound. (The full a/an rule has its own home
  later; here the pair is enough.)
- Cannot name a food from home → it may not be in the books'
  sets; name the nearest one that is (the sets grow as more
  countries' books are read — that is the course's own rule).
- This lesson's next door: Animals (same level).
