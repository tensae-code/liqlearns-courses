# Study and University Words

## Do it first

A young student starts at university. Try to talk about her
first weeks. Try to say:

- "First she does a ___ course — a course that gets her ready."
- "Her ___ is science — the part of study she chose."
- "She chose one ___ subject — a subject she may take or not
  take."

The words you were missing — foundation course, field,
optional — are this lesson: the words of starting university.

## Sub lesson 1 — Starting at university

A **foundation course** is a course that gets you ready for
university study — you take it before the main study begins.

Your **field** is the part of study you choose: "Her **field**
is medicine." "His **field** is engineering."

An **optional** subject is one you may choose or not choose:
"This subject is **optional** — you can take it, or leave it."

Say it whole: "Before my main study, I take a foundation
course. My field is history. I take one optional subject —
music."

*Difficulty: first steps — say each word inside the student's start, never alone.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Student life

To **participate** is to take part: "She **participates** in
class." "They **participate** in a sports team."

**Like-minded** people think in the same way as you: "At
university, he met **like-minded** friends — friends who love
science like him."

A **routine** is the things you do every day, in the same
order: "Her **routine**: she gets up at six, studies, then goes
to class."

Write a student's day with the words: one sentence with
participate, one with like-minded, one with routine.

*Difficulty: building — the life words join the start words in one student's story.*

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion about an imaginary student: their
foundation course, their field, one optional subject, and
their daily routine. Your companion asks: "Does she
participate?" — answer with a whole sentence.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write four sentences about a student starting university,
   using foundation course, field, optional and routine.
2. Write your own routine for a school day — five lines, in
   order.
3. Find one like-minded person — someone who likes what you
   like — and write one sentence about what you both like.
