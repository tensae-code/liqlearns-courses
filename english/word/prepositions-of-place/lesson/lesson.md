# Prepositions of Place

## Do it first

Here is a book. Watch: I put it ON the table. Now IN the box. Now UNDER the chair. Three little words told you where it went: on, in, under. Place words are tiny, but without them we cannot say where anything is. Try it — tell me where your pencil is right now.

## Sub lesson 1 — On, in, under

**On** — the book is ON the table. It touches the top.

**In** — the book is IN the box. The box holds it all around.

**Under** — the book is UNDER the chair. The chair is over it.

Bolivia's book says it with a pet: “The cat is on the chair. The dog is in the house. The bird is under the tree.”

Say it with me: on — top. In — inside. Under — below.

## Sub lesson 2 — Up and down

Two more place words from the same book.

**Up** — high. “The bird is up in the tree.” “The ball went up.”

**Down** — low. “The cat is down on the floor.” “The ball came down.”

Up and down answer “which way?” and “how high?”. Point up. Point down. Easy — and you will use them every day.

## Sub lesson 3 — The fuller set — in front of, next to, behind

The teacher's book for Year 1 gives the fuller set.

**In front of** — before a thing, facing it. “The dog is in front of the house.”

**Next to** — at the side of a thing, close. “The cat is next to the dog.”

**Behind** — at the back of a thing, hidden by it. “The hen is behind the tree.”

With on, in and under, you now have six place words. You can describe almost any picture in English.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion three sentences about your room: “My book is on the table. My bag is next to the chair. My shoes are under the bed.” They draw what you say — then check the drawing against the truth!

## Do It Yourself

Hide three things in your room. Write one true sentence for each with a place word from this lesson (on, in, under, up, down, in front of, next to, behind). Read them to someone at home and let them find the things from your sentences alone.
