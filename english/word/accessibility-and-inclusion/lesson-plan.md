# Lesson plan — Accessibility and Inclusion

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** Accessibility and Inclusion
**Lesson:** accessibility-and-inclusion — what accessibility means and how inclusion works — an audit of your own neighbourhood and the chart that organises the question
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Words for the World

**The one thing:** say what accessibility means, audit a real place for it, and use a K-L-W chart to organise what you know and learn about inclusion.

**This lesson's own level:** built from two sub lessons that open in order.

## Why this lesson exists
Uruguay's Grade 9 English book teaches accessibility as an action, not a definition to copy: define it, then go and audit your own neighbourhood and school, then organise the whole topic in a K-L-W chart with a reading about an inclusion advocate. The course held disability words and rights (in Disability and human rights, from Tunisia) but no lesson on accessibility itself — the barriers in the built world and how to check for them — so this lesson is built, from Uruguay's book alone.

## Sub lessons
1. **Sub lesson 1 — What accessibility means.** Uruguay's Grade 9 book defines accessibility, then sends learners to audit their own neighbourhood and school: what helps everyone get in, move around and take part — and what blocks them.
2. **Sub lesson 2 — Inclusion — everyone in.** A K-L-W chart (what I know, what I want to know, what I learned) organises the topic, and a reading about an inclusion advocate shows one person changing the answer.

Sub lesson 2 opens only when the prove-it check on sub lesson 1 is passed.
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is needed first. Disability and human rights sits nearby and can be tried first for the rights side — a soft hint, never a gate.

## What the learner can do when it ends
Define accessibility in the learner's own words, complete an audit of a real place naming what helps and what blocks, and fill a K-L-W chart on inclusion from first thought to last.

## What you need
A real place to audit: the learner's own street, school or home is the fieldwork site.
