# Lesson Plan — Irony

**Unit 43 — Words About Words.**

**One thing:** irony — when words or events mean the opposite of what you expect — and its story form, dramatic irony, where the audience knows more than the character.

## Sub lessons

1. **When words mean the opposite.** *Can-do: Spot irony in a worked paragraph: identify the irony and explain the meaning underneath — what is said on the surface, and what is really meant.*
2. **Dramatic irony.** *Can-do: Understand and find dramatic irony: the audience knows something a character does not, and watches the character walk towards it — the lesson's reading (a Hawthorne excerpt) carries it.*

(The gate pool for each sub lesson is every question marked with that sub lesson's number in this lesson's `questions/` files — the whole set, because no hardness ranking is written in questions/.)

## Teaching points

- Irony studied in a worked paragraph and a reading (item 402): the reading is an excerpt from Nathaniel Hawthorne's "The Minister's Black Veil"; learners identify the irony of a paragraph, explain its meaning, learn dramatic irony, and finish with peer assessment.
- Plot/reading summaries only; the excerpt itself is not transcribed.
