# Short Answer — Irony

(Every item is marked with the sub lesson it belongs to: (Sub lesson N). The gate pool for a sub lesson is all of that sub lesson's questions — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the whole set is the pool.)

1. Explain the irony: a shop called Always Open has a CLOSED sign. (Sub lesson 1)
   — Surface: the name promises it is always open. Truth: it is closed — the sign says the opposite of the name.
2. Write one ironic sentence about a school day. (Sub lesson 1)
   — Example: "Wonderful — another surprise test, just what I wanted."
3. What makes dramatic irony different from spoken irony? (Sub lesson 2)
   — In spoken irony a speaker means the opposite of their words; in dramatic irony the gap is between what the audience knows and what a character knows.
4. Give an example of dramatic irony from a story you know. (Sub lesson 2)
   — Example answer shape: the audience knows the letter is a trick; the hero reads it happily, not knowing.
