# Lesson plan — Phrasal verbs

**Course:** English · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** Phrasal verbs
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 33, "Phrasal
Verbs" — the level is this lesson (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the phrasal
verb system — a verb + particle whose meaning is its
own (not the verb's, not the particle's) — learned in
themed sets, and steered by its grammar: transitive vs
intransitive, separable vs non-separable with object
placement, and three-word phrasal verbs as the final
part.
**This lesson's own level:** it is a Word lesson, not a
Sentence lesson: its home is among the word-building
lessons, and it stands on everyday verb vocabulary.
**What the learner can do at the end:** read a phrasal
verb as one meaning, not two words; use the set's
themed families (friendship, environment, clothes,
travel) in true sentences; split a separable phrasal
verb around its object — and always around a pronoun;
keep a non-separable one's object after the whole
phrase; and carry three-word phrasal verbs (look
forward to, put up with, keep up with) as single units.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the everyday verb lessons.
  Recommended, never forced.
- The grammar half of this lesson (separable /
  non-separable, transitive / intransitive) rests on
  ONE witness: Bolivia's compendium is the only source
  in the whole read set that teaches it, and the
  lesson and sources say so plainly. The meaning sets
  are witnessed by five countries.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a jacket being put on, then
   taken off: two small verbs doing clothing work.**
   Written slot: "a picture of a jacket on, then off."
   What the student does: the student mimes both and
   says them: "put on", "take off".
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of a word-block PUT ON with a
   small block (IT) sliding between PUT and ON.**
   Written slot: "a picture of a word sliding between
   two halves." What the student does: the student
   slides the pronoun in and reads: "put it on" — never
   "put on it".
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of three chain links locked
   together: LOOK — FORWARD — TO.** Written slot: "a
   picture of a three-link chain." What the student
   does: the student tries to pull a link out, fails,
   and says the whole chain as one verb.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first** (lesson/lesson.md): guess six
   phrasal verbs from their sentences before any rule
   is given — feel that the meaning lives in the pair.
2. **The idea**: verb + particle = one new meaning —
   Vietnam's Grade 9 opens the topic (Unit 1) with its
   Remember box (go out, pass down, cut down on, run
   out of).
3. **Themed sets**: Cambodia's Grade 12 friendship set
   (Unit 1) and environment set (Unit 10); Mongolia's
   English X clothing set (Unit 5, opens p.72) and
   travel set (Unit 6, opens p.88); Sudan's SPINE 5
   two chapters of phrasal verbs (Ch.7–8).
4. **Transitive / intransitive**: Bolivia Year 3 —
   transitive phrasal verbs take an object (Topic 3,
   §3.2, student p.216); intransitive take none and
   their parts cannot split (Topic 4, §2.4, student
   p.219).
5. **Separable**: Bolivia Year 5, Topic 4, §5 (student
   p.213) — the object can stand between verb and
   particle; a pronoun MUST stand there. (The book's
   own example line prints "Rosa fill out the test" —
   a slip, recorded in the sources, never copied as a
   model.)
6. **Non-separable**: Bolivia Year 6, Topic 3, §1.3
   (student p.217) — the table of inseparable phrasal
   verbs (look after, come across, keep up with…);
   the object follows the whole phrase.
7. **Three-word phrasal verbs**: Vietnam's Grade 12,
   Unit 9 (printed pp.116–127) — live up to, put up
   with, get on with, look forward to, keep up with —
   the final part.
8. **Do it yourself** (the three closing tasks in the
   lesson): my morning in phrasal verbs; pronoun
   sliding; the three-link chains.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck on a meaning: do not split the pair — learn the
  pair as one word with a space in it, inside its set.
- Stuck on placement: ask "can it split?" If the book
  of your memory says separable, the pronoun goes
  between; if not sure, keep the whole phrase together
  and put the object after — wrong only for pronouns
  with separable verbs.
- Stuck on three-word verbs: treat the chain as one
  link-locked verb; the object comes after the last
  link, always.
- The course's road continues with the advanced Word
  levels; this lesson is the system's foundation.
