# Phrasal verbs — true or false

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Phrasal verbs — one meaning, in sets · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Phrasal verbs — the splitting grammar · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Phrasal verbs — three-word chains.

Say true or false. If it is false, say why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** A phrasal verb's meaning is just its verb's meaning plus its particle's meaning.
**Answer: False** — the pair carries its own meaning — look after is not look + after; learn the pair as one word.

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** "She looks the children after" is acceptable English.
**Answer: False** — look after is non-separable (Bolivia's Year 6 table): She looks after the children — the object follows the whole phrase.

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** Every country's book in this set teaches separable and non-separable phrasal verbs.
**Answer: False** — Bolivia alone teaches that grammar in the whole read set; the other four countries witness the meaning sets only.
