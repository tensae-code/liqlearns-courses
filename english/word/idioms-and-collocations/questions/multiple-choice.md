# Idioms and collocations — multiple choice

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Idioms as Whole Phrases · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Collocations and Their Partnerships · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Using Idioms and Collocations.

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** "It's raining cats and dogs" means — a) animals are falling b) it is raining heavily c) the pets are outside **Answer: b) it is raining heavily** — Sierra Leone's own gloss (JSS L111) — the phrase means what its words never say

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** An idiom is — a) a fixed expression whose meaning can't be worked out from its words b) any long word c) a spelling rule **Answer: a) a fixed expression whose meaning can't be worked out from its words** — Egypt's Idiom Tip, in its words (Sec 11 T1, SB p.13)

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** "She did me a big favour." The verb partnership is — a) make a favour b) do a favour c) have a favour **Answer: b) do a favour** — Libya G12's worked row: do a favour (U4 L3, p.44)

**4. (Sub lesson 3)** "The exam was a piece of cake" means the exam was — a) sweet b) served at lunch c) very easy **Answer: c) very easy** — Sierra Leone SSS1's own gloss of the idiom (L93–96)
