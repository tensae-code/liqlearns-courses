# Lesson plan — Idioms and collocations

**Course:** English · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** Idioms and collocations
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 43 (CATCH-UP-PLAN.md,
Levels 42–44 build list).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** words that travel in fixed company: idioms and proverbs, whose meaning lives in the WHOLE phrase, and collocations — the word partnerships (make a cake, do the washing-up, have a headache) that must be learned as wholes.
**What the learner can do at the end:** recognise an idiom and say its whole-phrase meaning; explain a proverb in plain words; and sort everyday verb partnerships under make, do and have without translating word by word.

## Before this lesson
- Word building and Word relationships are earlier lessons — if you know them, this one will be easier. Here the unit is bigger than one word: it is the fixed phrase.
- Recommended, never forced. This lesson stands alone for a
  learner who lands on it directly; anything earlier is a soft
  hint only ("if you know this one, this lesson will be easier").

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a train of word-carriages locked together, travelling as one — 'a piece of cake' written across all of them.** Written slot: "a picture of
   word-carriages locked into one train, moving as a single thing." What the student does: the student tries to uncouple one carriage, hears it stop making sense, and says the train's whole meaning
2. **Slot 2 — three labelled doors — MAKE, DO, HAVE — with phrase cards queuing at the right doors.** Written slot: "a picture of
   three doors labelled MAKE, DO and HAVE, with phrase cards waiting to enter." What the student does: the student sends six phrase cards to their doors before reading on

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first**: 'It's raining cats and dogs' — the learner draws what the words say, then what it means, and feels the gap.
2. **Idioms**: Egypt's Idiom Tip definition — a fixed multi-word expression whose meaning can't be deduced from its words (Sec 11 T1, U1 L1).
3. **Proverbs**: the saying that carries a lesson — Sierra Leone's Idioms and Proverbs lesson, from 'A friend in need'.
4. **Collocations**: words that habitually partner — Libya G7's play/ride/go/make matching (U3.6).
5. **make / do / have**: Libya G12's verb-collocation sort (U4 L3, p.44), with the G5 food-workbook partnerships (do the washing-up, make a cake).
6. **Idioms at work**: Uzbekistan's money-idiom matching set (EM9, U4).
7. **Do it yourself**: the closing tasks.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck translating an idiom word by word: stop. The phrase is one word in disguise — learn its whole meaning, the way Sierra Leone's L111 teaches 'raining cats and dogs' = raining heavily.
- Stuck on make vs do: there is no rule that saves you — Sierra Leone's SSS lessons state collocations must be memorised as they follow no rule. Learn the partnership, not a law.
- A proverb is not understood until it is said in your own plain words.
