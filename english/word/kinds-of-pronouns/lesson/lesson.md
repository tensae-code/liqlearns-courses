# Kinds of Pronouns

## Do it first

*I, you, he* are only the start of the pronoun family. The Philippines' Grade 10 book's REMEMBER box sets out the rest: pronouns that point, pronouns that ask, pronouns that speak of no one in particular, and pronouns that join. This lesson teaches the four kinds and the test that tells a pronoun from an adjective. (The personal pronouns' own cases are taught in *Pronoun Case*.)

## Sub lesson 1 — Pointing and asking pronouns

*Difficulty: first step — the ground of the lesson.*

**Demonstrative pronouns** point: *this* and *these* point near, *that* and *those* point far — "*This* is my leaf; *those* are yours." **Interrogative pronouns** ask: *who, whom, whose, what, which* — "*Who* painted the leaf? *What* happened in the storm?" The same words can do adjective work when they stand with a noun — "*this* leaf" (this = adjective, pointing at the leaf) against "*This* is the leaf" (this = pronoun, standing alone). The book's REMEMBER box insists on the distinction: a pronoun stands in the noun's place; an adjective accompanies the noun.

Try it now: Point and say: use each of this/that/these/those twice — once as a pronoun (alone) and once as an adjective (with a noun). Then ask three questions with who, what and which.

## Sub lesson 2 — Indefinite and relative pronouns

*Difficulty: builds on Sub lesson 1.*

**Indefinite pronouns** speak of people or things not named exactly: *someone, anyone, everyone, nobody, each, some, many, few, all* — "*Someone* left bread under their heads." **Relative pronouns** join a describing clause to its noun: *who* (people), *which* (things), *that* (both) — "the cobbler *who* freed the hand", "the balls *that* the bears swallowed". The adjective test still applies: "*Many* came" (pronoun) against "*many* children" (adjective). With the personal set from *Pronoun Case*, these four kinds complete the family the book names.

Try it now: Find the pronouns in these and name each kind: (1) Who left this here? (2) Nobody knew the balls that killed the bears. (3) Each of the children took one. Then write one sentence of your own for each kind.

## Do it yourself

Write a short paragraph (six to eight sentences) about a mystery in your school or street. Use at least one pronoun of each kind — demonstrative, interrogative, indefinite, relative — and underline each one, writing its kind in the margin. Check every underlined word with the test: does it stand alone in a noun's place?
