# Lesson plan — Interjections

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** Interjections
**Lesson:** interjections — the words that burst out — oh!, wow!, ouch! — feelings in a single word
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 6 — Word Kinds
**The one thing:** an interjection is a word thrown into speech to show a sudden feeling, and it stands apart from the sentence around it
**This lesson's own level:** Word-kind lesson: the bursting-out words first, then their place inside whole sentences.

## Why this lesson exists

No lesson housed the eighth word kind. Sierra Leone's SSS 2 handbook teaches "Interjections" by name (Term 3, Lesson 101), between its conjunction and preposition work. The 2026-11-08 re-verification sweep marked it NEW LESSON NEEDED. Under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06) it gets its own lesson, beside the other word kinds in Unit 6; its source is the handbook lesson named in lesson/sources.md.

## Sub lessons

1. **Words that burst out** — oh!, wow!, ouch!, hurrah!, alas! — one word carrying a whole feeling, usually with an exclamation mark
2. **Interjections in sentences** — where the burst-out word sits — at the start or dropped inside — and how the sentence closes around it

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is forced first — this lesson stands alone: every learner already uses these words in speech. It sits in Unit 6 with the other word kinds (nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs, pronouns, prepositions), each one lesson, one thing.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- Say what an interjection is.
- Name interjections for joy, surprise, pain and sorrow.
- Use an interjection at the start of a sentence, with its exclamation mark or comma.
- Tell an interjection apart from the other word kinds in a sentence.

## What you need
Nothing but voices — the words live in speech first.

## The run of the lesson
1. **Do it first** — the teacher drops a book (not on a foot). The class shouts what it feels, before the word "interjection" is taught (lesson/lesson.md).
2. Work through sub lesson 1: the bursting-out words, sorted by feeling.
3. Work through sub lesson 2: interjections inside whole sentences.
4. ADD AUDIO: six short shouts — joy, surprise, pain, sorrow, greeting, warning — the student names the feeling before the word is shown.

## Add-ons
- Feeling cards: a partner reads a card (“You won the prize!”, “You stepped on a thorn”) and you answer with the interjection only.
- Sentence surgery: take three plain sentences and drop a fitting interjection into each.

## If the learner is stuck
If a learner puts an interjection on every line, ration them: one per paragraph in writing work — the books' own use is sparing, a burst, not a habit. If the mark is missing, read the sentence aloud flat and then with the feeling; the voice shows where the "!" belongs.

## Teacher note
The lesson rests on the SSS 2 handbook's Lesson 101; the book's own follow-on (Lesson 102, the three small word kinds together) is stocked in connectors-and-clauses — recorded in lesson/sources.md so nothing is cited twice.
