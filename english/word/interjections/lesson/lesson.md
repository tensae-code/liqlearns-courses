# Interjections

## Do it first

Your teacher drops a book — clatter! What did the class just
shout? "Oh!" "Ah!" Maybe somebody laughed "Ha!"

Those little shouts are words with a job. This lesson is
about them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Words that burst out

An **interjection** is a word thrown into speech to show a
**sudden feeling**. It is usually one word, and it often
carries an exclamation mark — **!**

- Joy: **Hurrah!** We won!
- Surprise: **Wow!** Look at that rain!
- Pain: **Ouch!** The thorn!
- Sorrow: **Alas!** The boat is gone.
- Greeting and calling: **Hello!** **Hey!** Over here!
- A sudden stop: **Hush!** Someone is sleeping.

The interjection is not joined to the sentence's grammar. It
stands apart — a burst, then the sentence goes on.

Say one for each: you open a gift and love it; you see a
snake; you burn your finger; your friend arrives after a
long journey.

*Difficulty: first steps — every learner already owns these words in speech; the lesson only names them and sorts them by feeling.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Interjections in sentences

Inside writing, the burst-out word has two homes:

- **At the start**, with its mark or a comma after it:
  "**Oh!** The rain has stopped." / "**Well,** I think so."
- **Dropped inside**, set off by commas: "It is, **alas**,
  too late."

The stronger the feeling, the stronger the mark: a full burst
takes **!**, a soft one takes a comma. And one burst is
enough — a page sprinkled with interjections stops sounding
like feeling and starts sounding like noise.

Take three plain sentences. Put a fitting interjection into
each: one at the start with **!**, one at the start with a
comma, one dropped inside.

*Difficulty: building — placement and punctuation, and the sparing hand the books themselves use.*

## Say it to your companion

Explain an interjection to your companion: what it is, four
feelings with their words, and its two homes in a sentence.
Your companion reads you a plain sentence; you answer with
the sentence plus a fitting interjection. Then change places.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write six interjections, one for each feeling: joy,
   surprise, pain, sorrow, greeting, warning.
2. Write three sentences, each with an interjection — one
   with **!**, one with a comma after it, one dropped inside
   with commas around it.
3. Read a page of a story. Count its interjections. Is the
   writer's hand sparing or heavy?
