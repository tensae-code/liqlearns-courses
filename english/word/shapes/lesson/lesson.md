# Shapes

## Do it first

Look at the shapes on this page. Point to one. Do you know its name in English? Say it out loud — even a guess. Trying comes first here; then we learn every name together.

## Sub lesson 1 — The straight-edged shapes

Here are four shapes with straight sides.

A **square** has four sides, all the same. Look — one, two, three, four.

A **triangle** has three sides. One, two, three.

A **rectangle** has four sides too, but two are long and two are short.

A **rhombus** is a square tipped over, like a diamond standing on its corner.

Say them with me: square, triangle, rectangle, rhombus. Now point: where is the triangle?

## Sub lesson 2 — The round and sky shapes

These four have no straight sides — or they shine in the sky.

A **circle** is round, like a ball's face or the sun.

A **heart** is the shape we draw for love.

A **star** has five points and shines in the night sky.

A **crescent** is the moon when it is thin — a curved sliver of light.

Say them with me: circle, heart, star, crescent. Which one is in the sky at night? Both the star and the crescent!

## Sub lesson 3 — Counting shapes

Now we count shapes, the way your book does.

“How many circles are there?” Count with your finger: one, two, three. “There are three circles.”

We can ask about any shape: “How many triangles are there?” “How many stars are there?”

Count slowly and touch each shape once. That is the whole trick.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: “My favourite shape is the star.” Ask them: “How many squares are there?” — and point at a group of shapes for them to count.

## Do It Yourself

Draw all eight shapes on paper from memory. Name each one out loud as you draw it. Then walk around your home, find one real thing for three of the shapes, and say: “The clock is a circle.” “The window is a rectangle.”
