# Lesson plan — Shapes

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** Shapes
**Lesson:** shapes — the shapes of things: square, circle, triangle, rectangle, rhombus, heart, star, crescent
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Words for the World (with the other word sets)
**The one thing:** The names of the shapes, and asking how many shapes there are.
**This lesson's own level:** Word-set lesson: know the eight shape names by sight and say how many of a shape you can see.

## Why this lesson exists

No lesson in the course taught the shapes. Bolivia's Grade 2 book (Primaria 2) teaches the full shape set as one of its word units — square, circle, triangle, rectangle, rhombus, heart, star, crescent — and counts shapes with it. Under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06), a teaching item with no lesson gets a new lesson built; this is that lesson. Its only source is the Bolivia book named in lesson/sources.md.

## Sub lessons

1. The straight-edged shapes
2. The round and sky shapes
3. Counting shapes

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is needed before this lesson. It sits beside Colours and Numbers; a learner who knows the numbers to 10 will enjoy sub lesson 3 more, but no lesson is forced first — this lesson stands alone.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- Say the name of each of the eight shapes when you see it.
- Point to the right shape when you hear its name.
- Answer “How many circles are there?” with a number.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil or crayons. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson
1. ADD IMAGE: the eight shapes in a row, each one big and clear, no words under them. The learner points and guesses the names first — trying comes before telling.
2. ADD GIF (1–2 seconds): a square drawing itself, line by line, then the word SQUARE under it. The learner traces the air with a finger as it draws.
3. Work through sub lesson 1's four straight-edged shapes: say each name aloud, trace it, draw it.
4. ADD IMAGE: the circle, the heart, the star and the crescent moon in a night-sky picture. Work through sub lesson 2 the same way.
5. ADD VIDEO (short): a table full of mixed shapes; a hand counts the circles one by one — “How many circles are there? One, two, three. There are three circles.” Then the learner counts shapes in the room for sub lesson 3.

## Add-ons
- Draw-and-say: the teacher draws a shape slowly; the learner shouts its name before it is finished.
- Shape hunt: find one thing in the room for each shape and say its shape name.

## If the learner is stuck
Cut back to two shapes at a time (square and circle first — the pair every book starts from), let the learner hold or trace a real object of that shape, and only add the next shape when the two are sure.

## Teacher note
Bolivia's book teaches the shapes inside its numbers-and-shapes unit and counts with them at once; this lesson keeps that order — names first, counting shapes last. The book's own name for the part is kept as the searchable tag in lesson/sources.md.
