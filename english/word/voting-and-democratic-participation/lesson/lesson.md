# Voting and Democratic Participation

## Do it first
One card, one person, one choice — put the card in the box and you have done something powerful. But powerful how, exactly? Before you read on, answer three questions: Who is allowed to vote in your country? How often do elections happen? And what happens to a person who does not vote?

## Sub lesson 1 — Voting and elections
Uruguay's Grade 9 book draws voting as a bubble map: at the centre sits **voting**, and around it the words it touches — **government**, **legislature**, **elections**. A vote is how the people choose the government; an election is the counting of all the votes; the legislature is the body the winners go to sit in.

Then the book asks about Uruguay itself: what you need in order to vote, who the first woman voter was, how often elections come round, and what happens if you do not vote. In Uruguay voting is not only a right — it is a duty, and the book makes the learner find out what that means.

*Difficulty: Three map words plus a country's own voting facts, found out rather than given.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Democracy and other systems
The Grade 12 book starts with a survey — the president and ministers, the voter card (the *credencial*), first votes, how often elections are held — then reads what **democracy** is: a system born in ancient Greece, whose name means rule by the people, and which the book marks with three characteristics. Uruguay's version elects its leaders for five-year terms.

Against democracy the book sets the other systems: a **monarchy**, ruled by a king or queen; a **constitutional monarchy**, where the monarch shares the country with a constitution; and a **dictatorship**, where one ruler holds the power and votes do not choose. The class charts countries by system and finishes by arguing the live questions: Should voting be mandatory? Why are some young people not interested? What about gender quotas?

[Picture 0003]
*Difficulty: Systems must be told apart by who really holds the power — the lesson's one hard judgement.*

## Say it to your companion
Say democracy's story in two lines: where it began, and what its name means. Then name the three other systems from the lesson: monarchy, constitutional monarchy, dictatorship.
