# Space and Astronomy Words

## Do it first

Look up on a clear night. The lights you see have names, the dark between them has a name, and the things that happen up there — a shadow crossing the sun, a tail of light moving slowly — have names too. This lesson teaches those names: the words of space and astronomy.

## Sub lesson 1 — Space and astronomy words

The words of space:

- The **universe** is everything that exists — all the stars, all the space, all of it.
- A **galaxy** is a huge family of stars; our own galaxy is the Milky Way.
- A **star** is a great ball of hot gas that makes its own light; our **sun** is a star.
- A **planet** is a large body that goes round a star and shines with the star's light.
- An **orbit** is the path a body follows round another body.
- **Gravity** is the pull that holds the planets in their orbits and holds us to the earth.
- An **eclipse** is when one body moves into another's shadow — the moon darkening, or the sun covered.
- A **comet** is a body of ice and dust that grows a bright tail as it nears the sun.
- **Astronomy** is the study of everything beyond the earth; an event in the sky is an **astronomical** event.

*Difficulty: The whole sky, carried by nine words.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Studying the universe

How people study what sub lesson 1 names:

- An **astronomer** is a scientist who studies the universe.
- A **telescope** is the tool that makes far things look near, so astronomers can **observe** — watch carefully and record — the sky.
- An **observatory** is the building, often on a mountain under clear skies, where telescopes are kept and astronomers work.
- To **discover** is to find something that was always there but never seen before — a new star, a new kind of object.
- An **astronaut** is a person who travels into space; a **spacecraft** is the ship that carries them.

Armenia's own book is proud of its astronomers: the unit tells how Armenian scientists have added to the study of the universe — a small country with a long place in astronomy's story.

*Difficulty: The words at work — the watchers and their tools.*

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion one thing in the night sky you can name with the lesson's words, and one thing astronomers had to discover with telescopes.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the nine words of sub lesson 1 with one line of meaning for each.
2. Explain an eclipse in two lines, using orbit and shadow-side words from the lesson.
3. Write two lines: what an astronomer does, and the tools it needs.
