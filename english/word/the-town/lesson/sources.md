**Acceptance count: taught in 16 countries (Bangladesh, Egypt,
Mongolia, Laos, China, Indonesia, Iran, Sierra Leone, Jordan, Greece, Kazakhstan, Iraq, Ethiopia, South Africa, Syria, South Sudan).** Egypt
carries the first set twice (Primary 1's
eight words with their dialog; Primary 2's city set);
Bangladesh carries city life as a topic, against village life;
Mongolia carries a whole senior unit on the same world; China
carries a second town set located with near / next to /
behind and worked over maps; Indonesia carries a whole
neighbourhood on one map, with directions and the position
set; Iran carries the city described and located whole, and
the widest pictured places set in this lesson; Sierra Leone
carries the community word-web and directions by landmarks.
Jordan carries a town set located and directed in its Grade 3 booster, a whole Our town unit in Grade 4, and the buildings set with the parts of a building in Grade 6; Greece carries the city facilities set with its place phrases in Grade 4, the park and road signs of its Around the City unit, and directions over real maps in Grade 5; Kazakhstan carries the town buildings in its Grade 3 Express buildings module, the town set with directions language in its Grade 3 Macmillan Dictionary, and a town asked about and written up as a brochure in Grade 5 English Plus; Iraq carries a whole My town unit in Grade 3 — six town places on a street scene, the town described with there is / there isn't and directed over a map — and describing places as its own box in Grade 9 book; Ethiopia carries only the describing side of this lesson — town against country in its Grade 7 reading "Living in Rural and Urban Areas: Advantages and Disadvantages" with speaking on where one lives, and the big city described in its Grade 9 reading "Life in a Big City" — no town-places word set, locating frame or directions work is filed for Ethiopia, and none is claimed here; South Africa carries the town set — shops, the library, asking about places — in Grade 1 FAL Theme 12 "About town," flats, maps and directions in Grade 3 HL, and map work with directions again in Grade 6 FAL; Syria carries a places-to-go set and a then-and-now My Town unit in Grade 3, and a whole Places in My Town unit with a Giving Directions unit beside it in Grade 4 — Syria, unlike Ethiopia here, files the word set, the locating prepositions and the directions work together; South Sudan carries its town as "Our Payam" in Primary 3 — the Payam places named as one set, with the Fangak map to role-play directions over — and directions themselves as the Primary 5 health unit's language lesson, prepositions of place and direction phrases worked on a town map; Other
closed countries join as their books are read (see
READING-QUEUE.md).

South Sudan's town is the Payam, the local administrative town. Primary 3 Unit 3 "Our Payam" (printed pp.14–20) names the Payam places as one set (school, shop, grocery, post office; police post, hospital, hair salon, church, bank), works them through Jane's social-services text (Lainya Shopping Centre, a school and a hospital nearby), and closes with the story "Fangak Payam", whose printed map is role-played for directions (the chief's office, hospital north, police station south, the primary school west, past the Catholic Church). Primary 5 Unit 9 then teaches giving directions as its language lesson (printed pp.144–145): prepositions of place — along, between, near, behind, next to, "infront of" [as printed] — worked on a town map, then turn right/left, go past, go straight on and opposite used to write directions from Kakwa village to the hospital.
