# The town — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Word · Lesson: The town
Built from: Egypt, English Language Primary 1 Term 2, Unit 5
"Neighborhood" (the "Around My Town" lesson and its dialog) —
Egypt, Primary 2 Term 2, Unit 4 (the city set) — Bangladesh,
English for Today Class 3 (city life against village life) —
Mongolia, English VIII, Unit 5 "Around Town."

## Do it first
Walk down your own street in your head. You pass places you go
to every week. In your own language you know them all. Now
walk it the way Egypt's book does — with Sara and Ali:

"Let's walk around town, Ali!"
"Okay! Look, this is a **school**."
"And where do you go when you feel sick?"
"I go to the **hospital**."
"What do you see on the **street**?"
"I see a **truck**, a **bus**, and a **car**."
"Wow! And what's that big **building** over there?"
"That's a **shop**. We can buy things there."

## Then the explanation
The town's first set is Egypt's **eight words**, and the
dialog above carries all of them: **hospital, school, shop,
building, street, car, truck, bus**. Learn each place with
what it is FOR, and the name sticks to the job:

- **school** — where children learn.
- **hospital** — where you go when you feel sick.
- **shop** — where we buy things.
- **street** — where the car, the truck, and the bus go.
- **building** — any big house of rooms; a school and a
  hospital and a shop are all buildings too.

The later books widen the town. Egypt's city unit adds
**library** (borrow books), **post office** (send letters),
**supermarket** (the big food shop), **club**, and **city**
itself — the whole town's big name. The first set's own unit
adds **park** (play), **zoo** (see animals), and **beach**.
Mongolia's Grade 8 book gives the same world a whole unit,
"Around Town" — the set keeps growing the same way everywhere:
a place, its name, its job.

Bangladesh's book sets the town against its pair: Oyshi's
**city** life in one lesson, Osman's **village** life in the
lesson before it. Same words, two sizes of world — school,
library, shop stand in both.

## Say it to your companion
Walk your companion around YOUR town, in words. Name every
place you pass and say what it is for: "This is the hospital.
We go there when we feel sick." Then let your companion ask
the dialog's two questions — "Where do you go when you feel
sick?" "What do you see on the street?" — and answer for your
own street.

## Do It Yourself
1. Draw your street. Draw its places as boxes and label each
   one in English.
2. Write what each place is for, in the dialog's shapes:
   "I go to the ___ when ___."
3. Answer about your town: What do you see on the street?
   Write three things.
4. Say the eight words of the first set out loud, in order,
   pointing at each place on your drawing: hospital, school,
   shop, building, street, car, truck, bus.
