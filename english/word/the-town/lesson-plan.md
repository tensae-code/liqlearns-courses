# Lesson plan — The town

**Course:** English · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** The town
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 7, "Words for the World" — the second lesson of the level (CURRICULUM.md), after
The home.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the words for the
places in a town — and what each place is for.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The home (the learner
can already name the places inside a home; now the places
outside it) and the Nouns lesson under both. It needs nothing
else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The home. Recommended, never forced.
- Directions and where things are (next to, behind, turn
  left) are NOT this lesson — a different thing, its own
  lesson later. This lesson only names the places and what
  they are for.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name Egypt's eight town words: hospital, school, shop,
   building, street, car, truck, bus.
2. Name the wider set from the later books: library, post
   office, supermarket, park, zoo, city.
3. Say what each place is for, in the books' own dialog
   shapes: "Where do you go when you feel sick?" — "I go to
   the hospital." "What do you see on the street?" — "I see a
   truck, a bus, and a car."
4. Point at the places of their own town and name them.

## What you need
A walk, real or pictured. Paper and a pencil for the map step.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner pictures a walk down their
   own street and names every place they pass, in their own
   language; the lesson then gives the English names
   (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the eight town words with the Sara
   and Ali dialog that carries them; the wider set; each place
   with what it is for (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the start of the explanation: a picture of a
     street seen from above — an ambulance pulls up to a
     hospital, a bus stops, children walk into a school, each
     place's name popping big as it is used, 1–2 seconds per
     place — the student says each name as it pops.
   - ADD IMAGE under the wider set: a picture of a real town
     street with a library, a post office, a shop, and a park
     on it — the student points and names four places.
   - ADD AUDIO in the dialog part: the Sara and Ali dialog
     read aloud ("Let's walk around town! … I go to the
     hospital") — the student takes Ali's part the second time
     and answers the questions themselves.
3. **Say it to your companion** — walk your companion around
   your town in words: name each place and say what it is for
   (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the
     town walk in the super-simple voice (made later, when
     videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — draw your street, label its places,
   and answer the two dialog questions about your own town
   (lesson/lesson.md).

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes shop and supermarket → both are in the books: a shop
  is the general word (Egypt: "That's a shop. We can buy
  things there"); a supermarket is the big food shop (Egypt's
  city unit). The small word covers both until the big one is
  needed.
- Calls every big building a school → the dialog's test: what
  is the place FOR? Sick people go to the hospital; children
  learn at the school; things are bought at the shop.
- Forgets street → the street is not a building; it is where
  the car, truck, and bus go. "What do you see on the street?"
- This lesson's next door: Food and fruit (same level).
  Directions wait for their own lesson.
