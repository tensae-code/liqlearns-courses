# Beach and Seaside Words

## Do it first

The sea is near, the sun is out — but which beach? One has big waves, one is calm, one is crowded with tourists, one hides under cliffs. To choose, you need the seaside words. Learn them here, and you can ask any local: "Which beach is best for swimming?"

## Sub lesson 1 — Seaside words

*Very simple. Can-do: The place words: beach, lagoon, cliff, waves, mountain, swimming — and the judging words: breathtaking (so beautiful it stops your breath) and touristy (full of tourists).*

Walk along the coast and name what you see:

The **beach** — the sand where the land meets the sea. The **waves** — the water rolling in, one after another. A **lagoon** — calm, shallow water held apart from the open sea; the safe swimming place. A **cliff** — the steep rock face dropping to the water. Behind you, the **mountain**; below, the place for **swimming**.

Two judging words: **breathtaking** — so beautiful it takes your breath away ("The view is breathtaking"). **Touristy** — full of tourists and their shops; not always a compliment.

Try it now: describe a beach you know with three of these words.

## Sub lesson 2 — Choosing a beach

*Simple. Can-do: Use the words in advice: a local suggests beaches — Cabarete and Playa Dorada in the book — to a tourist who wants calm water; saying what each place is like and which fits.*

Words become useful when they answer a traveller's question. In the book's dialogue, a tourist wants calm water, and a local suggests two beaches: Cabarete and Playa Dorada — naming what each is like, so the tourist can choose.

The advice shape: the traveller says what they want ("I want calm water for swimming"), the local names a place and its quality ("Playa Dorada has no huge waves — it is perfect for swimming"), and a second choice stands by ("Cabarete is more touristy, with more people and music").

Try it now: a visitor asks you for a beach for children. Suggest one, and say why — calm water, soft sand, no big waves.

## Say it to your companion

Read this lesson aloud to your companion. Then play the dialogue: your companion is a tourist who wants calm water; you are the local who suggests two beaches.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write five seaside sentences about a real beach — or an imagined one.
2. Draw a coast and label: beach, lagoon, cliff, waves, mountain.
3. Write a two-line beach recommendation for a tourist who loves big waves.
4. Say which is more breathtaking: a lagoon at sunrise or a cliff in a storm — and why.
