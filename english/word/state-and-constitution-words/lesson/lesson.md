# State and Constitution Words

## Do it first

Your country has a flag, an anthem, and one big book of rules. Who leads the country? Who leads your town? This lesson teaches those words in English — and the word for being ready when the earth shakes.

## Sub lesson 1 — The constitution and its symbols

A country writes its rules in one book: the **constitution**. It opens with a **preamble** — the first words that say what the country is for. And a country carries **national symbols**: its **flag**, its **anthem**, its coat of arms — the signs that say "this is us" without a word of explaining.

Constitution, preamble, symbols: the words of a country's own house.

*Difficulty: The country's book, its opening words, its symbols.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Leaders and being prepared

A country is led in steps. The **president** leads the nation. A **governor** leads a state or region. A **mayor** leads a town or city. Nation, region, town — three steps, three leaders.

And the same lesson teaches **preparedness**: Venezuela's earth shakes, so its people learn what to do in an **earthquake** and an **emergency** — where to go, what to carry, how to stay calm. To be prepared is a civic word: it belongs beside the leaders, because keeping people safe is the state's first job.

*Difficulty: Three leaders in their steps — and the word that keeps people safe.*

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: your president, your governor if you know the name, and your mayor — and one thing your family would do in an earthquake.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the three leaders in their steps, with the place each one leads.
2. Write the national symbols of your country and one line about the flag.
3. Write a family emergency list in English: where to go, what to carry, whom to call.
