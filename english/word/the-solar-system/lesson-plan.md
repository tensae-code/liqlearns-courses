# Lesson plan — The Solar System

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** The Solar System
**Lesson:** the-solar-system — the universe and the planets: the
eight planets, the dwarf planets (Eris the largest of them;
Pluto made a dwarf planet), distances and sizes in space, and
moonlight as sunlight reflected off the moon, from the book's
astronaut dialogue
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Words for the World
**The one thing:** the book's space set, whole: planets and dwarf
planets first, then distances, sizes and moonlight.
**This lesson's own level:** Word lesson: the planets first, then
space's distances and light.

## Why this lesson exists

No lesson housed the space set. Rwanda's Primary 6 book's
environment units teach the universe, the planets and the dwarf
planets (printed pp.124–125), distances in space and
planet-diameter comparisons (printed pp.126–128), and an
astronaut dialogue that explains moonlight as reflected
sunlight (printed pp.129–130). The 2026-11-08 re-verification
sweep read the book page by page and marked all three parts

## Sub lessons

1. The planets and the dwarf planets — the universe, the eight planets, and the dwarf planets: Eris the largest, Pluto made a dwarf planet
2. Distances and sizes in space, and moonlight — planet diameters compared, and moonlight as reflected sunlight

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is forced first — this lesson stands alone: it starts
from "the sun, the moon and the stars are in space" and teaches
every word it uses.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- Name the eight planets in their order from the sun.
- Say what a dwarf planet is, name Eris as the largest of them, and say what happened to Pluto.
- Compare planets by size, and explain where moonlight comes from.

## What you need
A ball for the sun, smaller balls for the planets, and a torch
for the sunlight work.

## The run of the lesson
1. **Do it first** — the learner names everything they can see
   in the night sky before anyone teaches the set
   (lesson/lesson.md).
2. ADD IMAGE: the sun and the eight planets in a row, unnamed.
   The learner guesses which is Earth before the names are
   taught.
3. Work through sub lesson 1: the planets, then the dwarf
   planets.
4. Work through sub lesson 2: sizes and distances, then the
   moonlight dialogue work.
5. ADD VIDEO (short): a flight out from the sun past the
   planets — each time a planet passes, its name grows big on
   screen: MERCURY… VENUS… EARTH…

## Add-ons
- "Planet parade": eight learners stand in a line as the
  planets, in order from the sun, and each says their planet's
  name and one thing about it.
- Moonlight test: in a dark room, shine a torch (the sun) on a
  ball (the moon) and watch the lit side — the ball shines with
  the torch's light, the way the moon shines with the sun's.

## If the learner is stuck
Cut back to the sun, the Earth and the moon — where we live,
what lights our day, what lights our night — until they are
sure. Add the planets one at a time, outwards from the sun.

## Teacher note
The book teaches this set inside its environment units — space
is met as part of the world around the learner, and its
astronaut dialogue carries the moonlight explanation as
conversation. This lesson keeps both shapes.
