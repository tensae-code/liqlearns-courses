# The Solar System

## Do it first

Think of the night sky. Try to say:

- "In the sky I can see ___."
- "The moon shines because ___."

This lesson is space: the planets, how big they are, how far
away, and where the moon's light comes from.

## Sub lesson 1 — The planets and the dwarf planets

Everything — the stars, the sun, our Earth — is part of the
**universe**: all of space and everything in it.

Our sun has a family of **planets** that travel around it.
This family is the **solar system**. The planets, in their
order from the sun, are:

**Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus,
Neptune.**

- **Earth** is our planet — the third from the sun, the one we
  live on.
- **Jupiter** is the biggest planet of all.
- **Mercury** is the closest to the sun; **Neptune** is the
  farthest of the eight.

There are also smaller worlds called **dwarf planets**. The
largest dwarf planet is **Eris**. **Pluto**, which people long
called the ninth planet, was made a dwarf planet — so the
solar system has eight planets, and Pluto stands with the
dwarf planets.

Say it whole: "The solar system has eight planets. Eris is the
largest dwarf planet. Pluto is a dwarf planet now."

*Difficulty: first steps — the planets in their order from the sun, said as one whole line, never as single names alone.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Distances and sizes in space, and moonlight

Space is big, and its distances are big numbers. The planets
stand millions of kilometres from the sun and from each other,
and they are not the same size: Jupiter's diameter — the
distance straight through its middle — is the largest of all,
and the small planets' diameters are only a part of it.
Comparing planets by their diameters tells you which giants
and which small worlds you are looking at.

And the moon? The moon makes no light of its own.
**Moonlight is sunlight reflected off the moon**: the sun
shines on the moon, and the moon throws that light down to us,
the way a mirror throws light across a room. That is what the
book's astronauts explain in their dialogue — when you see the
moon shining, you are seeing the sun's light at second hand.

Ask and answer with a partner: "Where does moonlight come
from?" — "It is sunlight reflected off the moon." "Which
planet is the biggest?" — "Jupiter."

*Difficulty: building — sizes and distances compared, and the moonlight explanation said as a whole reason, not a single word.*

## Say it to your companion

Play the astronaut: your companion asks you three questions
about space — about the planets, about Pluto, about moonlight
— and you answer each one in a full sentence. Then change
places.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the eight planets in their order from the sun, and
   circle Earth.
2. Write two sentences: one about Eris and Pluto, and one
   explaining where moonlight comes from.
3. Draw the sun, the Earth and the moon, and draw the sun's
   light travelling to the moon and bouncing down to Earth.
