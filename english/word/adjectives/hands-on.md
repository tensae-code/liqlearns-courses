# Hands-on — Adjectives

The lesson with your hands and your eyes, not only your ears.

## Two of the same thing
Find two of the same noun in your home — two cups, two shirts,
two mangoes, two chairs. Hold one in each hand and describe the
difference out loud: "a clean cup — a dirty cup." "An old
shirt — a new shirt." The word that changed is the adjective;
the word that stayed is the noun. Five pairs of things minimum.

## The describing walk
Walk around your home or street. At every thing you pass, say
a pair out loud: "a tall tree." "A red door." "A hungry dog."
Ten pairs. If you can only name the thing and cannot paint it,
stop and look harder — every thing has something it is LIKE.

## Opposites with your face
With a partner: one says a pair — "a happy cat." The other
makes the face, then says the opposite pair — "an angry cat!"
(Bangladesh's own pictured pair.) Five rounds, then swap.
Happy/sad, big/small, clean/dirty, hot/cold — your face proves
you know the describing word.

## Turn it around
Take three of your pairs from the walk and say each one both
ways: "a cold drink — the drink is cold." Out loud, with the
thing in your hand. The adjective rides in front, or stands
after "is" — never anywhere else.
