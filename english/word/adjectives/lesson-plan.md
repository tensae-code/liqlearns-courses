# Lesson plan — Adjectives

**Course:** English · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** Adjectives
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2, Unit 1 "Word Kinds" —
the third lesson of the unit (CURRICULUM.md), after Nouns and
Verbs.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** an adjective is a
describing word — it tells us more about a noun: what it looks,
feels, sounds, or smells like, or what kind it is.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Nouns lesson (an
adjective is found by its noun — the books' own rule is that
adjectives and nouns go together). It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown changing — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Nouns — the naming words. An adjective
  describes a noun, so the learner must be able to find the noun
  first. Recommended, never forced.
- Comparing things (bigger, biggest) is NOT this lesson. Egypt's
  books teach the comparative and superlative rules as their own
  step (Primary 4 Term 1) — a different thing, its own lesson
  later. This lesson only names the describing word itself and
  its two homes: before its noun, and after "is."

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what an adjective is: a word that describes a noun.
2. Build adjective + noun pairs the way the books do: a fresh
   mango, a tall tree, a red breast.
3. Find the adjective in a short phrase or sentence by finding
   its noun first and asking "What is it like?"
4. Use an adjective in both its homes: before the noun (a cold
   ground) and after is/are (The ground is cold).
5. Sort describing words from naming and doing words in a mixed
   list.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner holds up any object and says
   only its name; then says what it is LIKE — big, old, red,
   rough — and feels the second word doing a different job
   (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — what an adjective is; adjectives and
   nouns go together; the books' pairs; the "What is it like?"
   test; the two homes (before the noun, after is)
   (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the start of the explanation: a picture of a
     small cat that grows big on the screen, 1–2 seconds, with
     "small cat" written under it first and "BIG cat" when it
     has grown — the student says both pairs out loud and hears
     which word changed.
   - ADD IMAGE under the pairs: a picture of a market stall or a
     busy street full of describable things — fruit, clothes,
     bikes, animals — the student says three adjective + noun
     pairs they can see in the picture.
   - ADD AUDIO in the two-homes part: a voice reading pairs both
     ways — "a fresh mango — the mango is fresh. A clean shirt —
     the shirt is clean" — the student repeats each pair and
     says the describing word alone.
3. **Say it to your companion** — explain what an adjective is,
   give three pairs, and run the "What is it like?" test on your
   companion (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the
     describing game in the super-simple voice (made later, when
     videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — describe five things in your home with a
   pair each, then turn two of them around after "is"
   (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Calls every word before a noun an adjective ("my book," "the
  ball") → go back to the test: does the word tell what the
  thing is LIKE? "My" says whose; "the" only points. Describing
  words answer "what is it like?"
- Cannot find the adjective → find the noun first. Adjectives
  and nouns go together (Libya's own rule): the describing word
  is the one holding the noun's hand, just before it or just
  after "is."
- Puts the adjective after the noun in the pair ("a mango
  fresh") → in the pair, the describing word rides in front:
  a fresh mango. After the noun it needs "is" to carry it: the
  mango is fresh.
- This lesson's next door: Adverbs — the words that describe a
  doing word (a different thing, its own lesson). Comparing
  (bigger, biggest) waits for its own lesson.
