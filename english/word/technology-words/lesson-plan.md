# Lesson plan — Technology words

**Course:** English · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** Technology words
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Words for the World —
the words for computers, phones and the internet
(CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the technology words —
the parts of a computer (monitor, keyboard, mouse, printer),
the things you do on the internet (visit a website, upload,
download, send a message), and the newest set: chatbot and
artificial intelligence.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The town and The
home (the places technology lives) and on Verbs (the doing
words upload, download, connect are verbs at work). It needs
nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown changing — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The Computer Parts** — Bolivia's six parts and what each one
   does. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **The Technology Actions** — the doing words used in sentences,
   upload told from download every time. (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **Gadgets and the Newest Words** — Sudan's gadgets named, and
   Vietnam's new set used in a sentence of your own. (Was depth
   Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking
is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set
is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Verbs (Level 6). Recommended, never
  forced — this lesson stands alone: every doing word is
  taught in its own sentence.
- How computers work inside is NOT this lesson. This lesson
  names the parts and the actions, the way the books do.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the parts of a computer from Bolivia's numbered
   list: monitor, keyboard, mouse, printer, drive, modem /
   router — and what each one does.
2. Say the internet actions: visit a website, surf the
   internet, upload a file, download an application, send a
   message; plug in, turn on, turn off, connect.
3. Use Sudan's gadget set: sat-nav, headphones, camera,
   webcam, mobile.
4. Say the newest words: artificial intelligence, chatbot,
   programme, analyse (Vietnam's Grade 12 set).

## What you need
Any phone or computer near you — its parts are the lesson's
pictures. Paper and a pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner touches (or points at) the
   parts of a real phone or computer: the screen, the
   buttons, the charger going in. The lesson then gives the
   English names (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the parts with Bolivia's numbered
   list; the doing words; the gadget set; the AI set
   (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the parts part: a picture of a computer on a
     desk, its parts lighting up one by one — monitor,
     keyboard, mouse, printer — 1–2 seconds each — the
     student names each part as it lights.
   - ADD IMAGE under the doing words: a picture of a phone
     screen showing a photo going up with an arrow — upload —
     and coming down — download — the student says which way
     the photo is travelling.
   - ADD AUDIO in the gadget part: a voice naming gadgets —
     sat-nav, headphones, webcam, mobile — the student points
     at the right object in the room, or mimes it, as each
     name is read.
3. **Say it to your companion** — give computer orders:
   "Turn on the computer. Connect the printer. Upload the
   photo." Your companion mimes each one (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the
     computer waking up in the super-simple voice (made
     later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — label a drawn computer and write the
   steps to upload a task, the way Bolivia's book asks
   (lesson/lesson.md).

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- GIF — a picture of a desk computer, parts lighting one by
  one → the student names each part as it lights.
- IMAGE — a picture of upload and download arrows on a phone
  → the student says which way the photo travels.
- AUDIO — gadget names read aloud → the student points or
  mimes each gadget.
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later) → the student
  gives the orders along with it.

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the
plan is the build sheet, so nothing the lesson needs gets forgotten.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes upload and download → the arrow is the meaning: up
  = from me to the internet; down = from the internet to me.
  Bolivia's task — upload a task to the Classroom platform —
  is the standing example.
- Calls the whole computer "the monitor" → Bolivia numbers
  the parts 1–6 for a reason: point at the part, not the
  machine, and the list separates them.
- Reads mouse as the animal → Sudan's neighbour lesson,
  Tanzania's reader and this one agree: context decides —
  beside a keyboard, a mouse is the pointing part (Bolivia's
  list says so in words: "pointing device").
- This lesson's next door: Working words (same level) — the
  office machines return there as job words; here they are
  named as things.
