**Acceptance count: taught in 19 countries (Bangladesh,
Egypt, Laos, China, Indonesia, Iran, Sierra Leone, Jordan, Greece, Kazakhstan, Iraq, Ethiopia, South Africa, Syria, South Sudan, Nepal, Armenia, Vietnam, Cambodia).** Egypt
carries the days first (its school-week
order, Saturday-first); Bangladesh carries the days on a
Sunday-first calendar, the months with their ordinal numbers,
the capital rule, and the days-in-a-month rhyme; China
carries the days on a class timetable and the months as
festival dates, with the thirty-days chant in its "Thirty
days has September" form; Indonesia carries the days as the
school week's subjects and the months with ordinal birthday
dates; Iran carries the week in its own shape — Saturday to
Wednesday as weekdays, Thursday and Friday as the weekend —
and the Gregorian months in its Photo Dictionary, its lesson
working in Persian-calendar months; Sierra Leone carries the
days only, named from word cards and ordered in a story. The
books start the printed week on different days — the lesson
shows each, per the standing rule for when books differ.
Jordan carries the days listened to and circled in its first Learning Club, the days with the week's activities and the months by seasons in Grade 3, and the thirty-days rhyme completed with month names in Grade 6; Greece carries both halves in full — the seven days acted by snowmen and traced as cut-out cards in Grade 2, all twelve months grouped by their seasons in the same pack, then the days on a Grade 4 school timetable and the months inside its Grade 4 time unit; Kazakhstan carries the days inside its Grade 3 Smiles Time module and all twelve months in the same book's Glossary, days and months-and-seasons in Grade 5 English Plus, and the months with ordinals 1st–100th in Grade 4 Top Stars; Iraq carries both halves in full — the seven days Sunday-first with their song in Grade 2, the months in a rhyme with birthdays in Grade 4, and the months whole again in Grade 7 book with the thirty-days rhyme, the full ordinals and written dates — so its week joins the Sunday-first books; Ethiopia carries both halves, with its own week order — the days Monday–Sunday taught as starting on Monday, with the "A week has seven days" song (printed p.31) — and the months as the Ethiopian calendar's 13 months mapped onto the Gregorian month names and seasons in Grade 3, with the "Thirty days have September…" rhyme (printed p.27); South Africa carries both halves in full — the days in Grade 1 HL Workbook 2, Grade 1 FAL and on a Grade 2 FAL calendar, and the months with birthday calendars in Grade 1 HL Workbook 2 and Grade 2 FAL, and on a Grade 3 FAL calendar; Syria carries both halves — the days with routines and a days song in Grade 2, the months with seasons and weather in Grade 3, and dates with ordinals in Grade 6; Syria's books do not state which day its school week starts on in the units filed, and no week-start order is claimed for it here; South Sudan joined 2026-11-03: months half only — month names worked inside the seasons in Primary 3 Unit 6, with no days-of-the-week teaching in the Primary 1–3 books filed; Other closed countries join as their books are read (see
READING-QUEUE.md).
Nepal joined 2026-11-03: it carries the days Sunday–Saturday in Grade 1 (Unit 9, Saturday the holiday), the months on a weather chart with the capital-letter rule for days and months in Grade 3 (Units 6 and 8), and Months of the Year as a named lesson in its Grade 2 teacher's guide, cited for its own teaching as a guide to a different, unheld workbook (Lesson 30, printed pp.112–126).
