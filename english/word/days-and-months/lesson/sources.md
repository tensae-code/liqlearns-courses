**Acceptance count: taught in 11 countries (Bangladesh,
Egypt, Laos, China, Indonesia, Iran, Sierra Leone, Jordan, Greece, Kazakhstan, Iraq).** Egypt
carries the days first (its school-week
order, Saturday-first); Bangladesh carries the days on a
Sunday-first calendar, the months with their ordinal numbers,
the capital rule, and the days-in-a-month rhyme; China
carries the days on a class timetable and the months as
festival dates, with the thirty-days chant in its "Thirty
days has September" form; Indonesia carries the days as the
school week's subjects and the months with ordinal birthday
dates; Iran carries the week in its own shape — Saturday to
Wednesday as weekdays, Thursday and Friday as the weekend —
and the Gregorian months in its Photo Dictionary, its lesson
working in Persian-calendar months; Sierra Leone carries the
days only, named from word cards and ordered in a story. The
books start the printed week on different days — the lesson
shows each, per the standing rule for when books differ.
Jordan carries the days listened to and circled in its first Learning Club, the days with the week's activities and the months by seasons in Grade 3, and the thirty-days rhyme completed with month names in Grade 6; Greece carries both halves in full — the seven days acted by snowmen and traced as cut-out cards in Grade 2, all twelve months grouped by their seasons in the same pack, then the days on a Grade 4 school timetable and the months inside its Grade 4 time unit; Kazakhstan carries the days inside its Grade 3 Smiles Time module and all twelve months in the same book's Glossary, days and months-and-seasons in Grade 5 English Plus, and the months with ordinals 1st–100th in Grade 4 Top Stars; Iraq carries both halves in full — the seven days Sunday-first with their song in Grade 2, the months in a rhyme with birthdays in Grade 4, and the months whole again in Grade 7 Book One with the thirty-days rhyme, the full ordinals and written dates — so its week joins the Sunday-first books; Other closed countries join as their books are read (see
READING-QUEUE.md).

**Laos Grade 4 added 2026-10-27** — *English for Lao Primary
Schools, Student's Book 2, Grade 4* (MoES/RIES, 2012; full read
in english/books/laos.md). Laos teaches the days in one lesson:
**all seven days printed on one page, Monday first** in the
page's arrangement (p.83), inside the frame **"What day is
today? — Today is Sunday. / Is it Monday today? — No. It's
Tuesday"** (p.82), with copy and gapped-name writing after
(p.85). So the week-order split now stands three ways — Egypt
Saturday-first, Bangladesh Sunday-first, Laos Monday-first —
shown to the learner as the books print it.

**Laos Grade 5 added 2026-10-27** — *Student's Book 3, Grade 5*
(MoES/RIES, 2012; full read in english/books/laos.md). Grade 5
completes the calendar: **all twelve months** (pp.28–36), the
**ordinals 1st–31st** printed numeral over word (p.33 and on),
and the date as a spoken frame — **"What's the date today? —
It's the 1st of January"** (p.32). The book's own slip is filed
with it: it prints a "31st September" (p.44), a date that cannot
exist — the learner is shown the months' real lengths against
it.

**Laos Grade 6 added 2026-10-27** — *English for Secondary
Schools, Book One* (MoES/RIES, 2010; full read in
english/books/laos.md). Secondary Laos prints its days
**Sunday-first** (p.93) — its own primary book printed Monday
first, so Laos itself carries both orders, and the course
shows both. The months come with the **thirty-days rhyme and
the leap year** (p.96), and the date is spoken in full: "the
first of January" (p.99).
