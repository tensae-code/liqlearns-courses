# Lesson plan — Days and months

**Course:** English · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** Days and months
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 9 — Numbers, Days, and Time — the second lesson of the unit (CURRICULUM.md),
after Numbers.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the seven days and the
twelve months — their names, their order, and before/after —
with every month carrying its number word (January is the
first month).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Numbers lesson
(a week is a count of 7; a year is a count of 12; the months'
numbers are the Numbers lesson's ordinals at work). It needs
nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown changing — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The Seven Days** — the seven days in order, and after/before and "What day is it today?" answered without stopping. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **The Twelve Months** — the twelve months in order, each with its number word ("September is the ninth month"), capitals right in writing. (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **The Calendar Machine** — the thirty-days rhyme, any month's day-count from it, and February's 28/29 and the leap year (365/366) whole. (Was depth Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Numbers. Recommended, never forced.
- Where the week STARTS is each country's own way, and the
  books show two: Egypt's list runs Saturday-first (its
  school week); Bangladesh's calendar prints Sunday-first.
  The lesson teaches the circle of days, and says plainly
  that countries start the printed week on different days —
  when books differ, the course shows both (standing rule).
- The seasons that ride the months are the Seasons material
  (Egypt's own Seasons unit) — named here, taught in their
  own place. Telling the time is the next lesson.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say the seven days in order and answer the books'
   questions: "How many days are there in a week?" — "There
   are 7 days." "What day is it today?" "What day comes
   after Monday? Before Saturday?"
2. Say the twelve months in order, each with its number:
   "January is the first month of the year… December is the
   twelfth month."
3. Write days and months with their capital first letters —
   the books' grammar rule.
4. Use the rhyme to say how many days a month holds, and
   know February's secret: 28 days, 29 in a leap year.

## What you need
A calendar — paper, wall, or phone. Paper and a pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner names today in their own
   language, then yesterday and tomorrow; the lesson gives
   the English circle (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the seven days as a circle with
   before/after; the twelve months as the year's line with
   their number words; the capital rule; the thirty-days
   rhyme (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the days part: a picture of a week wheel —
     seven day-cards in a ring — turning one step at a time,
     the day names popping as each card reaches the top,
     1–2 seconds per step — the student says the day at the
     top on each turn.
   - ADD IMAGE under the months: a picture of a real year
     calendar, twelve month pages fanned in a row → the
     student points along the fan and says each month with
     its number word — "January, the first month…"
   - ADD AUDIO in the rhyme part: the thirty-days rhyme read
     aloud in rhythm, twice — the student claps the month
     names the first time and says the rhyme with the voice
     the second.
3. **Say it to your companion** — run the before/after quiz
   and the month-number quiz on each other until neither of
   you stops to think (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the
     calendar song in the super-simple voice (made later,
     when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served
   at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — write your own week (what you do on
   each day) and your own year (your month, your family's
   months) (lesson/lesson.md).

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- GIF — a picture of a turning week wheel → the student says
  each day as it reaches the top.
- IMAGE — a picture of a fanned year calendar → the student
  names each month with its number word.
- AUDIO — the thirty-days rhyme read in rhythm → the student
  claps month names, then says it along.
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later) → the student
  sings the calendar along with it.

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the
plan is the build sheet, so nothing the lesson needs gets forgotten.

## If the learner is stuck
- Wednesday's spelling defeats everyone once → say it the
  way it is built: WED-nes-day, in three beats, the way
  Bangladesh's books stress the syllables in blue. The sound
  first; the letters follow.
- Mixes the month's name with its number → the sentence
  frame holds them together: "___ is the ___ month of the
  year." Never the name alone in this lesson.
- Asks why the week starts on different days in the two
  books → that IS the lesson's honesty point: the circle is
  the same everywhere; the starting card is each country's
  own. Their own country's calendar decides their line.
- This lesson's next door: Telling the time — the day cut
  into hours (same level, completing Unit 9).
