# Hands-on — Days and months

The lesson lived on a real calendar, the way the books print it.

## The week wheel
Draw a circle, cut it into seven slices, and write one day in
each — starting from YOUR calendar's first day. Pin a pointer
at the top. Every morning for a week, turn the pointer and
say the whole sentence: "Today is ___." Ask the wheel the
books' questions: what comes after today? Before?

## Your month
Find your birthday month on a real calendar. Say its whole
sentence: "___ is the ___ month of the year." Count its days
with your finger and check the count against the rhyme. Is
it a 30, a 31, or February?

## The unscramble race
Bangladesh's own game: write the twelve month names on
cards, cut each name's letters apart (or write them jumbled
the way its book does — brepestem, yarnjua, bedemcre), and
race a partner to rebuild the names and stand them in year
order. Capitals on the first letters — the grammar rule is
part of the race.

## The family year
On one sheet, draw the year's line — twelve boxes. In each
family month, write whose month it is (birthdays, holidays,
school starts). Read the line aloud in the book's sentences:
"March is the third month of the year. It is ___'s month."
