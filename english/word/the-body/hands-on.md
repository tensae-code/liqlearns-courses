# Hands-on — The body

The lesson on the only body you always carry.

## Listen and touch — the book's own game
Palestine's command, played for real: a partner (or a voice)
says a body word; you touch that part before the next word
comes. Start slow — head, nose, hand, leg. Speed up round by
round. A wrong touch restarts the round; three clean fast
rounds finish the game.

## The sense walk
Walk a room or a street corner using one sense at a time,
saying Egypt's sentences about real things: "I can see the
door with my eyes." "I can hear a bus with my ears." "I can
smell bread with my nose." Two true sentences per sense, ten
in all.

## Draw and label
Draw a big person on paper — or draw around a partner lying
on a big sheet, the way classes do. Label every part in
English together. Hang it where you dress; read the labels
aloud each morning for three days, touching each part as you
read it.

## Counting the body
Count aloud in English while touching: two eyes, two ears,
two hands, two legs, ten fingers, ten toes (find the word
toes in the drawing if your book's set grows) — and the
singles: one head, one nose, one mouth, one tongue. The
counting IS the review.
