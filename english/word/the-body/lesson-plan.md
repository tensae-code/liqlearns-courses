# Lesson plan — The body

**Course:** English · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** The body
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Words for the World — the fifth and last lesson of the unit (CURRICULUM.md).
Building it completes Unit 7.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the words for the parts
of the body — learned the way the books teach them: by
touching your own body and saying what each part does.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Nouns lesson
(body words are nouns) and the world sets before it. It needs
nothing else — the learner brings the teaching aid with them:
their own body.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds, and the body moves.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The Touched Seven** — Palestine's seven — head, hand, finger,
   leg, face, nose, mouth — touched and named. (Was depth Level
   1.)
2. **Parts at Work** — the five sense sentences, "I can see with
   my eyes"; the pairs-and-singles counting in its last paragraph
   is carried as Sub lesson 3 depth. (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **The Whole Body Set** — the added parts (hair, neck, elbow),
   the pairs and singles counted right, and swapped sense
   sentences corrected on sight. (Was depth Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking
is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set
is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Animals (the books teach animal parts
  and human parts side by side). Recommended, never forced.
- The five senses as a full lesson — what each sense is and
  how it works — is a science thing with its own home later;
  here the senses appear only as Egypt teaches them at this
  level: the body part + what it does ("I can see with my
  eyes"). Inside the body (heart, bones, blood) belongs to
  the science courses, not this word set.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name Palestine's set on their own body: head, hand, finger,
   leg, face, nose, mouth.
2. Name Egypt's sense parts: eyes, ears, nose, hands, tongue.
3. Say the five sense sentences: "I can see with my eyes." "I
   can hear with my ears." "I can smell with my nose." "I can
   taste with my tongue." "I can touch with my hands."
4. Point-and-name the whole set without stopping (the books'
   "Listen and touch" game, played in English).

## What you need
Only your own body and a mirror if you want one. Paper and a
pencil for the drawing step.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner points at parts of their own
   body and names them in their own language, top to bottom;
   the lesson then gives the English names (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — Palestine's seven words by listen-and-
   touch; Egypt's five sense sentences pairing part and job;
   the small extra set from Egypt's letter words — hair, neck,
   elbow (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the start of the explanation: a picture of a
     child who touches each part as its name pops big — head,
     nose, mouth, hand, finger, leg — 1–2 seconds per part —
     the student touches the same part on their own body and
     says the name with it.
   - ADD IMAGE under the sense sentences: a picture of a
     flower, a phone, an ice cream, music notes, and the moon
     in a row → the student points at each and says which body
     part it belongs to ("I can smell it with my nose").
   - ADD AUDIO in the sense part: the five sense sentences
     read aloud slowly — "I can see with my eyes…" — the
     student touches the part and repeats each sentence with
     the voice.
3. **Say it to your companion** — play Listen-and-touch with
   your companion: you say a part, they touch it and answer
   with its job where it has one (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the
     body tour in the super-simple voice (made later, when
     videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — draw a person, label ten parts, and
   write the five sense sentences from memory
   (lesson/lesson.md).

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- GIF — a picture of a child touching each body part as its
  name pops → the student touches the same part and says the
  name.
- IMAGE — a picture of five sense things (flower, phone, ice
  cream, music, moon) → the student says which body part each
  belongs to.
- AUDIO — the five sense sentences read aloud → the student
  touches the part and repeats.
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later) → the student
  does the body tour along with it.

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the
plan is the build sheet, so nothing the lesson needs gets forgotten.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes finger and hand → Palestine's set teaches them side
  by side: the hand is the whole thing; the fingers are its
  five small parts. Count them aloud while touching each.
- Says "I can see with my ears" → Egypt's own exercise is
  built exactly on that swap; laugh, trade the parts back,
  say both sentences right.
- Freezes on the whole list → the spine is seven words
  (Palestine). The sense parts are Egypt's layer on top, not
  a second gate.
- This lesson completes Unit 7, "Words for the World." Next:
  Unit 8, Using the Dictionary — the words become findable.
