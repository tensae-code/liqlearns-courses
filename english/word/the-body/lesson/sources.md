**Acceptance count: taught in 17 countries (Egypt, Palestine,
Laos, China, Mongolia, Indonesia, Iran, Sierra Leone, Jordan, Greece, Kazakhstan, Iraq, Ethiopia, South Africa, Syria, South Sudan, Nepal).**
Palestine carries the core seven by touch;
Egypt carries the parts at work in the five sense sentences,
plus three more inside its letter pages; China carries the
face and body sets with their commands; Mongolia carries the
body whole and in pieces, in pictures alone; Indonesia
carries the parts inside the hygiene habits; Iran carries
the parts named in injury talk and built into sentences;
Sierra Leone carries the first-year naming lesson with its
"Head and Shoulders" song. Jordan carries the eight-part set with has got description in Grade 1, and the widened set counted and moved by touch commands in Grade 2; Greece carries the body in pictures and songs in its two Workpacks — face pieces in Grade 1, the Pinocchio body and the counted monster features in Grade 2, with no printed body word list in either pack — and body parts as a vocabulary layer in its Grade 8 Beginners Workbook; Kazakhstan carries the face set taught by touch in Grade 1 Smiles, body parts with the actions that move them in its Grade 3 Express health module, and the body set with appearance description in Grade 5 Excel; Iraq carries the ten-part set with My … frames, monster descriptions and washing routines in Grade 1, and the body and head sets rebuilt with It has … counting in Grade 3; Ethiopia carries the twenty-part body set in Grade 1, with the "Head, shoulder, knees and toes" song (printed p.110) and the parts matched to their uses — eye to see, ear to hear, nose to smell, tongue to taste, finger to touch; South Africa carries the opening body set — head, ear, toe, thumb, hair — in Grade 1 HL, body parts with the "Head, shoulders, knees and toes" song in Grade 1 FAL, and labelled body parts with body-action verbs in Grade 2 FAL; Syria carries the six-part body set in Grade 1, the five senses with their verbs in Grade 3, and body idioms inside its Grade 12 scientific Workbook's human-body unit; South Sudan joined 2026-11-03: the fifteen-part body set named on a figure with the head-shoulders song and this/these naming in Primary 1, the guide's body-use sentences, and the set extended and counted in Primary 2; Nepal carries the body in Grade 1's touch-your-eye frames with my/his/her, and — as the guides' own teaching, those guides keying to newer workbooks that are not held — in the Grade 1 guide's Head and Shoulders and five-senses lessons and the Grade 2 guide's My Body lessons; Other finished
countries join as their books are read (see READING-QUEUE.md).
