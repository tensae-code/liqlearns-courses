# The body — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Word · Lesson: The body
Built from: Palestine, English for Palestine Grade 1 Part 1,
Unit 4 "My body" — Egypt, English Language Primary 1 Term 1,
Lesson 3 "My 5 Senses" and the letter-word pages.

## Do it first
Stand up. Touch the top of your head. Touch your nose. Open
and close your hand. You already know every part in this
lesson — you live in them. The books teach the English names
the same way you just did: **listen and touch**. Say the name
while your hand is on the part, and the word never leaves you.

## Then the explanation
Palestine's body unit gives the first seven, taught on the
child's own body: **head, hand, finger, leg, face, nose,
mouth**. Touch each one as you say it: head — the top, with
the face on its front. Face carries the nose and the mouth.
Hand ends the arm; its five small parts are the fingers. Leg
is what you stand on.

Egypt teaches the same body a second way — by what the parts
DO. Its senses lesson pairs five parts with five doing words,
in sentences you can say about yourself right now:

- **eyes** — "I can **see** with my eyes."
- **ears** — "I can **hear** with my ears."
- **nose** — "I can **smell** with my nose."
- **tongue** — "I can **taste** with my tongue."
- **hands** — "I can **touch** with my hands."

Aly and Omar end the dialog together: "We use our five senses
every day!" — "See, hear, smell, taste, and touch!" Count the
pairs: some parts come in twos (two eyes, two ears, two hands,
two legs), and some stand alone (one nose, one mouth, one
head, one tongue, ten fingers). The books say **my** with
every part — "my eyes," "my nose," "my hands" — because the
body is yours; the word rides along from the Pronouns lesson's
own box.

Egypt's letter pages add three more parts the older learners
meet there: **hair** (Hh), **neck** (Nn), **elbow** (Ee) — the
bend in the middle of the arm. Ten core words, three more on
top, and the five sentences that put them to work: that is
the body set at this level, whole.

## Say it to your companion
Play Listen-and-touch the way Palestine's book commands it:
you name a part, your companion touches it and answers with
its sentence where it has one — "Ears!" "I can hear with my
ears." Wrong touches get laughed at and fixed on the spot.
Finish with the full run, fast: head, face, nose, mouth, eyes,
ears, hair, neck, hand, finger, elbow, leg — no stopping.

## Do It Yourself
1. Draw a person — it can be you. Label ten body parts in
   English.
2. Write the five sense sentences from memory: see/eyes,
   hear/ears, smell/nose, taste/tongue, touch/hands.
3. Write three true sentences about your own body with my:
   "My hair is…" "My hands can…" — any true finish.
4. Say the whole body set aloud while touching each part,
   top to bottom, without looking at the lesson.
