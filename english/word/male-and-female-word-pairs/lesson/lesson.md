# Male and Female Word Pairs

## Do it first

Read these two words: **bride** and **bridegroom**. One names a woman on her wedding day. The other names the man. Words for people often come in twos like that — one for a male, one for a female. This lesson is about those pairs.

## Sub lesson 1 — Male words and female words

English has two sets of people words:

- **Masculine** words name **males** — men and boys: *brother, father, bridegroom, groomsmen*.
- **Feminine** words name **females** — women and girls: *girl, aunt, bride, bridesmaids, wife, sister*.

Guyana's Grade 3 book works with these words inside its wedding unit — a sister's wedding, with the bride arriving by car, the bridesmaids in long frilly dresses, and the groomsmen standing by. Every one of those words tells you, before anything else, whether the person is male or female.

Try it: say the word **aunt**. You already know the person is female. Say **father** — male, at once. That is the work these words do.

*Difficulty: first steps — two sets of words, sorted by whom they name.*

## Sub lesson 2 — The pairs

The words come in **pairs** — a masculine word matched with its feminine partner. The book pairs them like this:

- **brother — girl**
- **father — aunt**
- **bridegroom — bride**
- **groomsmen — bridesmaids**

Notice: the female word is not the male word with something added. *Bride* is its own word; *bridegroom* is its own word. Each side of the pair must be learned as a word in itself.

Now use them. When you write about a wedding, the pair words do the telling:

> The **bridegroom** waited at the church. The **bride** came by car with her **father**. Her **sister** and the **bridesmaids** stood near the door, and the **groomsmen** stood on the other side.

Every pair word in that paragraph names its person's side without one extra word of explaining. Choose the wrong side — call the bride a bridegroom — and the picture in the reader's head breaks.

*Difficulty: building — the pairs matched, then at work inside real sentences.*

## Say it to your companion

Name a pair and explain it to your companion: which word is masculine, which is feminine, and how you know. Then your companion names a person — an aunt, a brother, a bride — and you say which set the word belongs to.

## Do It Yourself

1. Sort these words into two sets, masculine and feminine: brother, bride, father, wife, groomsmen, girl, sister, bridesmaids, aunt, bridegroom.
2. Write three sentences about a wedding, using at least three pair words.
3. Find one more pair in a story you are reading, and write down both words.
