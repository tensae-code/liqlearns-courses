# Famous Paintings and Artists

## Do it first

Look at a painting and try: who painted it? Some paintings are so famous that the whole world knows the painter's name. This lesson gives you the names — the painters and their most famous paintings — and it starts, as it should, with two painters from a very small country.

## Sub lesson 1 — The painters and their paintings

*Difficulty: first steps. Names to learn in pairs — painter, painting.*

The set begins with **Liechtenstein's own two artists**. **Georg Malin** is in the set with his work **Z Dice**. **Martin Frommelt** is in the set with a painting that has no name — it is **untitled**. A country smaller than many cities stands in the same set as the greatest names in art.

Now the world names, painter by painter:

- **Leonardo da Vinci** — the **Mona Lisa**, the most famous painting in the world, a woman with a small, quiet smile.
- **Vincent van Gogh** — three of his paintings stand in the set; his wild, bright brushwork is known everywhere.
- **Pablo Picasso** — **Les Demoiselles d'Avignon**.
- **Roy Lichtenstein** — **Masterpiece**. Read his name carefully: Lichtenstein the artist is spelled almost like Liechtenstein the country, but they are two different names.
- **Friedensreich Hundertwasser** — **Sunflower and Circles**.
- **Jackson Pollock** — **Convergence**.
- **Wassily Kandinsky** — **White Point**, the work also known as Composition 8.
- **Gustav Klimt** — **The Tree of Life**.
- **Sandro Botticelli** — **The Birth of Venus**.
- **Andy Warhol** — **Marilyn**, his famous face of Marilyn Monroe.
- **Paul Cézanne** — **Harlequin**.
- **Franz Marc** — **The Little Blue Horse** — a horse, and it is blue.
- **James McNeill Whistler** — his painting stands in the set.
- **Boris Kustodiev** — **At Home**.
- The **Pahari School** of Chamba — **The Beginning of Spring**. This one was not painted by one man; a whole school of painters in the hills of India worked in this way.

Learn them as pairs: painter — painting. Mona Lisa — Leonardo. Tree of Life — Klimt. Little Blue Horse — Franz Marc. Z Dice — Georg Malin, from Liechtenstein.

## Sub lesson 2 — Talking about a famous painting

*Difficulty: a step up. Four things to say, in order.*

Knowing the names is the start. Now say something about a painting. The order is simple:

1. **Name it** — the painting's name and the artist's name: "This is The Beginning of Spring, by the Pahari School."
2. **Say what you can see** — the people, the things, the place in the picture.
3. **Name the colours** — are they bright or dark, light or dull? "Their dresses are as colourful as spring flowers."
4. **Give your opinion** — "I like the painting because it is colourful."

Four steps, and you can talk about any famous painting in the world.

## Say it to your companion

Take turns. One of you names a painting from the set; the other says who painted it, one thing in it, one colour word, and whether they like it. Then change over. Finish with the two Liechtenstein artists — say their names and their works from memory.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the pairs from memory: eight painters and their paintings. Check yourself against the lesson.
2. Choose one painting and write four sentences about it — name and artist, what you can see, its colours, your opinion.
3. Say which painting you would hang in your room, and why, in two sentences.
