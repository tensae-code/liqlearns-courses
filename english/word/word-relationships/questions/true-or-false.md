# Word relationships — true or false

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the
sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub
lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Same and Opposite · (Level 2) -> Sub
lesson 2 — Same Sound · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — One Word, Many Meanings.

Say true or false. If it is false, say why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** A synonym means the opposite of a word.
**False** — that is an antonym. A synonym means the same
or nearly the same.

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** Homophones sound the same but are spelled
differently and mean differently.
**True** — to/two, there/their (Vietnam, Grade 12, Unit 6).

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** Tanzania's word-relationship evidence
comes from all of its recorded English books.
**False** — it rests on ONE primary reader read in full
(Reading, Standard One, Pupil's Book Two); the other
recorded Tanzania books are link-only.

**4. (Sub lesson 3)** South Africa's Grade 5 Workbook 2 defines
and distinguishes homophones and homonyms.
**True** — the workbook's language spine, as filed.
