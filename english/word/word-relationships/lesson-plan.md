# Lesson plan — Word relationships

**Course:** English · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** Word relationships
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Words for the World —
how words stand toward each other: same, opposite, same
sound, many meanings (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the four relationships
— synonyms (same meaning: buy / purchase), antonyms
(opposite meaning: hot / cold), homophones (same sound,
different word: to / two), and homonyms (one word, more
than one meaning: mouse the animal, mouse the computer
part).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Using the
dictionary (Level 8's skill, met early here as a hint: an
entry gives synonyms and antonyms) and on Word building
(parts make words; this lesson is about whole words and
their partners). It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown changing — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Same and Opposite** — synonyms and antonyms from the books'
   pairs, without a miss. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Same Sound** — homophones told apart in sentences, choosing
   by meaning, not by ear. (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **One Word, Many Meanings** — a homonym's meanings shown in two
   sentences, and closest-meaning and opposite-meaning questions
   answered. (Was depth Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking
is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set
is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Word building (same level). Recommended,
  never forced — this lesson stands alone: every relationship
  is taught on word pairs the learner already knows.
- Word building takes words APART; this lesson keeps words
  WHOLE and pairs them. The two skills are neighbours, not
  the same thing.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Give a synonym — a word with the same or nearly the same
   meaning — from Tanzania's pairs: until/till, buy/purchase,
   hate/dislike, speak/talk, search/look for.
2. Give an antonym — the opposite — hot/cold, big/small,
   happy/sad — and use closest/opposite-meaning questions
   the way Vietnam's reviews ask them.
3. Tell homophones apart by meaning and spelling: to/two,
   there/their, allowed/aloud (Vietnam's set).
4. Show a homonym's two meanings in two sentences: the
   mouse that eats cheese; the mouse beside the keyboard.

## What you need
Word cards for pairing games. Paper and a pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner says a word — BIG — and
   finds two other words: one that means the same, one that
   means the opposite. The lesson then names all four
   relationships (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — synonyms from Tanzania's reader;
   antonyms; homophones heard before they are seen;
   homonyms and the two mouses (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the homophones part: a picture of the number
     2 and the word TO flashing in turn while one same sound
     plays, 1–2 seconds — same sound, two spellings — the
     student says both spellings aloud.
   - ADD IMAGE under homonyms: a picture of a real mouse
     (the animal) beside a computer mouse — the student
     points at each and says which mouse the sentence "The
     mouse is hungry" means.
   - ADD AUDIO at the start: a voice reading Tanzania's
     same-meaning pairs — until/till, buy/purchase — the
     student repeats the second word of each pair after the
     voice.
3. **Say it to your companion** — the pairing game: one
   names a word, the other answers with its synonym, then
   its antonym (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the
     word partners in the super-simple voice (made later,
     when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — build a partner page for six words:
   synonym, antonym, and one trap pair (lesson/lesson.md).

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- GIF — a picture of 2 and TO flashing to one same sound →
  the student says both spellings aloud.
- IMAGE — a picture of an animal mouse beside a computer
  mouse → the student says which mouse a sentence means.
- AUDIO — same-meaning pairs read aloud → the student
  repeats each pair's second word.
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later) → the student
  answers with partners along with it.

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the
plan is the build sheet, so nothing the lesson needs gets forgotten.

## If the learner is stuck
- Gives an antonym when asked for a synonym → the hands:
  thumbs together for SAME (synonym), thumbs apart for
  OPPOSITE (antonym). Tanzania's word for the first family
  is the book's own: "words with the same meaning."
- Writes two for the sound /tuː/ in every sentence → the
  ear cannot fix this one; the MEANING can: number two,
  movement to, also too. Read the sentence, ask which job
  the word is doing.
- Thinks homonym is a spelling mistake → Tanzania's reader
  itself plays the trap: Mr Mouse asks "Where is my mouth?"
  One word, two meanings is a real thing with a name.
- This lesson's next door: Using the dictionary (Level 8) —
  entries print synonyms and antonyms; this lesson teaches
  what those gifts mean.
