# Hands-on — Word relationships

The lesson with words in your hands, not only on the page.

## Partner cards
Write twelve word cards — big, hot, happy, buy, begin,
fast, cold, small, sad, sell, end, slow. Spread them face
up. Pair each card with its antonym, then go back and give
a spoken synonym for each pair's first word. Tanzania's
same-meaning pairs start the set: until/till, buy/purchase.

## The ear trap
A partner reads sentences aloud with a homophone hidden
inside: "I have two cats." "We walk to school." "Their
house is big." You write the word you hear — spelling it,
not saying it. The ear hears one sound; your hand must
choose. Five sentences, then swap reader and writer.

## Two-mouse drawing
Draw two mice: one eating cheese, one beside a keyboard.
Write one sentence under each, using mouse both times.
That drawing is a homonym, pinned to the wall.

## Closest and opposite
Play Vietnam's review game with any page of any book:
point at a word, and your partner must answer in five
seconds — closest in meaning, then opposite in meaning.
Ten words each.
