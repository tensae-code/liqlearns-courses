**Acceptance count: taught in 10 countries (Mongolia, North
Macedonia, Palestine, Rwanda, Bangladesh, Bolivia, China, Iran,
Indonesia, Mauritania).** Palestine carries the
first set with the Who's this? pattern; North Macedonia
carries the six-word labelled photograph, the naming frame
and the formal/informal pair side by side — its copy holds
only the book's opening (printed pp.3–11), and nothing
beyond that is claimed; Mongolia carries the wide tree —
in-laws, great grandfather, nephew — twice, at Grades 9
and 10; Rwanda carries the tree as a crossword map in its
final primary year; Bangladesh carries the family as a
senior reading on family bonds (added 2026-11-08 by the
re-verification sweep). China carries the family set a
second time up in Grade 4, widening it with uncles, aunts
and cousins (stocked 2026-11-08 by the re-verification
sweep). Iran carries the family set joined to a jobs set,
asked through the father's job, with the workbook practising
the same set (stocked 2026-11-09 by the re-verification
sweep merge). Indonesia carries the family set at primary
level — the members named as a chapter's key words in
Grade 3, with talk about where the family is from — and
repeats the set as a Chapter 0 recap at Grades 7 and 9
(stocked 2026-11-09 by the re-verification sweep merge). Other closed countries join as their
books are read (see READING-QUEUE.md).
Mauritania introduces the family in a written message in its
Grade 7 book (stocked 2026-11-09 by the Mauritania re-
verification sweep merge).
