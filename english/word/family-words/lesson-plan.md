# Lesson plan — Family words

**Course:** English · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** Family words
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Words for the World (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the kinship words
beyond mum and dad — grandfather, grandmother, uncle, aunt,
cousin, nephew, niece and the in-law words — set out on the
family tree, each word with its place.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The home (the
family lives there) and Level 1 reading. It needs nothing
else. If you know the home words already, this lesson will
be easier for you — you might try The home first.
Recommended, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Your Own Row and Your Parents** — father, mother, brother,
   sister, baby and me, with "Who's this? — This is my…". (Was
   depth Level 1.)
2. **The Whole Tree** — grandfather, grandmother, uncle, aunt and
   cousin in their tree places, in both registers. (Was depth
   Level 2.)
3. **The Wide Tree** — nephew, niece, the in-laws and great
   grandfather, and introducing a whole family. (Was depth Level
   3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking
is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set
is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The home (this level) and Nouns
  (Level 6). Recommended, never forced.
- The first family words (mum, dad, brother, sister) ride
  in with The home in the built course; this lesson's own
  work is the wider tree — grandparents, uncles, aunts,
  cousins and the married-in words.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the tree: grandfather, grandmother, father,
   mother, uncle, aunt, brother, sister, cousin.
2. Use both registers the books teach side by side:
   father/mother and daddy/mummy; grandfather and grandpa.
3. Answer Palestine's question: "Who's this?" — "This is
   my brother / my mum."
4. Place the married-in and next-generation words at
   Level 3: nephew, niece, mother-in-law, father-in-law,
   daughter-in-law, great grandfather.

## What you need
Your own family, in your head or in a photograph. Paper
for your tree.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner names their family in
   their own language, oldest to youngest; the lesson
   then gives the English tree (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the tree from the top: the
   grandparents, the parents' generation with uncle and
   aunt, your own generation with cousin; the two
   registers; the in-law words (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE at the start of the explanation: a picture
     of a family group photograph with grandfather,
     grandmother, father, mother, brother and sister —
     the student points and names each person before
     reading on (North Macedonia's Lesson 1 picture).
   - ADD GIF in the tree part: a picture of a family tree
     drawing itself branch by branch, grandparents at
     the top down to the children — the student names
     each word as its branch grows.
   - ADD AUDIO in the question part: a voice asking
     "Who's this?" about five family photographs — the
     student answers "This is my…" before the voice does.
3. **Say it to your companion** — show your companion your
   family (real or drawn) and introduce each person:
   "This is my grandfather, Bill" (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed —
     the family introductions in the super-simple voice
     (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — draw your family tree and label
   every person with their kinship word (lesson/lesson.md).

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- IMAGE — a picture of a labelled family group photograph
  → the student points and names each person.
- GIF — a picture of a family tree drawing itself branch
  by branch → the student names each word as it grows.
- AUDIO — "Who's this?" questions read aloud → the student
  answers "This is my…" first.
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later) → the
  student introduces along with it.

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the
plan is the build sheet, so nothing the lesson needs gets forgotten.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes uncle and cousin → the generation fixes it: an
  uncle stands in your parents' row; a cousin stands in
  yours. Point at the tree, not the word.
- Mixes nephew and niece → nephew is the boy, niece is
  the girl — the same split as brother and sister, one
  row down.
- This lesson's next door: The home — the house this
  family lives in.
