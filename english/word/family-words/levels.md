# Family words — questions by level

Practice by level, tied to the skill number. The questions live in
the type files; this file is the by-level view. The levels are the
depths inside this one thing — the kinship words — set by the
pattern across the countries' books: your own row first, then
the whole tree, then the married-in words.

## Level 1 — your own row and your parents
Level 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: name
father, mother, brother, sister, baby and me, and answer
"Who's this?" — "This is my…" — without a miss (Palestine).

## Level 2 — the whole tree
Level 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: name
grandfather, grandmother, uncle, aunt and cousin in their
tree places, in both registers — father and daddy, mother
and mummy — the way North Macedonia teaches them side by
side.

## Level 3 — the wide tree
Level 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: place
nephew, niece, mother-in-law, father-in-law,
daughter-in-law and great grandfather on the tree, and
introduce a whole family in the books' frames — "This is
my grandfather, Bill." "I'm Tracy and this is my family."
