# Media and Journalism

## Do it first

Think of the last news you heard. Where did it reach you — a paper, a radio, a screen? This lesson names the media and the parts of a newspaper, in English.

## Sub lesson 1 — The newspaper

A newspaper is built in parts. The **front page** carries the day's biggest news under its **headline** — the big line that names the story. Inside are the **sections** — sports, culture, economy — and the **articles** people write, with **photographs** beside them.

Name the parts and the paper stops being a pile of pages: it becomes a built thing you can read on purpose.

*Difficulty: The paper taken apart, part by part.*

## Sub lesson 2 — The media and their power

The **media of communication** carry news to everyone: the **press** — newspapers and magazines; **radio** — voices through the air; **television** — pictures and voices together.

The book names their weight plainly: the media are called the **third, fourth and fifth power** in a country — after the powers that govern, the powers that inform. Who informs, shapes what people know.

*Difficulty: The media weighed — a vocabulary lesson with an idea inside it.*

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion where your family gets its news, and which part of a newspaper you would read first.

## Do It Yourself

1. Find a newspaper (paper or online). Write its parts in English and one headline in your own words.
2. Write the three media and one thing each one is best at.
3. Ask someone at home which medium they trust most. Write their answer.
