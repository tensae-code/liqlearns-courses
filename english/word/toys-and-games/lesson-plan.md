# Lesson plan — Toys and Games

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** Toys and Games
**Lesson:** toys-and-games — naming toys, saying your favourite toy, and asking to borrow a game
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Words for the World
**The one thing:** Toy and game words — what children play with, named and talked about.
**This lesson's own level:** Word lesson (play): toy names, favourite-toy talk, and May I borrow…? for sharing games.

## Why this lesson exists

Indonesia's Grade 4 book teaches the toys set inside its toys chapter — naming toys, saying a favourite toy, and borrowing a board game — and no course lesson held it: the word lessons name school things, home things and town things, but not the things of play. This lesson is built from the placements under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06).

## Sub lessons

1. Toy words — naming what we play with
2. My favourite toy
3. Borrowing a game — May I borrow…?

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is needed before this lesson. Describing People carries the same book's toy described by shape and colour; this lesson names the toys themselves, and each stands alone.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- Name the common toys in English.
- Say your favourite toy and one thing about it.
- Ask to borrow a game politely, and answer such an ask.

## What you need
Nothing special — one real toy or game helps, or a picture of one.

## The run of the lesson
1. ADD IMAGE: a toy box opened — ball, doll, kite, train, board game. The learner names any they know before the words appear.
2. Teach sub lesson 1 by pointing: real toys first, pictures second.
3. ADD GIF (1–2 seconds): a child offering a board game to a friend — sharing is the point of sub lesson 3.
4. Sub lesson 2 is show-and-tell: each learner's favourite toy, one full sentence each.

## Add-ons
- Toy shop: learners "sell" a toy in one sentence: "This kite flies high!"
- Borrow chain: a game passes around the class; each hand-over needs a polite "May I borrow…?" and a kind answer.

## If the learner is stuck
Keep to six toy words, named by pointing, until they come without thinking. Favourite talk and borrowing are one step each after.

## Teacher note
Indonesia's Grade 4 toys chapter runs naming → favourite → borrowing inside one chapter; the lesson keeps that order. The chapter's "May I borrow…?" also stocks Asking Politely; here it is taught as the sharing move of play, there as the polite-request form.
