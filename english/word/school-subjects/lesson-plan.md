# Lesson plan — School subjects

**Course:** English · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** School subjects
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Words for the World (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the school-subject
words — Mathematics, Science, History, Geography, Art,
Music, English and their partners — each with the books'
liking frame: "I like… because it is…" and the timetable
they live on.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Level 1 reading
and Days and months (the timetable is a week). It needs
nothing else. If you know the days already, this lesson
will be easier for you — you might try Days and months
first. Recommended, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the subject set: Mathematics (Maths), Science,
   History, Geography, English, Art, Music, Physical
   Education, Technology, Computer, Social Studies.
2. Ask and answer: "What subjects do you study?" and
   "What's your favourite subject?"
3. Say a liking with its reason, Rwanda's shape: "I like
   Science because it is interesting." "I don't like…
   because it is difficult."
4. Read a timetable: subjects against days and times.

## What you need
Your own school timetable, or the memory of one. Your
real week is the material.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner names every subject
   they study or studied, in their own language; the
   lesson then gives the English names (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — Mexico's ten; Rwanda's eight on
   the timetable; the liking frame; favourites
   (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE at the start of the explanation: a picture
     of a school timetable for one week, subjects in
     coloured boxes — the student points and names five
     subjects before reading on.
   - ADD GIF in the liking part: a picture of a child's
     face lighting up at a Science book and frowning at
     a hard sums page, 1–2 seconds — the student says
     "I like Science because it is interesting" as the
     face lights.
   - ADD AUDIO in the question part: a voice asking
     "What's your favourite subject?" — the student
     answers with a subject and a because before the
     voice does.
3. **Say it to your companion** — compare timetables and
   favourites, each with its because (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed —
     the school-week report in the super-simple voice
     (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — your own timetable in English,
   with one liking sentence per day (lesson/lesson.md).

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes History and Geography → the books' own split:
  History is people and times past; Geography is places
  and the earth now. Mexico's list teaches them side by
  side for a reason.
- Says "I like Maths because it is easy" about a subject
  they hate → swap in the true subject; the frame only
  works when the feeling is real (Rwanda's adjectives:
  boring, difficult, easy, fantastic, interesting,
  tough…).
- This lesson's next door: Jobs — where each subject
  can take you.
