# Lesson plan — Money and prices

**Course:** English · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** Money and prices
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 7, "Words for the World" —
the words for buying, selling and what things cost
(CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the money words —
cost, price, coin, note, save, lend, borrow — the names of
currencies (riel, soum, pound, yen, euro), and the two price
questions: "How much is it?" and "How much does it cost?"
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Numbers (Level 9 —
a price is a number with a money word after it) and on The
town (the market and the shop are where prices are asked).
It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown changing — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Numbers (Level 9). Recommended, never
  forced — this lesson stands alone: the prices in it are
  small and written out in words as well as figures.
- Shopping talk — choosing, trying, paying in a full
  dialogue — is a conversation lesson of its own. This
  lesson is the money words and the price questions only.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Ask both price questions, singular and plural: "How much
   is a pencil?" "How much are the clothes?" "How much does
   an orange cost?" (Cambodia)
2. Answer with a price in a real currency: "An orange costs
   900 Riels." "One pencil costs 1,200 Riels." (Cambodia)
3. Name currencies from Sudan's set — pound, yen, euro —
   and use about/around for a price that is not exact.
4. Use Uzbekistan's money set in sentences: coin, note,
   exchange, cheque, account, income, salary, save, lend,
   borrow — and tell lend from borrow every time.

## What you need
A few coins or notes, or drawn money. Paper and a pencil
for a price list.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner holds up a thing — a pencil,
   a fruit — and asks in their own language what it costs.
   The lesson then gives the English questions
   (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — Cambodia's price ladder from
   hundreds to thousands; the currency names; Uzbekistan's
   money set with lend/borrow separated (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the questions part: a picture of a market
     stall, a price card flipping from one orange to three
     oranges, 1–2 seconds — "How much is it?" changing to
     "How much are they?" — the student asks the right
     question for each card.
   - ADD IMAGE under currencies: a picture of real coins and
     notes of different countries — the student names the
     money they know and finds the pound, the yen, the euro.
   - ADD AUDIO in the lend/borrow part: a voice lending a
     coin hand to hand — "I lend you 10. You borrow 10 from
     me." — the student points at who has the coin at each
     line.
3. **Say it to your companion** — a market game: one sells,
   one buys; every thing needs its price question and its
   price answer (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the
     market stall in the super-simple voice (made later,
     when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — write a price list for five things
   and the questions that go with them (lesson/lesson.md).

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- GIF — a picture of a market price card flipping, one thing
  to many → the student asks is/are with the card.
- IMAGE — a picture of coins and notes of different
  countries → the student names the currencies.
- AUDIO — lend and borrow read over a coin passing hands →
  the student points at who holds the coin.
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later) → the student
  asks the price questions along with it.

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the
plan is the build sheet, so nothing the lesson needs gets forgotten.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes "How much is…?" and "How much does it cost?" → both
  are right; Cambodia teaches both over the same stalls.
  Pick one per thing and finish the answer — the price is
  the point, not the frame.
- Says "How much is the clothes?" → one thing takes is, many
  things take are — Cambodia's pair is the drill: "How much
  is a …? / How much are the clothes?"
- Swaps lend and borrow → the hand teaches it: lend goes
  out of my hand, borrow comes into my hand. One coin, two
  directions, two words.
- This lesson's next door: the Shopping conversation lesson
  uses these questions inside a whole dialogue; the words
  here are the tools it runs on.
