# Science and Inventions

## Do it first

Scientists grow human embryos in a laboratory. Is that a wonder — or a step too far? This lesson reads the science first, then teaches you to argue about it.

## Sub lesson 1 — A new science idea — cloning

Grade 11 reads about **cloning**: scientists in Scotland, at the **Roslin Institute**, and the growing of **human embryos** in the laboratory.

The reading gives the science plainly — what cloning is, where it is done, and what it is for — and then steps back to let the reader weigh it.

*Difficulty: A hard new idea, read at the book's own pace.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Arguing about science

The book's second half is yours: **write an opinion essay** on the cloning reading. An opinion essay states your view, gives your reasons, and answers the other side.

Science gives the facts; the argument is about what to *do* with them — that argument is this sub lesson.

*Difficulty: From reading to judgement — a view, reasons, and the other side answered.*

## Say it to your companion

Give your companion your opinion on cloning in two sentences — view, then reason. They answer with the other side. Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write where the cloning reading is set and who works there.
2. Write three sentences of an opinion essay: your view, one reason, one answer to the other side.
3. Write one question you would ask the Roslin scientists.
