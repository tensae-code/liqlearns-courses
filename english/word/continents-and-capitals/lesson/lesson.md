# Continents and Capitals

## Do it first

Look at a map of the world. Find your own country. Try to say:

- "My country is ___."
- "It is on the continent of ___."
- "Its capital city is ___."

This lesson is the map's own words: continents, countries, and
capitals.

## Sub lesson 1 — The continents

The world is made of very big lands. Each very big land is a
**continent**.

On the world map you can see them: **Africa**, **Europe**,
**Asia**, **North America**, **South America**, **Australia**
and **Antarctica**.

- Africa is our continent — Rwanda is a country in Africa.
- Europe is north of Africa, across the sea.
- Asia is the biggest continent, east of Europe and Africa.
- North America and South America stand across the ocean, west
  of Africa.
- Australia is a continent and a country at the same time.
- Antarctica is the cold continent at the bottom of the world,
  covered in ice.

Say it whole: "A continent is a very big land. Africa is a
continent. Rwanda is a country in Africa."

*Difficulty: first steps — say each continent's name while pointing at it on the map, never from a bare list.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Countries and capitals

A continent holds many **countries**. Every country has a
**capital** — its chief city, where the country's government
sits.

Look closely at East Africa, the part of the map the books
work on. Rwanda's neighbours are **Uganda**, **Tanzania**,
**Burundi** and the **Democratic Republic of Congo**. Rwanda's
capital is **Kigali**.

The world's great river runs through this part of the map: the
**Nile**. It flows north through East Africa to Egypt and the
sea, and it is the longest river in the world — **6,670
kilometres** long.

Ask and answer with a partner: "What is the capital of
Rwanda?" — "Kigali." "Which river is the longest in the
world?" — "The Nile — 6,670 kilometres."

*Difficulty: building — countries and capitals are worked on the map, with the Nile's length as the set's measure fact.*

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion about your own country: its continent, its
capital, and one neighbour. Your companion asks one question —
"How long is the Nile?" or "What is the capital of ___?" — and
you answer. Then change places.

## Do It Yourself

1. On a world map, colour your own continent and write its
   name big across it.
2. Write the names of Rwanda's four East African neighbours
   and mark Rwanda's capital, Kigali.
3. Write two sentences about the Nile: where it flows, and how
   long it is.
