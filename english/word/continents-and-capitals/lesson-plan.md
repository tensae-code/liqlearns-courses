# Lesson plan — Continents and Capitals

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** Continents and Capitals
**Lesson:** continents-and-capitals — the world's big lands, the
countries on them, and each country's capital city; worked
closely on Rwanda and its East African neighbours, and on the
Nile, the world's longest river at 6,670 km
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Words for the World
**The one thing:** reading the world map the way the books teach
it — continents first, then countries and their capitals.
**This lesson's own level:** Word lesson: the continents first,
then countries and capitals on them.

## Why this lesson exists

No lesson housed the world-geography set. Rwanda's Primary 5
book teaches geography of the world — continents, countries and
capitals from a world map (printed pp.164–166) — and its
Primary 4 book's "About the World" text works East African
neighbours, capitals and the Nile at 6,670 km (printed
pp.111–113). The 2026-11-08 re-verification sweep read the
books page by page and marked both parts NEW LESSON NEEDED.
Under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06), a teaching
item with no lesson gets a new lesson built; this is that
lesson. Its sources are the Rwanda books named in
lesson/sources.md.

## Sub lessons

1. The continents — the world's big lands on the map
2. Countries and capitals — a country and its chief city, worked on East Africa and the Nile

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is forced first — this lesson stands alone: it starts
from "the world is made of big lands and many countries" and
teaches every word it uses.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- Say what a continent is and name the continents on the world map.
- Say what a capital is and name the capital of their own country.
- Name Rwanda's East African neighbours and place the Nile as the world's longest river, 6,670 km.

## What you need
A map of the world showing the continents and countries.

## The run of the lesson
1. **Do it first** — the learner finds their own country on the
   world map and names the big land it sits on before anyone
   teaches the words (lesson/lesson.md).
2. ADD IMAGE: the world map with the continents in different
   colours, unnamed. The learner guesses the big lands before
   the names are taught.
3. Work through sub lesson 1: the continents.
4. Work through sub lesson 2: countries and capitals, worked
   on East Africa, then the Nile.
5. ADD VIDEO (short): the map zooms from the whole world down
   to East Africa, then follows the Nile north — each time a
   name is used, it grows big on screen: CONTINENT… CAPITAL…
   THE NILE, 6,670 KILOMETRES…

## Add-ons
- "My continent": the class presents their own continent —
  its name, three of its countries, and the capital of their
  own country.
- River talk: name the longest river of your own continent
  the way the book names the Nile.

## If the learner is stuck
Cut back to two words — continent and capital — on the map of
their own country until they are sure. Add the rest one at a
time.

## Teacher note
The Primary 5 book's topic heading prints the word as "Naming
Continets" — a book error; the lesson teaches the word
corrected (continents), as the placements record. The books
teach the world from the map outwards — continents, then
countries and capitals — and this lesson keeps that shape.
