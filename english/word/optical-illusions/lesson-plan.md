# Lesson Plan — Optical Illusions

**One thing:** optical illusions — pictures that fool your eyes — the classic named illusions, and how to talk about what you see.

## Sub lessons

1. Two pictures in one — the Duck or Rabbit, the Cat and Mouse, the young-or-old woman: one picture that can be seen two ways, and saying what you see
2. Fooling size, shape and movement — the Tiger, the Mona Lisa illusion, the Chevreul illusion, the Missing Clown, the Fraser Spiral, Rotating Snakes, White's illusion, the Table illusion, the hole in your hand, another finger; choosing one illusion, finding out about it, and presenting it to the class

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Teaching points

- **An optical illusion is a picture that tricks your eyes.** You look — and your eyes tell you something that is not true, or two true things at once.
- **Two pictures in one:** the **Duck or Rabbit** is a duck's head looking left — turn it, and it is a rabbit looking right. The **Cat and Mouse** hides a mouse in the cat picture. The **young-or-old woman** is a young woman looking away — look again, and she is an old woman looking down. The talk that goes with them: "What do you see?" — "I see a duck." — "Look again!"
- **Fooling size, shape and movement:** the **Table illusion** — two tables that look different in size but are the same. The **Fraser Spiral** — circles that look like a spiral. **Rotating Snakes** — snakes that seem to move but hold still. **White's illusion** and the **Chevreul illusion** — the same grey looking light in one place and dark in another. The **Tiger** and the **Mona Lisa illusion** and the **Missing Clown** hide and change what you see. Two you do with your own body: the **hole in your hand** and **another finger**.
- **The class work:** choose one illusion, find out how it fools the eye, draw or print it, and present it to the class — show it, let everyone look, then explain.
