# Optical Illusions

## Do it first

Look at a picture of a duck. Now turn your head — is it a rabbit? Your eyes can be fooled, and the pictures that fool them have names. This lesson teaches the famous ones: first the pictures that are two pictures in one, then the ones that fool you about size, shape and movement.

## Sub lesson 1 — Two pictures in one

*Difficulty: first steps. Look, say what you see, look again.*

An **optical illusion** is a picture that tricks your eyes. The gentlest kind is one picture that can be seen **two ways**:

- **The Duck or Rabbit** — a head in profile. Look one way: a duck, its beak pointing left. Look again: a rabbit, its ears pointing right. It is both, and it is one drawing.
- **The Cat and Mouse** — a cat. But hide inside the cat's lines is a mouse. Find it!
- **The young-or-old woman** — a young woman in a hat, looking away over her shoulder. Look again, slowly: the young woman's chin is the old woman's nose — an old woman looking down. Two women, one picture.

The talk that belongs to these pictures is short: **"What do you see?"** — **"I see a duck."** — **"Look again!"** Nobody is wrong. The picture holds both.

## Sub lesson 2 — Fooling size, shape and movement

*Difficulty: a step up. The eye is fooled about how big, what shape, and whether it moves.*

The second family fools you about the world itself:

- **The Table illusion** — two tables, one looking long and thin, one looking short and wide. Measure them: they are exactly the same size and shape.
- **The Fraser Spiral** — rings that look like a spiral winding in. Trace one ring with your finger: it is a true circle, closed. There is no spiral.
- **Rotating Snakes** — circles of snakes that seem to turn and crawl. They never move. Only your eyes move.
- **White's illusion** and the **Chevreul illusion** — the same grey, in stripes or steps, looking light in one place and dark in another. The grey never changes; its neighbours change it.
- **The Tiger** and the **Missing Clown** — pictures that hide something in plain sight until your eye finds it; and the **Mona Lisa illusion**, where the famous face changes as you look.
- Two illusions live on your own body: the **hole in your hand** — look through a rolled paper tube with one eye, hold your other hand beside it, and a hole opens in your palm that is not there; and **another finger** — hold one finger up, look past it, and it doubles.

The unit's class work is the real end of this lesson: **choose one illusion, find out about it, and present it to the class** — show the picture, let everyone look and guess, then explain how it fools the eye.

## Say it to your companion

Show your companion one of the two-in-one pictures and ask: "What do you see?" When they answer, say "Look again!" and let them find the second picture. Then change over, and finish by explaining the Table illusion in your own words: what the eye says, and what the ruler says.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw or find the Duck or Rabbit and the young-or-old woman. Under each, write the two things the picture can be.
2. Do the hole-in-your-hand trick and the another-finger trick. Write one sentence for each: what you did, and what you saw.
3. Choose your favourite illusion from the lesson and write its name, what the eye thinks it sees, and what is really there.
