# World Landmarks

## Do it first

Think of the most famous place in your country. Try to say:

- "Its name is ___."
- "It is a ___ (a tower, a church, a wall, a statue)."
- "It stands in ___."

People all over the world know some buildings by name, the way
everyone knows your famous place. This lesson is those names:
the world's landmarks.

## Sub lesson 1 — The famous structures

A **landmark** is a famous building or structure that everyone
knows.

- **Big Ben** is the great clock tower in London. When people
  see the tower, they know the city.
- **St Basil's** is a church in Moscow with many coloured domes
  — round tops like onions.
- The **Giza Pyramids** stand in Egypt, near the Nile. They are
  very old stone triangles, built as tombs for kings.
- The **Great Wall** is in China. It is a very long wall that
  runs over hills and mountains for thousands of kilometres.

Say the four with their places: "Big Ben is in London. St
Basil's is in Moscow. The Giza Pyramids are in Egypt. The Great
Wall is in China."

*Difficulty: first steps — say each landmark inside its own sentence, with its place, never alone.*

## Sub lesson 2 — More landmarks, and where they stand

Two more landmarks complete the set:

- **Christ the Redeemer** is a very big statue of Christ on a
  mountain over the city of Rio de Janeiro, in Brazil. Its arms
  are open wide.
- The **Burj Khalifa** is in Dubai, in the United Arab
  Emirates. It is the tallest building in the world.

Now the whole set, each with its country:

- Big Ben — the United Kingdom (London)
- St Basil's — Russia (Moscow)
- The Giza Pyramids — Egypt (Giza)
- The Great Wall — China
- Christ the Redeemer — Brazil (Rio de Janeiro)
- The Burj Khalifa — the United Arab Emirates (Dubai)

Ask and answer with a partner: "Where is the Great Wall?" —
"It is in China." "What is the Burj Khalifa?" — "It is the
tallest building in the world, in Dubai."

*Difficulty: building — the names join their countries in one whole world set.*

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion about the six landmarks: the name, what it
is, and where it stands. Your companion listens and counts:
how many of the six did you place in the right country? Then
change places.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw two landmarks from this lesson and write the name and
   the country under each drawing.
2. Write three sentences about the most famous place in your
   own country: its name, what it is, and where it stands.
3. On a map of the world, find the six countries of this
   lesson: the United Kingdom, Russia, Egypt, China, Brazil
   and the United Arab Emirates.
