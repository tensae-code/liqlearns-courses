# Lesson plan — World Landmarks

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** World Landmarks
**Lesson:** world-landmarks — the famous buildings and structures
of the world that the books name: Big Ben, St Basil's, Christ the
Redeemer, the Burj Khalifa, the Giza Pyramids, the Great Wall —
and the countries they stand in
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Words for the World
**The one thing:** the world's famous landmark set, learned whole,
each landmark tied to the country it stands in, the way the
book's world unit presents them.
**This lesson's own level:** Word lesson: the landmarks first,
then where each one stands.

## Why this lesson exists

No lesson housed the world-landmark set. Rwanda's Primary 4
book teaches countries and famous structures — Big Ben, St
Basil's, Christ the Redeemer, the Burj Khalifa, the Giza
Pyramids and the Great Wall — with work on locating the
countries they stand in (printed pp.104–106). The 2026-11-08
re-verification sweep read the book page by page and marked
this part NEW LESSON NEEDED. Under the everything-goes-in
ruling (2026-11-06), a teaching item with no lesson gets a new
lesson built; this is that lesson. Its only source is the
Rwanda book named in lesson/sources.md.

## Sub lessons

1. The famous structures — Big Ben, St Basil's, the Giza Pyramids, the Great Wall
2. More landmarks, and where they stand — Christ the Redeemer, the Burj Khalifa, and locating every landmark's country

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is forced first — this lesson stands alone: it starts
from "some buildings are famous all over the world" and teaches
every word it uses.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- Name the six landmarks the book teaches and say what each one is.
- Say which country each landmark stands in.
- Talk about a famous place in their own country the same way.

## What you need
A map of the world, or a globe, and pictures of famous places if
you have them.

## The run of the lesson
1. **Do it first** — the learner names any famous building or
   place they know before anyone teaches the set
   (lesson/lesson.md).
2. ADD IMAGE: the six landmarks in a row, unnamed. The learner
   guesses which is which before the names are taught.
3. Work through sub lesson 1: the first four structures, each
   met inside its own sentence.
4. Work through sub lesson 2: the last two, then the country
   for every landmark.
5. ADD VIDEO (short): a flight over the world map — each time
   the flight lands, the landmark's name grows big on screen:
   BIG BEN… THE GREAT WALL…

## Add-ons
- "Our landmark": the class picks the most famous place in
  their own country and presents it with the lesson's pattern:
  its name, what it is, where it stands.
- Quiz a partner: "Where is the Burj Khalifa?" — "It is in
  Dubai, in the United Arab Emirates."

## If the learner is stuck
Cut back to three landmarks — the Giza Pyramids, the Great
Wall, Big Ben — drawn and named until they are sure. Add the
rest one at a time.

## Teacher note
The book teaches this set inside its world unit — the landmarks
are met as real places on the map, never as a bare list, and
this lesson keeps that shape: every name is tied to what the
thing is and the country it stands in.
