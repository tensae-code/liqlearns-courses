# Media Words

## Do it first

Newspapers, television, the internet — you meet the media every day. But can you name its parts in English? Bahrain's coursebook teaches the words as one set, sorted by channel. Learn the set — then talk about your own media use.

## Sub lesson 1 — Media words by channel

The book sorts the media words under three channels.

**Newspapers and magazines.** An **article** is a piece of writing in a newspaper or magazine. The **front page** is the first page, and the big title on it is the **headline**. A **journalist** is the person who writes the reports. An **advert** is a notice that tries to sell you something — adverts live in every channel.

**Television and radio.** A **channel** is one television or radio station's home — you change channel to change what you watch. A **programme** is one show on it. The **presenter** is the person who speaks to you and runs the show. A **celebrity** is a famous person, and a **drama** is a story played by actors. A **video** is a moving picture you can watch.

**Computers and the internet.** A **blog** is a page where a person writes their pieces for anyone to read. An **email** is a letter sent by computer. A site's first page is its **homepage**. A **social networking site** is a place where people meet and share online. **Spam** is email you never asked for — the junk kind.

The verbs go with the words: you **read** an article, **watch** an advert, **write** a blog or an email, **listen to** a programme, **use** or **go on** a social networking site, and **upload** a video — put it up for others to see.

Say the set in one breath: article, headline, front page, journalist, advert — channel, programme, presenter, celebrity, drama — blog, email, homepage, social networking site, spam, video.

Now write three true sentences about your own media use — one for each channel: "I read an article about…" / "I watch a programme called…" / "I go on…".

## Sub lesson 2 — TV programme kinds

Television programmes come in kinds, and each kind has a name.

A **game show** is a programme where people play games. A **quiz show** is where they answer questions — both kinds give prizes to the winners. A **current affairs programme** talks about the news and the big questions of today. A **cookery programme** shows cooking. A **talent show** is where singers, dancers and other performers show what they can do and someone chooses the best. A **chat show** is where a presenter talks with guests. A **wildlife documentary** is a true film about wild animals and their world. A **lifestyle show** is about homes, food, health and how people live.

Look at a day of television — on a TV guide, an app or a channel page — and sort the programmes into the eight kinds. Which kind do you watch most?

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion about your media: "I read… I watch… I go on…" — one true sentence for each channel. Then name your favourite programme kind and one programme of that kind. They must ask you one question about it — and you answer.

## Do It Yourself

Make your own media page on paper. Three boxes, one per channel. In each box, write the channel's words and one true sentence about yourself under it. Then list the eight TV programme kinds and write one programme name beside each kind — real ones you know, from your own television.
