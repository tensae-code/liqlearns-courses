# Lesson plan — Media Words

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** Media Words
**Lesson:** media-words — the words for newspapers and magazines, television and radio, and computers and the internet, and the kinds of TV programme
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Words for the World (with the other word sets)
**The one thing:** The media word set, sorted by channel the way Bahrain's coursebook sorts it, and the names of the kinds of TV programme.
**This lesson's own level:** Word-set lesson: sixteen media words in three channel groups, with the verbs that go with them, and eight TV programme kinds.

## Why this lesson exists

Bahrain's student-coursebook excerpt teaches the media vocabulary as a set (Unit 3 "The media": the word set at 3.1 and the TV programme genres at 3.3), and no course lesson housed that set — Podcast Words and Technology Words cover only slices of it. This lesson is built from those two placements under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06). The source is one 24-page excerpt of a student coursebook, cited as exactly that in lesson/sources.md.

## Sub lessons

1. Media words by channel
2. TV programme kinds

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is needed before this lesson. It sits with the Unit 7 word sets and beside Podcast Words and Technology Words; each stands alone.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- Name the media words and say which channel each belongs to: newspapers and magazines, television and radio, computers and the internet.
- Use the verbs that go with the words: read an article, watch an advert, listen to a programme, go on a social networking site, upload a video.
- Name the kinds of TV programme: game shows, quiz shows, current affairs, cookery programmes, talent shows, chat shows, wildlife documentaries and lifestyle shows.

## What you need
A newspaper, a phone or a TV guide, if you have one; paper for your own media list if not.

## The run of the lesson
1. ADD IMAGE: a newspaper front page, a television and a phone showing a homepage, side by side. The learner names any media words they already know before the set is taught.
2. ADD IMAGE: the sixteen words laid out under the book's three channel headings. Sub lesson 1 walks the channels word by word, then the verbs that go with them.
3. ADD VIDEO (short): a TV guide page turning, programme kind by programme kind. Sub lesson 2: the learner sorts a day's programmes into the eight kinds and picks the one they would watch.

## Add-ons
- Channel race: pairs sort the word cards under the three channels — some words live in more than one channel, and saying why wins the point.
- True about you: each learner writes three true sentences about their own media use, one per channel, using the book's collocations.

## If the learner is stuck
Teach the channels on different days if needed; the collocations matter as much as the word list — a learner who can say "I read an article" and "I uploaded a video" owns the words.
