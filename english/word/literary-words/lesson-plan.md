# Lesson plan — Literary words

**Course:** English · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** Literary words
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 43 — Words About Words (CATCH-UP-PLAN.md,
Units 42–44 build list).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the words for talking ABOUT stories and poems: the kinds of literature and genres, and the figures of speech — simile, metaphor, personification and their company — so a learner can name what a writer did.
**What the learner can do at the end:** name the five kinds of literature Egypt's book teaches and common genres; spot and name simile, metaphor and personification in a line; and use the story-words — plot, setting, character, theme — about a real book.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before

1. **The Kinds of Literature** — the kinds of literature, the genres
   inside them, and the story-words (plot, setting, characters,
   theme) — the genres and story-words sit here in the text's order

2. **Figures of Speech** — figures of speech — simile, metaphor,

3. **Talking About Books** — talking about books — using the kinds,
   genres, story-words and figure names about a real story or poem,
   and loving the words themselves. (Was depth Level 3.)

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — five labelled shelves — NOVELS, POETRY, DRAMA, FOLKTALES, ESSAYS — with books flying to their shelves.** Written slot: "a picture of
   five library shelves with the five kinds' names over them." What the student does: the student sends three named books to their shelves and says the kind aloud
2. **Slot 2 — a line of poetry wearing three masks — a mirror (simile), a mask that IS the face (metaphor), a smiling river (personification).** Written slot: "a picture of
   three masks over one poem line: mirror, face, and a river with a smile." What the student does: the student points at the mask each new line is wearing before reading on

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first**: three lines, three tricks — the learner feels simile, metaphor and personification before hearing their names.
2. **The kinds**: Egypt's five — novels, poetry, drama, folktales, essays (Sec 11 T1, U11 L1).
3. **Genres**: Jordan's film-genre set (G12 S2, U7, p.23) — kind inside kind.
4. **The story-words**: plot, setting, characters, theme — Ghana's JHS analysis set (curriculum evidence).
5. **Figures of speech**: simile vs metaphor (Sierra Leone L25), personification and the wider device set (Ghana, curriculum evidence); irony, euphemism, repetition (Sierra Leone JSS3 L98).
6. **Talking about a book**: Jordan's 'Pretty Words' — loving words themselves (G12 S2, p.21); Egypt's novel study with its Chapter Questions.
7. **Do it yourself**: the closing tasks.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck on simile vs metaphor: hunt the flag-words. 'Like' or 'as' present? Simile. The thing simply IS the other thing? Metaphor — Sierra Leone L25's own test.
- Stuck naming a kind: ask how it reaches you — read as prose (novel), in lines (poetry), performed (drama), handed down (folktale), arguing a point (essay).
- A figure is not decorative trivia: name what it DOES — what it makes you see.
