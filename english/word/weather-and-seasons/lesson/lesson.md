# Weather and seasons — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Word · Lesson: Weather and seasons
Built from: Albania, Akelius A2 Teacher's Guide, Chapter 32
"The weather" (overview pp.16–17) — Bolivia, Year 2 Topic 3
"The weather in Bolivia" (student p.170) — Cambodia,
English Grade 5, Chapter 6 (pp.114–131) — Mongolia, English
VI, Unit 9 "Weather and Seasons" (p.96) — Rwanda, Primary
Learner's English P4, Unit 4 "Weather" (pp.47–58).

*Speaker note: the explanation around the words is read in the
learner's own language; the English words themselves — the words
this lesson teaches — are never translated and are always read in
an English voice.*

## Do it first
Look out of the window. What is the sky doing right now —
sun, cloud, rain, wind? Say it in your own language. Every
day of your life starts with this question, and the books
teach it early for exactly that reason. Now the English
words, and the two questions the books drill: one for
today, one for yesterday.

## Sub lesson 1 — Today's Weather
The weather set. Cambodia's Grade 5 book opens its weather
chapter with "What's the weather like today? It's cloudy
and windy" — and the words gather around that question in
every country's book: **sunny, cloudy, rainy, windy,
snowy, stormy, hot, cold, warm, cool, dry, wet**. Bolivia
teaches them as anagrams to unscramble — SUNNY, CLOUDY,
RAINY, STORMY, SNOWY, WINDY, HOT, COLD. Mongolia's set adds
**foggy**. Behind the describing words stand the thing
words Albania lists: **cloud, rain, snow, sun, wind,
storm, thunder, rainbow, temperature**.

*[Slot: a picture of the same street in four weathers —
sunny, rainy, snowy, windy. Name each weather before you
read on.]*

## Sub lesson 2 — The Four Seasons
The four **seasons**: **spring, summer, autumn, winter**.
Albania's guide names the fourth season **fall** — the
American name for autumn; both names are in the books, and
either is a right answer on this course. Bolivia prints
its season grid as winter December–February, spring
March–May, summer June–August, autumn September–November
— the seasons belong to the months that way in Bolivia's
book; where you live, the same four names may sit on
different months, and the names are what this lesson
teaches. Albania compares inside the seasons: "Today is
colder than yesterday." "July is the hottest month."
"Summer is the hottest time of the year."

*[Slot: a picture of one tree changing through spring,
summer, autumn, winter. Say each season as it changes.]*

## Sub lesson 3 — Yesterday and Strong Weather
Now the two questions. For **today**, the present:
**"What's the weather like today?"** — "It's cloudy and
windy." (Cambodia.) For **yesterday**, Rwanda's book sets
the past pair beside it: **"What was the weather like
yesterday?"** — "It was …" (Rwanda P4, pp.49–50). One word
changes — is → was — and the question walks into the past.
Rwanda also asks it for last week, last month, last year.

Strong weather, at the top of the set. Cambodia's chapter
ends with **the flood** — "The floods can destroy the
roads" — and its rain words **drizzle, lightning,
rainbow, thunder**. India's books name the great storms:
**cyclone, gale, typhoon, tornado, hurricane, whirlwind**
— storm names matched to their definitions — and read a
whole unit on **the tsunami** of 26 December 2004.
Ecuador's climate text lists the extreme events: **heat
waves, droughts, wildfires, floods**. (One spelling fix,
taught corrected: Albania's list prints "lightening" —
the weather word is **lightning**.)

## Say it to your companion
Give your companion a weather report. Today first: "What's
the weather like today?" — answer for your real sky. Then
yesterday: "What was the weather like yesterday?" Then
your favourite season, and the weather you love in it.
Swap roles and do it again.

## Do It Yourself
1. Draw one place — your home, a tree, a street — four
   times, once per season. Label each drawing with its
   season and two weather words.
2. Write today's report: "It's … today."
3. Write yesterday's report in Rwanda's shape: "It was …
   yesterday."
4. Write the strong-weather words and one thing each one
   does: "The floods can destroy the roads."
