# Birthday Customs Around the World

## Do it first

How is a birthday marked in your home — a song, a cake, candles? Is it the same in every country? This lesson reads how birthdays are marked around the world, from Costa Rica's Grade 9 book.

## Sub lesson 1 — Birthday customs in different countries

A **custom** is a usual way of doing things in a place — what people do without thinking, because their people have always done it. Grade 9's reading travels: **birthday customs around the world**.

The reading's work is twofold: read how birthdays are marked in different countries, then correct sentences about those customs — the book tests the reading by planting wrong sentences for the learner to fix. (The text itself sits on picture pages; this lesson is stocked from the book's own exercise, exactly as the expansion read placed it.)

*Difficulty: One reading, held at the depth the book's own exercise proves.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Talking about birthdays

Now turn it around: how are birthdays marked where *you* live? A song? A special food? A number of pulls, bumps or candles?

Customs differ; none is “the right one”. The lesson's talk is comparison: in one country they do this, in ours we do that — said plainly, with the birthday words the reading gives.

*Difficulty: Your own custom, told in English, beside the world's.*

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion how birthdays are marked in your family. They ask one question about the custom. Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write what a custom is, in your own words.
2. Write three sentences about birthday customs — one about your place, two about other countries from the reading.
3. Correct this sentence the way the book does: “Every country marks birthdays in exactly the same way.”
