# Flags and National Symbols

## Do it first

Think of the flag of your country. What colours does it have? Does it have stripes? A star? A picture? Now ask the big question of this lesson: **what does each part stand for?** A flag is not just coloured cloth — every part of it is there for a reason. Try to guess what the parts of your flag stand for before you read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a flag stands for

*Difficulty: first step — for the youngest learners, and for anyone who has never asked what a flag means.*

A **flag** is a **symbol** of a country — a sign that stands for the whole country and all its people. And a symbol is never empty: the parts of a flag **stand for** things the country is proud of.

Read a flag the way Liberia's Grade 1 reader *Flag Day* reads the Liberian flag, part by part:

- **The red stripes** stand for **bravery** — being brave.
- **The white stripes** stand for **goodness**.
- **The star** stands for Liberia itself: **the first free country in Africa**.

See the way of it? Nobody just picked pretty colours. Each stripe and the star carry a meaning, and a person who knows the meanings can *read* the flag like a tiny story about the country.

Try it now: say Liberia's flag from memory — red for bravery, white for goodness, and the star for the first free country in Africa. Then look at any flag you can find and ask the same two questions: **What are its parts? What does each part stand for?**

## Sub lesson 2 — Honouring the flag

*Difficulty: builds on Sub lesson 1 — now the flag is not only read, it is respected.*

Because a flag stands for the whole country, people **honour** it — they treat it with respect. In the reader, Eli helps hold the Liberian flag, and learns how people honour it:

- People **stand** for the flag.
- They put a **hand over the heart** — the hand sits on the chest, over the heart, while the flag is honoured.
- They treat the flag carefully, because it is not only cloth — it is the country's sign.

And there is one more thing the flag does: it **gathers the people**. Under one flag, the people of a country are **one people, working together**. That is what Eli learns on Flag Day, and it is why the next day he watches the parade happily — the flag is his country's, and he is one of its people.

Try it now: stand up. Put your hand over your heart. Say one sentence: "Our flag stands for our country, and we are one people."
