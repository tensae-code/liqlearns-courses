# Happiness and Wellbeing

## Do it first

Who is the happiest person you know — and what does their day look like? This lesson reads about the man National Geographic called the world's happiest person, and counts his habits.

## Sub lesson 1 — The world's happiest person

Grade 11 reads National Geographic's portrait of **the world's happiest person** — a Costa Rican named **Alejandro Zúñiga**.

The reading does not crown him for money or fame. It looks at how he *lives* — and the lesson reads the habits with him.

*Difficulty: One man, one question — what makes him the happiest?*

## Sub lesson 2 — Habits for a happy life

The reading's list is plain: he **socializes six hours a day**; he **sleeps seven hours**; he **walks to work**; he **eats fruit and vegetables**; he **works 40 hours at a job he loves**; he **volunteers**; and he **plays soccer**.

People, rest, movement, real food, work that fits, giving time, play — wellbeing is built from ordinary bricks.

*Difficulty: Seven habits, all ordinary — the surprise is that together they top the world.*

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion three of the happiest person's habits from memory. They name the ones you missed. Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the seven habits as a list.
2. Mark each habit: do I do this now — yes, no, or a little?
3. Choose one habit to start this week and write your first step.
