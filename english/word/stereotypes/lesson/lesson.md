# Stereotypes

## Do it first

Someone says: "Girls can't fix cars." "Boys don't cry." "Old people can't learn new things." Each sentence takes ONE idea about a whole group and sticks it on every person in that group. That fixed idea has a name — a **stereotype** — and this lesson teaches the word and what is wrong with the idea.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a stereotype is

A **stereotype** is a fixed belief that everyone in a group is the same — that all of them think the same, act the same, can do the same things and cannot do others.

Fixed means it does not move: the belief stays even when the person in front of you is different from it. If you believe all girls are one way, you stop seeing THIS girl — you see your belief instead.

*Difficulty: One word for a belief that refuses to look at the person.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Seeing people as themselves

People in every group are different from each other — in what they like, what they can do, and what they want to learn. The cure for a stereotype is a real person: one girl who fixes the car, one boy who cries and is strong, one grandmother learning a new language, breaks the "all of them" claim — because a claim about ALL falls with a single one who is different.

When you meet a person, let the person tell you who they are. Do not let a fixed belief answer first.

*Difficulty: The belief lets go when a real person stands in front of it.*

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion one stereotype you have heard. Then say the words that break it: "Not all — I know someone who…"

## Do It Yourself

1. Write one stereotype you have heard about a group.
2. Under it, write one real person or story that shows it is not true for everyone.
3. Cross out the word "all" in your sentence and write what is left. That is the honest sentence.
