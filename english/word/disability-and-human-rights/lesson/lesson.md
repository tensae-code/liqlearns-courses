# Disability and Human Rights

## Do it first

Can a blind man do a job? Tunisia's Perform Year 1 book answers with a whole lesson: a blind man and his guide dog, at work. This lesson teaches the words of that text — disability, rights and equality — so you can talk about what every person is allowed to do.

## Sub lesson 1 — Disability words

- **blind** — not able to see.
- **disability** — a condition of body or mind that makes some things harder.
- **guide dog** — a dog trained to help a blind person move safely.
- **to be allowed to** — to have permission and the right: "He is allowed to bring his guide dog to work."

*Difficulty: Four words, all from one real working life.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Rights and equality for everyone

- **citizen** — a person who belongs to a country, with its rights.
- **equality** — the same rights and chances for everyone.
- **human rights** — the rights every person has, just for being a person.

Put them together: "A disabled citizen has the same rights as everyone. Equality means he is allowed to work, to travel and to live his life." That is the Perform lesson's own point: the man in the text works — his disability is part of his life, not the end of it.

*Difficulty: Three big words, used in true sentences about real people.*

## Say it to your companion

Your companion says: "A blind man cannot work." Answer with the lesson: what can he do, and which words prove it?

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the seven words with a short meaning for each.
2. Write two sentences: one about a guide dog, one about equality.
3. Look around your school: write one thing that helps a disabled person, and one thing that should change.
