# Volunteering and Charity Work

## Do it first

Some people work to help others and are not paid. Tunisia's Year 9 book gives a whole lesson to them. This lesson teaches the words for that work — volunteering and charity — and how to talk about helping in your own community.

## Sub lesson 1 — Volunteering words

- **voluntary work / volunteering** — work you do to help, without pay.
- **poverty** — being very poor; charity helps people in poverty.
- **blood donation** — giving some of your blood to a hospital, to save lives.
- **disabled** — a person whose body or mind works in a different way and may need help.
- **tolerance** — accepting people who are different from you.
- **planting trees** and **gathering litter** — two volunteering actions for the environment.

*Difficulty: Six words and two actions, each tied to a real helping job.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Helping in your community

Use the words about real help:

- "Volunteers **plant trees** at our school."
- "**Blood donation** saves lives."
- "We **gather litter** in the park on Saturdays."
- "Charity helps families in **poverty**, and **tolerance** helps everyone live together."

The Year 9 lesson sits inside the book's Civility module — helping is presented there as part of being a good citizen. (Its can/can't work for ability is stocked in can-for-ability's family of lessons; the offers of help in the next lesson of the book are stocked in making-offers.)

*Difficulty: The words, said about your own street and school.*

## Say it to your companion

Name one volunteering job you would like to do. Your companion asks: "Who does it help?" Answer with a lesson word.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the six words with a short meaning for each.
2. Write two sentences about volunteering in your community.
3. Do one small helping job this week — then write which lesson word it was.
