# Art Words

## Do it first

Look around the room you are in. Is there anything on the walls? A picture? A photograph? Something somebody made by hand?

People make things to be looked at, listened to and thought about. This lesson gives you the English words for those things — the kinds of art — and for the places where art lives.

## Sub lesson 1 — What is art? — the kinds of art

*Difficulty: first steps. You only need everyday words.*

Ask ten people "What is art?" and you will hear ten answers. But the kinds of art have names, and the names are the same everywhere:

- A **painting** is a picture made with paint.
- A **drawing** is a picture made with a pencil or a pen.
- A **sculpture** is a work made of stone, wood or metal that you can walk around.
- A **photograph** is a picture made with a camera.
- **Music** is art you listen to.
- **Dance** is art made with the moving body.
- **Theatre** is a story acted by people in front of you.
- A **film** is a story in moving pictures on a screen.

Say which kind each thing is: "This is a painting." "That is a sculpture." Some works mix kinds — a film has music in it — and that is fine. The words let you say what you see.

## Sub lesson 2 — Museums and places of interest

*Difficulty: a step up. You use the art words in real places now.*

Where does art live when nobody is making it?

- A **museum** is a building that keeps old and important things — pictures, tools, clothes, bones — so everyone can see them and learn.
- A **gallery** is a room or building that shows paintings and photographs.
- An **exhibition** is a show: a set of works put out together for a time, then taken away.

People visit museums and galleries to look slowly. And everybody likes different things. Say what you like, and give your reason with **because**: "I like this painting because the colours are bright." "I like the sculpture because it looks like a horse."

A **place of interest** is any place people visit because it is worth seeing — a museum, an old building, a famous bridge. Now you can name them all.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: one painting or drawing you have seen, one kind of art you like, and one place of interest you want to visit — with a reason for each: "I like sculpture because…" Then listen to theirs.

## Do It Yourself

1. Make one small work in any kind: draw a picture, take a photograph, or shape something from clay, paper or wood. Say its kind in English: "This is a drawing."
2. Write two sentences: the kind of art you like best, and why — with **because**.
3. Name one place of interest near your home and say what a visitor can see there.
