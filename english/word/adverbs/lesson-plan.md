# Lesson plan — Adverbs

**Course:** English · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** Adverbs
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2, Unit 1 "Word Kinds" —
the fourth lesson of the unit (CURRICULUM.md), after Nouns,
Verbs, and Adjectives.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** an adverb is a word that
describes a verb — first of all it tells HOW an action is done.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Verbs lesson (an
adverb is found by its verb, the way an adjective is found by
its noun) and the Adjectives lesson (most how-words are made
from adjectives). It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown moving — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds, and an adverb is a word
about moving, so this lesson's GIF carries the teaching itself.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Verbs and Adjectives. The adverb describes
  the verb and is mostly built from the adjective (quick →
  quickly), so both neighbours hold this lesson up. Recommended,
  never forced.
- The other adverb kinds — when, where, to what degree — are
  named in the books as later sets (Ghana adds time and place,
  then degree and reason; Egypt's later box sorts manner, place,
  time, frequency). This lesson teaches the how-words in full
  and the how-often words as its deepest level; the rest wait
  in the deeper levels of later books, named in sources.md.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what an adverb is: a word that describes a verb — it
   tells how an action is done.
2. Use the how-words with their verbs: run quickly, write
   carefully, sing loudly, draw quietly — and know the adverb
   usually comes after the verb (Bangladesh's own note).
3. Make an adverb from an adjective: add -ly (quick →
   quickly); y changes to i (happy → happily) — and handle the
   two rebels the books name: fast stays fast, good becomes
   well.
4. Find the adverb in a short sentence by asking "How did
   they do it?"
5. Answer "How often?" with the frequency ladder: always,
   usually, sometimes, never.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil, and room to move for the mime game.
Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner walks across the room twice:
   once slowly, once quickly — same verb, two different walks;
   the lesson shows the word that changed is a new kind of word
   (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — what an adverb is; the how-words after
   their verbs; making them with -ly (and the y → i change);
   the rebels fast and well; the "How did they do it?" test; the
   how-often ladder (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the start of the explanation: a picture of a
     boy walking, 1–2 seconds, the word "slowly" under him —
     then the same boy hurrying with "quickly" under him — the
     student copies each walk and says the word that matches
     the speed.
   - ADD IMAGE under the how-words: a picture of children in a
     classroom — one writing, one singing, one drawing, one
     running outside the window — the student says how each one
     is doing their action: carefully, loudly, quietly, quickly.
   - ADD AUDIO in the making part: a voice reading the building
     pairs — "quick — quickly. Loud — loudly. Happy —
     happily." — the student says each adverb after it and
     listens for the -ly arriving, and for the y turning to i.
3. **Say it to your companion** — explain what an adverb is,
   mime an action with a how-word for your companion to guess,
   and run the "How did they do it?" test together
   (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the
     how-game in the super-simple voice (made later, when
     videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md (Bangladesh's own mime game,
   widened).
6. **Do It Yourself** — describe how you do five things today;
   place yourself on the how-often ladder (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes adjective and adverb ("He sings beautiful") → the two
  tests side by side: describing a THING uses the adjective
  (a beautiful song); describing the DOING uses the adverb
  (he sings beautifully). Libya's own pair, from its fox story.
- Cannot find the adverb → find the verb first, then ask "How
  did they do it?" The answer is the adverb; it usually sits
  right after its verb.
- Writes "happily" as "happyly" → Egypt's rule: y changes to i
  before -ly. Happy → happily, angry → angrily.
- This lesson's next door: Pronouns — the words that stand in
  for nouns (the last lesson of this unit). When/where/degree
  adverbs wait in the deeper books.
