# Adverbs — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Word · Lesson: Adverbs
Built from: Bangladesh, English for Today Class 5 (the adverbs
box and the mime game) — Egypt, English Language Primary 5
Term 2 (making -ly adverbs) and Term 1 (how-often words) —
Libya, English for Libya Grade 8 (the fox story's grammar box)
— Ghana, NaCCA English Curriculum Basic 1–3 (the first naming
of adverbs).

## Do it first
Walk across the room. Just walk — nothing special. Now walk
again, but SLOWLY, like you are carrying a full cup of water.
Now QUICKLY, like you are late. Three walks, one verb: walk.
What changed? Not the action — the HOW. The words that changed
your walk — slowly, quickly — are the kind of word this lesson
is about.

## Then the explanation
An **adverb** is a word that describes a verb. First of all,
it tells **how** an action is done.

Bangladesh's Class 5 book says it in one line: "Adverbs say
how an action is done. The adverb usually comes after the
verb." Egypt's book says the same: "an adverb tells us how we
do something." So every how-word rides behind its doing word:
run **quickly**, write **carefully**, sing **loudly**, draw
**quietly** — Bangladesh's own mime-game pairs. Ghana's school
curriculum opens adverbs with exactly these: "He walked
**quickly** to the church." "She danced **beautifully**."

Most how-words are **made from adjectives** — you met those in
the last lesson. The making rule, from Egypt's book: add
**-ly** to the adjective. Quick → **quickly**. Loud →
**loudly**. Careful → **carefully**. And watch the y: when the
adjective ends in y, the y changes to i — happy → **happily**,
angry → **angrily**. Libya's fox story shows the whole move
inside one tale: "The bird's song was **beautiful**. The bird
sang **beautifully**. The fox laughed **loudly**. He shouted
**angrily**." The adjective paints the thing; the adverb
paints the doing. Same root, two jobs — that is the difference
between the last lesson and this one.

Two rebels, and the books name them honestly. **Fast** does not
change: "He has a fast car. He drives **fast**." And **good**
becomes a different word: "She is a good singer. She sings
**well**." Learn those two as they are; everything else takes
its -ly.

The test that finds an adverb: find the verb, then ask
**"How did they do it?"** She danced — how? Beautifully. He
walked — how? Quickly. The word that answers is the adverb.

One step deeper. Adverbs also answer **"How often?"** — the
frequency ladder Egypt's book draws with numbers: **always**
(all the time), **usually**, **sometimes**, **never** (not
even once). "I always brush my teeth." "He never runs."
Ghana teaches these beside the time words as the second
adverb set. The other sets — words for when and where, and
words for how much — come in the later books, and wait there
for their own deeper levels.

## Say it to your companion
Explain to your companion what an adverb is, in your own
words. Then play Bangladesh's mime game: mime an action with
its how-word hidden — run slowly, write carefully, sing
loudly — and let your companion guess both words: the verb
AND the adverb. Finish by asking each other the test question
on a new sentence: "How did they do it?"

## Do It Yourself
1. Write five things you do, each with its how-word: I read
   quietly. I run quickly… Say each pair out loud.
2. Make the adverbs: slow → ___, careful → ___, happy → ___,
   angry → ___. Then say the two rebels: fast → ___, good →
   ___.
3. Put yourself on the ladder: I ___ (always / usually /
   sometimes / never) eat breakfast. I ___ do my homework.
   I ___ run in the morning.
4. Find the adverb: "Mercy ate slowly." "The fox ran away
   quickly." "She sings well." Prove each with the test:
   "How did they do it?"
