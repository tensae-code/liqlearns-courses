# Hands-on — Adverbs

The lesson with your body, not only your eyes. (The first game is
Bangladesh's own, from its Class 5 book, p.27 — widened.)

## The mime game (the book's own)
One person draws or picks an action — run, write, sing, draw —
and a how-word — quickly, slowly, loudly, quietly — and mimes
the pair: writing very carefully, singing very loudly. The
other guesses BOTH words: the verb and the adverb. Five pairs
each, then swap. If your partner guesses the action but not the
how, mime it bigger — the how is the lesson.

## One verb, three walks
Walk across the room three times: slowly, quickly, angrily —
same verb, three adverbs, said out loud at each pass. Then do
the same with writing: write your name carefully, quickly,
happily. Feel how the word changes the doing.

## The sound walk
Go outside or to a window for two minutes. Every action you
hear or see, name its how-word out loud: the bird sings
loudly; the man walks slowly; the children run quickly. Eight
actions minimum.

## Ladder of your day
Draw the ladder on paper: always at the top, never at the
bottom, usually and sometimes between. Pin four true sentences
of your day to it: "I always wake early." "I sometimes run."
"I never shout." Say them to a partner; your partner checks
the ladder word is honest.
