# Lesson plan — Working words

**Course:** English · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** Working words
**SPECIALIST LESSON.** This is a specialist lesson, flagged
plainly: it teaches the English of three workplaces —
hotels, offices and tourism — from Greece's vocational
(EPAL) sector books. It is NOT a general Level 7 set like
the other lessons of this level. Learners who work, or will
work, in hotels, offices or tourism take it; other learners
can pass it by and lose nothing from the general course.
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Words for the World
— the specialist shelf of the unit (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the working word
sets of the hotel desk, the office and the tourist trade —
reception, reservation, guest, key; letter, memo, account,
inquiry; tour, guide, brochure, trip — and the phrases that
run them.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The town (the
hotel and the office as town places) and on Jobs-type words
met across Level 7. It needs nothing else — but it is
specialist: the words serve a workplace, not the whole
world.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown changing — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The Hotel Desk** — a guest's road worked in the Hotel book's
   own order — enquiry, reservation, deposit, checking in, key,
   room. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **The Office** — a business letter's parts named in their
   order, and the office set used in true sentences. (Was depth
   Level 2.)
3. **The Telephone and the Trade** — telephone messages taken and
   left with the desk's phrases, and the tourism set used
   correctly; the tourism words are first met on the hotel road in
   Sub lesson 1. (Was depth Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking
is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set
is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The town (Level 7) and Money and prices
  (same level — hotel rates and office accounts use them).
  Recommended, never forced — this lesson stands alone: each
  workplace set is taught whole.
- General job names for everyone are the Jobs lesson's
  thing. This lesson is the specialist vocabulary INSIDE
  three trades, from the trade books themselves.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Work the hotel desk set: enquiry, reservation/booking,
   brochure, rate, deposit, balance, check in, key,
   reception — and answer a guest's booking talk.
2. Work the office set: business letter parts (address,
   date, greeting, subject, signature), memo, telex/fax's
   modern heirs in the book's own frame, inquiry, order,
   complaint, account.
3. Work the tourism set: tour, travel agent, charter
   flight, airfare, half-board, full-board, bed and
   breakfast, self-catering.
4. Take a telephone message at a hotel desk the way the
   Hotel book's Unit 2 drills it: put through, hold on,
   call back, engaged.

## What you need
A desk or table to be the hotel desk. Paper for a
registration form and a message pad.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner stands behind a table that
   is a hotel desk and greets an arriving guest in their own
   language. The lesson then gives the desk's English
   (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the hotel set in the order a guest
   meets it (enquiry → reservation → checking in →
   facilities); the office letter and its parts; the tourism
   set (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the desk part: a picture of a hotel key card
     sliding across a reception desk to a guest's hand,
     1–2 seconds — the student says the desk's sentence:
     "Here is your key."
   - ADD IMAGE under the office part: a picture of a real
     business letter with its parts at their places —
     address, date, greeting, subject line, body, signature —
     the student names each part where it sits.
   - ADD AUDIO in the telephone part: a voice answering a
     hotel phone — "Good morning, Athenian Hotel. How can I
     help you?" — the student answers as the guest before
     the voice continues.
3. **Say it to your companion** — run the desk: one books by
   phone, the other receives, registers and hands over the
   key (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the
     hotel desk in the super-simple voice (made later, when
     videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — fill a registration form, write a
   one-line telephone message, and label a business letter
   (lesson/lesson.md).

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- GIF — a picture of a key card crossing the desk → the
  student says the desk's sentence.
- IMAGE — a picture of a business letter's parts in place →
  the student names each part.
- AUDIO — a hotel phone answered aloud → the student plays
  the guest first.
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later) → the student
  works the desk along with it.

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the
plan is the build sheet, so nothing the lesson needs gets forgotten.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes reservation and reception → the guest's road sorts
  them: first the RESERVATION (the booking, made before),
  then the RECEPTION (the desk, met on arrival). Before =
  reservation; the desk = reception.
- Writes a letter with no shape → the Office Clerks book's
  two opening pages are the shape itself: address, date,
  greeting, subject, body, close, signature — always in
  that order.
- Takes half-board as half a board → the board-basis set is
  a fixed little list in the Hotel book: half-board =
  bed, breakfast and one main meal; full-board = all meals;
  bed and breakfast speaks for itself.
- This is a specialist lesson: a learner who will not work
  in these trades may stop after Level 1 with the general
  course intact — that is by design, and it is said plainly
  here and in the lesson.
