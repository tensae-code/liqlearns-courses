# Working words — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Word · Lesson: Working words
Built from: Greece, English for the Hotel and Tourist
Trade (Grade 12 EPAL, Administration and Economics sector)
— Greece, English for office clerks (Grade 11 EPAL,
Administration and Economy sector).

**This is a specialist lesson.** It is flagged plainly, the
way this course flags specialist material: these are the
words of three workplaces — hotels, offices, tourism — from
Greece's vocational school books, the Αγγλικά (Anglika)
sector sets of the EPAL lyceums. If you work in a hotel, an
office or the tourist trade, or you are training to, this
lesson is for you. If you are not, you may pass it by: the
general course loses nothing.

A soft hint before you start: if you know The town and
Money and prices lessons, this one will be easier — the
hotel is a town place, and every desk talks about rates.
You do not need them first. Each set below is taught whole.

## Do it first
Stand behind a table. The table is a hotel desk. A guest
walks up — greet them in your own language, the way a good
desk does: welcome, ask how you can help, listen. You
already know the WORK. This lesson gives you the work's
English — the exact words and phrases the Greek trade
books teach for the desk, the office and the tour.

## Then the explanation
**The hotel — a guest's road.** The Hotel book teaches the
trade in the order a guest lives it, and the word set
follows the road:

1. **Making enquiries** (Unit 1): a customer asks about a
   holiday. The words: **enquiry, reservation / booking,
   brochure, charter flight, tour, airfare, rate, travel
   agent**. The desk's polite tools ride along: *Can /
   Could / May I…?*
2. **Making reservations** (Unit 2): the agent phones the
   hotel — two connecting rooms, a **double** and a
   **twin-bedded** room, ten nights, a nightly **rate** with
   breakfast, a **deposit** now and the **balance** two
   weeks before arrival, **confirmation** when the deposit
   lands.
3. **Checking in** (Unit 3): at **reception** the guest
   **registers** — name, nationality, address, passport
   number, arrival, nights, departure — the **porter** takes
   the bags, the desk hands over the **key**.
4. **Facilities** (Unit 4): what the hotel sells — 24-hour
   **room service**, laundry, currency exchange — and how a
   guest eats: **half-board, full-board, bed and breakfast,
   self-catering**.

> a picture of a hotel reception desk with a guest signing
> a registration form and a key card waiting beside it —
> the student names the three things the desk is doing:
> registering, welcoming, handing over the key.

**The office.** The Office Clerks book opens with the shape
of the trade's writing, before any unit: the anatomy of a
**business letter** — headed notepaper, the receiver's
address, the date, the greeting, the subject line, the
opening, the body, the complimentary close, the signature —
and the abbreviations of commercial correspondence. Its
units then live the clerk's year: getting a job, office
communications (a **memo**, a circular, a notice board),
and one whole export trade in letters — **inquiry, order,
acknowledgement, complaint** — each with its standard
phrases. The office set: **letter, memo, inquiry, order,
complaint, account, customer, firm**.

**The telephone.** Both trades live on the phone, and the
Hotel book's reservation unit drills its language whole:
answering, asking for a person, being **put through**, an
**engaged** line, taking and leaving a **message**, **hold
on**, **call back** — with the phrasal verbs that carry
them: *put through, get through, ring up, hold on, cut
off, hang up, call back*.

## Say it to your companion
Run the desk. Your companion phones as a travel agent:
they book two rooms for ten nights, ask the rate, offer a
deposit. You answer as the desk: take the booking, name the
rate, say when the balance is due, confirm. Then they
arrive: register them, take the passport number, call the
porter, hand over the key — "Here is your key. Enjoy your
stay." Swap roles, and the new agent changes the dates.

## Do It Yourself
1. Fill a registration form for an imagined guest: name,
   nationality, address, passport number, arrival, nights,
   departure — the Hotel book's own fields.
2. Write a telephone message: who called, for whom, what
   they said, call back.
3. Draw a business letter's shape and label its parts in
   order: address, date, greeting, subject, body, close,
   signature.
4. Write the board-basis list from memory and what each
   one feeds the guest: half-board, full-board, bed and
   breakfast, self-catering.
