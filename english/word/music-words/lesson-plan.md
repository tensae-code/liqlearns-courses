# Lesson plan — Music Words

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** Music Words
**Lesson:** music-words — the words for kinds of music and instruments, and talking about the music you love
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Words for the World (with the other word sets)
**The one thing:** The names of music genres and instruments, and saying what music you like and who your favourite artists are.
**This lesson's own level:** Word-set lesson: eleven genres, fourteen instruments, and the favourite-music talk that uses them.

## Why this lesson exists

Bolivia's Grade 8 book ends with a full music unit — genres and instruments as one word set, with favourite-music talk — and no course lesson carried music words. This lesson is built from that placement under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06).

## Sub lessons

1. Music genres
2. Musical instruments
3. Talking about favourite music

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is needed before this lesson. It sits with the other Unit 7 word sets (Sports, Jobs, Food and Fruit); each stands alone.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- Name the main music genres: rock, pop, jazz, electronic, hip hop, reggae, blues, folk music, country, rap, classical.
- Name the instruments: guitar, piano, violin, flute, drums, trumpet, saxophone, clarinet, bass guitar, cello, keyboard, harp, trombone, accordion, ukulele.
- Talk about your favourite music and artists.
- Say which instrument you would like to play.

## What you need
Music itself, if you can play a few short clips; pictures of instruments if you cannot.

## The run of the lesson
1. ADD IMAGE: four musicians in four styles — a rock band, a jazz player, a folk singer, an orchestra. The learner guesses each music kind before the words appear.
2. Genre round (sub lesson 1): one short clip or picture per genre; the learner calls the genre name.
3. ADD IMAGE: the instrument family laid out like a shop window. Sub lesson 2: the learner points and names; string, wind and percussion emerge as groups from the book's own ordering.
4. Sub lesson 3 is talk: “My favourite music is folk music. My favourite artist is… I would like to play the guitar.”

## Add-ons
- Genre charades: mime listening to a genre (head-banging rock, smooth jazz) — partners name the genre.
- Instrument shop: “I would like to buy a trumpet.” “Sorry, we have violins and flutes.”

## If the learner is stuck
Genres first, in fours; instruments by family groups (the ones you blow, the ones with strings, the ones you hit). Do not drill all twenty-five words in one sitting.

## Teacher note
One book slip is carried honestly: the book says “classical music… its lyrics touch the heart” of instrumental music (printed p. 59) — classical music played by instruments has no lyrics; the sentence is noted in lesson/sources.md and the genre is taught clean.
