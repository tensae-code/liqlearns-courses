# Lesson plan — Law and Crime Words

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** Law and Crime Words
**Lesson:** law-and-crime-words — the words of the court and the crime: the people (lawyer, judge, jury), the actions (arrest, accuse, charge), the trial, and its two ends — guilty or innocent, and the sentence
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 7 — Words for the World

**The one thing:** the learner can name the people and actions of a court case, follow a crime story in English, and say what the law asks of young people.

**This lesson's own level:** word level — it stands in Unit 7 (Words for the World) with the other word sets.

## Why this lesson exists
Yemen's Grade 11 book carries two Arts readers on the law: one teaches the law words themselves, with definitions, through the story of Doctor Crippen — the first criminal caught by radio — and one turns to young people and the law. No lesson in the course housed this set, so under everything-goes-in it is built here.

## Sub lessons
1. **The words of law and crime**
2. **A crime story, and young people and the law**
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is needed before this lesson.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- after sub lesson "The words of law and crime": use the court set — lawyer, judge, jury, court, trial, arrest, accuse, charge, guilty, innocent, sentence
- after sub lesson "A crime story, and young people and the law": follow the Crippen story as a crime story, and say in plain English what the law means for young people

## What you need
Nothing special.

## Add-ons

## If the learner is stuck
Start from a story the learner knows — someone was caught, someone was accused — and hang each new word on the story's next step.

## Teacher note
The set is Yemen's Grade 11 Arts readers 1 and 3 (see lesson/sources.md): a law vocabulary list with the book's own definitions, the Doctor Crippen story told as a plot, and a reader on young people and the law. The Crippen text is stocked as a plot summary only, never transcribed.
