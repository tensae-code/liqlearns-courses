# Culture and Heritage Words

## Do it first

Think of your own people. There is a food you eat on a special day, a song the old people sing, a way of greeting that is yours and not the world's. This lesson teaches the English words for those things — the words of culture and heritage.

## Sub lesson 1 — Culture words — values and traditions

The words of a people's way of life:

- **Culture** is the whole way of life of a people — their food, songs, stories, clothes and days.
- A **tradition** is something a people has done for a long time and keeps doing — a wedding dance done the same way for two hundred years.
- A **custom** is one usual way of doing a thing — a custom of taking off your shoes at the door.
- A **value** is something a people holds to be important and right — respect for old people is a value.
- A **belief** is something a people holds to be true.
- **Folk** points to the ordinary people's own things: folk songs, folk tales, folk dance.
- A **ceremony** is a special set of actions for a special day — a wedding ceremony, a naming ceremony.

Tradition and custom are close: a custom is one habit; a tradition is a custom that has lived long and is handed down.

*Difficulty: One people's whole way of life, carried by seven words.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Heritage — what a people keeps

Some of a culture is too precious to lose, and the book's unit turns to keeping it:

- **Heritage** is everything a people receives from those before them — old buildings, dances, stories, crafts and songs.
- To **preserve** a thing is to keep it safe so it does not disappear.
- A **craft** is a thing made skilfully by hand — weaving, pottery, carving — and the skill itself.
- A people **hands down** its heritage: the old teach the young, and the young keep it alive.

When a dance or a craft is in danger of disappearing, the world can name it as heritage and help preserve it — that is the work the same book unit reads about next, in its UNESCO lessons.

*Difficulty: The words at work — keeping a culture alive.*

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion one tradition of your own people and one piece of heritage you want preserved, using the lesson's words.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the seven words of sub lesson 1 with one line of meaning for each.
2. Name one custom and one tradition of your own people, and say how they are different.
3. Write two lines about a piece of heritage — a dance, a craft, a building — and what preserving it means.
