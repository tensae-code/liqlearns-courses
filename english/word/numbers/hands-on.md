# Hands-on — Numbers

The lesson counted on real things, the way every book starts it.

## Touch and count
Egypt's method, for real: line up things — pencils, spoons,
stones. Touch each one as you count it and slide it aside.
Touched means counted; a thing is never counted twice. Count
the line forward, then check it backward from the end.

## How-many hunt
Walk a room with a partner. They point and ask: "How many
windows are there?" You count aloud, touching or ticking,
and answer whole: "There are 2 windows." Ten questions each,
then swap. Windows, doors, chairs, pictures, shoes — the
room is the worksheet.

## The finger lines
Hold up fingers for the counts a partner calls: "Seven!"
"Twelve!" (both hands plus a head-nod for the extra two —
or toes if you are barefoot). Then the tens: "Thirty!" is
three full flashes of ten fingers. Fast rounds win laughs
and plant the words.

## Tens jumps
Stand. Jump and land on each ten: 10, 20, 30 … 100 — ten
jumps, arms up at 100. Back down the line in jumps: 100, 90,
80 … 10. The body learns the -ty line as landings, the way
the ears learn -teen as steps.
