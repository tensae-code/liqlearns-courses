# Lesson plan — Numbers

**Course:** English · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** Numbers
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 2, Unit 4 "Numbers, Days,
and Time" — the first lesson of the unit (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** counting in English —
each number as a figure, a word, and a count of real things,
1 to 20, then by tens to 100 — with the counting question the
books ask: "How many … are there?"
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Level 1 (the
Conversation lesson How old are you already spends numbers
1–10) and on the world sets (there must be things to count).
It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown changing — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds per count.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: any world set (home, animals, food) —
  counting needs countable things. Recommended, never forced.
- Adding and taking away with numbers is arithmetic — the
  Mathematics course's home; Palestine's Grade 1 book does
  print the first picture sums, and they are noted in the
  sources, but this lesson only counts and names. Ordinal
  numbers (first, second — the race words) grow in a later
  depth with the months.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Count 1–10 with figures and words, the way every book
   starts: traced, copied, counted on real things.
2. Count 11–20 and answer Egypt's question in its whole
   shape: "How many … are there?" — "There are 15 …"
3. Count by tens to 100 (10, 20, 30 … 100), Egypt's Primary 3
   line: "I can count by 10s to 100."
4. Count anything in the room and report the count in a
   whole sentence.

## What you need
Things to count — fingers first, then anything at hand.
Paper and a pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner counts their fingers, then
   the chairs in the room, in their own language; the lesson
   gives the English count (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — 1–10 three ways (figure, word,
   things); 11–20; How many/There are; the tens to 100
   (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the 1–10 part: a picture of apples landing in
     a row, one per second, the figure and the word growing
     with each landing — 1 one, 2 two, 3 three… — the student
     counts aloud with the landings.
   - ADD IMAGE under 11–20: a picture of a classroom wall
     number line, 1 to 20, with 11–20 standing big → the
     student reads the line forward, then back from 20.
   - ADD AUDIO in the How-many part: a voice asking "How many
     pencils are there?" over a pictured count, then answering
     "There are 3 pencils" — the student answers first for two
     more pictured counts.
3. **Say it to your companion** — count real things for your
   companion and answer their How-many questions in whole
   sentences (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the
     counting chant in the super-simple voice (made later,
     when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served
   at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — count five things in your home, write
   the counts as figures and words, and write three How-many
   answers (lesson/lesson.md).

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- GIF — a picture of apples landing one by one, figure and
  word growing with each → the student counts aloud with the
  landings.
- IMAGE — a picture of a 1–20 number line → the student reads
  it forward and back.
- AUDIO — "How many pencils are there?" asked and answered →
  the student answers two more counts first.
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later) → the student
  counts along with it.

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the
plan is the build sheet, so nothing the lesson needs gets forgotten.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes thirteen and thirty → the books' own split: the
  -teen numbers live in 11–20 and are counted one by one;
  the -ty numbers are the tens and are counted by jumps.
  Say the two lines side by side until the ears split them.
- Counts the things but loses the line → Egypt's method:
  touch each thing as you count it, and move it aside; a
  thing touched is a thing counted once.
- Knows figures, not words → the word is the figure said
  aloud; trace the word the way Palestine traces the figure,
  saying it as the pencil moves.
- This lesson's next door: Days and months (same unit) —
  numbers meet the calendar.
