**Acceptance count: taught in 6 countries (Egypt, India,
Palestine, Laos, China, Mongolia).** Palestine carries the youngest path (numbers
inside the word units, traced); Egypt carries the counting
question and the climb (1–5, 11–20, by tens to 100); India
carries the same 1–20 in its first book; China carries the
whole climb again with its counting questions, prices,
ordinals as dates, and measuring; Mongolia carries 1–10 as
picture characters over counted sets. Other finished
countries join as their books are read (see READING-QUEUE.md).

**Laos Grade 3 added 2026-10-27** — *English for Primary Schools,
Textbook Grade 3* (MoES/RIES, 2021 revised edition; full read in
english/books/laos.md). Numbers come in two lessons, both counted
on things: **1–5** sung and counted on pigs — "1 pig / 2 pigs,"
the plural -s from the very first count (p.33) — and **6–10**
counted on fruit: six apples, seven mangoes, eight bananas, nine
oranges, ten papayas (p.87). On that same page the counted fruit
turns into **five picture addition sums** to complete — counting
and first adding on one page, the same next step Palestine takes
in its Revision unit.

**Laos Grade 4 added 2026-10-27** — *English for Lao Primary
Schools, Student's Book 2, Grade 4* (MoES/RIES, 2012; full read
in english/books/laos.md). Grade 4 climbs in three steps, always
word + numeral together: **11–20** printed over their numerals
(p.6), **21–30** in full (p.24), **31–50** as a numeral-over-word
grid (p.71). **How many…?** arrives as its own question (p.23).
The counted numbers turn into sums whose answers are written as
English words — "12+5= 17 seventeen" (p.26), again at 31–50
(p.73) — and the final revision drills the teen/ty pairs by ear
(15/50, 13/30, 14/40…, p.135) and plays Bingo to 50 (p.138).
Same country as the Grade 3 block above, the next book up — the
count does not change.

**Laos Grade 5 added 2026-10-27** — *Student's Book 3, Grade 5*
(MoES/RIES, 2012; full read in english/books/laos.md). The climb
finishes: **50–100** in tens then in full (pp.15–18), sums now
running to 100 (85−15, 100:2, p.16), and a brand-new number use —
**telephone numbers read digit by digit, zero read as "o"**:
"What's your telephone number? — It's 537 889" (pp.10–14), with
a whole song that sings three numbers (p.14).
