# Lesson plan — Using the dictionary

**Course:** English · **Title:** Word · **Lesson:** Using the dictionary
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 8, "Using the Dictionary" — the level is this one lesson (CURRICULUM.md). It comes
after the kinds of words and the words for the world, and
gives the tool that finds any word. The platform's own
dictionary is a future update, filed in DICTIONARY.md —
this lesson teaches the skill it will run on.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** how a dictionary is
built — words stand in letter order — and how to use that
order to find a word and read what its entry gives.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Alphabet lesson
(letter order IS the alphabet, used as a tool) and on the
Word Kinds level (an entry's "word class" is the kinds the
learner already knows: noun, verb, adjective…). It needs
nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown changing — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the Alphabet and the Word Kinds level.
  Recommended, never forced.
- A contents page — the OTHER finding tool, Bangladesh's
  same unit Lesson 1 — is a different thing and keeps its
  own place; this lesson is the dictionary only. Synonyms
  and antonyms as a full study grow later; here they appear
  as what an entry gives.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Put words in dictionary order: by the first letter, then
   by the next (the books' own run: dark, deep, doctor).
2. Find a word fast with guide words — the two words at the
   top of a dictionary page that fence what the page holds
   (find the words that live between 'tea' and 'technique').
3. Read an entry and take what it gives: spelling,
   pronunciation, meaning, word class — with examples in
   italics, and synonyms/antonyms when they are printed.
4. Know that one word can carry more than one meaning —
   Bangladesh's 'dry' holds three — and check which one fits.

## What you need
Any dictionary — a paper one, a book's back pages (Mongolia's
books carry their own dictionary), or a phone. Paper and a
pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner writes five words they know
   on five slips and races to stand them in letter order —
   badly, probably; the lesson then gives the order's rule
   (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — letter order letter by letter;
   guide words; the entry and its gifts; many meanings
   (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the letter-order part: a picture of five word
     cards shuffling themselves into line — cat, apple, dog,
     ball, egg — 1–2 seconds, first letters popping as they
     settle — the student says the order rule aloud as the
     cards land.
   - ADD IMAGE under guide words: a picture of a real
     dictionary page's top, with its two guide words standing
     big and the words they fence below — the student says
     whether 'teapot' sleeps on that page or not, and why.
   - ADD AUDIO in the entry part: a voice reading the entry
     for 'fit' the way Bangladesh prints it — the word, how
     it sounds, its class, its meanings, an example — the
     student names each gift as the voice gives it.
3. **Say it to your companion** — give your companion a word
   to find; watch their hands use the order and the guide
   words; then swap (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the
     finding race in the super-simple voice (made later, when
     videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served
   at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — sort a ten-word list, find three words
   in a real dictionary, and write out one full entry by hand
   (lesson/lesson.md).

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- GIF — a picture of word cards shuffling into letter order →
  the student says the order rule aloud.
- IMAGE — a picture of a dictionary page top with guide words
  → the student judges which words sleep on that page.
- AUDIO — the 'fit' entry read aloud → the student names each
  gift as it comes.
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later) → the student
  races the finding along with it.

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the
plan is the build sheet, so nothing the lesson needs gets forgotten.

## If the learner is stuck
- Sorts by the FIRST letter only and stalls inside one letter
  → the books' own next step: when the first letters tie,
  the NEXT letter decides (dark before deep: a before e).
  The alphabet song, walked slowly, is the ladder.
- Opens the dictionary at random and leafs → guide words
  first, always: read the page tops, not the pages. Half the
  book, then half again — the search halves each time.
- Copies a meaning that does not fit their sentence → 'dry'
  is the standing warning: read ALL the meanings, then pick
  the one that fits the sentence in front of you.
- This lesson completes Level 8. Next: Level 9, Numbers,
  Days, and Time.
