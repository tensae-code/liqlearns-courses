# Hands-on — Using the dictionary

The lesson with a real dictionary open in front of you.

## The card sort — for real
Write ten words from this course on ten slips of paper (or ten
stones, ten bottle tops). Shuffle. Stand them in dictionary
order as fast as you can, saying the rule as you go: "First
letters… a, b, d… the d's tie — second letters decide." Time
yourself; race a partner; the order is the game.

## The fence hunt
Open a dictionary (or a book with a back dictionary —
Mongolia's Grade 10 book, a Marigold) at any page. Read ONLY
the guide words aloud, then say three words that must sleep
on that page and one that cannot. Check yourself by reading
the page. Five pages, five fences.

## Find and read
From a hat or a partner's mouth, draw a word. Find it the
fast way — halves and fences, never leafing — and read its
whole entry aloud: spelling, class, meaning, the italic
example. Three words. Whoever finds theirs first without
leafing wins the round.

## My own page
Make one dictionary page of your home: pick five words from
your kitchen or your street, put them in order on a sheet,
and write each one's class and meaning in your own words,
with one example sentence in italics. Your page follows the
same rules as the big book's — because it IS the same machine.
