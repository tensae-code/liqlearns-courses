# Transport — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Word · Lesson: Transport
Built from: Palestine, English for Palestine Grade 1
Part 2, Unit 11 "Transport" (p.10) — Bolivia, Year 2
Topic 2, §3.4 "means of transportation" — Mongolia,
English VIII, Unit 5 "Around Town" (p.64) and Unit 6
"Going Places" (p.78) — Rwanda, Primary Learner's
English P4, Unit 3 (pp.43–46) and Learner's Book P5,
Unit 6 "Transport" (pp.108–124).

*Speaker note: the explanation around the words is read in the
learner's own language; the English words themselves — the words
this lesson teaches — are never translated and are always read in
an English voice.*

## Do it first
How did you get here today? And how did you travel last
week — every journey, on wheels, on water, in the air, on
your own feet? Name them in your own language. Transport
words are journey words, and you have been collecting
journeys all your life. Now their English names.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Street Five
Palestine's first-year book teaches **five**: **car, bus,
bike, van, taxi** — with counting riding on them: **"How
many taxis? Five taxis."** "How many taxis and bikes?"
The set grows by adding like a sum.

*[Slot: a picture of a street with a car, a bus, a bike,
a van and a taxi. Point and name each one before you read
on.]*

## Sub lesson 2 — The Full Set, Sorted
The full street: **train, lorry, motorcycle, ambulance**
join the five; Bolivia's chart and Rwanda's lists add
**plane, helicopter, boat** and the oldest transport of
all, the **horse**. Bolivia teaches them already sorted —
**by water, by land, by air** — and Rwanda's P5 book
makes the same sort its unit's work: transport vocabulary
classified by sea, land and air.

*[Slot: a picture of vehicles moving into three groups —
land, water, air. Say each vehicle's name and its group
as it moves.]*

Land: car, bus, bike, van, taxi, train, lorry, motorcycle,
ambulance, horse. Water: boat, ferry. Air: plane,
helicopter. Mongolia's "Going Places" chart works the
same map with four roads out: air, road, sea, railway —
and its unusual-transport reading crosses the world on a
toboggan, a bamboo train and an ice boat.

## Sub lesson 3 — Travelling
The set's own pattern is **travel by**: **"travel by
bus," "travel by train," "travel by plane"** (Rwanda,
p.110) — one shape for every vehicle, and one exception
kept beside it like a warning sign: walking takes no by.
You **travel on foot**.

## Say it to your companion
Ask each other: "How do you travel to school?" Answer
whole: "I travel by bus." / "I travel on foot." Then
three dream journeys — across water, into the sky, to a
far city — each with its vehicle and its by. Swap and
ask again.

## Do It Yourself
1. Draw your journey to school. Label every vehicle in
   it — and your feet, if they carried you.
2. Write the whole set into three boxes: land, water,
   air.
3. Write three travel-by sentences about your family's
   real journeys, and one on-foot sentence.
4. Count a street from a window for one minute and write
   Palestine's question and answer for what you saw:
   "How many…? …"
