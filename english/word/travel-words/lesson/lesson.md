# Travel Words

## Do it first

Plan a trip out loud. You are taking a friend around your town
for one day. Try to say:

- "We will ___ by bus."
- "We ___ at the old town at nine."
- "We will ___ my cousin there."
- "We will ___ at five."

The little words you were missing — travel, arrive, meet, leave —
are this lesson: the words a tour runs on.

## Sub lesson 1 — Going on a tour

A **tour** is a trip to see places. When you go on a tour, you
**travel** — you move from place to place.

- "We **travel** by bus."
- "We **arrive** at the town at nine." — arrive means: you get
  there.
- "We **meet** Sara at the gate." — meet means: you come
  together with a person.
- "We **leave** at five." — leave means: you go away from the
  place.

The tour has an order. "**Next**, we visit the market."
**Tomorrow** is the day after today: "Tomorrow we travel to the
sea."

Say the whole day with the words: "We leave home at eight. We
travel by bus. We arrive at nine. We meet our guide. Next, we
visit the old town. Tomorrow, we travel again."

*Difficulty: first steps — say each word inside the tour's day, never alone.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Words the tour needs

A **ruin** is an old place that is broken by time — old walls,
an old castle. Tours visit ruins: "We walk in the ruin."

**Before** means earlier than: "We eat **before** we leave."
**Near** means not far: "The sea is **near** our town."
**Float** means to stay on top of water without going down:
at the Dead Sea, you **float** — the water holds you up.
**Our** means it belongs to us: "**Our** tour starts at nine.
**Our** bus is here."

Make your own "Best of our town" tour: five stops, and use
every word of this lesson in it — tour, travel, arrive, meet,
leave, next, tomorrow, ruin, before, near, float (if your town
has water), our.

*Difficulty: building — the describing words join the moving words in one whole tour.*

## Say it to your companion

Explain your one-day tour to your companion with the lesson's
words. Your companion listens and counts: how many of the
twelve words did you use? Then change places.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write a plan for a one-day tour of your town: five stops,
   with times, using arrive, leave, next and tomorrow.
2. Write three sentences about a ruin near you — or a ruin you
   know — using ruin, old and near.
3. Draw the tour: draw your bus, your first stop, and a person
   floating on water; write the word under each drawing.
