# Cultures and Traditions

## Do it first

Think of one thing your family does at every celebration — a food, a song, a way of dressing. Where did it come from? This lesson teaches the words for peoples, traditions and cultures — and what happens when cultures meet.

## Sub lesson 1 — The peoples of a country

Every country has peoples who were there first. Costa Rica's Grade 10 book reads about its indigenous peoples today: the Boruca, who were never conquered; the Chorotegans of Nicoya; and the Maleku, the smallest and most endangered people. The readings also name the hard side: loss of territory and discrimination.

The same book gives an overview of traditions and culture in Costa Rica: **quedar bien** (making a good impression), **la hora tica** — “Tico time”, arriving relaxed and late — and Tico slang: **pura vida** (pure life — hello, thanks, all good), **mae** (friend), **tuanis** (cool), **qué chiva** (how great). Dance, music and theatre are part of the tradition too. And identity shows in daily life: surnames, brands, and a large middle class.

*Difficulty: Real readings about real peoples — names and ways of life to remember.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Kinds of culture

Culture is not one block. The book sets **high culture** — the arts a society keeps in theatres, museums and concert halls — against **popular culture**, the music, shows and styles most people enjoy every day. Neither is “better”; they are different shelves of the same house.

Inside one culture there are smaller worlds. A **subculture** is a group with its own style inside the bigger culture. A **counterculture** goes further: it pushes against the main culture's ways. The book follows one example — how the American hipster changed over time.

*Difficulty: Three near words — high, popular, sub-, counter- — told apart by what each group does.*

## Sub lesson 3 — Cultures meet and change

Cultures do not stay still. **Cultural diffusion** is the spread of ways of living from one group to another — food, music, words, clothes travel. **Globalization** is that spread grown worldwide.

When one part of life changes faster than another, the book calls the gap **cultural lag**: the tools arrive before the habits do. Technology is the fastest mover — it changes how people talk, work, celebrate and keep their traditions, for good and for ill.

*Difficulty: Ideas about change itself — diffusion, lag, globalization — each with its own name.*

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion one tradition of your place. They guess: is it high culture, popular culture, or a subculture's way? Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the names of three peoples or groups from this lesson and one thing each is known for.
2. Write one tradition of your own place and say where it comes from.
3. Find one thing in your home that came from another culture. Write how it travelled to you — that is diffusion.
