# Types of Resources

## Do it first

A farmer grows rice. A river waters his field. A tractor ploughs it. Three different kinds of help made that rice — a person's work, a gift of nature, and a thing people built. English sorts them into three words: human, natural and capital resources. Learn to sort them, and you can describe how any community lives.

## Sub lesson 1 — Three kinds of resources

*Very simple. Can-do: Sort the world's useful things into three kinds: human resources (people's labour, skills and knowledge), natural resources (from nature, used in natural form) and capital resources (man-made tools, machines and buildings).*

Everything a community uses to live and work is a **resource**, and resources come in three kinds:

**Human resources** — the people: their labour (the work of their hands), their skills (what they know how to do) and their knowledge (what they know). A teacher, a farmer, a doctor — all human resources.

**Natural resources** — what nature gives, used in its natural form: land, water, trees, fish, sun, wind.

**Capital resources** — what people make to do work with: tools, machines and buildings. A plough, a bus, a school, a shop.

Try it now: sort these — a nurse (human), a river (natural), a tractor (capital), a cook's skill (human), a forest (natural), a bakery's oven (capital).

## Sub lesson 2 — Resources in my community

*Simple. Can-do: Read a community through its resources: the colmado (neighbourhood store) and the people, land and tools around it — who gives the labour, what nature gives, what was built.*

Now read your own community the same way. The Dominican book starts with the **colmado** — the small neighbourhood store on the corner. Look at it closely: the shopkeeper's work and knowledge are human resources; the rice, fruit and water it sells come from natural resources; the shop building, its shelves and its fridge are capital resources. One small store uses all three kinds at once.

Walk your street with the same eyes: a farm, a school, a bus stop, a workshop. For each, name one resource of every kind.

Try it now: take one place you know — a market, your school, your home — and list its human, natural and capital resources in three lines.

## Say it to your companion

Read this lesson aloud to your companion. Then describe the colmado (or a shop near you) with one resource of each kind.

## Do It Yourself

1. Make a three-column chart of your community: human | natural | capital, with five entries in each.
2. Sort ten things from your home into the three kinds.
3. Say what kind of resource a library book is — and what kind the librarian is.
4. Write three sentences: one for each kind of resource in a farm.
