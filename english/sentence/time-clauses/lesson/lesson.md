# Time Clauses

## Sub lesson 1 — When, while, as, as soon as — joining two times

A time clause tells WHEN the main action happens.

- **when** — at that time: *When I got home, I did my homework.*
- **while / as** — during the time that: *While I was cooking, the phone rang.*
- **as soon as** — immediately after: *As soon as the bell rang, we left.*
- **before / after / until** work the same way: *Wash your hands before you eat.*
- The time clause can come first or second. When it comes first, put a comma after it.

Do it: join these with *when*: "The lesson ended. We went home." Then join them with *as soon as* and say what changes.

## Sub lesson 2 — Time clauses with the future — no will after when

When the main action is in the future, the time clause stays in the PRESENT form — no *will* inside it.

- *When I **get** home, I will phone you.* (not *when I will get home*)
- *As soon as the rain **stops**, we will go out.*
- *I will wait until you **are** ready.*
- With the past perfect, the time word orders two past actions: *When I had finished, I went to bed. While she had been waiting…* — the *had* action is the earlier one.

Do it: fix this sentence: "When I will see him tomorrow, I will tell him." Then make two true sentences about tomorrow with *when* and *as soon as*.
