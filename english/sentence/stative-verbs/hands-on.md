# Hands-on — Stative verbs

You do it, we guide you. Everything here is done in the
learner's real day, with a companion or the Explain
Companion. The TARGET forms are always said in English;
the talk around them may be in the learner's own language.

1. **The verb sort, live.** Your companion reads twenty verbs (know, run, own, write, believe, seem, cook, love, hear, drive, want, read, possess, taste, swim, understand, feel, jump, realise, play). At each verb, slap the table for ACTION (can wear -ing) or raise a flat, still hand for STATIVE. Three misses and the list restarts.
2. **Border-verb theatre.** Mime have, see and think in both their lives: owning vs eating (have), seeing a bird vs seeing a doctor (see), holding an opinion vs turning an idea over (think). The watcher must say the sentence for each mime in the right coat — simple for the state, continuous only for the action.
3. **Mirror descriptions.** Take turns describing each other's REAL state with linking verbs only: "You look tired. You seem happy. Your voice sounds hoarse." Five bridges each — be, seem, look, feel, sound all used; an adverb after a bridge (*looks tiredly) is called out and repaired on the spot.
