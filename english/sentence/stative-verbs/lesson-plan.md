# Lesson plan — Stative verbs

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Stative verbs
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 30, "Dressing a Sentence" (CURRICULUM.md / CATCH-UP-PLAN.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** stative verbs — the verbs of thinking, feeling, owning and sensing (know, believe, love, own, seem) that name a state, not an action, and so refuse the continuous coat — plus the linking verbs (be, seem, look, feel) that join a subject to its description.
**What the learner can do at the end:** sort stative from action verbs on sight and keep statives in the simple coat (I know, not *I am knowing); handle the honest border — have, see, think change coats when their meaning changes (state vs action); and build linking-verb sentences whole: subject + be/seem/look/feel + adjective.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Present actions (the present continuous) and Describing words in a sentence. Recommended, never forced — this lesson stands alone for a learner who lands on it directly (DIRECT-ENTRY rule).
- Speaker convention: the explanation is read in the learner's main language; the TARGET forms are never translated and are read in the English voice.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a head filled with still water, captioned KNOW, beside a picture of hands splashing, captioned SWIM. What the student does: the student says which picture can wear -ing and which cannot, before the word stative is named.**
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of a girl looking in a mirror: the mirror shows her tired face, captioned SHE LOOKS TIRED. What the student does: the student names the bridge word (looks) and the description it carries (tired).**
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of a man holding two hats: one labelled HAVE = OWN, one labelled HAVE = EAT. What the student does: the student tries -ing on both hats and hears only one of them break.**

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first**: three things the learner knows, loves and owns, said in the simple coat in their own language.
2. **The full statement**: stative verbs in four groups — thoughts/opinions, feelings/emotions, the senses, possessions/measurements — not used in the continuous form; have, see, think can be state or action by context (Egypt Secondary 11 Term 2, Stative Verbs box, p.9).
3. **The first naming**: be, love, like, want, (look = seem), (have = possess) (Egypt, Secondary run, as filed in the map).
4. **The error-correction unit**: state verbs — imagine, know, realise, feel, believe, understand — with the rule and error correction on it (Cambodia Grade 12, Unit 23, printed pp.123–128).
5. **Present simple vs continuous + stative verbs** as one grammar set (Malaysia Form 3, Unit 1, pp.5–16).
6. **Senses and emotions take simple forms** (Nicaragua Grade 10, p.94).
7. **Stative verbs in the continuous form + Linking verbs** — the pair as Vietnam's Grade 11 book names and boxes it (Unit 3, Grammar box heading, printed p.30).
8. **Do it yourself**: the three closing tasks.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck on a border verb: ask what the verb MEANS in this sentence — owning/having as a state (I have a car — no -ing) against an action in progress (I am having lunch — -ing is fine).
- Stuck on look/feel: after a linking verb the description is an ADJECTIVE (she looks tired), never an adverb — the bridge carries descriptions of the subject.
- Stuck on seem: seem needs no object and no -ing coat for itself; what follows it is a description or a to-phrase (seems tired / seems to know).
- This lesson's next door: Too, enough, so, such (same level) — pushing a description until a result follows.
