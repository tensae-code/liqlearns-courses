# Stative verbs — true or false

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to, by what the item actually asks. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Stative Verbs — the Rule and Its Families · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Stative Verbs — the Honest Border: Three Movers, except bridge items, which are Sub lesson 3 — Linking Verbs — the Bridges, and the senses-and-emotions simple-forms item, which is Sub lesson 1 · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3.

Say true or false. If it is false, say why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** "I am wanting a new phone" is the careful, rule-following way to say it. **Answer: False** — want is on Egypt's own stative list — "I want a new phone"; statives are not used in the continuous form.

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** "I am having a car" is right, because have can take -ing. **Answer: False** — having here means OWNING — a state; the -ing opens only when have means an action (having lunch, having a shower).

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** "The soup tastes good" and "She looks tired" use taste and look as linking verbs. **Answer: True** — both join the subject to an adjective description — Vietnam's Unit 3 pairing.
