# Video script — Speech marks

Super-simple voice. Read slow. The learner hears this
before or beside the written lesson.

---

Musa said something.
You want to write his EXACT words.
Not your words. His words.

English puts the exact words in a cage.
The cage is two little marks. And two more at the end.
Speech marks. Inverted commas.

Musa said, "I am ready."

Look at the pieces.
Musa said — that is the frame.
Then a comma. The comma stands at the door.
Then the cage opens.
Capital letter. I. The speech starts big.
I am ready.
Then a full stop. Inside the cage.
Then the cage closes.

The full stop lives inside.
The question mark lives inside too.
"Are you ready?" Musa asked.

Sometimes the frame comes after.
"I am ready," Musa said.
Look. The full stop became a comma.
Because the sentence is not finished.
Musa said is still coming.

Sometimes the frame cuts the speech in two.
"I am," said Musa, "ready."
Two cages. One for each half.
Two sets of marks.

And in a story?
A new speaker. A new line.
"I am ready," said Musa.
"Then let us go," said Amina.
One line each. The eye knows who speaks.

Exact words. In the cage.
Everything else outside.

Now you try.
Take one thing your friend said today.
Write the frame. The comma. The cage.
Capital at the start. Full stop inside.
