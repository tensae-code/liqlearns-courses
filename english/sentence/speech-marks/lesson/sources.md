**Acceptance count: taught in 4 countries (Malaysia,
Rwanda, South Africa, Indonesia).** Malaysia's evidence is its Year
6 Scheme of Work — a teacher planning document, not a
pupil's textbook — and is worded as mapped coverage
throughout: Unit 1 of the map sets direct speech
punctuation as the year's opening language work. Rwanda
teaches speech marks for reporting exact words at
Pupil's Book 6, Unit 6, and practises the conversion
from a said-that report into quoted speech. South Africa
teaches the punctuation with rule and examples in Grade
5 Home Language Workbook 1 (printed p.17), and its Grade
6 Workbook 1 carries the split-speech piece — two sets
of inverted commas around a mid-speech reporting clause
— under a worksheet heading, "Reported speech", that
mislabels its own content; the lesson teaches the
punctuation and names the mislabel, exactly as filed.
Indonesia practises feelings vocabulary with quotation
marks in its Grade 8 feelings chapter (stocked 2026-11-09
by the re-verification sweep merge).
No other closed country's filing teaches direct-speech
punctuation as a named system; the lesson does not claim
them.
