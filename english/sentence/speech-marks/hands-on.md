# Hands-on — Speech marks

You do it, we guide you. Everything here is done with a
companion or the Explain Companion, on paper.

1. **Quote the morning.** The companion says four short
   sentences across the morning (I am hungry. Are you
   coming? Watch out! I lost my pen.). The learner writes
   each one as direct speech with its frame — said, asked,
   shouted — comma on the doorstep, capital at the
   opening, the ending inside the marks. A mark outside
   its cage costs a point.
2. **The split line.** Take one long sentence — "I will
   come to your house after school and help you." Break
   it with its frame in the middle: "I will come to your
   house," said Ana, "after school and help you." Two
   cages, comma inside the first, the second half
   starting small. Three sentences, split three ways.
3. **The staircase dialogue.** Write a six-line dialogue
   between two people at a market — a price asked, a price
   refused, a deal. Every new speaker drops to a new line
   with full marks. Then read it aloud in two voices,
   changing exactly where the lines change.
4. **The Rwanda conversion.** The companion gives three
   said-that reports (Kabanda said that he was hungry.
   Munezero said that the pumpkin was ready.). The
   learner converts each into direct speech with speech
   marks — Rwanda's own Unit 6 exercise (p.84) — then
   converts one of their own quotations from Activity 1
   back into a report, and says which version keeps the
   exact words.
