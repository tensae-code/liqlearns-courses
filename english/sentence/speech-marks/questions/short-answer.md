# Speech marks — short answer

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Speech Marks Cage and Capital · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Speech Marks Frame Positions · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Speech Marks Split Speech and Conversion.

Answer in whole sentences.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Write it correctly: The teacher said
class is finished.
**Answer: The teacher said, "Class is finished."**
— comma before the quote, capital C to open the speech,
full stop inside the closing mark.

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** Write it with the frame after: Ana
asks if you are ready.
**Answer: "Are you ready?" Ana asked.** — the question
mark stays inside the marks because it belongs to the
speech, and the frame follows outside.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Write the dialogue: Musa says he is
ready; Amina answers that they can go now.
**Answer: "I am ready," said Musa. / "Then we can go
now," said Amina.** — two speakers on two lines, each
line caged; a new speaker always drops to a new line.

**4. (Sub lesson 3)** Split it with its frame: I will finish
the work before dark (said Tom).
**Answer: "I will finish the work," said Tom, "before
dark."** — the split speech wears two sets of marks,
a comma inside the first cage, and the second half
opens small (South Africa, Grade 6 Workbook 1, p.12).
