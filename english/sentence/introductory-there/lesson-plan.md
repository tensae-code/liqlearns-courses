# Lesson plan — Introductory There

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Introductory There
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 10 — Building a Sentence
(CURRICULUM.md) — a sweep addition beside the unit's two
sentence-building lessons.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the sentence opener
**there is / there are** — the *there* that points nowhere
and only opens the sentence, with the real subject coming
after the verb: *There is a book on the desk. There are two
books on the desk.*
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Building a
Sentence (the learner already knows a sentence names a
subject and says what it does or is) and on subject-verb
agreement's ground rule that the verb fits its subject. It
needs nothing else.
**Why this lesson exists:** Bangladesh's Class 8 (Dakhil)
book teaches introductory "there" outright — a Language
Point box inside Unit 8's newspaper lesson ("The First
Newspaper," printed p.99) names it: *There is/are…* with
the real subject after the verb. The 2026-11-08
re-verification sweep re-read the book page by page and
rebuilt its placements from the PDF itself (420 teaching
items; 364 already in the course); this Language Point was
filed in the old country filing but homeless — no lesson
housed it. Under the everything-goes-in ruling
(2026-11-06) it gets its own lesson.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **There that points nowhere** — the opener there is not the place word: it does not point at anything; it only starts the sentence, and the real subject comes after the verb.
2. **There is — one thing** — There is opens a sentence about one thing: the real subject after is is one — There is a book on the desk.
3. **There are — more than one** — There are opens a sentence about more than one: the real subject after are is plural — There are two books on the desk. The choice between is and are is made by the real subject that follows, never by the there that opens.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Building a Sentence, so the words
  subject and verb are already friends. Recommended, never
  forced — the lesson stands alone.
- See also: subject-verb-agreement — the same fitting rule
  (the verb fits its real subject) worked across all
  subjects; and giving-directions, where the other there
  lives — the place word that points ("over there").

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Open a sentence with There is or There are and know the
   there points nowhere — it only introduces.
2. Find the real subject after the verb in a there-sentence.
3. Choose is for one thing and are for more than one, by
   the real subject that follows.
4. Tell the opener there apart from the place word there.

## What you need
Two or three small things on a table — a book, a pen, a
cup — to open sentences about. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner puts one book on the table
   and tries to say what is there without the word "book"
   first; then opens with "There is…" and feels the
   sentence start before its subject arrives
   (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the pointing there against the
   opener there; is for one, are for more than one; the
   real subject after the verb decides (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the start of the explanation: a picture of
     a hand pointing far away with the word "there" under
     it, then a sentence opening like a door — "There is a
     book…" — the student says which there is which.
   - ADD IMAGE under the choosing test: a picture of a
     desk with one book, then the same desk with three
     books — the student opens a sentence on each picture:
     "There is…" / "There are…".
3. **Say it to your companion** — explain in your own
   words why the opener there points nowhere, then open
   sentences on things in the room for each other
   (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed —
     the table game in the super-simple voice (made later,
     when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice,
   short-answer, fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching —
   served at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md (the one-and-many table game).
6. **Do It Yourself** — five opener sentences of your own,
   two with is and three with are, said out loud
   (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Says "There is two books" → the verb does not fit the
  there; it fits the real subject. Cover the there, read
  what follows — two books — and the verb must be are.
- Reads the opener as the place word ("there = at that
  place") → point at something and say "there!" — that
  there points. Then open "There is a book on the desk" —
  nothing is being pointed at; the sentence is only being
  introduced.
- This lesson's next door: subject-verb-agreement — the
  fitting rule everywhere, not only after there.
