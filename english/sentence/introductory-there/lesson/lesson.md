# Introductory There — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Sentence · Lesson: Introductory There
Built from: Bangladesh, English for Today, Dakhil Class
Eight, Unit 8 "News! News! News!", Lesson 2 "The First
Newspaper" — its Language Point box (printed p.99), which
teaches introductory "there": *There is/are…* with the
real subject after the verb. The example sentences in this
lesson are the course's own workings on that named
pattern; no sentence is quoted from the book.

## Do it first
Put one book on the table. Now tell me about it — but you
are not allowed to start with the word "book." Hard,
isn't it? English has a door-opener for exactly this
moment: "___ is a book on the table." The word that fills
the gap is **there** — and it is not doing the job you
think. This lesson is about that opener: **there is** and
**there are**.

## Sub lesson 1 — There that points nowhere
You already know one **there**: the pointing word. "The
ball is over **there**!" — that there points at a place.
The there in "There is a book on the table" points at
nothing. Nobody is showing you a place. This there only
**opens** the sentence — it steps in first, like a
doorman, and lets the real subject walk in behind the
verb. Read it slowly: "There — is — **a book** — on the
table." The sentence is about the book. The book is the
real subject, and it comes **after** the verb, not
before it. That is the whole secret of this pattern: the
subject arrives late, and there holds the door.

## Sub lesson 2 — There is, one thing
When the real subject is **one thing**, the verb is
**is**: "**There is** a book on the table." "**There is**
a school in our village." "**There is** a cat at the
door." One book, one school, one cat — one thing each
time, so is each time. Say the three sentences out loud
and tap the table on the real subject each time: book,
school, cat — always after the verb, always one.

## Sub lesson 3 — There are, more than one
When the real subject is **more than one**, the verb
changes: "**There are** three books on the table."
"**There are** many students in the class." "**There
are** two cats at the door." Now the choosing test —
because the subject arrives late, your ear wants to fit
the verb to the there. Do not. Cover the there with your
hand and read what follows: "…are three books" — three
books, more than one, so **are**. "…is one book" — one,
so **is**. The there never decides. The real subject
after the verb always decides.

## Say it to your companion
Explain to your companion, in your own words, why the
there in "There is a book on the table" does not point
at anything. Then play the test on each other: one of you
says a there-sentence with the verb left out — "There
___ a pen on the desk," "There ___ four pens on the
desk" — and the other must answer with the verb AND the
reason: "are, because four pens is more than one."

## Do It Yourself
1. Finish with is or are, and say why: "There ___ a cup
   on the table." "There ___ two chairs in the room."
   "There ___ a teacher in the class." "There ___
   thirty students in the class."
2. Open two sentences of your own about your own room:
   one with There is, one with There are. Say both out
   loud.
3. Point across the room and say "There!" — the place
   word. Then open a sentence about the same thing with
   the opener: "There is…" or "There are…". Say which
   there was which, and how you know.
