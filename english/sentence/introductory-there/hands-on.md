# Hands-on — Introductory There

The lesson with your hands, not only your eyes.

## One and many (the lesson's own game)
You need a table and a handful of small things — books,
pens, cups, stones. One person sets the table, the other
opens the sentence.
- Put ONE book on the table. The opener says: "There is a
  book on the table."
- Add two more books. The opener must start again: "There
  are three books on the table."
- Mix it up: sometimes one thing, sometimes many, sometimes
  change the thing itself (one pen; four pens; one cup).
  Ten turns, then swap. A wrong is/are costs the turn — run
  the test out loud: cover the there, read the real subject,
  count it.

## Point or open?
One person acts, the other judges. The actor either POINTS
at something across the room and says "There!" — the place
word — or opens a sentence: "There is… / There are…". The
judge must say which there it was, and how they know:
pointing, or only introducing?

## Around the room
Walk to three places in the room — the door, the window, a
shelf. At each one, open two sentences about what is there:
one with There is (one thing) and one with There are (more
than one thing). Say them out loud as you look.
