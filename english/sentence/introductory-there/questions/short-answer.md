# Short answer — Introductory There

Sub-lesson markers: every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to — Sub lesson 1 — There that points nowhere · Sub lesson 2 — There is, one thing · Sub lesson 3 — There are, more than one. This lesson was built new with its sub lessons (Bangladesh re-verification sweep merge, 2026-11-08), so its markers are original — there are no older depth layers to map from.

Answer in a few words, or one whole sentence where it says.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** In "There is a book on the table," does the word "there" point at a place? Say what it does instead.
**Answer:** No — it points nowhere; it only opens (introduces) the sentence.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** In "There are two cats at the door," what is the real subject, and where does it stand in the sentence?
**Answer:** The real subject is "two cats," and it stands after the verb.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Open a sentence about one pen on the desk, starting with "There."
**Answer:** "There is a pen on the desk." (Any true one-thing sentence opened with There is.)

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** Why does "There is a school in our village" take "is" and not "are"?
**Answer:** Because the real subject — a school — is one thing.

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Open a sentence about four pens on the desk, starting with "There."
**Answer:** "There are four pens on the desk." (Any true more-than-one sentence opened with There are.)

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** A learner says "There is two books on the table." What is wrong, and how do you fix it?
**Answer:** The verb does not fit the real subject: two books is more than one, so it must be "There are two books on the table."
