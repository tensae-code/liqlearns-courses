# Matching — Introductory There

Sub-lesson markers: every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to — Sub lesson 1 — There that points nowhere · Sub lesson 2 — There is, one thing · Sub lesson 3 — There are, more than one. This lesson was built new with its sub lessons (Bangladesh re-verification sweep merge, 2026-11-08), so its markers are original — there are no older depth layers to map from.

Match each left item with the one right item that belongs with it.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Match the "there" to its job:
- A. "The ball is over there!"
- B. "There is a ball on the grass."
- 1. the pointing word — it shows a place
- 2. the opener — it only introduces the sentence
**Answer: A–1, B–2** — A's there points across the field; B's there points nowhere and lets the real subject, a ball, come after the verb.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** Match each sentence to its real subject:
- A. "There is a book on the table."
- B. "There are two books on the table."
- 1. two books
- 2. a book
**Answer: A–2, B–1** — the real subject stands after the verb in both.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Match each opening to the one thing it introduces:
- A. "There is a pen…"
- B. "There is a school…"
- 1. …in our village.
- 2. …on the desk.
**Answer: A–2, B–1** — one pen on the desk; one school in our village.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** Match each gap to its word:
- A. "There ___ a cup on the table."
- B. "___ is a cat at the door."
- 1. There
- 2. is
**Answer: A–2, B–1** — one thing each time: the opener There, and is for the single subject.

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Match each opening to the many things it introduces:
- A. "There are three books…"
- B. "There are thirty students…"
- 1. …in the class.
- 2. …on the table.
**Answer: A–2, B–1** — three books on the table; thirty students in the class.

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** Match each sentence to its fix:
- A. "There is two chairs in the room."
- B. "There are one chair in the room."
- 1. "There are two chairs in the room."
- 2. "There is one chair in the room."
**Answer: A–1, B–2** — the verb must fit the real subject: two chairs takes are; one chair takes is.
