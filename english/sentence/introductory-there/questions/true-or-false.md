# True or false — Introductory There

Sub-lesson markers: every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to — Sub lesson 1 — There that points nowhere · Sub lesson 2 — There is, one thing · Sub lesson 3 — There are, more than one. This lesson was built new with its sub lessons (Bangladesh re-verification sweep merge, 2026-11-08), so its markers are original — there are no older depth layers to map from.

Say true or false, and fix what is false.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** In "There is a book on the table," the word "there" points at the table.
**Answer: False** — this there points nowhere; it only opens the sentence. (The pointing word is the one in "over there!")

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** In a there-sentence, the real subject comes before the verb.
**Answer: False** — the real subject comes after the verb: "There is **a book** on the table."

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** "There is a cat at the door" is the right opening for one cat.
**Answer: True** — one thing takes There is.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** "There is three books on the table" is the right opening.
**Answer: False** — three books is more than one; the opening must be "There are three books on the table." There is is only for one thing.

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** "There are many students in the class" is right.
**Answer: True** — many students is more than one, so are.

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** To choose is or are, you fit the verb to the word "there."
**Answer: False** — the verb fits the real subject after it: cover the there, read what follows, and count that.
