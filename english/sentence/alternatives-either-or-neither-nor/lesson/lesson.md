# Alternatives — Either…Or, Neither…Nor

## Do it first

A stall offers you two drinks. You can take only one. Try: "I will have either… or…" Go on — in this lesson you will learn the choice words: either…or, neither…nor, and whether.

## Sub lesson 1 — Either…or — one of the two

When the choice is open between two, English frames it with **either…or**:

- "I will have **either** a coke **or** a fanta."
- "**Either** walk **or** ride — choose."
- Either stands before the first choice, or before the second; one of the two will happen.

Offer me a choice now: two drinks, either…or.

## Sub lesson 2 — Neither…nor — not one, not the other

When both choices are refused, the frame turns: **neither…nor**:

- "He **neither** lives at a university residence **nor** has easy circumstances."
- "Neither the bus nor the train came." — neither…nor already carries the not; no second negative.
- The verb follows the nearer side: "Neither the teacher **nor** the students **were** there."

Refuse two things now, in one sentence.

## Sub lesson 3 — Whether or if — naming the choice

A choice can be named as a thing, with **whether** or **if**:

- "I don't know **whether** to stay **or** go." (whether carries its own or)
- "Ask him **if** he is coming." / "Ask him **whether** he is coming **or** not."
- The naming words: a choice itself is an **alternative** — "This idea is an alternative" — and a **possibility**.

Name one open choice of yours with whether.

## Say it to your companion

Offer your companion three either…or choices (food, place, time). They answer one with a choice, one with neither…nor, and turn the third into a whether sentence. Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

Write six sentences: two either…or (one with nouns, one with verbs), two neither…nor, and two whether/if sentences about real choices in your life. Read them aloud.
