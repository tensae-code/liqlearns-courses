## True or false

1. "Either…or" means both will happen. (Sub lesson 1)
   **Answer: False** — one of the two will happen.

2. "Neither…nor" needs another not in the sentence. (Sub lesson 2)
   **Answer: False** — neither already carries the not.

3. In "Neither the teacher nor the students were there", the verb follows the nearer side. (Sub lesson 2)
   **Answer: True** — students is nearer → were.

4. "Whether" can carry its own or: "whether to stay or go". (Sub lesson 3)
   **Answer: True** — whether…or names the two sides.

5. "If" and "whether" can both name a choice after ask. (Sub lesson 3)
   **Answer: True** — ask him if/whether he is coming.
