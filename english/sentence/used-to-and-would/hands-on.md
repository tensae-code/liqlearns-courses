# Hands-on — Used to and would

You do it, we guide you. Everything here is done with a
companion or the Explain Companion.

1. **Then and now.** Partners interview each other
   about age ten: home, school, games, food. Every
   answer must come as a used-to sentence, then a
   now-sentence. Five rounds each way, with at least
   one Did you use to…? question per round.
2. **The storyteller's fingers.** One partner sets a
   past scene ("When we visited Grandmother's village…")
   and must then land three would-actions in a row, one
   per finger held up. A state smuggled in with would
   loses the round.
3. **Refusals.** The companion makes silly requests in
   the past ("Ask Grandfather to dance!"). The learner
   answers with wouldn't: "He wouldn't dance. He
   wouldn't even stand up."
4. **Getting used to it.** Each partner names one thing
   they are getting used to now — a place, a tool, a
   sound, a food — and says it in three forms: I am used
   to…, I am getting used to…, I am not used to…,
   choosing the true one and defending it.
