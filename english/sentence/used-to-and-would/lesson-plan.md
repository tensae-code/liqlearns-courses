# Lesson plan — Used to and would

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Used to and would
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 32 — "Habits and
Orders" — the first lesson of the level (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** talking about
the past that is finished — **used to** for past habits
AND past states, **would** for repeated past actions
only, and the different animal **be used to / get used
to** (being accustomed now).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the past
simple (the plain past these forms comment on) and
opens the level that ends with Exclamations and orders.
**What the learner can do at the end:** say what they
used to do and used to be; ask "Did you use to…?" and
answer "I didn't use to…" in the standard forms; use
would for a string of repeated past actions in a story
and never with a state; refuse with wouldn't; and keep
used to (past, finished) clean apart from be/get used
to (accustomed, present) — including spotting the one
book rule in the set that is wrong and must not be
followed (Afghanistan's, corrected below and in the
sources).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Used to and would — the finished past** — used to for past
   habits and past states, both finished, in its three forms, with
   use to bare after did. (Was depth Level 1.)

2. **Used to and would — would for repeated actions** — would
   brought in for repeated past actions once the past scene is set,
   kept away from states, and refused with wouldn't. (Was depth
   Level 2.)

3. **Used to and would — the accustomed family** — be used to / get
   used to + -ing for being accustomed now, kept apart from the
   finished-past used to. (Was depth Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the past simple lessons.
  Recommended, never forced.
- Afghanistan's Grade 11 book prints "Do not use used
  to in a question or a negative." That rule is
  NON-STANDARD — every other witness in the set, and
  Egypt's Primary 6 paradigm in full, uses Did … use
  to? and didn't use to. The lesson teaches the
  standard forms and the sources record the correction,
  per the course rule: book errors are taught corrected.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of an old photograph beside a
   new one: the same street, the same person, everything
   changed.** Written slot: "a picture of then and now."
   What the student does: the student points at the old
   photo and says one used-to sentence; at the new one,
   and says what is true now.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of a storyteller by a fire,
   holding up fingers one by one: every summer, every
   evening.** Written slot: "a picture of a storyteller
   counting repeated days." What the student does: the
   student tells three would-actions in a row, one per
   finger.
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of new shoes: stiff on day one,
   soft and shaped to the foot a month later.** Written
   slot: "a picture of shoes getting comfortable." What
   the student does: the student says "I am getting used
   to…" about something real in their own life.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first** (lesson/lesson.md): a then-and-now
   list — three things that were true of the learner at
   ten that are not true now.
2. **Used to for habits and states**: Egypt's Secondary
   11 Past Habits box (Term 2, Unit 9, printed p.29)
   states the double job — past situations AND regular
   habits, often set against the present; Nepal's
   Grade 7 Grammar II (Unit 8) and Uzbekistan's
   English Matters 8 chart (Unit 7, p.76) teach the
   same core.
3. **The full paradigm**: Egypt's Primary 6 (Term 2,
   Unit 4, printed p.67) prints all three forms —
   Used to / Didn't use to / Did … use to — with used
   to + base verb.
4. **Would for repeated actions**: Egypt's box again —
   would + infinitive for past habits and typical
   behaviour, NOT for past situations; Cambodia's
   Grade 12 (Unit 4) adds the filed restriction: would
   is NOT used with stative verbs; Sudan's Book 6,
   Lesson 5 "Hobbies: Then and Now" (p.45) pairs the
   two forms on one page.
5. **The Afghanistan correction**: its Grade 11 unit
   (pp.68–70) teaches used to well but forbids the
   question and negative — taught corrected, recorded
   in the sources.
6. **Be used to / get used to**: Cambodia's Grade 12,
   Unit 28 (be used to / be getting used to / not be
   used to) and its Units 34–35 consolidation against
   used to; Malaysia's Form 3 grammar spread (printed
   p.25) and Form 5 (Grammar 2, printed p.23) teach
   both families on one page.
7. **Do it yourself** (the three closing tasks in the
   lesson): then and now in five lines; the story with
   would; getting used to it.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck between used to and would: ask "is it a state
  or a repeated action?" States (live, be, have, know)
  take used to only; actions can take either, and would
  needs its past scene set first.
- Stuck on the question form: the did carries the past,
  so use to sheds its -d: Did you USE to…? — Egypt's
  Primary 6 paradigm is the picture to copy.
- Stuck between used to and be used to: ask "finished
  past, or accustomed now?" Finished → used to + verb.
  Accustomed now → be used to + -ing/noun.
- This level's next door: Exclamations and orders —
  the sentence forms that command and cry out.
