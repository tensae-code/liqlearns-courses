# Inversion

## Do it first

A friend says: "I can swim." You answer: "So can I." Look at your answer — the words *can* and *I* have changed places. English sometimes turns a sentence around on purpose, and the turn has a name: **inversion**. This lesson teaches the two inversion patterns Eritrea's Grade 12 book names.

## Sub lesson 1 — So do I — agreeing, turned around

When you agree with what someone just said, English can answer with **So** — and then the helper verb steps in front of the person (printed pp.108–139 in the Grade 12 book's Unit 5 work): *I do my homework every day. — **So do I.*** *She came early. — **So did he.*** *They will help us. — **So will we.*** The helper must be the statement's own helper: a *do* statement takes *do*, a past statement takes *did*, a *will* statement takes *will*. If the statement has no helper, English lends it one — *do* for the present, *did* for the past. The turn is small, but it is exact: So + helper + person, never So + person + helper.

*Difficulty: One pattern, but the helper must match the statement's tense and verb — the matching is the skill.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Not only … but … — fronting the first part

The second pattern stresses the first of two things by moving it to the front — and the fronted part inverts too: *Not only did he pass the examination, but he came first in the school.* In the plain order you would say *He not only passed…, but he also came first*; fronting **Not only** lifts the first fact up and makes the reader wait for the second. Notice what inverts: only the fronted part — *Not only **did he** pass* — while the *but* part keeps its plain order: *but **he came** first*. One turn per sentence, in the part that moved.

*Difficulty: Holding both halves at once: the fronted half inverted, the second half plain — and keeping the two facts truly paired (both about the same subject, both worth saying).*

## Say it to your companion

Your companion makes a statement — "I like injera", "I went to the market yesterday", "I can ride a bicycle". You answer with So and the right helper. Then your companion pairs two facts about one person, and you rebuild the pair with Not only at the front. Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write five statements and answer each with So: one present, one past, one with will, one with can, one with a helper of your choice.
2. Write three Not only … but … sentences about real people or true things, and check: fronted part inverted, but part plain.
3. Take one of your Not only sentences and write it in the plain order (…not only… but also…). Which one says it stronger? Say why in one line.
