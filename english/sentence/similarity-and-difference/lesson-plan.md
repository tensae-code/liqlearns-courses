# Lesson plan — Similarity and Difference

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** Similarity and Difference
**Lesson:** similarity-and-difference — saying things match or do not: the same as, similar to, different from, whereas
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 28 — Comparing More Deeply
**The one thing:** The matching words: similarity (look like, alike, the same as, similar to) against difference (different from, unlike, whereas, not the same).
**This lesson's own level:** Sentence lesson (matching and mismatching): look like + noun; be alike; the same as; similar to; different from; unlike + noun; whereas joining two halves; the not-forms (not the same as, not similar to).

## Why this lesson exists

Mauritania teaches similarity and difference as named lessons in its Grade 9 book and repeats them near-verbatim in Grade 10 — look like, the same as, similar to against different from, unlike, whereas — and the course's comparing unit held stronger-comparisons and double-comparatives but no lesson for matching and mismatching themselves. This lesson is built from the placements under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06).

## Sub lessons

1. The same — look like, alike, the same as
2. Similar to — nearly the same
3. Different from — the difference words
4. Whereas — two sides in one sentence

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is needed before this lesson. Stronger Comparisons grades things (bigger, best); this lesson matches them (same, different), and each stands alone.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- Say two things match: "They look alike." "His phone is the same as yours."
- Say things nearly match: "Her car is similar to mine."
- Say things do not match: "Unlike Nouakchott, Boutilimitt is smaller."
- Join two sides with whereas.

## What you need
Nothing special — pairs of things in the room, same and different.

## The run of the lesson
1. ADD IMAGE: two brothers in the same shirt beside a third boy in a different one. The learner tries a matching sentence before any rule appears.
2. Teach sub lesson 1 on the class itself: who looks like whom, who has the same bag.
3. ADD GIF (1–2 seconds): two circles sliding together until they overlap but do not merge — similar, not the same.
4. Sub lesson 4 builds whereas sentences from the class's own pairs: homes, towns, subjects.

## Add-ons
- Same hunt: learners find the two most alike things in the room and defend the match with three forms.
- Twin towns: pairs describe their home towns with one same, one similar, one different, one whereas.

## If the learner is stuck
Keep to "the same as" and "different from" until matching and mismatching are both sayable. Similar and whereas are one step each after.

## Teacher note
The Grade 9 book teaches similarity and difference as back-to-back named lessons; the Grade 10 book repeats both nearly word for word (the sweep tags them duplicates) — one lesson in the course carries both books' sets. The Grade 9 book's own model sentence at printed p.21, "My English is no like natives", is wrong as printed; the lesson teaches the corrected form.
