## True or false

1. "The same as" is for things that nearly match. (Sub lesson 1)
   **Answer: False** — the same as is a full match; similar to is the near one.

2. "Alike" comes after the verb: "They are alike." (Sub lesson 1)
   **Answer: True** — alike sits after be.

3. "Different to" is this lesson's form. (Sub lesson 3)
   **Answer: False** — the books' form is different from.

4. "Whereas" joins two sides in one sentence. (Sub lesson 4)
   **Answer: True** — the hinge in the middle.

5. The book's sentence "My English is no like natives" is correct. (Sub lesson 2)
   **Answer: False** — it is wrong as printed; taught corrected.

6. "Not similar to" is a difference form. (Sub lesson 3)
   **Answer: True** — the not-forms turn similarity into difference.
