# The Future Perfect

## Sub lesson 1 — Will have done — the shape

The future perfect looks back from a point in the future.

- You take **will have** and add the **past participle**: *will have finished, will have read, will have left.*
- The time point usually comes with **by**: *By Friday, I will have finished the book.* *By 2030, they will have built the bridge.*
- The negative is **will not have** (won't have): *By June, she won't have saved enough.*
- Questions swap will to the front: *Will you have eaten by eight?*

Do it: say three things that will have happened in your own life by the end of this year. Use *will have* each time.

## Sub lesson 2 — Using it — deadlines and things finished by a set time

Use the future perfect when the finishing point matters more than the action.

- Deadlines: *The teacher will have marked the tests by Monday.*
- Plans and journeys: *By the time we arrive, the film will have started.*
- Counting forward: *In June, we will have lived here for ten years* — the action is complete AT that point, even if it also continues.
- Common mistake: do not use *will* after *by the time* for the earlier action — the *will have* part carries the future: *By the time you read this, I will have left.*

Do it: write two sentences about a school project — one thing that will have been finished by Friday, and one thing that won't have been finished.

## Say it to your companion

Read the key sentences aloud and explain them to your companion in your own words — what the thing is, and one example of your own.

## Do It Yourself

Close the lesson and use the thing once in real life today — say it, write it, or notice it — then say how it went in one sentence.
