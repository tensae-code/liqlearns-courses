**Acceptance count: taught in 20 countries (Bolivia, Ghana,
Mongolia, Laos, China, Indonesia, Iran, Sierra Leone,
Jordan, Greece, Kazakhstan, Iraq, Ethiopia, South Africa, Syria, South Sudan, Nepal, Armenia, Vietnam, Cambodia).** Mongolia boxes the -ed frame and drills the
movers by name; Bolivia states the whole paradigm including
the Did/didn't law; Ghana seats the simple past where
stories live; China adds the past staged regular first,
irregular second, each with its own formation table, plus
was/were past states; Indonesia makes the past simple the
recount tense of a whole primary chapter, prints its
irregular table, and states the system outright at
Grade 8; Iran runs regular then irregular past on the
same affirmative/negative/question frame, with a 70-verb
table behind it; Sierra Leone pairs the -ed lesson with
its irregular table and time markers, then rebuilds the
past regular-first for the senior exam; Jordan
stages the past in Grade 4 — be first, then regular,
then irregular — sets the past continuous against it
in Grade 5, and re-teaches the simple past in Grade 8.
Greece stages the past in Grade 5 — affirmative first, then negatives and questions, then the continuous and the two pasts together — combines the two boxes in Grade 6, and rebuilds the narrative past at Grade 7. Kazakhstan starts the lexical past at Grade 4 in Smiles (the other Grade 4 books stop at *was/were*), stages regular and irregular past inside one Grade 5 Excel module, and gives the tense its questions and negatives in both Grade 5 and Grade 6 English Plus. Iraq carries the past first in its Grade 3 stories, teaches it explicitly in Grade 4 — went, saw, had, did, played, was/were, did-questions and There was/were — and tables the past forms whole in its Grade 7 Student's Book reference. Ethiopia introduces the simple past in Grade 4 with regular and irregular forms, teaches it in full — affirmative, negative and interrogative — in Grade 5, returns to it in Grade 7, and contrasts it with the present perfect in Grade 9. South Africa makes the past a spine of the whole set: Grade 1 Workbook 2 rewrites in the past with -ed and "Yesterday I…" strips, Grade 2 Workbook 2 sets out the -ed system with its rule and the irregular verbs, Grade 4 Workbook 1 tabulates regular and irregular past forms, and Grade 2 FAL teaches past -ed heard as /t/ after a past-tense narrative. Syria compresses the whole past system into Grade 3's final units — was/were and there was/were, regular -ed, could/couldn't — then stages it again in Grade 4, regular past first and irregular past with did/didn't second. South Sudan joined 2026-11-03: its Primary 4 Pupil's Book states the past tense in a Unit 8 NOTE with its conversion table, and its Secondary 1 Student's Book teaches the past simple in full — -ed spelling rules, didn't negatives and did questions — in Unit 3. Nepal carries the past from Grade 2, where past continuous and past simple arrive together, through did/didn't phone talk in Grade 3 and present→past conversion in Grade 4, to Grade 5's did-questions, past perfect and four-past-tenses table, and Grade 6's past simple and past continuous unitArmenia sets past simple against past continuous in Grade 7, teaches the past perfect and used to in Grade 8, and revises the past tenses whole in Grade 12. ; Other closed countries join as their books
are read (see READING-QUEUE.md).
