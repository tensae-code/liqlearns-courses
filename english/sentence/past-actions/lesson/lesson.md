# Past actions — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Sentence · Lesson: Past actions
Built from: Mongolia, English VI (the past simple box and
the "Do you remember these verbs?" matching list) —
Bolivia, the ministry teacher compendium (the past paradigm:
-ed frame, didn't + base, "Did + subject + base") — Ghana,
the NaCCA B4–B6 curriculum (the simple past strand).

## Do it first
Tell three true things about yesterday: you woke, you ate,
you went somewhere. In your own language the verbs changed
shape to say "finished, before now." English changes them
too — one coat for most verbs, special shapes for the famous
few. Yesterday is the friendliest text there is: you already
know everything that happened. Only the coats are new.

## Then the explanation
**The -ed coat — the regular crowd.** Most verbs step into
the past by taking **-ed**: play→**played**, walk→walked,
cook→cooked, watch→watched, visit→visited. Same coat for
every who — I played, she played, they played. (The past
asks for no -s guard; the coat is the whole change.) Its
spellings ride along: verbs ending in e take just -d
(live→lived); short verbs double (stop→stopped) — the same
sewing you met in -ing.

**The irregular movers.** The most-used verbs refuse the
coat and change their whole shape — Mongolia's book stops
and drills them as a matching list, "Do you remember these
verbs?": **go→went, eat→ate, see→saw, come→came, take→took,
give→gave, drink→drank, run→ran, swim→swam, write→wrote,
read→read** (same spelling, new sound: "red"). Learn them
as pairs, like faces with two names — because that is what
they are: one verb, a present face and a past face.

**Be in the past — was and were.** Be wears two shapes:
**was** for I, he, she, it — **were** for you, we, they.
"I was at home." "They were happy." Questions swap as
always: "Was she at school?" "Were you ready?"

**Asking and denying — Did and didn't.** Bolivia's paradigm
gives the whole frame: statements run subject + verb-ed;
questions run **Did + subject + base verb**; negatives run
**didn't + base**. Hear the law inside it — the same
one-mark law as does and the -s: DID already carries the
past, so the verb steps back to its base: "Did you **walk**
yesterday?" never "Did you walked." "I **didn't eat**
breakfast," never "didn't ate." One past mark per sentence,
and did is holding it. Short answers ride the same word:
"Yes, I did." "No, he didn't."

**The clue words.** The past announces itself: **yesterday,
last night / last week / last year, …ago** (two days ago).
And the past is the story tense: Ghana's curriculum opens
the simple past (with its neighbour the past continuous —
"was walking when…" — waiting for a later level) exactly
where stories live, in narratives and recounts. Tell
anything that finished, and this is the coat it wears: "The
crow lived in a nest. One day it flew to the river." Every
story you will retell in Level 4 walks in these shapes.

## Say it to your companion
The yesterday interview. Ask five Did questions with
different verbs — eat, go, see, play, read: "What did you
eat yesterday?" needs a question word too (Unit 2's machine,
past coat). Answers whole, in the past: "I ate rice and
fish." Then swap — and the asker must use at least two
irregular movers among the five. Count the coats between
you: regulars on the rack, movers on the shelf.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write your yesterday in six sentences — three regular
   coats, three movers, be at least once (was/were).
2. Ask a friend three Did questions; write their true
   answers, whole.
3. Take one every-day paragraph from Present actions and
   push the whole thing into yesterday.
4. Deny three things you did NOT do yesterday with didn't +
   base — true denials only.
