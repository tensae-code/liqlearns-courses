# Hands-on — Past actions

The lesson told about real yesterdays.

## The coat rack
Two wall lists, started today and kept growing: THE RACK
(regulars, with their -ed forms) and THE SHELF (movers, as
pairs). Every past verb you meet this week goes on its list
in both faces. Mongolia's matching list is the shelf's first
ten. A verb you cannot place is a question to bring back —
the rack is the lesson made visible.

## Yesterday line
Draw your yesterday as a line of six boxes, waking to
sleeping. In each box, one past sentence, whole: "I woke up
at six." "I ate bread." Read the line aloud start to finish —
that is a recount, the past doing its story job.

## Did-detective
A partner tells three things about their yesterday — two
true, one false. You may ask only Did questions to hunt the
false one ("Did you really swim yesterday?"). Their answers
must be whole and past. The false one usually trips on its
own coat.

## Photo recount
Take any photograph of a finished moment (yours, a book's,
a magazine's). Say five past sentences about what happened
just before the picture: who came, what they ate, where they
went. The picture is frozen; the past around it is yours to
tell.
