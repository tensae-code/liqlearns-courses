# Lesson plan — Past actions

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Past actions
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 12 — Now, Before, Later — the second lesson of the unit (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** telling what already
happened — the verb's past coat: -ed for the regular crowd,
their own past forms for the irregular movers, was/were for
be, and Did/didn't for asking and denying, where the verb
steps back to its base.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Present actions
(same verbs, other time) and Unit 11's machinery (Did is the
engine's past twin). It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown changing — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The -Ed Coat** — put any regular verb into the past (spellings included: lived, stopped), for every who, without a miss. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **The Movers and Be** — the great movers by heart as pairs (go–went, eat–ate, see–saw, come–came, take–took, give–gave, drink–drank, run–ran), and was/were placed by the who every time. (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **Did, Didn't, and the Retelling** — ask and deny with the verb stepped back to base after Did/didn't — never two past marks — tell the past by its clue words, and retell a short finished story in whole past sentences. (Was depth Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Present actions. Recommended, never
  forced.
- The past continuous ("was walking when…") is named in
  Ghana's strand beside the simple past; it is a deeper
  layer for later levels — this lesson plants the simple
  past whole, and names the neighbour honestly.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Put regular verbs into the past with -ed (and its
   spellings): played, walked, cooked, watched.
2. Use the great irregular movers by heart: go→went,
   eat→ate, see→saw, come→came, take→took, give→gave,
   drink→drank, run→ran (Mongolia's own matching list is the
   drill's seed).
3. Ask and deny in the past: "Did you walk?" — verb back to
   base after Did; "didn't + base" for the negative
   (Bolivia's paradigm whole).
4. Tell a short finished story — yesterday, last week, …ago
   as the clue words.

## What you need
Yesterday — your own. Paper and a pencil; a partner for the
retelling.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner tells three true things
   they did yesterday in their own language, then hears how
   English coats each verb for the past (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the -ed coat and its spellings; the
   irregular movers; was/were; Did and didn't (the verb steps
   back); the clue words (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the movers part: a picture of GO walking
     through a door marked BEFORE and coming out changed —
     WENT — its letters reshuffled, 1–2 seconds — the student
     says the pair (go–went, eat–ate) as each verb passes
     through.
   - ADD IMAGE under the coat part: a picture of a tailor's
     rack — one coat labelled -ED fitting most verbs, and a
     shelf of odd-shaped coats with the movers' names
     (WENT, ATE, SAW) → the student sorts eight verbs to the
     rack or the shelf.
   - ADD AUDIO in the Did part: a voice asking past
     questions ("Did you walk yesterday?") and denying
     ("No, I didn't walk") — the student answers six with
     true answers about their own yesterday.
3. **Say it to your companion** — the yesterday interview:
   "What did you eat? Where did you go?" — answers whole in
   the past (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — a
     day replayed in the past tense, in the super-simple
     voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served
   at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — write your yesterday in six past
   sentences; ask a friend three Did questions and write
   their true answers (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- "Did you went?" → Did already carries the past; the verb
  steps back to base after it — the same one-mark law as
  does and the -s. One past mark per sentence, and did is
  holding it.
- "eated / goed" → the movers are learned as PAIRS, like
  names: go–went is one word with two faces. Mongolia's
  matching list exists for exactly this.
- -ed swallowed in speech → the three sounds of -ed
  (/t/ walked, /d/ played, /ɪd/ wanted) — hear them in the
  audio slot; say the coat aloud every time while learning.
- Mixes was/were → be's past has two shapes only: was for
  I/he/she/it, were for you/we/they — the Pronouns lesson's
  map carries them exactly.
