# Hands-on — Participle clauses

You do it, we guide you. Everything here is done with a
companion or the Explain Companion.

1. **Shrink and grow.** The companion reads full
   relative clauses ("the woman who is carrying a
   baby," "the song that was written in 1900"). The
   learner shrinks each to its participle, then grows
   it back. One point per clean shrink, one per clean
   growth; a passive shrunk to -ing (or the reverse)
   must be rebuilt. (Egypt's Secondary 12 pair is the
   model: talking / stolen.)
2. **The two-action morning.** Narrate your morning in
   four sentences, each opening with having: "Having
   woken late, I ran." "Having missed the bus, I
   walked." The companion checks the law each time —
   same subject, first action finished.
3. **The description race.** Describe a busy scene in
   the room or outside the window using only participle
   clauses stuck on nouns: "the man selling bread,"
   "the children chased by the dog," "the bicycle
   leaning on the wall." Five descriptions, a mix of
   active and passive.
4. **The repair shop.** The companion reads broken
   sentences ("Opening the box, the keys fell out."
   "Having eaten, the plate was empty."). The learner
   names the false subject the participle points at,
   then repairs the sentence two ways: change the main
   subject, or grow the clause back in full.
