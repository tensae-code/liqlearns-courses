# Lesson plan — Participle clauses

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Participle clauses
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 23 — Joining with Who and Which — the third lesson of the unit
(CATCH-UP-PLAN.md), after Non-defining relative clauses.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the participle
clause — the relative clause shrunk to its participle:
present participle for an active meaning (the man
talking… = who is talking…), past participle for a
passive one (the car stolen… = which was stolen…), and
the perfect participle (having finished…) for the
action that finished first — all under one law: the
clause's hidden subject is the main sentence's subject.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The present
perfect (the past participle is already the learner's)
and on Relative clauses (every participle clause here
can be un-shrunk back into one). Perfect GERUNDS
(having done after a verb) belong to the next level's
gerunds lesson — here having done works only as a
clause, with its own subject seat in the main sentence.
**What the learner can do at the end:** shrink a
relative clause to its participle and grow it back;
choose -ing for active and the past participle for
passive; put the earlier action in the perfect
participle; and spot a misplaced participle — one whose
hidden subject is not the main subject.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Participle Clauses Reduced Relatives** — shrink who/which + be clauses both ways — present participle for active, past participle for passive — and grow them back without changing the meaning. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Participle Clauses Reason, Time and Perfect Participle** — open a sentence with a participle clause for reason or time, with the main subject doing the participle's work — the perfect participle for the action that finished first. (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **Participle Clauses Hidden-Subject Law** — catch and repair a misplaced participle — name the wrong subject the clause points at and rebuild the sentence so the participle's doer and the main subject are one. (Was depth Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Relative clauses and The present
  perfect (for the participle shapes). Recommended,
  never forced — the un-shrinking test below rebuilds
  both on the spot.
- The speaker button reads the explanations in the
  learner's own language; the target forms (talking,
  stolen, having finished and every model clause) stay

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a long sentence card,
   "the man who is talking to my father," folding down
   like a concertina to a short card, "the man talking
   to my father."** Written slot: "a picture of a clause
   folding down to its participle." What the student
   does: the student unfolds it again aloud — who is
   talking — to prove nothing was lost.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of a stolen car with a tag:
   "the car stolen last night," the tag's missing words
   (which was) shown as ghosts.** Written slot: "a
   picture of a passive clause with its missing words
   as ghosts." What the student does: the student says
   the ghost words — the past participle always hides a
   passive.
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of two runners: the first
   crossing a line marked HAVING FINISHED, the second
   starting marked THEN.** Written slot: "a picture of
   the earlier action finishing first." What the student
   does: the student orders two actions of their own
   morning with having: "Having eaten, I…"
4. **Slot 4 — a picture of a dog wearing a sign "Walking
   down the street" while its owner waves from across
   the road, with a red cross over the sentence "Walking
   down the street, the dog barked at me" used wrongly.**
   Written slot: "a picture of a participle looking for
   its real subject." What the student does: the student
   says who is REALLY walking in the broken sentence —
   and why the participle points at the dog.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first** (lesson/lesson.md): one long
   description of a man in a station, said first in the
   learner's own language, then shrunk.
2. **The reduced relative — active**: present
   participle = who/which + is/was + -ing (Egypt,
   Secondary 12 Term 2: "the man talking to my father").
3. **The reduced relative — passive**: past participle
   = who/which + was + participle (Egypt: "the car
   stolen last night").
4. **Free participle clauses**: reason and time —
   Armenia Grade 11 §5.2 teaches participle clauses to
   express reason, in its artifacts text (pp.132–135);
   Vietnam Grade 11 Unit 5 names present and past
   participle clauses as its grammar (pp.52–61).
5. **The perfect participle**: having + past participle
   for the action that finished first (Armenia §5.2
   includes it; Vietnam Grade 11 Unit 7 pairs perfect
   participle clauses with perfect gerunds).
6. **The hidden-subject law and the misplaced
   participle**: Ethiopia Grade 12 revises participles
   and participle phrases beside misplaced participles
   and modifiers (Unit 3) — the trap this lesson names.
7. **Do it yourself** (the three closing tasks in the
   lesson): the shrink-and-grow round, the two-action
   morning, the repair shop.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck on which participle: un-shrink. Grow the clause
  back to who/which + be: if the be-form is active
  (is talking), take -ing; if it is passive (was
  stolen), take the past participle.
- Stuck on the perfect participle: ask which action
  finished FIRST. That one wears having; the main verb
  carries the second.
- This lesson's next door: Level 24 opens with Gerunds
  and infinitives — where having done returns as a
  verb's object, a different seat from this lesson's
  clause.
