# The passive in every tense — true or false

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to, by the coat the item actually asks about. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — The Passive — Continuous: the Work Is Moving, and Sub lesson 2 — The Passive — Perfect: the Work Stands Finished · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 3 — The Passive — Modal and Future: Rules and Tomorrow, and Sub lesson 4 — The Passive After Verbs — Being and To Be · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 5 — Two-Object Passives, and Sub lesson 6 — The Impersonal Passive — No Owner; each item is marked with the one coat it asks about.

Say true or false. If it is false, say why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** The participle changes its shape in every passive coat. **Answer: False** — the participle never changes; the be-family word in front of it carries the tense — being, been, be.

**2. (Sub lesson 6)** "He is said to be rich" and "It is said that he is rich" report the same rumour. **Answer: True** — the personal and impersonal frames are two cameras on one report (Malaysia, Form 5, p.89).

**3. (Sub lesson 6)** There is a passive form for the future continuous (will be being built) in Malaysia's Form 5 treatment. **Answer: False** — the book's Grammar Reference states plainly there is none — nor for the perfect continuous tenses — as filed.
