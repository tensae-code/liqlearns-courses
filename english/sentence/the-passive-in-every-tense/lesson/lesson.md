# The passive in every tense — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Sentence · Lesson: The passive in every tense
Built from: Kuwait, English Pearls 9B (p.38) and 7A — Yemen, English Course for Yemen Book 5 (Grade 11) — Malaysia, Form 5 textbook, Unit 7 (pp.87–89) and Form 3 Close-Up B1, Unit 9 — Bolivia, Year 6, Topic 5 (p.221) — Nepal, English 10 Teacher's Guide, Unit 8 (teacher's-guide evidence) — Greece (for-and-against formal style) — Egypt (continuous and perfect passive boxes, via EGYPT-BOOK-MAP.md).

*Soft hint only: if you know The passive (be + participle, present and past) and The present perfect, this lesson will be easier — but it stands alone. The basic coats are named in one line and not retaught. Start here if this is the thing you want to learn.*

**How this lesson is read aloud.** The speaker button reads the explanation in your own main language. The TARGET forms — the English shapes in **bold** — are never translated and are always read in the English voice, then the reading returns to your language. The target is the thing itself; the words around it are the explanation.

## Do it first
Walk past any building site and say, in your own language, what is happening to the bridge, the road, the house — not who is doing it. Then repeat one rumour the same way: "people say he is rich." English has a passive coat for every one of those moments — for work still moving, work finished, rules, tomorrow, and whispers with no owner. The basic coats (is built, was built) belong to The passive; this lesson builds every coat after them.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Passive — Continuous: the Work Is Moving
**Continuous passives — the work is moving.** **Is being + participle** (present) and **was being + participle** (past): "The solar panels **are being installed**." "The road **was being repaired** all week." Kuwait's Pearls 9B states this coat as a named set — present continuous, past continuous and present perfect passives together (p.38) — and Egypt boxes the present continuous passive on its own page in the run ("is being built"). The receiver is mid-process: not planned, not finished — being done.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Passive — Perfect: the Work Stands Finished
**Perfect passives — the work stands finished.** **Has/have been + participle** and **had been + participle**: "Three new schools **have been built**." "The letter **had been sent** before we called." Kuwait 9B teaches this coat on the same page (p.38); Yemen's Book 5 (Grade 11) extends its passive line to the present-perfect passive alongside the modal and future coats; Egypt's Secondary 12 book boxes the present perfect passive in the same lesson as its active twin (have/has + been + participle).

## Sub lesson 3 — The Passive — Modal and Future: Rules and Tomorrow
**Modal and future passives — rules and tomorrow.** After any modal, be stays plain: **must be done**, **can be turned**, **should be checked** — Kuwait's Pearls 7A is a present-system book whose passive is exactly this present/modal pair. The future coat is **will be + participle**: "The project **will be finished** next Friday" (Yemen Book 5 carries the future passive in the same extension). Bolivia's Year 6 process text — "How is plastic made? Step by step" (Topic 5, p.221) — runs its whole explanation in these coats: the polymer **is used**, plastic **is made** from natural gas which **can be found**…; Bolivia's six-year spine names the Year 6 achievement plainly: the passive voice in six tenses.

## Sub lesson 4 — The Passive After Verbs — Being and To Be
**Passives after verbs — being and to be.** A passive can follow another verb as a gerund or infinitive: "He hates **being watched**." "The form needs **to be signed**." Malaysia's Form 5 book teaches the passive in full, and its own honest summary lists the set: all common tenses, **gerund, infinitive and modal passives**, and the get-passive in its Grammar Reference (Unit 7). Nepal's Grade 10 **Teacher's Guide** — teacher's-guide evidence, worded as such — names the same ground from the teaching side: its hobbies unit's grammar is "passives including 'being' and 'having' passives" (Unit 8), **having been + participle** being the perfect-gerund coat: "**Having been warned** twice, he stopped."

## Sub lesson 5 — Two-Object Passives
**Two-object passives.** Verbs like give, send, teach and show carry two receivers — a person and a thing — and EITHER can take the front seat: "They gave **Maria a prize**" → "**Maria was given** a prize" / "**A prize was given to Maria**." When the thing leads, the person returns behind **to**. Malaysia's Form 5 full-passive treatment carries this ground inside its "passive in full" set (Unit 7, as filed).

## Sub lesson 6 — The Impersonal Passive — No Owner
**The impersonal passive — the whisper with no owner.** Malaysia's Form 5 book teaches it by name as **impersonal and personal structures** (Unit 7, Grammar 2, p.89): "**It is believed that** the city is 500 years old" — a whole sentence held behind It is said / known / believed / thought that… — and its personal twin: "**He is believed to be** 500 years old" → built as subject + **is said / known / believed / thought to + infinitive**: "People say he is rich" → "**It is said that** he is rich" / "**He is said to be** rich." The same book's set carries the rides-in form **should be allowed to**: "Children **should be allowed to** choose." Greece's for-and-against essay teaching explains WHY this coat exists: its formal-style checklist names **impersonal constructions and the passive voice** together as marks of formal writing — when the writer must sound like no one in particular, the impersonal passive is the coat.

1. **Media slot 1 — a picture of a bridge in three frames: workers building it now, the finished bridge, and a sign saying WILL OPEN FRIDAY. What you do: match one passive coat to each frame — is being built / has been built / will be opened.**
2. **Media slot 2 — a picture of a locked gate with a notice: VISITORS MUST BE ACCOMPANIED. What you do: read the modal passive aloud and say who is missing from it (the doer).**
3. **Media slot 3 — a picture of a crowd whispering, with a speech bubble: PEOPLE SAY HE IS RICH. What you do: rebuild the whisper both impersonal ways — It is said that… / He is said to….**

## Say it to your companion
Report your street like a news reader and a rumour-monger in turns. Your companion names a coat — continuous, perfect, modal, future, impersonal — and you must produce one true sentence about your street in that coat before passing the turn. A coat may not repeat until all five have been worn.

## Do it yourself
1. **One building, three coats.** Write one real unfinished work near you in the continuous, perfect (for its finished parts) and future passives.
2. **Both receivers.** Take "They sent the school a letter" and "The teacher showed us a film" — write BOTH passive turns of each.
3. **Whisper both ways.** Take two things "people say" about a famous person or place and write each in both impersonal frames: It is said that… and He/She is said to….
