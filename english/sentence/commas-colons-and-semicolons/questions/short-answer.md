# Short Answer — Commas, Colons and Semicolons

(Every item is marked with the sub lesson it belongs to: (Sub lesson N). The gate pool for a sub lesson is all of that sub lesson's questions — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the whole set is the pool.)

1. What is the difference between a comma and a full stop? (Sub lesson 1)
   — A comma is a small pause inside a sentence and the sentence runs on after it; a full stop ends the sentence.
2. Add the missing comma and say what it fixes: "After eating the children washed the dishes." (Sub lesson 1)
   — "After eating, the children washed the dishes." The comma separates the opening part, so the children are not the ones being eaten.
3. When do you choose a colon over a semicolon? (Sub lesson 2)
   — A colon points forward to a list or explanation the sentence promises; a semicolon joins two closely linked parts sideways — choose by the job.
4. Write the punctuation: "Bring these _ a pen, a book, a ruler." (Sub lesson 2)
   — A colon — "Bring these: a pen, a book, a ruler."
