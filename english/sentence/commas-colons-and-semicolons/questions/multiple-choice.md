# Multiple Choice — Commas, Colons and Semicolons

(Every item is marked with the sub lesson it belongs to: (Sub lesson N). The gate pool for a sub lesson is all of that sub lesson's questions — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the whole set is the pool.)

1. A comma in a sentence is… (Sub lesson 1)
   a) A full stop
   b) A small pause and separator — the sentence runs on after it ✓
   c) The end of the sentence
   d) Only for lists

2. Which sentence uses its comma well? (Sub lesson 1)
   a) I bought, apples oranges and, pears
   b) I bought apples, oranges and pears ✓
   c) I bought apples oranges, and pears.
   d) I, bought apples oranges and pears

3. The best test for a comma is… (Sub lesson 1)
   a) One comma per line
   b) The reading voice: a small pause or separation that keeps the meaning clear ✓
   c) As many as possible
   d) Commas are never needed

4. A colon (:) mostly… (Sub lesson 2)
   a) Ends a sentence
   b) Points forward to a list or explanation the sentence has promised ✓
   c) Joins two sentences loosely
   d) Marks a question

5. A semicolon (;) is… (Sub lesson 2)
   a) The same as a comma
   b) Stronger than a comma and weaker than a full stop ✓
   c) Only for dates
   d) A typing mistake

6. Which sentence uses a semicolon well? (Sub lesson 2)
   a) The rain stopped; the match began. ✓
   b) The rain; stopped the match began.
   c) The rain stopped, the; match began.
   d) The rain stopped; and began.
