# Hands-On — Commas, Colons and Semicolons

Things you do with real people, real pages and real screens. No special materials needed beyond what is named.

1. **Three ways.** Write one sentence with no commas, too many commas, and commas just right. Read all three aloud to a partner and vote on the clear one.
2. **Mark the marks.** From any page of a book, copy one sentence with a comma, one with a colon and one with a semicolon (or write your own if the page lacks one). Name each mark's job.
3. **Join and point.** Take four short sentences. Join one pair with a semicolon; rewrite the other pair so a colon introduces a list.
4. **Comma rescue.** Write a sentence that goes wrong without its comma (like "Let's eat Grandma"). Swap with a partner and fix each other's.
