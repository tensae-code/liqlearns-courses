# Lesson plan — Joining with and and but

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Joining with and and but
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 13 — Joining and Describing — the first lesson of the unit (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** joining words and
whole sentences with AND (it adds — same direction) and BUT
(it turns — a contrast), and letting a shared who ride once
("The boy woke up and prayed").
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Building a
sentence (what is being joined is whole thoughts) and on
every list sentence since Level 1 ("I like rice and fish").
It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown changing — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **And, the Adder** — join words, lists, and sentences with and; drop a shared who the way Ghana's example does; place the list's commas and final and. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **But, the Turner** — join with but where a real turn stands, and feel a false but (no turn) as wrong without being told. (Was depth Level 2. The lists paragraph stands after the but paragraph in the text and is kept in this sub lesson; its list work is Sub lesson 1's depth, revisited there.)
3. **Choosing Cold** — given bare pairs, pick the true joiner every time and defend it by the meaning's direction; name or as the choice-brother and leave because/so and however for their own homes. (Was depth Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Building a sentence. Recommended,
  never forced.
- OR stands in Ghana's list beside and/but (and, but, or,
  nor) — named here as the choice-brother; because/so join
  CAUSES and results and have their own later home — Ghana's
  own widening order is followed, not rushed.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Join words and lists with and: "rice and fish," "Mita,
   Pavel and Orpa."
2. Join sentences with and, dropping a shared who: "The boy
   woke up and prayed" (Ghana's own worked example).
3. Join with but when the second half TURNS against the
   first: "The mango is sweet but small."
4. Hear the difference cold: and continues the road, but
   bends it — and pick the right joiner from the meaning,
   not the habit.

## What you need
Two sentences about your own morning. Paper and a pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner says two true sentences
   about this morning; the lesson joins them twice — once
   with and, once with but — and the learner feels which
   join is true (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — and the adder; but the turner; the
   shared-who drop; the comma in lists (shown, from Level
   2's own lists); or named (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the turn part: a picture of a road running
     straight through an AND gate, then the same road
     bending sharply at a BUT gate, 1–2 seconds — the student
     calls "straight!" or "bend!" for six sentence pairs as
     they pass the gates.
   - ADD IMAGE under the adder part: a picture of Ghana's
     own worked example as a two-panel strip — a boy waking,
     a boy praying, the two panels stitched by the word AND
     into one strip → the student stitches three panel pairs
     the same way.
   - ADD AUDIO in the meaning part: a voice reading joined
     pairs, pausing before the joiner — the student shouts
     "and!" or "but!" before the voice lands it, from the
     meaning alone.
3. **Say it to your companion** — chain talk: build a shared
   day sentence by sentence, each new sentence joined with
   and or but, the joiner chosen by truth (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the
     road and the bend, in the super-simple voice (made
     later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — write your morning in four sentences,
   then join them into two with the true joiners; retell one
   Level 2 description with a but-turn in it (lesson/lesson.md).

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- GIF — a picture of a road passing an AND gate straight and
  bending at a BUT gate → the student calls the direction for
  six pairs.
- IMAGE — a picture of the woke/prayed panels stitched by
  AND → the student stitches three pairs.
- AUDIO — joined pairs read with a pause before the joiner →
  the student calls the joiner from the meaning.
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later) → the student
  joins along with it.

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the
plan is the build sheet, so nothing the lesson needs gets forgotten.

## If the learner is stuck
- Joins everything with and → the truth test: say the pair
  with BUT aloud; if the meaning fights ("I like tea but I
  like coffee" — no fight there), and was right. The joiner
  follows the meaning's direction, not a coin flip.
- Repeats the who every time ("The boy woke up and the boy
  prayed") → Ghana's example drops it ONCE the who is
  shared; read both aloud — the short one is the book's own
  finished shape. (Keeping the who is never WRONG, only
  heavier; say so honestly.)
- Runs sentences together with and-and-and → one join per
  pair is the lesson's measure; a page of single sentences
  is better writing than one endless chain. Level 4 grows
  the longer weaves.
