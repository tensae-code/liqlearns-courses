# Hands-on — Joining with and and but

The lesson played with real sentence pairs.

## Stitch the panels
Draw (or cut from a magazine) six small pictures of doings:
wake, pray, eat, run, read, sleep. Deal two at a time and
stitch them into one spoken sentence with and, the who
riding once — Ghana's own panels, your own pictures. Then
deal a pair that FIGHTS (sun/rain, hungry/full) and stitch
with but.

## The joiner shout
A caller reads bare pairs slowly, with a long pause at the
join. Players shout AND or BUT into the pause — from the
meaning alone. A wrong shout must be defended or dropped:
the defence ("there IS a turn: he wanted out, rain kept him
in") is worth more than the shout.

## Morning chains
The lesson's chain talk, written: in pairs, build a morning
of eight sentences where every sentence after the first is
joined to the growing chain by its true joiner, written
down as you go. Read the final chain aloud — one road, with
its bends standing where the morning truly bent.

## List the market
Write tonight's real shopping as one list sentence (commas,
final and), then grow it into two joined sentences about who
buys and who carries: and for the same direction, but if the
load turns against someone. True lists only — the lesson
rides on the real errand.
