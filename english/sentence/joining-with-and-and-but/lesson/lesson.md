# Joining with and and but — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Sentence · Lesson: Joining with and and but
Built from: Ghana, the NaCCA curricula (Basic 3: compound
sentences open — the coordinating conjunction AND, then AND
and BUT; the JHS conjunctions list and combination drill) —
Bolivia, the ministry teacher compendium (linking words:
but the conjunction, however its adverb neighbour).

## Do it first
Say two true sentences about this morning: "I woke up
early." "I drank tea." Now hear them as one: "I woke up
early **and** I drank tea." Nothing was added but one small
word — and the two thoughts became one road. Try the other
joiner: "I woke up early **but** I drank tea." Something
feels wrong — no turn happened between waking and tea. The
joiners are not glue; they are DIRECTIONS. This lesson
teaches the two first directions.

## Then the explanation

## Sub lesson 1 — And, the Adder

**AND — the adder.** Ghana's curriculum opens compound
sentences with it, worked in full: "The boy woke up" + "The
boy prayed" → "**The boy woke up and prayed**." And joins
what travels the SAME direction: two doings by one who, two
things in one list ("rice **and** fish," the word levels' own
sentences), two whole sentences. When the who is shared, it
rides once — the second "the boy" falls away and the
sentence runs light. (Keeping it is never wrong, only
heavier; the book's finished shape drops it.)

## Sub lesson 2 — But, the Turner

**BUT — the turner.** The same unit seats AND and BUT
together because they are opposites in direction: but joins
what turns. "The mango is sweet **but** small." "I wanted to
play, **but** it rained." "She is small **but** strong."
Before but, the road runs one way; after but, it bends. If
there is no bend, but is the wrong joiner — that is the
whole test, and it never fails: read the pair, feel for the
turn.

**Lists and the last and.** In a list, commas carry the
middle and and carries the last pair: "Mita, Pavel **and**
Orpa" — the dress you met in the Food lesson's shopping
sentences. One and per list, standing at the end like a
gate.

## Sub lesson 3 — Choosing Cold

**The family, named.** Ghana's later list gives the four
coordinators: **and, but, or, nor**. Or is the choice-brother
— "Tea or coffee?" — named here, drilled in its own time.
Because and so, which join causes to results, widen the
family later in Ghana's own order ("Conjunctions widen:
because, since, so, although"). And Bolivia posts the
warning for the road ahead: **but** is a conjunction — it
joins INSIDE one sentence — while **however** is an adverb
neighbour that turns from a new sentence. Here, but is
yours; however waits.

**One join per pair.** A joined sentence is two thoughts
with one direction between them. Chain three, four, five
with and-and-and and the road becomes a swamp — the books
join in pairs and let full stops do the rest. The Paragraph level will
teach the longer weaves; this lesson's mastery is the clean
pair, the true joiner, every time.

## Say it to your companion
Chain talk. Build a shared day, sentence by sentence: your
companion opens with one true sentence; you must join YOUR
sentence to theirs with and or but — chosen by the truth of
the direction — and say the whole growing chain aloud each
time. Six rounds. If a joiner is called wrong by the other
player ("no turn there!"), the pair stops and argues the
meaning — the argument is the lesson marking itself.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write your morning in four short sentences. Join them
   into two, with the TRUE joiner for each pair.
2. Join these pairs both ways and say which is true for
   you: "I like football. I like music." · "The day was hot.
   We played outside."
3. Take any description of yours from the word levels (a food, an
   animal) and add one but-turn to it that is TRUE.
4. Write a three-item list sentence with commas and its
   final and — a real shopping list for tonight.
