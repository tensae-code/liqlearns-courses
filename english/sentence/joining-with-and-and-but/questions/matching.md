# Joining with and and but — matching

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every set's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — And, the Adder · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — But, the Turner · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Choosing Cold · (Level 1–2) -> Sub lessons 1–2 — directions (Set 2).
Match each pair to its true joiner, and each joiner to its
direction.

**Set 1 (Sub lesson 3)** — bare pairs, cold.
1. "It is big." / "It is heavy." — **and**
2. "It is big." / "It is light to carry." — **but**
3. "Will you take tea?" / "Will you take coffee?" (one
   answer expected) — **or**
4. "She sang." / "She danced." — **and**
5. "He studied hard." / "He failed the test." — **but**
6. "We ate." / "We drank." — **and**

**Set 2 (Sub lessons 1–2)** — directions.
1. and — **goes straight: adds, same direction**
2. but — **bends: turns against what came before**
3. or — **offers a choice**
4. nor — **the negative brother of or (named, not drilled)**

**Set 3 (Sub lesson 2)** — finished joins.
1. "The boy woke up" + "The boy prayed" — **"The boy woke
   up and prayed"** (Ghana's example)
2. "I want fufu" + "I want kenkey" (the JHS drill, one dish
   tonight) — **"I want fufu or kenkey"**
3. "The soup is hot" + "the soup is good" (against the
   heat) — **"The soup is hot but good"**
