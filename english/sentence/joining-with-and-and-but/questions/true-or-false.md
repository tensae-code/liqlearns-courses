# Joining with and and but — true or false

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — And, the Adder · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — But, the Turner · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Choosing Cold.
Say true or false. If it is false, say why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** "The boy woke up and the boy prayed" is
wrong English.
**False** — it is heavier, not wrong. Ghana's finished shape
drops the shared who; the kept who breaks no law.

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** But can join any two sentences.
**False** — but needs a TURN between the halves. No bend, no
but.

**3. (Sub lesson 1)** In a list, and stands before the last item:
"rice, fish and oil."
**True** — commas carry the middle; and is the gate at the
end.

**4. (Sub lesson 3)** Because is one of the four coordinators
Ghana lists first.
**False** — the first four are and, but, or, nor. Because
widens the family later.

**5. (Sub lesson 2)** "I wanted to swim but the river was dry" has
a real turn in it.
**True** — wanting runs toward the water; the dry river
bends the road back.

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** A page joined with and-and-and in one long
chain is this lesson's best writing.
**False** — one true join per pair; full stops do the rest.
The longer weaves are Level 4's work.
