# Joining with and and but — short answer

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — And, the Adder · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — But, the Turner · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Choosing Cold.
Answer in a word or a short line.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Join with the who riding once: "Mita
sings. Mita dances."
**Answer:** "Mita sings and dances."

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** Dress the list: "We bought rice fish and
oil" — place the comma.
**Answer:** "We bought rice, fish and oil" — commas carry
the middle; and carries the last pair.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Join with a turn: "He is poor. He is
happy."
**Answer:** "He is poor but happy" (or "…but he is happy") —
the bend is real: poor one way, happy against it.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** Say why this join limps: "I like tea but I
like coffee."
**Answer:** no turn stands between the halves — both run
the same way; and is the true joiner ("I like tea and I
like coffee").

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Name Ghana's four coordinators.
**Answer:** and, but, or, nor.

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** Which family members widen the set later,
in Ghana's order?
**Answer:** because, since, so, although — causes and
results have their own later home.
