# Future perfect — true or false

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Future Perfect Shape · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Future Perfect By-Point and Twin · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Future Perfect Neighbours.

Say true or false. If it is false, say why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** "I will have went by then" is a good future-perfect sentence. **Answer: False** — went is the past tense; the corrected rule takes the participle: "I will have gone."

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** The future perfect can stand with no future point at all — no by, no time, nothing. **Answer: False** — the tense always leans on its by-point (by Friday, by the time…, in three days' time — Egypt's box).

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** South Africa's model "I will have spent all my money by this time next year" is a correct future-perfect sentence, even though its printed rule line is wrong. **Answer: True** — spent is the true participle and the by-point stands; the model is right, the rule line is the slip.
