# Future perfect — true or false

Say true or false. If it is false, say why.

**1. (Level 1)** "I will have went by then" is a good future-perfect sentence. **Answer: False** — went is the past tense; the corrected rule takes the participle: "I will have gone."

**2. (Level 2)** The future perfect can stand with no future point at all — no by, no time, nothing. **Answer: False** — the tense always leans on its by-point (by Friday, by the time…, in three days' time — Egypt's box).

**3. (Level 3)** South Africa's model "I will have spent all my money by this time next year" is a correct future-perfect sentence, even though its printed rule line is wrong. **Answer: True** — spent is the true participle and the by-point stands; the model is right, the rule line is the slip.
