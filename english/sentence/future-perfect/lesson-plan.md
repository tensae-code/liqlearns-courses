# Lesson plan — Future perfect

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Future perfect
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 20 — The Future in Detail (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the future perfect — **will have + past participle** — a thing that will be FINISHED by a future point: by Friday, by the time you arrive, it will have happened. Its continuous twin, **will have been + verb-ing**, measures how long it will have been going on by then.
**What the learner can do at the end:** build will have + participle and will have been + -ing; anchor both to a by-point (by Friday, by the time…); state South Africa's printed rule corrected; and sort this tense from the future continuous live.

1. **Future Perfect Shape** — build will have + past participle for every who — past participle, never past tense; the correction block that states that rule sits in Sub lesson 2's text by its position. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Future Perfect By-Point and Twin** — anchor every sentence to its by-point (by, by the time, before, in …'s time), and build the continuous twin with its for-measure up to the point. (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **Future Perfect Neighbours** — sort live at one future point: moving then (future continuous), finished by then (this tense, simple), stretch measured to then (this tense, continuous); the asking-and-denying block sits in this sub lesson's text. (Was depth Level 3.)

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The future continuous (its pair — the moving scene) and The present perfect (have + participle, one step forward). Recommended, never forced — this lesson stands alone for a learner who lands on it directly (DIRECT-ENTRY rule).
- Speaker convention: the explanation is read in the learner's main language; the TARGET forms are never translated and are read in the English voice.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a Friday calendar page with a finished, ticked work-list lying beside it, seen as if standing on Friday looking back.** What the student does: the student says the look-back sentence — "By Friday, I will have finished" — before the shape is named.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of two signposts at one future point: one showing a runner mid-stride, one showing the same runner holding a finished medal.** What the student does: the student sorts the two sentences — will be running (moving at the point) against will have finished (done by the point).
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of a two-year calendar strip with a small book drawn on every month, ending at a circled June.** What the student does: the student measures the stretch: "By June, I will have been learning for two years."

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first**: standing at a future point, looking back at a finished thing, in the learner's own language.
2. **The shape**: will have + past participle (Egypt Sec 11 T2, Future Perfect box p.19).
3. **The anchor**: by / by the time / before / in three days' time; South Africa's model sentence (WB2, printed pp.50–51).
4. **The correction**: South Africa's printed "past tense" rule is wrong — past participle; taught corrected, stated plainly.
5. **The continuous twin**: will have been + -ing for the stretch up to the point (Malaysia F5 U5 Grammar 2, p.63; F4 Module 4 for the simple form).
6. **The wider witnesses**: Nepal G7 U15 / G8 U7; Uzbekistan EM9 U2.
7. **Asking and denying; the neighbour sorted**: Will you have finished by six? At the point moving (continuous) versus by the point finished (perfect).
8. **Do it yourself**: the three closing tasks, including correcting the book in writing.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck on the participle: it is the same third shape as every perfect — gone, written, finished — never the past simple's shape (went, wrote). That is exactly the slip in South Africa's printed rule.
- Stuck on the anchor: this tense cannot float — find its by-point first (by Friday, by the time…); if no future point stands in the sentence, another future form is probably the coat.
- Stuck between the twins: finished by the point → simple (will have finished); how long it will have been going on → continuous (will have been learning for two years).
- This lesson's next door: Plans in the past (same level) — the future seen from inside a past story.
