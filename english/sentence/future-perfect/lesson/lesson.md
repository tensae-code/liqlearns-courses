# Future perfect — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Sentence · Lesson: Future perfect
Built from: Egypt, Secondary 11 Term 2 (the Future Perfect box, p.19) — Malaysia, Form 4 (Module 4) and Form 5 (Unit 5, Grammar 2, p.63) — Nepal, Grade 7 (Unit 15) and Grade 8 (Unit 7) — Uzbekistan, English Matters 9 (Unit 2) — South Africa, English Home Language Grade 6 Workbook 2 (worksheet 88, printed pp.50–51 — its printed formation rule is WRONG and is taught corrected here; see Sources).

*Soft hint only: if you know The future continuous (its pair — the moving scene) and The present perfect (have + participle, one step forward), this lesson will be easier — but it stands alone. Start here if this is the thing you want to learn.*

**How this lesson is read aloud.** The speaker button reads the explanation in your own main language. The TARGET forms — the English shapes in **bold** — are never translated and are always read in the English voice, then the reading returns to your language. The target is the thing itself; the words around it are the explanation.

## Do it first
Stand at a point in the future in your own language and look back: "By Friday, I will have finished the work. By the time you come home, dinner will have been cooked." From that future point, the thing is already DONE. English builds that look-back with will have + the participle — the present perfect's machinery, aimed forward.

## Sub lesson 1 — Future Perfect Shape
**The shape.** The future perfect is **will have + the past participle**: I **will have finished**. She **will have left**. They **will have built** it. Egypt's Secondary 11 Term 2 book boxes it in exactly these pieces (the Future Perfect box, p.19): will have + past participle, for actions completed before a specific time in the future or before another future action.

## Sub lesson 2 — Future Perfect By-Point and Twin
**Its anchor — the by-point.** This tense never floats; it always leans on a future point, and its travelling words name it: **by** Friday, **by** 2030, **by the time** you arrive, **before** the bell, **in three days' time** (Egypt's box lists by / by the time / before and the in-time frame). South Africa's Grade 6 Workbook 2 gives a child's model of it: "I will have spent all my money **by this time next year**" (worksheet 88, printed pp.50–51).

**A correction, stated plainly.** South Africa's same worksheet prints the formation rule as "will + have plus the **past tense** of the verb." That is wrong: the form required is the **past participle**, not the past tense — will have **gone** (not *will have went*), will have **written** (not *will have wrote*). This lesson teaches the corrected rule; the book's model sentence above is right, its rule line is not.

**The continuous twin.** **Will have been + verb-ing** measures the stretch up to the by-point: "By June, I **will have been learning** English for two years." Malaysia's Form 5 book teaches the pair together — future perfect simple, future perfect continuous, and their time words (Unit 5, Grammar 2, p.63) — and Form 4 meets the simple form at Module 4. Nepal teaches the tense at Grade 7 (Unit 15) and Grade 8 (Unit 7); Uzbekistan's English Matters 9 carries it at Unit 2 beside the future continuous.

## Sub lesson 3 — Future Perfect Neighbours
**Asking, denying, and the neighbour.** **Will** you **have finished** by six? — Yes, I will. The not-form: It **won't have arrived** by then. Beside its pair from the last lesson: the future continuous paints the action MOVING at the point (*at six, I will be working*); this tense looks at it FINISHED by the point (*by six, I will have finished*).

1. **Media slot 1 — a picture of a Friday calendar page with a finished, ticked work-list lying beside it, seen as if standing on Friday looking back.** What you do: the student says the look-back sentence — "By Friday, I will have finished" — before the shape is named.
2. **Media slot 2 — a picture of two signposts at one future point: one showing a runner mid-stride, one showing the same runner holding a finished medal.** What you do: the student sorts the two sentences — will be running (moving at the point) against will have finished (done by the point).
3. **Media slot 3 — a picture of a two-year calendar strip with a small book drawn on every month, ending at a circled June.** What you do: the student measures the stretch: "By June, I will have been learning for two years."

## Say it to your companion
Play the look-back with your companion. One names a future point — "by Sunday," "by the time school opens," "by next year." The other must answer with one thing that will have finished by then, whole: will have + participle. Then one stretch answer in the twin: will have been + -ing, with its measure. Four points each.

## Do it yourself
1. **By-points of your own.** Write three true future-perfect sentences: one by a day, one by the time (an event), one in (time)'s time.
2. **The twin measure.** Write two will have been sentences about real stretches — learning, living somewhere, working — each with for and its future point.
3. **Correct the book.** Copy South Africa's printed rule — "will + have plus the past tense of the verb" — then write the corrected rule under it, with one example that proves it (gone, not went).
