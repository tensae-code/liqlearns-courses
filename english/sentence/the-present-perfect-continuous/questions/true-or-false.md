# The present perfect continuous — true or false

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — The Present Perfect Continuous's Shape · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — The Stretch and Its Measures · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — The Marks — Just Stopped (item 5) and Sub lesson 4 — Recent Habits and the Neighbours (item 4, the count is the simple perfect's job).

Say true or false. If it is false, say why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** In this tense, been changes with the
who — "I been, she beens."
**False** — been never changes; have/has carries the
who, and the verb always wears -ing.

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** "Since" names a length of time, like
two hours.
**False** — since names the starting point (since four
o'clock, since Tuesday); for names the length (Libya,
Grade 10, p.13).

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** "It has been raining for two hours" can
be said while the rain is still falling.
**True** — the tense describes action that continues
from the past into now (Libya, Grade 8, p.38).

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** "I have been reading all morning" tells
you how many books I finished.
**False** — it watches the moving action and says
nothing about a count; counting the done is the simple
perfect's job ("I have read three books").

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** A stopped action can still wear this
tense when its mark shows now — "I'm tired because I
have been studying all night."
**True** — Egypt's box gives exactly this seat: recently
finished, with a visible present result (Secondary 12,
pp.17–19).
