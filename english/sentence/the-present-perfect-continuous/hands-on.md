# Hands-on — The present perfect continuous

You do it, we guide you. Done in the learner's real day,
with a companion or the Explain Companion.

1. **The ten-minute watch (Libya Grade 10's own lesson).**
   Watch people — family, street, a shop — for ten
   minutes. Report each person the book's way: a name, and
   what they have been doing: "Nadia has been making
   lunch." Five people minimum; the companion checks that
   every action is still moving, not finished.
2. **The two measures.** Take one real wait or one real
   job of work from today. Say it twice — once with since
   (the start), once with for (the length) — the bus-stop
   drill: We've been waiting since ten o'clock; we've
   been waiting for one hour. Three actions, both ways.
3. **Read the marks.** Hunt three footprints: wet ground,
   tired eyes, paint on hands, a full basket, an empty
   plate still warm. For each, say the just-stopped
   action that left it: It has been raining. She has been
   painting. They have been eating. The companion asks
   "how do you know?" — the mark is the answer.
4. **The lately report.** Tell your companion your last
   seven days in this tense — what you have been doing
   lately (learning, working, travelling, resting). Four
   sentences; habits of the stretch, not single acts.
