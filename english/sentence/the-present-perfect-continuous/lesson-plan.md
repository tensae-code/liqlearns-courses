# Lesson plan — The present perfect continuous

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** The present perfect continuous
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 18 — Deeper Sentences —
the second lesson of the unit (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the present perfect
continuous — have/has + been + verb-ing — the tense that
watches an action still moving from a start before now,
measured with for and since, and reads the marks of an
action that has just stopped.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The present
perfect (this level's first lesson — been is its
participle machinery), on Present actions' -ing coat
(Unit 12), and on Telling the time's for/since groundwork
(Unit 9).
**What the learner can do at the end:** build the tense
for every who (statement, not-form, question); answer
"What have you been doing?" and "How long…?"; place for
and since without a swap; and tell the continuous from
the simple perfect and from the present continuous.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The Present Perfect Continuous's Shape** —
   have/has + been + verb-ing for every who, statements,
   not-forms and questions — been never changes. (Was
   depth Level 1.)
2. **The Stretch and Its Measures** — the action still
   moving from a past start, and its measures: for the
   length, since the starting point. (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **The Marks — Just Stopped** — a visible mark explained
   by an action that has just stopped. (Was the marks
   half of depth Level 3.)
4. **Recent Habits and the Neighbours** — what you have
   been doing lately, and the live choice between this
   tense, the simple perfect (a count, a result) and the
   present continuous (only this minute); the Asking and
   denying close also stands here. (Was the neighbours
   half of depth Level 3.)
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The present perfect — the learner
  who has not met have/has + participle will trip on been.
  Recommended, never forced.
- The PAST perfect continuous (had been going) is a
  different thing and is not taught here; it belongs to
  the deeper past work later in the Sentence title.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a GIF (1–2 seconds) of rain falling on a
   street, looping, with a clock in the corner spinning
   from four o'clock to now.** Written slot: "a picture of
   rain that started at four o'clock and is still falling
   now." What the student does: the student says when it
   started and that it has not stopped — the two facts
   this tense always carries together.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of a tired student at a desk
   covered in books, rubbing their eyes.** Written slot:
   "a picture of someone tired because they have been
   studying all night." What the student does: before
   reading on, the student guesses the cause-sentence —
   the action has stopped, its mark (tiredness) is what
   shows.
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of a bus stop with a small crowd,
   a clock showing ten, and the same crowd still waiting
   as the clock shows eleven.** Written slot: "a picture
   of people who have been waiting since ten o'clock."
   What the student does: the student measures the wait
   twice — since ten (the start) and for one hour (the
   length) — and hears both measures name the same wait.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first** (lesson/lesson.md): two true sentences
   about the learner's own day — things started earlier
   and still going.
2. **The shape**: have/has + been + verb-ing; every piece
   an old friend (perfect partner, be's participle, the
   -ing coat).
3. **Its job**: action that continues from the past
   (Libya Grade 8's box, p.38) — the stretch, not the
   result.
4. **The question it answers**: "What have they been
   doing?" (Libya Grade 10, p.13) — reporting people by
   their ongoing actions.
5. **For and since**: length vs starting point, drilled
   on Libya Grade 10's Work Book sentences.
6. **Just stopped, marks showing**: Egypt's second seat —
   "I'm tired because I have been studying all night"
   (Secondary 12, pp.17–19).
7. **Recent habits**: Libya Grade 9's box — "I've been
   speaking English every day" (p.40).
8. **The neighbours**: simple perfect counts the done
   (How many?); this tense watches the moving (How
   long?); the present continuous is only this minute.
9. **Asking and denying**: Have you been swimming a lot?
   He hasn't been working.
10. **Do it yourself** (the three closing tasks): the
    what-have-they-been-doing watch, the day as a stretch,
    reading the marks.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck on been: it never changes — have/has carries the
  who, been is always been, the verb always wears -ing.
  Three fixed pieces; only the first moves.
- Stuck on for/since: ask "is it a length or a starting
  point?" Lengths (two hours, ten minutes, three days)
  take for; points (Tuesday, four o'clock, 2021) take
  since — Libya Grade 10's Work Book keys are the drill.
- Stuck between the two perfects: ask what the sentence
  points at — a finished count or result (simple) or the
  moving action and its length (continuous).
- This lesson's next door: The passive (same level) —
  the sentence turned around.
