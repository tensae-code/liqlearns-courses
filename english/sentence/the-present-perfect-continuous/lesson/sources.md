**Acceptance count: taught in 14 countries (Egypt,
Libya, Sierra Leone, Jordan, Greece, Kazakhstan, Iraq, Ethiopia, South Sudan, Nepal, Armenia, Cambodia, Pakistan, Sudan).** Libya meets the tense at Grade 8 inside a
records lesson, names it for recent habits at Grade 9,
and drills it as a full lesson with for/since at Grade
10. Egypt states the complete box at Secondary 12 — the
last tense its run boxes. Sierra Leone holds the tense
to the final year and teaches it in two SSS3 lessons —
the still-going form, then its questions and negatives —
inside its complete 12-tense system. Jordan teaches the
tense at Grade 10 as one lesson with its simple twin —
Present Perfect Simple and Continuous together — and
contrasts it again inside Grade 12's whole continuous-
and-perfect system. Greece teaches this tense earliest of all — at Grade 6 in primary, with for/since on a timeline — states the simple/continuous contrast formally at Grade 9, and matches the continuous form's uses to examples again in the EPAL book. Kazakhstan brings the tense in at Grade 8 — named in Excel's tense revision and contrasted with its simple twin in Laser — and works the simple/continuous pair again at Grade 9 in both Full Blast! and English Plus. Iraq brings the tense in late and states its arrival plainly: not at Grade 9, where no held book teaches it, but at Grade 10 in the "Writing home" lesson; Grade 11 revises it with for/since in "What have they been doing?", and Grade 12 sets the simple form against the continuous inside its rebuilt perfect system, with the continuous box printed in the Activity Book. Ethiopia teaches the tense in Grade 9 — Unit 8 presents present perfect and present perfect continuous together with forms tables, negatives, questions and since/for, and Unit 10 revises it in its four-tense table. South Sudan joined 2026-11-03: Secondary 1 teaches the tense outright in its Student's Book Unit 2 language practice — has/have + been + -ing, with for/since durations and recently/lately without duration — and its Teacher's Guide re-teaches it with fuller rules and answers. Nepal teaches the tense at Grade 9 — present perfect and present perfect continuous together in Unit 15, after the four present tenses are identified and practised at Grade 8 — and its Grade 9 teacher's guide, a non-companion DRAFT held as an incomplete excerpt, teaches it with since/for in its own Unit 19. Other closed countries join
as their books are read (see READING-QUEUE.md).
