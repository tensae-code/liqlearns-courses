# The present perfect continuous — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Sentence · Lesson: The present
perfect continuous
Built from: Libya, English for Libya Grade 8 Course Book
(Unit 3, p.38), Grade 9 Course Book (p.40, Grammar
Reference p.101), Grade 10 Course Book (p.13, Work Book
pp.11–12, Reference p.100) — Egypt, Secondary 12 Term 1
(Unit 1, pp.17–19).

## Do it first
Think about your morning. You woke up, and some things
started then and are STILL going: you have been awake for
hours. Maybe it has been raining since you left home.
Maybe you have been waiting for something all day. In the
last lesson the perfect counted what is DONE — I've
cleaned the windows, finished, result on show. This
lesson watches the other picture: the action itself,
still moving, started before now and not stopped yet.
Say two true ones about your own day before you read on.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Present Perfect Continuous's Shape
**The shape.** The present perfect continuous is
**have/has + been + verb-ing**: The pilot **has been
flying** for two hours. I **have been waiting** for the
bus since four o'clock. (Libya's Grade 8 book boxes it in
exactly this company.) Every piece is an old friend:
have/has from the present perfect, been — be's participle
— in the middle, and the -ing coat from the present
continuous on the verb. Egypt's Secondary 12 box states
the build in the same pieces: have/has + been + present
participle (-ing).

## Sub lesson 2 — The Stretch and Its Measures
**Its job — the action still moving.** Libya's box names
it: "describing action that **continues from the past**."
The tense does not point at a finished result; it points
at the stretch of action itself, from its start before
now up to now. That is why its favourite question is
Libya Grade 10's lesson title: "**What have they been
doing?**" — Huda? She's **been taking** photos. Adam?
He's **been playing** tennis. Not what is finished — what
has been going on.

**How long — for and since.** Because the tense is about
a stretch, it almost always carries the stretch's measure,
and the books drill the pair until it is a reflex
(Libya Grade 10's Work Book): **for** + the length —
for two hours, for ten minutes, for two days; **since**
+ the starting point — since four o'clock, since
Tuesday, since ten o'clock. "We've **been waiting** for a
bus **since** ten o'clock." "It's **been raining for**
two hours." "She's **been crying for** ten minutes."

## Sub lesson 3 — The Marks — Just Stopped
**Just stopped — the marks still show.** Egypt's box adds
the second seat: an action recently finished, with its
result still visible NOW. "I'm tired **because I have
been studying** all night." The studying has stopped —
the tiredness is its footprint. The tense holds actions
whose marks are still on the present: red eyes from
crying, wet streets from rain, paint on hands from
painting.

## Sub lesson 4 — Recent Habits and the Neighbours
**Recent habits — what you have been doing lately.**
Libya's Grade 9 box is named "Talking about recent
habits": "I've **been speaking** English every day."
"**Have you been going** to the beach?" A habit of the
last stretch of your life — not forever (that is the
present simple's seat), and not one finished act — the
run of it, lately.

**The two neighbours, kept apart.** Beside the present
perfect simple: the simple counts the DONE and its result
— I've **read** three books (three, finished, counted).
The continuous watches the MOVING — I've **been reading**
all morning (no count; the action is the picture).
Questions show the split: "How many have you read?" asks
the simple; "How long have you been reading?" asks the
continuous. Beside the present continuous: that one is
only NOW (I am waiting — this minute); the perfect
continuous reaches back to the start (I have been waiting
SINCE four o'clock — the waiting began before now and is
still going). Egypt's run makes the point by its history:
its books asked for this tense in tasks from Secondary 10
and only boxed it at Secondary 12 — it is the last of the
perfect family to be stated, and it completes the set.

**Asking and denying.** The partner steps forward as
always: **Have** you **been swimming** a lot? **Has** she
**been sleeping**? The not-form folds inside: He **hasn't
been working**. (Libya Grade 10's Work Book keys drill
exactly these: hasn't been sleeping, have been playing,
hasn't been working.)

## Do it yourself
1. **What have they been doing?** Watch your home or
   street for ten minutes. Report five people in this
   tense — She's been cooking since noon. They've been
   playing for an hour. No finished acts allowed.
2. **Your day as a stretch.** Say four true sentences
   about today — I have been awake since six. I have
   been learning for two hours — two with for, two with
   since, and swap partners with your companion to check.
3. **Read the marks.** Find three footprints of finished
   actions around you (wet ground, tired eyes, a full
   basket) and explain each one: It has been raining.
   She has been crying. We have been picking olives.
