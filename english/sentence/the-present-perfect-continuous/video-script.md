# Video script — The present perfect continuous

Super-simple voice. Read slow. The learner hears this
before or beside the written lesson.

---

It started raining at four o'clock.
Look — it is still raining.

It has been raining since four o'clock.
It has been raining for two hours.

That is today's shape.

Have. Has. Been. And the verb with -ing.

I have been waiting.
She has been cooking.
They have been playing.

Been never changes.
The verb always wears -ing.

This shape watches the action moving.
Not finished. Not counted.
Moving.

What have they been doing?
Huda has been taking photos.
Adam has been playing tennis.

How long?
For two hours. That is the length.
Since Tuesday. That is the start.
For — the length.
Since — the start.

Sometimes the action just stopped.
But you can see its marks.

I'm tired because I have been studying all night.
The streets are wet. It has been raining.
Her eyes are red. She has been crying.

And lately — your recent days:
I've been speaking English every day.
Have you been going to the beach?

Not done. Not counted.
Still going — or just stopped, with its marks showing.

Now you try.
What have you been doing today?
I've been learning since morning.
I've been waiting for an hour.

Say it true. Say it about your own day.
