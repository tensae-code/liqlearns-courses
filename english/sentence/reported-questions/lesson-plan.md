# Lesson plan — Reported questions

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Reported questions
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 22, "Telling What
People Said" — the second lesson of the level
(CURRICULUM.md), after Reported statements.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** reporting a
question — a yes/no question travels with if/whether, a
wh- question travels with its wh- word, and both arrive
in statement order with the tense stepped back and no
question mark — plus the polite indirect frames (I was
wondering…, Could I ask you…?) that ask without asking
directly.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Reported
statements (the same backshift bridge) and on Subject
and object questions (the wh- machinery). Reported
commands follow it inside this level.
**What the learner can do at the end:** report any
yes/no question with asked if/whether; report any wh-
question with its word in front and statement order
after it; drop do/did on the bridge; and wrap a question
in a polite frame when the direct shape would sound
blunt.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Reported statements (same level —
  the backshift and pronoun turns come straight back)
  and Asking with question words. Recommended, never
  forced.
- A reported question is not a question any more: nobody
  answers it, and it wears no question mark. That single
  fact unlocks the whole lesson.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a question losing its hooks:
   a big "?" and an upside-down word order on the left of
   a bridge, plain straight words on the right.** Written
   slot: "a picture of a question straightening out as it
   crosses the bridge." What the student does: the
   student walks "Did you see it?" across and watches did
   fall off the bridge — "He asked if I had seen it."
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of two doors into the same room:
   one labelled IF, one labelled WHETHER, both opening
   onto a yes/no sign.** Written slot: "a picture of if
   and whether as two doors to the same room." What the
   student does: the student reports one yes/no question
   through each door and hears the room is the same.
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of a wh- word (WHERE) standing at
   the front of a train, pulling straight statement cars
   behind it: you / live / here in a row.** Written slot:
   "a picture of the wh- word as the engine of a straight
   train." What the student does: the student builds the
   train — wh- word first, then the words in statement
   order, no swapping.
4. **Slot 4 — a picture of a question wrapped in a soft
   cushion before it is handed to a stranger: the cushion
   labelled I WAS WONDERING…** Written slot: "a picture
   of a question carried on a cushion." What the student
   does: the student wraps one blunt question — "Where
   is the station?" — in the cushion and hands it over.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first** (lesson/lesson.md): the interview
   carried home — a stranger's two questions retold to
   family, first in the learner's own language.
2. **The straightening**: a reported question is a
   statement about a question — statement word order, no
   question mark, the helper do/did dropped (Kuwait's
   p.45 rule set, stated whole).
3. **Yes/no questions**: asked + **if / whether** +
   statement, tense stepped back (Mauritania's Lesson 14
   with its tense-correspondence chart; Nepal Grade 8
   Unit 3).
4. **Wh- questions**: the wh- word keeps the front seat,
   the rest runs straight, backshift as in statements
   (Mauritania's Lesson 12 with its own chart,
   pp.38–39).
5. **ask / wonder / want to know**: the reporting verbs
   for questions (Vietnam's Grade 9 verbs; Sudan's
   interview retold).
6. **The polite frames**: I was wondering…, Could I ask
   you…?, Do you have any idea…? — Cambodia Grade 12's
   Unit 3 system, with Grade 11's teacher's-book Unit
   24B doing the same conversion work.
7. **China's cousin shape**: object clauses — the same
   straight order living inside polite requests as a
   clause (Could you tell me where…?), taught by China
   as object clauses, never named reported speech;
   worded as such.
8. **The limits, stated**: Vietnam's Grade 9 book
   teaches yes/no reported questions ONLY; Sudan's SPINE
   6 teaches indirect questions and NO reported
   statements. Each country's limit rides with its
   citation.
9. **Do it yourself** (the three closing tasks in the
   lesson): the interview report, the two doors, the
   cushioned question.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck on word order: a reported question is not a
  question — say it as news. "He asked where I live"
  runs in the order of "I live here", never "live I".
- Stuck on did: did was only the question's climbing
  rope; on the bridge the tense itself carries the past
  — asked if I had seen it.
- Stuck between if and whether: both doors open to the
  same room for a plain yes/no report; whether is the
  one to pick when an "or not" or a choice is near.
- This lesson's next door: Reported commands (same
  level) — the bridge again, for orders and requests.
