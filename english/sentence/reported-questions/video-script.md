# Video script — Reported questions

Super-simple voice. Read slow. The learner hears this
before or beside the written lesson.

---

He asks you: "Do you like tea?"
Later, you tell your friend.
"He asked if I liked tea."

Look what happened.
The question became a statement.
No question mark.
No did. No do.
The words stand straight.
And the tense stepped back. Like → liked.

A yes/no question travels with IF.
Or with WHETHER. Two doors, one room.
"Are you tired?" → She asked if I was tired.
"Can you swim?" → He asked whether I could swim.

A wh- question keeps its word.
"Where do you live?"
→ He asked where I lived.
"Where" stays in front.
After it: straight words. I lived.
Not: lived I. Never that.

"What did you buy?"
→ She asked what I had bought.
Did is gone. The past perfect carries the past.

The asking verbs: asked.
Also: wondered. Wanted to know.
She wanted to know if I was ready.

Now the soft way.
You ask a stranger. A direct question can sound hard.
So we wrap it.
I was wondering where the station is.
Could I ask you what time it opens?
Do you have any idea when the bus comes?

The cushion is soft. Inside, the words stand straight.
Where the station IS. Not: where is the station.

Now you try.
Ask me two questions.
Then tell the wall what I asked.
If for yes/no. The wh- word for the others.
Straight words. One step back. No question mark.
