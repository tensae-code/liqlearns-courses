# Lesson plan — Asking yes/no questions

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Asking yes/no questions
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3, Unit 2 "Questions" —
the first lesson of the unit (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** how to turn a statement
into a question that can be answered yes or no — the helping
word moves to the front — and how to answer it: short, or
whole.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Building a sentence
(a question is a statement with its front swapped) and the
Verbs/Pronouns lessons (do/does ride on who; be rides on its
forms). It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown changing — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Building a sentence; Verbs (the do/does
  engine); Pronouns (who rides in the question). Recommended,
  never forced.
- Questions with what/where/when/who are the NEXT lesson —
  a different shape (a question word leads, and the answer
  is a thing, not yes/no). Past-tense questions (Did you…?)
  belong to Unit 3 with the past itself.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Turn a be-statement into a question by the swap: "She is
   happy" → "Is she happy?" — and answer it: "Yes, she is." /
   "No, she isn't."
2. Turn a doing-statement into a question with do/does:
   "You like mango" → "Do you like mango?" "He plays" →
   "Does he play?" — and hear the -s move house (plays →
   Does he play, never "Does he plays").
3. Answer in whole sentences when the talk wants whole
   sentences (the course's standing answer rule).
4. Ask with the rising voice the books teach: for yes/no
   questions the voice usually goes UP; the answer's voice
   comes down (Bangladesh's intonation focus).

## What you need
Nothing but a partner's voice — or your companion. Paper for
the written shapes.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner makes three true statements
   about the room; the lesson swaps each one into a question
   live, and the learner answers their own questions
   (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the swap (be); the do/does engine;
   short answers; the rising voice (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the swap part: a picture of a sentence strip
     — SHE IS HAPPY — whose IS block slides to the front and
     a question mark stamps the end, 1–2 seconds — the
     student says the statement, then the question, as the
     block moves.
   - ADD IMAGE under do/does: a picture of two doors — DO
     (I, you, we, they behind it) and DOES (he, she, it
     behind it) → the student picks the door for five whos
     and says why.
   - ADD AUDIO in the voice part: a voice asking six yes/no
     questions with the rising voice, answers falling — the
     student draws the rise in the air with a finger for each
     question, then asks three back with the same rise.
3. **Say it to your companion** — the guessing game: think of
   an animal/food/place from Level 2; your companion gets ten
   yes/no questions to find it (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the
     swap shown on sentence strips, in the super-simple voice
     (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served
   at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — write five statements about your
   family, swap each into a question, and answer both ways
   (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Asks "You are happy?" and stops → that shape lives in
  speech (the rising voice carries it), and the books' written
  shape is the swap; teach the swap as the safe written form,
  and name the spoken shortcut honestly as a shortcut.
- "Does he plays?" → the -s moved house: does carries the -s
  now, so the verb stands bare. One -s per sentence, and
  does is holding it.
- Answers everything with bare "Yes." → short answers carry
  the who: "Yes, I do." "No, she isn't." The tail is what
  makes it English, not just an answer.
- This unit's next lesson: Asking with question words —
  what, where, when, who.
