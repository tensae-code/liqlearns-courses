**Acceptance count: taught in 9 countries (Bangladesh,
Bolivia, Mongolia, Laos, China, Indonesia, Iran,
Sierra Leone, Jordan).** Bolivia boxes the do/does engine;
Mongolia works the be-swap on its doing-now questions;
Bangladesh teaches the voice the question rides on; China
adds the full shelf of shapes across its primary run — be,
do, does, did and can questions, each with its short answers,
from family and fruit up to the past tense; Indonesia asks
Is there…? over the school and states the past Did/was-were
question lesson outright; Iran tables the yes-no forms and
gives both the be and the do/does question their rising
voice; Sierra Leone makes the question by the be-swap,
states the rising-voice rule, and forms and answers
simple-present questions as a system; Jordan
asks do, does, be, have-got and can yes/no questions
with their short answers across Grades 2–3. Other
closed countries join as their books are read (see
READING-QUEUE.md).

**Laos Grade 3 added 2026-10-27** — *English for Primary Schools,
Textbook Grade 3* (MoES/RIES, 2021 revised edition; full read in
english/books/laos.md). Laos grows each yes/no question out of the
statement lesson just before it, always with the short answers:
**"Is it a pig? — Yes, it is. / Is it a cow? — No, it isn't"**
(headed Grammar box, p.64, after the animals set), **"Do you
like…? — Yes, I do. / No, I don't"** (p.86, after I like / I don't
like, p.82), and **"Can you…? — Yes, I can. / No, I can't"**
(p.104, after I can / I can't, p.100). The toy-box guessing chant
runs the Is it a…? pattern as a game (p.105).
