# Hands-on — Asking yes/no questions

The lesson played aloud, the way its books drill it in pairs.

## The ten-question hunt
The lesson's game, for real: one player thinks of a thing
from the world sets (an animal, a food, a home thing, a town
place). The other asks only yes/no questions — swap or engine,
built right — and writes each answer's tail ("Yes, it does.")
as a clue list. Ten questions to name the thing. The clue
list at the end should read like a description.

## Swap strips
Write five statements on strips. Physically cut each strip
between the who and the be (or lay a DO/DOES card at the
front). Read the statement; slide the cut; read the question.
The hands learn the move before the mouth does.

## Voice arrows
Bangladesh's pairwork, done standing: ask your six questions
tracing a rising arrow in the air with your finger; your
partner answers tracing a falling one. If the finger forgets,
the voice did too — ask again.

## Interview minute
One minute, one partner, only yes/no questions — about their
day, their food, their town. Count how many whole questions
you can build and answer before the minute ends. Then swap.
The score is the count of RIGHT shapes, not raw speed.
