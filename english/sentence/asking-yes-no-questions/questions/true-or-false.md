# Asking yes/no questions — true or false

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — The Be-Swap · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — The Do/Does Engine · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Voice and Whole Answers.
Say true or false. If it is false, say why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** "Is she happy?" is the question form of
"She is happy."
**True** — the swap.

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** "Does he plays football?" is right.
**False** — "Does he play football?" The -s moved onto
does; the verb stands bare.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Do goes with he, she, and it.
**False** — does goes with he/she/it; do goes with I, you,
we, they.

**4. (Sub lesson 1)** A yes/no question can be answered with a
whole sentence.
**True** — short is quick; whole is sure. Both are right
shapes here.

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** The voice falls at the end of a yes/no
question.
**False** — it rises; the ANSWER's voice falls (Bangladesh,
Class 4 U22).

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** "You are happy?" with a rising voice can be
understood in speech, but the written standard shape is the
swap.
**True** — the lesson names the shortcut honestly and plants
the swap.
