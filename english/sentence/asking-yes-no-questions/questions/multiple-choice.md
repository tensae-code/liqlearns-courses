# Asking yes/no questions — multiple choice

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — The Be-Swap · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — The Do/Does Engine · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Voice and Whole Answers.
Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Turn it into a question: "She is happy."
a) Is she happy?  b) She happy is?  c) Happy she is?
**Answer: a) Is she happy?** — the be word walks to the
front.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** "Are they ready?" — the true short answer
(body language: they nod) is —
a) Yes, they are.  b) Yes, they is.  c) Yes, ready.
**Answer: a) Yes, they are.** — the answer rides the same be
word.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** "______ he play football?"
a) Do  b) Does  c) Is
**Answer: b) Does** — he/she/it take does (Bolivia's
DO–DOES box).

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** Which question is built right?
a) Does she likes rice?  b) Does she like rice?  c) Do she
like rice?
**Answer: b)** — does carries the -s; the verb stands bare.

**5. (Sub lesson 2)** "Do you walk to school?" — a whole-sentence
answer is —
a) Yes.  b) Yes, I do.  c) Yes, I walk to school every day.
**Answer: c)** — b) is the right short answer; c) is the
whole answer this course aims at.

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** For a yes/no question, the voice usually
goes ______; the answer's voice goes ______.
a) down — up  b) up — down  c) flat — flat
**Answer: b) up — down** (Bangladesh's intonation focus,
Class 4, p.45).
