# Lesson plan — Participles as Verb Forms

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** Participles as Verb Forms
**Lesson:** participles-as-verb-forms — the verb's two participle shapes — walking and walked, eating and eaten — that the continuous and perfect tenses are built from
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 19 — Earlier Than the Past
**The one thing:** every verb carries two participle forms, and the tense system is built out of them: be + present participle makes the continuous; have + past participle makes the perfect
**This lesson's own level:** Verb-form lesson: the -ing form first, then the third form, regular and irregular.

## Why this lesson exists

No lesson taught the participle forms themselves. Sierra Leone's SSS 2 handbook gives them two lessons of their own — "Present Participle" and "Past Participle" (Term 3, Lessons 129–130) — because everything in the tense system hangs on them. The 2026-11-08 re-verification sweep marked both NEW LESSON NEEDED. Under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06) they get their own lesson, placed with the perfect tenses they build; its sources are the handbook lessons named in lesson/sources.md.

## Sub lessons

1. **The present participle** — the verb + -ing form — walking, eating, going — and what it builds with be: the continuous tenses
2. **The past participle** — the verb's third form — walked, eaten, gone — regular -ed and the irregular sets, and what it builds with have: the perfect tenses, and the passive with be

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is forced first — this lesson stands alone: it starts from plain verbs the learner already knows. It stands under the-present-perfect, the-past-perfect and the continuous lessons, which use these forms on every line.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- Make the present participle of a verb, including the spelling changes (run → running, make → making).
- Make the past participle of regular verbs and of the common irregular verbs (eat → eaten, go → gone, see → seen).
- Say what each form builds: be + present participle = continuous; have + past participle = perfect.
- Name the participle in a sentence and say which form it is.

## What you need
A list of common verbs — the learner's own reading supplies more.

## The run of the lesson
1. **Do it first** — the learner reads "She has eaten" and "She is eating" and tries to say what shape "eaten" and "eating" are, before the name "participle" is taught (lesson/lesson.md).
2. Work through sub lesson 1: the present participle and its spellings.
3. Work through sub lesson 2: the past participle, regular and irregular.
4. ADD IMAGE: a verb card that splits three ways — walk / walking / walked — with "be" over the second and "have" over the third: the student builds the same split for eat, go, see.

## Add-ons
- Three-form races: a verb is called; partners race to say its three forms — base, -ing, past participle.
- Irregular hunt: in today's reading, find three past participles and name their base verbs.

## If the learner is stuck
If the two participles blur, fix the partners: -ing walks with be, the third form walks with have. Say it as a chant until the pairing is automatic. If irregular forms will not stick, learn them as whole triples (eat–ate–eaten), never as single words.

## Teacher note
Participle-clauses and participle-adjectives stay in their own lessons — this lesson is the forms themselves, as the handbook teaches them, ahead of its own third lesson on irregular past participles (stocked in verbs).
