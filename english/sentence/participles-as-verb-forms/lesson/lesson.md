# Participles as Verb Forms

## Do it first

Read these two sentences:

- "She **is eating**."
- "She **has eaten**."

Both grow out of the verb **eat**. "Eating" and "eaten" are
two shapes of that one verb — and the whole tense system is
built from shapes like them. This lesson teaches the two
shapes themselves.

## Sub lesson 1 — The present participle

The **present participle** is the verb's **-ing** form:

- walk → **walking** · eat → **eating** · go → **going**

Mind the spellings:

- Ends in silent **e**: drop it — mak**e** → **making**, writ**e** → **writing**.
- Short verb, one vowel, one consonant: double the last
  letter — **run → running**, **sit → sitting**, **swim → swimming**.

Its job: with **be**, it builds the continuous tenses —
"I **am walking**", "She **was eating**", "They **will be
going**". Wherever you see be + -ing, a present participle is
at work.

*Difficulty: first steps — one form, two spelling rules, one partner (be) to remember it by.*

## Sub lesson 2 — The past participle

The **past participle** is the verb's **third form**:

- Regular verbs: verb + **-ed** — walk → **walked**, watch → **watched**, play → **played**.
- Irregular verbs: the form must be learned with the verb —
  eat → **eaten**, go → **gone**, see → **seen**, write →
  **written**, take → **taken**, speak → **spoken**.

Learn irregular verbs as triples: **eat – ate – eaten**,
**go – went – gone**, **see – saw – seen**. The third word is
the past participle.

Its jobs: with **have**, it builds the perfect tenses — "I
**have walked**", "She **had eaten**" — and with **be**, the
passive — "It **was written** by hand".

So the whole system hangs on two shapes:

- **be + present participle** → the action going on.
- **have + past participle** → the action complete.

*Difficulty: building — the irregular triples are memory work; the two pairings (be + -ing, have + third form) are the map that organises them.*

## Say it to your companion

Explain the two participles to your companion: how each is
made, the spelling rules of the -ing form, three irregular
triples, and what each form builds. Your companion calls a
verb; you answer with both participles. Then change places.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write both participles for: play, run, make, swim, take,
   speak, write, watch.
2. Write three triples for irregular verbs you know, and one
   sentence for each third form with **have**.
3. In a page you are reading, find two present participles
   and two past participles. Name their verbs and their jobs.
