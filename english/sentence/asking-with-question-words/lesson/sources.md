**Acceptance count: taught in 23 countries (Bangladesh, Egypt,
Mongolia, Laos, China, Indonesia, Iran, Sierra Leone,
Jordan, Greece, Kazakhstan, Iraq, Ethiopia, South Africa, Syria, South Sudan, Nepal, Armenia, Vietnam, Cambodia, Pakistan, Sudan, Kuwait).** Mongolia tables the family with its answer
shapes; Bangladesh gives the words their voice; Egypt has
drilled the questions and whole answers since the youngest
books; China adds the words worked as whole units — where,
how many, whose, how much — and a page that sorts the family
by the answer each word takes; Indonesia tables the family
as reference and states the past Wh- lesson outright; Iran
gives the family a whole grammar lesson across be and
do/does, with its falling voice; Sierra Leone frames
occasion talk on the six words and names them as the
question mark's signals; Jordan
drills what/who/how-old in Grade 1, when and how much
in Grade 3, and How…? questions in Grade 5. Greece works how many / how much as a taught pair and what are you doing? at Grade 4, gathers the Wh- family in its Grade 4 final self-assessment, and sets wh-questions as a lesson's grammar in its Grade 7 Beginners workbook. Kazakhstan names question words as a module point at Grade 5, lists who / where / when / what at Grade 3, and teaches the family whole — preposition placement and subject–object questions included — at Grade 9. Iraq systematises the wh- words as a set in its Grade 4 Pupil's Book — who, what, when, where, how, why — and returns to present and past simple question formation in depth in its Grade 9 Student's Book. Ethiopia teaches the Wh-question words as a named lesson in Grade 1 — what, where, why, how, who, which, when — works who/what/how/why questions in its role-model unit, and returns to 'Wh…' questions in Grade 4 (printed p.63). South Africa stocks this lesson in full: its Grade 3 FAL Workbook 1 sets out the wh-question words in a table and has learners make their own questions, Grade 5 HL turns statements into questions with where, what and when and frames a news report with who, what, when, where, why, and Grade 6 FAL runs an alien interview on wh- questions. Syria works wh-questions in its Grade 5 senses unit, names question words in its Grade 5 jobs unit, and gives the family a whole unit at Grade 8 — question words as the Student's Book's grammar point and a full question-words table, who/whom included, in the Workbook. South Sudan joined 2026-11-03: its Primary 1 Teacher's Guide names the question words as a class with model questions, and its Primary 3 Pupil's Book works where-questions in the Payam unit. Nepal carries the question words in Grade 6's transformation and question-practice work, in Grade 8's question-formation grammar and Grade 9's wh-question grammar, and — as its own teaching — in the Grade 6 guide's whole wh-question unit; Armenia checks the question words as a set — How old / What colour / When / Where — in English 3, Part 1's Progress Check Units 1–3 (printed pp.94–99), and teaches wh-questions as a headed grammar lesson in English 6, Unit 2.2 (printed pp.35–39), worked again with statements in Unit 2.4 (printed pp.45–47). Kuwait works wh-questions with be in Grade 1, past-simple wh-questions and wh-questions with do/does in Grade 2, and past-simple questions again in Grade 4. Other closed countries join as their books are read
(see READING-QUEUE.md).
