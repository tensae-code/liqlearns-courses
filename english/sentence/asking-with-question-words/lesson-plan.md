# Lesson plan — Asking with question words

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Asking with question words
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 11, "Questions" —
the second lesson of the level (CURRICULUM.md). Building it
completes Level 11.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** questions that hunt a
THING, not a yes or no — a question word leads (what, where,
when, who, why, how, how many), the yes/no machinery runs
behind it, and the answer is the thing hunted, given whole.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands directly on Asking
yes/no questions (the swap and the engine are reused whole)
and on the world lessons (the answers live there). It needs
nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown changing — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Asking yes/no questions. Recommended,
  never forced — but this lesson truly spends that machinery.
- "Who" as the WHO of the sentence ("Who eats meat?") asks
  without the engine — planted here as the one honest
  exception, explained in the lesson, not hidden.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Match each question word to its prey: what → a thing,
   where → a place, when → a time, who → a person, why → a
   reason, how → a way, how many → a count.
2. Build the questions whole: question word + the swap or
   the engine + the rest — "Where do you go when you're
   sick?" "What does the tiger eat?" "When does Mita get up?"
3. Answer in whole sentences (this level's third strand, and
   the course's standing rule): the answer hands over the
   thing hunted, inside a full statement.
4. Ask with the falling voice Bangladesh teaches for these
   questions — the voice goes DOWN for What/When/Where/Why/
   Who/How questions, as it does for statements.

## What you need
A partner or your companion; the world sets from Level 2 as
the answer field. Paper and a pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner answers a yes/no question
   with yes; then feels how little it gives; the same ground
   re-asked with a question word hands over the thing itself
   (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the words and their prey; the build
   (word + machinery); the who-exception; whole answers; the
   falling voice (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the build part: a picture of a question
     assembling in blocks — WHERE + DO + YOU + GO — the
     question word block landing first and standing tallest,
     1–2 seconds — the student says what each new question
     hunts as it assembles.
   - ADD IMAGE under the prey part: a picture of seven
     doors, one per question word, each opening on its prey
     (a thing, a place, a clock, a face, a because, a way, a
     count) → the student names the word for five doors from
     the prey alone.
   - ADD AUDIO in the voice part: a voice asking Wh-
     questions with the falling voice and answering whole —
     the student traces the fall in the air, then asks three
     back with the same fall.
3. **Say it to your companion** — interview: five questions,
   each with a different word, about your companion's real
   day; answers whole (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the
     interview in the super-simple voice (made later, when
     videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served
   at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — write one question per word about a
   friend, and the whole answers you expect (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Asks "What you do?" (no engine) → last lesson's engine
  still runs behind the word: word + DO/DOES + who + verb.
  Build it in blocks until the engine is automatic.
- "What does the tiger eats?" → the -s is on does again;
  the verb stands bare. Same house rule as yes/no.
- Answers "School." to "Where do you go?" → the whole-answer
  rule: hand over the prey inside a sentence — "I go to
  school." Short answers belong to yes/no; thing-answers
  stand whole.
- Mixes when and where → prey check: if the answer could be
  a place, the word is where; if it could be a time, when.
  The answer chooses the word.
- This lesson completes Level 11. Next: Level 12 — Now, Before,
  Later (the times of verbs).
