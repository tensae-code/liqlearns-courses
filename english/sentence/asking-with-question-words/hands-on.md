# Hands-on — Asking with question words

The lesson as real interviews, the way the books pair learners.

## The five-word interview
Interview a real person — family, friend, teacher — with one
question per word: what (they eat), where (they work or
study), when (they get up), who (their friend), how (they
come). Write each answer whole, in their words made into
sentences. Read the interview back to them; they check the
answers are true.

## Prey cards
Make seven cards: THING, PLACE, TIME, PERSON, REASON, WAY,
COUNT. A partner asks any question; you hold up the prey
card before answering — then answer whole. Twenty questions.
The card must rise before the mouth opens: word chosen by
prey, always.

## The detective round
One player thinks of a Level 2 thing (a food, an animal, a
place). This time NO yes/no questions are allowed — only
thing-questions, which give more per ask: "Where does it
live?" "What does it eat?" Find it in five questions.
Compare with the yes/no game's ten: thing-questions hunt
faster because each answer carries prey.

## Falling-voice drill
Stand back to back with a partner (voices only, the books'
listening way). Ask six Wh- questions; your partner traces
the fall in the air where you cannot see — no, hear it: if
your voice rises by habit, they call "rise!" and you ask
again, falling.
