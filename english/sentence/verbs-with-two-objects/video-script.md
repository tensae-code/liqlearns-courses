# Video script — Verbs with two objects

Super-simple voice. Read slow. The learner hears this
before or beside the written lesson.

---

Some verbs carry two things.
A person. And a thing.

I sent Mary some flowers.
Mary — the person. She gets them.
Flowers — the thing. They travel.
One verb: sent. Two loads.

That is shape one.
Person first. Thing second.
I gave my brother a book.
She showed me the photo.
He told us a story.

Now the seesaw. The loads can swap.
I sent some flowers to Mary.
The thing first now. Then TO. Then the person.

Give it to her. Send it to me. Show it to them.
The meaning does not move. Only the seats.

Some verbs take FOR, not to.
I bought a book for Mary.
She cooked dinner for us.
He made a cake for me.
Buy, cook, make, get. The work is done FOR the person.
No travelling parcel. A favour, done.

Careful with small words now — pronouns.
It and her.
Give it to her. Good. Safe.
Give her it. Wobbly. Everyday English avoids it.
With pronouns, take the to-shape.

But full names are free both ways.
Give Mary the book. Give the book to Mary.
Both stand.

Now you try.
One giving. Two shapes.
Tell me a story — no. Tell a story to me.
Send her a message. Send a message to her.

Two loads. Two seats. One verb carries both.
