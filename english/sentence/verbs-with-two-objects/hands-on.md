# Hands-on — Verbs with two objects

You do it, we guide you. Everything here is done with
real objects in the room and a companion or the Explain
Companion.

1. **The giving round.** Put five small objects on the
   table. The learner must hand each one over with a
   full sentence BEFORE the hand moves: "I'm giving
   you the red pen." The companion names the two objects
   back — person, thing — after each delivery. Five
   deliveries, no silent hands.
2. **The seesaw swaps.** The companion reads shape-A
   sentences ("She sent her teacher a card." "He bought
   his mother a scarf."). The learner rebuilds each in
   shape B — to for the card, for the scarf — then the
   companion reads shape-B sentences and the learner
   swaps back. Eight swaps; a lost object or a wrong
   signpost word restarts the sentence.
   (Mongolia English IX's own pair, played as a game.)
3. **The pronoun post.** The objects shrink to it/them
   and the people to me/her/us. The learner "posts"
   each item with a pronoun sentence — "Give it to her."
   "Send them to me." — and must use the to-shape every
   time; a "give her it" is returned to sender.
4. **The favour kitchen.** Mime three favours (cooking,
   making a card, finding a lost key) for the companion.
   Each is reported with for: "I cooked eggs for you."
   Then three handings-over reported with to. The
   companion polices the signposts.
