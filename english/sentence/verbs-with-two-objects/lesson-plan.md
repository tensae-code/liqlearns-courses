# Lesson plan — Verbs with two objects

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Verbs with two objects
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 24, "Two Shapes After
a Verb" — the third lesson of the level
(CATCH-UP-PLAN.md), after The causative.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the two-object
verb — give, send, show and their family carry a PERSON
and a THING in one sentence, in two shapes: verb +
person + thing (I sent Mary some flowers) and verb +
thing + to/for + person (I sent some flowers to Mary) —
with to for the handing-over verbs and for for the
doing-for verbs, and the pronoun rule that turns the
second shape into the safe one.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Building a
sentence (the object is already known; here it splits
into two) and closes Level 24. Its evidence is two
countries deep, stated plainly: Cambodia's Grade 11
course — teacher's-book spreads, the student's book
not held — and Mongolia's English IX.
**What the learner can do at the end:** name the two
objects in any give/send/show sentence and say which
is the person and which the thing; rebuild any
two-object sentence in the other shape; and choose to
or for by the verb's family without stopping to think.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Building a sentence. Recommended,
  never forced — the lesson opens by splitting one
  object into two, live.
- A sourcing note carried openly: Cambodia's half of
  this lesson is known from the Grade 11 Teacher's
  Book, which presents the unit in spreads; the Grade
  11 student's book is not held, and no part of the
  lesson claims it is.
- The speaker button reads the explanations in the
  learner's own language; the target forms (the two
  shapes themselves) stay in English, never translated.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a delivery: a boy on a
   bicycle handing a parcel to a girl, with two arrows
   leaving the verb GIVE — one to the girl (PERSON),
   one to the parcel (THING).** Written slot: "a picture
   of one verb carrying two things." What the student
   does: the student names both arrows' loads before
   reading on — person first, thing second.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of the same delivery drawn two
   ways side by side: "I sent Mary some flowers" and
   "I sent some flowers to Mary," with the two loads
   swapping seats on a seesaw.** Written slot: "a
   picture of the two shapes on a seesaw." What the
   student does: the student reads both aloud and says
   what moved and what small word arrived (to).
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of two signposts: TO (a hand
   passing a parcel across) and FOR (a pair of hands
   cooking a meal for someone seated).** Written slot:
   "a picture of the to signpost and the for signpost."
   What the student does: the student sends three verbs
   down each road aloud — give, send, show to TO; buy,
   cook, make to FOR.
4. **Slot 4 — a picture of a tiny parcel labelled IT
   bouncing off the sentence "Give her it" and landing
   safely in "Give it to her."** Written slot: "a
   picture of it changing seats." What the student
   does: the student says both pronoun sentences and
   hears why the thing-pronoun travels in the to-shape.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first** (lesson/lesson.md): one act of
   giving in the learner's own room, said first in the
   learner's own language — who got it, what it was.
2. **The two objects named**: direct (the thing) and
   indirect (the person) — Cambodia Grade 11, Unit 15A
   "verbs with two objects; direct vs indirect objects"
   (teacher's-book spreads, pp.81–86).
3. **Shape A — person + thing**: Mongolia English IX,
   Unit 7's own pair in its Grammar Reference: "I sent
   Mary some flowers."
4. **Shape B — thing + to + person**: the same pair's
   second half: "I sent some flowers to Mary." The swap
   is free for the handing-over verbs: give, send,
   show, lend, pass, offer, tell (a story).
5. **The for family**: buy, cook, make, get, find,
   build — these swap with for, not to: "I bought Mary
   a book / I bought a book for Mary."
6. **Pronouns change the safe shape**: give it to her
   (not give her it, in careful everyday English);
   give her the book stays fine with full nouns.
7. **Do it yourself** (the three closing tasks in the
   lesson): the giving round, the seesaw swaps, the
   pronoun post.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck on which object is which: ask the sentence two
  questions — WHAT was given? (the thing, direct) and
  WHO received it? (the person, indirect). Mongolia's
  pair answers both about the same flowers.
- Stuck on to or for: handing ACROSS takes to; doing
  FOR someone's benefit takes for. If the thing travels
  from hand to hand, to; if the work is done so the
  person has it, for.
- This level's next door: Level 25 opens the wishing
  machinery — wish and if only — where these same verbs
  start carrying dreams instead of parcels.
