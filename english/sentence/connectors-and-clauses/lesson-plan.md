# Lesson plan — Connectors and clauses

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Connectors and clauses
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 31, "Linking and
Fixing Sentences" — the first lesson of the level
(CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the wider joining
system beyond and/but — adverbial clauses and connectors
of time, reason, purpose, contrast and concession, with
their positions and punctuation: while / although /
however / on the other hand; because / because of; so
that / in order to; in spite of / despite; therefore /
consequently / as a result.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Joining with
and and but (the first two directions) and on Building a
sentence. Subject-verb agreement, Fixing sentence errors
and Cleft sentences follow it inside this level.
**What the learner can do at the end:** choose a
connector by the job it does; build a clause with
although, while, when, before, after and until; keep
because (clause) apart from because of (noun); keep
although (clause) apart from in spite of (noun); and
punctuate however, therefore and on the other hand as
sentence connectors, not as glue inside a clause.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Joining with and and but (and adds,
  but turns — this lesson is the wider system only, those
  two are not re-taught) and Building a sentence.
  Recommended, never forced.
- Reported speech, relative clauses and conditionals use
  clauses too, but each is its own lesson. Here the
  clause is a time, reason, purpose or contrast carrier
  only.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of five roads leaving one town,
   each with a sign: WHEN, BECAUSE, SO THAT, ALTHOUGH,
   THEREFORE.** Written slot: "a picture of five roads,
   five signs." What the student does: the student points
   at the sign that matches a pair of sentences they want
   to join — the job is chosen before the word.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of a clause card sliding to the
   front of a sentence, a comma appearing behind it.**
   Written slot: "a picture of a clause moving to the
   front, with a comma." What the student does: the
   student moves one although-clause to the front aloud
   and puts the comma in with their finger.
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of two doors: BECAUSE with a
   whole sentence walking through it, BECAUSE OF with
   only a noun walking through it.** Written slot: "a
   picture of because and because of as two doors." What
   the student does: the student sends "the rain" through
   one door and "it rained" through the other.
4. **Slot 4 — a picture of HOWEVER standing at the start
   of a new sentence in a tall hat, while BUT stays
   inside its sentence.** Written slot: "a picture of
   however starting a new sentence." What the student
   does: the student rewrites one but-sentence with
   however and places the full stop and the comma.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first** (lesson/lesson.md): five pairs of true
   sentences about the learner's day, each pair asking
   for a different join — time, reason, purpose,
   contrast, result.
2. **The five jobs**: time, reason, purpose, contrast /
   concession, result — Nepal's Grade 8 set (because /
   because of, so that, in order to, although, however,
   therefore, in spite of, Unit 15) and Vietnam's Grade
   11 table by function (Unit 9).
3. **Time clauses**: before, after, until, when, while —
   Bolivia's form lesson: a subordinate clause showing
   the time, which can join sentences or go to the
   beginning to emphasise the time (Year 5, Topic 4).
4. **Reason and purpose**: because + clause against
   because of + noun (Nepal Grade 10, Unit 8); so that
   and in order to for purpose; Mauritania's cause-effect
   set (because, since, as, that's why, thus, hence,
   therefore, consequently — Lessons 5–6).
5. **Contrast and concession**: while, although, however,
   on the other hand with their position and punctuation
   rules (Kuwait, Pearls 9B, Unit 6, Lesson 2, p.33);
   although + clause against in spite of / despite +
   noun (Nepal Grade 10, Unit 9); Libya's contrast-linker
   hunt (however / whereas / while).
6. **Sentence connectors**: however, therefore,
   consequently, on the other hand, as a result — a new
   sentence (or after a semicolon), a comma after the
   connector; never a comma alone between two sentences
   (that error is Fixing sentence errors' territory,
   named here only as a warning).
7. **Do it yourself** (the three closing tasks in the
   lesson): the five joins, the front-door move, the
   rewrite with however.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck choosing: name the job first in your own
  language — "why? when? what for? but still? so?" — the
  answer picks the connector family.
- Stuck on because of / in spite of: look at what
  follows. A whole clause (somebody does something)
  takes because / although; a noun alone takes because
  of / in spite of.
- Stuck on however: it does not join inside a sentence
  the way but does. Give it its own sentence, a full stop
  before it and a comma after it.
- This level's next door: Subject-verb agreement — the
  joined sentence still needs its verb to match its
  subject.
