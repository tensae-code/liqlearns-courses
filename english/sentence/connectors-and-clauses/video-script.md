# Video script — Connectors and clauses

Super-simple voice. Read slow. The learner hears this
before or beside the written lesson.

---

Two sentences stand alone.
"I was tired." "I went to bed early."
Now join them. But how?
First ask: what is the job?

Is it time? Use WHEN.
"When I got home, I ate."
Is it a reason? Use BECAUSE.
"I went to bed because I was tired."
Is it a purpose? Use SO THAT.
"I saved money so that I could buy a book."
Is it a contrast? Use ALTHOUGH.
"Although it rained, we played outside."
Is it a result? Use THEREFORE.
"It rained. Therefore, we stayed at home."

Watch the doors.
BECAUSE takes a whole sentence after it.
Because it rained.
BECAUSE OF takes only a noun.
Because of the rain.
ALTHOUGH takes a whole sentence.
Although it rained…
IN SPITE OF takes only a noun.
In spite of the rain…

And HOWEVER is not BUT.
But stays inside one sentence.
However starts a new one.
"It was late. However, we kept working."
Full stop before. Comma after.

A clause can walk to the front.
"After I finished my work, I went to the park."
When it walks to the front, a comma walks with it.

Now you try.
Take two true sentences.
Name the job. Pick the word. Join them.
