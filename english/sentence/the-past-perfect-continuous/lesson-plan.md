# Lesson plan — The past perfect continuous

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** The past perfect continuous
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 19, "Earlier Than the Past" (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the past perfect continuous — **had been + verb-ing** — an action that was still moving, for a stretch, up to a point in the past: how long something had been going on before another past moment.
**What the learner can do at the end:** build had been + verb-ing for every who; measure the stretch with for and since up to a past point; and explain a past mark (tired eyes, wet ground) with the action that had been going on.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The past perfect (had, one step back) and The present perfect continuous (been + -ing, the moving stretch). Recommended, never forced — this lesson stands alone for a learner who lands on it directly (DIRECT-ENTRY rule).
- Speaker convention: the explanation is read in the learner's main language; the TARGET forms are never translated and are read in the English voice.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a runner sitting down, out of breath, with a clock behind her showing a full hour of running before she stopped.** What the student does: the student says the cause sentence first — she was exhausted BECAUSE she had been running — before reading the shape.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of a queue outside a door at nine o'clock, the same queue still there at ten, drawn as one long stretch.** What the student does: the student measures the wait twice — since nine (the start) and for one hour (the length) — both aimed at the past moment when the door opened.
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of wet earth and full buckets under a tree in the morning, the rain clouds already gone.** What the student does: the student names the action that had been going on in the night, from its marks alone.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first**: a past moment with something already going on before it, in the learner's own language.
2. **The shape**: had been + verb-ing; had and been never change (Egypt Sec 12 T2 box).
3. **The job**: duration up to a past point, NOT a single completed action — Egypt's own border, with its exhausted-runner model.
4. **For and since aimed at the past**: for two hours, all morning, since 9 a.m.
5. **The wider witnesses**: Malaysia F3 U7 / F5 U4; Pakistan English X U11 rule box; Sudan SPINE 5 Ch.7; Venezuela G11 guide L14, cited as teacher's-guide evidence.
6. **Asking and denying**: Had you been waiting long? He hadn't been sleeping.
7. **The border**: finished step → past perfect; moving stretch → this tense.
8. **Do it yourself**: the three closing tasks.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck on been: it never changes in any perfect continuous — had/has carries the time, been is always been.
- Stuck choosing the twin: ask what the sentence points at — a finished count (simple) or a length of moving action (continuous).
- Stuck on the point it aims at: this tense always needs its past moment — find the later past action first, then stretch back from it.
- This lesson's next door: The past continuous (same level) — the past scene itself, moving.
