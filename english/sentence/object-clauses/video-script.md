# Video script — Object clauses

Super-simple voice. Read slow. The learner hears this
before or beside the written lesson.

---

Some sentences carry another sentence inside.
Like a box carries a letter.

"I think that the shop is open."
Open the box. Inside is a whole sentence:
"the shop is open."
It has its own subject. Its own verb.
It is doing a job: it is the object of THINK.
What do I think? That the shop is open.

The little word THAT holds the box.
And often you can drop it.
"I think the shop is open."
Same meaning. The books say THAT often goes away.

After SURE the box stays strong.
"I'm sure that he will come."
"There's no doubt that she is right."
Sure. Certain. No doubt. All carry a THAT-clause.

A question can ride in a box too.
"Is he coming?" Put it inside:
"Tell me if he is coming."
"Tell me whether he is coming."
Look: no flip. No DO. Statement order.
And WH- questions ride the same way:
"Where does he live?" becomes
"Tell me where he lives."

One warning. This box is not reported speech.
"I know that she is tired" is a thought, now.
"She said that she was tired" is words, replayed.
Two doors. Learn which is which.

Now you try.
Think something. Know something. Be sure of something.
Say each one with its box.
