# Will for the Future

## Do it first

What will happen tomorrow? Will it rain? Will you be happy? Will a friend call? Make three guesses now — out loud. English's word for all of them is **will**, and it never changes its shape. This lesson teaches will's two big jobs and its questions.

## Sub lesson 1 — Will for predictions

A **prediction** says what we think will happen: “It **will rain** tomorrow.” “She **will win** the race.” “They **will come** at six.”

Will never changes: I will, you will, he will, we will, they will. And after will, the verb stays plain — will rain, will win, will come.

Bolivia's Grade 10 book teaches this with predictions for Pachamama, Mother Earth: what will happen to our planet if we care for her — and if we do not.

Predict three things about next week. Start each with “I think… will…” if you are not sure — predictions are guesses with grammar.

## Sub lesson 2 — Will for decisions made now

Will's second job: a **decision you make at the moment of speaking**. The phone rings — “**I'll get it!**” You see a friend carrying heavy bags — “**I'll help you.**” (“I'll” is will made short.)

The negative is **won't** (will not): “I won't forget.” “He won't be late.” Won't can also refuse: “I won't do that.”

Notice the difference: a plan you made yesterday is not this. Will-for-decisions is born in the same second as the words.

## Sub lesson 3 — Will questions and future time expressions

Questions put will first: “**Will you** travel next year?” Short answers: “Yes, I will.” “No, I won't.”

Future time words travel with will: **tomorrow, tonight, soon, next week, next month, next year, in the future**. “We will visit Uyuni next year.” “It will be cold tonight.”

Full drill: “Will it rain tomorrow?” “No, it won't. It will be sunny.” Weather, plans, promises — will handles them all.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: one prediction about tomorrow, one about next year, and one instant decision (“I'll…”) about helping them. They answer one of your predictions with “Yes, it will” or “No, it won't.”

## Do It Yourself

Write six will sentences: two predictions about your town, two about yourself, one promise with “I'll…”, and one “Will you…?” question to ask a friend tomorrow — then ask it.
