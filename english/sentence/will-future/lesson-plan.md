# Lesson plan — Will for the Future

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** Will for the Future
**Lesson:** will-future — the will future: predictions, decisions made now, questions, and future time words
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 20 — The Future in Detail
**The one thing:** Will + the plain verb for the future: predictions about what will happen, and decisions made at the moment of speaking.
**This lesson's own level:** Grammar lesson (future): will never changes; won't for the negative; Will you…? questions with short answers; future time expressions (tomorrow, next week, soon…).

## Why this lesson exists

Bolivia teaches will in two full treatments and the course's future lessons went to going to and the future continuous first: the Grade 10 book's WILL unit (predictions, moment-of-speaking decisions, a Pachamama-predictions reading) and the compendium's Year 4 WILL spread — the paradigm the old filing admittedly left unread (see sources). This lesson is built from those placements under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06).

## Sub lessons

1. Will for predictions
2. Will for decisions made now
3. Will questions and future time expressions

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is needed before this lesson. Going To for Later is its sister (plans already made); this lesson stands alone and teaches will's own jobs.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- Predict with will: “It will rain. She will win.”
- Decide now with will: “I'll help you. I'll take the bus.”
- Ask “Will you…?”, answer with won't, and use future time words (tomorrow, next week, soon).

## What you need
Nothing special — your own future is the material.

## The run of the lesson
1. ADD IMAGE: a fortune-teller's ball over a village — inside it, rain tomorrow, a girl winning a race. The learner guesses the sentences before will is named.
2. ADD GIF (1–2 seconds): a phone rings; a hand reaches for it — “I'll get it!” The decision and the words happen in the same second: that is will's second job.
3. Predictions round: the learner predicts tomorrow's weather, next week's game, and their own next birthday with will.
4. Sub lesson 3 drills questions and time words: “Will you travel next year?” “No, I won't. I will stay here.”

## Add-ons
- Prediction chain: each learner adds one “will” prediction about the class's next year; the class votes most likely.
- Decision drama: the teacher drops things, phones ring, doors knock — the learner must answer each with an instant “I'll…!”

## If the learner is stuck
If will and going to get mixed, keep the two jobs separate for now: will predicts and decides on the spot; plans you already made belong to the sister lesson.
