# Lesson plan — Plans in the past

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Plans in the past
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 20 — The Future in Detail (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** plans in the past — the future seen from inside a past story: **was/were going to** for a past plan, **was/were about to** for the moment just before, **was/were supposed to** for what was expected, and **was/were + to-infinitive** for a formal arranged fate — forms that let a story say what was coming, whether it happened or not.
**What the learner can do at the end:** tell a past plan with was going to (and show whether it happened); place an action on the edge with was about to; report an unkept expectation with was supposed to; and read the formal was + to-infinitive in stories.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees

1. **Plans in the Past Plan and Edge** — build was/were going to and was/were about to for every who, with the base verb standing plain after to, and catch an about-to moment with a when-clause. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Plans in the Past Expectation** — report arrangements with was/were supposed to — kept and broken. (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **Plans in the Past Formal Coat and Story** — read and write was/were + to-infinitive as the formal arranged future, and run a full past-plan story mixing the four coats; the asking-and-denying block, which runs across all the lesson's forms, sits in this sub lesson's text by its position. (Was depth Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Going to: later (the present-day plan — this lesson moves that same frame one step back). Recommended, never forced — this lesson stands alone for a learner who lands on it directly (DIRECT-ENTRY rule).
- Speaker convention: the explanation is read in the learner's main language; the TARGET forms are never translated and are read in the English voice.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of an old photograph of a family beside a packed car, with a dark storm cloud drawn over the road ahead.** What the student does: the student tells the plan the photograph holds — "They were going to travel…" — and guesses the but that the cloud promises.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of a boy standing at an open door with his bag on, one foot raised, as a telephone rings behind him.** What the student does: the student places him on the edge in one sentence: "He was about to leave when…"
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of a notice board with a meeting time pinned at six o'clock and an empty, dark room beneath it.** What the student does: the student reports the expectation and its failure: "They were supposed to meet at six, but…"

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first**: a true past plan in the learner's own language, with its ending.
2. **The frame**: the future in the past — Libya G12's own lesson name, taught at U3 L5 (p.34) and U4 L5 (p.46).
3. **Was/were going to**: the past plan and its friend but… (Libya's Mr Dodd and burglar models, p.46).
4. **Was/were about to**: the edge of the moment, caught by when (China G9 U12, p.92 frame).
5. **Was/were supposed to**: the expectation, kept or broken (China G9 U10, Grammar Focus p.76, in its past coat).
6. **Was/were + to-infinitive**: the formal arranged future, met in reading.
7. **Asking and denying**: Was he going to come? I wasn't supposed to tell.
8. **Do it yourself**: the three closing tasks.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck on which coat: ask what the story means — a plan (going to), a second before (about to), an expectation or rule (supposed to), or a formal arrangement in print (bare to-infinitive).
- Stuck on was/were: it is be's past, carried whole from the past continuous — was for I/he/she/it, were for you/we/they.
- Stuck assuming the plan failed: was going to does not itself say failure — the story must. Look for the but; without it, ask the teller.
