# Lesson plan — Have and Has (things you own)

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** Have and Has (things you own)
**Lesson:** have-and-has — saying what you and other people own:
**have** with I / you / we / they, **has** with he / she / it, the
negatives don't have / doesn't have, and the questions Do you
have…? / Does he have…?
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 10 — Building a Sentence (with the
other sentence frames; its nearest neighbours are
building-a-sentence, which carries the be paradigm and the simple
present, and mine-and-yours, which carries the possessives)
**The one thing:** the have/has system for saying what someone
owns — positive, negative and question forms, with the third-person
change (have → has, and doesn't have, never "doesn't has").
**This lesson's own level:** Core sentence-frame lesson: the learner
can say what they own, what another person owns, what someone does
not own, and ask about owning.

## Why this lesson exists

No lesson in the course taught have and has as its own thing.
building-a-sentence carries the be paradigm and the simple present;
mine-and-yours carries the possessive words; the "I have a…"
pattern appears inside word lessons as a chunk. But the full
have/has system — has for he/she/it, doesn't have for the negative,
Does he have…? for questions — had no home. Nicaragua's books
teach it as a named part twice over: Activity Book 3 works it as
the Grade 3 pattern (have/has at printed pp.22, 24; the questions
Do/Does…have? at printed pp.28–30), and the Grade 7 Student's Book
carries have/has through its Unit 2 (printed pp.42–48). The
2026-11-08 re-verification sweep re-read Nicaragua's books, rebuilt
the placements from the books themselves (389 teaching items; 5
already in the course, 382 missing) and marked this part NEW
LESSON NEEDED. Under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06),
a teaching item with no lesson gets a new lesson built; this is
that lesson. Its sources are the Nicaragua books named in
lesson/sources.md.

## Sub lessons

1. I / you / we / they have
2. He / she / it has
3. Don't have / doesn't have
4. Do you have…? / Does he have…?

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is forced first. A learner who has met building-a-sentence
will find the third-person change familiar — it is the same -s the
simple present takes — but this lesson stands alone: it teaches
the have/has forms from the start.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- Say what they own and what groups own: "I have a book." "They
  have two dogs."
- Say what one other person owns: "She has a red bag." "He has a
  bike."
- Say what someone does not own: "I don't have a car." "He doesn't
  have a phone."
- Ask and answer: "Do you have a pen? — Yes, I do." "Does she have
  a sister? — No, she doesn't."

## What you need
Real things to point at and hold: a book, a pen, a bag, a phone or
a picture of one. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson
1. **Do it first** — hold up three things of your own and try
   "I have…" for each before anyone tells you (lesson/lesson.md).
2. ADD IMAGE: two children with their school things — one with a
   book and a bag, one with a bike — each thing big and clear. The
   learner tries "She has…" before the teaching.
3. Work through sub lesson 1: have with I / you / we / they.
4. Work through sub lesson 2: has with he / she / it — the -s
   change.
5. Work through sub lesson 3: don't have / doesn't have — and the
   trap: doesn't takes plain have.
6. Work through sub lesson 4: Do you have…? / Does he have…? with
   the short answers.
7. ADD VIDEO (short): a market stall — the seller shows what he
   has, names what he doesn't have, and answers a buyer's
   questions.

## Add-ons
- Show and tell: each learner brings three things and says one
  "I have…" sentence for each; a partner then reports "He has…".
- The guessing game: hide a thing in a bag. "Do you have a pen in
  the bag?" — "No, I don't. I have a book."
- Chain: "I have a dog." — "She has a dog and a cat." — "He has a
  dog, a cat and a bike." Each learner adds one thing.

## If the learner is stuck
Cut back to two frames only: "I have a book." and "She has a
book." Hold the thing, say the sentence, swap the person, say it
again. Add the negative and the question only when those two are
sure.

## Teacher note
Nicaragua's Activity Book 3 teaches have/has inside its school
supplies unit — the pattern grows out of naming what is in your
bag (printed pp.22, 24) — and returns to it for the questions
Do/Does…have? in the toys unit (printed pp.28–30). The Grade 7
Student's Book carries have/has again through its Unit 2
(printed pp.42–48). This lesson keeps that order: positives
first, the third-person change, then negatives, then questions.
The Grade 1 book's "I have a (colour) (clothes item)" pattern
(printed pp.34–37) is the learner's first meeting with have as a
chunk; it is stocked in clothes, and cross-cited here.
