# Quantifiers — short answer

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Quantifiers — Some, Any, Much and Many · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Quantifiers — Small Amounts, Plenty, Enough, Less and Fewer · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Quantifiers — Partitives and Containers.

Answer in whole sentences.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Ask a friend whether there is milk in the house.
**Answer: Is there any milk? / Do we have any milk?** — a question about an uncountable takes any.

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** The soup has far too much salt in it. Say so.
**Answer: There is too much salt in the soup.** — too much + uncountable for the spill.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Say that four chairs are not sufficient for six people, using enough.
**Answer: Four chairs are not enough. / We don't have enough chairs.** — enough before a noun, or after be with not.

**4. (Sub lesson 3)** Write a shopping line for water, milk and pasta, each by its partitive.
**Answer: A bottle of water, a carton of milk, and a packet of pasta, please.** — Mongolia's Quantity Nouns set (VI U8, p.88).
