# Quantifiers — multiple choice

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Quantifiers — Some, Any, Much and Many · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Quantifiers — Small Amounts, Plenty, Enough, Less and Fewer · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Quantifiers — Partitives and Containers.

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** "Do you have ______ brothers or sisters?"
a) some  b) any  c) much
**Answer: b) any** — questions take any (Cambodia G9 U7's some/any set).

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** "How ______ water do we need?"
a) many  b) much  c) few
**Answer: b) much** — water is uncountable: how much, not how many (Mexico G7 P13).

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Only three friends came, and you are content — it was a nice small group. "______ friends came."
a) Few  b) A few  c) Little
**Answer: b) A few** — a few = some, with the smile; bare few complains (Nepal G10 U13).

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** "There were ______ people in the room — no one could move."
a) too much  b) too many  c) plenty
**Answer: b) too many** — people are countable, and the amount is a spill: too many (Cambodia G9 U7).

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** "Can I have ______, please?" (asking for bread at a shop)
a) a loaf of bread  b) a bread  c) few bread
**Answer: a) a loaf of bread** — bread is uncountable; the partitive gives it a handle (Mongolia VI U8, p.88; Libya's shop set).
