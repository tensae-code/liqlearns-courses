# Hands-on — Quantifiers

You do it, we guide you. Everything here is done in the
learner's real kitchen, with a companion or the Explain
Companion.

1. **The shelf report (no numbers allowed).** Open a
   cupboard or fridge. Report ten things with quantity
   words only — some, a few, plenty of, too much, not
   enough. Your companion bans every exact number: a
   number restarts the shelf.
2. **The smile and the complaint.** Hold up a small
   handful of beans twice. First praise it (a few
   beans — enough for soup), then complain about the
   same handful (few beans — almost none!). Feel the
   little word a flip the meaning on the same facts.
3. **Shop with handles (Libya's and Mongolia's game).**
   One person is the shopkeeper, one the buyer, with
   Libya's shop set on the table in pictures or real:
   a loaf of bread, a bunch of carrots, a can of
   lemonade, a bottle of water, a bag of rice, a tin of
   beans, a box of eggs. Buy five things in full
   sentences — "Can I have two tins of beans, please?"
   — then report the shopping: "I bought a box of
   eggs." (Both frames are the books' own.)
4. **Enough, judged.** Pour water into three glasses:
   too little, enough, too much. Your companion judges
   each glass in a full sentence, then swaps the seats:
   enough water, big enough, not enough. Then do the
   same with countable things and fewer/less.
