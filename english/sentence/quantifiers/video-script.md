# Video script — Quantifiers

Super-simple voice. Read slow. The learner hears this
before or beside the written lesson.

---

How much? How many? No numbers today. Only quantity words.

Some bread. Some apples.
SOME is for yes sentences. I have some rice.
ANY is for questions and no sentences.
Do you have any rice? I don't have any rice.
But an offer takes some: Would you like some tea?

MUCH for things you cannot count. Much water. Much rice.
MANY for things you can count. Many apples. Many people.

A FEW apples — a small handful. Good.
FEW apples — almost none. A sad word.
A LITTLE rice — a small amount. Good.
LITTLE rice — almost none.

PLENTY OF rice — more than enough. A happy word.
TOO MUCH rice — spilling. Too many people — no room.
ENOUGH rice — just right. Not too little. Not too much.
Enough comes after a describing word: big enough.
And before a noun: enough rice.

Less rice — you cannot count rice.
Fewer apples — you can count apples.
Less for pouring things. Fewer for counting things.

Now shopping. Rice has no plural. So give it a handle.
A loaf of bread. A bottle of water.
A packet of sweets. A kilo of rice. A jar of jam.
A cup of tea. A bowl of soup. A tin of beans.

Now you try.
Look in your kitchen.
Say five things with quantity words.
Some… A few… Too much… Plenty of…
And buy one thing: a loaf of bread, please.
