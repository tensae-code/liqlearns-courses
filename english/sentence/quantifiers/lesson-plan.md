# Lesson plan — Quantifiers

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Quantifiers
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 27, "The Small Words
That Point" — the second lesson of the level (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** saying HOW MUCH
and HOW MANY without a number — **some/any**, **much/
many**, **a few / a little** (and their bare twins few /
little), **plenty of**, **too much / too many**,
**enough**, **less / fewer** — and the partitives that
make uncountable things countable: **a loaf of bread,
a bottle of water…**
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Articles
(a/an is the smallest quantifier) and on the
countable/uncountable split. Someone, anyone, no one
follows it inside this level — the same some/any logic,
worn by people.
**What the learner can do at the end:** ask and answer
How much / How many with the whole quantity family,
choosing by countability and by sentence kind; praise
with plenty of, complain with too much/many, judge with
enough; compare with less/fewer; and shop with
partitives.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Articles. Recommended, never
  forced — this lesson stands alone for a learner who
  lands on it directly (DIRECT-ENTRY rule); countability
  is restated here.
- Speaker convention: the explanation is read in the
  learner's main language; the TARGET forms (some, any,
  a few, too much, a loaf of…) are never translated and
  are read in the English voice.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of two bowls: one with three
   spoonfuls of rice left, one heaped high.** Written
   slot: "a picture of a little against plenty — the
   same food, two quantities." What the student does:
   the student names each bowl before the words are
   given: "a little rice" — "plenty of rice."
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of a shopping basket holding a
   loaf, a bottle, a packet and a tin, each labelled
   with its partitive.** Written slot: "a picture of
   partitives — the words that give uncountable things
   a handle." What the student does: the student reads
   the basket aloud: a loaf of bread, a bottle of…
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of a cup filled exactly to the
   line marked ENOUGH, beside a cup spilling over.**
   Written slot: "a picture of enough against too much."
   What the student does: the student judges both cups
   in full sentences before reading the rule.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first**: the kitchen shelf — how much of
   everything, in the learner's own language, no exact
   numbers allowed.
2. **Countable vs uncountable**: Bolivia's Year 2 book
   tables the contrast whole (Theme 4: countable take
   a/an or a number; uncountable — milk, rice, love —
   do not); Mexico's Grade 7 Project 13 sorts countable
   posts/comments against uncountable information/time/
   fun, with how many / how much (pp.117–118).
3. **Some / any**: Cambodia's Grade 9 book teaches
   some/any with much/many and too much/too many inside
   its healthy-eating unit (Unit 7, printed pp.50–55);
   Malaysia Form 3 Unit 1 teaches countable/uncountable
   nouns and quantifiers together (pp.5–16).
4. **A few / a little, and the bare pair**: a few/a
   little = some, and enough-ish; few/little = not
   many/much (Nepal Grade 10 Unit 13's quantifier set:
   many/much, (a) few/(a) little, pp.157–171).
5. **Plenty of, too much/many, enough**: Cambodia's
   Grade 9 market and recipe units run How many/How
   much with a few/a little, a lot, plenty of (Units 20–
   21, printed pp.128–139); Cambodia's Grade 8 unit adds
   too much/too many/enough to the first conditional's
   kitchen (Unit 8).
6. **Less / fewer**: fewer for countable, less for
   uncountable.
7. **Partitives and containers**: Mongolia's Grade 6
   book prints a QUANTITY NOUNS set — a loaf of bread,
   a slice of…, a glass of…, a cup of…, a bottle of…, a
   bowl of…, a packet of…, a carton of…, a kilo of…, a
   jar of… (Unit 8, 5a, p.88); Libya's "At the Shop"
   unit's quantity set — loaf of bread, bunch of
   carrots, can of lemonade, bottle of water, bag of
   rice, tin of beans, box of eggs (book pp.104–105);
   Kuwait's Pearls 7A stocks the quantity family at
   Lesson 2 level in its present-system book.
8. **Do it yourself**: the three closing tasks.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck on much vs many: count it. If you can put a
  number and an -s on it (three apples), it is many's
  ground; if it pours or piles (rice, water), much's.
- Stuck on few vs a few: a few is a small handful and
  the speaker is content; bare few is a complaint —
  "almost none". The little word a carries the smile.
- This lesson's next door: Someone, anyone, no one
  (same level) — this same some/any machine, for
  people, things and places.
