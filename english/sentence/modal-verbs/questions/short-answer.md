## Short answer

1. Say one strong rule for drivers with must. (Sub lesson 1)
   **Answer: “Drivers must stop at the red light.”** (any strong rule) — must = strongest obligation.

2. Say one forbidden thing with mustn't. (Sub lesson 1)
   **Answer: “You mustn't be late.”** (any prohibition) — mustn't = not allowed.

3. Give a sick friend advice with should. (Sub lesson 2)
   **Answer: “You should rest.”** (any kind advice) — should = advice.

4. Say one personal duty with should. (Sub lesson 2)
   **Answer: “I should call my grandmother.”** (any personal obligation) — should is weaker than must.

5. Which is stronger: must or should? (Sub lesson 2)
   **Answer: Must.** — must is iron; should is advice.

6. Say one duty the spoken way. (Sub lesson 3)
   **Answer: “I have to go now.”** (any have to sentence) — speech uses have to in place of must.

7. Is have to a modal verb, in the compendium's words? (Sub lesson 3)
   **Answer: No — “not a modal verb”; it behaves like a normal verb.** — the book's own statement.
