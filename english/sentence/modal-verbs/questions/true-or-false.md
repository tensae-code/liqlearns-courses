## True or false

1. Must changes with the person: “he musts”. (Sub lesson 1)
   **Answer: False** — must never changes: he must, she must.

2. Mustn't means “not needed”. (Sub lesson 1)
   **Answer: False** — mustn't means not allowed; “not needed” is don't have to.

3. Should is stronger than must. (Sub lesson 2)
   **Answer: False** — should is weaker: advice, not law.

4. “You should rest” is advice. (Sub lesson 2)
   **Answer: True** — should gives advice and kind obligation.

5. The compendium says have to is a modal verb. (Sub lesson 3)
   **Answer: False** — it says have to is “not a modal verb”, used in speech in place of must.

6. Must to is the spoken form, in the compendium's comparison. (Sub lesson 3)
   **Answer: False** — must to is the official/written form; the spoken form is have to.
