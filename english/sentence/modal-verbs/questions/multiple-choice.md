## Multiple choice

1. “You ___ stop at the red light.” (the law) (Sub lesson 1)
   a) should  b) must  c) might yesterday
   **Answer: b) must** — laws and strong rules take must.

2. “Students ___ run in the hall.” (forbidden) (Sub lesson 1)
   a) mustn't  b) don't have to  c) should
   **Answer: a) mustn't** — forbidden = mustn't.

3. He ___ wear a uniform at his school. (Sub lesson 1)
   a) musts  b) must  c) to must
   **Answer: b) must** — must never changes, even for he.

4. “You look tired. You ___ rest.” (advice) (Sub lesson 2)
   a) must  b) mustn't  c) should
   **Answer: c) should** — advice takes should.

5. “We ___ help our friends.” (the right, kind thing) (Sub lesson 2)
   a) should  b) mustn't  c) are should
   **Answer: a) should** — should carries weaker, personal obligation.

6. “You ___ stay up so late.” (advice against) (Sub lesson 2)
   a) should  b) shouldn't  c) must to
   **Answer: b) shouldn't** — shouldn't is should's negative.

7. In the compendium's comparison, the official WRITTEN rule-form is… (Sub lesson 3)
   a) must to  b) should  c) gonna
   **Answer: a) must to** — the book gives must to for official/written rules.

8. In SPEECH, in place of must, people use… (Sub lesson 3)
   a) must to  b) have to  c) should to
   **Answer: b) have to** — speech uses have to; and the book notes have to is not a modal verb.
