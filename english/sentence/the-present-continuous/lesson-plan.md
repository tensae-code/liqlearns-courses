# Lesson plan — The Present Continuous

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** The Present Continuous
**Lesson:** the-present-continuous — saying what is happening now: I am reading; What are you doing?
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 12 — Now, Before, Later
**The one thing:** The present continuous — am/is/are + verb-ing for actions happening now.
**This lesson's own level:** Grammar lesson (time): be + -ing for now-actions; all persons; What are you doing? questions; the now/habit contrast with the present simple.

## Why this lesson exists

Indonesia's Grade 3 book teaches the present continuous across a whole run of chapters — "What are you doing?" over chores, play and classroom actions — and the course had the Past Continuous but no Present Continuous lesson: the now-form was met only inside other lessons. This lesson is built from the placements under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06).

## Sub lessons

1. I am doing it now — am + verb-ing
2. He is, we are — the be forms for everyone
3. What are you doing? — questions
4. Now or every day? — continuous against simple present

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is needed before this lesson. Present Actions teaches the simple present for habits; this lesson teaches the now-form, and each stands alone.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- Say what is happening now: "I am reading." "She is cooking."
- Use am/is/are correctly for every person.
- Ask and answer "What are you doing?"
- Tell now-actions from habits: "I am reading now" against "I read every day."

## What you need
Nothing special — things happening in the room are the material.

## The run of the lesson
1. ADD IMAGE: a busy kitchen — mother cooking, boy reading, cat sleeping. The learner guesses what each is doing before the form appears.
2. Teach sub lesson 1 on the learner themself: stand, sit, write — "You are writing NOW."
3. ADD GIF (1–2 seconds): a clock hand sweeping — the -ing form is the clock's form: happening at this moment.
4. Sub lesson 4 sorts cards: NOW (am reading) against EVERY DAY (read) — the contrast the books build between this work and the daily-routine work.

## Add-ons
- Mime and guess: one mimes an action; the class asks "Are you swimming?" — "No, I'm not. I'm fishing!"
- Window watch: watch outside for one minute and report five "-ing" sentences.

## If the learner is stuck
Keep to "I am + -ing" on real actions in the room until the shape is automatic. The other be forms and the habit contrast come one step each after.

## Teacher note
Indonesia's Grade 3 book spends Chapters 5–8 on this form because it is the child's first tense beyond naming; the lesson keeps that patience — one be form at a time, always on actions the learner can see.
