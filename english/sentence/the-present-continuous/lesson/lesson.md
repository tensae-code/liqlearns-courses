# The Present Continuous

## Do it first

Look around the room. What is happening RIGHT NOW? Someone is sitting. Maybe someone is talking, or a bird is singing outside. Try to say one thing with "is …-ing": "She is reading." Go on — in this lesson you will learn the form for actions happening now.

## Sub lesson 1 — I am doing it now — am + verb-ing

For actions happening now, English uses **am + verb-ing**:

"**I am reading.**" "I am writing." "I am listening to you."

Indonesia's Grade 3 book builds whole chapters on this one shape — "What are you doing?" asked over cooking, cleaning, playing — because it answers the child's favourite question: what is happening?

Do three now, about yourself: mime an action and say "I am …-ing."

## Sub lesson 2 — He is, we are — the be forms for everyone

The -ing never changes. Only the little word before it changes:

- I **am** reading.
- He / she / it **is** reading.
- You / we / they **are** reading.

Indonesia's chapters drill the set on families and classes: "Mother is cooking. Father is working. The children are playing."

Say three about the people near you: one is, two are.

## Sub lesson 3 — What are you doing? — questions

Ask with be first: "**What are you doing?**" — "I am drawing." "**Is she sleeping?**" — "No, she isn't. She is studying."

Short forms help: I'm, you're, he's, we're — "I'm cooking." "They're playing."

The Grade 3 book asks the question on every page of these chapters — it is the engine of the whole form.

Ask me now: "What are you doing?" Then answer mine about someone you can see.

## Sub lesson 4 — Now or every day? — continuous against simple present

Two forms, two meanings:

- **Now:** "I am reading." (at this moment)
- **Every day:** "I read every day." (a habit — Present Actions' form)

Indonesia's books teach both to the same children: routines in the daily-life chapters, -ing in the doing chapters. The words now / right now point to -ing; every day / usually point to the habit form.

Sort these: "She is cooking ___." "She cooks ___." (now / every day)

## Say it to your companion

Watch your companion for ten seconds. Say three true sentences about what they are doing ("You are sitting. You are holding a book…"). Then swap. Finish by asking each other "What are you doing?" and answering truly.

## Do It Yourself

Write six sentences: three about what people around you are doing now (one am, one is, one are), one question ("What is … doing?"), and two contrast sentences — one now, one every day. Read them aloud.
