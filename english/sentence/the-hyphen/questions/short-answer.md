## Short answer

1. Name two joining jobs of the hyphen. (Sub lesson 1)
   **Answer:** Any two of: spelling out numbers (twenty-one); linking words into one idea (well-known, mother-in-law); tying a whole phrase used as an adjective before a noun (a ten-year-old boy).

2. "He resigned." "He re-signed." What changes? (Sub lesson 2)
   **Answer:** Without the hyphen he left his job; with the hyphen he signed again and stayed. The hyphen alone carries the difference.

3. Your friend writes "a well known singer". Fix it and say why. (Sub lesson 1)
   **Answer:** "A well-known singer" — the phrase stands before the noun and works as one describing word, so the hyphen ties it together.

4. What is a suspended hyphen? Give the lesson's example. (Sub lesson 3)
   **Answer:** A hyphen that hangs in the air and waits for a shared ending: "two- and three-year-olds."
