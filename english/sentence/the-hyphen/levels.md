# Levels — The Hyphen

This file is the sub-lesson depth record for the lesson: what
each sub lesson holds, in teaching order. The old layer wording
is not used; the sub lessons below are the lesson's own parts,
exactly as listed in lesson-plan.md.

| Sub lesson | What it holds |
|---|---|
| Sub lesson 1 — The joining line | The hyphen itself — a short line that joins words into one idea: numbers spelled out (twenty-one), linked words (well-known, mother-in-law), and whole phrases used as adjectives before a noun (a ten-year-old boy). From Lynne Truss's "The Hyphen" in Iceland's *Spotlight 10*, printed pp.78–79. |
| Sub lesson 2 — Keeping meanings apart | The meaning-changing hyphen: re-formed against reformed; prefixes and letter collision (re-enter, re-sign against resign); spelling a word aloud letter by letter (J-A-N-E). Same source, printed pp.78–79. |
| Sub lesson 3 — Hyphens in the flow of writing | Suspended hyphens (two- and three-year-olds), a long word split at the end of a line, and hyphens showing hesitation in written dialogue. Same source, printed pp.78–79. |

Pass mark: the sources state no pass mark and none is invented. In
this course a sub lesson opens when the learner proves the previous
one with the prove-it check (at most 5 questions, the sub lesson's
whole question set as the pool).
