# Lesson plan — Relative clauses

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Relative clauses
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 23, "Joining with Who
and Which" — the first lesson of the level
(CATCH-UP-PLAN.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the defining
relative clause — joining two sentences into one with
who / which / that / where / when / whose, so the second
sentence becomes a label stuck on a noun in the first —
and the one licensed shortcut: the joining word may be
dropped when it is the object, never when it is the
subject.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Building a
sentence (a sentence can carry extra pieces) and on
Mine and yours (whose is the possession word the family
already owns). The comma kind — extra information that
can be lifted out — is the NEXT lesson in this level,
not this one.
**What the learner can do at the end:** choose the right
joining word for a person, thing, place, time or
possession; join two sentences in the right order; drop
the joining word exactly where the books allow it and
keep it everywhere else.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Building a sentence and Mine and
  yours (whose). Recommended, never forced.
- The speaker button reads the explanations in the
  learner's own language; the target forms (who, which,
  that, where, when, whose and every model clause) are
  always heard in English and never translated.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of two separate sentence cards,
   "The woman — She wears a red coat," sliding together
   into one card: "The woman that wears the red coat."**
   Written slot: "a picture of two sentences becoming
   one." What the student does: the student says which
   word did the joining, and points at the noun the
   clause is stuck to.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of five signposts — a person, a
   box, a house, a clock, a key in a hand — each with its
   word: who, which, where, when, whose.** Written slot:
   "a picture of the five signposts and their words."
   What the student does: the student points at each
   picture and says its word before reading the table.
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of a sticker being peeled off a
   parcel: on the sticker, "(that) I bought yesterday,"
   and the parcel still labelled "the book."** Written
   slot: "a picture of the joining word peeling away."
   What the student does: the student tries peeling the
   word off a subject clause too — and hears why it will
   not come off there.
4. **Slot 4 — a picture of a market stall with ten hats,
   one red hat circled.** Written slot: "a picture of one
   hat picked out of many." What the student does: the
   student says the clause that picks it out — "the hat
   that is red" — because picking out WHICH one is this
   clause's whole job.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first** (lesson/lesson.md): two short
   sentences about one woman, said first in the learner's
   own language, then joined.
2. **The join**: the second sentence becomes a clause
   stuck on a noun; the repeated word is replaced by the
   joining word (Cambodia Grade 9, Unit 19, rule box,
   printed p.122).
3. **The word by what it points back to**: who people,
   which things/animals, that people or things, where
   places, when time, whose possession (Egypt,
   Preparatory 9 Term 2, Unit 8, Tip p.29).
4. **Subject seat, object seat**: the joining word can
   be the clause's subject ("the man who lives here") or
   its object ("the book that I bought").
5. **The drop**: an object joining word may be dropped;
   a subject one may not (Vietnam Grade 9, Unit 9;
   Egypt's Secondary 11 box states the same rule).
6. **Whose, where, when in work**: Nepal Grade 8
   (who/where/whose/which), Kuwait Over to You 10B
   (who/which/where/whose), Cambodia Grade 12's space
   unit (who/which/that/where/when).
7. **Do it yourself** (the three closing tasks in the
   lesson): the stall round, the family labels, the
   drop-or-keep drill.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck on who vs which: ask what the word points back
  to — a person takes who, a thing takes which, and
  that covers both (Egypt's Tip, in those terms).
- Stuck on the drop: find the clause's own subject. If
  another word does the verb's work after the joining
  word (I bought, you saw), the joining word is the
  object and may go. If the joining word itself does
  it, it stays.
- This lesson's next door: Non-defining relative
  clauses — the same words between commas, carrying
  extra information instead of picking out which one.
