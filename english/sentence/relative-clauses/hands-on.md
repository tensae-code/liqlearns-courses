# Hands-on — Relative clauses

You do it, we guide you. Everything here is done in the
learner's real room, with a companion or the Explain
Companion.

1. **The stall round.** Lay out six objects, two of them
   alike (two cups, two books). The companion may only
   ask for one with a relative clause — "Give me the cup
   that is empty" — and the learner hands over the right
   one. A clause that fits both objects must be rebuilt
   until it picks out one.
2. **The family labels.** Take a family photo (or draw
   one). Label every person with a clause: "This is my
   uncle, who lives in…" — no, commas are the next
   lesson; here the clause must pick the person out of a
   group: "the uncle who lives by the sea," "the cousin
   whose bicycle is red." Five labels, spoken first,
   then written.
3. **The drop-or-keep drill.** The companion reads ten
   clauses; the learner shouts DROP or KEEP for the
   joining word and says why — object (drop allowed) or
   subject (keep). Use the books' own test: is there
   another doer after the word? (Vietnam Grade 9,
   Unit 9.)
4. **Two sentences, one breath.** The companion says two
   short sentences about one thing ("The shop is new.
   The shop sells bread."). The learner joins them in
   one breath with which/that/where as fits, then swaps
   roles. Five joins each way.
