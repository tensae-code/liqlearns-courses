# Video script — Relative clauses

Super-simple voice. Read slow. The learner hears this
before or beside the written lesson.

---

Two sentences.
The woman wears a red coat.
I saw her yesterday.

English can join them.
The woman that I saw yesterday wears a red coat.
One sentence now. Not two.

The small word does the joining.
Who. Which. That. Where. When. Whose.

Who is for people.
The man who lives here.
Which is for things.
The book which I bought.
That is for people and things.
The woman that wears red. The book that I bought.

Where is for places.
The house where I was born.
When is for times.
The day when we met.
Whose is for owning.
The girl whose bag is red. The bag is hers.

Now a secret. Sometimes the small word can go.
The book that I bought.
The book I bought.
Both are good.

But listen. The word can go only in one place.
I bought the book. I is the doer in that part.
That is the thing bought. So that can go.

The man who lives here.
Who does the living. Who is the doer.
Who cannot go. Never.

So ask: does the small word do the work?
Yes — keep it.
No — it can go.

Now you try.
Join two sentences.
The dog ran away. It bit me.
The dog that bit me ran away.

One sentence. One small word. The job is done.
