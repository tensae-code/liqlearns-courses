# Short Answer — Structures of Modification

(Every item is marked with the sub lesson it belongs to: (Sub lesson N). The gate pool for a sub lesson is all of that sub lesson's questions — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the whole set is the pool.)

1. Distinguish an adjective series from coordinated adjectives. (Sub lesson 1)
   — A series stacks modifiers of different kinds in a fixed approach to the head (small, green, wooden box); coordinated adjectives are equals of the same rank — comma'd, reversible, joinable by 'and' (a cold, wet night).

2. Give one construction for each modifier of an adjective head: qualifier, noun, verb, phrase. (Sub lesson 2)
   — Qualifier: very cold. Noun: stone cold. Verb: freezing cold (or hard to say). Phrase: cold to the bone.

3. What does the adverb-over-a-whole-predication case show? (Sub lesson 1)
   — That an adverb can attach not to a word but to the whole statement ('Fortunately, the fire lit'), commenting on it entire — modification's reach is the clause itself.

4. Why does place matter for an adverb near a prepositional phrase? (Sub lesson 3)
   — Because attachment decides meaning: before the phrase it may modify one head, after it another; the reader reads whichever attachment the writer's placement makes.
