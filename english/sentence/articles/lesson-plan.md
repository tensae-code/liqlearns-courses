# Lesson plan — Articles

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Articles
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 27, "The Small Words
That Point" — the first lesson of the level (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the articles as
ONE system — **a/an** (one, new, a member of a kind),
**the** (the one we both know), and **zero** (no article:
kinds in general, most names) — including the article
rules for geographical names.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Building a
sentence and the noun lessons; Quantifiers follows it
inside this level (a/an is the doorway to some/any).
**What the learner can do at the end:** choose a, an,
the or nothing for a new noun, a known noun, a kind in
general, and the map's names — rivers, seas, mountain
ranges with the; single peaks, lakes, towns and most
countries without.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Building a sentence. Recommended,
  never forced — this lesson stands alone for a learner
  who lands on it directly (DIRECT-ENTRY rule).
- Speaker convention: the explanation is read in the
  learner's main language; the TARGET forms (a, an, the
  and the zero choice itself) are never translated and
  are read in the English voice.
- Rwanda teaches NO articles system and is cited
  nowhere in this lesson.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a market stall with one red
   apple held up, the rest a blur of fruit behind it.**
   Written slot: "a picture of ONE apple picked out of
   the many." What the student does: the student says
   "an apple" for any one, then "the apple" for THIS
   one in the hand — and hears the pointing change.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of a world map with a river, a
   sea, one mountain, a lake and a town lit up.**
   Written slot: "a picture of the map's names, some
   wearing the, some bare." What the student does: the
   student points at each name and guesses its coat
   before the rule is read.
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of children eating fruit at a
   table, captioned only FRUIT IS GOOD FOR YOU.**
   Written slot: "a picture of a kind spoken of in
   general — no article at all." What the student does:
   the student says the caption, then tries it with the
   and hears it break.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first**: one apple at a market — the learner
   buys ANY apple, then points at THE apple, in their
   own language.
2. **A/an — one, new, a member**: a before a consonant
   sound, an before a vowel sound; Bolivia's Year 1
   book meets articles inside its school theme (T3);
   Nepal's Grade 6 book opens its named grammar
   syllabus with articles a/an/the (Unit 1).
3. **The — the one we both know**: second mention, the
   only one, the one the place fixes.
4. **Zero — kinds in general and most names**: Cambodia's
   Grade 12 book teaches articles a/an/the/zero in a
   Look box on specific vs. general reference (Unit 9,
   pp.47–52); Malaysia Form 3 Unit 3 groups articles
   (a/an/the/zero) as one grammar family (pp.31–42);
   Vietnam Grade 12 Unit 2 heads its box "Articles
   (review and extension)" (printed p.22).
5. **The map's coats**: Armenia's Grade 10 book teaches
   articles with geographical names (Unit 4); Sudan's
   SPINE 6 practises a/an/no article inside its
   countable/uncountable work (Chapter 4, pp.51–64);
   Nepal returns to articles at Grade 10 (Unit 11,
   pp.78–86); South Africa's HL Grade 5 Workbook 1
   carries definite/indefinite article work in its
   teaching order.
6. **Do it yourself**: the three closing tasks.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck choosing: ask the two questions in order —
  "am I pointing at one we both know?" (the) — "or
  naming one new member of a kind?" (a/an) — "or the
  kind itself, in general?" (zero).
- Stuck on a vs an: listen, don't look. An stands
  before a SOUND that starts like a vowel: an hour
  (the h is silent), a university (it starts with a
  "yoo" sound).
- This lesson's next door: Quantifiers (same level) —
  how much of the thing the article just pointed at.
