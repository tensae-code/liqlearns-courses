# Hands-on — Double comparatives

You do it, we guide you. Everything here is done in the
learner's real day, with a companion or the Explain
Companion. The TARGET forms are always said in English;
the talk around them may be in the learner's own language.

1. **The rope game.** With a companion, hold an imaginary rope. One names a lever — "the more I practise…" — the other must finish the locked sentence before letting go of the rope: "…the better I play." Five levers each, levers swapped.
2. **The snowball report.** Pick one real thing that is changing step by step (a plant, a pile of homework, the heat at noon). Report it three times in comparative-and-comparative, each report one step further on: bigger and bigger, hotter and hotter.
3. **Error hunt, live.** Your companion reads out six sentences, two of them carrying *very better* / *very more easy*-type errors. Catch each error, say the corrected sentence whole (much better / far easier, or a double shape), and name the rule: very never grades a comparative.
