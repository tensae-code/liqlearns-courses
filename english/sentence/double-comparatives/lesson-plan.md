# Lesson plan — Double comparatives

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Double comparatives
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 28 — "Comparing More Deeply" (CURRICULUM.md / CATCH-UP-PLAN.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the two locked comparison shapes: the more…the more (two changes rising together — the more you read, the faster you learn) and comparative-and-comparative (bigger and bigger — one thing changing step by step).
**What the learner can do at the end:** build the more…the more with adjectives, adverbs and noun phrases on both sides; build comparative-and-comparative for gradual change; keep the two shapes apart live; and correct the error form *very better* when it appears.

1. **Double Comparatives — the Locked Pair** — the locked pair: the + comparative …, the + comparative … — two changes rising together, the lever first. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Double Comparatives — Gradual Change** — gradual change: comparative-and-comparative — one thing changing step by step (bigger and bigger, more and more difficult). (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **Double Comparatives — the Shape in Use** — a use witness, worded honestly: China's Grade 9 book carries the shape in its dialogues only — use-evidence, never taught there. (No separate old depth layer: this paragraph is the lesson's honesty note on its sources, standing between the gradual-change part and the error part.)
4. **Double Comparatives — the Error to Correct** — the error to correct: a comparative never takes very — *very better* and its family are wrong; grade with much / far / a lot, or lock the comparison into one of the two shapes. (Was depth Level 3, with Sub lesson 3.)

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Stronger comparisons (the graders) and the basic comparatives. Recommended, never forced — this lesson stands alone for a learner who lands on it directly (DIRECT-ENTRY rule).
- Speaker convention: the explanation is read in the learner's main language; the TARGET forms are never translated and are read in the English voice.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a staircase with two climbers: every step the first climber rises, the second rises too, locked together. What the student does: the student says the locked sentence — "The higher we climb, the colder it gets" — before the shape is named.**
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of a snowball rolling downhill, drawn three times, each time bigger. What the student does: the student names the step-by-step change: "bigger and bigger."**
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of a reading lamp over a book, the light growing as the pages turn. What the student does: the student links the two changes: "The more you read, the more you know."**

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first**: two things that change together in the learner's real life, said first in their own language.
2. **The locked pair**: the + comparative …, the + comparative … (Greece, Hotel & Tourist Trade, Unit 4, printed pp.67–82; Applied Arts, pp.50–53).
3. **Gradual change**: comparative-and-comparative (Greece, Applied Arts tables, pp.50–51; Vietnam Grade 12, Unit 4, pp.48–59).
4. **The city lesson**: double comparatives on city change (Vietnam Grade 9, Unit 2, pp.18–27).
5. **Use, not teaching**: the shape living inside China's reading dialogues — a use witness only.
6. **The error to correct**: *very better* is wrong — very never grades a comparative.
7. **Do it yourself**: the three closing tasks.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck on which side leads: the first the-clause is the lever (the more you read), the second is what follows (the faster you learn) — swap them and the meaning swaps too.
- Stuck between the two shapes: two changes locked together → the more…the more; one thing changing step by step → bigger and bigger.
- Stuck on *very better*: very grades plain adjectives (very big); comparatives take much/far/a lot or the double shapes — never very.
