# Stronger comparisons — short answer

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to, by what the item actually asks. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Stronger Comparisons — Big Gaps, Big Graders, except small-gap items, which are Sub lesson 2 — Stronger Comparisons — Small Gaps, Small Graders · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 3 — Stronger Comparisons — No Gap: the As…As Family, except small-gap items, which are Sub lesson 2 · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 4 — Stronger Comparisons — Adverbs Compare Too, except items about absolute adjectives, which are Sub lesson 5 — Stronger Comparisons — Absolute Adjectives.

Answer in a word or a whole sentence, as asked.

**1. (Sub lesson 2)** Compare two pencils on your desk with a small-gap grader, in one whole sentence. **Answer: (Learner's own true sentence, e.g. "This pencil is a bit longer than that one.")** — the grader must match the small real gap.

**2. (Sub lesson 3)** Say that two runners finished level, then that one was a hair behind. Two sentences. **Answer: "They ran as fast as each other. One was not quite as fast as the other."** — as…as for the tie; not quite as…as for a hair short (Cambodia, Grade 12, Unit 28).

**3. (Sub lesson 5)** Why is "more empty" refused? Say it in one line, then say the sentence right. **Answer: Empty is absolute — a thing is empty or it is not, so it takes no grader: "The bottle is empty."** — absolute adjectives take no more/most (Egypt, Secondary 12, Unit 11).
