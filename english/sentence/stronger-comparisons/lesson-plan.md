# Lesson plan — Stronger comparisons

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Stronger comparisons
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 28, "Comparing More Deeply" (CURRICULUM.md / CATCH-UP-PLAN.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** how to say HOW MUCH bigger, smaller, faster or more equal a comparison is — the degree words that stand in front of a comparative (a lot / far / much for a big gap, a little / a bit / slightly for a small one), the as…as family for equality and near-equality, adverb comparison, and the absolute adjectives that take no degree at all.
**What the learner can do at the end:** grade any comparison big or small (far bigger, slightly cheaper); run the whole as…as family (as tall as, not as tall as, not quite as tall as, not nearly as tall as, almost as tall as, exactly the same as); compare adverbs (faster, more quickly, better); and leave absolute adjectives (perfect, unique, dead, empty) ungraded.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the basic comparative and superlative lessons (bigger, more beautiful, the biggest). Recommended, never forced — this lesson stands alone for a learner who lands on it directly (DIRECT-ENTRY rule).
- Speaker convention: the explanation is read in the learner's main language; the TARGET forms are never translated and are read in the English voice.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of two mountains, one towering far above the other, with the words FAR HIGHER under the tall one. What the student does: the student says the graded sentence — "This mountain is far higher than that one" — before the degree words are named.**
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of two cups of tea, one full to the brim and one a sip lower, with the words A LITTLE LESS under the lower cup. What the student does: the student grades the small gap — small gaps take a little / a bit, never far.**
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of two runners crossing a line at exactly the same moment, shoulder to shoulder. What the student does: the student calls the tie — "as fast as" — then moves one runner one step back for "not quite as fast as."**

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first**: two things in the room compared plainly, then the gap sized in the learner's own language.
2. **Big-gap graders**: a lot / far / much + comparative (Egypt Secondary 12, Unit 11; Cambodia Grade 12, Unit 9).
3. **Small-gap graders**: a little / a bit / slightly + comparative (Egypt Secondary 11 Term 2, Comparative Phrases box, p.26; Egypt Secondary 12, Unit 11).
4. **The as…as family**: as…as, not as…as, almost as…as, not quite as…, not nearly as…, exactly the same as (Egypt, p.26; Nepal Grade 7, Unit 9, pp.88–96; Cambodia Grade 12, Unit 28).
5. **Adverb comparison**: -er for short adverbs, more for -ly adverbs, better/worse (Nepal Grade 7, Unit 9).
6. **Absolute adjectives**: perfect, unique, dead, empty take no more/most (Egypt Secondary 12, Unit 11).
7. **Do it yourself**: the three closing tasks.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck on big vs small graders: far/a lot/much stretch the gap; a little/a bit/slightly shrink it — a small gap with far sounds as wrong as a huge gap with a bit.
- Stuck on as…as: the adjective or adverb keeps its PLAIN shape inside the frame (as tall as, never *as taller as*).
- Stuck on absolutes: if a thing cannot be half-true (dead, empty, unique), it cannot be graded either — say it plain.
- This lesson's next door: Double comparatives (same level) — two comparisons locked together, changing together.
