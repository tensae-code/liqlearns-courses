# Reported Offers, Requests and Suggestions

## Do it first

Ana said three things yesterday:

- "I will carry your bag."
- "Can you open the window?"
- "Let's visit the museum."

One is an offer — she gives her own help. One is a request —
she asks for help. One is a suggestion — she puts an idea to
the group. When you tell someone else what Ana said, each
kind has its own reporting verb:

- She **offered to carry** my bag.
- She **asked me to open** the window.
- She **suggested visiting** the museum.

This lesson teaches the three kinds, one at a time.

## Sub lesson 1 — Reported offers

**Friendly opener.** An offer is a person giving their own
help: "I will help you." "Shall I carry that?" When you
report an offer, the verb is **offered**, and the offered
action follows with **to**.

**The shape of it.**
- "I will carry your bag." → He **offered to carry** my bag.
- "Shall I open the door?" → She **offered to open** the
  door.
- "I will lend you my book." → He **offered to lend** me his
  book.

The person in the report changes (your bag → my bag), but
the shape never does: **offered + to + verb**.

**Work it.**
- "I will drive you home." → She offered to drive me home.
- "I will pay for the tickets." → They offered to pay for
  the tickets.

**Watch out.** Do not report an offer with "said": "He said
to carry my bag" loses the offer. The verb *offered* is
what carries the kindness into the report.

*Difficulty: first steps.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Reported requests

**Friendly opener.** A request asks someone else to do
something: "Can you open the window?" "Please wait here."
Reported, the verb is **asked** (or the more formal
**requested**), then the person, then **to**.

**The shape of it.**
- "Can you open the window?" → She **asked me to open** the
  window.
- "Please sit down." → He **asked us to sit** down.
- "Could you wait here?" → They **asked him to wait** there.

Polite requests wear polite shapes: **Can you…?**,
**Could you…?**, **Would you…?** All three report the same
way — asked + person + to.

**Work it.**
- "Would you lend me your pen?" → She asked me to lend her
  my pen.
- "Can you come early?" → He asked us to come early.

**Watch out.** A request has a person in the middle — asked
**me** to open — because somebody is being asked. An offer
has nobody in the middle: offered to carry.

*Difficulty: building up.*

## The companion

In pairs: one of you makes an offer ("I will help you with
the bags") and a request ("Could you hold this?"). The other
reports both to the class: "She offered to help me… She
asked me to hold…" Then change places.

## Sub lesson 3 — Reported suggestions

**Friendly opener.** A suggestion puts an idea to the
group: "Let's visit the museum." "Why don't we rest?"
"We could ask the teacher." Reported, the verb is
**suggested** — or **proposed** or **recommended** — and it
has three shapes after it.

**The shape of it.**
- **suggested + -ing**: "Let's visit the museum." → They
  **suggested visiting** the museum.
- **suggested + that…**: "We should leave early." → She
  **suggested that we leave** early.
- **suggested + to**: a suggestion can also be reported with
  to: "Let's ask her." → He **suggested to ask** her —
  though the -ing shape is the one you will meet most.

**Work it.**
- "Let's practise every day." → They suggested practising
  every day.
- "Why don't we take the bus?" → She suggested taking the
  bus.

**Watch out.** Never "suggested me to…". A suggestion is an
idea for the group, not an order to a person — keep the
person out of the middle, as with offers.

*Difficulty: building up.*

## Sub lesson 4 — Choosing the right one

**Friendly opener.** Now the real skill: hear the sentence,
name its kind, then report it with its own verb. Offer,
request or suggestion — the kind decides the verb.

**The shape of it.**
- Gives their own help → **offer**: "I will cook tonight."
  → He offered to cook that night.
- Asks someone to do something → **request**: "Can you
  bring water?" → She asked me to bring water.
- Puts an idea to the group → **suggestion**: "Let's eat
  outside." → They suggested eating outside.

**Work it.**
- "Shall I carry that?" — an offer → She offered to carry
  it.
- "Please close the door." — a request → He asked us to
  close the door.
- "Why don't we rest?" — a suggestion → He suggested
  resting.

**Watch out.** The words alone can fool you: "I will help"
is an offer because the speaker gives; "Will you help?" is
a request because the hearer is asked. Ask who does the
action — that names the kind.

*Difficulty: the full move.*

## Do It Yourself

Write three sentences someone said to you this week — one
offer, one request, one suggestion — and report each one
with its own verb: offered, asked, suggested. You have told
what people said, each in its own shape.
