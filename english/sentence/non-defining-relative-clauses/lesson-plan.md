# Lesson plan — Non-defining relative clauses

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Non-defining relative clauses
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 23, "Joining with Who
and Which" — the second lesson of the level
(CATCH-UP-PLAN.md), after Relative clauses.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the non-defining
relative clause — the same joining words set between
commas, carrying EXTRA information: the noun is already
fully named, the clause can be lifted out and the
sentence still stands — with its three hard rules: the
commas stay, that is never used, and the pronoun can
never be dropped — plus the whole-sentence which, which
comments on everything before it.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands directly on
Relative clauses (the defining system, this level's
first lesson) — the words are the same, the job is
different. Recommended, never forced: a learner can
start here and pick up the defining half from the
explanation's contrast.
**What the learner can do at the end:** tell a defining
clause from a non-defining one by the commas and by the
lift-out test; build extra-information clauses with
who/which/whose/where/when; and comment on a whole
sentence with which.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Relative clauses (defining). The
  lift-out test below restates the difference, so this
  lesson still stands alone.
- The speaker button reads the explanations in the
  learner's own language; the target forms (the clauses
  themselves, the commas' work, which for a whole
  sentence) are always heard in English, never
  translated.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a sentence as a train: the
   main carriage "My uncle lives in Cairo," and a side
   carriage in brackets, "who is a doctor," being lifted
   off by a crane while the train still runs.** Written
   slot: "a picture of a clause being lifted out of a
   sentence that still works." What the student does:
   the student reads the sentence with the carriage on,
   then off — and hears that both are complete.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of two commas as two hands
   holding a clause above a sentence, with the word
   THAT crossed out underneath.** Written slot: "a
   picture of commas holding a clause, with that crossed
   out." What the student does: the student says the
   three rules aloud — commas stay, no that, no drop —
   before reading on.
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of a whole street scene with an
   arrow looping from the whole scene to one word:
   WHICH.** Written slot: "a picture of which pointing
   at a whole sentence." What the student does: the
   student finishes the sentence — "It rained all day,
   which…" — with a comment of their own.
4. **Slot 4 — a picture of a famous person's name card,
   "Mr An," with a ribbon of extra facts tied to it.**
   Written slot: "a picture of extra facts tied to a
   name that needs no picking out." What the student
   does: the student says why no defining clause is
   needed: the name already says who.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first** (lesson/lesson.md): one named person,
   one extra fact, said first in the learner's own
   language — with and without the extra.
2. **The lift-out test**: extra information, set off by
   commas; the sentence still makes sense without the
   clause (Vietnam Grade 9, Unit 10, in its own terms).
3. **The three hard rules**: commas stay; that never
   used; the pronoun can never be omitted (Egypt,
   Secondary 11 Term 1, Grammar Box p.77; Vietnam
   Grade 9, Unit 10).
4. **The family at work**: who/which/whose/where/when
   between commas (Egypt's box includes whose; Malaysia
   Form 3 Unit 4 and Form 4 Module 2 teach defining and
   non-defining side by side).
5. **The whole-sentence which**: the clause comments on
   everything before it (Vietnam Grade 12, Unit 3 —
   the grammar heading itself, printed p.35).
6. **A Level-3 corner — prepositions**: preposition
   final or fronted inside relative clauses, defining
   vs non-defining (Yemen, Pupil's Book 6, Language
   review 4, p.15).
7. **Do it yourself** (the three closing tasks in the
   lesson): the name-card facts, the lift-out check,
   the whole-sentence comments.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck on which kind: cover the clause with a hand.
  If the sentence still names its person or thing
  fully, the clause is extra — commas, non-defining.
  If covering it leaves "which one?" hanging, it is
  defining — no commas.
- Stuck on that: in this lesson that is simply banned;
  swap in who or which and put the commas back.
- This lesson's next door: Participle clauses — where
  these very clauses shrink to their participle
  (the man talking… = the man who is talking…).
