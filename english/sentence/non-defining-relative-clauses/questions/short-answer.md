# Non-defining relative clauses — short answer

Answer in whole sentences.

**1. (Level 1)** Add one extra fact, between commas,
to: "My grandmother lives in a village."
**Answer: My grandmother, who is eighty, lives in a
village.** (Any true extra fact with commas and who
earns it — the name needs no picking out.)

**2. (Level 2)** Rewrite with whose: "Mr Lee teaches
us. His daughter is in my class."
**Answer: Mr Lee, whose daughter is in my class,
teaches us.** — whose carries possession between
commas (Egypt, Grammar Box p.77).

**3. (Level 3)** React to the whole fact with which:
"The school closed early today."
**Answer: The school closed early today, which pleased
the children.** (Any which-comment on the whole
sentence earns it; Vietnam, Grade 12, Unit 3.)

**4. (Level 3)** Say why this sentence is wrong, and
fix it: "My brother, that lives in Spain, is a cook."
**Answer: That is banned in a non-defining clause —
"My brother, who lives in Spain, is a cook."** — the
commas were right; the word was not.
