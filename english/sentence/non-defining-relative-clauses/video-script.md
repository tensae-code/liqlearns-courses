# Video script — Non-defining relative clauses

Super-simple voice. Read slow. The learner hears this
before or beside the written lesson.

---

My uncle lives in Cairo.
You know my uncle. The name is enough.

Now I add a fact.
My uncle, who is a doctor, lives in Cairo.

See the commas? Two small hands.
They hold the extra fact.
Who is a doctor. Extra. Not needed to know the man.

Take it out.
My uncle lives in Cairo.
The sentence still works. The man is still my uncle.

That is the test. Lift it out. Does the sentence stand?
Yes — the clause was extra.

Three rules. Listen well.

One. The commas stay. Never drop them.
Two. That is not allowed here. Use who or which.
Three. The small word cannot go. Never drop who.
Never drop which.

Now a bigger trick.
It rained all day, which spoiled our trip.

Which does not point at one word.
Which points at ALL of it. The rain all day.
The whole sentence. Then the comment: it spoiled our trip.

He passed his exam, which surprised everyone.
She left early, which was fine.

Now you try.
Take a name. Add one extra fact, with commas.
My teacher, who loves music, …
My town, which is very old, …

Extra facts, held by commas. That is all.
