# Hands-on — Too, enough, so, such

You do it, we guide you. Everything here is done in the
learner's real day, with a companion or the Explain
Companion. The TARGET forms are always said in English;
the talk around them may be in the learner's own language.

1. **The door game.** Find five real objects (a bag, a chair, a box, a book, a bottle). For each, say the pair: one too-sentence that closes the door ("too heavy to carry upstairs") and one enough-sentence that opens it ("light enough to carry in one hand") — the adjective must change sides honestly. Your companion checks word order: too before, enough after.
2. **Result chains.** One person offers a pusher half — "The music was so loud that…", "It was such a long film that…", "He spoke so quietly that…" — the other must finish with a TRUE result clause. Six chains, so and such alternating strictly; a noun after so (or no noun after such) breaks the chain and it restarts.
3. **Intensifier auction.** A companion names a plain adjective (tired, hot, cold, sure, perfect). Bid it up with the books' set — very, really, extremely, absolutely, utterly — and defend your winning bid: which intensifiers can this adjective take? (absolutely wants absolutes: absolutely perfect/sure; very wants gradables: very tired.) Three adjectives, full set argued.
