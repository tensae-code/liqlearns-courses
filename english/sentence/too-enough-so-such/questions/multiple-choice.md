# Too, enough, so, such — multiple choice

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to, by the pusher the item actually asks about. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Too — the Door Closes, and Sub lesson 2 — Enough — the Door Opens · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 3 — So…That — a Result After an Adjective or Adverb, and Sub lesson 4 — Such…That — a Result After a Noun Phrase · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 5 — The Wider Intensifier Set, except items that ask about one pusher's own seat, which are marked with that pusher's sub lesson.

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** "The suitcase is ______ to carry upstairs." a) too heavy  b) heavy enough  c) so heavy **Answer: a) too heavy** — too + adjective + to-infinitive: the door closes — Bolivia's own example family, "too heavy" (Year 6, p.217).

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** "He is ______ to lift the box — watch him." a) too strong  b) strong enough  c) enough strong **Answer: b) strong enough** — enough stands AFTER its adjective (Nepal, Grade 8, Unit 18).

**3. (Sub lesson 4)** "It was ______ cold day that school closed." a) so  b) such a  c) too **Answer: b) such a** — a noun phrase (a cold day) follows → such, with a/an for a countable singular (Kuwait 9B, p.62).

**4. (Sub lesson 5)** "______ perfect! Not one mistake." a) Very  b) Absolutely  c) Too **Answer: b) Absolutely** — absolute adjectives take absolutely / completely / utterly — Bolivia's wider intensifier list (Year 4, p.197).
