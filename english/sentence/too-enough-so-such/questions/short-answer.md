# Too, enough, so, such — short answer

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to, by the pusher the item actually asks about. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Too — the Door Closes, and Sub lesson 2 — Enough — the Door Opens · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 3 — So…That — a Result After an Adjective or Adverb, and Sub lesson 4 — Such…That — a Result After a Noun Phrase · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 5 — The Wider Intensifier Set, except items that ask about one pusher's own seat, which are marked with that pusher's sub lesson.

Answer in a word or a whole sentence, as asked.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Take "the box is heavy": close its door with too, whole sentence. **Answer: (Learner's own, e.g. "The box is too heavy to lift.")** — too before the adjective, to-infinitive after it.

**2. (Sub lesson 4)** Say that a film was long, with such, and give its result. **Answer: (Learner's own, e.g. "It was such a long film that we left early.")** — such (a) + adjective + noun + that + result (Vietnam, Grade 12, Unit 7).

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Why is "enough strong to lift it" wrong? Say it right in one line. **Answer: Enough stands AFTER its adjective: "strong enough to lift it."** — the one seat-swap this family never allows (Nepal, Grade 8, Unit 18).
