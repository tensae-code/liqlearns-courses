# Lesson plan — Too, enough, so, such

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Too, enough, so, such
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 30, "Dressing a Sentence" (CURRICULUM.md / CATCH-UP-PLAN.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the four result-pushers: too + adjective + to-infinitive (more than is wanted — it blocks), adjective + enough + to-infinitive (as much as is needed — it opens), so + adjective/adverb + that (a result follows), and such (a/an) + noun + that — plus the wider intensifier set the books list around them (very, really, extremely, absolutely…).
**What the learner can do at the end:** build too heavy to lift and strong enough to lift for the same object and hear the door close and open; run so…that for adjectives and adverbs and such…that for noun phrases, with a/an placed right; and choose the intensifier that fits the adjective's kind (very tired, absolutely perfect).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Stronger comparisons and Gerunds and infinitives (the to-form after too/enough). Recommended, never forced — this lesson stands alone for a learner who lands on it directly (DIRECT-ENTRY rule).
- Speaker convention: the explanation is read in the learner's main language; the TARGET forms are never translated and are read in the English voice.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a small boy straining at a huge stone, captioned TOO HEAVY TO LIFT, beside the same boy, grown, lifting a smaller stone, captioned STRONG ENOUGH TO LIFT IT. What the student does: the student says both sentences and points at the door each one opens or closes.**
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of a runner crossing a finish line while the crowd gasps, captioned SHE RAN SO FAST THAT…. What the student does: the student finishes the that-clause with a true result.**
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of a single enormous cake on a tiny table, captioned SUCH A BIG CAKE THAT…. What the student does: the student names what follows such (a/an + adjective + noun) against what follows so (adjective alone).**

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first**: one thing in the room that is too heavy, too hot or too far, said first in the learner's own language.
2. **Too and enough**: too + adjective + to-infinitive; adjective + enough (Nepal, Grade 8, Unit 18, Grammar II — see Sources note on the grade).
3. **Enough and too, the book rules**: TOO = more than enough, not satisfactory ("That cell phone is too expensive"); ENOUGH = satisfactory ("This laptop is cheap enough. I will take it") (Bolivia Year 6, Topic 3, p.217).
4. **The intensifier set**: too/really/very + the wider list — absolutely, completely, extremely, highly, rather, really, so, too, totally, utterly, very (Bolivia Year 4, Topic 3, p.197).
5. **Linkers of result**: so … that / such … that, with form rules for adjectives, adverbs and countable/uncountable nouns (Kuwait Pearls 9B, Lesson 6, p.62).
6. **So and such** as the unit's grammar, beside comparison of adjectives and adverbs (Malaysia Form 3, Unit 12).
7. **Adverbial clauses of result**: so … that / such … that (Vietnam Grade 12, Unit 7, Grammar box, printed p.91).
8. **Do it yourself**: the three closing tasks.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck on too vs enough: too stands BEFORE the adjective and closes the door (too heavy to lift); enough stands AFTER the adjective and opens it (light enough to lift).
- Stuck on so vs such: so takes the adjective or adverb alone (so fast that); such takes a noun phrase (such a fast car that) — if a noun stands there, it is such.
- Stuck on a/an after such: countable singular nouns take a/an (such a day); plurals and uncountables take none (such good weather).
