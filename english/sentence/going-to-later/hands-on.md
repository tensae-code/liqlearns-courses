# Hands-on — Going to: later

The lesson pointed at a real tomorrow.

## The weekend wall-plan
Draw tomorrow and the weekend as three boxes on paper —
TOMORROW / SATURDAY / SUNDAY. In each box, two going-to
sentences about your real life, whole and true. Pin it where
you will see it in the morning. As the days pass, tick each
plan that truly happened; cross none — plans that moved are
next week's first sentences ("I am going to visit — this
week, really").

## Sky-signs walk
Walk outside with a partner for five minutes. Every visible
sign of a coming thing earns a prediction, said whole: the
clouds, a laden bicycle, a queue at the pump, washing on
the line, a gathering smell of rain. Back inside, write the
three best — with the SIGN named in each ("The sky is black
over the hill, so…").

## The plans interview, live
Bolivia's exercise with real stakes: interview a family
member about next week — five going-to questions, their
true plans written whole. Read it back to them on the day:
how much of the future the two of you said right?

## Decision theatre
Pairs act two tiny scenes: (1) a plan made last week, told
today — going to; (2) a phone ringing NOW, answered on the
spot — will. The class (or your partner) calls the border:
"before!" or "now!" The border is the lesson; the acting is
just its clothes.
