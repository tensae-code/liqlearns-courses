# Going to: later — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Sentence · Lesson: Going to: later
Built from: Bolivia, the ministry teacher compendium (the BE
GOING TO full table, its use note, and the plans interviews)
— Mongolia, English VI (the will box) — Ghana, the NaCCA
curricula (scheduled future; future-form questions).

## Do it first
Say three true things about tomorrow — things already
decided: you will wake somewhere, go somewhere, eat
something. In your own language those sentences lean FORWARD
— decided now, happening later. English leans them with one
frame you can already half-build, because it is made of be —
which you own — plus the words **going to**: "I **am going
to** visit my grandmother." Unit 3's line is now complete in
your hands: NOW (present actions), BEFORE (past actions),
LATER (this lesson).

## Then the explanation
**The frame.** Bolivia's book tables it whole — affirmative,
negative, interrogative, short answers, for all persons, on
the verb *visit*: **subject + be + going to + verb**.
"I am going to visit." "She **is** going to visit." "They
**are** going to visit." Be changes by the who — am, is, are
— exactly as it did in the right-now face; after going to,
the verb stands at its **base**: visit, go, study — never
"going to visits," never "going to went." Going to holds
the future mark; the verb stays plain. Its own completion
exercise runs the frame on real plans: "My parents **are
going to go** shopping on Sunday."

**Its two jobs.** The book's note names them: **a future
idea, an intention, or a prediction.**
1. **Plans and intentions** — decided BEFORE speaking: "He's
   going to study at UMSA." (Bolivia's example.) "We are
   going to play on Saturday." The plan exists already; the
   sentence reports it.
2. **Predictions you can already see coming** — decided by
   the signs, not by you: "Look at those clouds. It **is
   going to rain**." "Watch — the baby is going to cry."
   The future is visible in the present; going to says so.

**Asking and denying.** Questions swap be to the front —
"**Are** you going to study?" "What **is** she going to
do?" — and short answers ride be: "Yes, I am." "No, they
aren't." Negatives put not after be: "He **isn't** going to
come." Bolivia follows the table with interviews about
future plans — the frame exists to be asked with.

**The neighbours, named honestly.** Two other futures stand
near, and the books keep them apart. **Will** (Mongolia's
box: will/won't + base) is the future of the speaking moment
— decisions made WHILE speaking ("The phone is ringing —
I'll answer it," Bolivia's note: spontaneous decision),
promises, and offers. **The scheduled present** (Ghana) tells
timetable futures in the plain present: "The bus leaves
early tomorrow." This lesson plants going to whole; will
has its own deeper home later. Just keep the border: decided
before → going to; decided now → will; printed on a
timetable → plain present.

**The clue words.** Later announces itself like the past
did: **tomorrow, tonight, next (week/year), soon, later**.
Where a past sentence reaches for yesterday, a going-to
sentence reaches for tomorrow — the line Unit 3 has been
drawing all along, now walked in both directions.

## Say it to your companion
The plans interview — Bolivia's own exercise form. Five
questions about the weekend, each opening differently:
"What are you going to do on Saturday?" "Who are you going
to visit?" "Where are you going to go?" Answers whole and
TRUE — plans you really hold. Then swap, and the new asker
must add one sky-signs prediction about the weekend weather,
from the signs outside the window right now.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write tomorrow in four going-to sentences — true plans,
   all different whos (I, he/she, we, they) so be changes
   four times.
2. Write next week in two more.
3. Give two sky-signs predictions about today (clouds,
   wind, a queue, a pot on the fire — real signs you can
   see).
4. Ask a friend three going-to questions about their
   weekend; write their plans whole.
