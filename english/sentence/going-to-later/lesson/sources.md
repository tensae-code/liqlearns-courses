**Acceptance count: taught in 7 countries (Bolivia, Ghana,
Mongolia, Laos, China, Iran, Sierra Leone).** Bolivia tables going to whole and names its
jobs; Mongolia boxes the will neighbour; Ghana keeps the
scheduled present as the third future; China adds be going
to as a full system with its time words, will worked for
plans and promises, and the two futures set side by side in
one table; Iran tables will whole and sets be going to
beside it as its own table, then speaks the two as one
plans strategy; Sierra Leone teaches will/shall and
going to in one JSS pair, then gives going to its own
senior lesson for plans and immediate-future conclusions.
Indonesia was checked and adds nothing here: no future
form is taught in any of its read books — will/going to
occur only incidentally inside other texts (see
english/books/indonesia.md, honest negatives). Other finished
countries join as their books are read (see
READING-QUEUE.md).

**Laos Grade 7 added 2026-10-27** — *English for Secondary
Schools, Book Two* (MoES/RIES; full read in
english/books/laos.md). Laos's first going-to system is also
its fullest: the complete **be going to** table with short
answers, plans spoken off a work plan, a weekend and a holiday
(Unit 2, pp.14–26). One honesty note, filed with it: **will**
appears in this book only in untabled use — offers and one
prediction model — and is never taught as a system; the course
does not stock a Laos will from it.

**Laos Grade 9 added 2026-10-27** — *English for Secondary
Schools, Book Four* (MoES/RIES, 2013; full read in
english/books/laos.md). Laos divides the future for the first
time, in one Grammar note (p.131): **be going to for decided
plans** against **will with maybe / probably / I think / I
guess for the undecided** — the two futures side by side,
named by how sure the speaker is.

**Laos Grade 11 added 2026-10-27** — *English 6 for Lao
Secondary Schools* (MoES/RIES, 2015; full read in
english/books/laos.md). The series' clearest contrast note on
the two futures stands here (p.12): **will** against **be
going to**, set side by side — sharpening the decided/undecided
split Book Four first named.
