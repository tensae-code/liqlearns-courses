# Lesson plan — Going to: later

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Going to: later
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 12, "Now, Before, Later" — the third lesson of the level (CURRICULUM.md).
Building it completes Level 12.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** talking about LATER
with **be going to + verb** — plans and intentions already
decided, and predictions you can already see coming — with
be changing by the who, questions by the swap, negatives by
not.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Present actions
(be's forms and the swap) and Past actions (the time line
this lesson completes: now — before — later). It needs
nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown changing — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Present actions; Past actions.
  Recommended, never forced.
- Will is this lesson's named neighbour — the future of
  decisions made at the speaking moment and of promises —
  it gets its own deeper treatment later; here the learner
  must only not confuse the two. Ghana's scheduled-future
  simple present ("The bus leaves early tomorrow") is named
  as a third small neighbour, honestly, not taught.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say plans and intentions whole: "I am going to visit my
   grandmother." "They are going to go shopping on Sunday"
   (Bolivia's own exercise sentence).
2. Change be by the who (am/is/are) and keep the verb at
   base after going to — never "going to visits."
3. Ask and deny: "Are you going to study?" "He isn't going
   to come." Short answers ride be.
4. Read the sky-signs predictions: "Look at the clouds —
   it is going to rain."

## What you need
A real tomorrow to plan. Paper and a pencil; a partner for
the plans interview.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner says three true things
   about tomorrow in their own language — things already
   decided — and hears English's decided-future shape
   (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the frame (be + going to + verb);
   the two jobs (plans/intentions; seen-coming predictions —
   Bolivia's own note: "future idea, intention or
   prediction"); negatives and questions; the neighbours
   (will; the scheduled present) (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the frame part: a picture of a path drawn
     from TODAY to TOMORROW with a figure already walking it
     — the sentence builds under the path: SHE · IS · GOING
     TO · VISIT — the GOING TO block glowing like the path
     itself, 1–2 seconds — the student says two plans as the
     figure walks.
   - ADD IMAGE under predictions: a picture of dark clouds
     over a dry field, a boy looking up — the sentence "It is
     going to rain" printed under it → the student gives two
     more seen-coming predictions for the picture (the wind,
     the washing on the line).
   - ADD AUDIO in the questions part: a voice running
     Bolivia's plans interview ("What are you going to do on
     Sunday?") — the student answers with their real Sunday,
     whole.
3. **Say it to your companion** — the plans interview, as
   Bolivia's book runs it: five going-to questions about the
   weekend; answers whole and true (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed —
     tomorrow planned aloud, in the super-simple voice (made
     later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served
   at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — write tomorrow and next week in six
   going-to sentences; ask a friend three and write their
   plans (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- "I going to visit" → be is the engine of the frame; no be,
  no going-to. Am/is/are first — the Present actions map of
  be carries it.
- "going to visits / going to went" → after going to, the
  verb stands at BASE — going to holds the future mark,
  like did held the past. One mark per sentence, always.
- Uses going to for a decision made THIS second ("The phone
  is ringing — I'm going to answer it," decided now) → that
  is will's home ground (Bolivia's note: spontaneous
  decision). Name the border kindly: decided BEFORE speaking
  → going to; decided WHILE speaking → will.
- This lesson completes Level 12. Next: Level 13 — Joining and
  Describing (and/but; describing words inside sentences;
  mine and yours).
