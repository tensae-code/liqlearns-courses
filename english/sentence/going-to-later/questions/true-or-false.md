# Going to: later — true or false

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — The Frame · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — The Two Jobs · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — The Borders.
Say true or false. If it is false, say why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** "I going to visit" is a whole frame.
**False** — be is the engine: "I AM going to visit."

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** After going to, the verb takes -s for
he/she/it.
**False** — the verb stands at BASE for every who: "She is
going to visit," never "visits." Going to holds the mark.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** A prediction needs a visible sign in this
lesson.
**True** — going to predicts what can already be seen
coming (the clouds). Blind guesses are will's looser home.

**4. (Sub lesson 3)** "The train leaves at six tomorrow" is a
mistake because the future needs going to.
**False** — timetable futures wear the plain present
(Ghana). Three futures, three homes.

**5. (Sub lesson 2)** Short answers to going-to questions ride
be: "Yes, I am."
**True** — the swap's family: be asks, be answers.

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** Going to and will mean exactly the same
thing, always.
**False** — decided before → going to; decided while
speaking → will. The books keep the border.
