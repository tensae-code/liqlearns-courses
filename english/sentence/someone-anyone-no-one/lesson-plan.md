# Lesson plan — Someone, anyone, no one

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Someone, anyone, no one
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 27, "The Small Words
That Point" — the third lesson of the level (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the indefinite
pronouns as ONE grid — **some- / any- / no- / every-**
crossed with **-one/-body** (people), **-thing**
(things) and **-where** (places): someone, anyone, no
one, everyone; something, anything, nothing,
everything; somewhere, anywhere, nowhere, everywhere —
chosen by the same some/any law as the quantifiers.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Quantifiers
(some/any comes straight back, now wearing people).
Reflexive pronouns closes the level after it — the grid
words return there (do it yourself).
**What the learner can do at the end:** build all
sixteen grid words from the two axes; choose some- in
positive sentences and offers, any- in questions and
negatives; say nobody/nothing/nowhere without a second
negative; and answer with the short forms (No one.
Nothing. Nowhere.).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Quantifiers. Recommended, never
  forced — this lesson stands alone for a learner who
  lands on it directly (DIRECT-ENTRY rule); the
  some/any law is restated here.
- Speaker convention: the explanation is read in the
  learner's main language; the TARGET forms (someone,
  anyone, no one, everybody…) are never translated and
  are read in the English voice.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a grid, four rows (SOME,
   ANY, NO, EVERY) against three doors (a person, a
   thing, a place), sixteen cells glowing.** Written
   slot: "a picture of the grid — every word is one row
   through one door." What the student does: the
   student points at three cells and builds their words
   before the grid is taught.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of an empty chair at a full
   table, a plate set before it.** Written slot: "a
   picture of NO ONE — the place is ready, the person
   is not there." What the student does: the student
   answers the picture's question: "Who is sitting
   there?" — "No one."
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of a knock at a door at night,
   a speech bubble asking IS ANYONE HOME?** Written
   slot: "a picture of a question-word wearing any-."
   What the student does: the student shouts the
   question, then answers it both ways: "Yes — someone
   is home." "No — no one is home."

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first**: the knock at the door — who is
   there? The learner's language answers; English's
   grid is the lesson.
2. **The two axes**: some/any/no/every × one/thing/
   where. Bolivia's Year 6 book prints a SOMEONE –
   ANYONE box (Theme 6): someone with positive
   statements or questions expecting a positive answer
   ("Someone ate the cake", "Does someone want
   pizza?"); anyone with negative statements and
   questions.
3. **The some/any law, on people**: the Quantifiers law
   unchanged — some in positive and offers, any in
   questions and negatives.
4. **The no- family stands alone**: no one/nothing/
   nowhere carry the negative themselves — the verb
   stays positive ("Nobody came", never "Nobody didn't
   come"); Cambodia's Grade 12 book works indefinite
   pronouns (something, anything, anyone, everybody)
   inside its child-exploitation unit (Unit 24, printed
   pp.129–134).
5. **Every- — the full set**: everyone/everything/
   everywhere, with a singular verb ("Everyone is
   here"); Mongolia's English X prints INDEFINITE
   PRONOUNS as a discovery set — everyone/someone/
   everything/something — in its Good Citizen unit
   (Unit 9, p.136).
6. **The full list, with referents**: South Africa's
   HL Grade 6 Workbook 2 teaches indefinite pronouns
   as a full list, with referents — "an indefinite
   pronoun refers to a non-specified person or thing"
   (printed p.9).
7. **Do it yourself**: the three closing tasks.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck building a word: name the door first (person,
  thing or place), then the row (some, any, no, every).
  The word is the crossing; there is nothing else to
  memorise.
- Stuck doubling a negative: if the grid word already
  says no (no one, nothing, nowhere), the verb must
  stay positive. One no per sentence.
- This lesson's next door: Reflexive pronouns (same
  level) — the words that point BACK at the doer.
