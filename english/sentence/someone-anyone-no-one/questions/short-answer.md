# Someone, anyone, no one — short answer

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — The Grid and the Some/Any Law · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — The No Row, Standing Alone · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — The Every Row and the Whole Grid.

Answer in whole sentences — or, where the grid allows, in its own short form.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** A cake has vanished from the kitchen table. Say what must be true, in one sentence.
**Answer: Someone ate the cake. (Bolivia's own box sentence.)** — positive statement, some- row.

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** "What did you do on Sunday?" Answer with the no- row for places: you stayed home all day.
**Answer: I went nowhere. / Nowhere — I stayed home.** — nowhere carries the no; the verb (went) stays positive.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Correct this sentence: "I didn't tell nobody about it."
**Answer: I didn't tell anybody about it. / I told nobody about it.** — one no per sentence: either the verb carries it (didn't + anybody) or the grid word does (nobody).

**4. (Sub lesson 3)** Say one sentence about a full classroom using the every- row, with the right verb.
**Answer: Everyone is listening. (Any every- sentence with a singular verb.)** — everyone takes is, never are.
