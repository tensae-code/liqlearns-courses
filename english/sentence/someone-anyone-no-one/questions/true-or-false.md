# Someone, anyone, no one — true or false

Say true or false. If it is false, say why.

**1. (Level 1)** "Does someone want pizza?" can be right, when the speaker expects a yes.
**Answer: True** — Bolivia's box states it: someone serves questions with positive answers expected.

**2. (Level 2)** "Nothing didn't happen" is a good English sentence.
**Answer: False** — two nos: nothing already carries the negative. Say "Nothing happened."

**3. (Level 3)** "Everyone are here" is correct, because everyone means many people.
**Answer: False** — the every- row takes a singular verb: "Everyone is here" (South Africa's list work).

**4. (Level 3)** The grid has sixteen words: four rows (some/any/no/every) through three doors (people/things/places), with -one/-body twins for people.
**Answer: True** — the whole family is the crossing; South Africa's definition covers it: a non-specified person or thing (HL G6 WB2, printed p.9).
