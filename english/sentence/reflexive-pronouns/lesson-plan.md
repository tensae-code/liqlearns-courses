# Lesson plan — Reflexive pronouns

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Reflexive pronouns
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 27 — "The Small Words
That Point" — the fourth and last lesson of the level
(CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the words that
point BACK at the doer — **myself, yourself, himself,
herself, itself, ourselves, yourselves, themselves** —
in their three jobs: the action returns to the doer
(She cut herself), emphasis (I did it myself), and
**by + reflexive** for alone/unaided (He lives by
himself) — plus **each other / one another**, the
pointing word that goes between two or more, never back.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the pronoun
lessons and closes Unit 27; its each other is the grid
words' partner (someone… each other).
**What the learner can do at the end:** build all eight
reflexives (with the two irregular middles — my-self,
our-selves, them-selves); use them after verbs whose
action returns; add one for emphasis; say alone with
by + reflexive; and split reflexive from each other
without a miss.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Reflexive Pronouns — the Forms and the Returning Action** — the eight forms (myself…themselves, -selves in the plurals) and job one: the action returns — doer and done-to are the same person. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Reflexive Pronouns — Emphasis and By + Reflexive** — job two, emphasis (the reflexive as a spotlight beside the doer or at the sentence's end), and job three, by + reflexive for alone/unaided — the lesson text takes emphasis before by, and both stand in this sub lesson. (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **Reflexive or Each Other?** — each other / one another for the action that goes between people — the crossing arrows, never a reflexive. (Was depth Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the personal pronouns (me, you,
  him… — the reflexives are built on them). Recommended,
  never forced — this lesson stands alone for a learner
  who lands on it directly (DIRECT-ENTRY rule); the
  eight forms are built from scratch here.
- Speaker convention: the explanation is read in the
  learner's main language; the TARGET forms (myself,
  by herself, each other…) are never translated and are
  read in the English voice.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a mirror with a boy looking
   into it, the boy and his reflection doing the same
   action — washing a face.** Written slot: "a picture
   of the reflexive idea — the doer and the done-to are
   the same person." What the student does: the student
   says both names for the reflection before the rule:
   "himself."
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of a small girl tying her own
   shoelaces, tongue out in effort, a grown-up's hands
   held up in surrender beside her.** Written slot: "a
   picture of BY HERSELF — alone, and proud of it."
   What the student does: the student says the sentence
   the picture wants: "She did it by herself."
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of two friends shaking hands,
   each pointing at the OTHER, with two arrows crossing
   between them.** Written slot: "a picture of EACH
   OTHER — the arrows cross; they never bend back."
   What the student does: the student says what the
   friends do for each other, then says what each does
   for himself, and hears the arrows change.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first**: the morning mirror — three things
   the learner does TO THEMSELVES before breakfast,
   in their own language.
2. **The eight forms**: pronoun + self/selves; the
   irregular middles (myself from my, ourselves/
   yourselves/themselves with -selves); Bolivia's Year 6
   book prints the full reflexive set as its Theme 7
   grammar (§1).
3. **Job one — the action returns**: cut yourself,
   teach yourself, enjoy yourselves; Nepal's Grade 6
   book teaches reflexive pronouns inside its Unit 4,
   in the modal unit's company (may/must/should…).
4. **Job two — emphasis**: I did it myself; the queen
   herself.
5. **By + reflexive — alone**: Yemen's Book 6 gathers
   reflexive pronouns in its Language review 10 (p.39),
   the review that also gathers past deduction and
   verb + object + to-infinitive; its safety unit's
   warnings run on reflexives ("You'll cut yourself",
   Pupil's Book 5's set, carried in the review).
6. **Each other / one another**: the crossing arrows;
   Malaysia's Form 5 book works the reflexive set with
   each other / one another at Unit 12.
7. **The split, drilled**: same person → reflexive;
   crossing between persons → each other.
8. **Do it yourself**: the three closing tasks.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck building a form: start from the object pronoun
  (me, you, him, her, it, us, you, them), then fix the
  two rebels — my and our/their plural selves: MYself
  (not meself), OURselves, THEMselves.
- Stuck choosing reflexive vs each other: draw the
  arrow. If it bends back to the doer, reflexive; if it
  crosses to another person, each other.
- This lesson closes Unit 27; the course's next door
  is Unit 28 — Comparing More Deeply.
