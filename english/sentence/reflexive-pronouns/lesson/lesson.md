# Reflexive pronouns — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Sentence · Lesson: Reflexive pronouns
Built from: Bolivia, Year 6 (Theme 7, §1 — the full
reflexive set) — Malaysia, Form 5 (Unit 12) — Nepal,
English Grade 6 (Unit 4) — Yemen, English Course for
Yemen Book 6 (Language review 10, p.39).

## Do it first
Think of your morning in front of the mirror. You
washed your face. You dressed. You may have cut
yourself shaving or nicked yourself on a zip. In your
own language, those sentences carry a word — or a verb
shape — that says the doer and the done-to are the
SAME person. English says it with a family of eight
words ending in -self or -selves, and this lesson is
that family and its three jobs, plus the one word-pair
that must never be confused with it. If you know the
object pronouns (me, you, him…), you hold the family's
building blocks; if not, start here anyway — the forms
are built from scratch below.

## Then the explanation
## Sub lesson 1 — Reflexive Pronouns — the Forms and the Returning Action
**The eight forms.** Object pronoun + **self** (one
person) or **selves** (more than one): **myself,
yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves,
yourselves, themselves**. Two rebels to fix at once:
the first is built on MY, not me — **myself**, never
"meself" — and the plurals wear **-selves**: ourSELVES,
themSELVES. Bolivia's Year 6 book prints the full set
as its Theme 7 grammar (§1: REFLEXIVE PRONOUNS — the
full set), in the same theme that carries ought to and
might have. [a picture of a mirror with a boy looking
into it, the boy and his reflection doing the same
action — washing a face] The reflection's name is the
lesson: himself.

**Job one — the action returns.** When doer and
done-to are one person, English will not repeat the
name or use a plain object pronoun; the reflexive
takes the seat: "She cut **herself**." "He taught
**himself** English." "Enjoy **yourselves**!" "The
cat washed **itself**." Never "She cut her" for her
own finger — her points at a DIFFERENT woman. Nepal's
Grade 6 book teaches reflexive pronouns inside its
Unit 4, in the company of the modal set (may/must/
should/ought to/needn't) — the pointing-back words
learned beside the duty words.

## Sub lesson 2 — Reflexive Pronouns — Emphasis and By + Reflexive
**Job two — emphasis.** A reflexive can stand beside
the doer, or at the sentence's end, just to say NO
HELP, NO DOUBT: "I did it **myself**." "The head
teacher **herself** came to the door." Delete it and
the sentence still stands — it is a spotlight, not a
brick.

**Job three — by + reflexive: alone.** **By himself**
= alone, unaided. "He lives **by himself**." "She
travelled **by herself**." "Did you make it **by
yourself**?" Yemen's Book 6 gathers reflexive
pronouns in its Language review 10 (p.39) — the
review that also gathers past deduction — and the
course's safety warnings run on the returning action:
"You'll cut **yourself**." "You'll poison **yourself**."
[a picture of a small girl tying her own shoelaces,
tongue out in effort, a grown-up's hands held up beside
her] Say the picture's sentence: "She did it by
herself."

## Sub lesson 3 — Reflexive or Each Other?
**Each other / one another — the crossing arrows.**
When the action goes BETWEEN people, English refuses
the reflexive and uses **each other** (or **one
another**, the same thing in a slightly more formal
coat): "They help **each other**." "We write to **one
another**." "The two friends looked at **each other**."
Malaysia's Form 5 book works the reflexive set
together with each other / one another at Unit 12 —
the pair taught as one choice, which is how this
lesson leaves them. The test is an arrow: [a picture
of two friends shaking hands, each pointing at the
OTHER, two arrows crossing between them] If the arrow
bends back to the doer — reflexive. If it crosses to
another person — each other. "They cut **themselves**"
means each cut his own finger; "They cut **each
other**" is a fight. The arrow is the whole law.

## Do it yourself
1. **The morning report.** Say six sentences about
   your real morning, each with a different reflexive
   (yourself is allowed twice if the people change:
   tell a partner's morning too — himself, herself).
   Every arrow must bend back.
2. **Alone and emphasis.** Say one true by-sentence
   (I … by myself) and one emphasis sentence about
   someone you admire (My grandmother herself…). Then
   delete the emphasis reflexive aloud and hear the
   sentence survive, dimmer.
3. **Back or across.** Your companion says six mixed
   sentences about your family or class — half
   reflexive, half each other. You must call "back!"
   or "across!" for each, and repair any sentence
   whose arrow and word disagree. Six clean calls is
   the pass.
