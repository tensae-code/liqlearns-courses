# Phrases

## Do it first

Read this sentence:

"The tall boy in the red shirt kicked the ball into the net."

Its words travel in groups. "The tall boy" goes together.
"In the red shirt" goes together. "Into the net" goes
together. Each group does one job. This lesson is about those
groups.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a phrase is

A **phrase** is a group of words that works as **one piece**
inside a sentence.

- "in the red shirt" — one piece: it tells which boy.
- "into the net" — one piece: it tells where the ball went.

A phrase has **no subject and verb of its own**. That is what
keeps it a phrase: "the tall boy" cannot stand alone as a
sentence, and neither can "into the net". A phrase lives
inside a sentence and does one job there.

Find the phrases: "My little sister / with the broken cup /
ran / to the door." Three phrases ride in that sentence —
which are they?

*Difficulty: first steps — grouping by the job, not by counting words; a phrase can be two words or six.*

## Sub lesson 2 — The kinds of phrases

A phrase is named for the job it does:

- A **noun phrase** does a noun's job — it names the person
  or thing: "**the tall boy** kicked the ball."
- An **adjectival phrase** does an adjective's job — it
  describes: "the boy **in the red shirt**."
- A **verb phrase** does the verb's job — the action words
  together: "She **has been working** all day."
- An **adverbial phrase** does an adverb's job — it tells
  where, when or how: "He kicked it **into the net**."

Same sentence, four kinds at work: "**The tall boy** **in
the red shirt** **has kicked** the ball **into the net**."

*Difficulty: building — naming by the job done, the same test as sub lesson 1, now with four names.*

## Say it to your companion

Explain a phrase to your companion: what it is, why it cannot
stand alone, and the four kinds with one example each. Your
companion gives you a sentence; you pick out its phrases and
name their kinds. Then change places.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write a sentence with exactly two phrases in it. Bracket
   them and name their kinds.
2. Grow "The dog slept" with one phrase of each kind, one
   at a time. Write the sentence at each step.
3. In a page you are reading, find one phrase of each kind
   and copy the four phrases out in a row.
