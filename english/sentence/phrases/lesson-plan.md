# Lesson plan — Phrases

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** Phrases
**Lesson:** phrases — groups of words that work as one piece inside a sentence — and their kinds
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 13 — Joining and Describing
**The one thing:** a phrase is a group of words that does one job together, without being a whole sentence
**This lesson's own level:** Sentence lesson: the phrase itself first, then its four kinds the book names.

## Why this lesson exists

No lesson taught the phrase itself. The course has noun-phrases (one kind), and Sierra Leone's SSS 2 book opens its phrase work with the general lesson — "Types of Phrases" (Term 2, Lesson 51) — before working the kinds one by one. The 2026-11-08 re-verification sweep marked it NEW LESSON NEEDED. Under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06) it gets its own lesson; its source is the handbook lesson named in lesson/sources.md.

## Sub lessons

1. **What a phrase is** — a word-group that works as one piece; it has no subject-and-verb of its own, so it cannot stand as a sentence
2. **The kinds of phrases** — noun phrases, adjectival phrases, verb phrases and adverbial phrases — each named for the job it does

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is forced first — this lesson stands alone: it starts from whole sentences the learner can already build (building-a-sentence helps, and noun-phrases is its nearest neighbour, but neither is required first).

## What the learner can do when it ends
- Say what a phrase is and how it differs from a sentence.
- Find the phrases inside a sentence.
- Name a phrase's kind by the job it does: noun, adjectival, verb, adverbial.
- Build a sentence and grow it with one phrase of each kind.

## What you need
Sentences to take apart — any page of the learner's reading book will do.

## The run of the lesson
1. **Do it first** — the learner looks at "The tall boy in the red shirt kicked the ball into the net" and groups its words into the pieces that work together, before the word "phrase" is taught (lesson/lesson.md).
2. Work through sub lesson 1: what a phrase is.
3. Work through sub lesson 2: the four kinds.
4. ADD IMAGE: one long sentence with its phrases bracketed in four colours, one colour per kind — the student brackets the phrases of a fresh sentence the same way.

## Add-ons
- Phrase hunt: in today's reading, find one phrase of each kind and write the four in a row.
- Grow it: start from "The dog slept." Each partner adds one phrase; the sentence must still stand.

## If the learner is stuck
If a learner calls every word-group a phrase, use the test: does it do ONE job together? If it has its own subject and verb, it is not a phrase — it is on its way to being a clause or a sentence (connectors-and-clauses takes it from there).

## Teacher note
The general lesson rests on the SSS 2 handbook's Lesson 51; its follow-on lessons (52 and 56) are stocked in nouns and adverbs, where their kinds belong — the split is recorded in lesson/sources.md so no item is cited twice.
