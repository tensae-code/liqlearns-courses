# Lesson plan — The Colon

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** The Colon
**Lesson:** the-colon — the mark that opens the door to a list: it goes after "as follows" and "the following"
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 10 — Building a Sentence
**The one thing:** a colon (:) stands just before a list, after the words "as follows" or "the following" — it tells the reader: here comes the list
**This lesson's own level:** Punctuation lesson: the mark first, then the two signal phrases that carry it in real writing.

## Why this lesson exists

No lesson taught the colon. Barbados's Class 4 syllabus requires it by name — "Use a colon after 'as follows' and 'the following'" — in its writing-conventions objectives. The 2026-11-08 re-verification sweep marked it NEW LESSON NEEDED. Under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06) it gets its own lesson; its single source is the syllabus named in lesson/sources.md, curriculum evidence cited as such — Barbados publishes no pupil textbooks.

## Sub lessons

1. **The mark that opens a list** — the colon itself: two dots, one over the other, standing at the end of the words that promise a list
2. **After "as follows" and "the following"** — the two signal phrases, written and punctuated in real sentences with real lists

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is forced first — this lesson stands alone: it needs only sentences the learner can already write. The capital-and-full-stop lesson helps but is not required first.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- Name the colon and say what job it does.
- Write "as follows:" and "the following:" with the colon in place.
- Write a short list after the colon, with commas between the items.
- Spot a missing colon in a sentence that promises a list.

## What you need
Something to write with.

## The run of the lesson
1. **Do it first** — the learner writes the sentence "You need the following things for school" and tries to finish it with a list, before the mark is taught (lesson/lesson.md).
2. Work through sub lesson 1: the mark that opens a list.
3. Work through sub lesson 2: after "as follows" and "the following".
4. ADD AUDIO: the two signal phrases read aloud with a pause where the colon stands — the learner hears the colon as a stop-and-wait.

## Add-ons
- Classroom lists dictation: one learner reads a list sentence aloud, colon pause and all; the partner writes it, colon in place.
- Notice hunt: learners look through their own exercise books for "as follows" and "the following" and check whether the colon stands there.

## If the learner is stuck
If a learner puts the colon in the middle of a list, the promise words have been missed — go back to the two signal phrases and let the colon ride only on them until the pattern holds.
