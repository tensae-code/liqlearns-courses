# The Colon

## Do it first

Write this sentence and finish it: "For school you need the
following things" — then write the things: a book, a pencil,
a bag.

Look at the place where the promise ends and the list
begins. Something belongs there — a mark that tells the
reader: stop, the list is coming now. Here it is.

## Sub lesson 1 — The mark that opens a list

This is a **colon**:  **:**   Two dots, one standing over
the other.

The colon has one job in this lesson: it **opens the door
to a list**. It stands at the end of the words that promise
a list, and it says to the reader: *here comes the list.*

> You need the following things for school**:** a book, a
> pencil, a bag.

See where it stands — after the promise, before the first
thing in the list. When you read it aloud, your voice stops
and waits a moment at the colon. That stop is the colon
working.

*Difficulty: first steps — one mark, one job, always in the same place: after the promise, before the list.*

## Sub lesson 2 — After "as follows" and "the following"

English has two promise phrases that carry the colon.
Barbados's Class 4 syllabus names them both. The colon goes
**after** them:

> The rules are as follows**:** we listen, we share, we
> clean up.
>
> Bring the following**:** a cup, a spoon, a plate.

Work through what each sentence does:

1. The promise: "The rules are as follows" / "Bring the
   following".
2. The colon — the door opens.
3. The list itself: the items in a line, a **comma**
   between them, a **full stop** at the end.

Now check a broken one: "The rules are as follows we
listen, we share, we clean up." The promise runs straight
into the list — the door never opens. Put the colon back
and read it again. Hear the stop? That stop is how you know
the colon is home.

*Difficulty: building — the colon rides on its two signal phrases; the list after it keeps its commas and its full stop.*

## Say it to your companion

Explain the colon to your companion: what the mark looks
like, the one job it does, and the two phrases it follows.
Then dictate one list sentence to them — and check their
colon, not only their list.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write two sentences with "as follows:" — one about class
   rules, one about the steps of making tea. Give each its
   list.
2. Write two sentences with "the following:" — the things
   in your bag, and the things a farmer needs.
3. Read all four aloud. At every colon, stop and wait —
   then read the list.
