# Lesson plan — Present actions

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Present actions
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 12 — Now, Before, Later — the first lesson of the unit (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** saying what happens in
the present — its two faces: the EVERY-DAY face (routines
and truths: the plain verb, with -s for he/she/it) and the
RIGHT-NOW face (be + verb-ing), and knowing which face a
sentence is wearing.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Verbs lesson
(the doing words and their -s) and on every routine sentence
the learner has met since Level 1 — this lesson names the
system those sentences were already using. It needs nothing
else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown changing — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds, and now-actions are
movement itself.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The Every-Day Face** — build routine and truth sentences with the plain verb, the -s standing guard on every he/she/it, and ask them with the engine. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **The Right-Now Face** — build be + -ing sentences whole (both parts showing), sew the -ing spellings right (running, making), and ask them with the swap. (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **Choosing the Face** — read the clue words and pick the face every time, refuse the -ing coat for the verbs that refuse it, and retell one scene in both faces. (Was depth Level 3. The questions-on-both-faces paragraph stands at the end of the text and is kept in this sub lesson.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Verbs; Asking yes/no questions (its
  machinery asks both faces). Recommended, never forced.
- Before (the past) and Later (going to) are this level's
  next two lessons — the same verb wearing its other times.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say every-day truths and routines with the plain verb —
   and the -s for he/she/it: "Lemons are sour." "Mita gets
   up at six." "He walks to school."
2. Say what is happening RIGHT NOW with be + verb-ing: "I am
   reading." "She is cooking." "They are playing."
3. Ask and answer in both faces (swap/engine on the simple;
   the be-swap on the -ing face): "Does he walk?" "Is she
   cooking?"
4. Choose the face by the clue words: every day, usually,
   always → every-day face; now, look!, listen! → right-now
   face (Libya's reference names the uses outright).

## What you need
A window or a room where things are happening. Paper and a
pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner says what people in sight
   are doing RIGHT NOW, then what those same people do EVERY
   DAY; their own language keeps the two apart without
   thinking — the lesson gives English's two coats
   (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the every-day face and its -s; the
   right-now face and its making (-ing spellings the books
   box: run→running, make→making); the clue words; questions
   on both faces (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the right-now part: a picture of a boy
     running in place, his sentence building under him —
     HE · IS · RUNN–ING — the -ING block bouncing as his legs
     move, 1–2 seconds — the student says the sentence with
     the bounce, then freezes it into the every-day face:
     "He runs."
   - ADD IMAGE under the clue words: a picture of two
     calendars — one marked EVERY DAY with a sun that repeats,
     one marked NOW with a single shining moment → the
     student points each pictured sentence to its calendar.
   - ADD AUDIO in the questions part: a voice asking pairs —
     "Does he walk to school?" / "Is he walking now?" — the
     student answers each in its own face, whole.
3. **Say it to your companion** — narrate your companion for
   one minute (right-now face), then interview them about
   their every-day face (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the
     two faces shown on one boy's day, in the super-simple
     voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served
   at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — write your day twice: five every-day
   sentences, then five right-now sentences about THIS moment
   (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- -s dropped in speech ("He walk") → the Verbs lesson's
  ladder: the -s is small in sound and big in law; he/she/it
  never travel without it in the every-day face.
- -ing on a truth ("Lemons are being sour") → Libya's own
  warning list: some verbs refuse the -ing coat — be, like,
  know, see, understand. Truths and feelings wear the plain
  face.
- Double -ing errors ("is reads") → the right-now face has
  TWO parts and both must show: be + -ing. One without the
  other is half a coat.
- run → runing → the spelling boxes: short verbs double
  their last letter (run→running, swim→swimming); e drops
  (make→making). The books box these; drill the pair, not
  the rule-list.
- Next in this level: Past actions — the same verbs, before.
