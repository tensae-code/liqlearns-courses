# Present actions — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Sentence · Lesson: Present actions
Built from: Egypt, English Language Primary 3 (the Daily
Routines lesson and its Present Simple box) — Mongolia,
English VI (the present continuous table and questions box)
— Libya, the grammar reference filed with its books (Unit 1:
present simple and present continuous, their uses) — Ghana,
the NaCCA curricula (Action Words: the simple present).

## Do it first
Look around and say two things about one person in sight:
what they are doing RIGHT NOW, and what they do EVERY DAY.
"He is drinking tea." "He drinks tea every morning." Your
own language kept those apart without asking you. English
keeps them apart with two different coats on the verb — and
you have been meeting both coats since the first routines.
This lesson names them.

## Then the explanation

## Sub lesson 1 — The Every-Day Face

**The every-day face — the present simple.** For things that
happen again and again, and for truths that simply stand,
the verb wears its plain form: "I wake up at 7." "We go to
school at 8." Egypt's routines box gives this face its full
spelling lesson, and Ghana's curriculum names its jobs:
**habitual actions and simple truths** — "Lemons are sour."
The one law to keep from the Verbs lesson: with he, she, or
it, the verb takes **-s**: "Mita get**s** up at six." "He
walk**s** to school." "The cow eat**s** grass." Libya's
reference states this face's use in four words: **facts and
habits**.

## Sub lesson 2 — The Right-Now Face

**The right-now face — be + verb-ing.** For what is
happening NOW, around now, the verb wears be + -ing:
Mongolia's table builds it whole — subject + be + verb-ing:
"I **am reading**." "She **is cooking**." "They **are
playing**." Libya's reference names its uses: **happening
now / around now, changing situations**. The -ing coat has
its sewing rules, which the books box: most verbs just take
-ing (read→reading); a short verb doubles its last letter
(run→**running**, swim→swimming); a verb ending in e drops
it (make→**making**, write→writing).

## Sub lesson 3 — Choosing the Face

**Choosing the face.** The sentence itself usually carries
the clue: *every day, always, usually, on Fridays* → the
every-day face. *Now, right now, look!, listen!* → the
right-now face: "Listen! The baby **is crying**." And
Libya's reference posts the warning the exams love: **some
verbs refuse the -ing coat — be, like, know, see,
understand.** "I know the answer" never becomes "I am
knowing." Truths, feelings, and knowing wear the plain face
even when they are true right now.

**Questions on both faces.** You own both machines already.
The every-day face asks with the engine: "Does he walk to
school?" "Do you wake up at 7?" The right-now face asks with
the swap, because be is already standing there: "Is she
cooking?" "Are you reading?" — Mongolia's questions box
drills exactly that swap. Two faces, two question machines,
and you built both in Unit 2.

## Say it to your companion
Narrate and interview. For one minute, narrate everything
your companion does, right-now face, live: "You are sitting.
You are smiling. Now you are writing." Then interview their
every-day face: "Do you wake up early? Does your mother cook
on Fridays?" Five questions. Swap roles — and the narrator
must catch one -ing spelling aloud (running, making) and
say how it was sewn.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write five every-day sentences about your family's day —
   he/she/it in at least three of them, -s standing guard.
2. Write five right-now sentences about this exact moment —
   you, the room, the sounds outside.
3. Rewrite two of your every-day sentences as right-now
   ones, and one right-now sentence as an every-day truth.
4. Write one question for each face and answer both whole.
