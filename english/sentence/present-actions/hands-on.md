# Hands-on — Present actions

The lesson lived in a real room, in real time.

## Live narration
Watch a person (or a street through a window) for two
minutes and narrate, aloud, in the right-now face: "A man is
crossing. He is carrying a bag. Now he is waiting." A partner
ticks a mark for every full be + -ing that lands. Ten clean
sentences finish the round.

## The same person, two faces
Pick one person you know well. On paper, two columns: EVERY
DAY and RIGHT NOW. Fill the left with five truths about their
routines; fill the right — by looking, phoning, or picturing —
with five things they are doing now. Read both columns aloud;
the faces must never borrow each other's coats.

## Clue-word snap
Cards with clue phrases: every day, now, on Fridays, listen!,
usually, right now, always, look! A caller reads a half
sentence ("She ___ to school"); slap the right clue card and
finish the sentence in the face the clue demands. The pair
(clue + face) must agree — that agreement is the game.

## Freeze and thaw
Mime an action while a partner narrates you in the right-now
face. At "FREEZE!", stop mid-move; the narrator switches to
the every-day face and tells your habit ("He runs every
morning"). Thaw, new action. The switch — not the mime — is
what is being practised.
