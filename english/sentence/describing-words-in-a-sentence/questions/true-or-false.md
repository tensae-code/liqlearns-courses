# Describing words in a sentence — true or false

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Home One: Before the Noun · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Home Two: After Be · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Moving and Stacking · except item 6, which asks stacking two describers before a noun — Sub lesson 3's work — and is Sub lesson 3.
Say true or false. If it is false, say why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** In English the describing word stands AFTER
its noun: "a mango fresh."
**False** — before: "a fresh mango." Every one of
Bangladesh's eight pairs seats it first.

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** "The mango fresh" is a finished sentence.
**False** — home two needs its verb: "The mango IS fresh."

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** "The flowers smell nice" is the same home
as "The mango is fresh."
**True** — noun + linking verb + description; smell carries
the description as be does (Mongolia).

**4. (Sub lesson 3)** Moving a truth between homes changes what
is true.
**False** — the mango stays fresh; only the sentence's shape
changes — packing the description in, or announcing it.

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** "A bigger mango" belongs to this lesson's
plain seatings.
**False** — bigger is a COMPARING shape; Mongolia's own box
sends comparing to its own discovery. Today: big, seated
plain.

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** Two describing words can share one noun:
"a new, clean shirt."
**True** — stacked before the noun, comma between equals.
