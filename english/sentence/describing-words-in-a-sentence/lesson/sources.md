**Acceptance count: taught in 4 countries (Bangladesh,
Egypt, Mongolia, Laos).** Bangladesh pictures the before-home in
eight pairs; Mongolia names the describing law, writes the
sense-verb home, and posts the comparing border; Egypt's
youngest books seat description after be from the first
year. Other finished countries join as their books are read
(see READING-QUEUE.md).

**Laos Grade 4 added 2026-10-27** — *English for Lao Primary
Schools, Student's Book 2, Grade 4* (MoES/RIES, 2012; full read
in english/books/laos.md). Two Laos layers. Describing people:
**"He's tall / She's short," old and young**, with the opposites
printed as ≠ pairs — "old ≠ young · tall ≠ short" (pp.44–45).
And the book's first describing word BEFORE the thing it
describes: shapes taught first, then colour + shape together —
**"What shape is it? — It's a cross. / What colour is it? —
It's red. / What is it? — It's a red cross"** (p.117), the set
worked over six shapes (p.118: a purple triangle, a blue
rectangle, a yellow circle, a white cube, a red cylinder, an
orange cross — a/an chosen by the colour's first sound).

**Laos Grade 7 added 2026-10-27** — *English for Secondary
Schools, Book Two* (MoES/RIES; full read in
english/books/laos.md). Comparing arrives as a full system:
**-er / -est**, the spelling rules (hot → hotter, fat →
fatter, heavy → heavier), **more / the most** for long words,
and the irregular set good/better/best, bad/worse/worst,
much/more/most (Unit 9, pp.116–123), worked over people, class
surveys, and two cities compared in a text (pp.124–126).

**Laos Grade 8 added 2026-10-27** — *English for Secondary
Schools, Book Three* (MoES/RIES, 2012; full read in
english/books/laos.md). The comparing system returns over
real data: an **eight-country data table** — size, population,
distances — compared in sentences (pp.74–77) and finished as a
two-paragraph written comparison (p.78).

**Laos Grade 10 added 2026-10-27** — *English 5 for Lao
Secondary Schools* (MoES/RIES, 2014; full read in
english/books/laos.md). The **superlative gets its own full
treatment** — usage and formation rules together (pp.113–114)
— completing the comparing system Book Two opened.
