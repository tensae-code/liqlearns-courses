# Hands-on — Describing words in a sentence

The lesson done on the real things around you.

## The eight pairs, live
Bangladesh's page, rebuilt in your room: find (or draw) two
mangoes-or-anythings that differ — fresh/bad, clean/dirty,
new/old, happy/angry for a pet or a picture. Label each with
its pair, word before thing, spoken and written. Eight
labels finish the set.

## Home-swap relay
Cards carry truths in home one ("a clean shirt"). A player
reads one; the next must answer with the same truth in home
two ("The shirt is clean") before the count of five. Miss
the be, drop the word after the noun — the card returns to
the pile. The pile shrinking is the score.

## Sense walk
Walk the kitchen or the garden with a partner. At each stop,
one sense-sentence, whole: "The flowers smell sweet." "The
soup tastes hot." Five stops each, senses alternating. Back
at the table, write them — home two must keep its verbs
standing.

## Two-word portrait
Portrait of a person you love, in six sentences, every
sentence carrying a describing word, homes mixed, at least
one stack ("her kind, tired hands") and one but-turn. Read
it to them if you can — portraits are for giving away.
