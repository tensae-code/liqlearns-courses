# Lesson plan — Describing words in a sentence

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Describing words in a sentence
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 13 — Joining and Describing — the second lesson of the unit (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** where a describing
word (an adjective) SITS in a sentence — its two homes:
BEFORE the noun it describes ("a fresh mango"), and AFTER
be, describing the subject ("The mango is fresh") — and how
the two homes say the same truth from two sides.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Adjectives
lesson (what describing words are — Level 2) and Building a
sentence. Level 2 named the words; this lesson seats them.
It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown changing — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Home One: Before the Noun** — seat any describing word directly before its noun, in pairs and inside whole sentences, in Bangladesh's own pictured order. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Home Two: After Be** — describe subjects across be with the verb standing (never "The mango fresh"), and land descriptions after the sense verbs (smell, taste) the same way. (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **Moving and Stacking** — move any truth between the two homes without breaking it, stack two describers before a noun with their comma, and keep comparing forms parked for their own lesson. (Was depth Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Adjectives (Level 2); Joining with and
  and but. Recommended, never forced.
- Comparing (bigger, the biggest) is NOT here — Mongolia
  seats comparatives as their own discovery ("adjectives
  describe nouns; comparing takes comparative forms"). This
  lesson keeps the plain describing word and seats it whole.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Place a describing word before its noun in any sentence:
   "a clean shirt," "an old bike," "the happy cat"
   (Bangladesh's own pictured pairs).
2. Describe after be: "The shirt is clean." "The flowers
   smell nice" (the sense-verb cousin Mongolia writes).
3. Move a truth between the two homes without breaking it:
   "a fresh mango" ↔ "The mango is fresh."
4. Stack two describing words before a noun in the order
   the ear likes, and join described things with and/but
   (the unit's own joiners at work).

## What you need
Things in the room worth describing. Paper and a pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner hears "a mango" and "a fresh
   mango" and says what the second picture gives that the
   first one lacks (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the before-home; the after-be home;
   moving between them; stacking; the sense-verb cousin
   (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the homes part: a picture of the word FRESH
     travelling — first landing BEFORE a mango picture,
     then sliding after THE MANGO IS — the sentence
     rebuilding under each landing, 1–2 seconds — the
     student says both sentences as the word travels.
   - ADD IMAGE under the before-home: a picture of
     Bangladesh's own eight pairs — a fresh mango / a bad
     mango, a clean shirt / a dirty shirt, a new bike / an
     old bike, a happy cat / an angry cat → the student
     covers the words and describes each picture with its
     pair, then uncovers to check.
   - ADD AUDIO in the stacking part: a voice describing
     room things in both homes, in pairs — the student
     repeats each pair and swaps the home on the third.
3. **Say it to your companion** — describe-and-guess: one
   describes a classmate's thing using both homes; the other
   points and names it (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the
     travelling word, in the super-simple voice (made later,
     when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served
   at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — describe your room in six sentences,
   three per home; describe one food with a but-turn
   (lesson/lesson.md).

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- GIF — a picture of the word FRESH travelling between its
  two homes → the student says both sentences as it moves.
- IMAGE — a picture of the eight pictured pairs → the
  student describes each picture, then checks.
- AUDIO — descriptions read in home-pairs → the student
  repeats and swaps the home.
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later) → the student
  describes along with it.

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the
plan is the build sheet, so nothing the lesson needs gets forgotten.

## If the learner is stuck
- Puts the word after the noun ("a mango fresh") → their
  other language may seat it there; English's before-home is
  fixed. The GIF's landing order is the drill: word first,
  thing after.
- Forgets be in the second home ("The mango fresh") → the
  after-home is a whole sentence, and a sentence needs its
  verb: be is the verb carrying the description. Who + be +
  word — the Building-a-sentence frame, wearing description.
- Compares when not asked ("a bigger mango") → comparing is
  a real next step (Mongolia's own next box) — praise it,
  park it, finish this lesson's plain seatings first.
- Next, and last in this level: Mine and yours.
