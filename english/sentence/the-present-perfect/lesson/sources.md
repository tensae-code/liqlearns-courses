**Acceptance count: taught in 6 countries (Egypt,
Libya, Laos, China, Iran, Sierra Leone).** Libya introduces the tense at Grade 8 with the
song and the grammar box, guards the no-exact-time rule in
its Teacher's Book, sets the ever/never/yet/already box at
Grade 9, and states the whole entry again in its Grammar
Reference. Egypt teaches the same tense at Primary 6 with
the full structure table and the two uses (still going;
result we can see). China witnesses it at review level
only — its Grade 9 book's new perfect teaching is the
past perfect, not this tense. Iran tables the tense whole
at Vision 2 with since/for/yet/ever and runs its speaking
strategy on Have you ever …?; Sierra Leone teaches it
three times up the ladder — JSS1 (still-true and
no-specific-time uses), JSS3 (for/since), SSS1 (the four
time words ever/never/already/yet). Indonesia was checked
for this tense on 2026-11-03 and never teaches it (its
books' own honest negatives say so at Grades 6 and 9).
Other closed countries join as their
books are read (see READING-QUEUE.md).

**Laos Grade 8 added 2026-10-27** — *English for Secondary
Schools, Book Three* (MoES/RIES, 2012; full read in
english/books/laos.md). Laos gives the tense a whole unit
(Unit 4): **just** first (pp.38–39), the full regular and
irregular **participle tables** with **gone to / been to**
(p.42), **for** and **How long have you…?** (pp.43–45),
**since** with the series' first real definition of the tense
(pp.46–47), and **ever / never** returning later at the
doctor's (pp.136–140). The honest limit, filed with it: Laos
teaches this tense whole but **never teaches the present
perfect continuous** — that lesson draws nothing from Laos.

**Laos Grade 10 added 2026-10-27** — *English 5 for Lao
