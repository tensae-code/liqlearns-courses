# Lesson plan — The present perfect

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** The present perfect
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 18 — Deeper Sentences —
the first lesson of the unit (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the present perfect —
have/has + the past participle — the tense that ties a past
action to NOW: just happened, result visible, still going,
or lived experience — and its bright line: it never carries
an exact finished time.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Past actions
(Level 12 — the -ed coat and the movers come straight back
as participles) and on Present actions (the now it ties
to). It is the first of the deeper sentence machinery;
the present perfect continuous, the passive, and
conditionals follow it inside this level.
**What the learner can do at the end:** build the tense for
every who (statement, not-form, question, short answer);
name its four jobs; place ever, never, already, yet and
just; and switch to the past simple the moment an exact
time appears.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The Present Perfect's Shape** — have/has + the past
   participle, the regulars and the movers, shrunk forms
   included. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **The Job — the Tie to Now** — just happened inside
   the open stretch, a result you can see now, and
   started-then-still-going with for and since. (Was
   depth Level 2.)
3. **The Travel Words and the Bright Line** — ever,
   never, already, yet and just in their seats; no exact
   finished time, ever — that switches the sentence to
   the past simple. The Asking close (question form and
   short answers) also stands here, at the explanation's
   end. (Was depth Level 3.)
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Past actions (the participle IS the
  past shape, worn after have/has) and Building a sentence.
  Recommended, never forced.
- The present perfect CONTINUOUS (I've been learning…) is
  a different thing — the next lesson in this level. Here
  the participle only, no -ing form after been.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a GIF (1–2 seconds) of a window being wiped
   clean, ending spotless.** Written slot: "a picture of a
   dirty window becoming clean, then the words I'VE CLEANED
   THE WINDOW." What the student does: before reading on,
   the student says what they can SEE now — the result —
   because that visible result is this tense's home.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of a week's calendar, Monday to
   today circled as one stretch, with Thursday inside it
   NOT singled out.** Written slot: "a picture of this week
   as one open stretch of days." What the student does:
   the student points at the whole stretch and says "this
   week" — the period this tense may name — then tries
   pointing at one finished day and hears why the tense
   breaks there.
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of a life's suitcase being packed
   with experience cards (a beach, a train, a mountain).**
   Written slot: "a picture of a suitcase holding the
   things a person has done in their life." What the
   student does: the student asks the suitcase one Have
   you ever…? question — experience is counted over a
   whole life up to now, not over a day.
4. **Slot 4 — a short animation (3–4 seconds) of the word
   YESTERDAY walking toward a present-perfect sentence
   and bouncing off it, while the same sentence in the
   past simple opens its door.** Written slot: "a picture
   of the word yesterday being turned away by I've cooked,
   and welcomed by I cooked." What the student does: the
   student calls out which coat each new sentence must
   wear before answering it.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first** (lesson/lesson.md): the done-room —
   three things somebody has done here, results visible,
   said first in the learner's own language.
2. **The shape**: have/has + the past participle; has with
   he/she/it, have with I/you/we/they (Egypt's Tip);
   contractions I've, he's, haven't, hasn't.
3. **The participle**: the -ed coat again (cleaned,
   painted, finished); the movers as triples (go–went–gone,
   see–saw–seen, eat–ate–eaten); Libya's song hunt — find
   the participles that are different (swept, tidied).
4. **Job one — just happened / the open stretch**: just
   now, today, this week, this month, this year (Libya
   Grade 8, p.32).
5. **Job two — the visible result**: happened recently,
   results we can see today (Egypt, p.47); Libya Grade 9's
   name for it — the effect on the present.
6. **Job three — started then, still going**: Egypt's
   second use, with for and since naming the stretch.
7. **Job four — experience**: Have you ever been to
   France? — any time in the past (Libya Work Book);
   ever, never, already, yet take their seats, and just
   sits between have/has and the participle.
8. **The bright line**: the period may be named, an exact
   time may not (Libya Teacher's Book); the two-coat
   conversation — ever-question in the perfect, the dated
   answer in the past simple.
9. **Asking**: Has Gwen cooked…? Yes, she has. Have they
   washed…? No, they haven't. (Libya, p.33.)
10. **Do it yourself** (the three closing tasks in the
    lesson): the done-room report, the ever interview,
    the week in one breath.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck on the participle: it is the past's third shape —
  drill the triples aloud (go–went–gone) the way Libya's
  Work Book tables lay them out, then re-enter.
- Stuck on have vs has: Egypt's Tip as a chant — has for
  he, she, it; have for all the rest.
- Stuck on perfect vs past: ask one question — "can I name
  the exact finished time?" If yes, past simple; if the
  time is a stretch reaching now, or no time at all,
  present perfect.
- This lesson's next door: The present perfect continuous
  (same level) — the same tie to now, with the action
  still moving.
