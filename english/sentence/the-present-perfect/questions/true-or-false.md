# The present perfect — true or false

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — The Present Perfect's Shape · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 3 — The Travel Words and the Bright Line (item 3: a named period that reaches now is the bright line's own distinction) · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3.

Say true or false. If it is false, say why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** The present perfect is have/has + the
past participle.
**True** — the shape as Libya's Grade 8 grammar box
states it, p.32.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** "Has" goes with I, you, we and they.
**False** — has goes with he, she and it; have goes with
I, you, we and they (Egypt's Tip, p.47).

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** "I have cooked chicken this week" is a
good present-perfect sentence.
**True** — this week is a period that reaches now; the
tense may name the period (Libya, Teacher's Book).

**4. (Sub lesson 3)** "I have cooked chicken on Thursday" is
also good.
**False** — Thursday is an exact finished time; the
present perfect does not say exactly when. Say "I cooked
chicken on Thursday." (Libya, Teacher's Book.)

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** In "Have you ever been to France? — Yes,
I went there last year," both tenses are in their right
seats.
**True** — the question asks about any time in the past;
the answer names a definite time, so the past simple
answers (Libya, Work Book, Unit 5, Lesson 3).
