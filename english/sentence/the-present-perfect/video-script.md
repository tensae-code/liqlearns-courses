# Video script — The present perfect

Super-simple voice. Read slow. The learner hears this
before or beside the written lesson.

---

Look at the room.

Somebody has shut the door.
Somebody has swept the floor.
You can see it. It is done. And you can see it NOW.

English has a shape for that.

I have cleaned the window.
He has washed the car.

Have. Has. And the third shape of the verb.
I have cleaned.
She has cleaned.
They have cleaned.

Has goes with he, she, it.
Have goes with I, you, we, they.

Most verbs wear the -ed coat.
Clean — cleaned.
Paint — painted.
Finish — finished.

Some famous verbs change shape.
Go — gone.
See — seen.
Eat — eaten.
Write — written.

Now. When do we use this shape?

One. It just happened.
I've just read a story.

Two. The result is here. You can see it.
I have lost my key. Now I cannot open the door.

Three. It started before. It is still going.
I have learned English for two years.
She has lived here since Monday.

Four. Your whole life. Any time.
Have you ever been to the sea?
I've never seen snow.

Ever asks.
Never says no, for all my life.
Already says: sooner than you think.
Yet waits at the end. Not yet.

One big rule. Listen.

This shape does NOT say the exact time.
I have cooked chicken this week. Good.
I have cooked chicken on Thursday. No. Wrong.

An exact time? A finished day?
Then use the past.
Have you ever been to France?
Yes. I went there last year.

Ever — the perfect.
Last year — the past.
Each one in its own seat.

Now you try.
Look at your room.
Say five things.
I've made my bed.
Mum has cooked lunch.
Somebody has opened the window.

Done — and you can see it now.
