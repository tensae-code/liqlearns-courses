# Borrow and Lend

## Do it first

Hold out your hand: "Can I borrow your pen?" Now hand yours over: "I can lend you mine." Two sentences, one pen, two directions. This lesson teaches the two words that keep the directions straight.

## Sub lesson 1 — Borrow — taking for a while

When a thing moves **to** you for a while, you **borrow** it. "Can I borrow your eraser?" — you ask, you take, you use it, you give it back. The borrower is the taker.

Borrow always points at the person who receives: I borrow, you borrow, she borrows — from someone. "She borrowed a book from the library." The book travelled to her, and it will travel back.

*Difficulty: One direction at a time — borrow points at the taker.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Lend — giving for a while

When a thing moves **from** you for a while, you **lend** it. "I can lend you my eraser." — you give, they use it, they give it back. The lender is the giver.

Same loan, other end: "She lent me her book." Say the pair together and they lock: you **borrow from** a person; you **lend to** a person. Borrow takes, lend gives — and both promise the thing comes home.

*Difficulty: The mirror direction — and the from/to that locks the pair.*

## Say it to your companion

Ask your companion to borrow something on the table. Then offer to lend them something of yours. Two directions, two sentences.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write two sentences: one thing you borrowed this month, one thing you lent.
2. Write a request: "Can I borrow your ___?" and a kind answer with lend.
3. Teach the pair to someone at home with a real pen, moving it both ways.
