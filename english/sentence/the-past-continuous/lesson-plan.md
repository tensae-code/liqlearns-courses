# Lesson plan — The past continuous

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** The past continuous
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 19 — Earlier Than the Past (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the past continuous as a SYSTEM — **was/were + verb-ing** — the past scene in motion: one long action cut across by a short one (interrupted), and two long actions moving side by side (simultaneous). This lesson does not reteach the basic past simple; it builds the moving background the past simple stands on.
**What the learner can do at the end:** set a past scene with was/were + -ing; cut it with a past-simple action using when; run two scenes side by side with while; and ask and deny in the tense.

1. **Past Continuous Shape and Scene** — build was/were + verb-ing for every who, and set a scene at a named past time. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Past Continuous Interrupted Scene** — join a moving scene to a sharp past-simple cut with when, in both orders, with while only before the past continuous. (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **Past Continuous Two Scenes and Story** — run two simultaneous scenes with while, and open a mini-story with a scene-setting sentence; the asking-and-denying block and the what-this-lesson-does-not-do block sit in this sub lesson's text by their position. (Was depth Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Past actions (the past simple is this lesson's cutting edge — it is NOT retaught here) and Present actions (the -ing coat, moved one step back). Recommended, never forced — this lesson stands alone for a learner who lands on it directly (DIRECT-ENTRY rule).
- Speaker convention: the explanation is read in the learner's main language; the TARGET forms are never translated and are read in the English voice.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a kitchen scene at night, one person stirring a pot and another reading, both mid-action, with a telephone on the wall just starting to ring.** What the student does: the student names the two moving actions first (both wear was/were + -ing), then the sharp one (the ring — past simple) — the whole system in one picture.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of rain beginning to fall on a boy walking home with a bag on his shoulder.** What the student does: before reading on, the student says the long action and the cutting action in their own language, then hears which coat each wears in English.
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of two clocks showing the same hour over two different rooms, people busy in both at once.** What the student does: the student joins the two scenes with while, one sentence, both sides in the past continuous.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first**: a frozen moving moment in the learner's own language, then its sharp cut.
2. **The shape**: was/were + verb-ing (Bolivia Y3 T6 table; Mongolia VIII U4).
3. **Job one — interrupted**: long action in this tense, short cut in the past simple, joined by when (Vietnam G9 U4; Kuwait Skyline 4A with when, 5B with while).
4. **The joining rule**: when before either tense; while only before the past continuous (Vietnam G9 U4, as printed).
5. **Job two — simultaneous**: both sides in this tense with while (Cambodia G8 U29–30; Sudan SPINE 5 Ch.5; Mongolia IX U5).
6. **Asking and denying**: Was she sleeping? They weren't listening.
7. **The honest border**: the past simple is not retaught — it appears only as the cut and the main line.
8. **Do it yourself**: the three closing tasks.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck on was/were: it is be's past — was for I/he/she/it, were for you/we/they, exactly as in the past of be already learned.
- Stuck choosing the coat for an action: ask "is it the moving scene or the sharp cut?" Long and moving → this tense; short and sharp → past simple.
- Stuck on while: while may only hold a past-continuous side — if the other side is a short cut, use when.
- This lesson's next door: Level 20 — The Future in Detail, opening with the future continuous: the same moving scene, aimed forward.
