# The past continuous — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Sentence · Lesson: The past continuous
Built from: Bolivia, the ministry teacher compendium (Year 3, Topic 6) — Cambodia, Grade 8 (Units 29–30) — Mongolia, English VIII (Unit 4) and English IX (Unit 5) — Kuwait, Skyline 4A and Skyline 5B — Vietnam, Grade 9 (Unit 4) — Sudan, SPINE 5 (Chapter 5).

*Soft hint only: if you know Past actions (the past simple is this lesson's cutting edge — it is NOT retaught here) and Present actions (the -ing coat, moved one step back), this lesson will be easier — but it stands alone. Start here if this is the thing you want to learn.*

**How this lesson is read aloud.** The speaker button reads the explanation in your own main language. The TARGET forms — the English shapes in **bold** — are never translated and are always read in the English voice, then the reading returns to your language. The target is the thing itself; the words around it are the explanation.

## Do it first
Freeze a moment from yesterday in your own language, like a photograph that still moves: "At eight last night, I was cooking. My brother was reading." Then one sharp thing cuts across it: "…when the phone rang." English keeps the moving scene in one coat — was/were + -ing — and the sharp cut in the past simple you already own. This lesson is the system of those two coats together.

## Sub lesson 1 — Past Continuous Shape and Scene
**The shape.** The past continuous is **was/were + verb-ing**: I **was cooking**. She **was reading**. They **were playing**. Was goes with I/he/she/it, were with you/we/they — the same was/were you met as be's past. Mongolia's books build it as the story's background (English VIII, Unit 4; English IX, Unit 5), and Bolivia's compendium tables it whole at Year 3 (Topic 6).

## Sub lesson 2 — Past Continuous Interrupted Scene
**Job one — the interrupted scene.** A long action is moving; a short action cuts across it. The long one wears this tense, the short one wears the past simple, and **when** joins them: "I **was walking** home **when** it started to rain." Vietnam's Grade 9 book (Unit 4) states the pair in just these terms — actions happening at a particular past time, and longer actions interrupted by a past simple action — and adds the joining rule its page prints: **when** can stand before either tense, **while** only before the past continuous. Kuwait walks the same border in steps: Skyline 4A teaches the tense with when, Skyline 5B returns to it with while.

## Sub lesson 3 — Past Continuous Two Scenes and Story
**Job two — two scenes side by side.** When two long actions move together, BOTH wear this tense, joined by **while**: "**While** I **was cooking**, he **was washing** the dishes." Cambodia's Grade 8 book works the tense across two units (Units 29–30) as story background, and Sudan's SPINE 5 sets it as Chapter 5's tense work, inside stories where scenes must be set before events can land.

**Asking and denying.** Was steps to the front: **Was** she **sleeping**? — Yes, she was. / No, she wasn't. The not-form folds inside: They **weren't listening**.

**What this lesson does NOT do.** It does not reteach the past simple — that coat is finished work (Past actions). Here the past simple appears only in its working role: the short, sharp action that cuts the scene, and the story's main line that the scene stands behind.

1. **Media slot 1 — a picture of a kitchen scene at night, one person stirring a pot and another reading, both mid-action, with a telephone on the wall just starting to ring.** What you do: the student names the two moving actions first (both wear was/were + -ing), then the sharp one (the ring — past simple) — the whole system in one picture.
2. **Media slot 2 — a picture of rain beginning to fall on a boy walking home with a bag on his shoulder.** What you do: before reading on, the student says the long action and the cutting action in their own language, then hears which coat each wears in English.
3. **Media slot 3 — a picture of two clocks showing the same hour over two different rooms, people busy in both at once.** What you do: the student joins the two scenes with while, one sentence, both sides in the past continuous.

## Say it to your companion
Set a scene for your companion: "Last night at nine, I was … and my family was/were …" Then let your companion throw in a sharp interruption — a knock, a call, a power cut — and you must catch it live: "…when the lights went out." Three scenes each; the cutter changes every time.

## Do it yourself
1. **The cut scene.** Write three interrupted scenes about real past moments: one long was/were action, one sharp past-simple cut, joined by when.
2. **Side by side.** Write two while sentences about the same past hour in your home — two people, two moving actions.
3. **Scene, then story.** Write a five-sentence mini-story: sentence one sets the scene in this tense; the rest run in the past simple; one more past-continuous sentence must appear when a second scene opens.
