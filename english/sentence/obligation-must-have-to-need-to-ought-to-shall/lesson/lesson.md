# Obligation Compared — Must, Have To, Need To, Ought To, Shall

## Do it first

Two rules pull at you: the school says come at eight; you yourself want to be early. Two different weights. Try: "I have to come at eight. I must be early." Go on — in this lesson you will learn the five words of duty and what each one weighs.

## Sub lesson 1 — Must and have to — the inside rule and the outside rule

**Must** and **have to** both carry duty — the difference is where the rule lives:

- "**I must** study tonight." — the rule is **inside**: my own decision, my own conscience.
- "**I have to** wear the uniform." — the rule is **outside**: the school, the job, the law says so.
- Both take the base verb: must go, have to go (has to for he/she).

Sort three duties now: inside (must) or outside (have to)?

## Sub lesson 2 — Need to — what is necessary

**Need to** names necessity — the thing cannot be skipped:

- "**You need to** rest." "We **need to** leave now."
- No necessity: "**You needn't** come." / "You **don't need to** come." — needn't takes the **bare verb**.
- Careful: the book's exercise offers "needn't to" as a candidate form — that is **ungrammatical**. Never needn't to.

Say one necessity and one non-necessity about tomorrow.

## Sub lesson 3 — Ought to — the weaker, moral duty

**Ought to** is duty as advice — the moral, weaker word:

- "**You ought to apologize.**" — it is the right thing, but no law forces it.
- "Employees **ought to** be honest." — a should with weight, softer than must.
- Ought to + base verb; the to never drops.

Give two ought to advices: one to a friend, one to a worker.

## Sub lesson 4 — Shall — the formal word of rules

**Shall** is the formal word — the word of written rules, laws and company policies:

- "**Employees shall follow company policies.**"
- In plain speech that same rule becomes have to: "Employees have to follow company policies."
- You will meet shall in documents and interviews, rarely in talk.

Read this rule aloud with shall, then say it plainly with have to.

## Say it to your companion

Trade duties with your companion: each names three — one personal, one from a rule book, one piece of moral advice. The other must clothe each in the right word (must / have to / need to / ought to / shall) and say where the rule lives. Swap.

## Do It Yourself

Write five duty sentences, one per word: a must from your own conscience, a have to from an outside rule, a need to of necessity, an ought to of advice, and a shall copied as a formal rule. Read them aloud with the right weight.
