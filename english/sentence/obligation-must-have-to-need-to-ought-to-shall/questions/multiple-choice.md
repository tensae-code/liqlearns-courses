## Multiple choice

1. The school rule book says: wear the uniform. You say: (Sub lesson 1)
   a) "I must wear it — my own idea."
   b) "I have to wear it."
   c) "I ought to wear it — maybe."
   **Answer: b) "I have to wear it."** — an outside rule takes have to.

2. Your own conscience says: study tonight. You say: (Sub lesson 1)
   a) "I have to study — the law says."
   b) "I must study tonight."
   c) "I shall study, signed."
   **Answer: b) "I must study tonight."** — the inside rule takes must.

3. "You ___ rest — your body cannot skip it." (Sub lesson 2)
   a) need to
   b) shall
   c) ought
   **Answer: a) need to** — necessity takes need to.

4. The correct no-necessity form is: (Sub lesson 2)
   a) "You needn't to come."
   b) "You needn't come."
   c) "You need to not come."
   **Answer: b) "You needn't come."** — needn't takes the bare verb; needn't to is ungrammatical.

5. "You ___ apologize." (moral advice, no law) (Sub lesson 3)
   a) shall
   b) ought to
   c) needn't
   **Answer: b) ought to** — ought to is the moral, weaker duty.

6. "Employees ___ follow company policies." (in the written policy) (Sub lesson 4)
   a) shall
   b) feel like
   c) ought
   **Answer: a) shall** — shall is the formal word of written rules.

7. In plain speech, the shall rule becomes: (Sub lesson 4)
   a) "Employees must to follow…"
   b) "Employees have to follow company policies."
   c) "Employees ought follow…"
   **Answer: b) "Employees have to follow company policies."** — plain speech says the outside rule with have to.
