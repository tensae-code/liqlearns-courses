# Levels — Obligation Compared — Must, Have To, Need To, Ought To, Shall

This file is the sub-lesson depth record for the lesson (restructure
2026-11-08): what each sub lesson holds, in teaching order. The old
layer wording is not used; the sub lessons below are the lesson's own
parts, exactly as listed in lesson-plan.md.

| Sub lesson | What it holds |
|---|---|
| Sub lesson 1 — Must and have to — the inside rule and the outside rule | Must (the speaker's own rule, personal/internal) against have to (rules from outside — law, job, others). |
| Sub lesson 2 — Need to — what is necessary | Need to for necessity; needn't / don't need to for no necessity — and the book's ungrammatical candidate "needn't to" refused. |
| Sub lesson 3 — Ought to — the weaker, moral duty | Ought to: duty as advice — moral, weaker than must (you ought to apologize). |
| Sub lesson 4 — Shall — the formal word of rules | Shall in formal and legal wording: employees shall follow company policies. |

Pass mark: the sources state no pass mark and none is invented. In this course a sub lesson opens when the learner proves the previous one with the prove-it check (at most 5 questions, the sub lesson's whole question set as the pool).
