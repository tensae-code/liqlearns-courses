# Lesson plan — Wish and if only

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Wish and if only
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 25, "Wishing and Imagining" —
the first lesson of the level (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** wishing about a world
that is not real — **wish / if only + past simple** for a
present regret, **wish / if only + past perfect** for a past
regret, **wish / if only + would** for a change you want, and
**wish / if only + could** for an ability you do not have.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Past actions (the
past coat is borrowed, not past in meaning) and on
Conditionals (the second conditional is its close cousin).
More conditionals follows it inside this level.
**What the learner can do at the end:** say what they wish
were different NOW, what they wish had been different THEN,
ask the world to change with would, and name a missing
ability with could — and hear if only as the same wish,
said stronger.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Conditionals (If I had wings, I would
  fly — the same unreal past coat) and Past actions.
  Recommended, never forced — this lesson stands alone for
  a learner who lands on it directly (DIRECT-ENTRY rule).
- Speaker convention: the explanation is read in the
  learner's main language; the TARGET forms (I wish I knew,
  if only…, I wish you would…) are never translated and are
  read in the English voice.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a boy looking at rain through a
   window, a football waiting dry and useless by the door.**
   Written slot: "a picture of a wish about NOW — the rain
   is real, the wish is not." What the student does: the
   student says the wish before reading the rule: "I wish
   it weren't raining."
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of an empty bus stop at night, one
   bus already far away, its lights small.** Written slot:
   "a picture of a wish about THEN — a past that cannot
   change." What the student does: the student says the
   past wish: "If only I had left earlier."
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of a sister borrowing a dress, the
   owner pointing at the clock.** Written slot: "a picture
   of a wish for a CHANGE — somebody to start or stop
   something." What the student does: the student says it
   with would: "I wish you would listen to me."

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first** (lesson/lesson.md): three wishes in the
   learner's own language — one about now, one about then,
   one asking somebody to change.
2. **Wish + past simple — the present regret**: Bolivia's
   Year 6 book states the whole wish system (Theme 4):
   "I wish I ate more vegetables — but I don't"; Nepal's
   Grade 7 book teaches I wish + past form (Unit 4);
   Vietnam's Grade 9 book teaches wish + past simple
   (Unit 4, printed p.43); Kuwait's Over to You 10B teaches
   wish + past simple (Unit 8, pp.15–20).
3. **Wish + past perfect — the past regret**: "I wish I
   hadn't done it" (Bolivia); Kuwait 10B pairs past simple
   and past perfect wishes in the same unit.
4. **Wish + would — the wanted change**: for something to
   happen, or someone to start or stop an annoying thing
   (Bolivia: "I wish you wouldn't borrow my clothes!");
   Malaysia's Form 4 book teaches wish / if only with past
   simple, would/could + infinitive, and past perfect
   (Module 8).
5. **Wish + could — the missing ability**: "I wish I could
   swim" (Malaysia Form 4's would/could seat).
6. **If only**: the same three shapes, said stronger —
   Malaysia's Form 4 teaches wish and if only together;
   Malaysia's Form 5 book practises wish / if only only as
   single items inside a Review exercise (printed p.82) —
   practice, not a taught structure, and worded so here.
7. **Do it yourself** (the three closing tasks).

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck on now vs then: ask "is the regret about today, or
  about a finished day?" Today takes the past simple coat;
  a finished day takes had + participle.
- Stuck reaching for would about yourself: would wishes
  point OUT — at the weather, at other people, at things.
  For your own missing ability, use could.
- This lesson's next door: More conditionals (same level)
  — the if-machine that wishes borrow their coats from.
