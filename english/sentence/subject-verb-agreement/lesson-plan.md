# Lesson plan — Subject-verb agreement

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Subject-verb agreement
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 31, "Linking and
Fixing Sentences" — the second lesson of the level
(CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** concord — the
verb matches its true subject, even when the subject is
tricky: one of + plural noun, quantities taken as one
lump (twenty dollars is), uncountable nouns, collective
and plural-form nouns (police were), and the traps laid
by of-phrases, there is/are, and either/or.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Building a
sentence (subject and verb named) and on the tense
lessons (the verb shapes it chooses between). Fixing
sentence errors follows it in this level.
**What the learner can do at the end:** find the true
subject under every decoration; choose is/are, was/were,
has/have and the -s form for one of, each, quantities,
uncountables, police/people, news/maths subjects, and
subjects joined by and, or and nor.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Building a sentence and Nouns
  (countable against uncountable). Recommended, never
  forced.
- Pronoun agreement (a singular antecedent takes a
  singular pronoun) rides along in Sierra Leone's
  lessons and is named here; the lesson's one thing is
  the verb.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a balance: a subject on one
   side, a verb on the other, tipping level only when
   they match.** Written slot: "a picture of a subject
   and a verb balancing." What the student does: the
   student says the pair aloud — "the police … were" —
   and hears the balance settle.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of a crowd of friends with ONE
   of them circled in gold, the others grey.** Written
   slot: "a picture of one circled out of many." What
   the student does: the student points at the circled
   one and says "one … is" — the verb answers to the one,
   not to the crowd.
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of a single banknote labelled
   TWENTY DOLLARS, weighed as one lump on a scale.**
   Written slot: "a picture of twenty dollars as one
   lump." What the student does: the student weighs a
   price, a time and a distance the same way: "Ten
   kilometres is far."
4. **Slot 4 — a picture of a sentence with a grey
   of-phrase curtain between the subject and the verb,
   the curtain being pulled aside.** Written slot: "a
   picture of an of-phrase pulled aside." What the
   student does: the student covers the of-phrase with a
   hand and reads the skeleton that remains.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first** (lesson/lesson.md): three sentences
   about the learner's own class where the verb choice
   is not obvious — one of my friends…, the police…,
   twenty dollars….
2. **The base rule**: a singular subject takes a
   singular verb, a plural subject a plural verb —
   Sierra Leone states it at SSS1, SSS2 and SSS3.
3. **Nepal's tricky three**: one of my friends is…;
   twenty dollars is…; police were… (Grade 7, Unit 14,
   pp.142–153) — each unpacked into its own rule.
4. **The of-phrase trap**: the subject standing before
   an of-phrase decides the verb (Sierra Leone SSS2 and
   SSS3 state the rule in those terms).
5. **Quantities as one lump, and uncountables**:
   money, time and distance measured as one sum are
   singular; uncountable nouns (information, furniture,
   bread) are singular.
6. **Plural-form and collective nouns**: police, people,
   cattle are plural; news, maths, measles end in -s but
   are singular; a collective (team, family) is singular
   when it acts as one body.
7. **Joiners**: and makes plural; or/nor and either…or
   answer to the nearer subject; there is/are answers to
   what follows.
8. **South Africa's early spine**: there is/are and
   farmers plough/ploughs (HL Grade 5 Workbook 1, p.28);
   is/are (HL Grade 6 Workbook 1, p.30) — the same rule
   at workbook age.
9. **Do it yourself** (the three closing tasks in the
   lesson): the tricky-three report, the curtain test,
   the price-and-distance set.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck on any sentence: pull the curtain aside — cross
  out every phrase between subject and verb and read the
  skeleton.
- Stuck on one of: ask "how many does the verb talk
  about?" One. The verb is singular.
- Stuck on a quantity: ask "is it counted piece by
  piece, or weighed as one lump?" A price, a time, a
  distance is weighed — singular.
- This level's next door: Fixing sentence errors — a
  wrong verb is one of the errors that lesson's clinic
  repairs, among fragments and splices.
