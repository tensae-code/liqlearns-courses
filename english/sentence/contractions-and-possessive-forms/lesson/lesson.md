# Contractions and Possessive Forms

## Do it first

Say these about yourself, as fast as you can:

- "I am a student. I do not like rain. This book is mine —
  it is Sara's book." — wait. Whose book is it?

When we talk fast, "I am" becomes "**I'm**" all by itself.
And a name can hold a thing as its own: "**Sara's** book."
Both of those use the same little mark — the apostrophe —
and they are two different jobs. That is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Short forms (contractions)

A **contraction** is a short form: two words made into one,
with letters left out. The apostrophe stands where the
letters fell out.

- I am → **I'm** · you are → **you're** · he is → **he's** ·
  she is → **she's** · it is → **it's** · we are → **we're** ·
  they are → **they're**
- do not → **don't** · does not → **doesn't** · did not →
  **didn't**
- cannot → **can't** · will not → **won't**

Say them and write them: "I'm a student." "She's my
friend." "We don't live here." "He can't swim."

*Difficulty: first steps — one family of forms at a time, each inside a sentence about you.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Possessive forms, and a paragraph about yourself

A **possessive form** shows that a thing belongs to someone.
For one person, put **'s** after the name:

- **Sara's book** — the book belongs to Sara.
- **the boy's ball** — the ball belongs to the boy.
- **my teacher's pen.**

Never mix the two jobs: **it's** with an apostrophe is the
short form of "it is". The belonging word for "it" is **its**
with NO apostrophe: "The dog wagged **its** tail."

Now the writing the book sets: a paragraph about yourself.
Write five or six sentences about you — your name, your
home, your family, what you like — and use both jobs in it:
short forms and one possessive form.

*Difficulty: building — two jobs to keep apart, then both used in one real paragraph.*

## Say it to your companion

Read your paragraph to your companion. Your companion
listens for the two jobs and names each one they hear:
"short form!" — "possessive!" Then change places.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write these in short form: I am · she is · do not ·
   cannot · they are · it is.
2. Hold up three things in the room and write whose they
   are, with possessive forms.
3. Write your own paragraph about yourself — six sentences,
   with at least two short forms and one possessive form.
