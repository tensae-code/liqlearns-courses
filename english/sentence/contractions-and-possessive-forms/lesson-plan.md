# Lesson plan — Contractions and Possessive Forms

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** Contractions and Possessive Forms
**Lesson:** contractions-and-possessive-forms — the two jobs of
the apostrophe: short forms (I'm, don't, can't) and possessive
forms (Sara's book), and writing a paragraph about yourself
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 10 — Building a Sentence
**The one thing:** the apostrophe's two jobs — leaving letters
out in a short form, and showing that a thing belongs to
someone in a possessive form — never mixed up.
**This lesson's own level:** Sentence-mechanics lesson: the
short forms first, then the possessive forms and the
self-paragraph the book sets beside them.

## Why this lesson exists

No lesson housed this writing-mechanics part. Palestine's
Grade 10 book teaches it as a named writing part of its
free-time unit: short forms (contractions) and possessive
forms, with writing a paragraph about oneself (Unit 3,
printed p.43). The 2026-11-08 re-verification sweep read the
book page by page for the first time and marked this part NEW
LESSON NEEDED. Under the everything-goes-in ruling
(2026-11-06), a teaching item with no lesson gets a new lesson
built; this is that lesson. Its only source is the Palestine
book named in lesson/sources.md.

## Sub lessons

1. Short forms — contractions
2. Possessive forms — and a paragraph about yourself

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is forced first — this lesson stands alone: it starts
from "say it short" and teaches both apostrophe jobs and every
form it uses.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- Write and read the common short forms: I'm, you're, he's,
  don't, can't, it's.
- Write possessive forms with 's: Sara's book, the boy's ball.
- Tell the two jobs apart — and write a short paragraph about
  themselves using both.

## What you need
Paper, a pencil, and things in the room that belong to people.

## The run of the lesson
1. **Do it first** — say three sentences about yourself as
   fast as you can; hear how the short forms come by
   themselves (lesson/lesson.md).
2. ADD IMAGE: two sentences side by side — "I am happy" and
   "I'm happy" — with the missing letters shown falling out,
   and an apostrophe standing where they were.
3. Work through sub lesson 1: the short forms, one family at
   a time.
4. Work through sub lesson 2: the possessive forms, then the
   paragraph about yourself.
5. ADD VIDEO (short): words shrinking into short forms on
   screen — I AM → I'M, DO NOT → DON'T — then a name growing
   an 's to hold its book: SARA → SARA'S BOOK.

## Add-ons
- Classroom hunt: hold up a thing and say whose it is, with
  the possessive form: "This is Ali's pen."
- Rewrite race: take five full sentences and write each one
  in its short form.

## If the learner is stuck
Cut back to one family — I am → I'm, do not → don't — and the
possessive of their own name, until they are sure. Add the
rest one family at a time.

## Teacher note
The book sets these two mechanics together in one writing
part, and this lesson keeps them together but apart: the two
jobs are taught in two sub lessons and named every time, so
the learner never mixes them. The forms taught are the
mechanics the book names — short forms and possessive forms;
the example sentences are the course's own workings on them.
