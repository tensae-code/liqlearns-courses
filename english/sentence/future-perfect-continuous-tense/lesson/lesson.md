# Future Perfect Continuous Tense

## Do it first

Answer this, any way you can:

"Next June, how long will you have been learning English?"

Hard, yes? You need a tense that stands at a moment in the
**future** and looks at an action **still running** there —
and says **how long** it has been running. That tense is this
lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — The shape

The future perfect continuous is built in four blocks:

**will + have + been + verb-ing**

- "By June, I **will have been learning** English for three
  years."
- "This time tomorrow, she **will have been travelling**
  for ten hours."

Every block has its job: **will** puts us in the future,
**have been** says the action started before that point, and
the **-ing** form says it is still running there.

It always hangs on a **future point**: *by June*, *by Friday*,
*this time next year*, *when you arrive*. Without the point,
the tense has nowhere to stand.

Tell it from its neighbours:

- Future continuous: "At nine I **will be working**." (in
  progress then — no measure.)
- Future perfect: "By nine I **will have finished**." (done
  by then.)
- Future perfect continuous: "By nine I **will have been
  working** for five hours." (still running then — and
  measured.)

*Difficulty: first steps — four blocks in a fixed order, always hung on a future point the learner can point at.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Using it: for, since, questions, negatives

The tense's favourite words are the measures:

- **for** + a length: "…will have been raining **for three
  days**."
- **since** + a starting point: "…will have been living here
  **since 2023**."

**Questions** turn will around: "**Will** you **have been
waiting** long when I arrive?" — and the natural question of
the tense: "How long will you have been…?"

**Negatives** put not after will: "By June, we **will not
have been studying** for long — only a month."

Say three true ones about yourself: one with **for**, one
with **since**, one as a question to your partner.

*Difficulty: building — the measures are the tense's real work; questions and negatives move one block each, never the whole shape.*

## Say it to your companion

Explain the tense to your companion: its four blocks, its
future point, its two measures, and how it differs from
future-perfect. Your companion names a future point; you
answer with a true sentence on it. Then change places.

## Do It Yourself

1. Build the tense for: (work) by Friday, (rain) by morning,
   (live here) by next year — one sentence each, with a
   measure.
2. Write the question and the negative of: "By June, she
   will have been teaching for ten years."
3. Write three true sentences about your own next year —
   school, family, a skill — each with **for** or **since**.
