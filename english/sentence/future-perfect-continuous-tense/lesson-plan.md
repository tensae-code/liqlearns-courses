# Lesson plan — Future Perfect Continuous Tense

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** Future Perfect Continuous Tense
**Lesson:** future-perfect-continuous-tense — will have been + verb-ing — an action still going on at a future point, told with how long it has been going on
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 20 — The Future in Detail
**The one thing:** one tense that looks forward to a future moment and measures an action still running at that moment by its length
**This lesson's own level:** Future lesson: the shape first, then its use with for/since and in questions and negatives.

## Why this lesson exists

No lesson housed the third future shape. Unit 20 holds future-continuous (in progress then) and future-perfect (finished by then); Sierra Leone's SSS 3 handbook teaches the remaining one by name — "Future Perfect Continuous Tense" (Term 3, Lessons 99–100): in progress at that future moment, and measured. The 2026-11-08 re-verification sweep marked both lessons NEW LESSON NEEDED. Under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06) the topic gets its own lesson; its sources are the handbook lessons named in lesson/sources.md.

## Sub lessons

1. **The shape** — will have been + the present participle, hung on a future point — "by June", "this time next year"
2. **Using it** — measuring the running action with for and since; questions (Will you have been…?) and negatives (will not have been…)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is forced first — this lesson stands alone, but it stands third in its family: future-continuous and future-perfect teach its two halves, and the-present-perfect-continuous is its mirror looking back from now. A learner who knows those three will find this one quick; a learner who does not can still build it here from the parts, which are all named inside the lesson.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- Build the tense: will have been + verb-ing, on a future point.
- Say how it differs from future-perfect (finished by then) and future-continuous (in progress then, unmeasured).
- Use it with for and since to measure the action's length at the future point.
- Make its question and negative forms.

## What you need
A calendar or a timeline the learner can point at — the tense lives on future points.

## The run of the lesson
1. **Do it first** — the learner answers "Next June, how long will you have been learning English?" in their own words, before the tense's shape is taught (lesson/lesson.md).
2. Work through sub lesson 1: the shape, piece by piece.
3. Work through sub lesson 2: measuring with for/since; questions and negatives.
4. ADD IMAGE: a timeline running to "next June", with an action bar still running at the mark and its length written under it — the student draws the same bar for their own action.

## Add-ons
- Future diary: write three true sentences about next year — one for school, one for family, one for a skill — each with its measure (for three years, since 2024).
- Point races: a future point is called (“by December!”); partners race to build a true sentence on it.

## If the learner is stuck
If the shape collapses into future-perfect, use the test: has the action STOPPED at the future point, or is it still RUNNING? Stopped — perfect. Running and measured — this tense. If the pieces tumble (will been have), build it in order on the board: will → have → been → -ing, one block at a time.

## Teacher note
The handbook teaches this tense last in its tense arc, at SSS 3 — the lesson keeps that depth: it is the final future shape, and it closes Unit 20's set beside future-continuous and future-perfect.
