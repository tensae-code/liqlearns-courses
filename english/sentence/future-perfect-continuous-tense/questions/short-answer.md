## Short answer

1. Build the future perfect continuous of "rain" on the point "by morning", with a measure. (Sub lessons 1–2)
   **Answer:** "By morning, it will have been raining for (six) hours" — will have been + raining, hung on "by morning", measured with for.

2. How does this tense differ from the future perfect? (Sub lesson 1)
   **Answer:** The future perfect's action is finished by the future point; this tense's action is still running at the point — and the tense says how long it has been running.

3. Make the question of "By June, she will have been teaching for ten years." (Sub lesson 2)
   **Answer:** "Will she have been teaching for ten years by June?" (or "How long will she have been teaching by June?")

4. Why does the tense take "for three years" but "since 2023"? (Sub lesson 2)
   **Answer:** For measures a length; since names the starting point.
