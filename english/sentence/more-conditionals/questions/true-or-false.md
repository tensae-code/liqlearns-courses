# More conditionals — true or false

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Mixed Conditionals · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Inverted Conditionals · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Unless and Provided That, except items about subjunctive were, which are Sub lesson 4 — Subjunctive Were.

Say true or false. If it is false, say why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** In a mixed conditional, the two halves must always keep the same time.
**Answer: False** — mixing means the halves keep different times on purpose: a past condition can carry a present result (Armenia, §6.2).

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** "Had I known" and "If I had known" mean the same thing.
**Answer: True** — inversion drops if and steps had first; the meaning does not move (Syria, G12 U12).

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** "Unless you hurry" means "if you hurry".
**Answer: False** — unless means if…NOT: unless you hurry = if you don't hurry (Nepal, G10 U7).

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** In "If I were you", were is the right form even though the who is I.
**Answer: True** — subjunctive were serves every who in unreal conditions (South Africa's mood teaching, G5 HL WB2).
