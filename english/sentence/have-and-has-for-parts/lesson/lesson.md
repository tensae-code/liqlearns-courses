# Have and Has for Parts

## Do it first

Point at a bike — a real one, or a picture — and say what it has: wheels? a chain? brakes? You are not saying the bike is yours. You are naming the bike by its parts. English does that with one small word: **has**.

## Sub lesson 1 — Saying what a thing has

*Difficulty: first steps. One frame, many things.*

You already know **have** for owning: "I have a bike." This lesson turns the word around. Now the **thing** is the one that has:

- **The bike has 2 brakes.**
- **It has four wheels.**
- The car **has** an engine.
- The boat **has** a sail.

The frame is always the same: **The [thing] has [part].** One thing — a bike, a car, a boat, a house — takes **has**. Point at the thing, name a part, and join them with **has**. Part by part, you can describe any machine in the world: what is it made of? Answer with has.

Say it about the bike: it has two wheels, two pedals, a chain, a saddle, handlebars, a lamp. Six true sentences, one frame.

## Sub lesson 2 — What it doesn't have, and inventing a vehicle

*Difficulty: a step up. The missing parts, then a vehicle of your own.*

Things are also known by what they **don't** have. The book's own example says it plainly:

- **The bike doesn't have a paddle.**

A bike has pedals for your feet; it has no paddle for the water — that belongs to a canoe. **doesn't have** is the frame for the missing part: the car doesn't have a sail; the boat doesn't have wheels; the house doesn't have pedals.

Now the making task the book sets: **invent a vehicle** — one that exists only in your head — and **present it by its parts**: "It has four wheels. It has a big sail. It doesn't have an engine. It has wings!" Has and doesn't have, together, are a whole description of your machine.

## Say it to your companion

Describe a real thing near you by its parts — five has-sentences and one doesn't have-sentence — without naming the thing. Your companion guesses what it is. Then swap: their thing, your guessing.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write five sentences about a bike, a car or a boat: what it has. Use has in every sentence.
2. Write two doesn't have sentences about the same thing — parts it truly lacks.
3. Invent a vehicle. Draw it, and write its presentation: at least four sentences with has and doesn't have.
