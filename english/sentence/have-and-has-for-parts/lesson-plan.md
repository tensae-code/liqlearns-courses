# Lesson Plan — Have and Has for Parts

**One thing:** saying what a thing HAS — naming a thing by its parts: "The bike has 2 brakes" — and what it does not have.

## Sub lessons

1. Saying what a thing has — has for one thing: "The bike has 2 brakes", "It has four wheels…"; naming a vehicle or a thing part by part
2. What it doesn't have, and inventing a vehicle — "The bike doesn't have a paddle" (as the book prints it); inventing a vehicle of your own and presenting it by its parts

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Teaching points

- **This lesson is have/has for PARTS, not for owning.** have-and-has teaches "I have a bike" — the bike is yours. Here the bike itself is the owner: **the bike has** brakes, a chain, two wheels. The thing holds its parts the way a person holds their things.
- **has goes with one thing:** the bike **has**, the car **has**, it **has**. The frame is: **The [thing] has [number] [part].** — "The bike has 2 brakes."
- **The negative:** the bike **doesn't have** a paddle — the book's own example, printed with "paddle" where the unit's word is pedal; the point stands and is taught with the book's sentence named as the book's.
- **The making task:** invent a vehicle — your own idea — and present it by its parts: "It has four wheels. It has a big sail. It doesn't have an engine." Parts are how you describe a machine.
