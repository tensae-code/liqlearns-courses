# Lesson plan — Exclamations and orders

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Exclamations and orders
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 32 — "Habits and
Orders" — the second and last lesson of the level
(CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the two
sentence types that do not state but ACT — the
imperative (orders, instructions, directions, safety
rules) and the exclamation (What a…! / How…!) — with
the exclamation mark doing its filed job: strong
instructions and strong feelings.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Building a
sentence (the statement these two types stand beside)
and completes the sentence-types family.
**What the learner can do at the end:** give and soften
orders (bare verb; Don't…; Please…), follow and give
directions in imperatives, phrase safety rules with
must/mustn't, and cry out feeling in the two exclamation
shapes — What a + noun phrase! and How + adjective /
adverb! — ending each with its mark.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Exclamations and orders — the order shape** — affirmative and
   negative imperatives with the bare verb and no subject, softened
   with please. (Was depth Level 1.)

2. **Exclamations and orders — orders at work** — direction chains
   in imperatives, and safety rules with must/mustn't beside Don't.
   (Was depth Level 2.)

3. **Exclamations and orders — the cry** — What a…! and How…!
   exclamations on their right shapes, and the exclamation mark
   placed for strong instructions and strong feelings. (Was depth
   Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a road sign with an arrow and
   the words TURN LEFT, a finger pointing the way.**
   Written slot: "a picture of a direction sign." What
   the student does: the student gives the direction
   aloud as an imperative: "Turn left."
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of a child about to touch a hot
   pot, a mother's hand raised: the safety order.**
   Written slot: "a picture of a warning moment." What
   the student does: the student says the negative order
   fast and loud: "Don't touch!"
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of a crowd at a performance,
   faces lit up, mouths open: the cry of feeling.**
   Written slot: "a picture of an amazed crowd." What
   the student does: the student tries both cry shapes:
   "What a performance!" and "How they sing!"

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first** (lesson/lesson.md): sort six short
   utterances — statement, order, cry — before any
   names are given.
2. **The imperative shape**: base verb, no subject —
   Bolivia's Year 1 IMPERATIVES box prints the formula
   VERB + (COMPLEMENT) (student p.173); Nicaragua's
   Grammar Tip (Activity Book 3, p.13) adds the uses:
   orders, invitations, advice — and "no subject
   pronoun is used."
3. **The negative and the softener**: Don't + verb
   (Bolivia's box; Egypt's Primary 3, printed p.8);
   Please makes it polite (Nicaragua, p.13).
4. **Directions and rules**: Kuwait's Skyline 5B
   Unit 7, Lesson 2 — imperatives for commands,
   instructions and directions on a map; Rwanda's
   Pupil's Book 6, Unit 9 — imperatives (p.113) inside
   a mother–child dialogue of real commands
   (pp.114–115); Egypt's Primary 3 adds must/mustn't
   for rules (printed p.14).
5. **The exclamation mark**: Egypt's Primary 3 teaches
   it twice over (printed pp.8 and 19): commands end
   with it "because they are strong instructions", and
   it stands after strong feelings — surprise,
   excitement, warning.
6. **The two cry shapes**: What a + noun phrase!
   ("What a performance!" — the very title of Egypt's
   Secondary 11 Term 2 Unit 11, printed p.52) and
   How + adjective/adverb! The Egypt filing gives no
   grammar box for the shapes; the lesson says so and
   teaches the standard shapes under Egypt's filed
   exclamation-mark teaching.
7. **Do it yourself** (the three closing tasks in the
   lesson): the direction chain, the rule board, two
   cries.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck on an order's shape: drop the subject, keep the
  bare verb — if a "you" is sitting in it, it is not
  yet an imperative.
- Stuck between a statement and a cry: statements end
  in a full stop and tell; cries end in ! and feel. Say
  it aloud — the voice knows.
- Stuck choosing What or How: a thing or person in the
  cry takes What a…!; a quality or a manner takes
  How…!
- The course's next door (Level 33): Phrasal verbs —
  small verbs carrying big meanings.
