# Video script — Exclamations and orders

Super-simple voice. Read slow. The learner hears this
before or beside the written lesson.

---

Some sentences do not tell. They ACT.

"Stand up."
No YOU. Just the verb. Bare and strong.
That is an order. An imperative.
"Sit down, please." Please makes it soft.
"Don't run." DON'T plus the verb stops the action.
"Don't touch the pot!" — a warning.

Orders also show the way.
"Turn left. Go straight. Walk forward."
Step by step. Like a map speaking.

Rules take MUST and MUSTN'T.
"You must wash your hands."
"You mustn't shout in the library."

And feelings cry out.
"What a performance!"
WHAT A, then the thing. Then the mark: !
"How beautifully they sing!"
HOW, then how good, how strong, how fast.
Egypt's little book tells us why the mark stands:
for strong instructions, and for strong feelings.
Surprise. Excitement. Warning.

Say it with me.
"Don't be late!"
"What a day!"
"How fast he runs!"

Now you try.
One order. One warning. One cry of joy.
