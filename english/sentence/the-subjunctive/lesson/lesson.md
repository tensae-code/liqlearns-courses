# The Subjunctive

## Do it first

Read this sentence from Tunisia's Skills for Life book: "It is important that he **come** on time." Come — not *comes*. Something special is happening to the verb. That special form is the subjunctive, and this lesson teaches exactly the pattern the book teaches: the plain verb form after expressions of importance and advice.

## Sub lesson 1 — The pattern after importance expressions

After expressions like **It is important that…** and **It is advisable that…**, the verb in the that-clause takes its plain form — the form you find in the dictionary, with nothing added:

- "It is important that he **come** on time." (not *comes*)
- "It is advisable that a scientist **be** patient." — **be** is the plain form of the verb be, used for every person.

The meaning: the sentence is not reporting a fact; it is saying what should happen. English marks that with the plain form.

*Difficulty: One pattern — an importance expression, then the plain verb.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Using the subjunctive carefully

- The plain form is the same for all persons: "that I **be**", "that she **be**", "that they **be**"; "that he **come**", "that they **come**".
- It belongs to formal and careful English — advice, rules, recommendations: "It is important that every pupil **bring** the book."
- Ordinary statements keep ordinary forms: "He comes on time every day" is a fact, and keeps *comes*. The subjunctive appears only where the importance/advice expression calls for it.

*Difficulty: Knowing where the pattern lives — and where it does not.*

## Say it to your companion

Give your companion one piece of formal advice with "It is advisable that…". They repeat it and check: is the verb plain? Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the book's two sentences from memory, and underline the subjunctive verbs.
2. Write two sentences of your own: one with "It is important that…", one with "It is advisable that…".
3. Write one ordinary fact sentence with *comes* — and one line saying why it does not take the subjunctive.
