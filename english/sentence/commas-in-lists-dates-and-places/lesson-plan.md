# Lesson Plan — Commas in Lists, Dates and Places

**One thing:** the comma (,) has three jobs — it stands between the words in a series (a list inside a sentence), between a city and its country, and between the day and the year in a date.

## Sub lessons

1. Commas in a list — a comma between each word in a series, so the words do not run together
2. Commas in places and dates — a comma between the city and the country, and between the day and the year in a date

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Teaching points

- A **series** is a list of words inside one sentence: the comma stands between them — "mangoes, bananas, oranges and pineapple".
- The last two words of a series are joined by **and**, with no comma before the and in the book's pattern: "…, oranges and pineapple".
- A place is written with a comma between the city and the country: "Georgetown, Guyana".
- A date is written with a comma between the day and the year: "23rd February, 1970" — Guyana became a Co-operative Republic on 23rd February, 1970 (the book's own closing-review sentence).
- The comma is a small pause, not a stop: the sentence runs on after it, unlike the full stop.
