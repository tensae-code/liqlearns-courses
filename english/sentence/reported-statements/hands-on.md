# Hands-on — Reported statements

You do it, we guide you. Everything here is done with a
companion or the Explain Companion.

1. **The bridge walk.** The companion says five
   sentences across the tense ladder (I am busy. I made
   tea. I have lost my key. I will help you. I can run
   fast.). The learner carries each one across the
   bridge: "He said that…" — one step back, pronouns
   turned. Any sentence that arrives unchanged (except a
   truth) walks back and crosses again.
2. **The telephone chain.** Three people (or the learner
   in three chairs): the first whispers a message with a
   time word in it — "I will finish this today." Each
   next person reports it onward. At the end, read the
   first and last versions side by side: will → would,
   today → that day, I turning at every chair.
3. **The truth test.** The companion mixes five ordinary
   sentences with three truths (Water boils at 100
   degrees. The earth goes round the sun.). The learner
   reports all eight; the truths must stay in the present
   while everything else steps down. Nepal's books run
   exactly this test on "The teacher told us that the Sun
   sets in the west."
4. **News desk.** The companion is a bank manager, a
   player, a head teacher — with three prepared quote
   cards ("Our bank will open a new branch." "I scored
   two goals."). The learner is the reporter and writes
   the news lines, choosing a verb from the bank each
   time: said, added, pointed out, remarked — and one
   line in the impersonal frame: It is said that…
