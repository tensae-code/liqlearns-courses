# Lesson plan — Reported statements

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Reported statements
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 22, "Telling What
People Said" — the first lesson of the level
(CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** reporting a
statement — moving another person's words into your own
sentence with say/tell, one step back in tense
(backshift), the pronouns and time/place words turned
with it — and the one family that refuses to move:
universal truths keep the present tense.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the whole
tense ladder (every rung is a backshift step) and opens
Level 22; reported questions and reported commands
follow it inside this level.
**What the learner can do at the end:** report any
statement with the full backshift chart; choose say vs
tell and place the person correctly; turn pronouns and
today/here/this with the reporting; hold a universal
truth in the present; and colour the report with the
reporting-verb bank (add, remark, point out, whisper,
yell…) and Jordan's impersonal frames (It is said
that…).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Past actions and The present
  perfect (backshift walks exactly those coats), and
  Question tags / Subject and object questions (Level 21
  closes just before). Recommended, never forced.
- Speech marks (this level's last lesson) quote the
  EXACT words; this lesson retells them. Keep the two
  apart from the first minute.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a message being carried across
   a bridge by a runner: the words leave one person's
   mouth in a speech bubble, cross the bridge, and arrive
   changed — "I am tired" entering, "she was tired"
   leaving.** Written slot: "a picture of words crossing
   a bridge and changing their coats." What the student
   does: the student walks one sentence across the bridge
   aloud, changing it piece by piece at each step.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of a staircase with the tenses on
   its steps: present at the top, past one step down,
   past perfect at the bottom, and a sign: EVERY REPORT
   WALKS ONE STEP DOWN.** Written slot: "a picture of the
   tense staircase." What the student does: the student
   points to a tense, steps down one, and says the
   reported shape.
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of the sun rising in the east,
   drawn inside a gold frame that never changes, while
   ordinary sentences around it wear changing coats.**
   Written slot: "a picture of a truth that never moves."
   What the student does: the student reports "The sun
   rises in the east" and hears why this one sentence
   refuses the staircase.
4. **Slot 4 — a picture of two doors: SAY with nobody
   standing at it, TELL with a person standing at it
   receiving the words.** Written slot: "a picture of say
   and tell as two doors." What the student does: before
   choosing, the student asks "is a person receiving
   this?" — if yes, tell + the person; if no, say.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first** (lesson/lesson.md): the carried
   message — a friend's sentence retold to a third
   person, first in the learner's own language.
2. **The frame**: say (that)… / tell me (that)… — tell
   always carries its person; say does not (Cambodia
   Grade 12's say vs tell work; Malaysia Form 3).
3. **The staircase — backshift in full**: Mauritania's
   chart (present→past, past→past perfect, present
   perfect→past perfect, continuous→past continuous,
   will→would, shall→should, can→could, may→might,
   must→had to; would/could/should/might/ought to stay).
4. **Pronouns and pointing words turn too**: I→he/she,
   my→his/her; today→that day, here→there, this→that
   (Nepal Grade 8's list, p.253).
5. **say/tell collocations**: say sorry, say yes; tell
   the truth, a lie, a joke, a secret (Cambodia Grade
   12, Unit 16).
6. **The truth exception**: universal truths and facts
   keep the present tense (Nepal Grade 7's truths-only
   pass; Grade 8 and Grade 10 state the same stay).
7. **The name of the machine**: Armenia's sequence of
   tenses — the tenses agree across the report.
8. **The reporting-verb bank**: say/tell first, then
   add, remark, point out, whisper, yell (Bolivia's
   list) and Jordan's Grade 12 verbs with the impersonal
   frames — It is said that…, He is said to….
9. **Where the countries differ**: Bolivia's table
   keeps the past simple in place on its past row; the
   main line here (Mauritania, Nepal, Malaysia) steps it
   down to the past perfect, with the past simple also
   heard when the time is still clear. The difference is
   shown, not hidden.
10. **Do it yourself** (the three closing tasks in the
    lesson): the bridge walk, the truth test, the news
    report.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck on backshift: the staircase picture — find the
  tense, walk one step down. If the words were spoken a
  moment ago and are still true this minute, books allow
  the step to be skipped; when in doubt, step down.
- Stuck on say/tell: tell needs its person like a
  parcel needs an address — "She told me…", never "She
  told that…" with nobody there. Say takes the words
  alone, or "said to me".
- Stuck on pronouns: stand where the REPORTER stands,
  not where the first speaker stood — her "I" is your
  "she", her "my" is your "her".
- This lesson's next door: Reported questions (same
  level) — the same bridge, for questions.
