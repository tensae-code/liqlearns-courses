**Acceptance count: taught in 9 countries (Armenia,
Bolivia, Cambodia, Jordan, Kuwait, Malaysia, Mauritania,
Nepal, Sudan).** Mauritania prints the fullest single
chart and works the pronoun changes one by one before
it. Cambodia names the machine the one-step-back rule at
Grade 9 and splits say/tell into collocations at Grade
12. Nepal is the set's deepest ladder: truths-only at
Grade 7 (its limit stated plainly), the full system at
Grade 8, and named units for statements, questions and
commands at Grade 10. Armenia supplies the system's
formal name, sequence of tenses. Jordan caps the lesson
with the reporting-verb bank and the impersonal frames;
its Grade 12 books teach no basic backshift unit — they
build on it as prior knowledge, as filed. Bolivia's
table is a full witness with two printed differences,
both shown in the lesson rather than silently corrected:
its past row keeps the past simple, and one would-row
prints "has to" unshifted; the lesson teaches the
majority line (past perfect; had to). Sudan's Grade 9
book introduces the shape in a Look box and with says;
Kuwait's 10B converts a whole text and gathers the
changes in its reference.
