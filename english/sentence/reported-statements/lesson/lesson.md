## Do it first
Your friend says to you: "I am hungry. I will buy bread
today." An hour later you meet her sister, who asks:
what did she say? You do not play a recording. You carry
the message across in your own sentence — and your own
language bends the words a little as you carry them.
English bends them by fixed rules: the tense steps back,
the pronouns turn, "today" turns too. If you know the
tenses — those earlier lessons help here — you own every
piece of this. Start here if you like; the whole machine
is explained below. (Quoting her EXACT words, with speech
marks, is a different lesson in this level — here we
retell.)

## Sub lesson 1 — Reported Statements Frame and Staircase
**The frame.** A report is one sentence carrying another:
**She said (that) she was hungry.** The word **that** can
stay or go. Two verbs open the frame, and they are not
twins. **Say** takes the words alone: "He **said** (that)
he was tired." **Tell** must carry its person — the one
who hears it: "He **told me** (that) he was tired."
Cambodia's Grade 12 book works the pair directly (Unit
16), and Malaysia's Form 3 teaches statements with
backshift, pronoun and time-expression changes and say
vs tell in the same unit (Unit 10).

**The staircase — backshift.** Because the words were
spoken BEFORE you report them, the tense walks one step
back. Mauritania's Grade 12 chart (Lesson 11, p.35) is
the fullest in the set, and Cambodia's Grade 9 book
names the whole idea the **one step back rule** (Unit
31): present simple → **past simple** ("I like tea" →
he said he **liked** tea) · present continuous → **past
continuous** (am living → **was living**) · past simple
→ **past perfect** (bought → **had bought**) · present
perfect → **past perfect** (haven't seen → **hadn't
seen**) · will → **would** · shall → **should** · can →
**could** · may → **might** · must → **had to**. Five
verbs refuse the stairs because they are already at the
bottom: **would, could, should, might, ought to** do not
change. [a picture of a staircase with the tenses on its
steps, every reported sentence walking one step down]
Walk one sentence down it now, out loud.

## Sub lesson 2 — Reported Statements Pronouns and Pointing Words
**Pronouns and pointing words turn too.** The reporter
stands in a different place from the first speaker, so
the little words turn: I → he/she, we → they, my → his/
her (Mauritania works the pronoun and possessive changes
one by one before its chart). The words that point at
the first speaker's here-and-now turn with them — Nepal's
Grade 8 book lists the turns (p.253): **today → that
day, here → there, this → that** (and with them: now →
then, tomorrow → the next day, yesterday → the day
before). Sudan's Grade 9 book introduces the whole move
in a Look box with the shape bare: direct words beside
"He said he wanted…" (Unit 5, Lesson 5), and practises
reporting with **says** for things still standing
("Samira says her ambition is to expand", Unit 6).

## Sub lesson 3 — Reported Statements Truths and Verb Bank
**say/tell collocations.** The pair also split a set of
fixed phrases — Cambodia Grade 12 lists them: you
**say** sorry, say yes, say a prayer; you **tell** the
truth, **tell** a lie, **tell** a joke, **tell** a
secret. Kuwait's Over to You 10B practises statements
with backshift by converting a bank manager's whole text
(Unit 9), and its grammar reference gathers the tense,
pronoun, time and place changes together with other
reporting verbs (reference pp.65–70).

**The name of the machine.** Armenia's Grade 10 book
gives the system its formal name — the **sequence of
tenses** (agreement of tenses), a Grammar Spot worked on
a passage from Wilde's *The Canterville Ghost*: once the
reporting verb stands in the past, every tense after it
must agree by stepping back. Same machine, grown-up
name.

**The truth exception.** One family refuses the stairs:
a **universal truth** or standing fact keeps the present
tense, because it is as true at reporting time as at
speaking time. Nepal's Grade 7 book teaches reporting
through this door alone — its Unit 19 reports statements
only to show that facts and truths stay present: "The
teacher told us that the Sun **sets** in the west." Its
honest limit is stated in the filing and kept here: that
book teaches no backshift and no reported questions or
commands — truths only. Grade 8 (Unit 19, reported speech
in full) and Grade 10 (Unit 2, reported statements with
backshift and reporting verbs) state the same stay
inside the whole system.

**The reporting-verb bank.** Said and told are the plain
carriers; the books stock richer ones. Bolivia's Year 6
table (Topic 2, p.215) — a full backshift table of its
own — lists the reporting verbs **tell, say, add, point
out, remark, whisper, yell, order, ask**: whisper and
yell carry the voice with them. Malaysia's Form 5
devotes a second grammar pass to reporting verbs and
their patterns (Unit 10, Grammar 2, p.127), and Jordan's
Grade 12 builds a whole unit-half on them: Semester 1's
Unit 7 teaches reporting verbs (5A, p.22) on top of its
reported-speech pass, and Semester 2's Unit 6 adds the
**impersonal frames** — "**It is said that** the bridge
is a hundred years old", "He **is said to** know five
languages" — where no speaker is named at all.

**Where the countries differ.** One honest difference,
shown rather than hidden: Bolivia's printed table keeps
the **past simple in place** on its past row ("I bought
a laptop" → "He said he bought a laptop", as printed),
where Mauritania's chart steps it down to the past
perfect. The main line taught here is the majority one —
step it down (had bought) — and the stepped-down form is
never wrong; Bolivia's kept form is heard where the time
stays perfectly clear. Bolivia's table also prints one
unshifted "has to" inside a would-row; this lesson
teaches the corrected step (had to), as its own must →
had to row requires.

## Do it yourself
1. **The bridge walk.** Take five direct sentences —
   one present, one past, one perfect, one will, one can
   — and report each with said (that)…: every tense one
   step down, every pronoun turned, and one today →
   that day inside the set.
2. **The truth test.** Report these two the teacher's
   way: "Water boils at 100 degrees." "I boiled the
   water this morning." Only one of them may keep its
   present tense — say why, in one line.
3. **The news report.** Invent a short announcement by a
   head teacher or a bank manager (three sentences), then
   write the news version: one line with told us, one
   with added or pointed out from the verb bank, and one
   impersonal line — It is said that…
