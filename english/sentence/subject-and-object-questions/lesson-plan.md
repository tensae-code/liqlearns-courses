# Lesson plan — Subject and object questions

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Subject and object questions
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 21, "Questions That
Check" — the second lesson of the level (CURRICULUM.md),
after Question tags.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the two shapes of
a who/what question — when the question word IS the
subject, it asks with no helper and the verb keeps its
statement shape ("Who broke it?"); when it asks about
the object, the ordinary question machine runs ("Who did
you see?") — plus the negative question ("Didn't you
see it?") that checks in surprise.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Building a
sentence (subject and object are named there) and on
Asking with question words (the helper machinery comes
straight back). It closes Level 21.
**What the learner can do at the end:** decide, for any
missing piece, whether it is the doer or the done-to;
build the matching question with no stray did in subject
questions and no missing did in object questions; and
use a negative question to check in surprise.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Building a sentence (who does it /
  what receives it) and Asking with question words.
  Recommended, never forced.
- Question tags (the lesson before this one) check a
  whole statement; this lesson checks which piece of the
  sentence is missing. Different machines, same level.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a broken cup on a kitchen
   floor, with two children pointing at it and a big
   question mark over the EMPTY space where the breaker
   stood.** Written slot: "a picture of a broken cup with
   nobody beside it." What the student does: the student
   asks the picture its question — "Who broke it?" — and
   hears that no did is needed: the missing piece is the
   doer himself.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of a girl at a market stall
   holding up an empty basket, looking along a row of
   fruit.** Written slot: "a picture of a shopper whose
   basket is still empty." What the student does: the
   student asks what she bought — "What did she buy?" —
   and hears did arrive, because the missing piece is the
   thing bought, not the buyer.
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of an arrow leaving a boy's hand
   and landing on a ball: DOER on the left, DONE-TO on
   the right, the arrow labelled with a verb.** Written
   slot: "a picture of an action arrow from the doer to
   the done-to." What the student does: before asking any
   question in this lesson, the student points — is my
   missing piece at the arrow's start or at its end?
4. **Slot 4 — a picture of a boy at an empty door,
   eyebrows up in surprise, with a clock showing the
   lesson already started.** Written slot: "a picture of
   surprise at something that did NOT happen." What the
   student does: the student says the negative question —
   "Didn't you come?" — and hears it is a surprised check,
   not an ordinary question.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first** (lesson/lesson.md): the broken cup —
   two questions about one accident, said first in the
   learner's own language.
2. **The answer test**: answer the question in your
   head; the piece your answer replaces tells you which
   question you are holding (Cambodia Grade 11's
   distinguishing work, Unit 31B).
3. **Subject questions**: the question word stands in
   the subject's seat — no do/does/did, the verb wears
   its ordinary statement shape, third person -s included
   ("Who lives here?"; Cambodia Grade 11 Unit 31B;
   Malaysia Form 3 Unit 7).
4. **Object questions**: the ordinary machine — helper
   first, subject, plain verb ("Who did you see?"; "What
   did she buy?"; Cambodia Grade 11 Unit 31A teaches
   object questions first, before the subject kind).
5. **The two side by side**: one accident, both
   questions — "Who broke the cup?" / "What did the boy
   break?" (Cambodia Grade 12 Unit 11B sets subject and
   object questions together, on its space quiz).
6. **Negative questions**: Didn't you…? Aren't you…? —
   the surprised check, named in Malaysia Form 3 Unit 7's
   grammar list beside subject and object questions.
7. **Do it yourself** (the three closing tasks in the
   lesson): the accident report, the market round, the
   surprise check.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck on which shape: answer the question with a full
  sentence. If your answer's FIRST piece (the doer) is
  what the question wants, it is a subject question — no
  did. If the answer's LAST piece is wanted, run the
  ordinary machine with did.
- Stuck on the -s: in a subject question the verb cannot
  see its subject (the subject is the question), so it
  dresses as if the answer were he/she/it: Who lives…?
  What happens…?
- This level's next door: Level 22, reported speech —
  where these same questions get retold in other words.
