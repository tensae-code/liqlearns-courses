# Hands-on — Subject and object questions

You do it, we guide you. Everything here is done with a
companion or the Explain Companion, out loud.

1. **The accident report.** Stage a tiny accident with
   objects on a table (a cup "falls", a paper tears, a
   chair moves). One person is the detective and must ask
   six questions — three about the doer (Who moved the
   chair?) and three about the done-to (What did you
   move?). The other answers in full sentences. Swap
   roles; a stray did in a subject question costs a point,
   a missing did in an object question costs a point.
2. **The market round.** Lay out five objects. One
   person secretly "buys" (touches) one and the detective
   asks object questions — What did you buy? Which one
   did you choose? Then the buyer hides and the detective
   asks the stall-keeper subject questions — Who bought
   the bread? Who paid with coins?
3. **The answer test, as a game.** The companion says an
   answer sentence — "The cat drank the milk." The
   learner must ask BOTH questions that fit it: Who drank
   the milk? (doer) and What did the cat drink? (done-to).
   Five sentences, ten questions.
4. **The surprise check.** The companion acts out small
   surprises: an empty chair where a friend should sit, an
   uneaten lunch, homework not done. For each one the
   learner makes the negative question — Didn't you eat?
   Aren't you coming? Didn't she do it? — eyebrows up,
   the surprised voice.
