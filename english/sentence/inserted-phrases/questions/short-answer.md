## Short answer

1. Fence the insertion: "The market which opens at dawn sells fresh fish." (Sub lesson 1)
   **Answer:** "The market, which opens at dawn, sells fresh fish." — two commas around "which opens at dawn".

2. Prove with the lift-out test that the fenced part of "My teacher, a kind woman, tells stories" is an insertion. (Sub lesson 1)
   **Answer:** Lift out "a kind woman" and "My teacher tells stories" still stands whole — so it is an insertion, correctly fenced.

3. This sentence is wrongly fenced: "The bus, which was late arrived at noon." Why? (Sub lesson 1)
   **Answer:** Only one comma stands — the fence opens before the insertion but never closes after it. An insertion in mid-sentence takes a pair: "The bus, which was late, arrived at noon."

4. Re-fence this insertion loudly: "The winner (a girl from our class) held up the cup." (Sub lesson 2)
   **Answer:** "The winner — a girl from our class — held up the cup." Dashes replace parentheses for emphasis.

5. A quotation reads: "He promised the road by June." Inside it, clarify who "he" is. (Sub lesson 2)
   **Answer:** "He [the mayor] promised the road by June." The added words sit in square brackets, confessing they are yours.
