# Lesson plan — Participle adjectives

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Participle adjectives
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 30, "Dressing a Sentence" (CURRICULUM.md / CATCH-UP-PLAN.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the -ed / -ing adjective pair made from one verb — bored vs boring, tired vs tiring, interested vs interesting: -ed names the feeling in the person who feels it, -ing names the thing that causes the feeling.
**What the learner can do at the end:** choose the right coat for any feeler/cause pair live (the film is boring, I am bored); build the pair from a new verb (surprise, excite, confuse, amaze); and repair the classic swap — *I am boring — on sight, saying who feels and what causes it.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Describing words in a sentence and Adjective order (same level). Recommended, never forced — this lesson stands alone for a learner who lands on it directly (DIRECT-ENTRY rule).
- Speaker convention: the explanation is read in the learner's main language; the TARGET forms are never translated and are read in the English voice.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a boy asleep in front of a bright, loud television, the boy captioned BORED and the television captioned BORING. What the student does: the student points at each caption and says who feels it and what causes it, before the rule is named.**
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of a roller coaster: the riders' faces excited, the ride itself drawn wild and fast. What the student does: the student dresses both sides — the riders are excited; the ride is exciting.**
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of a student staring at a page of tangled arrows, a question mark over her head. What the student does: the student says both sentences: "She is confused. The page is confusing."**

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first**: one film or lesson that made the learner sleepy, named in their own language — who felt it, what caused it.
2. **The contrast sets**: "I feel bored. This movie bores me. This movie is boring" (Albania, Akelius Chapter 36 — teacher's-guide evidence, worded so).
3. **The feelings unit**: –ing vs –ed adjectives (boring/bored, amazing/amazed) to describe situations and feelings (Cambodia Grade 10, Unit 11, printed pp.59–64).
4. **Describing people**: participle adjectives as a named objective (Mongolia IX, Unit 1).
5. **The pair in review**: "The movie was very boring / The student was very tired" (Egypt, Secondary 12 Term 1, Review 2).
6. **Do it yourself**: the three closing tasks.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck on a swap: ask the two questions — WHO feels it? (-ed) WHAT causes it? (-ing) A person can be both in different sentences (a boring teacher bores; the teacher is boring, the class is bored).
- Stuck on spelling: the pair is built from the verb (bore → bored/boring; tire → tired/tiring); the coat changes, the verb's body does not.
- Stuck on *I am boring: it is grammatical — it means YOU bore other people. If you mean the feeling, the coat is -ed.
- This lesson's next door: Stative verbs (same level) — the verbs of feeling and thinking that refuse the continuous coat.
