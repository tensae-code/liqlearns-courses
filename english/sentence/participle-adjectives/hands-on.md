# Hands-on — Participle adjectives

You do it, we guide you. Everything here is done in the
learner's real day, with a companion or the Explain
Companion. The TARGET forms are always said in English;
the talk around them may be in the learner's own language.

1. **Feelings charades.** One person mimes a feeling (bored, excited, confused, surprised) without words. The other must say BOTH sentences before the next mime: "You are bored. The lesson is boring." — feeler with -ed, invented cause with -ing. Six feelings each.
2. **The film-review pair.** Talk about three real films, books or lessons you both know. For each, produce the pair together: the thing reviewed in -ing, yourselves in -ed ("The match was exciting. We were excited."). Disagree freely — the coats must still land on the right owners.
3. **Swap patrol.** Your companion reads eight sentences, three of them swapped (*I am interesting in football. The news was shocked.). Catch each swap, say who feels and what causes, and read the repaired sentence whole.
