# Lesson plan — The Ellipsis

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** The Ellipsis
**Lesson:** the-ellipsis — the three dots that stand where words are left out, where speech pauses, and where speech trails away
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 10 — Building a Sentence
**The one thing:** an ellipsis (…) is three dots in a row: it stands in the place of words that are left out, it marks a pause, and it shows speech trailing off unfinished
**This lesson's own level:** Punctuation lesson: the mark first, then its jobs — leaving words out, then pausing and trailing off.

## Why this lesson exists

No lesson taught the ellipsis itself. Jamaica's Grades 7–9 English Language/Literature guide names it three school years running in its own right — "the ellipsis: replacing repeated words in dialogue" (Grade 8), "ellipsis in writing: marking pauses and trailing off in drama dialogue; shortening long quotations in news reports" (Grade 9) — and the Grade 6 Exploratory Core guide lists ellipses among the punctuation marks to be used appropriately. The routing was checked before building: capital-and-full-stop carries one appendix row (Syria) that names the ellipsis in a whole list of marks, and the grammar lessons use the word for words left understood in clauses — neither teaches the mark and its jobs. The expansion diff marked the topic NEW LESSON NEEDED. Under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06) it gets its own lesson; its sources are the Jamaica guides named in lesson/sources.md, curriculum evidence cited as such — the rows rest on no pupil's book.

## Sub lessons

1. **Leaving words out** — the ellipsis stands where words are dropped: repeated words left out in dialogue, and long quotations shortened without changing what they say
2. **Pauses and speech that trails off** — the ellipsis marks a pause, a hesitation, and speech that fades away unfinished; the mark takes its place among the other punctuation marks

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is forced first — this lesson stands alone: it needs only sentences and dialogue the learner can already read and write. The capital-and-full-stop and speech-marks lessons help but are not required first.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- Name the ellipsis and show what it looks like: three dots in a row.
- Use an ellipsis where repeated words are left out of dialogue.
- Shorten a long quotation with an ellipsis, keeping the meaning whole.
- Write dialogue where a speaker pauses, hesitates, or trails off, marked with an ellipsis.
- Tell the ellipsis apart from a full stop and from the other marks it stands beside.

## What you need
Something to write with.

## The run of the lesson
1. **Do it first** — the learner reads a short dialogue with a repeated line and tries to shorten it before the mark is taught (lesson/lesson.md).
2. Work through sub lesson 1: leaving words out.
3. Work through sub lesson 2: pauses and speech that trails off.
4. ADD AUDIO: the dialogue read aloud twice — once with the pause the ellipsis marks, once flat — the learner hears what the three dots do.

## Add-ons
- Dialogue trim: learners take a page of dialogue from a story they know and mark every place a repeated word can be dropped behind an ellipsis.
- Trail-off reading: in pairs, one learner reads trail-off lines aloud, fading the voice exactly where the dots stand; the partner points to the mark as it is heard.

## If the learner is stuck
If a learner scatters dots everywhere, go back to the two jobs: the dots must either stand for words that are truly left out, or mark a real pause or trail-off in speech. If neither is true, the dots come out.
