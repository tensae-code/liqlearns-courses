## Short answer

1. Write Ben's answer shorter, with an ellipsis: Ana asks, "Are you walking to school?" Ben answers in full: "I am walking to school." (Sub lesson 1)
   **Answer:** "I am walking …" — the repeated words "to school" fall away behind the dots.

2. A speaker's sentence is: "We cleaned the yard, we swept the steps, we washed the gate and we fed the goats." A report prints: "We cleaned the yard … and we fed the goats." What did the ellipsis do, and what must the reader trust? (Sub lesson 1)
   **Answer:** It cut the middle steps out of the quotation; the reader trusts that what stays still says what the speaker said.

3. Write one line of dialogue where the speaker pauses to think, and mark the pause. (Sub lesson 2)
   **Answer:** Any line with an ellipsis at a thinking pause, e.g. "The train leaves at … let me check … six."

4. Write one line of dialogue that trails off unfinished, and mark it. (Sub lesson 2)
   **Answer:** Any unfinished line closed by an ellipsis, e.g. "I wish I had never opened that door …"

5. How is an ellipsis different from a full stop? (Sub lesson 2)
   **Answer:** A full stop ends a finished sentence; an ellipsis shows something unfinished — words left out, a pause, or speech fading away.
