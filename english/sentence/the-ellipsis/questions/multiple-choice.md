## Multiple choice

1. What does an ellipsis look like? (Sub lesson 1)
   a) Two dots, one over the other  b) Three dots in a row  c) A short line
   **Answer: b) Three dots in a row.**

2. "Are you going to market?" — "I am going …" What do the dots stand for? (Sub lesson 1)
   a) The words left out — "to market"  b) The end of the talk  c) A question
   **Answer: a)** — the ellipsis replaces the repeated words.

3. A news report shortens a long sentence with an ellipsis. What must stay true? (Sub lesson 1)
   a) The shortened sentence must keep the speaker's meaning  b) The shortened sentence must be louder  c) The dots must come first
   **Answer: a)** — the cut must never change what the speaker said.

4. "If only I had caught the bus …" What are the dots doing here? (Sub lesson 2)
   a) Ending the sentence  b) Showing speech trailing off unfinished  c) Joining two lists
   **Answer: b)** — the voice fades and the sentence never ends.

5. "I think the shop opens at … nine? No, ten." What do the dots mark? (Sub lesson 2)
   a) A pause while the speaker thinks  b) Words quoted from a book  c) The end of a list
   **Answer: a)** — a pause, a hesitation inside the speech.
