**Acceptance count: taught in 16 countries (Egypt, Mongolia, Laos, China, Indonesia, Iran, Sierra Leone, Jordan, Kazakhstan, Iraq, Ethiopia, South Africa, Syria, Nepal, Armenia, Vietnam).**
Mongolia's "That's Mine" unit carries the whole lesson —
the 's box, the whose matching, the three-rung box, the
game; Egypt's youngest books hold the partner forms from
the first terms; China adds the complete system twice over
— the 's answers and standalone mine/yours in one unit,
then the full pronoun set, its included, in another;
Indonesia works the 's and adjective rungs under a Whose
question and pairs personal with possessive forms at
Grade 7; Iran sets 's against of and tables the full
adjective set; Sierra Leone tables the standalone
pronouns whole in JSS 1 Term 2 and sets pronoun against
adjective as one contrast in Term 3; Jordan works the
partner adjectives our/your/their in Grade 3, answers
Whose…? with the standalone mine and his/hers in the
same grade, and explains the 's shop names as of in
Grade 5; Kazakhstan names possessive pronouns at
Grade 4 in two series at once — beside the possessive
adjectives in the jobs module, and as the mine/yours
pair in Top Stars — and pairs possessive adjectives
and pronouns again at Grade 5; Iraq teaches the pronouns
against the adjectives at Grade 5 — lost luggage at the
airport in the Pupil's Book, and the stand-alone rule
stated in the Activity Book's LOOK box, the noun after
*mine* shown crossed out. Ethiopia teaches possession as a named grammar point in Grade 4 — pronouns and possession with his/her and "Whose…?" — and meets possessive pronouns in a Grade 5 possessives exercise that uses "ours"; it does not table the full standalone mine/yours system. South Africa stocks this lesson in full: Grade 4 HL teaches possessive pronouns with the adjective and pronoun forms contrasted — my/mine, your/yours — Grade 3 HL teaches the possessive pronoun set, and Grade 3 FAL teaches my, your, his, her, its, our, their; the workbook sets present adjective and pronoun forms together under the one possessive heading. Syria teaches the standalone set whole in Grade 5 — mine, yours, his, hers, ours, theirs under a Whose…? question — and sets possessive adjectives against possessive pronouns in Grade 6. Nepal carries mine and yours from Grade 2's "Mine is big," through Grade 3's whose-chant pronoun set (keyed by its true companion guide), to Grade 5's whole possessions unit with the I/my/mine table; Armenia carries possessive pronouns as a headed grammar lesson in Grade 4 — the my / your / his / her / our / their set against the subject pronouns, with the your/you're contrast (Unit 1.2, printed p.17) — and the standalone forms (mine/yours type) at work for comparisons in Grade 7 (Unit 2, printed p.35); Other closed countries join as their
books are read (see READING-QUEUE.md).
