# Hands-on — Mine and yours

The lesson played at a real lost-property table.

## The lost-property office
As in the lesson, but kept going all week: any thing found
ownerless in the house or class goes to the office pile.
Claims are made aloud, in full sentences, in the right
form, before the thing is released — and the office keeps a
written claims book: thing, claimant, the exact sentence
that won it. Wrong forms are copied into the book too,
crossed out and corrected: the book of errors is the real
textbook here.

## Ladder labels
Label your own school things three ways each — sticky notes
down the ladder: "Sara's pen / her pen / hers." A week of
reading your own labels at every use burns the ladder in
deeper than any drill.

## Whose line
Stand in a line with your things held up. A caller walks
the line asking the book's question — "Whose is this?" —
randomly. Owners answer with the alone word; neighbours
answer for each other with name-tags. Speed rises only when
the forms stay right.

## Family shelf
Photograph or draw your family's shelf (shoes, cups, tools).
Under each thing, write its owner THREE ways. The shelf
poster goes on the wall; visitors get quizzed — the house
learns the lesson by living under it.
