# Mine and yours — true or false

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — The Name-Tag and the Partner Forms · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — The Alone Forms · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — The Law Under Pressure.
Say true or false. If it is false, say why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** "Carlo's hat" and "his hat" can claim the
same hat.
**True** — two rungs of one ladder (Mongolia's boxes).

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** "Yours book is here" is fine, because yours
is a strong word.
**False** — alone forms never hold a noun after them: "Your
book is here," or "The book is yours."

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** His changes shape between the families.
**False** — his is the one word that stays: "his hat" /
"the hat is his."

**4. (Sub lesson 3)** "Its" has an alone form like the others,
just rarely used.
**False** — there is no alone form of its in English. The
table's gap is the truth.

**5. (Sub lesson 1)** A partner form can end a sentence: "The
pen is my."
**False** — it stops dead; partner forms must hold a noun.
"The pen is mine."

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** "Whose is it?" is answered fastest with an
alone form.
**True** — "It's hers." The name-tag answers fullest;
rungs are chosen, not ranked in worth.
