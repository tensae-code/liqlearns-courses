# Mine and yours — short answer

Answer in a word or a short line.

**1. (Level 1)** Climb the ladder for Sam's pens (Mongolia's
own): name-tag, partner, alone.
**Answer:** "Sam's pens — his pens — the pens are his."

**2. (Level 1)** Partner forms, by owner: I / you / she / we
/ they.
**Answer:** my — your — her — our — their.

**3. (Level 2)** Alone forms, same owners.
**Answer:** mine — yours — hers — ours — theirs.

**4. (Level 2)** Answer the book's game question: "Is it
yours, Naraa?" — it is Pavel's.
**Answer:** "No, it's his." (or the fullest rung: "No, it's
Pavel's.")

**5. (Level 3)** Fix it and name the fault: "The bag is her."
**Answer:** "The bag is hers" — a partner form cannot stand
alone at the end; the alone twin takes its place.

**6. (Level 3)** Why does the alone family have a gap at
"its"?
**Answer:** English simply has no alone form for its — the
gap is real, printed in the table as a gap, not an error.
