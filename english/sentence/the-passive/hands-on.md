# Hands-on — The passive

You do it, we guide you. Done with real things, in the
learner's real world.

1. **The famous-names quiz (Mongolia's own game).** Make
   five quiz cards the way the inventions unit does: a
   thing, a year, a name — "The first photograph was
   taken by Joseph Niepce." Trade cards with your
   companion; the reader hides the name, the guesser
   must answer in a full passive sentence to win the
   card.
2. **Turn the room around.** Pick five things in the
   room and say who made, built, painted, wrote or
   placed them — then turn every sentence around. Two
   must drop their doer (unknown or obvious): say why
   each drop is honest.
3. **The process walk.** Follow one made thing from
   start to finish — bread, a shirt, a cup of tea — and
   narrate it the passive way, the way processes are
   always told: the wheat is grown, it is cut, it is
   ground, the bread is baked, it is sold. No hands
   unless a hand is the famous part.
4. **The news minute.** Report three things that
   happened this week at school or on your street as a
   news reader: one with by (the name is the news), two
   without (unknown or obvious). Your companion checks
   each choice.
