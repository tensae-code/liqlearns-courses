# Lesson plan — The passive

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** The passive
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 18 — Deeper Sentences —
the third lesson of the unit (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the passive — be +
the past participle — the sentence turned around so the
receiver stands at the front, with by to name the doer
when the doer matters, and be wearing every tense coat
(present, past, future, perfect, modal).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The present
perfect (been is already inside it) and on Past actions'
participles (Unit 12). Its perfect coat is the last two
lessons' machinery turned around.
**What the learner can do at the end:** turn any active
sentence around and back; build the passive in the
present, past and future coats; add the perfect coat
(has been done) and the modal coat (can be turned); drop
or keep by on purpose; and say which chair the camera
sits on, and why.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The Turn-Around** — be + the past participle: turn
   an active sentence around and back again in the
   present and past coats, with by + the doer when the
   name is given. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Dropping the Doer** — most of the time by walks off:
   the doer unknown, obvious or not the point. (Was the
   dropping half of depth Level 2; the text stands before
   the coats.)
3. **The Coats** — be carries the tense: the future coat
   (will be), the modal coat (can be), the perfect coat
   (has/have been), and Libya's continuous row. (Was the
   coats half of depth Level 2.)
4. **Choosing the Coat's Owner** — active when the doer
   is the story, passive when the receiver is: choose on
   purpose and defend it. (Was depth Level 3.)
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The present perfect — its participle
  and its been are this lesson's moving parts.
  Recommended, never forced.
- The doer-dropping reasons (unknown, obvious, not the
  point) are taught inside this lesson, not assumed.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a light bulb with two arrows
   circling it: one pointing from Edison to the bulb,
   one from the bulb back to Edison.** Written slot: "a
   picture of Edison and the light bulb swapping places
   at the front of a sentence." What the student does:
   the student says the fact both ways — Edison invented
   the bulb; the bulb was invented by Edison — and feels
   the camera move.
2. **Slot 2 — a GIF (1–2 seconds) of a painting on an
   auction wall, a SOLD sign landing on it, no people
   anywhere.** Written slot: "a picture of a painting
   being sold, with nobody in the picture." What the
   student does: the student tries to answer "who sold
   it?" and discovers the sentence never needed the
   answer — that is the doer dropped on purpose.
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of five coats hanging on one
   hook, labelled NOW, BEFORE, LATER, DONE-BY-NOW, CAN.**
   Written slot: "a picture of the word be trying on five
   tense coats while the participle stands still." What
   the student does: the student dresses "The door is /
   was / will be / has been / can be locked" coat by coat
   and hears that only be changes.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first** (lesson/lesson.md): three no-hands
   facts — was invented, was painted, was sold — and the
   missing doers noticed.
2. **The shape**: be + past participle; be carries time
   and who.
3. **The turn-around**: Mongolia's Edison pair (p.86) —
   receiver to the front, doer behind by.
4. **By + agent**: famous names kept (Starley, Niepce —
   the inventions quiz, p.84); Red Sunset's two passives,
   one with by, one without (p.83).
5. **Dropping the doer**: unknown, obvious, not the
   point — the news/science seat.
6. **The coats**: present / past / future together
   (Mongolia, p.76); the perfect coat (Egypt Prep 9 T1,
   Tips p.17 — The homework has been done); the future
   coat against its active twin (Egypt, Tip p.45); the
   modal coat (Mongolia, p.131 — can be turned); Libya's
   five-tense table (p.82) as the whole row in one view,
   continuous coat named.
7. **Choosing**: doer is the story → active; receiver is
   the story, or doer unknown/obvious → passive.
8. **Do it yourself** (the three closing tasks): the
   invention quiz, turning the day around, the no-hands
   report.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck on the shape: participle never changes — only
  be moves. Dress the coats aloud: is locked, was
  locked, will be locked, has been locked, can be
  locked.
- Stuck on by: ask "is the doer's name the news?" If
  yes, keep by; if the name is unknown, obvious, or
  boring, drop it — the books' own sentences do both.
- Stuck turning around: find the receiver first (what
  did the verb hit?), put it at the front, then be +
  participle, then by + the old subject if it earns its
  place.
- This lesson's next door: Conditionals (same level) —
  sentences hung on if.
