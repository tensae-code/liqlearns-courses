# The passive — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Sentence · Lesson: The passive
Built from: Mongolia, English X (Unit 6, pp.83–86; Unit 5,
p.76; Unit 8, p.131; Grammar Reference) — Libya, English
for Libya Grade 9 Course Book (Unit 7, p.82) and the
Grammar Reference (pp.105–106) — Egypt, Preparatory 9
Term 1 (Unit 1, Tips p.17; Unit 3, Tip p.45) and
Preparatory 8 Term 1.

## Do it first
Read these facts the way the world reports them: "The
first sewing machine **was invented** in the 1880s."
"Red Sunset **was painted** in 1686." "In 1981 it **was
sold** for $500,000." Something is missing from every one
— the hands. Who sewed, who painted, who sold? The
sentences do not care. They put the THING it happened to
at the front and let the doer walk off. You already talk
this way about your own day: "my phone was stolen," "the
door was locked," "lunch is ready at one — it is made by
one." This lesson gives that move its name and its shape.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Turn-Around
**The shape.** The passive is **be + the past
participle** (Mongolia's Grammar Reference states it in
those words). The participle — the third shape from the
last two lessons — does the naming; **be** carries the
time and the who: The light bulb **was invented**. The
boots **are made** of leather. It **will be finished** on
Friday.

**The turn-around.** Mongolia's Grade 10 book discovers
it from a pair, side by side: active — "Edison **invented
the light bulb** in 1879." passive — "**The light bulb**
was invented **by Edison** in 1879." Watch what moved:
the receiver (the light bulb) steps to the front and
becomes the subject; the verb turns into be + participle;
the doer (Edison) slides to the end behind **by** —
kept, because here the doer is the famous part. The
book's unit even sets its aim in those words: the passive
with by + agent, with special emphasis on the names of
famous inventors. Its quiz runs on it: "The bicycle was
invented in 1885 **by Starley**." "The first photograph
was taken **by Joseph Niepce**."

## Sub lesson 2 — Dropping the Doer
**Dropping the doer.** Most of the time, by walks off.
Three reasons cover nearly every passive you will meet:
the doer is **unknown** ("My phone was stolen" — by whom?
nobody knows); the doer is **obvious** ("The thief was
arrested" — by the police, who else?); the doer is **not
the point** ("In 1981 it was sold for $500,000" — the
price is the news, not the seller; "Football boots are
made of leather or nylon" — factories make them, everyone
knows). News, science, instructions and processes live in
this shape because the world mostly reports what happened
to things.

## Sub lesson 3 — The Coats
**The coats — be carries the tense.** The participle
never changes; be wears every tense the sentence needs.
Mongolia's Grade 10 discovery lays the first three out
together (present is/are + participle, past was/were,
future will be), Libya's Grade 9 book prints the full
five-tense table on one page, and Egypt completes the
line:

- **Present**: The collections **are updated**
  frequently. Tea **is grown** in India.
- **Past**: The light bulb **was invented** in 1879.
  Red Sunset **was painted** in 1686.
- **Future**: **will be** + participle — Egypt's Tip
  shows it against its active pair: "We will finish this
  project next Friday" → "This project **will be
  finished** next Friday."
- **Perfect**: **have/has + been** + participle — Egypt
  again, where the perfect arrives active and passive
  together: "The homework **has been done**." "The
  Pyramids **have been visited**." (It is the present
  perfect you just built, turned around: been was already
  waiting inside it.)
- **Modal**: **can be** + participle — Mongolia: "Plastic
  bottles **can be turned** into a recycling bin." Egypt
  teaches the modal passive a year before its perfect
  coat; possibility and rules love this shape (it can be
  recycled; it must be worn).

Libya's table adds the continuous coat — **is being
built** — for a receiver mid-process; it is the same be
at work, wearing -ing's neighbour. Learn the five coats
as one row: am/is/are, was/were, will be, have/has been,
can be — all + the same participle.

## Sub lesson 4 — Choosing the Coat's Owner
**Choosing the coat's owner.** Active when the doer is
the story ("Edison invented the light bulb" — a lesson
about Edison). Passive when the receiver is the story
("The light bulb was invented in 1879" — a lesson about
the bulb), or when the doer is unknown, obvious, or
everyone. Neither is politer or grander; each puts the
camera on a different chair.

## Do it yourself
1. **The invention quiz, your turn.** Write five facts
   about things you use — who made them, when — the way
   Mongolia's quiz asks them: "The bicycle was invented
   in 1885 by Starley." Keep by where the name is famous;
   drop it where it is not.
2. **Turn the day around.** Take five active sentences
   from your own day (Mum cooked lunch. The teacher
   locked the door.) and turn each one around. Read both
   aloud and say which chair the camera should sit on —
   and why.
3. **The no-hands report.** Report your school or street
   as a news reader for one minute: what is built, sold,
   cleaned, repaired, grown there — no doers allowed
   unless their name is the news.
