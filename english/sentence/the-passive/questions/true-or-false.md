# The passive — true or false

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — The Turn-Around (item 1), except item 2, be wears the tense coats -> Sub lesson 3 — The Coats · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Dropping the Doer (item 3) · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 (item 4, the perfect coat) and Sub lesson 4 — Choosing the Coat's Owner (item 5).

Say true or false. If it is false, say why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** The passive is formed with be + past
participle.
**True** — Mongolia's discovery states it in those
words (English X, p.86).

**2. (Sub lesson 3)** In the passive, the participle changes
with every tense.
**False** — the participle never changes; be wears the
tense coats (is, was, will be, has been, can be).

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Every passive sentence must name its
doer with by.
**False** — by names the doer when the doer matters
(famous names); unknown, obvious or unimportant doers
are dropped ("In 1981 it was sold for $500,000" —
Mongolia, p.83).

**4. (Sub lesson 3)** "The homework has been done" is the
present perfect turned around.
**True** — have/has + been + participle: the perfect's
own been carries the passive (Egypt, Prep 9 T1, Tips
p.17).

**5. (Sub lesson 4)** The passive is always the politer,
grander choice.
**False** — neither coat is grander; the active puts the
camera on the doer, the passive on the receiver. Choose
by whose story it is.
