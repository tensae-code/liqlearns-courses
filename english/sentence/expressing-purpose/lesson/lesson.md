# Expressing Purpose

## Do it first

Pick up the nearest tool — a pen, a brush, a phone. What is it for? Try: "It is used for…" Go on — in this lesson you will learn the purpose words: what things are for, and what you act for.

## Sub lesson 1 — What is it used for? — used for + -ing

Every tool has a job, and English names it with **used for**:

- "What is a hoe **used for**?" — "A hoe is **used for making** the soil loose."
- "A toothbrush is **used for brushing** teeth."
- used for + **-ing** (or a noun: used for water).

Name three classroom tools and their jobs now.

## Sub lesson 2 — In order to, so as to — your aim, with to

Your own aim folds short with **to**:

- "**In order to** succeed, you need to work hard." — "I work hard **in order to** succeed."
- "**So as to** pass, she studies nightly." — **so as to** is in order to's twin, a little more formal.
- Both take the **base verb**: in order to succeed, so as to pass — never a whole clause after them.

Say two of your aims with in order to.

## Sub lesson 3 — So that — purpose with a whole clause

The aim can also open into a whole clause with **so that**:

- "I study **so that** I can succeed." (so that + subject + can/will + verb)
- "He saved money **so that** his children could learn."
- Test: if the aim needs its own subject or a can, it takes **so that**; if it is the same actor folded short, in order to / so as to.

Turn your two aims from sub lesson 2 into so that clauses.

## Say it to your companion

Show your companion three objects, one at a time. They must give each its job with used for. Then they give you two of their life aims — you return each twice: once with in order to, once with so that. Swap.

## Do It Yourself

Write six sentences: three tools with used for, two of your aims with in order to and so as to, and one aim with so that. Read them aloud.
