# Lesson plan — Building a sentence

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Building a sentence
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 10, "Building a Sentence" — the first lesson of the level (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** what a sentence is —
words in order that make a whole thought, built as WHO +
DOES WHAT: a subject, a verb, and the rest that completes it.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on all the word
levels before it —
the word kinds are its bricks (the WHO is a noun or pronoun;
the DOES WHAT is a verb) — and it is the first lesson of the
Sentence title. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown changing — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the whole Word Kinds level (nouns,
  verbs, pronouns above all). Recommended, never forced.
- The sentence's dress — capital letter first, full stop
  last — is the NEXT lesson in this level; here it appears
  only as the frame every example wears. Tenses (now, before,
  later) are Level 12; questions are Level 11. This
  lesson is the plain statement, built right.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Tell a sentence from a not-sentence: a sentence makes a
   whole thought; "the big cat" alone leaves you waiting.
2. Split any plain sentence into its parts: the WHO (subject)
   and the DOES WHAT (the verb with its rest — Bolivia's
   Subject + Verb + Complement).
3. Build sentences from a three-column table the way
   Bangladesh's books drill them — seven subjects, seven
   verbs, seven endings, all true at once.
4. Fix a jumbled sentence by putting its words in the
   sentence order (the word-order work every book's checks
   use).

## What you need
Word cards or slips of paper. A pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner says about a picture (or
   out the window) first in broken pieces — "cat… black…
   roof…" — then as one whole thought; the lesson names what
   changed (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — whole thought test; the two parts
   (subject/predicate in Ghana's naming; S+V+C in Bolivia's);
   building from tables; order (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the parts part: a picture of word blocks —
     THE CAT · SAT · ON THE MAT — shuffling out of order and
     then clicking into line, the WHO block and the DOES-WHAT
     block in two colours, 1–2 seconds — the student says
     which colour is the who and which is the does-what as
     they land.
   - ADD IMAGE under the tables part: a picture of a
     three-column table like Bangladesh's — seven animals,
     eats, seven foods — with one built row standing lit →
     the student builds two more rows aloud from the columns
     in the image.
   - ADD AUDIO in the whole-thought part: a voice reading
     pairs — "the big cat…" (trailing off) against "The big
     cat sleeps." (whole) — the student calls "waiting!" or
     "whole!" before each pair ends.
3. **Say it to your companion** — build sentences about your
   companion from the parts: they name a WHO, you add a DOES
   WHAT, they judge: whole or waiting? (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the
     blocks building sentences, in the super-simple voice
     (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served
   at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — write five whole sentences about your
   day, split each into WHO / DOES WHAT, and unjumble three
   broken ones (lesson/lesson.md).

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- GIF — a picture of word blocks clicking into sentence
  order, in two colours (who / does-what) → the student names
  each colour's job as the blocks land.
- IMAGE — a picture of a three-column building table → the
  student builds two rows aloud.
- AUDIO — whole thoughts read against trailing pieces → the
  student calls "whole!" or "waiting!"
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later) → the student
  builds along with it.

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the
plan is the build sheet, so nothing the lesson needs gets forgotten.

## If the learner is stuck
- Cannot feel whole-vs-waiting → the hand test: say the piece
  and stop. If your listener asks "and…?" it is waiting. A
  sentence lets the listener rest.
- Loses the verb → every sentence must DO or BE something;
  hunt the doing word first (the Verbs lesson's test), the
  WHO is whoever does it.
- Builds "The cat. Sleeps." as two lines → spoken, it is one
  thought; written, the full stop is the frame — next
  lesson's whole job.
- Word order flipped from their first language → order IS
  meaning in English ("Dog bites man" ≠ "Man bites dog");
  Mongolia's checks drill word order for exactly this.
- This level's next lesson: Capital letter and full stop —
  the sentence's dress.
