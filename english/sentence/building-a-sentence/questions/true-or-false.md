# Building a sentence — true or false

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Whole or Waiting · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — The Two Parts · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Order and Growth.
Say true or false. If it is false, say why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** "On the big roof" is a sentence.
**False** — it leaves you waiting: no subject doing
anything, no whole thought.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** A sentence can be only two words long.
**True** — "Birds fly" is whole: subject + verb, thought
finished.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** The complement can be left off when the
verb completes itself.
**True** — Bolivia's formula needs S + V always; C grows the
sentence when the verb calls for it.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** In "The parrot eats fruits," the verb is
"fruits."
**False** — the verb is "eats"; "fruits" is the complement.
The parrot is the subject.

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** "Grass eats the cow" and "The cow eats
grass" mean the same.
**False** — same words, opposite meaning. Order is meaning
in English.

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** A waiting-piece can be fixed by adding a
verb.
**True** — "The big cat" + "sleeps" stands whole; the verb
is the leg that was missing.
