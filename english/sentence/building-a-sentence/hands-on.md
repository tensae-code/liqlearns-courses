# Hands-on — Building a sentence

The lesson built with your hands, the way Bangladesh's tables
build it.

## Block sentences
Write words on slips: five WHOs (cat, Mita, the tiger, I, my
mother), five DOINGS (eats, sleeps, is, goes, reads), five
RESTS (grass, at six, happy, to school, a book). Lay out a
row — one of each — and read the sentence aloud. Whole?
Waiting? Shuffle the DOINGS and rebuild. Ten sentences, each
judged out loud before the next is laid.

## The table, live
Make Bangladesh's table on paper: seven animals, one verb
(eats), seven foods. Build all seven rows aloud, then write
three. Then a second table of your own: seven people × one
verb × seven places. The columns are training wheels; ride
them until the building is balance, not effort.

## Jumble jar
Write each word of a true sentence on its own slip, shake
the slips in a jar, tip them out, and rebuild the sentence
against a partner's clock. Five sentences, growing longer:
three words, four, five, six. Order is the game.

## Window sentences
Sit where you can see out. Say one whole sentence about
everything you see move: "A bus goes past." "Two boys run."
Write the best five and split them into WHO / DOES WHAT with
two colours of pencil.
