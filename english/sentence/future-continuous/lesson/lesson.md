# Future continuous — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Sentence · Lesson: Future continuous
Built from: Egypt, Secondary 11 Term 2 — Bolivia, the ministry teacher compendium (Year 4, Topic 5.2; the FUTURE CONTINUOUS heading also stands at Year-level work with its five-sentence writing task) — Malaysia, Form 5 (Unit 5, Grammar 2, p.63) — Uzbekistan, English Matters 9 (Unit 2).

*Soft hint only: if you know Going to: later (plans) and The past continuous (the moving scene — this lesson aims the same scene forward), this lesson will be easier — but it stands alone. Start here if this is the thing you want to learn.*

**How this lesson is read aloud.** The speaker button reads the explanation in your own main language. The TARGET forms — the English shapes in **bold** — are never translated and are always read in the English voice, then the reading returns to your language. The target is the thing itself; the words around it are the explanation.

## Do it first
Picture tomorrow at one exact moment — say, tomorrow at eight in the morning — in your own language, as a moving scene: "At eight tomorrow, I will be travelling. The sun will be shining. My family will be eating." Nothing finished, nothing just decided — the film of tomorrow, paused mid-action. English builds that picture with will be + -ing, and you own every piece.

## Sub lesson 1 — Future Continuous Shape
**The shape.** The future continuous is **will be + verb-ing**: I **will be working**. She **will be sleeping**. They **will be travelling**. Will never changes, be never changes; the verb wears the -ing coat — the same moving coat as the past continuous, aimed forward instead of back. Egypt's Secondary 11 Term 2 run carries the form in its future work, and Bolivia's compendium prints a FUTURE CONTINUOUS heading with the full affirmative, negative and interrogative set.

## Sub lesson 2 — Future Continuous Future Moment
**Its one job — in progress at a future moment.** The tense always stands at a future POINT and looks at the action moving around it: "**This time tomorrow**, I **will be travelling** to Santa Cruz" — Bolivia's own writing-task model ("I will be travelling to Santa Cruz the next Monday"). Its favourite time words name the point: **this time tomorrow**, **at eight tonight**, **all day tomorrow**, **the next Monday**. Malaysia's Form 5 book teaches it at Unit 5 beside the future perfect — the moving scene and the finished-by-then result as a pair (Grammar 2, p.63). Uzbekistan's English Matters 9 reaches it at Unit 2, in the set's grammar peak.

**Asking and denying.** Will steps to the front: **Will** you **be using** your car tonight? — Yes, I will. / No, I won't. That question is also the polite way to ask about someone's plans without pushing — you ask about the scene, not their decision. The not-form: He **won't be coming** this week.

## Sub lesson 3 — Future Continuous Neighbours
**The neighbours, kept apart.** A decision made while speaking wears plain will (*I'll get the door!*). A plan decided before wears going to (*We are going to visit on Sunday*). This tense is neither: it paints the action MOVING at a future moment, whatever was decided. "At nine, I will be studying" — the film, not the decision.

1. **Media slot 1 — a picture of a train window view at sunrise tomorrow, fields moving past, with a clock showing eight o'clock.** What you do: the student says what the traveller will be doing at that clock time — one will be + -ing sentence — before reading the shape.
2. **Media slot 2 — a picture of a house drawn at night with every room lit and busy — cooking, reading, mending — under the words THIS TIME TOMORROW.** What you do: the student gives one moving action for each room, all in this tense.
3. **Media slot 3 — a picture of a calendar page for next Monday with a small aeroplane drawn across the whole day.** What you do: the student says the all-day sentence: "Next Monday, I will be travelling all day."

## Say it to your companion
Play the future camera with your companion. One names a future moment — "tomorrow at six," "this time next week," "at midnight tonight." The other must answer with the moving scene, whole: what they will be doing, and what two other people will be doing at that same moment. Five moments each.

## Do it yourself
1. **This time tomorrow.** Write four true will be sentences about tomorrow at four different clock times.
2. **The polite ask.** Write three questions in this tense to ask a family member about tonight (Will you be using…? Will you be going…?) — then really ask one, and write the answer.
3. **Sort the futures.** Write one sentence each about next week: a decision made now (plain will), a plan already made (going to), and a scene in progress at a named moment (this tense). Label each.
