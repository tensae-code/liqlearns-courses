# Lesson plan — Capital letter and full stop

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Capital letter and full stop
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3, Unit 1 "Building a
Sentence" — the second lesson of the unit (CURRICULUM.md).
Building it completes Unit 1.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** how a written sentence
is dressed — a CAPITAL letter opens it, a FULL STOP closes
it — and which words wear capitals wherever they stand:
names of persons, places, days, and months.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands directly on Building a
sentence (the learner can build the whole thought; now they
dress it for the page). It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown changing — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Building a sentence. Recommended,
  never forced.
- The question mark is named here as the full stop's sibling
  (Ghana opens punctuation with the pair), but asking
  questions is Unit 2's own lesson — here it only closes a
  written question. Commas have their own later home;
  Bangladesh's first punctuation exercise already seats the
  comma beside the pair, and it is shown, not taught, here.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Open every written sentence with a capital letter and
   close it with a full stop — Ghana's strand in one line:
   capitals "to begin the first word of a sentence," FULL
   STOPS "at the end of sentences."
2. Give capitals to the names that always wear them:
   persons, places, days of the week, months (Bangladesh's
   grammar focus box).
3. Dress a bare paragraph: find where each sentence begins
   and ends, and dress all of them.
4. Close a written question with a question mark, not a full
   stop.

## What you need
Paper and two pencils — one for the words, one (any other
colour) for the dressing.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner reads a bare, undressed
   paragraph aloud and feels the sentences run together; the
   lesson then dresses the same paragraph (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the opening capital; the closing
   stop; the always-capital names; the question mark
   (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the start: a picture of a bare sentence whose
     first letter GROWS tall and turns capital while a full
     stop stamps itself at the end, 1–2 seconds — the student
     says "capital opens, full stop closes" as it happens.
   - ADD IMAGE under the names part: a picture of a school
     signboard and a calendar page — names of a place, a
     person, a day, and a month standing in them → the student
     points at every capital and says why it stands tall.
   - ADD AUDIO in the dressing part: a voice reading the bare
     paragraph twice — first run-together, then with clear
     pauses at each full stop — the student raises a hand at
     every pause and says "stop."
3. **Say it to your companion** — dictate: your companion
   writes your sentences bare; you dress them together and
   read them back (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the
     dressing in the super-simple voice (made later, when
     videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — dress three bare sentences, write two
   of your own dressed from birth, and find the capitals in
   one page of any book (lesson/lesson.md).

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- GIF — a picture of a letter growing capital and a full stop
  stamping the end → the student says the rule as it happens.
- IMAGE — a picture of a signboard and calendar page → the
  student points at each capital and says why.
- AUDIO — a paragraph read bare, then dressed with pauses →
  the student raises a hand at every stop.
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later) → the student
  dresses along with it.

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the
plan is the build sheet, so nothing the lesson needs gets forgotten.

## If the learner is stuck
- Dresses every word's first letter → the rule is about
  PLACES, not words: the sentence's first word, and names.
  Two places only in this lesson.
- Forgets the stop but keeps the capital → read their page
  aloud in one breath, no pausing; the ear begs for the stop
  before the eye learns it.
- Capitals on common nouns ("Cat") → Bangladesh's box says
  names of persons, places and things — a NAME, not a kind.
  "Mita" wears one; "girl" does not, though both are her.
- This lesson completes Unit 1. Next: Unit 2 — Questions.
