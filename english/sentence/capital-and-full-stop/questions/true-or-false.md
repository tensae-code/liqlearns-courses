# Capital letter and full stop — true or false

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — The Two Edges · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — The Names · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Whole Texts.
Say true or false. If it is false, say why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Every word in a sentence starts with a
capital.
**False** — only the first word, and the names. The rest
stand small.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** A full stop closes a statement.
**True** (Ghana, B2.5.2.1).

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** "cat" needs a capital because it is the name
of an animal kind.
**False** — kinds stay small. A NAME — one particular cat,
"Nini" — wears the capital.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** Days of the week take capitals.
**True** — Ghana's strand says so outright; months too
(Bangladesh's rule).

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** A question can close with a full stop if it
is a quiet one.
**False** — questions close with a question mark, loud or
quiet (Ghana's pair).

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** The spoken voice does not need capitals or
stops to be understood.
**True** — the dress is for the PAGE: the ear hears pauses;
the eye needs the marks. (That is why dictation dresses by
ear.)
