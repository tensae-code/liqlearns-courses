# Capital letter and full stop — true or false

Say true or false. If it is false, say why.

**1. (Level 1)** Every word in a sentence starts with a
capital.
**False** — only the first word, and the names. The rest
stand small.

**2. (Level 1)** A full stop closes a statement.
**True** (Ghana, B2.5.2.1).

**3. (Level 2)** "cat" needs a capital because it is the name
of an animal kind.
**False** — kinds stay small. A NAME — one particular cat,
"Nini" — wears the capital.

**4. (Level 2)** Days of the week take capitals.
**True** — Ghana's strand says so outright; months too
(Bangladesh's rule).

**5. (Level 3)** A question can close with a full stop if it
is a quiet one.
**False** — questions close with a question mark, loud or
quiet (Ghana's pair).

**6. (Level 3)** The spoken voice does not need capitals or
stops to be understood.
**True** — the dress is for the PAGE: the ear hears pauses;
the eye needs the marks. (That is why dictation dresses by
ear.)
