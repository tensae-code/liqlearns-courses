# Hands-on — Capital letter and full stop

The lesson done with two pencils on real pages.

## Two-pencil dressing
Copy a bare paragraph from the lesson (or write your own day
in four bare sentences). First pencil: the words. Second
pencil — a different colour: grow every opening letter,
stamp every stop, grow every name. The coloured marks ARE
the lesson; a page dressed in two colours shows its own
rules.

## Capital hunt
Open any book, any page. Hunt capitals for two minutes and
sort them into two piles as you find them: OPENINGS (first
words) and NAMES (persons, places, days, months). Count the
piles. Which rule dresses more of a real page?

## The bare dictation
A partner reads a dressed paragraph aloud, slowly. Write it
exactly as it should stand — capitals and stops placed as
you hear the pauses. Then compare with the book's page,
mark by mark. Every miss names its rule.

## Name signs
Make three small signs for your room: your name, your
town's name, today's day-name — each with its first letter
big and coloured. Signs dress names the way sentences do:
the name stands tall because it is a name.
