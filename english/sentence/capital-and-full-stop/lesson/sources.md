Ghana, Indonesia, Iran, Sierra Leone, Jordan, Kazakhstan, Iraq, Ethiopia, South Africa, Syria, South Sudan, Nepal, Armenia, Cambodia, Pakistan, Sudan).** Ghana states the rules as curriculum strands;

in red, and drills the stop/question-mark pair in its first
punctuation exercises; Indonesia names the marks and
practises them sentence by sentence; Iran corrects
proper-noun capitals and sets an unpunctuated text to
punctuate; Sierra Leone teaches the capital-to-start /
stop-at-the-end pair at Primary 2, names the marks with
the capital rule in JSS 1, and gives the terminal stops
their own lesson pair in SSS 1; Jordan adds the
capital rung only — capital letters named in a Grade 4
writing check; Kazakhstan names capital letters as a
writing point at Grade 5 and capital letters and
punctuation together as explicit writing teaching at
Grades 7 and 9 — the pair is genuinely taught as a
point, but the Kazakhstan filing does not break the
punctuation down mark by mark, so no first-word-capital
or full-stop-at-the-end rule is claimed for it beyond
the naming. Iraq teaches the pair outright from the first year: its Grade 1 Teacher's Book introduces capitals in Unit 6 as beginning names and sentences and explains the sentence as starting with a capital and ending with a full stop, the Grade 1 Activities form the capitals, the Grade 3 Activity Book copies and corrects capitalised sentences, and the Grade 4 Activity Book states the rule — a capital letter starts a sentence, a full stop ends a sentence. Ethiopia lists the capital-letter rules and sets full stop against question mark in Grade 3, gives a whole writing lesson to "Punctuation Marks and Capital Letters" in Grade 4 (printed p.80), and revisits capitalisation on Grade 7's punctuation ladder.
