**Acceptance count: taught in 7 countries (Bolivia, Iran, Indonesia, Mauritania, India, Nicaragua, North Macedonia).** North Macedonia joins with the Oscar ability frame as Teacher's Book evidence from its Grade 2 course — the sweep's placements marked this NEW LESSON NEEDED: can for ability; it stocks here, in the lesson built for exactly that (routing noted 2026-11-11 by the re-verification sweep merge). India joins with can/cannot ability sentences as Class 3 talk time — the sweep placements marked this NEW LESSON NEEDED: ability-with-can; it joins here because this lesson already teaches exactly that (routing noted in the sweep merge, 2026-11-09) (stocked 2026-11-09 by the India re-verification sweep merge).
Mauritania teaches can/can't for ability in Grade 7 and the
full can/could range — ability, probability, suggestion,
surprise — in Grade 12 (stocked 2026-11-09 by the Mauritania
re-verification sweep merge).

This lesson exists because the 2026-11-08 re-verification sweep re-read Bolivia's books page by page and found can/could taught in five places with no lesson of their own in the course — the course's modal lessons began higher up (certainty, perfects), and the base was missing. Under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06) a new lesson was built for it. One honesty note, from the sweep's flag 7: the Grade 8 student book's unit titled “Feelings and emotions” (printed pp. 49–54) teaches NO feelings vocabulary — its body is a Cochabamba reading, connectors and/but, a hobbies list and CAN. The CAN rows above come from that unit's body. (Feelings vocabulary itself lives in the compendium's Year 2 list of 17, stocked in Feelings.) The compendium entries are teacher-compilation evidence, cited as such, never as student's books.

Iran joins (stocked 2026-11-09 by the re-verification sweep merge): Prospect 2 teaches can/can't for ability in Grade 8, used but deliberately unnamed — Iran's own Prospect 2 preface states grammar naming starts in Prospect 3 — and its workbook practises the same set.

Indonesia joins (stocked 2026-11-09 by the re-verification sweep merge): its Grade 4 book teaches can/cannot for ability in primary school — "I can see the trees. I cannot catch the ball." The same book pair's permission face (the Grade 5 library rules) is housed in the sibling lesson Can for Rules.

Searchable tag: the books' own names for these parts are “Modal verb CAN” (Primaria 4), “CAN” (Secundaria 2), “COULD” (Secundaria 3) and “Can” / “Could” (compendium Years 2–3); Iran's part is “My Abilities” (Prospect 2, Lesson 3).
