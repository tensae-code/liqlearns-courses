# Can for Ability

## Do it first

What can you do? Swim? Sing? Ride a bike? Say three things about yourself, starting “I can…”. Go on — try before we start. In this lesson you will learn to say what you can do, ask others what they can do, and talk about what you could do when you were small.

## Sub lesson 1 — Can — I can, you can

**Can** says you are able to do something. And here is the gift: **can never changes**. I can, you can, he can, she can, we can, they can. No -s, ever.

After can, the verb stays plain: “I can swim.” “She can play the piano.” “They can read English.”

Bolivia's Grade 4 book asks it as a game: “Can you drive a car?” “Can you fly?” Some answers are yes, some are funny no's — and every answer teaches the form.

Say three about yourself now: “I can…”

## Sub lesson 2 — Can't and Can you…? questions

Not able? Use **can't**: “I can't fly.” “He can't swim.”

Asking is just as easy — put can first: “**Can you** swim?” Short answers: “Yes, I can.” “No, I can't.”

Notice: no do, no does, no -s anywhere. Can does all the work.

Ask me: “Can you speak English?” Yes, I can. Now ask about a friend: “Can she dance?”

## Sub lesson 3 — Could — ability in the past

**Could** is can in the past: what you were able to do then.

“When I was five, I could ride a bike.” “She could read when she was four.” “We couldn't swim last year.” (couldn't = could not).

Questions work the same way: “Could you walk at one?” “Yes, I could.”

Think of one thing you could do at seven that you can do better now — and one thing you couldn't do then but can do today.

## Sub lesson 4 — Could for possibility, suggestions and requests

Could has three more jobs, all from Bolivia's Grade 9 book.

**Possibility:** “It could rain today.” “He could be at home.” (Maybe — not sure.)

**Suggestions:** “We could go to the park.” “You could try again.”

**Polite requests:** “Could you open the window, please?” “Could you help me?” Could is softer and politer than can in requests.

So could looks backwards (past ability) and forwards (possibility, ideas, polite asks). The sentence around it tells you which.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: two things you can do, one thing you can't, one thing you could do when you were small. Then ask them a polite question with “Could you…?” — and let them answer.

## Do It Yourself

Write six sentences: two “I can…”, one “I can't…”, two “When I was small, I could / couldn't…”, and one polite “Could you…?” to ask someone at home today.
