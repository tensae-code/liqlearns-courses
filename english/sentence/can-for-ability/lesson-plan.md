# Lesson plan — Can for Ability

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** Can for Ability
**Lesson:** can-for-ability — saying what you are able to do: I can swim; Could you help me?
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 26 — How Sure Are You? (the modal family, at its base)
**The one thing:** Can and could for ability — what you can do now, and what you could do in the past — plus could for possibility, suggestions and polite requests.
**This lesson's own level:** Grammar lesson (modal base): can is the same for every person; can't for not able; Can you…? to ask; could for past ability, possibility, suggestions and requests.

## Why this lesson exists

Bolivia teaches can/could in five places and none had a lesson: the Grade 4 book's can/can't chart, the Grade 8 book's full CAN unit (inside the unit its title calls “Feelings and emotions” — see the note in sources), the Grade 9 book's full COULD unit, and the compendium's CAN (Year 2) and COULD (Year 3) spreads. The course's modal lessons start higher up (certainty, perfects); the base was missing. This lesson is built from the placements under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06).

## Sub lessons

1. Can — I can, you can
2. Can't and Can you…? questions
3. Could — ability in the past
4. Could for possibility, suggestions and requests

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is needed before this lesson. It is the base of the modal family: Modal Verbs and How Sure You Are build on it, but each of them also stands alone.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- Say what you can and can't do, with can the same for I, you, he, she, we, they.
- Ask “Can you…?” and answer “Yes, I can / No, I can't.”
- Say what you could do in the past.
- Use could for possibility, suggestions and polite requests.

## What you need
Nothing special — your own abilities are the material.

## The run of the lesson
1. ADD IMAGE: three people doing things — swimming, riding a bike, playing the piano — with CAN over them. The learner says what they can do before any rule appears.
2. Teach sub lesson 1 with the learner's own life: “I can swim. She can sing. We can read.” One form for everybody.
3. ADD GIF (1–2 seconds): a child tries to lift a heavy box and shakes her head — “I can't!” Then a question mark pops: “Can you help me?”
4. Sub lesson 3 moves to the past: “When I was five, I could ride a bike.” The learner tells one thing they could do young and one they couldn't.
5. Sub lesson 4 widens could: possibility (“It could rain”), a suggestion (“We could go to the park”), a polite request (“Could you open the window?”).

## Add-ons
- Can-chain: each learner adds one true “I can…”; the next repeats all before and adds their own.
- Could-interview: ask three people “What could you do when you were seven?” and report back.

## If the learner is stuck
Keep to can/can't in the present until the form is automatic — no -s, no do/does. Could is one step after, and only for the past at first.

## Teacher note
