# Polite Indirect Questions

## Do it first

Imagine you are new in a town. You are standing in the street and
you do not know where the shop is. A kind person walks past. You
can ask directly: "Where is the shop?" But there is a politer
way — a way that puts your question inside another question. Try
to say it before anyone tells you. Trying comes first here. Then
we learn the frame together, and the one change inside it that
you must watch.

## Sub lesson 1 — Can you tell me…? — the polite frame

The polite way starts with a frame:

- **Can you tell me** where the shop is?
- **Could you tell me** where the bank is?
- **Can you tell me** what time it is?

Your real question — where the shop is — rides inside the frame.
The frame does the polite work: you are asking the person to
tell you something. Say the frame out loud until it is smooth:
"Can you tell me…?" "Could you tell me…?" Then hang three
different questions on it: where the market is, where the station
is, what time it is.

*Difficulty: first steps — one frame, Can/Could you tell me…?, with your question riding inside it.*

## Sub lesson 2 — The statement order inside

Now the one change you must watch. In a direct question, the word
order flips: "Where **is** the shop?" But inside the polite
frame, the inside part keeps **statement order** — the same order
as a plain sentence:

- Direct: Where **is the shop**?
- Indirect: Can you tell me where **the shop is**?

- Direct: What time **is it**?
- Indirect: Do you know what time **it is**?

Never flip the inside: "Can you tell me where is the shop?" is
wrong. The shop stays in front of is: "where the shop is."

Practise the flip-and-unflip: say the direct question, then wrap
it: "Where is the bank?" → "Can you tell me where the bank is?"
"When does the bus leave?" → "Do you know when the bus leaves?"

*Difficulty: building — the frame is easy; the work is keeping the inside in statement order: where the shop IS.*

## Sub lesson 3 — Do you know…? — more frames

Can you tell me…? is not the only frame. The other everyday one
asks what the person knows:

- **Do you know** where the station is?
- **Do you know** when the shop opens?
- **Do you know** how much the ticket costs?

The rule inside is the same: statement order, no flip. "Do you
know where the station is?" — never "where is the station" inside
it.

Now use both frames about your own town. Ask a partner five
polite questions — where the market is, when the bus leaves, how
much a ticket costs, where the clinic is, what time the library
opens. Your partner answers each one in a full sentence: "The
market is next to the church."

*Difficulty: full use — both frames, Do you know…? and Can you tell me…?, with the inside always in statement order.*

## Say it to your companion

Explain to your companion, in your own words, what changes when a
question goes inside the polite frame — and what must never
change. Then test each other: your companion says a direct
question — "Where is the post office?" — and you must wrap it in
a polite frame, with the order right, before they count to
three.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write three polite questions you could ask in a new town, with
   Can you tell me…? — about a shop, a bank and a bus stop.
2. Write two with Do you know…? — about a time and a price.
3. Say all five out loud to someone at home, and write their
   answers in full sentences.
