# Lesson plan — Cleft sentences

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Cleft sentences
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 31 — "Linking and
Fixing Sentences" — the fourth lesson of the level
(CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the cleft
sentence — It is / It was + the focused part + that /
who… — the shape that picks one part of a sentence up
and puts a light on it, for emphasis.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Building a
sentence (the plain sentence it re-lights) and on
relative clauses (the that/who tail it borrows). Its
unit-mate in Jordan's book, negative inversion, is
taught beside it as a neighbour, not as a second thing.
**What the learner can do at the end:** turn a plain
sentence into a cleft that focuses the person, the
thing, the time or the place; choose that or who; keep
is for now-focus and was for past-focus; and recognise
negative inversion (Never before had I…) as the same
emphasis family in its flipped dress.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Cleft sentences — the shape** — It is / It was + the focused
   part + that/who, built from a plain sentence without breaking the
   rest of it. (Was depth Level 1.)

2. **Cleft sentences — moving the light** — the same event re-lit on
   its time, place or reason; the who-or-that paragraph rides in
   this sub lesson, though the rule is first stated in Sub lesson 1.
   (Was depth Level 2.)

3. **Cleft sentences — the emphasis family** — negative inversion
   read as emphasis and kept apart from the cleft, and the cleft
   used to correct a wrong claim politely. (Was depth Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Building a sentence and Relative
  clauses. Recommended, never forced.
- The evidence base is narrow and is stated plainly in
  the lesson and the sources: only two books in the
  whole read set teach cleft sentences — Jordan's Grade
  12 and Vietnam's Grade 11. Nothing is padded.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a stage with one actor under
   a spotlight and the rest in shadow.** Written slot:
   "a picture of one actor in a spotlight." What the
   student does: the student points at the lit actor and
   says the cleft: "It was Sara who scored."
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of a plain sentence as a row of
   blocks, one block being lifted out and placed after
   IT WAS on a plinth.** Written slot: "a picture of one
   block lifted onto a plinth." What the student does:
   the student lifts a different block — the time, the
   place — and hears the focus move.
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of a sentence flipped upside
   down, its first word NEVER wearing a crown: negative
   inversion.** Written slot: "a picture of a flipped
   sentence." What the student does: the student reads
   "Never before had I…" aloud in the flipped order and
   feels the stress land.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first** (lesson/lesson.md): one plain
   sentence about a real event, then three questions —
   WHO did it? WHEN? WHERE? — each answer wanting the
   light in a different place.
2. **The shape**: It is/was + focus + that/who — Vietnam
   states the grammar heading in those words (Grade 11,
   Unit 8, grammar box, printed p.88).
3. **Choosing the tail**: who for people, that for
   things, times and places; the rest of the sentence
   follows unchanged.
4. **Is against was**: the focus of a present truth
   takes is; a past event takes was.
5. **The Jordan unit**: inversion and cleft sentences
   taught together for emphasis (High Note Grade 12,
   Semester 1, Unit 2, 3A, pp.18–19), built on the
   "Speeding in the sand" text — "Never before had I
   experienced such intense heat."
6. **Negative inversion as neighbour**: Never before /
   Not only / Rarely at the front flip the helper in
   front of the subject — Jordan's Workbook drills
   intonation in cleft sentences in the same unit
   (Unit 2, 4B).
7. **Do it yourself** (the three closing tasks in the
   lesson): one event, three lights; the correction
   cleft; the never-before sentence.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck building: start from the plain sentence, circle
  the part to light, lift it behind It was, and let the
  rest follow after that/who.
- Stuck on who or that: people take who; everything
  else in this lesson takes that.
- Stuck on inversion: it is a neighbour, not the
  lesson's test — read it, feel the stress, and return
  to the cleft.
- This level's next door: Object clauses, the level's
  fifth lesson — another that-clause, doing a different
  job: carrying what somebody thinks or says.
