# Hands-on — Fixing sentence errors

You do it, we guide you. Everything here is done with a
companion or the Explain Companion.

1. **The five-name round.** The companion reads broken
   sentences from any book, worksheet or the learner's
   own old writing. The learner must name the error
   before fixing it — fragment, run-on, splice, dangling,
   parallelism. A fix without the name scores half.
2. **Splice surgery.** Five comma splices on cards. The
   learner repairs each one a different way in turn:
   full stop, semicolon, and/but/so, a fronted clause —
   and reads each repair aloud to hear the joint hold.
3. **Whose hat is it?** The companion invents dangling
   openers ("Driving home, the lights…", "Full of water,
   the boy carried…"). The learner must ask "who?" and
   rebuild the sentence with the true doer in place.
4. **March the list.** The learner writes a real list
   from their life — three things they did, want or like
   — deliberately out of step, then marches it into one
   shape and reads both versions aloud.
