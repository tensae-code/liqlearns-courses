# Lesson plan — Fixing sentence errors

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Fixing sentence errors
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 31, "Linking and
Fixing Sentences" — the third lesson of the level
(CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** spotting and
fixing the five classic sentence errors — fragments,
run-ons, comma splices, dangling modifiers and faulty
parallelism — each with its own repair.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Building a
sentence (what a complete sentence is), Connectors and
clauses (the joins a splice misuses) and Subject-verb
agreement (one of the clinic's regular patients).
Cleft sentences close the level after it.
**What the learner can do at the end:** name the error
in a broken sentence; complete a fragment; split or
properly join a run-on; repair a comma splice four ways;
re-seat a dangling modifier next to its true doer; and
straighten a list into parallel shape.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Building a sentence and Connectors
  and clauses. Recommended, never forced.
- A wrong verb (agreement) is repaired in its own
  lesson; wordiness is named in Ethiopia's strand and
  pointed at here, but it is not one of this lesson's
  five.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a train with one carriage
   missing its wheels: the fragment.** Written slot: "a
   picture of a carriage that cannot run." What the
   student does: the student says what the carriage
   needs before it can move — a subject or a verb.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of two trains crashing through
   each other on one track with no signal: the run-on.**
   Written slot: "a picture of two sentences with no
   signal between them." What the student does: the
   student puts a signal in — a full stop, a semicolon,
   or a joining word.
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of a hat floating in the air
   with nobody under it, a man walking in beside it: the
   dangling modifier.** Written slot: "a picture of a
   description with nobody wearing it." What the student
   does: the student moves the man under the hat — the
   doer next to the description.
4. **Slot 4 — a picture of three soldiers marching in
   step and a fourth dancing: the broken list.** Written
   slot: "a picture of a list out of step." What the
   student does: the student calls the fourth item back
   into step — same shape as the rest.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first** (lesson/lesson.md): five broken
   sentences, one of each kind, to judge before any
   names are given.
2. **The fragment**: no subject, no verb, or a dependent
   clause standing alone — Ethiopia Grade 11 names
   fragments, run-ons and comma splices together in its
   first writing section; Grade 12 opens with fragments
   again.
3. **The run-on and the comma splice**: two sentences
   crashed with nothing, or only a comma, between them —
   and the four repairs (full stop, semicolon,
   coordinator, subordinator). Ethiopia Grade 12's Unit
   2 writing is run-on sentences; its anchor "correct
   run-on sentences" is filed at printed p.24.
4. **The dangling modifier**: the opening description
   whose doer is missing or wrong — Ethiopia Grade 12,
   Unit 4 writing (objectives, printed p.74); misplaced
   modifiers ride in Unit 3.
5. **Faulty parallelism**: list items in mixed shapes —
   Ethiopia Grade 12, Unit 6 writing, "Faulty
   Parallelism", printed p.151.
6. **The clinic**: Ethiopia Grade 12, Unit 10 —
   "Common Mistakes in Sentence Writing", fragments,
   run-ons and parallelism revised, closing with
   error-identification items (printed p.257).
7. **The witnesses**: Sierra Leone's "Writing Complete
   Sentences" — what makes a sentence complete,
   correcting fragments and run-on sentences (SSS2 Term
   1, Lesson 40, p.100); Ghana's NaCCA curriculum —
   identify, use, and correct errors in paragraphs
   (curriculum evidence: Ghana holds no pupil textbook).
8. **Do it yourself** (the three closing tasks in the
   lesson): the five-name round, the splice surgery, the
   list straightened.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck naming an error: ask the clinic's three
  questions in order — Is anything missing? Is too much
  crashed together? Is anything hanging or out of step?
- Stuck on a fragment: read it aloud and ask "who? did
  what?" — the missing answer is the repair.
- Stuck on a splice: remember a comma is a pause, not a
  joint. Joints are full stops, semicolons and joining
  words.
- This level's next door: Cleft sentences — sentences
  rebuilt on purpose, for emphasis, instead of by
  accident.
