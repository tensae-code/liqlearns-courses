# Video script — Fixing sentence errors

Super-simple voice. Read slow. The learner hears this
before or beside the written lesson.

---

A sentence can break in five ways.
Learn their faces.

One. The fragment.
"Running down the road."
Who is running? No subject. No full verb.
It cannot stand. It is a piece, not a sentence.
Fix it: give it a doer. "The boy was running down the
road."

Two. The run-on.
"The bell rang the pupils ran out the rain started."
Three sentences. No signals. A crash.
Fix it: stop them apart. Or join them properly.

Three. The comma splice.
"The bell rang, the pupils ran out."
A comma is only a pause. It is not a joint.
Fix it four ways.
A full stop. A semicolon.
A joining word: and, but, so.
Or make one half a clause: "When the bell rang, the
pupils ran out."

Four. The dangling modifier.
"Walking to school, the rain soaked my book."
Who was walking? The rain? No.
The description hangs in the air.
Fix it: put the doer next to it.
"Walking to school, I felt the rain soak my book."

Five. Faulty parallelism.
"She likes reading, to swim, and dance."
Three items, three shapes. Out of step.
Fix it: one shape for all.
"She likes reading, swimming, and dancing."

Now you try.
I will say a broken sentence.
You name the break. Then you fix it.
