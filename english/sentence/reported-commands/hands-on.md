# Hands-on — Reported commands

You do it, we guide you. Everything here is done with a
companion or the Explain Companion.

1. **The home orders.** The companion plays a parent,
   teacher or coach and fires six real-shaped orders and
   requests (Close the window. Don't run in the corridor.
   Please bring me the book.). The learner reports each
   one at once to an imaginary sibling: "She told me to
   close the window." A misplaced not costs a point.
2. **The force ladder.** One command — "Wait here" — is
   said four ways: a friend hoping, a teacher speaking
   plainly, an official ordering, a doctor advising rest
   instead. The learner reports each with its verb:
   asked, told, ordered, advised. Then the companion
   shuffles the reports and the learner must say which
   scene each came from, by the verb alone.
3. **Warning signs.** Walk the home or school (or a
   picture page of signs): Wet floor. No swimming. Don't
   touch. The learner turns each sign into a report —
   "The sign warned us not to swim" — negatives always
   with not before to.
4. **Rwanda's scene.** Act the Mother–Gatete dialogue
   from the filing's summary: a mother gives chore
   commands (sweep, prepare tea, take the note and buy
   bread). The learner, as a neighbour child, reports
   all of them in one breath: "Mother told Gatete to
   sweep the house, to prepare tea…" — then adds one
   Don't of their own, reported with not to.
