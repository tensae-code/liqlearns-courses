# Reported commands — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Sentence · Lesson: Reported commands
Built from: Mauritania, Study English, Grade 12 (Lesson
13, pp.40–41) — Nepal, English Grade 8 (Unit 5, and Unit
19) and Grade 10 (Unit 4) — Rwanda, English for Rwandan
Primary Schools, Pupil's Book 6 (Unit 9) — Vietnam,
Tiếng Anh 12 (Unit 10).

## Do it first
Think of home this morning. Somebody said: "Close the
window." Somebody said: "Don't leave your bag there."
Somebody said, more softly: "Please bring me the salt."
Later, your brother asks: what did they tell you to do?
You carry the orders to him in new sentences — and your
own language changes the bare command into something
carried. English's carried shape is this lesson. It
stands near Reported statements — that lesson helps
here — but start here if you like: a command has no
tense to step back, so the whole move is explained
below, and it is a small one.

## Sub lesson 1 — Reported Commands Shape
**The shape.** A command, reported, freezes into an
infinitive and rides behind a reporting verb with its
person in front of it: **verb + person + to + verb**.
Rwanda's Pupil's Book 6 teaches it with one model
sentence, filed exactly: "**Mother told Gatete to sweep
the house**" (Unit 9, p.115) — told (the reporting
verb, in its past), Gatete (the person the command
hit), to sweep (the frozen command). Mauritania's Grade
12 book states the same conversion in its restaurant
lesson (Lesson 13): imperative → to-infinitive (p.41).
Nothing steps back, because a command carries no tense:
"Sit down" has no past to retreat into — it simply
freezes behind **to**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Reported Commands Negative
**The negative: not stands before to.** A Don't-command
reports with **not + to-infinitive**: "Don't touch" →
He told me **not to touch** it. Mauritania's own filed
practice item is a table manners one: "not to eat with
fingers" (p.41). The not always stands guard in front of
the to — in every book in this lesson, that is its seat.

## Sub lesson 3 — Reported Commands Verb Force
**The verb carries the force.** The plain carrier is
**told**, and a polite request rides on **asked**:
"Please help me" → She **asked** me to help her. Nepal's
Grade 8 book (Unit 5) sets the four-verb family for this
whole territory — reported speech for imperatives,
requests, advice and warnings: **told / asked / advised /
warned + to-infinitive**. So counsel takes advise ("Rest
today" → The doctor **advised** me to rest), and danger
takes warn, usually with the negative ("Don't swim here"
→ He **warned** us **not to** swim there). Nepal returns
to the pattern as a named unit at Grade 10 — Unit 4,
"Reporting Commands", commands and requests — and
assembles it again inside Grade 8's full reporting unit
(Unit 19).

**The eleven-verb bank.** Vietnam's Grade 12 book narrows
its whole reported-speech year to this lesson's territory
— orders, requests, offers and advice (Unit 10) — and
stocks the widest verb bank in the set: **ask, tell,
advise, command, order, instruct, invite, encourage,
persuade, remind, warn**, all with object +
to-infinitive. The ladder of force runs through it:
invite and ask at the gentle end, tell plain in the
middle, order / command / instruct where power speaks,
encourage / persuade where the will must be moved,
remind where memory is the point, warn where danger is.
The same unit marks the bank's border, as filed: **offer,
propose, agree, demand** ride in that book without an
object in front of the infinitive. [a picture of eleven
messengers in different uniforms, each carrying the same
parcel — the words "WAIT HERE" — from a whispering
friend to a guard] Hand one command to three messengers
now and hear the force move while the shape stands still.

**The Rwanda limit, worded plainly.** In Rwanda's
Pupil's Book 6, reported commands are the **only**
reporting the book teaches: its filing's honest negatives
state that reporting in that book is limited to reported
commands with told + infinitive (Unit 9) and the
direct-speech punctuation of Unit 6 — there are no
reported statements and no reported questions in it.
(Filing check, recorded here because the catch-up plan
named the wrong book: this evidence stands in Pupil's
Book **6**, Unit 9, pp.110–123, grammar at p.115.
Rwanda's P.5 book teaches no reported speech at all —
its Unit 9 is agriculture.) The limit does not shrink
the pattern: Rwanda's single model sentence carries the
whole shape of this lesson.

## Do it yourself
1. **The home orders.** Report three real-shaped
   instructions from your home or school — one plain
   order, one polite request, one Don't — with told,
   asked, and a not-to report, each in its right shape.
2. **The force ladder.** Take "Wait here" and report it
   four ways — a friend (asked), a teacher (told), an
   official (ordered), a guard at a gate (commanded) —
   then add a doctor's counsel (advised) and a warning
   about a wet floor (warned … not to).
3. **The warning signs.** Turn three signs or safety
   rules — No running. Don't touch the wires. Keep off
   the grass. — into reports a teacher could say: The
   sign warned us not to… The rule told us to…
