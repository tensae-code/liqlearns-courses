# Lesson plan — Reported commands

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Reported commands
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 22 — Telling What People Said — the third lesson of the unit
(CURRICULUM.md), after Reported statements and Reported
questions.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** reporting an
order, a request, advice or a warning — the command
becomes an infinitive carried by a reporting verb with
its person in front of it: told me to go, asked her to
wait, advised us to rest, warned them not to touch —
negatives carried by not + to-infinitive.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Reported
statements (the telling frame) and on the imperative
lesson (the bare command being reported). Speech marks
close the level after it.
**What the learner can do at the end:** report any
command or request with verb + person + to-infinitive;
choose the verb by force — order/command/instruct for
orders, tell plain, ask for requests, advise and warn
for counsel and danger, with Vietnam's eleven-verb bank;
and place the not before the to in every negative.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Reported Commands Shape** — report any plain command with told/asked + the person it hit + to-infinitive, the command's own verb frozen unchanged behind the to. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Reported Commands Negative** — report any Don't… command with not + to-infinitive, the not always standing guard before the to — and keep positive and negative reports apart live, in pairs. (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **Reported Commands Verb Force** — choose the reporting verb by how the words were said — order, command, instruct for power; ask for a request; advise for counsel; warn for danger; encourage, remind, invite, persuade from the wider bank — without breaking the shape. (Was depth Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the imperative (orders in their
  bare shape) and Reported statements (tell + person
  comes straight back). Recommended, never forced.
- No backshift lives here: a command has no tense to
  step back. Its verb freezes into the infinitive — that
  is the whole move, and the lesson keeps to it.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of an order turning into a
   parcel: a captain's shouted "Sit down!" on the left,
   the same words packed into a box labelled TOLD HIM TO
   SIT DOWN on the right, carried by a messenger.**
   Written slot: "a picture of an order packed for
   carrying." What the student does: the student packs
   one shouted command into its reported parcel aloud.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of a row of eleven messengers in
   different uniforms, from a whispering friend (ask) to
   a guard (command), each carrying the same parcel.**
   Written slot: "a picture of eleven messengers of
   different strengths." What the student does: the
   student hands one command — "Wait here" — to three
   different messengers and hears the force change:
   asked me to wait, told me to wait, ordered me to wait.
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of a NOT standing guard in front
   of a TO sign on a road: the traveller meets NOT first,
   then TO.** Written slot: "a picture of not standing
   before to." What the student does: the student reports
   "Don't touch!" and places the not where the picture
   puts it — warned me NOT TO touch.
4. **Slot 4 — a picture of a mother telling a boy to
   sweep a house, a broom in his hand (Rwanda's own
   scene).** Written slot: "a picture of a mother, a boy
   and a broom." What the student does: the student says
   Rwanda's filed sentence — "Mother told Gatete to
   sweep the house" — then swaps the verb: asked, ordered.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first** (lesson/lesson.md): the carried order
   — a parent's three instructions at home, retold to a
   sibling, first in the learner's own language.
2. **The shape**: reporting verb + person + to-infinitive
   ("Mother told Gatete to sweep the house" — Rwanda's
   model, p.115; Mauritania: imperative → to-infinitive,
   p.41).
3. **The negative**: not + to-infinitive — "not to eat
   with fingers" is Mauritania's own filed practice
   (p.41); Vietnam states the same placement.
4. **Choosing the verb by force**: Nepal's Grade 8 set —
   told / asked / advised / warned + to-infinitive for
   imperatives, requests, advice and warnings (Unit 5);
   Nepal Grade 10's named unit, Reporting Commands
   (Unit 4).
5. **The eleven-verb bank**: Vietnam Grade 12's list —
   ask, tell, advise, command, order, instruct, invite,
   encourage, persuade, remind, warn — with object +
   to-infinitive; and its border: offer/propose/agree/
   demand ride without an object in that book's
   teaching.
6. **The Rwanda limit, worded plainly**: in Rwanda's
   Pupil's Book 6, reported commands are the ONLY
   reporting taught — no reported statements or
   questions stand in that book. (Plan anchor check: the
   filing places this in P.6, Unit 9 — not P.5; P.5
   teaches no reported speech at all.)
7. **Do it yourself** (the three closing tasks in the
   lesson): the home orders, the force ladder, the
   warning signs.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck on the shape: a command has no tense, so there
  is nothing to step back — freeze the verb behind to,
  and put the person it hit in front of the to.
- Stuck on the not: the not guards the to — it always
  stands BEFORE it: not to go, never "to not go" in this
  lesson's books.
- Stuck choosing the verb: ask how the words were said.
  A shout from power → order/command. A hope from a
  friend → ask. Counsel → advise. Danger → warn.
- This level's next door: Speech marks — the exact
  words, quoted, where reporting lets go and quoting
  takes over.
