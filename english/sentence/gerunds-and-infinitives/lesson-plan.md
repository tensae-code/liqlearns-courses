# Lesson plan — Gerunds and infinitives

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Gerunds and infinitives
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 24, "Two Shapes After
a Verb" — the first lesson of the level
(CATCH-UP-PLAN.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the two shapes a
verb takes after another verb — the -ing shape (gerund)
and the to shape (infinitive) — and how the governing
verb chooses: -ing only, to only, both with no change,
both with a meaning change (remember/forget/stop) —
plus the bare infinitive after make/let, the perfect
gerund (having done), and the to-infinitive that hangs
on a noun (the first person to…).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Present
actions (the -ing shape) and on The present perfect
(having done is built from its machinery). Participle
clauses are the lesson before; there having done works
as a clause in front of a sentence — here it works as a
verb's own object, a different seat, and the lesson
says so.
**What the learner can do at the end:** follow the four
verb classes without a list in hand for the common
verbs; say what remember/stop/forget mean in both
shapes and never swap them by accident; use the bare
infinitive after make/let; and put an earlier action
into having done.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Present actions and The present
  perfect. Recommended, never forced — every shape is
  built from its parts inside the explanation.
- The speaker button reads the explanations in the
  learner's own language; the target forms (the verb
  patterns themselves) stay in English, never
  translated.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a railway junction: one verb
   at the points, two tracks leaving it — one labelled
   -ING, one labelled TO.** Written slot: "a picture of
   a verb choosing between two tracks." What the
   student does: the student sends three verbs down
   their tracks aloud — enjoy → -ing, want → to, like →
   either — before reading the classes.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of two doors: one marked
   REMEMBER TO LOCK (a hand still holding the key,
   before) and one marked REMEMBER LOCKING (the same
   hand thinking back, after).** Written slot: "a
   picture of remember looking forward and backward."
   What the student does: the student points at the
   door that means "do not forget to do it" and the
   door that means "I still see myself doing it."
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of a gatekeeper (MAKE) pushing
   a cart with no TO sign on it, and a second keeper
   (LET) holding the gate open.** Written slot: "a
   picture of make and let taking the bare verb."
   What the student does: the student says one make
   sentence and one let sentence with NO to — and
   hears the gap where to would break them.
4. **Slot 4 — a picture of a queue: the first person in
   it wearing a sash "the first person TO arrive."**
   Written slot: "a picture of the first person to
   arrive." What the student does: the student names
   two more firsts of their own — the last to leave,
   the next to speak — with the to-shape on the noun.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first** (lesson/lesson.md): three things you
   like doing, want to do, and finished doing — said
   first in the learner's own language, then watched as
   English sorts them onto two tracks.
2. **The two lists**: to only (want, promise, need,
   refuse, decide) and -ing only (enjoy, finish, keep,
   avoid) — Egypt, Preparatory 9 Term 2, Unit 9,
   Tip p.42; Sudan's SPINE 5 Chapter 9 and SPINE 6
   Chapter 8 Grammar Corners work the same two lists.
3. **Both, no change**: love, like, hate, prefer, start,
   begin, continue (Egypt, Secondary 10 Term 1, Unit 4,
   p.63, the four-class system).
4. **Both, meaning changes**: remember, forget, regret,
   stop, try (Egypt's fourth class; Cambodia Grade 10
   Unit 25 teaches to vs -ing with remember/forget;
   Cambodia Grade 9 Unit 29 contrasts forget to call
   with forget calling).
5. **The bare infinitive**: after make and let
   (Malaysia Form 5, Unit 9, Grammar 1, p.113, which
   teaches gerunds/infinitives and make & let in one
   spread), and after modals, would rather, had better
   (Malaysia Form 3, Unit 11).
6. **The gerund at work**: as a sentence's subject
   (Mongolia English X, Unit 5: "Wearing clothes serves
   many purposes") and object (Vietnam Grade 11,
   Unit 4: gerunds as subjects and objects).
7. **The perfect gerund**: having done for the action
   that came first (Vietnam Grade 11, Unit 7 — the
   grammar box heading itself, printed p.78).
8. **The to-infinitive on a noun**: the first / last /
   next + noun + to (Vietnam Grade 11, Unit 6:
   to-infinitive clauses modifying nouns with ordinals
   and superlatives).
9. **Do it yourself** (the three closing tasks in the
   lesson): the two-track sort, the remember drill, the
   first-and-last round.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck on a verb's track: the lists are habits, not
  logic — drill the Egypt four classes as two short
  chants (to-list, -ing-list) and let the both-classes
  announce themselves by the meaning test below.
- Stuck on remember/stop: ask "before or after?" To
  looks forward to an action not yet done (remember TO
  lock = do not forget); -ing looks back at one already
  done (remember locking = I can still see it).
- This lesson's next door: The causative — where
  make/let return with people between them and the
  verb (make someone do), and have/get take the past
  participle.
