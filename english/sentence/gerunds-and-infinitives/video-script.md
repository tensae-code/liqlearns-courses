# Video script — Gerunds and infinitives

Super-simple voice. Read slow. The learner hears this
before or beside the written lesson.

---

After a verb, another verb can come.
But it must change its coat.

Two coats. Listen.
I enjoy swimming. The -ing coat.
I want to swim. The to coat.

Every verb chooses its coat.
Enjoy takes -ing. Enjoy swimming. Never: enjoy to swim.
Want takes to. Want to swim. Never: want swimming.

Some verbs take -ing only.
Enjoy. Finish. Keep. Avoid.
I finished reading. Keep going. Avoid being late.

Some verbs take to only.
Want. Promise. Need. Refuse. Decide.
She promised to come. He refused to pay.

Some take both. No change.
I like swimming. I like to swim.
Start, begin, love, hate. Both are fine.

But be careful. Some take both — and the meaning moves.

Remember to lock the door.
That is BEFORE. Do not forget. Lock it later.
Remember locking the door.
That is AFTER. You see it in your mind. You did it.

Stop to rest. You stop walking — to rest.
Stop resting. You finish resting. Stop for good.

Forget to call. You did not do it.
Forget calling. You did it — and lost the memory.

After make and let, no coat at all. The bare verb.
They made me wait. She let me go.
No to. Never to.

One more shape. The action finished first.
He admitted having taken the money.
Having taken. Done before the admitting.
That is the perfect gerund.

And on a noun: the first person to arrive.
The last student to leave.
First, last, next — then to.

Now you try.
Three verbs. Three coats.
Enjoy. Want. Remember.
Say one true sentence with each.
