# Lesson plan — Question tags

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Question tags
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 21 — Questions That Check — the first lesson of the unit (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the question tag —
the tiny question hung on the end of a statement ("You
are coming, aren't you?") — built by the opposite-polarity
rule, with its special cases, and read by its voice:
rising for a real question, falling for a check.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Asking yes/no
questions and Asking with question words (the auxiliary
machinery comes straight back), and on the tense lessons
whose auxiliaries the tag copies. Subject and object
questions follow it inside this level.
**What the learner can do at the end:** tag any statement
(positive or negative, any auxiliary, do-support when
there is none); handle aren't I, Let's → shall we,
commands → will/won't you, indefinite subjects → they,
and negative words taking a positive tag; and say the
tag rising or falling on purpose.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Question Tags Mirror Rule** — tag any plain statement — positive takes a negative tag, negative takes a positive tag — copying the statement's own auxiliary, and bring in do/does/did when the statement has no auxiliary to copy. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Question Tags Special Cases** — run the exceptions without a miss — aren't I for I am, shall we for Let's, will/won't you for commands, and the indefinite crowd (somebody, everybody, nobody, everyone) answered by they in the tag. (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **Question Tags Negative Words and Two Voices** — spot the hidden negative (never, seldom, rarely, few, little) and give it a positive tag, and choose the voice on purpose: rising when you truly do not know, falling when you are checking; the tags-on-the-perfect block sits in this sub lesson's text by its position, though its have/had copying belongs to Sub lesson 1. (Was depth Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Asking yes/no questions (the tag IS
  a yes/no question, shrunk) and Present actions / Past
  actions (the auxiliaries the tag must copy).
  Recommended, never forced.
- Reported questions are a different thing — a later
  level. Here the tag checks the speaker's own statement.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of two friends at a bus stop, one
   pointing at the timetable and looking at the other with
   eyebrows DOWN, sure of himself.** Written slot: "a
   picture of a speaker checking something he is almost
   sure of." What the student does: the student says the
   sentence with a FALLING voice — "The bus comes at six,
   doesn't it?" — the check, not a real question.
2. **Slot 2 — the same picture redrawn with eyebrows UP
   and shoulders shrugged.** Written slot: "a picture of
   a speaker who really does not know." What the student
   does: the student says the same sentence with a RISING
   voice and hears how the meaning changes.
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of a mirror held up to a
   sentence: the statement on the left reads CAN, the
   reflection on the right reads CAN'T.** Written slot:
   "a picture of a tag as a mirror image of its statement
   — same auxiliary, opposite sign." What the student
   does: the student names the auxiliary in three
   statements and flips it in the mirror each time.
4. **Slot 4 — a picture of a crowd of small figures
   (somebody, everybody, nobody, few people) all pointing
   to one big word: THEY.** Written slot: "a picture of
   the indefinite crowd turning into they." What the
   student does: the student gives each crowd-word its
   tag out loud before reading the rule.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first** (lesson/lesson.md): checking a friend —
   three things the learner is almost sure of, said first
   in the learner's own language, then tagged in English.
2. **The mirror rule**: positive statement → negative
   tag; negative statement → positive tag (Mauritania's
   polarity rule; Mongolia's discovery in the same words).
3. **Copy the auxiliary**: is/can/will/have step into the
   tag; no auxiliary → do/does/did (Egypt's eight-rule
   box; Mauritania's do-support).
4. **The pronoun seat**: the subject shrinks to its
   pronoun in the tag; the indefinite crowd — somebody,
   everybody, nobody — takes **they** (Mauritania, p.50;
   Egypt's everyone example).
5. **The special cases**: I am → aren't I; Let's → shall
   we; commands → will you? / won't you? (Egypt's box;
   Mauritania, pp.46–47).
6. **Negative words**: never, seldom, rarely, few,
   little, nobody make the statement negative already —
   the tag goes positive: "She seldom comes late, does
   she?" (Mauritania, p.50).
7. **The two voices**: rising = a real question, the
   speaker is not sure; falling = a check, the speaker
   expects agreement (Kuwait's intonation guidance,
   p.57; Mongolia: tags "ask for agreement" / check
   whether something is true).
8. **Tags on the perfect**: Venezuela's teacher's guide
   works tag questions in the present and past perfect
   with its auxiliary HAVE chart — teacher's-guide
   evidence, stated as such.
9. **Do it yourself** (the three closing tasks in the
   lesson): the check-list, the crowd round, the two
   voices.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck on the auxiliary: find the statement's helper
  verb first and copy it — the tag never invents its own.
  No helper? do/does/did steps in, tense and person
  riding on it.
- Stuck on polarity: ask "is there a not, or a
  never-word, hiding in my statement?" One negative
  anywhere flips the tag positive.
- Stuck on the crowd words: somebody, everybody, nobody
  all answer to they — Egypt's own example is "Everyone
  knows the answer, don't they?"
- This lesson's next door: Subject and object questions
  (same level) — the other way a question checks who did
  what.
