# Hands-on — Question tags

You do it, we guide you. Everything here is done with a
companion or the Explain Companion, out loud.

1. **The check-list.** Write five things you are almost
   sure about your home or school (the door is locked,
   lunch is at one, your friend has a bicycle…). Tag each
   one and say it with the FALLING voice. Your companion
   answers each check: Yes, it is. / No, it isn't. Score:
   one point per tag with the right auxiliary and the
   right flip.
2. **The crowd round.** One person says a crowd sentence
   — Everybody likes tea. Somebody took my pen. Nobody
   was late. Few people know this road. — and the other
   must answer with the tag only (…don't they? …did
   they?). The crowd always answers to they; a sentence
   with never, seldom or few takes a positive tag.
3. **The two voices.** Take one sentence — The shop is
   open, isn't it? — and say it twice: falling (you are
   checking the times you remember) and rising (you truly
   do not know). Your companion must say which voice they
   heard and what the speaker wanted: a yes, or the truth.
4. **Command tags.** Give five friendly orders with tags
   — Pass the salt, will you? Sit down, won't you? — the
   way Mauritania's and Egypt's books set commands. Then
   one Let's sentence with shall we, and one I am
   sentence with aren't I. The special cases, said out
   loud until they are boring.
