# How sure you are — short answer

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — How Sure You Are — the Sure Ends · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — How Sure You Are — the Possible Middle · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — How Sure You Are — the Whole Ladder Live, except items that ask only about can't be or about repairing mustn't, which are Sub lesson 1 by what they ask.

Answer in whole sentences.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Your friend's shoes are soaking wet and his hair is wet. What do you deduce?
**Answer: It must be raining outside. / He must be wet from the rain.** — must be, on a proving sign (95–100%).

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** You hear a small noise in the garden at night. You are not very sure. Say the deduction.
**Answer: It might be a cat. (It may/could be… earns it if you price it at 50%.)** — might prices the guess at 30% (Egypt).

**3. (Sub lesson 1)** A classmate says: "The teacher mustn't be in school today — her room is locked." Repair the sentence.
**Answer: The teacher can't be in school today — her room is locked.** — deductions of NOT-true take can't be; mustn't only forbids.

**4. (Sub lesson 3)** Look at a closed door with light under it and music behind it. Give two deductions on different rungs, with their signs.
**Answer: Someone must be in the room — there is light and music. It might be my brother — he loves that song.** (Any two rungs, each with its sign named.)
