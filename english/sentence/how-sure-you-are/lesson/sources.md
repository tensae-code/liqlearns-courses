**Acceptance count: taught in 10 countries (Cambodia,
Egypt, Malaysia, Nepal, Yemen, Bolivia, China, Iran, Mauritania, Mexico).** Egypt prices the
whole ladder in print — must 95–100% true, may/could
50%, might 30%, can't 95–100% NOT true — and no other
book in the set prints numbers. Yemen plays the ladder
as a guessing game from photo fragments; Cambodia
gives it a mysteries unit, on teacher's-book evidence;
Malaysia groups certainty/deduction inside its modal
units at Forms 3 and 5; Nepal works deduction inside
its Grade 7 rules unit. The build plan also named a
Nepal Grade 7 Unit 15 anchor for this lesson; the
filing's Unit 15 deduction evidence was not confirmed
(the held Grade 7 deduction unit is Unit 10), so only
Unit 10 is cited. China carries the deduction set twice
up: guessing feelings from a story in Grade 6, and at
Grade 9 a whole mystery unit — modal inference over
clues and the "It must belong to Carla" story (stocked
2026-11-08 by the re-verification sweep). Iran teaches
modals as its Grade 10 fourth lesson's grammar, with
workbook practice (stocked 2026-11-09 by the
re-verification sweep merge).
Mauritania grades the modals in Grade 12 (may/might; the
prediction shades) and charts the whole system — ability,
deduction, requests, certainty — in Grade 13 (stocked
2026-11-09 by the Mauritania re-verification sweep merge). Mexico teaches the modal family across two grades: must / have to / should and the prohibition-and-permission pair in Grade 7 with a full modal review, and have to plus a closing modal review in Grade 8 (stocked 2026-11-09 by the Mexico re-verification sweep merge).
