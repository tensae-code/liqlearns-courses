# Lesson plan — How sure you are

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** How sure you are
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 26, "How Sure Are You?" —
the first lesson of the level (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** deduction about
NOW — reading the signs in front of you and saying how
sure you are: **must be** (sure it is true), **can't be**
(sure it is NOT true), and **may / could / might be**
(possible), with Egypt's printed certainty numbers:
must 95–100%, may/could 50%, might 30%, can't 95–100% NOT.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the modal
lessons (can, must, may, might as helpers) and leads to
Modal perfects, its past twin, inside this level.
**What the learner can do at the end:** look at a sign,
choose the right certainty word, and say the number of
their sureness — and never use mustn't where can't be
belongs.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the modal basics (must for rules,
  can for ability — this lesson gives the same words a
  second job, guessing). Recommended, never forced —
  this lesson stands alone for a learner who lands on
  it directly (DIRECT-ENTRY rule).
- Speaker convention: the explanation is read in the
  learner's main language; the TARGET forms (must be,
  can't be, might be…) are never translated and are read
  in the English voice.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a wet umbrella dripping by a
   door, a puddle spreading under it.** Written slot:
   "a picture of a sign you can read — the umbrella says
   it is raining, without words." What the student does:
   the student makes the deduction before the rule:
   "It must be raining outside."
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of a certainty ladder with four
   rungs marked 100, 50, 30, 0, a climber standing on the
   50 rung.** Written slot: "a picture of Egypt's
   certainty numbers as a ladder." What the student does:
   the student climbs: says one must, one may, one
   might sentence about the same picture.
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of a boy in a thick coat,
   sweating, under a hot sun, beside a sign reading
   40°.** Written slot: "a picture of a deduction that
   fails — he can't be cold." What the student does: the
   student says the can't sentence and the sign that
   proves it.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first**: three signs in the learner's room,
   three guesses in their own language — one sure, one
   half-sure, one unsure.
2. **The job**: deduction — guessing from signs, not
   knowing from being told.
3. **Must be — sure it is true**: Egypt Secondary 12
   Term 1, Unit 3, Language in Context (pp.40–43):
   must + base = 95–100% sure it's true.
4. **Can't be — sure it is NOT true**: the same box:
   can't = 95–100% sure it's NOT true. The trap named:
   mustn't is a rule word, never a deduction word.
5. **May / could be — half sure**: 50% possibility
   (Egypt's numbers).
6. **Might be — less sure**: 30% possibility (Egypt).
7. **The set in the wild**: Yemen Book 6, Unit 5 —
   children deduce what a found object is (might be,
   may be, must be, could be) from close-up photo
   fragments (5.2, p.34); Cambodia Grade 11, Unit 25
   "Mysteries" — modals of probability and possibility
   for common mysteries (Teacher's Book spreads,
   pp.135–140); Malaysia Form 3 Unit 8 (certainty /
   deduction among its modal groups) and Form 5 Unit 6;
   Nepal Grade 7 Unit 10 — deduction uses (can't be,
   must be, might have gone) inside its modal unit
   (pp.97–106).
8. **Do it yourself**: the three closing tasks.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck between may and might: both mean possible —
  Egypt's ladder puts may/could at 50 and might at 30.
  If you would bet half, say may; a third, say might.
- Stuck on can't be vs mustn't: mustn't forbids
  ("You mustn't run" = do not run). A deduction of
  NOT-true is always can't be, never mustn't.
- This lesson's next door: Modal perfects (same level)
  — the same ladder, aimed at the past.
