# Lesson plan — Noun Phrases

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** Noun Phrases
**Lesson:** noun-phrases — building the noun group: describing words and phrases that travel with a noun
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 13 — Joining and Describing
**The one thing:** The noun phrase — a noun with its describing words before it and its describing phrases after it.
**This lesson's own level:** Grammar lesson (sentence building): adjective + noun groups, stacked adjectives, noun phrases of the advanced books, and adjective phrases as noun-phrase parts.

## Why this lesson exists

Indonesia teaches the noun group in three books and none had a lesson: Grade 9's noun groups, Grade 11 English for Change's adjective phrases, and Grade 11 Tingkat Lanjut's noun phrases named as such. The course's describing lessons teach adjectives alone; the group they live in had no home. This lesson is built from the placements under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06).

## Sub lessons

1. The noun group — describing word + noun
2. Stacking — more than one describing word
3. Noun phrases — the advanced books' named form
4. Adjective phrases inside noun phrases

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is needed before this lesson. Describing Words in a Sentence gives the describing words; this lesson builds the groups they form, and each stands alone.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- Build a noun group: "a big red ball."
- Stack describing words in the book-taught order.
- Read and build the advanced noun phrase, with words after the noun too.
- Use an adjective phrase as part of a noun phrase.

## What you need
Nothing special — things in the room to describe.

## The run of the lesson
1. ADD IMAGE: a plain apple, then a big apple, then a big red apple in a wooden bowl — the phrase grows as the picture grows. The learner says what changed.
2. Teach sub lesson 1 by growing one noun at a time on real objects.
3. ADD GIF (1–2 seconds): blocks stacking onto a noun — each block is one more describing word.
4. Sub lesson 4 reads phrases from stories: find the noun, then everything that travels with it.

## Add-ons
- Grow-it chain: each learner adds one describing word to the class noun — "a cat… a black cat… a small black cat…" — until it breaks, laughing.
- Phrase hunt: in any story page, find three noun phrases and read them aloud whole.

## If the learner is stuck
Keep to two-word groups (describing word + noun) on real things until they are automatic. Stacking and after-noun phrases come one step each after.

## Teacher note
Indonesia's Grade 9 book calls them noun groups; the senior books name the noun phrase and stock it with adjective phrases. The lesson keeps the books' own ladder: group, stack, named phrase, phrase inside phrase.
