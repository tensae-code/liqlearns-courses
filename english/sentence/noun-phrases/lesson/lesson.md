# Noun Phrases

## Do it first

Say "apple." Now say it better — a big apple. Now even better — a big red apple. You just built a noun phrase without knowing its name. In this lesson you will learn how describing words group around a noun, how to stack them, and how the senior books' noun phrases carry phrases inside them.

## Sub lesson 1 — The noun group — describing word + noun

A **noun group** is a noun with its describing words travelling with it:

"a **small cat**" — "**the tall tree**" — "my **new shoes**"

Indonesia's Grade 9 book teaches noun groups as their own grammar — because a sentence is built of groups, not single words. The noun is the head; the describing words stand **before** it.

Build three about your room: "the ___ door", "my ___ book", "a ___ chair".

## Sub lesson 2 — Stacking — more than one describing word

Groups can grow: "a **big red** ball" — "the **small wooden** box" — "her **two young** brothers."

The order Indonesia's books teach is the adjective order's order (Adjective Order carries it fully): size and number before colour, colour before the noun. One word is a start; two or three make the picture exact.

Grow this one step by step: "a dog → a big dog → a big black dog → my big black dog."

## Sub lesson 3 — Noun phrases — the advanced books' named form

The senior books name the whole group a **noun phrase** — and it can carry words **after** the noun too:

"the man **in the blue shirt**" — "a house **on the hill**" — "the story **about a brave girl**"

Indonesia's Grade 11 Tingkat Lanjut teaches noun phrases by name. Everything in the phrase points at one thing — the head noun — and the whole phrase does one job in the sentence.

Find the head noun in each phrase above. Then build one: a person + words after.

## Sub lesson 4 — Adjective phrases inside noun phrases

A describing word can itself be a little phrase: "**full of water**" — "**afraid of dogs**" — "**rich in vitamins**." These are **adjective phrases**, and they sit after the noun:

"a bottle **full of water**" — "a boy **afraid of dogs**" — "food **rich in vitamins**"

Indonesia's Grade 11 English for Change teaches adjective phrases inside its describing work. Now your noun phrases can carry real information, not just colour and size.

Build two: one about a person, one about a thing near you.

## Say it to your companion

Describe something in the room with a three-part noun phrase — your companion must point at it. Then swap: a phrase with words after the noun, and one with an adjective phrase. No pointing at the wrong thing!

## Do It Yourself

Write five noun phrases: two simple groups, one stacked group, one with words after the noun, and one with an adjective phrase. Then use two of them in full sentences.
