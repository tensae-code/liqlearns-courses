# The causative — short answer

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Causative Have/Get Something Done · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Causative Tenses and Active Family · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Causative Force, Permission and Arrangement.

Answer in whole sentences.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Report it: a tailor made your new
shirt (you arranged it, the tailor sewed it).
**Answer: I had my shirt made.** (got is equally good)
— arranger + thing + participle (Malaysia, Form 4,
Module 8).

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** Tomorrow, the dentist will examine
your teeth — you arranged it. Say it.
**Answer: I will have my teeth examined tomorrow.** —
the future coat on have, the rest unmoved.

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** Your little brother was allowed to
stay up late. Report it with let.
**Answer: Our parents let him stay up late.** (Any
let + person + bare verb earns it; permission is let's
job — Egypt's box.)

**4. (Sub lesson 3)** Say the difference: "The car was
repaired" vs "Dad had the car repaired."
**Answer: The first reports only the car's story; the
second keeps Dad in the sentence as the arranger.** —
the plain passive has no arranger; the causative is
built around one.
