# Lesson plan — The causative

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** The causative
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 24, "Two Shapes After
a Verb" — the second lesson of the level
(CATCH-UP-PLAN.md), after Gerunds and infinitives.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the causative —
saying that you ARRANGE for something to be done or
for someone to do it, instead of doing it yourself:
have/get + object + past participle (I had my hair
cut), and the active family have/get someone to do,
with make (forced) and let (permission) beside them.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The passive
(the past participle does the same work here) and on
Gerunds and infinitives (make/let + the bare verb
returns, now with a person between). China's make +
person rides in as an example inside make — China
teaches make + somebody, not the have/get system, and
the sources say so.
**What the learner can do at the end:** report any
arranged job in the have/get + object + participle
shape, in present, past and future; ask someone
through have (bare verb) or get (to-verb); and choose
make for forced, let for allowed, have/get for arranged.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The passive and Gerunds and
  infinitives. Recommended, never forced — the shapes
  are rebuilt from their parts in the explanation.
- The speaker button reads the explanations in the
  learner's own language; the target forms (had my hair
  cut, made me wait…) stay in English, never translated.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a boy in a barber's chair,
   scissors in the BARBER's hands, the boy's own hands
   empty, under the words I HAD MY HAIR CUT.** Written
   slot: "a picture of someone else doing the job."
   What the student does: the student points at who
   holds the scissors — and says why the sentence does
   not need his name.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of three doors: ARRANGE
   (have/get), FORCE (make), ALLOW (let), each with a
   different face behind it.** Written slot: "a picture
   of the three doors of the causative family." What
   the student does: the student sends one sentence
   through each door aloud before reading the family
   table.
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of a broken bicycle being
   handed across a counter to a mechanic, with the
   owner's speech bubble: "I'm having it repaired."**
   Written slot: "a picture of a thing handed over to be
   done." What the student does: the student names the
   three pieces — haver, thing, participle — in order.
4. **Slot 4 — a picture of two sentences side by side:
   "The mechanic repaired my bike" and "I had my bike
   repaired," with the camera arrow pointing at the
   mechanic in one and at I in the other.** Written
   slot: "a picture of the camera changing chairs."
   What the student does: the student says whose story
   each sentence tells — the doer's, or the arranger's.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first** (lesson/lesson.md): three jobs other
   people do for you — hair, shoes, phone — said first
   in the learner's own language.
2. **The passive causative**: have/get + object + past
   participle (Egypt, Secondary 12 Term 1, Unit 5,
   Language in Context, pp.74–76: its example "She is
   getting her eyes checked"; Malaysia Form 3 Unit 11
   and Form 4 Module 8 teach have/get something done).
3. **Moving have through the tenses**: had, am having,
   will have, have had — the object and participle
   never change their seats.
4. **The active causative**: have + person + bare verb;
   get + person + to-verb (Egypt's box sets the pair
   side by side; Malaysia Form 5 Unit 11, Grammar 1,
   p.139 teaches have someone do something beside
   have/get something done; Vietnam Grade 12 Unit 6
   names active and passive causatives together).
5. **Make, let, have someone do**: Nepal Grade 8
   Unit 6 lists the family in one grammar line —
   causatives have/get something done; make/let/have/
   get someone do. Make = forced (Egypt's box: MAKE
   forced, LET and ALLOW permission), and China's
   Grade 9 example rides in here: make + somebody +
   bare infinitive — "Sad movies make me cry"
   (Unit 11, Grammar Focus p.84).
6. **Causative or plain passive?** The arranger stands
   in the causative; the plain passive has no arranger
   and cares about the thing.
7. **Do it yourself** (the three closing tasks in the
   lesson): the arranged week, the three doors, the
   errand report.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck between cut and to cut in "I had my hair cut":
  ask who did it. If the named person ARRANGED it and
  a thing received it, the shape is object + participle
  — the participle never takes to after have/get.
- Stuck on have vs get with a person: have takes the
  bare verb (I had him check it), get takes to (I got
  him to check it) — Egypt's box is the picture to keep.
- This lesson's next door: Verbs with two objects —
  where give/send/show carry a person AND a thing, the
  two passengers this lesson's errands keep meeting.
