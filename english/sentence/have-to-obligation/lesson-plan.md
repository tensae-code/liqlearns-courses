# Lesson plan — Have To for Obligation

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** Have To for Obligation
**Lesson:** have-to-obligation — what you must do: I have to study; She has to work; I don't have to go
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 26 — How Sure Are You? (the modal family, at its base)
**The one thing:** Have to and has to for things that must be done; had to for the past; don't/doesn't/didn't have to for things that are not needed.
**This lesson's own level:** Grammar lesson (obligation): have to + plain verb; has to for he/she/it; had to for everyone in the past; negatives and questions with do/does/did.

## Why this lesson exists

Bolivia's Grade 9 book teaches have to in full — the fullest treatment in the set — and its compendium carries a “Have to” spread whose table in fact teaches possession (see the honesty note in sources). No course lesson taught have to as obligation on its own. This lesson is built from those placements under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06).

## Sub lessons

1. Have to / has to — obligation now
2. Had to — obligation in the past
3. Don't have to — not needed

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is needed before this lesson. Modal Verbs teaches must and should beside it; each lesson stands alone, and together they complete the obligation family.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- Say obligations with have to (I/you/we/they) and has to (he/she/it).
- Put obligations in the past with had to.
- Say a thing is NOT needed with don't/doesn't/didn't have to — and know how that differs from mustn't.

## What you need
Nothing special — the duties of your own day are the material.

## The run of the lesson
1. ADD IMAGE: a morning list — get up, brush teeth, go to school, do homework — with HAVE TO over it. The learner says which ones they have to do before any rule is told.
2. Teach sub lesson 1 on the learner's own day: “I have to get up early. She has to walk to school.” Watch the has.
3. ADD GIF (1–2 seconds): yesterday's list fades in with HAD TO over it. Sub lesson 2: the same duties, one day back.
4. Sub lesson 3 is the freeing one: Sunday's list with lines crossed out — “I don't have to get up early!” — and the warning: don't have to (not needed) is not mustn't (not allowed).

## Add-ons
- Duty chain: each learner names one duty of the person on their left: “Maria has to cook today.”
- Sunday game: take turns freeing each other — “You don't have to wash the dishes!”

## If the learner is stuck
If has to is the only stumble, drill the third person with names of family members: “Mum has to…, Dad has to…” until it is automatic.

## Teacher note
The Grade 9 book itself misuses the negative once — “We don't have to miss Oruro's carnival” (printed p. 53), where it means mustn't; the lesson teaches the true difference and the slip is recorded in lesson/sources.md. The compendium spread is cited honestly in sources: its table teaches possession, and obligation appears only in its follow-up writing prompt.
