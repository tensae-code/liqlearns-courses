## True or false

1. “She have to work” is correct. (Sub lesson 1)
   **Answer: False** — she takes has to: “She has to work.”

2. After “does”, we use “have to”, not “has to”. (Sub lesson 1)
   **Answer: True** — “Does she have to come?”

3. Had to is the past of both have to and has to. (Sub lesson 2)
   **Answer: True** — one form for every person in the past.

4. “Did you had to go?” is correct. (Sub lesson 2)
   **Answer: False** — after did: “Did you have to go?”

5. “Don't have to” means “not allowed”. (Sub lesson 3)
   **Answer: False** — don't have to means not needed; mustn't means not allowed.

6. “We don't have to miss the carnival” correctly means “we must not miss it”. (Sub lesson 3)
   **Answer: False** — that is the Bolivia book's own mix-up (printed p. 53): don't have to means missing it is not needed, the opposite of the book's meaning.
