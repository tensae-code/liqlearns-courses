# Have To for Obligation

## Do it first

Think of your day. Some things you do because you want to. Some things you do because you must. “I have to get up early. I have to do my homework.” Say two things you have to do today — try it now, before the rules. Then we will put the whole family of have to in order: now, in the past, and the happy one — what you don't have to do.

## Sub lesson 1 — Have to / has to — obligation now

**Have to** + the plain verb says a thing must be done: “I have to study.” “You have to wear a uniform.” “We have to help at home.”

For he, she and it, it becomes **has to**: “She has to work today.” “He has to catch the bus.”

Questions and negatives use do/does: “Do you have to go?” “She doesn't have to come.” Notice: after do/does it is always **have to**, never has to.

Say yours: “I have to… My mother has to…”

## Sub lesson 2 — Had to — obligation in the past

Yesterday's duties take **had to** — one form for everybody: “I had to get up at five.” “She had to stay at home.” “They had to wait.”

Questions and negatives use did: “Did you have to work yesterday?” “We didn't have to go to school — it was a holiday.”

Tell me two things you had to do yesterday. Had to does the past all by itself — no has, no have.

## Sub lesson 3 — Don't have to — not needed

Now the freeing form. **Don't have to** says a thing is **not needed** — you may do it or not: “It's Sunday. I don't have to get up early.” “She doesn't have to cook; we are eating out.” “We didn't have to pay; it was free.”

Careful — this is NOT the same as **mustn't**. Mustn't says **not allowed**: “You mustn't run here.” Don't have to says **no need**: “You don't have to run — walk, we have time.”

Even Bolivia's book mixes these once (it prints “We don't have to miss Oruro's carnival” meaning we must not miss it). You now know better than the book: not needed = don't have to; not allowed = mustn't.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion: one thing you have to do today, one thing someone in your family has to do, one thing you had to do yesterday, and one thing you don't have to do this weekend. Four sentences, four forms.

## Do It Yourself

Write your duty list for tomorrow (three have to / has to sentences about you and your family), then write yesterday's list again with had to, and finish with the best sentence of all: two things you don't have to do on Sunday.
