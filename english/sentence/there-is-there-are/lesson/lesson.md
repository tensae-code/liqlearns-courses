# There Is and There Are

## Do it first

Look around your room. There is a table. There are some chairs. You just used the two sentences this whole lesson is about — English's way of saying what a place has. Point at one thing near you and say “There is a…”. Then point at several things and try “There are…”. That is the game; now the rules.

## Sub lesson 1 — There is — one thing

**There is** says one thing exists: “There is a park in my town.” “There is a book on the table.”

Short form: “There's a park.”

There is also goes with things we do not count: “There is milk in the glass.” “There is water in the bottle.” Milk and water take **is**, always — remember that; even Bolivia's book slips on it once (its own some/any page fixes it).

Say it about your room: “There is a…”

## Sub lesson 2 — There are — more than one

**There are** says more than one thing exists: “There are two parks.” “There are four chairs.”

Numbers and words like some and many love there are: “There are many students.” “There are some books.”

Two plurals never change their shape — Grade 7's book names them: “There are five **sheep**.” “There are three **fish**.” Sheep and fish stay the same for one and for many.

Count your room: “There are … windows.”

## Sub lesson 3 — Questions and negatives

Ask with is/are first: “**Is there** a clock?” “**Are there** any apples?” Short answers: “Yes, there is.” “No, there aren't.”

Ask the number: “**How many** chairs **are there**?” “There are four.”

Say what is NOT there: “There **isn't** any milk.” “There **aren't** any eggs.” Notice **any** in negatives and questions — some lives in positive sentences.

Now describe the picture in your head of a perfect park: what is there, what is not there, and how many trees are there?

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion about your street: two “there is” sentences, two “there are”, and one thing that is not there (“There isn't a…”). They must ask you one “How many…?” question.

## Do It Yourself

Describe your room in six sentences: two with there is, two with there are (one with a number), one negative (there isn't/aren't any…), and one answer to “How many … are there?” Write them, then read them aloud standing in the room itself.
