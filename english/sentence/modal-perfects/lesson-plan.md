# Lesson plan — Modal perfects

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Modal perfects
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 26 — "How Sure Are You?" —
the second lesson of the level (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the certainty
ladder aimed at the PAST — modal + **have** + past
participle: **must have** (sure it happened), **can't
have** (sure it didn't), **may / might / could have**
(it possibly happened), **should have / shouldn't have**
(criticism: the right thing was not done), and
**needn't have** (it was done, but it wasn't necessary).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on How sure you
are (the same ladder about now) and The past perfect
(the have + participle machinery). It closes Level 26.
**What the learner can do at the end:** deduce about a
finished day with the right rung; criticise a past
action — their own or another's — with should have;
and say an effort was wasted with needn't have, without
mixing it with didn't need to.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Modal Perfects — Deduction About the Past** — the shape (modal + have + past participle, have never changing) and deduction about the past: must have, can't have, may/might have at Egypt's prices. The shape paragraph is kept in this sub lesson, before the deduction paragraph. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Modal Perfects — Criticism and Regret** — criticism and regret: should have for the right thing not done, shouldn't have for the wrong thing done — blame and regret, not deduction. (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **Could Have — the Open Door** — could have: the past held a real possibility that nobody used. (Was the first half of depth Level 3.)
4. **Needn't Have — Done, But Not Needed** — needn't have: the action was done, but it was not needed — held apart from didn't need to. (Was the second half of depth Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: How sure you are (the present
  ladder — this lesson is that ladder plus have) and
  The past perfect. Recommended, never forced — this
  lesson stands alone for a learner who lands on it
  directly (DIRECT-ENTRY rule); the present ladder is
  restated here in one breath.
- Speaker convention: the explanation is read in the
  learner's main language; the TARGET forms (must have,
  should have, needn't have…) are never translated and
  are read in the English voice.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of an empty plate with crumbs,
   a chair pushed back, a door just closed.** Written
   slot: "a picture of signs about a finished moment —
   the eating is over, the signs remain." What the
   student does: the student deduces before the rule:
   "Somebody must have eaten here."
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of a boy standing in rain
   without a coat, an umbrella hanging unused at home
   by the door.** Written slot: "a picture of a should
   have — the right thing, not done." What the student
   does: the student says the criticism kindly: "You
   should have taken your umbrella."
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of a woman carrying five heavy
   bags up a hill, while an empty bus passes her.**
   Written slot: "a picture of a needn't have — the
   hard thing was done, and it was not needed." What
   the student does: the student says it: "You needn't
   have carried them."

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first**: the finished breakfast table — three
   deductions about a past the learner did not see.
2. **The shape**: modal + have + past participle; have
   never changes (no has), and in speech it shrinks —
   must've, should've.
3. **Deduction about the past**: Egypt's box gives the
   past half of its ladder in the same place as the
   present (Sec 12 T1, pp.40–43): must have / may have
   / might have / can't have + past participle.
4. **Criticism — should have / shouldn't have**: the
   same Egypt box names them BLAME AND REGRET; Nepal's
   Grade 9 Teacher's Guide gives Unit 14, "Criticising",
   to present perfect with should ("You shouldn't
   have…"; "should have kept the receipt") —
   teacher's-guide evidence, worded so.
5. **Possibility that never happened — could have**:
   "We could have won" (the door was open; nobody
   walked through it).
6. **Needn't have — done but not needed**: against
   didn't need to (not done, or unknown); Venezuela's
   Grade 11 guide, Lesson 17, plans should have /
   could have / might have — use and structure,
   affirmative/negative/interrogative — teacher's-guide
   evidence; Bolivia's Year 6 book works "might have"
   for conjectures in past time (Theme 6); Malaysia's
   Form 4 Module 7 stocks the modal-perfect set.
7. **Do it yourself**: the three closing tasks.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck choosing the rung: it is the present ladder
  with have bolted on. Ask the present question first
  (must be? can't be? might be?) — then move it into
  the past with have + participle.
- Stuck on should have vs must have: must have GUESSES
  (the sign proves it); should have JUDGES (the right
  thing was not done). One looks at signs, one at duty.
- This lesson's next door: Level 27 — The Small Words
  That Point, opening with articles.
