# Modal perfects — true or false

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Modal Perfects — Deduction About the Past · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Modal Perfects — Criticism and Regret · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Could Have — the Open Door, except items about needn't have, which are Sub lesson 4 — Needn't Have — Done, But Not Needed.

Say true or false. If it is false, say why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** After a modal, have can change to has with he/she/it: "She must has left."
**Answer: False** — have never changes after a modal: "She must have left."

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** "Must have" and "should have" do the same job.
**Answer: False** — must have GUESSES from signs; should have JUDGES against duty (Egypt's box sets deduction and blame side by side).

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** "You should have kept the receipt" is criticism of something the person failed to do.
**Answer: True** — Nepal's anchor sentence (TG G9 U14, p.110): the keeping was the right thing, and it was not done.

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** "I needn't have cooked" and "I didn't need to cook" say exactly the same thing.
**Answer: False** — needn't have says the cooking WAS done but not needed; didn't need to leaves open whether it was done at all.
