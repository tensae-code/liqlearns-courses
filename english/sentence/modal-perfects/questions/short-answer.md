# Modal perfects — short answer

Answer in whole sentences.

**1. (Level 1)** The window is open, the room is cold, and nobody is home. Deduce one thing that cannot have happened, and one that must have.
**Answer: The window can't have opened itself. Somebody must have opened it before leaving.** — can't have for the impossible, must have for the proved (any pair with signs named earns it).

**2. (Level 2)** Yesterday you stayed up very late and today you cannot think. Criticise yourself.
**Answer: I shouldn't have stayed up so late. / I should have gone to bed early.** — should have for the right thing not done.

**3. (Level 3)** Your team lost a game it nearly won. Say the could-have sentence.
**Answer: We could have won the game.** — a real past possibility, unused (Venezuela TG G11 L17).

**4. (Level 3)** You made sandwiches for guests who, it turned out, had already eaten. Say it with needn't have.
**Answer: I needn't have made the sandwiches.** — done, but not needed; if the thing was not done at all, needn't have may not be said.
