# Modal perfects — short answer

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Modal Perfects — Deduction About the Past · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Modal Perfects — Criticism and Regret · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Could Have — the Open Door, except items about needn't have, which are Sub lesson 4 — Needn't Have — Done, But Not Needed.

Answer in whole sentences.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** The window is open, the room is cold, and nobody is home. Deduce one thing that cannot have happened, and one that must have.
**Answer: The window can't have opened itself. Somebody must have opened it before leaving.** — can't have for the impossible, must have for the proved (any pair with signs named earns it).

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** Yesterday you stayed up very late and today you cannot think. Criticise yourself.
**Answer: I shouldn't have stayed up so late. / I should have gone to bed early.** — should have for the right thing not done.

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** Your team lost a game it nearly won. Say the could-have sentence.
**Answer: We could have won the game.** — a real past possibility, unused (Venezuela TG G11 L17).

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** You made sandwiches for guests who, it turned out, had already eaten. Say it with needn't have.
**Answer: I needn't have made the sandwiches.** — done, but not needed; if the thing was not done at all, needn't have may not be said.
