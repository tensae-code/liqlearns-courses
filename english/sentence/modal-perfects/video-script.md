# Video script — Modal perfects

Super-simple voice. Read slow. The learner hears this
before or beside the written lesson.

---

Yesterday is gone. But its signs are here.
Now we guess about the past.

The plate is empty. Crumbs are on the table.
Somebody MUST HAVE eaten here.
Must have — I am sure it happened.

The door is locked. The key is here. Nobody went out.
He CAN'T HAVE left.
Can't have — I am sure it did NOT happen.

Maybe it happened. Maybe not.
She MIGHT HAVE gone to the shop.
He MAY HAVE taken the bus.
Could have is also possible.
We COULD HAVE won the game. But we did not.

Now a different job. Not guessing. Judging.
You are wet. Your umbrella is at home.
You SHOULD HAVE taken your umbrella.
Should have — the right thing. You did not do it.
You SHOULDN'T HAVE run. Now your leg hurts.
Shouldn't have — the wrong thing. You did it.

And one more.
You carried five heavy bags up the hill.
But the bus was coming. It was empty.
You NEEDN'T HAVE carried them.
Needn't have — you did it. But you did not need to.

The shape is one shape.
Must have. Can't have. Might have.
Should have. Needn't have.
The modal, then HAVE, then the third shape of the verb.
Have never changes. Not has. Have.

Now you try.
One guess about yesterday: must have.
One judgement: should have.
One wasted thing: needn't have.
