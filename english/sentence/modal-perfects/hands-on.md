# Hands-on — Modal perfects

You do it, we guide you. Everything here is done with a
companion or the Explain Companion, out loud.

1. **The detective table.** Your companion secretly
   acts out a small finished scene at a table (eating,
   writing, packing) and leaves the signs. You may not
   ask what happened — only deduce: three must have,
   two can't have, one might have, each with its sign.
   Then the curtain lifts: how many deductions stood?
2. **The receipt game (Nepal's own scene).** One person
   plays the customer who returned a radio without its
   receipt; the other is the friend who told them so
   before: "You should have kept the receipt." Then
   trade three real small regrets of the week — each
   must be said twice: once as criticism (I should
   have…) and once as its shouldn't-have twin where
   one fits.
3. **The wasted-effort hunt.** Find three things in
   your home that were done but needn't have been: food
   cooked for guests who cancelled, a long walk a bus
   would have covered, water boiled twice. Say each
   with needn't have — and check: it WAS done. If it
   was not done, it may not wear this shape.
4. **The open door.** Each person names one could have
   from their own life — a game that could have been
   won, a bus that could have been caught. The
   companion's job: ask "was the door really open?"
   A could have must have had a real open door.
