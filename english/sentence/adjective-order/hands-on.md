# Hands-on — Adjective order

You do it, we guide you. Everything here is done in the
learner's real day, with a companion or the Explain
Companion. The TARGET forms are always said in English;
the talk around them may be in the learner's own language.

1. **The shelf walk.** Lay eight pieces of paper on the floor in the book order (quantity → material). A companion calls out adjectives for one real object; you must stand on each word's box as you say it, then say the whole stack standing at the noun. Three objects, stacks growing 2 → 3 → 4 words.
2. **Scramble and repair.** Write six adjective stacks on cards, three in order and three scrambled. Swap decks with a companion: read each aloud, keep the right ones, and repair the scrambled ones by moving words — naming the box each word moved to.
3. **Shop-window descriptions.** Describe three things in a real or pictured shop window for a buyer who cannot see them: each description must carry at least three adjectives in order, ending in material (a small square wooden box). The companion draws or finds the thing from the words alone.
