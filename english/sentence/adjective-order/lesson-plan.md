# Lesson plan — Adjective order

**Course:** English · **Title:** Sentence · **Lesson:** Adjective order
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 30, "Dressing a Sentence" (CURRICULUM.md / CATCH-UP-PLAN.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the fixed order adjectives must stand in when they stack before a noun — quantity, quality/opinion, size, shape, age, colour, nationality/origin, material — and why a stack in the wrong order sounds broken even when every word is right.
**What the learner can do at the end:** stack two, three and four adjectives in the book order without a swap (a small old brown bag; two bad small old red American plastic shoes in the books' own worked example); place quantity first and material last, just before the noun; and repair a scrambled stack live.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Describing words in a sentence (the two homes of a describing word). Recommended, never forced — this lesson stands alone for a learner who lands on it directly (DIRECT-ENTRY rule).
- Speaker convention: the explanation is read in the learner's main language; the TARGET forms are never translated and are read in the English voice.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a shelf with eight labelled boxes in a row — NUMBER, QUALITY, SIZE, SHAPE, AGE, COLOUR, ORIGIN, MATERIAL — and a bag waiting at the end. What the student does: the student walks three adjectives along the shelf into their boxes before the noun is named.**
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of two shoes: one captioned TWO BAD SMALL OLD RED PLASTIC SHOES in order, one with the same words scrambled. What the student does: the student reads both aloud and points at the one that sounds right, before the rule is stated.**
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of a small old brown bag beside a pretty silk Japanese kimono. What the student does: the student names which adjective in each stack names size, age, colour, quality, material, origin — Mongolia's own worked examples.**

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first**: describe one real object with three adjectives in the learner's own order, then hear the English order.
2. **The full table**: Quantity · Quality · Size · Shape · Age · Color · Nationality · Material (Bolivia Year 6, Topic 5.1, p.221).
3. **The discovery examples**: a small old brown bag — SIZE AGE COLOR; a pretty silk Japanese kimono — QUALITY MATERIAL ORIGIN (Mongolia IX, Unit 4, p.52).
4. **The second table**: opinion, size, age, shape, colour, nationality, material (Sudan SPINE 6, Chapter 7, Grammar Corner).
5. **The practice ground**: more than one adjective in the right order — "a big, modern TV", "the big, square, yellow blanket" (Cambodia Grade 10, Unit 21, printed pp.111–116).
6. **The ground rule under it all**: the adjective comes before the noun (Egypt, Adjectives and Adverbs box, Secondary 11 Term 2, p.54).
7. **Do it yourself**: the three closing tasks.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck on a long stack: build it from the noun backwards — material first in your head (nearest the noun), then origin, colour, age, shape, size, quality, quantity at the far end.
- Stuck between quality and opinion: both live in the same early box in the books' tables (Bolivia's Quality, Sudan's opinion) — either word order inside that one box is not today's fight.
- Stuck on commas: short stacks in speech usually run together; the books' own examples write some with commas (a big, modern TV) — the ORDER is the rule, the comma is a pause.
- This lesson's next door: Participle adjectives (same level) — describing words made from verbs: bored and boring.
