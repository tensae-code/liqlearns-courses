# English course — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

His order (2026-10-25): build from the bottom. A learner does not start
at greetings — nobody learns to say hi before the letter. They have to
know how to read first. The course path climbs by the size of the thing:

**Alphabet → Phonics → Word → Sentence → Paragraph → Essay → Story → Novel**

That ladder IS the path order (recommended, never forced — a learner
can jump in higher, per the prerequisite rule; the "start here instead"
path points down this ladder to the bottom).

The titles, in ladder order:

1. **Alphabet** — the letters themselves: their shapes, their names.
   This is where the course starts, and where the KG books start.
2. **Phonics** — the sounds the letters make, and putting sounds
   together to read words: blending, two-letter sounds, blends, word
   families. (Split from the old "Letter" title by his ruling,
   2026-10-26: only the alphabet lesson was really about letters —
   the rest is sounds and reading words. "Letter" also names the
   letter you write and send, which will be its own thing later;
   the two meanings no longer share one title.)
3. **Word** — the word kinds: nouns, verbs, adjectives, and the rest,
   each as its own lesson under this title.
4. **Sentence** — one idea, whole and ended properly.
5. **Paragraph** — sentences together making one bigger idea.
6. **Essay** — paragraphs together making a whole piece about one thing.
7. **Story** — a story: people, a place, things happening.
8. **Novel** — the long story: many chapters, one big world.

Speaking sits in the ladder at the Sentence stage — a conversation is
sentences spoken between people:
- **Conversation** — talking with another person: opening a talk,
  giving your name, asking how someone is, introducing a friend,
  asking someone's name, asking someone's age, closing the talk. Its
  subtitles (each ONE lesson, one thing): hello-and-your-name ·
  how-are-you · good-morning-and-good-afternoon ·
  introducing-a-friend · whats-his-and-her-name · how-old-are-you ·
  making-offers · making-suggestions · apologising · inviting ·
  asking-politely · agreeing-and-disagreeing · telephoning ·
  shopping · congratulating-and-wishing · giving-advice ·
  giving-directions (the last 11 added 2026-11-07 by the catch-up
  build — every conversation function the read books teach).
  (His ruling, 2026-10-25: "Greetings" was too small a name for this —
  hi and how-are-you are only its opening; introducing yourself and
  the rest are conversation, so the title is Conversation. And asking
  someone's age is part of a conversation too — Age is NOT a title of
  its own; how-old-are-you sits inside Conversation. This supersedes
  the earlier same-day arrangement that kept Age as its own title.)

Titles use the common-sense name of the thing itself (his rule) — never
a book's unit name, never a story or character name. A book's own name
for a part lives in that lesson's sources.md, as a tag. More titles are
added the same way as the books are gone through, only when a country's
book actually teaches that part.

At the bottom of the English path stands **Pencil and Lines** as
**Unit 1** (his correction, 2026-10-27) — and it is also its own
normal course, the shared prerequisite before every language course
(holding a pencil; drawing and understanding lines). It began as a
lesson in this folder (Letter → before-the-letters) and was promoted
by his ruling, 2026-10-25. The English path therefore reads: Pencil
and Lines → Alphabet → Phonics → Word → Sentence → Paragraph →
Essay → Story → Novel.
