# English course — titles

Titles use the common-sense name of the thing itself (his rule,
2026-10-25) — what the part IS, in the words most countries' books use.
Never a book's unit name, never a story or character name. A book's own
name for a part lives in that lesson's sources.md, as a tag.

The titles named by him, as the starting list:

- **Alphabet** — the letters, their shapes and their sounds
- **Words** — the word kinds: nouns, verbs, adjectives, and the rest,
  each as its own lesson under this title
- **Greetings** — saying hello, giving your name, saying goodbye
- **Sentence** — one idea, whole and ended properly
- **Paragraph** — sentences together making one bigger idea
- **Letter** — writing a letter to a person

More titles get added the same way as the books are gone through —
named by what the thing is (Numbers, Sounds, Reading, and so on), only
when a country's book actually teaches that part.

Built so far: Greetings → hello-and-your-name (from Egypt, Primary 1).
