# English course — CURRICULUM (the path)

His order (2026-10-25): the course folders alone don't show what comes
first and what comes next — so this file is the plan a learner follows:
**four levels**, starting from someone who knows no English at all and
ending able to read and write real English. Inside a level, **units**,
and inside a unit, the lessons in the order they are taken. The lessons
themselves live in their title folders (see TITLES.md); this plan is
the path through them.

**Levels, not terms (his ruling, 2026-10-26):** the plan's four parts
were first called terms, like a school year. They are LEVELS now: a
learner climbs to the next level when they are ready, not when a
calendar says so. Same order, same lessons — only the name and the
meaning changed: a level is climbed at the learner's own pace.

**How the levels work (his correction, 2026-10-25):** the level
descriptions below were his EXAMPLE, not sealed boxes. A level is a
BASE for the next level — Level 1 is the base Level 2 stands on, Level
2 is the base for Level 3, and so on up. A level is not "only" its
named topics: some lessons are huge and grow past a neat slot, and new
material slots in wherever its base is ready. What the levels fix is
the ORDER — what must be standing before the next thing can stand on
it — not a wall around each level's topics.

## The first page (what learners are told)

Everything in this course comes from government textbooks from around
the world — government-approved books only; that is the research this
course is built on. The one exception is the **Tip boxes**: a tip in a
box is from us, the people making LiqLearns — a clearer way to say a
thing, or help from other material — written to help you understand.
Tips are ours; the lessons are the books'.

The plan is built from the books actually read so far (Egypt, Libya,
Nicaragua) and GROWS as each next finished country's books are read —
a new book's units are slotted into the level they belong to, in
order. (Each book's stocking notes — which level its basics feed —
are kept with the book's own map, in EGYPT-BOOK-MAP.md and
books/<country>.md, not in this plan.) Lessons already built are
marked ✅; the rest are named from the book maps already read and are
built as the reading reaches them.

**Before Level 1 — Pencil and Lines** (its own course, prerequisite,
recommended never forced): holding a pencil · drawing lines ✅

## Level 1 — Letters, sounds, and first words
*The bottom of the ladder. End of level: the learner reads and writes
every letter, sounds out new words, and can say hello and their name.*

- **Unit 1 — The Alphabet**: the letters, their shapes and sounds
  (the-alphabet ✅) — Egypt KG1/KG2 teach 13 letters + 13 letters;
  Primary 1 re-teaches all 26 in sounds order.
- **Unit 2 — Sounds Together**: putting sounds together to read a word
  (blending-sounds ✅ · two-letter-sounds ✅)
- **Unit 3 — First Words**: reading whole little words (blends ✅ ·
  word-families ✅ — the -at, -an, -ig families and their kin)
- **Unit 4 — Conversation**: hello-and-your-name ✅ · how-are-you ✅ ·
  good-morning-and-good-afternoon ✅ · introducing-a-friend ✅ ·
  whats-his-and-her-name ✅ · how-old-are-you ✅

## After Level 4
The ladder keeps climbing — deeper sentence work (Libya G8–G10's spine:
present perfect, the passive, conditionals), longer essays, and the
Story → Novel titles. Later levels get their own plans here, in this
file, as the reading reaches those books.
