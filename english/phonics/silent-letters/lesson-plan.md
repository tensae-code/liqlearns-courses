# Lesson plan — Silent letters

**Course:** English · **Title:** Phonics · **Lesson:** Silent letters
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Sounds Together
— inside the unit with Blending sounds and Two-letter
sounds (CURRICULUM.md; CATCH-UP-PLAN.md places the
pronunciation extensions inside Unit 3).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the letters you
can SEE in a word but do not SAY — and the families they
travel in: kn-, wr-, silent b after m, silent l in
-lk words, silent gh, silent h, silent w.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **What a silent letter is** — a letter in a word that is not pronounced when the word is spoken (Ecuador's definition); Kuwait's frame for every family: "A letter you see, but you don't say." (Was depth Level 1's opening — the word.)
2. **The kn- family** — the k keeps quiet: knife, knight, knit, knot, knob, knee — when kn starts a word, say only the n. (Was part of depth Levels 1–2 — the word, then the family.)
3. **The wr- family** — the w keeps quiet: write, wrong, wrap, wrinkle, wreck — when wr starts a word, say only the r. (Was part of depth Levels 1–2.)
4. **The -mb family** — the b keeps quiet after m: comb, lamb, climb, thumb — and the trap taught corrected here: knowledge has no b; its silent letter is the k of the kn- family. (Was part of depth Levels 1–2.)
5. **The -lk family** — the l keeps quiet: walk, talk, chalk — when you meet lk after a, say the k and let the l rest. (Was part of depth Levels 1–2.)
6. **The -gh family** — g and h keep quiet together: bright, through, bought, night, high, straight. (Was part of depth Levels 1–2.)
7. **Silent h and the rest** — hour, honest, heir, and India's other groups — gnat, tongue, whistle, could, often — and Barbados names silent letters as set work in its word-attack list. (Was part of depth Levels 1–2.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Blending sounds (sounds pushed
  together — a silent letter is the sound that is NOT
  there). Recommended, never forced.
- Spelling the words is writing work; this lesson is the
  SAYING: which letter keeps quiet.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a silent letter is, in Ecuador's words: a
   letter in a word that is not pronounced when the word
   is spoken (Medium text, Worksheet 6, printed p.35).
2. Read the families aloud with the quiet letter kept
   quiet: kn- (knife, knight, knit, knot, knob, knee);
   wr- (write, wrong, wrap); -mb (comb, lamb, climb,
   thumb); -lk (walk, talk, chalk); -gh (bright, through,
   bought); h (hour, honest); w (wrinkle, wrong).
3. Use Kuwait's frame for every family: "A letter you
   see, but you don't say" (Skyline 5B).
4. Catch the trap words: knowledge starts with a silent
   k, not a silent b — Ecuador's own card files it wrong,
   and this lesson teaches it corrected.

## What you need
Nothing but a voice. Paper and a pencil for the sorting
step.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner reads "knife" and
   "knight" aloud as spelled, hears the k that is not
   there, and says the words the English way
   (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD AUDIO at the top of the lesson: the words knife,
     knight, comb, walk said slowly, the silent letter's
     place marked by a tiny silence — the student says
     each word after it, keeping the quiet letter quiet.
2. **The explanation** — the definition and the families,
   book by book: Ecuador's picture-card families (K, B,
   H, W); Kuwait's four spelling families (wr-, -mb,
   -lk, -gh); India's picked-out lists; Barbados's
   syllabus naming (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE under the families: a picture of six word
     cards — knife, comb, walk, bright, hour, write —
     each with its silent letter drawn in pale grey —
     the student says each word and points at the grey
     letter they did not say.
   - ADD GIF: a picture that moves — the word WRINKLE
     shrinking its w away as a mouth says "rinkle",
     1–2 seconds — the student says the word as the
     letter fades.
3. **Say it to your companion** — read the family lists
   aloud; the companion points at any letter they HEARD
   that should have stayed quiet (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed —
     the families read in the super-simple voice (made
     later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — Ecuador's own closing task: pick
   five words from the lists, make a sentence for each,
   and read them aloud (Worksheet 6, printed p.41).

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- AUDIO — the family words read slowly → the student
  repeats, keeping the quiet letter quiet.
- IMAGE — a picture of word cards with the silent letter
  in pale grey → the student says the word, points at grey.
- GIF — a picture that moves: the silent letter fading as
  the word is said → the student says it as it fades.
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later) → the student
  reads each family along with it.

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the
plan is the build sheet, so nothing the lesson needs gets forgotten.

## If the learner is stuck
- Says every letter they see → cover the word, say it from
  the picture first (Ecuador's cards are picture cards),
  then uncover and find the grey letter.
- Mixes the families → one family a day, Kuwait's way:
  one family per unit, with its frame sentence said first:
  "A letter you see, but you don't say."
- This lesson's neighbours: Two-letter sounds (before —
  letters that DO sound together); The three sounds of
  -ed (next door — another spelling that is not said the
  way it looks).
