# Silent letters — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Phonics · Lesson: Silent letters
Built from: Ecuador, English — Medium Sublevel Student
Text for Curricular Transition (2024), Worksheet 6
"Mastering Silent Letters in English Pronunciation"
(printed pp.35–41) — Kuwait, Skyline English Student's
Book 5B (the silent-letter families wr-, -mb, -lk, -gh)
— India, the Marigold books and the later NCERT books
(silent letters picked out) — Barbados, Primary Language
Arts Syllabi, Classes 1–4 (silent letters named in the
word-attack list) — syllabus evidence: Barbados has no
government pupil textbook for Language Arts.

This lesson is ONE thing only: letters you can see in a
word but do not say when you speak the word. Joining
sounds that ARE there is Blending sounds, next door.

A note on voices: the explanations in this lesson can be
read to you in your own language with the speaker button.
The words being practised — knife, comb, walk and the
rest — are the TARGET: they are never translated, and
the reader says them in the English voice, then comes
back to yours.

If you have done Blending sounds first, this lesson will
be easier for you — you might try it first. You do not
have to: everything you need is on this page.

## Do it first
Read these two words aloud, saying every letter you see:
**knife** — **knight**. Now listen to how English
actually says them: "nife", "night". One letter in each
word never made a sound. Which letter? Say the words
again, slowly, and find the letter your mouth skipped.

Media slot — a picture of the word KNIFE with its first
letter drawn in pale grey, beside a real knife. What you
do: say the word, then point at the grey letter — the
letter you saw but did not say.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a silent letter is

**What a silent letter is.** Ecuador's book defines it
in so many words: silent letters are letters in a word
that are **not pronounced when the word is spoken**; the
book adds that they often show the word's origin or tell
two sound-alike words apart, and that they occur in
vowels and consonants alike. Its opening examples:
**knight, gnome, honor, debt, subtle** (printed p.35).
Kuwait's book gives the same idea as a rhyme-frame to say
before every family: **"A letter you see, but you don't
say."**

## Sub lesson 2 — The kn- family

**Family 1 — kn-: the k keeps quiet.** Ecuador's Silent
'K' picture cards (printed p.36): **knife, knight, knit,
knot, knob, knee**. India's books pick out the same
family — knit and knife stand in its Class 4 list, and a
later book groups it whole: **kn-: knee, knife, knot,
know**. When kn starts a word, say only the n.

## Sub lesson 3 — The wr- family

**Family 2 — wr-: the w keeps quiet.** Kuwait teaches
this family first, with **wrap** (Student's Book 5B,
Unit 5). Ecuador's Silent 'W' cards (printed pp.39–40):
**write, wrist, wrinkle, wrong, wreck, wristband**.
India's Class 3 book names the same pair of facts in one
line: the **w in wrinkle** has no sound. When wr starts
a word, say only the r.

## Sub lesson 4 — The -mb family

**Family 3 — -mb: the b keeps quiet after m.** Ecuador's
Silent 'B' cards (printed pp.37–38): **comb, doubt,
limb, thumb, womb, plumber, thumbprint** — doubt's b is
quiet in the middle; in the rest the b sits after m at
the word's end and keeps quiet there. Kuwait's family
is exactly that end-of-word set: **lamb, climb, thumb**
(Student's Book 5B, Unit 6). India's lists carry comb's
cousins doubt and climb too. One card in Ecuador's set
is filed WRONG, and this lesson teaches it corrected:
**knowledge** is printed among the silent-B cards, but
the word has no letter b in it at all — its silent
letter is the k of Family 1. (The book's slip is
recorded in this lesson's sources; the word is taught
here where it belongs.)

## Sub lesson 5 — The -lk family

**Family 4 — -lk: the l keeps quiet.** Kuwait's family
(Student's Book 5B, Unit 7): **walk, talk, chalk**.
India's Class 3 book states it as a single fact — the
**l in talk** is silent — and its Class 3 and Class 4
lists keep picking the family out: walk, talk, calm,
chalk. When you meet lk after a, say the k and let the
l rest.

## Sub lesson 6 — The -gh family

**Family 5 — -gh: g and h keep quiet together.**
Kuwait's family (Student's Book 5B, Unit 8): **through,
bright, bought**. India's lists are full of it — night,
high, straight — and a later Indian book groups it:
**-gh-: neighbour, daughter, fight, straight**. The gh
often stands where an older sound used to be; today it
is written, not said.

## Sub lesson 7 — Silent h and the rest

**Family 6 — silent h, and the rest of India's groups.**
Ecuador's Silent 'H' cards (printed pp.38–39): **hour,
honor, honest, hourglass** — and **vehicle**, whose h is
silent only in some varieties of English, as the filing
honestly notes; learn hour and honest as the sure ones.
India's later grouping adds **h-: honest, honour, hour,
heir**, and a handful more: **gnat** (silent g),
**tongue**, **chalk**, **whistle** (silent t). India's
Class 4 picked-out list is the family's whole playground
in one line: **hour, knit, wrong, doubt, often, know,
night, could, write, knife, high, walk** — could hides
a silent l, often a quiet t in many mouths, know a
silent k.

**Barbados names the skill.** In Barbados's syllabi,
silent letters stand in the word-attack list itself —
the Class 1 body's Reading list (printed pp.28–35) and
the Scope and Sequence row "Contractions, blends (three
letters etc.) and silent letters" (Class 1, printed
p.3) — syllabus evidence that the skill is set work, in
the ministry's own order, beside the blends and digraphs.

Media slot — a picture of six word cards — knife, comb,
walk, bright, hour, write — each with its silent letter
in pale grey. What you do: say each word aloud, then
point at the grey letter you did not say, and name its
family.

## Say it to your companion
Read the six family lists aloud, one family at a time,
saying Kuwait's frame before each: "A letter you see,
but you don't say." Your companion's job: raise a hand
the moment they HEAR a letter that should have stayed
quiet. Swap when you finish — reader becomes listener.

## Do It Yourself
1. Sort these into families and read each family aloud:
   knee, lamb, talk, bright, honest, wrong, thumb,
   chalk, knife, climb, hour, write.
2. Do Ecuador's closing task (printed p.41): pick five
   words from this lesson, make a sentence for each,
   and read your sentences aloud.
3. Hunt one page of any English book you own: find two
   words with a silent letter, write them, and grey the
   quiet letter with your pencil.
