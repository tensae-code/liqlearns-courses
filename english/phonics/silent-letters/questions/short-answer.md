# Silent letters — short answer

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 4 — The -mb family · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 3 — The wr- family (item 2 names all four of Kuwait's families — Sub lessons 3–6 — and is marked for the first of them), and Sub lesson 7 — Silent h and the rest (India's Class 4 picked-out list stands in that part) · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 1 — What a silent letter is (why silent letters are worth learning stands in Ecuador's definition part).

Answer in a word or a short line.

**1. (Sub lesson 4)** Say "comb" aloud. Which letter did your mouth skip?
**Answer: b** — in the -mb family the b after m is not pronounced (Ecuador, printed p.37; Kuwait, Unit 6).

**2. (Sub lesson 3)** Name Kuwait's four silent-letter families and one word from each.
**Answer: wr- (wrap), -mb (lamb / climb / thumb), -lk (walk / talk / chalk), -gh (through / bright / bought)** — Skyline 5B, Units 5–8, one family per unit.

**3. (Sub lesson 7)** India's Class 4 list picks out twelve words with silent letters. Name any four and the letter that is quiet in each.
**Answer: any four of hour (h), knit (k), wrong (w), doubt (b), know (k), night (gh), could (l), write (w), knife (k), high (gh), walk (l), often (t in many mouths)** — Marigold Book Four, Unit 7.

**4. (Sub lesson 1)** Why does Ecuador's book say silent letters are worth learning, beyond the spelling?
**Answer: they often show the word's origin, and they can tell two sound-alike words apart (differentiate homophones)** — Worksheet 6, printed p.35.
