## Egypt, Primary 3 Term 2 (filed 2026-10-25 — book fully read, EGYPT-BOOK-MAP.md)

The vowel-sounds depth this book adds (Units 2–5 phonics):
- **ea and ee — the long-e team** (p.29): "Both 'ea' and 'ee' make
  the long /iː/ sound, like in see" — ea usually in the middle:
  eat, meat, beans · ee in the middle or at the end: cheese,
  green, sweet, tree.
- **Magic e** (p.48): "Some words have a magic 'e' at the end. It
  doesn't make a sound, but it makes the vowel before it to say
  its name" — mad → made, kit → kite, hop → hope. The CVCe pairs
  complete the long-vowel families this lesson started from short
  vowels: the silent e turns the short-vowel family word into its
  long-vowel partner.
- **R-controlled vowels er / ar / or** (p.67): "er like in
  teacher" (computer, teacher, player, letter) · "ar like in car"
  (car, star, park, guitar, farmer) · "or like in corn" (corn,
  horn, fork, short, storm).
- **Long and short oo** (p.83): "The letters 'oo' can be tricky
  because they can sound different" — long oo /uː/: moon, zoo,
  pool, food · short oo /ʊ/: book, foot, look, cook, wood, hook.
- **Long and short ow** (p.90): long ow /oʊ/: snow, grow, window,
  throw, know · short ow /aʊ/: cow, now, owl, how, brown.

## Egypt — Primary 4 Term 1 (read 2026-10-25)
- **Short vs long vowels, defined in plain words** (U1
  Pronunciation, p.17): short vowel sounds are quick and do NOT
  say the letter's name; long vowel sounds say the letter's name.
  Worked pairs: hat/plate, pot/home, pen/feed.
- **Silent e, restated** (U5 Pronunciation, p.89): a silent e at
  the end of a word, after one consonant, makes the vowel before
  it a long vowel — rope, slide, cube, face, stone, flute, gate,
  nine. (Third statement of the magic-e rule in the Egypt run:
  P3 T2 p.48, here p.89.)
Same country, deeper layer — the country count does not change.

## Egypt — English Primary 4 Term 2 (2025/2026) — added 2026-10-25

- **R-controlled vowels COMPLETED to all five** (U2 L3
  Pronunciation, p.36): the book's rule — the letter r changes
  the sound of the vowel before it. P3 T2 taught er/ar/or; this
  book's table runs all five families: ar (star, far), or
  (world, work), er (teacher, runner), ir (birthday, shirt), ur
  (fur, nurse) — with a sort-the-words table across er/ir/ur/
  ar/or.
- **oi / oy — one sound, two spellings, position decides** (U5
  L3 Pronunciation, p.87): both spellings say the same sound;
  oi usually sits in the MIDDLE of a word (coin, boil, point,
  noise) and oy usually at the END (boy, toy, enjoy, joy) —
  where the sound sits in the word tells you which spelling to
  write. Same family as P3 T2's ea/ee (two spellings, one
  sound). A Tip on the next page defines rhyming words (words
  that sound the same at the end).
