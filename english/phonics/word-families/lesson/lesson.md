# Word families — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Phonics · Lesson: Word families
Built from: Egypt, English Primary 1 Term 2 — and Laos, English
for Primary Schools Grade 3.
This lesson is ONE thing only: words that share the same ending —
same letters, same sound — so when you know one, its whole family
opens. The single letters and their blends live in their own
lessons, before this one.

## Do it first
Say this word: cat. Now change only the first letter: mat. Now
again: hat. Three words, one family — you read two of them for
free. Feel how little work the second and third were? That is the
whole lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Families with short a

So some words are cousins: they end the same way, in letters and
in sound. Egypt's book teaches them family by family — each word
printed letter by letter with a dot under each letter and a
blending arrow, then read-and-match work, choosing the right word,
and a rhyming chant, because families rhyme.

The book builds one family set for each short vowel:

- **Short a:** -an (pan, man) · -ap (map, cap) · -at (mat, hat)

## Sub lesson 2 — Families with short e

- **Short e:** -et (pet, jet, wet) · -en (pen, hen, ten)

## Sub lesson 3 — Families with short i

- **Short i:** -ig (big, fig, dig) · -in (bin, fin, win) ·
  -ip (hip, dip, lip)

## Sub lesson 4 — Families with short o

- **Short o:** -og (dog, fog, log) · -op (mop, hop, pop) ·
  -ot (pot, hot, dot)

## Sub lesson 5 — Families with short u

- **Short u:** -ug (hug, jug, mug) · -un (bun, run, sun) ·
  -ut (hut, nut, cut)

Fifteen families, five vowels. Learn the ending once, and every
first letter you put in front of it is a new word you can already
read. That is why the book does families after blends: the family
is where blending starts paying rent.

Later in the same units, the book also has the learner rearrange
letters into the family words and sort words into their family
boxes — the family is a thing you can hold, not just a sound you
can hear.

## Sub lesson 6 — Growing a family

Laos's book plays the swap as the game itself. It starts from
**hat** and changes only the first letter — hat, rat, cat, mat —
then asks the child straight out: "What is the first sound?" The
family is built by swapping, one letter at a time, and the child
names what changed.

## Say it to your companion
Take the -at family. Start from cat and grow it: change only the
first letter, twice. Then explain why the second and third words
were almost free. (The ending was already known.)

## Do It Yourself
1. Write the -an, -ap and -at families from memory. Check them
   against the lists above.
2. Pick one family and say a rhyming line with all its book words
   in it, out loud, three times.
3. Sort these into their families: pan, jet, dig, cap, wet, fig.
   (Answers: -an pan · -et jet, wet · -ig dig, fig · -ap cap.)
