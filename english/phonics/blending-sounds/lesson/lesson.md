# Blending sounds — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Phonics · Lesson: Blending sounds
Built from: Libya, English for Libya Primary 1 — Egypt, English
KG1 / Primary 1 — and Laos, English for Primary Schools Grade 3.
This lesson is ONE thing only: pushing letter sounds together until
they become a word. Knowing the letters themselves is the lesson
before this one (The alphabet); two-letter sounds and word families
are their own lessons next door.

## Do it first
Say these sounds slowly, one by one: s… a… t… Now say them again,
faster, with no gaps: s-a-t… sat! You just read a word. That pushing
together is the whole lesson.

## Then the explanation
So a letter alone is a sound. A word is sounds holding hands. When
you read, your mouth does this: it says each sound, then slides
them together until the gaps close and the word stands up.

Libya's book does exactly this in its second unit. Right after the
alphabet, it takes the first sounds group — s, a, t, p, i, n — and
blends them at once into whole words: **sit, pat, tin, pin.**

- s-i-t → sit
- p-a-t → pat
- t-i-n → tin
- p-i-n → pin

Egypt's books are built on the same idea: their letters are taught
in a special order — a few at a time, chosen so that words open
early. Egypt's first letters are T and I, then S, A and N: with a
handful of letters, in the right order, a child can already blend
real words instead of waiting for all 26.

Laos's book puts the slide in the child's hands. The child blends
with **letter tiles** — loose letters on the desk, pushed together
one sound at a time: c-a-p… cap, c-a-t… cat. Its sounds arrive in
the order s, a, t, i, p, n, and the tiles come out as soon as there
are enough sounds to build real words with.

Slow first. Sound, sound, sound — then slide. When the slide is
smooth, you are reading.

## Say it to your companion
Blend "pat" for your companion, slowly, then smooth. Then explain:
what changed between the slow way and the smooth way? (The gaps
closed — the sounds became one word.)

## Do It Yourself
1. Blend these out loud, slow then smooth: sit · pat · tin · pin.
2. Write each word, then write the three sounds under it, one sound
   under each letter.
3. Mark the word that took the longest slide. That word is
   tomorrow's first practice.
4. If you have letter cards or tiles, build cap and cat the Laos
   way: push the tiles together as you slide the sounds.
