# Lesson Plan — The Three Sounds of the -s Endings

**Unit 3 — Sounds Together.**

**One thing:** the three sounds of the final -s / -es endings — /s/, /z/ and /ɪz/ — and how to hear and say the right one.

## Sub lessons

1. **The three sounds.** *Can-do: Hear that the -s ending is not one sound but three: /s/ as in books, /z/ as in plays, and /ɪz/ as in watches — the /ɪz/ sound adds a whole syllable.*
2. **Choosing the right sound.** *Can-do: Pick the sound by the sound before it: quiet (voiceless) sounds take /s/, voiced sounds take /z/, and s/z/sh/ch sounds take /ɪz/ — in plural nouns and in third-person verbs alike.*

(The gate pool for each sub lesson is every question marked with that sub lesson's number in this lesson's `questions/` files — the whole set, because no hardness ranking is written in questions/.)

## Teaching points

- Pronunciation of the final -s / -es endings (item 142): /s/, /z/ and /ɪz/ — the -s counterpart of the -ed endings taught in the-three-sounds-of-ed.
