# The Three Sounds of the -s Endings

## Do it first

Say these two words: *books* and *dogs*. The last sound is not the same! One hisses like a snake — /s/. The other buzzes like a bee — /z/. And *watches* grows a whole extra beat: /ɪz/. By the end of this lesson your ear will know which of the three sounds an -s ending makes, every time.

## Sub lesson 1 — The three sounds

*Very simple. Can-do: Hear that the -s ending is not one sound but three: /s/ as in books, /z/ as in plays, and /ɪz/ as in watches — the /ɪz/ sound adds a whole syllable.*

The letter **s** at the end of a word is a shape-shifter. It makes three sounds:

- **/s/** — a quiet hiss: *books, cats, sleeps, laughs*
- **/z/** — a buzzing sound: *dogs, plays, pens, runs*
- **/ɪz/** — a whole extra syllable, "iz": *watches, buses, wishes, judges*

You meet this ending everywhere: on plural nouns (*two books*) and on he/she verbs (*she plays*). The sound is chosen by the word, not by you — the sound just before the ending decides it.

Try it now: say *books, dogs, watches* slowly, three times. Feel the hiss, the buzz, and the extra beat.

## Sub lesson 2 — Choosing the right sound

*Simple. Can-do: Pick the sound by the sound before it: quiet (voiceless) sounds take /s/, voiced sounds take /z/, and s/z/sh/ch sounds take /ɪz/ — in plural nouns and in third-person verbs alike.*

Here is the choosing rule. Listen to the **last sound of the word before the -s**:

- If it is a **quiet sound** (p, t, k, f, th) — the ending is **/s/**: *stops, sits, books, laughs*.
- If it is a **voiced sound** — your throat buzzes when you say it (b, d, g, v, l, m, n, r, or any vowel) — the ending is **/z/**: *robs, reads, dogs, plays, pens*.
- If it is a **hissing sound** (s, z, sh, ch, j) — the ending is **/ɪz/** and the word grows a syllable: *buses, buzzes, wishes, watches, judges*.

Try it now: sort these words into the three groups: *cups, girls, boxes, sings, classes, doors*. (Answers: /s/ cups; /z/ girls, sings, doors; /ɪz/ boxes, classes.)

## Say it to your companion

Read the lesson aloud to your companion. Then say ten -s words and let your companion name each ending's sound: hiss, buzz, or extra beat.

## Do It Yourself

1. Find five plural words in a book you are reading and say each ending's sound.
2. Say the sentence "She watches, plays and reads" — name the sound of each -s ending.
3. Make your own three lists: three /s/ words, three /z/ words, three /ɪz/ words.
4. Teach the choosing rule to someone at home with cups, dogs and buses.
