# Lesson plan — Dictation

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** Dictation
**Lesson:** dictation — someone speaks; you write exactly what you hear — then you check it
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Sounds Together
**The one thing:** dictation turns hearing into writing: listen whole, write exactly, check honestly
**This lesson's own level:** Phonics-to-writing lesson: the writing first, then the checking, which is where dictation teaches.

## Why this lesson exists

No lesson taught dictation itself. Sierra Leone's Primary 4 Term 1 plans name it alone, as its own lesson (Lesson 35, "Dictation"): the teacher speaks, the pupils write what they hear. The 2026-11-08 re-verification sweep marked it NEW LESSON NEEDED. Under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06) it gets its own lesson; its single source is the plan named in lesson/sources.md, teacher evidence cited as such.

## Sub lessons

1. **Listen and write** — hear a word, then a sentence, and write exactly what was said — sounds into letters, capitals and full stops in place
2. **Check what you wrote** — hear it again and compare, word by word; fix the sounds, the capitals and the stops

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is forced first — this lesson stands alone: it needs only letters the learner can already write. The alphabet and word-building lessons help but are not required first.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- Listen to a spoken word and write it correctly.
- Listen to a spoken sentence and write it whole — capital letter, spaces, full stop.
- Check your writing against a second hearing and fix what is wrong.
- Say why the checking half is where dictation teaches the most.

## What you need
A speaker (teacher, partner, or a recording) and something to write with.

## The run of the lesson
1. **Do it first** — the teacher says one sentence once; the learner writes what they hear, before the two halves are taught (lesson/lesson.md).
2. Work through sub lesson 1: listen and write.
3. Work through sub lesson 2: check what you wrote.
4. ADD AUDIO: three spoken sentences at dictation speed, each said twice with a pause — the student's own dictation set.

## Add-ons
- Partner dictation: partners take turns being the speaker, five words each; the speaker also judges the checking.
- Running dictation for a class that is ready: a sentence is posted outside the door; runners hear-read it, run back, and dictate it to their writer.

## If the learner is stuck
If a learner writes nothing, the sentence was too long — cut to three words and build up. If the writing is right but the checking finds nothing to fix week after week, the sentences are too easy: add words the learner often misspells.

## Teacher note
The lesson stands on one Sierra Leone plan (Primary 4 Term 1, Lesson 35), stated plainly in lesson/sources.md. The JSS handbooks' "Dictation and Spelling" lessons are stocked in word-building, where their spelling half belongs; this lesson keeps dictation's own half — the listening and the checking.
