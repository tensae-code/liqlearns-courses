# Dictation

## Do it first

Your teacher (or your partner) will say one sentence, one
time only. Write exactly what you hear. Ready?

"The mango tree stands behind our school."

Now look at what you wrote. Is every word there? Is every
sound right? That gap — between what was said and what you
wrote — is what dictation trains. Here is how it works.

## Sub lesson 1 — Listen and write

Dictation has one rule: **write what you hear, exactly.**

- Listen to the **whole** word or sentence first. Do not
  write while the first sound is still arriving.
- Then write it: sounds into letters, word by word.
- In a sentence, keep the sentence's own clothes on it — a
  **capital letter** at the start, **spaces** between words,
  a **full stop** at the end.

Try it: "Rain fell on the roof all night." Hear it whole.
Write it. Three words first if you need: "Rain fell hard."

*Difficulty: first steps — short pieces, said clearly, heard whole before any writing.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Check what you wrote

Dictation's teaching lives in its second half.

The speaker says it **again**. Now compare, word by word:

1. Is every word there — none added, none lost?
2. Is every word's spelling the sounds you hear now?
3. Capital at the start? Full stop at the end?

Fix what is wrong. Do not rub out the first try and hide it —
cross it out with one line, write the fix above it, and look
at the difference. The difference is the lesson: it shows
your ears and your hand exactly where they disagree.

*Difficulty: building — the checking is honest work: find the gap, name it, fix it, keep it visible.*

## Say it to your companion

Explain dictation to your companion: the rule (write what you
hear, exactly), the listen-whole-first move, and the three
checks. Then dictate one sentence to them — and judge their
checking, not only their writing.

## Do It Yourself

1. Ask someone to dictate three words you often misspell.
   Write, then check against a second saying.
2. Dictate one sentence to a partner. Let them write and
   check. Then change places.
3. Keep a dictation page in your book: every fixed word goes
   on it. Look at it before the next dictation.
