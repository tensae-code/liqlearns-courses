# Tongue Twisters

## Do it first

Say this out loud, as fast as you can: “Black bug bit a big black bear.”

Did your tongue trip? Good — that is the game. A tongue twister is a short line built to trip your tongue. This lesson teaches you the way the books teach it: slow first, then fast, and never lose a sound on the way.

## Sub lesson 1 — Slow and clear

*Difficulty: first step — for the youngest learners, and for anyone meeting tongue twisters for the first time.*

A tongue twister is not hard because the words are hard. It is hard because the sounds sit close together and your mouth must change shape quickly. The fix the books use is simple: **slow**.

First, listen to the whole twister. Then say it slowly, sound by sound:

- “Black bug bit a big black bear.” The tricky sound is /bl/ — it comes again and again: **bl**ack… **bl**ack… Your lips and tongue must land on it every time.
- “Six silly snakes sneak in the room.” The tricky sound is /s/ — si**x**… **s**illy… **s**nakes… **s**neak. The /s/ keeps coming, in the middle of words and at the start.

Say each twister three times, very slowly. If a sound drops out, stop, find that sound, and say just that word slowly. Then start the line again.

Try it now: pick one twister and say it five times, slowly, touching each sound. No speed yet — clear is the goal.

## Sub lesson 2 — Faster, but still clear

*Difficulty: builds on Sub lesson 1 — the same twisters, at speed.*

Now the game starts. Say the twister once slowly. Then a little faster. Then a little faster again. Each time, the sounds must stay clear — that is the rule of the game. Speed that loses sounds is not winning; it is just noise.

When your tongue trips — and it will — do not push on. Go back to slow, fix the sound that fell, and climb up again. That climb, slow to fast, is the whole skill.

Perform it: stand up, say your twister for someone else, as fast as you can while every word stays clear. Let them listen for the tricky sound — /bl/ or /s/ — every time it comes.

Try it now: take the twister you practised in Sub lesson 1 and climb: slow, medium, fast, fastest-clear. Stop at your fastest CLEAR speed. That speed is yours to beat tomorrow.

## Say it to your companion

Read this lesson aloud to your companion — a toy, a pet, a person. Then perform both twisters for them: once slowly, once at your fastest clear speed. Explain, in your own words: why do we start slow?

## Do It Yourself

1. Say “Black bug bit a big black bear” five times slowly, then climb to your fastest clear speed.
2. Say “Six silly snakes sneak in the room” the same way — listen for every /s/.
3. Teach one twister to someone else: slow first, then faster, the way you learned it.
4. Count: how many times can you say one twister clearly, without a trip, in a row?
