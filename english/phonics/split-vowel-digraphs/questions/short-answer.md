# Short Answer — Split Vowel Digraphs (Magic E)

(Every item is marked with the sub lesson it belongs to: (Sub lesson N). The gate pool for a sub lesson is all of that sub lesson's questions — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the whole set is the pool.)

1. Write the word hope with each sound in its own box, the workbook way. Which two letters share one sound? (Sub lesson 1)
   — h / o_e / p: the o and the final e share the long o sound.
2. What happens to the word mad when you add e at the end? (Sub lesson 1)
   — It becomes made, and the a changes from its short sound to its long sound.
3. Name one o_e word and one u_e word from the workbook lists. (Sub lesson 1)
   — o_e: rode / broke / woke / home / note (any one). u_e: cute / use / tune / dune / tube (any one).
4. Read delete and inside aloud. Which split digraph does each one use? (Sub lesson 2)
   — delete: e_e. inside: i_e.
5. Why is the e in alone called part of a split digraph? (Sub lesson 2)
   — Because it works together with the o, split by one consonant (n), to make the long o sound.
