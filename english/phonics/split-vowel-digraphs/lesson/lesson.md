# Split Vowel Digraphs (Magic E)

## Do it first

Read these two words: **hop** and **hope**. They differ by one letter — the e at the end — and that e changes everything: the o says its short sound in hop, and its long sound, its own name, in hope. Try cub and cube, plan and plane, hid and hide. The e does not make a sound itself. It reaches back over one consonant and changes the vowel before it. That pattern is this lesson's one thing.

## Sub lesson 1 — The split digraphs one at a time — a_e, e_e, i_e, o_e, u_e

*Difficulty: first step — the pattern itself, one spelling at a time.*

A **split digraph** is one sound written in two parts: a vowel letter, then one consonant, then **e**. The two parts of the spelling are split by the consonant, and together they make the vowel's long sound.

- **a_e** — made, snake, late, cake, take, game, same, came, gave, state
- **e_e** — these, theme, eve, delete, extreme (the same weeks also practise ee: teeth, need, seem, sheet, cheek)
- **i_e** — ride, slide, life, while, time, like, fire, white, inside, five
- **o_e** — rode, broke, woke, home, close, drove, note, rope, choke, nose
- **u_e** — cute, use, tune, dune, rude, flute, June, prune, mute, tube

Read each list aloud. In every word the vowel says its name — a, e, i, o, u — and the final e is silent. Now drop the e in your mind: mad, them, rid, rod, cut. The vowel falls back to its short sound. The e is doing the work.

Try it now: read plan, plane, hid, hide, hop, hope, cub, cube, pet, Pete. Say which word of each pair has the long sound, and point at the e that makes it so.

## Sub lesson 2 — Split digraphs mixed in harder words

*Difficulty: builds on Sub lesson 1 — the spellings now come mixed, in longer words.*

Books do not sort their words by spelling. In the Class III workbook the split digraphs return all together, at harder words: **amaze, plane, while, inside, close, alone, rude, cube, these, delete**.

Work word by word: find the vowel, find the e one consonant later, and let the vowel say its name. a-MAZE. in-SIDE. a-LONE. de-LETE. The pattern is the same one you learned in Sub lesson 1 — only the words are bigger.

Try it now: read amaze, inside, alone, delete aloud. Then write each word sound by sound, one sound in each box, the way the workbooks do it: the split digraph's two letters carry one sound between them.
