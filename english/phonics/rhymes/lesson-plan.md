# Lesson Plan — Rhymes

Unit 3 — Sounds Together. Planned shape: three sub lessons —
first hearing rhymes (listening to rhymes and songs and
joining in), then saying rhymes whole (reciting rhymes and
tongue-twisters with their rhythm and actions), and last
making rhymes (interpreting them in your own words, talking
about what rhymes and songs do for us, and composing short
rhymes of your own). The lesson is built from Ghana's NaCCA
Basic 1–3 curriculum, whose oral-language strand opens with
rhymes at every level: Rhymes is Strand 1's first two
standards at Basics 1, 2 and 3 alike. The course's
word-families lesson teaches rhyming endings inside words;
this lesson teaches the rhymes themselves — the little poems
learners hear, say and make.

## Sub lessons

1. **Hearing rhymes** — listen to rhymes and familiar songs,
   and join in.
2. **Saying rhymes** — recite rhymes and tongue-twisters with
   rhythm, expression and actions.
3. **Making rhymes** — say what a rhyme means in your own
   words, talk about what rhymes and songs do for us, and
   compose a short rhyme.

Gate pool for each sub lesson's prove-it check: the items of the
earlier sub lessons, hardest first.
