# Multiple Choice — Rhymes

Choose the one right answer.

1. Where do rhyming words usually live in a rhyme? (Sub lesson 1)
   - A) At the starts of the lines
   - B) At the ends of the lines
   - C) Only in the title
   - D) Hidden in the middle of one word
   **Answer: B**

2. Do wall and fall rhyme? (Sub lesson 1)
   - A) No, because they start with different letters
   - B) Yes — rhyming is about sound, and their ending sounds match
   - C) Only when written
   - D) Only in songs
   **Answer: B**

3. When you listen to a rhyme, joining in is: (Sub lesson 1)
   - A) Forbidden until the end
   - B) Part of the listening itself
   - C) Only for the teacher
   - D) A punishment
   **Answer: B**

4. A tongue-twister is: (Sub lesson 2)
   - A) A line built to trip the tongue, practised slowly first, then faster
   - B) A very long poem
   - C) A rhyme with no rhythm
   - D) A song in another language
   **Answer: A**

5. What makes saying a rhyme "saying it well"? (Sub lesson 2)
   - A) Shouting it
   - B) Finishing first
   - C) A steady beat, expression, and the actions the rhyme carries
   - D) Reading it silently
   **Answer: C**

6. With a tongue-twister, the right way to speed up is: (Sub lesson 2)
   - A) As fast as possible at once
   - B) Clearly first, then a little faster each time
   - C) Never say it aloud
   - D) Only backwards
   **Answer: B**

7. To interpret a rhyme means: (Sub lesson 3)
   - A) To copy it neatly
   - B) To tell its little story in your own plain words
   - C) To translate every word
   - D) To sing it louder
   **Answer: B**

8. To compose a two-line rhyme, your second line must: (Sub lesson 3)
   - A) Start with the same letter
   - B) Be longer than the first
   - C) End with a sound that matches the first line's end — and still make sense
   - D) Use no verbs
   **Answer: C**

9. Which ending completes the rhyme "The hen ran under the tree, / …"? (Sub lesson 3)
   - A) "…and drank some water."
   - B) "…where no one could see."
   - C) "…because it was Tuesday."
   - D) "…with a loud bark."
   **Answer: B**

10. One thing rhymes and songs do for us is: (Sub lesson 3)
    - A) They make books heavier
    - B) They help memory by carrying language in a shape the mouth enjoys
    - C) They replace all other lessons
    - D) They hide the meaning of words
    **Answer: B**
