# Sight Words

## Do it first

Look at this word: **the**. Try to sound it out — t-h-e gives you nothing you can use. And yet you read it just now without stopping, because you know it whole, at a glance. That is a sight word. Some words appear on every page, and some refuse to be sounded out — so a reader learns them whole, by sight, the way you know a face.

## Sub lesson 1 — The first sight words

Eritrea's Grade 1 book teaches sight words in sets, worked the same way each time: read the words, match them, write them. The first set (printed pp.56–58) is **book, and, you, can, little**. The second set (printed pp.64–65) is **come, go, help, I, three**. Learn them as whole shapes: say the word, look at its letters in order, cover it, write it, check it. Ten words — and already whole lines of the book open up: *I can go. You can come. Come and help.*

*Difficulty: Ten very common words, each learned whole; the only trap is letters that look alike — book and look, come and home — so the matching work in the book matters.*

## Sub lesson 2 — More sight words, read in sentences

The Grade 1 book's third set (printed pp.72–73) is **me, play, in, good, four**; its fourth (printed pp.82–83) is **big, six, see, the, run**; its fifth (printed pp.89–90) is **to, what, where, make, look**. The Grade 2 book then builds set after set through the whole year — **the, and, go** (printed p.7), **what, come, to** (p.16), **this, is, a** (p.27), **in, help, look** (p.34), **he, she, of, it** (p.43), **yes, that, at, they** (p.45), and more sets after those. The way to hold them is to meet them inside reading: a sight word is not finished when you can read it on a card — it is finished when your eye takes it in stride inside a sentence and does not stop.

*Difficulty: The words themselves are short; the work is speed and certainty inside real reading, not on a list.*

## Say it to your companion

Your companion shows you a sight word — a card, or a finger under a word in a book. You read it at a glance, no sounding out. Miss one, and that word goes on your practice list. Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the five Grade 1 sets out from memory, set by set. Check them against this lesson and mark any word you missed.
2. Take a page you can read and underline every sight word from this lesson. Count them — that count is why these words come first.
3. Make five sentence cards, each using one word from the fifth set (to, what, where, make, look).
