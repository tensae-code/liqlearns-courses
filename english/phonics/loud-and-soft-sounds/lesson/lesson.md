# Loud Sounds and Soft Sounds

## Do it first

Clap your hands once, hard. Now clap once, very gently. Same hands — but the first clap was **loud** and the second was **soft**. The whole world of sound sorts this way. This lesson teaches your ear the two sides.

## Sub lesson 1 — Loud sounds

Some things make loud sounds: a drum, a bell, a dog barking, thunder, a whistle, a car's horn. A loud sound can be heard far away, and it can wake you up.

Say the word with the sound: drum — BOOM. Bell — DING DING. Dog — WOOF WOOF. When you hear a loud sound, your ear knows: something big, or something near, or something calling for attention.

*Difficulty: The sounds that carry far and wake you up.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Soft sounds

Some things make soft sounds: a whisper, a cat's purr, rain on leaves, a mouse, your own breathing, a page turning. A soft sound is heard only when you are near — and only when you listen.

Whisper your name. That is a soft sound. Now say it out loud. Same name — two strengths of sound. Sorting things by the noise they make — loud or soft — is one of the first things your ear learns to do, and reading aloud will ask your ear to do it with your own voice.

*Difficulty: The sounds you must lean in to hear.*

## Say it to your companion

Name three loud sounds and three soft sounds for your companion. Your companion must guess: loud or soft? Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Walk around your home and listen. Write down one loud sound and one soft sound you hear.
2. Make a loud sound with your hands. Make a soft sound with your hands.
3. Whisper a word to someone. Then say it loudly. Ask them which was which.
