# Two-letter sounds — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Phonics · Lesson: Two-letter sounds
Built from: Libya, English for Libya Primary 1 and 2 — Egypt,
English Primary 1 Term 2 — and Laos, English for Primary Schools
Grade 3 (the ck pair).
This lesson is ONE thing only: two letters that together make ONE
sound. Single letters are The alphabet; blends — two letters where
you hear BOTH sounds — are a different lesson next door.

## Do it first
Say the sound of c. Now say the sound of h. Now put them together
the book's way: ch — as in the sound that starts "chair". One sound,
made of two letters. Try sh, too. You just did the whole lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — The pairs set by set

So most letters hold one sound each. But English has sounds that no
single letter owns. For those, two letters team up — and together
they say one new sound that is neither of them alone.

Libya's Grade 1 book teaches them set by set across the year,
after the single letters:

- **ai · oa · ie · ee · or** (Unit 5)
- **ng** (Unit 6) — and **oo**, which does two jobs: the long oo in
  spoon and zoo, and the short oo in foot and book.
- **ch · sh · th** (Unit 7) — and th also does two jobs: the quiet
  th in mouth and three, and the buzzing th in father and this.
- **qu · ou · oi · ue · er · ar** (Unit 8)

## Sub lesson 2 — The pairs again

Grade 2 then re-teaches them in a spiral: oa · ee · ue · oo (both)
· th (both) · ch · sh come round again with the same words.

Egypt meets one of them inside a letter lesson: its letter Qq is
taught as **qu** — quiet, quack, queen, question — because in
English, q travels with u and the pair says one sound-pair, /kw/,
as one team.

## Sub lesson 3 — More pairs: wh · ph · ck · kn · wr

Libya's sets are not the whole team. The other books carry
five more pairs, and each is taught the same way — two
letters, one sound:

- **wh** — Jordan's Grade 3 book teaches it with question
  words and sea words together: **what, when, wheel,
  whistle, whale** (Semester 1, Unit 4, printed pp.32–39).
- **ph** says the sound of f. Pakistan's Grade 1 book meets
  it at the start of a word — **phone** (Unit 4, printed
  p.33) — and a unit later at both ends of words: **photo**
  at the start, **graph** at the end (Unit 5, printed
  pp.43–45). Iraq's Grade 4 activity book puts it on one
  phonics page: phone, photo, elephant (Unit 3, printed
  pp.36–51).
- **ck** — the pair that ends a word after a short vowel:
  **clock, black, backpack, truck** (Jordan, Grade 3,
  Semester 2, Unit 7, printed pp.24–31). Armenia's Grade 3
  book builds it as a sum: "SO+CK = sock" (Unit 2.7).
- **kn** — here one letter is silent: the k says nothing,
  and the pair says only n. Sierra Leone's Primary 2 plans
  teach it inside a story, "The Knife" (Term 2, Lessons
  76–77); Nepal's Grade 2 book gives the set: **knee, knot,
  knit, knife** (printed pp.13–55).
- **wr** — the w is the silent one, and the pair says only
  r: **write**. Sierra Leone teaches it inside a poem,
  "Wiggle Your Fingers" (Primary 2, Term 2, Lesson 99).

## Sub lesson 4 — One sound, more spellings

The pairs you know are not the only spellings of their
sounds. The books teach the same long sounds spelled other
ways, as pairs of their own:

- Long a is also spelled **ay**: Malaysia's phonics section
  sets **ay/ai** side by side (Get Smart Plus 4, printed
  pp.125–128) — rain and day carry the same sound.
- Long e is also spelled **ea**: Iraq's Grade 4 activity
  book teaches **ee / ea** making the same sound — **sea,
  see, cheese, teacher, bee** (Unit 5, printed pp.56–71).
- Long i is also spelled **igh** and **ie**: Ecuador's
  Grade 3 book teaches long i three ways — **igh** in high,
  night, light, right (printed pp.58–59), and **ie** in tie,
  pie (printed pp.60–61).
- Long o is also spelled **ow** — but ow does two jobs, and
  China's Grade 5 book sorts them side by side: **cow, down,
  how, now** against **snow, slow, window, yellow** (Volume
  1, Unit 3, printed p.26). The snow group is the oa sound
  wearing a new spelling.
- Long u is also spelled **ui**: Ecuador's book sets long u
  as **ue and ui** together (Grade 3, Units 3–5 Phonics,
  printed pp.96–99).
- The r-sounds have more spellings than er and ar: China's
  Grade 4 book teaches the r-controlled sounds as one ladder
  — **er · ir/ur/er · ar · or** — so **ir** and **ur** say
  the sound er says (Volume 2, Let's spell pages, printed
  pp.6, 16, 26, 40, 50; review p.60).

## Sub lesson 5 — The cover test

The test that tells a two-letter sound from a blend: cover one
letter. If the sound that is left is not half the word's sound —
if the two letters were really saying one thing together — it is
a two-letter sound.

## Say it to your companion
Name the two two-job pairs (oo, th) and say both jobs of each, with
the book's words. Then explain the cover test in your own words.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write the pairs down in their book sets, and say each sound
   out loud three times.
2. Say these book words slowly and find the pair inside:
   spoon · foot · mouth · father · queen.
3. Mark the pair you find hardest. That pair is tomorrow's first
   practice.
4. Find the pair inside the new words: whale · clock · knife ·
   phone · night · snow — and say which of them were new today.
