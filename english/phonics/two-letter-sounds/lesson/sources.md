only — two letters saying one sound is a different thing from the

### Egypt — Primary 3 Term 1 (read 2026-10-25) — the pair-sounds book
The fullest two-letter-sound set in any book read so far, each with
the book's own stated rule (full map: EGYPT-BOOK-MAP.md):
- **wh** (Digraph "wh", p.12): what, when, where, whale, wheel,
  white (+ why in the fill-ins).
- **x says /gz/** (Phonics Focus, p.35): exam, exit, example,
  examine, exist.
- **ph says /f/** (Phonics Focus, p.38): phone, photo, elephant,
  graph, alphabet.
- **Double consonant endings** (p.49) — the book states the rule:
  after a short vowel sound, words ending in F, L, S or Z often
  double the letter: ff (cuff, off, staff), ll (bell, pull, tell),
  ss (grass, less, miss), zz (buzz, fuzz, jazz).
- **ck says /k/** (p.53): "The letters 'ck' together make one /k/
  sound" — duck, sock, back, neck, truck.
- **Hard c / Soft c** (pp.73, 76): c says /k/ before a, o, u or as
  final ck (cat, came, cold, carry); c says /s/ before e, i, y
  (face, nice, city, ice, pencil, cent) — sorted side by side.
- **Hard g / Soft g** (pp.87, 93): g says /g/ before a, o, u or at
  a word's end (game, dog, gum, green); g says /dʒ/ before e, i, y
  (giraffe, Egypt, page, huge, magic).
Same country, deeper layer — the country count does not change.

## Egypt — Primary 4 Term 1 (read 2026-10-25)
- **The f / v pair** (U2 Pronunciation, p.32): /f/ is voiceless —
  the throat doesn't vibrate (fish, forest, factory, flag); /v/
  is voiced — the throat vibrates (van, violin, view, vest).
- **The p / b pair** (U4 Pronunciation, p.69): p is voiceless —
  lips together, blow out air (peak, pan, pond); b is voiced —
  lips together, use your voice (bear, bin, bat).
- The voicing thread now runs th (P3 T1) → f/v → p/b across the
  Egypt books: same letter-shape pairs, told apart by the voice.
Same country, deeper layer — the country count does not change.

## Egypt — English Primary 4 Term 2 (2025/2026) — added 2026-10-25

- **Silent letters (k, l)** (U1 L3 Pronunciation, p.21): the
  letter k is not pronounced when it comes before n at the
  start of a word (knock, knife, knee, know); the letter l is
  not pronounced in some words, especially after a or o (walk,
  talk, half, folk — and calm in the exercises).
- **Silent letters (b, w)** (U3 L3 Pronunciation, p.52): the
  letter b is hardly heard when it comes after m at the end of
  a word (lamb, thumb, comb, climb); the letter w is not
  pronounced when it comes before r at the beginning of a word
  (write, wrong, wrist, wrap).

## Egypt — English Primary 5 Term 1 (2026/2027) — added 2026-10-25

- **Vowel digraphs** (U3 L3 Pronunciation, p.51): two vowels
  together make just ONE sound — ea in "leap" says the long
  e; oo in "flood" says a short u; oa in "boat" says the long
  o. (The book spells it "diagraph" once in an exercise
  heading, as printed.)
- **Trigraphs** (U4 L3 Pronunciation, p.69): a group of THREE
  letters that represents a single sound — dge says "j"
  (bridge is pronounced "brij"), tch says "ch" (match →
  "mach"), igh says "i" (right → "rite"). The letter-group
  line grows: digraph (two letters) → trigraph (three).

## Egypt — English Primary 6 Term 1 (2025/2026) — added 2026-10-26

- **The /ǝr/ sound** (U1 Pronunciation, p.15): the learner
  builds the sound by filling the missing letters of
  words that end in it (b_ _ _d, st_ _ _ _, squ_ _ _ _),
  then the book tells how to SAY it: open your mouth
  slightly, say "eh," then add the /r/ sound — it is ONE
  sound, like in "wear" or "stare." The sound at the end
  of teacher / farmer / ladder gets its own named lesson
  moment for the first time in the run.
- **/e/ vs /ɪ/ — both with articulation instructions**
  (U2 Pronunciation, p.30): /e/ — open your mouth
  slightly, tongue low-mid and front (ten /ten/, hen,
  bed). /ɪ/ — tongue high but relaxed, lips and jaw
  relaxed, tongue tip just behind the bottom front
  teeth (sit, bit, in). The short-i sound of the early
  books is finally given its own how-to-say panel, set
  against its nearest neighbour.
- **The -ed three sounds — the voicing RULE stated**
  (U5 Pronunciation, p.78): Primary 4 Term 2 gave this
  as a tip with examples; this book prints the rule
  itself. Past-tense -ed is heard three ways: /t/ after
  voiceless sounds (p, k, f, s, ch, sh — liked,
  watched, kissed); /d/ after vowels or voiced sounds
  (b, g, v, l, m, n, r, z — played, cleaned, called);
  /ɪd/ after t or d (wanted, needed). The sound the
  ending makes is decided by the sound before it.

## Egypt — English Primary 6 Term 2 (2025/2026) — added 2026-10-26

- **Triphthongs** (U5 Pronunciation, p.85): a triphthong
  is a vowel sound that combines THREE vowel sounds
  together in ONE syllable. Two families are taught:
  ow-er (hour, our, flower — "start with 'ow,' then
  glide to 'er'") and ay-er (layer, player, mayor). The
  letter-group line completes its climb in the Egypt
  run: digraph (two letters, one sound) → trigraph
  (three letters, one sound) → triphthong (three vowel
  sounds gliding inside one syllable).
