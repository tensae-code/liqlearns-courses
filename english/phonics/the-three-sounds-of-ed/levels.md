# The three sounds of -ed — questions by level

Practice by level, tied to the skill number. The questions live in
the type files; this file is the by-level view. The levels are the
depths inside this one thing — one spelling, three sounds — set by
the pattern across the countries' books: hearing the three sounds
first (Bolivia's lists), then the rule that chooses, then sorting
new words live and meeting the describing-word corner case (India).

## Level 1 — hear the three
Level 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: read
Bolivia's three lists aloud — helped /t/, played /d/,
wanted /ɪd/ — and hear that only the /ɪd/ family adds a
whole syllable.

## Level 2 — the choosing rule
Level 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: state
and use the rule — after t or d, /ɪd/; after a voiceless
sound, /t/; after a voiced sound or a vowel, /d/ — and
prove it with the throat buzz test on new words.

## Level 3 — sort anything, and the corner case
Level 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: sort
unseen regular past verbs into the three families
without a miss, and read the describing-word -ed the
full way India teaches it — a learn-ed pundit, said
"learn-id", rhyming with created and waited.
