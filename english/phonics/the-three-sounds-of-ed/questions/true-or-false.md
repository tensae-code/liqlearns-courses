# The three sounds of -ed — true or false

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — The three lists of -ed · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 1 — The three lists of -ed (both its items ask the lists' own words and which list adds a beat) · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 5 — The describing-word -ed.

Say true or false. If it is false, say why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** The letters -ed are always pronounced the same way.
**Answer: False** — one spelling, three sounds: /t/, /d/, /ɪd/ (Bolivia, Year 3, Topic 4, student p.219).

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** "Helped" and "looked" end with the same -ed sound.
**Answer: True** — both are /t/: p and k are voiceless sounds, and both words stand in Bolivia's /t/ list.

**3. (Sub lesson 1)** The -ed in "played" adds a whole new syllable, said "play-ed".
**Answer: False** — played is one beat with a /d/ ending; only verbs ending in t or d give -ed its own syllable (wanted, needed).

**4. (Sub lesson 5)** In "I learnt my lesson", the -ed of a describing word is what India teaches.
**Answer: False** — India's contrast is the other way: "I learnt" is the action word; the full-said "learn-id" belongs to the describing word, a learn-ed pundit (Marigold Book Four).
