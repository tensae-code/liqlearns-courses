# The three sounds of -ed — short answer

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — The three lists of -ed · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — The choosing rule for -ed, and Sub lesson 3 — The buzz test (split item by item) · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 2 — The choosing rule for -ed (sorting new words is the rule applied).

Answer in a word or a short line.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Name the three sounds the letters -ed can make.
**Answer: /t/, /d/ and /ɪd/** — Bolivia's three lists, Year 3, Topic 4, student p.219.

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** State the choosing rule in one line.
**Answer: after t or d, say /ɪd/; after a voiceless sound, say /t/; after a voiced sound or a vowel, say /d/** — the sound just before the -ed decides.

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** How does the buzz test work?
**Answer: put two fingers on your throat as you say the verb's last sound — if it buzzes (voiced) the -ed is /d/; if it does not buzz (voiceless) the -ed is /t/** — and t/d endings take /ɪd/ before the test is needed.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** Sort these into the three families: stopped, opened, planted, looked.
**Answer: /t/ — stopped, looked (p and k do not buzz); /d/ — opened (n buzzes); /ɪd/ — planted (ends in t)** — Bolivia's rule applied to new words.
