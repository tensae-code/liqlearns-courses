# Lesson plan — The three sounds of -ed

**Course:** English · **Title:** Phonics · **Lesson:** The three sounds of -ed
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 3, "Sounds Together"
— inside the level with Blending sounds and Silent
letters (CURRICULUM.md; CATCH-UP-PLAN.md places the
pronunciation extensions inside Level 3).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the letters -ed
are said three different ways — /t/, /d/ and /ɪd/ — and
the sound before them chooses which: after t or d,
/ɪd/ (a whole new syllable); after a quiet (voiceless)
sound, /t/; after a voiced sound or a vowel, /d/.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Past actions (-ed is its coat — this
  lesson is how the coat SOUNDS) and Silent letters
  (another spelling that is not said the way it looks).
  Recommended, never forced.
- The -ed SPELLING rules (double the consonant, y → ied)
  are Past actions' work. Here only the three sounds.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Sort any regular past verb into its sound family from
   Bolivia's own lists (Year 3, Topic 4, student p.219):
   /t/ — helped, looked, washed, watched, kissed,
   danced, fixed · /d/ — damaged, called, cleaned,
   offered, loved, amazed, enjoyed, followed, used ·
   /ɪd/ — wanted, visited, needed, started.
2. State the choosing rule: the sound just before -ed
   decides — t/d take /ɪd/, voiceless takes /t/, voiced
   and vowels take /d/.
3. Feel the rule in the throat: voiced sounds buzz,
   voiceless sounds do not.
4. Meet India's corner case: the describing-word -ed is
   said in full — a learn-ed pundit, said "learn-id",
   rhyming with created and waited (Marigold Class 4).

## What you need
Nothing but a voice — and two fingers on your throat for
the buzz test.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner reads "helped, played,
   wanted" aloud as spelled — one -ed, three sounds —
   and sorts them by ear before any rule is given
   (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD AUDIO at the top of the lesson: the three words
     helped /t/, played /d/, wanted /ɪd/ said slowly,
     each family's sound stretched — the student says
     each word after it and points at its family number.
2. **The explanation** — Bolivia's three lists; the
   choosing rule; the buzz test; Bolivia's second
   presentation of the same three sounds in its Year 5
   review; India's full-said -ed in describing words
   (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE under the lists: a picture of three
     baskets marked /t/, /d/, /ɪd/ with Bolivia's verbs
     as cards falling into them — the student calls out
     each card's basket before it lands.
   - ADD GIF: a picture that moves — a hand on a throat,
     the throat glowing on the /d/ words and staying
     dark on the /t/ words, 1–2 seconds — the student
     does the buzz test along with it.
3. **Say it to your companion** — deal the verb cards;
   the companion says the word, the learner names its
   sound and why (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed —
     the three families in the super-simple voice (made
     later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — sort ten past verbs from any page
   you read today into the three families, then read
   each family aloud (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Says "help-ed" with a full extra syllable everywhere →
  the extra syllable belongs ONLY after t and d (wanted,
  needed). Every other -ed is a single sound riding on
  the word: help-t, play-d.
- Cannot hear /t/ against /d/ → the buzz test: fingers
  on the throat. looked — no buzz before the ending, so
  /t/. loved — buzz, so /d/.
- Barbados's -ed ride-along: Barbados's syllabi stock
  -ed/-ied among the suffixes to read and spell (Classes
  1–2 bodies) — syllabus evidence for practice lists,
  not for the three sounds themselves, which are
  Bolivia's (and, for the describing word, India's).
- This lesson's neighbours: Silent letters (before);
  Stress and intonation (next door).
