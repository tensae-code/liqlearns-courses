# The three sounds of -ed — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Phonics · Lesson: The three sounds of -ed
Built from: Bolivia, Textos de Aprendizaje — Lengua
Extranjera, Year 3 (Grade 9), Topic 4 (student p.219):
the -ed pronunciation lists /t/ /d/ /ɪd/, with the same
three sounds presented again in the Year 5 review —
India, Marigold Book Four (-ed pronunciation: the full
said -ed of describing words) — Barbados, Primary
Language Arts Syllabi (the -ed/-ied suffix stocked for
reading and spelling) — syllabus evidence.

This lesson is ONE thing only: how the letters -ed
SOUND. The -ed spelling rules and the past tense itself
belong to Past actions, in the Sentence title.

A note on voices: the explanations in this lesson can be
read to you in your own language with the speaker button.
The words being practised — helped, played, wanted and
the rest — are the TARGET: they are never translated,
and the reader says them in the English voice, then
comes back to yours.

If you have done Past actions first, this lesson will be
easier for you — you might try it first. You do not have
to: everything you need is on this page.

## Do it first
Say these three words aloud, slowly: **helped** —
**played** — **wanted**. All three end in the same two
letters, -ed. Now say them again and listen only to the
very end of each word. Do the three endings sound the
same? Say them a third time. Your ear already knows the
secret of this lesson: one spelling, three sounds.

Media slot — a picture of the words HELPED, PLAYED,
WANTED in a row, each -ed in a different colour. What
you do: say each word and hold up one, two or three
fingers for the sound you hear at the end — a small t,
a small d, or a whole new beat.

## Then the explanation

## Sub lesson 1 — The three lists of -ed

**Bolivia's three lists.** Bolivia's Year 3 book
teaches the pronunciation of -ed as three lists, side
by side (Topic 4, student p.219):

- **/t/** — helped, looked, washed, watched, kissed,
  danced, fixed
- **/d/** — damaged, called, cleaned, offered, loved,
  amazed, enjoyed, followed, used
- **/ɪd/** — wanted, visited, needed, started

Read them again and feel the difference. In the /t/ and
/d/ lists, -ed adds NO new syllable: helped is one beat,
cleaned is one beat. Only the /ɪd/ list adds a beat:
want-ed, need-ed — two beats where the verb alone had
one. (Bolivia's list prints one word where the rule
below puts it in another family — breathed, printed
among the /t/ words. Breathed ends in a voiced sound,
so its -ed is said /d/: this lesson teaches the rule
corrected and leaves that word out of its lists, as the
sources record.)

## Sub lesson 2 — The choosing rule for -ed

**The choosing rule.** The sound just BEFORE the -ed
chooses which of the three it will be:

- After **t** or **d**, -ed is said **/ɪd/** — a whole
  new syllable. The mouth cannot stack t on t or d on
  d without a beat between them: wanted, visited,
  needed, started, waited, accepted.
- After a **voiceless** sound — a sound with no throat
  buzz: p, k, f, s, sh, ch, x — -ed is said **/t/**:
  helped (p), looked (k), washed (sh), watched (ch),
  kissed (s), danced (s), fixed (x).
- After a **voiced** sound — a sound with the buzz: b,
  g, v, z, m, n, l, r — and after every **vowel** sound,
  -ed is said **/d/**: loved, cleaned, called, played,
  enjoyed, used, followed.

## Sub lesson 3 — The buzz test

**The buzz test.** Put two fingers on your throat. Say
"sss" — no buzz. Say "zzz" — buzz. That buzz is the
whole test: if the verb's last sound buzzes, the -ed
rides on as /d/; if it does not buzz, the -ed comes out
/t/; and if the verb already ends in t or d, the -ed
takes its own beat, /ɪd/.

## Sub lesson 4 — The three sounds come back

**Bolivia teaches it twice.** The same three sounds
come back in the book's Year 5 review presentation,
sorted the same way: /d/ — played, listened, exercised;
 /t/ — liked, washed, shopped; /ɪd/ — wanted, needed,
with the reminder that regular verbs add -ed. Two
presentations, one rule: the sound before decides.

## Sub lesson 5 — The describing-word -ed

**India's corner case: the describing word.** India's
Marigold Book Four teaches one place where -ed is
always said in full, whichever sound came before. "We
say: I **learnt** my lesson (action word). But: A
**learn-ed pundit** came (describing word)" — when -ed
is part of a describing word, it is pronounced
"learn-id", rhyming with created and waited. The book
asks learners to tick the words ending in that /ɪd/
sound: accepted, irritated, completed, challenged. The
ordinary past-tense rule still rules ordinary verbs;
the describing word is the named exception to meet, not
a new rule to use everywhere.

## Sub lesson 6 — -ed in spelling practice

**Barbados keeps the spelling in practice.** Barbados's
syllabi stock the suffixes -s/-es/-ies/**-ed**/-ied/-ing
among the word-building work of Classes 1–2 — syllabus
evidence that -ed words are read and spelled from the
first years. The three SOUNDS of that suffix are
Bolivia's teaching (with India's corner case); Barbados
stocks the practice, and this lesson says so plainly.

Media slot — a picture of three baskets marked /t/, /d/
and /ɪd/, with verb cards — washed, loved, needed,
fixed, played, started — falling toward them. What you
do: call out each card's basket before it lands, and
say the sound before the -ed that sent it there.

## Say it to your companion
Take Bolivia's three lists. Your companion points at
any word; you say the word aloud, name its sound — /t/,
/d/ or /ɪd/ — and give the reason ("washed — /t/,
because sh does not buzz"). Ten words each, then swap.

## Do It Yourself
1. Sort these into the three families and read each
   family aloud: stopped, opened, waited, looked,
   enjoyed, planted, washed, called, needed, fixed.
2. Buzz-test five past verbs from a page you read
   today: fingers on throat, family named, word said.
3. Read India's pair aloud the two ways: "I learnt my
   lesson." — "A learn-ed pundit came." Feel where the
   -ed takes its full beat, and why.
