# Alternative Spellings — Soft c and g, -dge, -tch

## Do it first

Read these words: **face, dance, large, bridge, match**. The c in face does not say k. The g in large does not say g. The end of bridge is not written j, and the end of match is not written ch. English has special spellings for these sounds — this lesson teaches them, one family at a time, from the word lists of Bhutan's Class III spelling workbook.

## Sub lesson 1 — Soft c — c as s in -ce, final -se, and s as z in -se

*Difficulty: first step — the letter c with its soft sound, and the -se endings.*

When c stands before e, it says **s**. The workbook's -ce week: **face, place, space, dance, peace, since, race, fence, chance, rice**.

The ending **-se** also carries the s sound: **horse, mouse, goose, house, loose, nurse, rinse, geese, grease, curse**.

But listen carefully — in some -se words the s buzzes like **z**: **surprise, please, cheese, praise, noise, tease, browse, ease, poise, raise**. Same spelling, two sounds. Your ear tells you which: the z words buzz in your throat.

Try it now: read face, dance, horse, please aloud. Say which words buzz (z) and which hiss (s).

## Sub lesson 2 — Soft g and the -dge / -tch endings

*Difficulty: builds on Sub lesson 1 — the same softening idea for g, plus the two endings that follow short vowels.*

When g stands before e, it says **j**: **large, change, page, stage, huge, cage, hinge, range, tinge, plunge**.

After a short vowel, English writes the j sound **-dge**: **bridge, edge, pledge, hedge, wedge, judge, trudge, dodge, lodge, nudge**. And it writes the ch sound **-tch**: **match, catch, snatch, sketch, stitch, switch, fetch, stretch, itch, hatch**.

The rule that ties them together: a short vowel needs the extra letter (d or t) to guard it — so ju in judge would turn long without the d, and mach would not say match without the t.

Try it now: read bridge, judge, match, catch aloud. Then cover and write them. Check: -dge or -tch, never a bare j or ch after the short vowel.
