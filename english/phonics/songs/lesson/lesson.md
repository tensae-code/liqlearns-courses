# Songs

## Do it first

Think of a song you know by heart. Now answer two questions
about it:

- What is the song about?
- What does the song want you to feel, or to do?

If you can answer, you already know the secret of this
lesson: a song is not only a tune. It carries words, and the
words carry a message.

## Sub lesson 1 — Listening to songs

**Friendly opener.** Everybody hears songs. This lesson
asks for more: listen to a song attentively, and sing it
with understanding.

**The shape of it.** Ghana's curriculum gives songs their
own place in oral language. Listening to a song well means:
- Hear the words, not only the tune. The tune carries the
  words; the words carry the meaning.
- Follow the song's build: many songs tell a small story or
  return to one main line (the chorus) again and again.
- Sing with understanding: know what the lines you sing are
  saying. A song sung by noise alone is only half sung.
- Listen more than once. The second hearing catches what
  the first one missed.

**Work it.**
- The chorus is the line that returns: it usually holds the
  song's heart. Listen for what repeats.
- If a song tells of a journey, list its stops in order
  after one hearing; check them on the second.

**Watch out.** A catchy tune can smuggle careless words past
you. If you would not say the line in plain speech, do not
let the tune say it for you — understanding comes first.

*Difficulty: first steps.*

## The companion

Sing your companion a song you know. Then explain, in two
plain sentences, what it is about. If you cannot explain
it, listen again together until you can.

## Sub lesson 2 — What songs carry

**Friendly opener.** Songs teach. Ghana's standards say it
in so many words: at Basic 4, identify and discuss the
values in songs; at Basic 5, explain the central messages
in songs.

**The shape of it.**
- A song's **values** are the good things it praises or the
  bad things it warns against: hard work, kindness, honesty,
  patience, love of home and country.
- A song's **central message** is the one big thing it
  says — the line you would write if you had to shrink the
  whole song to a sentence.
- To find them, ask: What does this song praise? What does
  it warn against? If everyone did what this song says,
  what would change?

**Work it.**
- A song about a farmer who rises early and works steadily
  praises hard work and patience; its central message might
  be: steady work feeds the family.
- A song warning that a liar ends alone carries the value
  of honesty, taught by its opposite.

**Watch out.** Not every line carries the message. A song
may mention rain, roads and rivers on its way; the message
is what those things are gathered to say. Judge by the
whole song, not by one bright line.

*Difficulty: the full move.*

## Do It Yourself

Take one song you know well. Write its central message in
one sentence, and name two values it carries. You have read
a song the way this lesson teaches.
