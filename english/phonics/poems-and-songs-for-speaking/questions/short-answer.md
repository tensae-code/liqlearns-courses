# Short Answer — Poems and Songs for Speaking

(Every item is marked with the sub lesson it belongs to: (Sub lesson N). The gate pool for a sub lesson is all of that sub lesson's questions — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the whole set is the pool.)

1. Why does every topic in Gabon's Year 1 curriculum carry a poem and a song with it? (Sub lesson 1)
   *Answer: So the topic's words and structures are spoken again in verse — verse is a taught component of every topic, the speaking practice that rides on the topic just taught.*

2. What do you listen for, the first time you hear a topic's poem? (Sub lesson 1)
   *Answer: The words you already know from the topic, and where the beat falls.*

3. Name the four things that make a recitation good. (Sub lesson 2)
   *Answer: Correct pronunciation, intonation (the voice rising and falling with the meaning), rhythm kept, and the actions/gestures done with the words.*

4. A learner recites only the two lines she likes and sits down. What is missing? (Sub lesson 2)
   *Answer: The rest of the poem — reciting means saying the poem whole, in order, out loud.*

5. How do the hands help the singing? (Sub lesson 3)
   *Answer: Clapping (or stamping) to the rhythm keeps the beat, so the words stay in line and everyone sings together.*

6. When is a song in this lesson finished? (Sub lesson 3)
   *Answer: When it has been sung whole, to the end, with every word clear inside the tune.*
