# True or False — Poems and Songs for Speaking

(Every item is marked with the sub lesson it belongs to: (Sub lesson N). The gate pool for a sub lesson is all of that sub lesson's questions — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the whole set is the pool.)

1. In the curriculum this lesson comes from, only some topics have a poem and a song. (Sub lesson 1)
   *False — a named poem and a named song accompany every topic, in every stage.*

2. You should listen to the poem and the song before you perform them. (Sub lesson 1)
   *True.*

3. Joining in on the repeated parts is part of listening work. (Sub lesson 1)
   *True.*

4. Reciting a poem quietly, in your head, counts as a recitation. (Sub lesson 2)
   *False — reciting is saying the poem whole, out loud.*

5. In a recitation, the actions are done after the poem is finished. (Sub lesson 2)
   *False — the actions are done with the words, as you say them.*

6. A flat voice, with no rising and falling, is good intonation. (Sub lesson 2)
   *False — intonation is the voice rising and falling with the meaning.*

7. A song can stop at the hard line and still count as sung whole. (Sub lesson 3)
   *False — the song is sung from the first verse to the end.*

8. Clapping or stamping to the rhythm helps keep the song together. (Sub lesson 3)
   *True.*
