# Lesson Plan — Poems and Songs for Speaking

**One thing:** verse as speaking practice — after a topic is learned, the class hears the short poem and the song that ride on that topic, says the poem whole out loud, and sings the song whole, with clear pronunciation, good intonation, the rhythm kept, and the actions done with the words.

Unit 3 — Sounds Together. Planned shape: three sub lessons — first listening to the topic's poem and song and joining in, then reciting the poem whole with its rhythm and actions, and last singing the song whole, clapping or stamping to its rhythm. The lesson is built from Gabon's Year 1 English curriculum, whose five stages (paliers) name Verse as a taught component of every topic: a named poem and a named song accompany every topic, and the pupil listens to them, recites the poem and sings the song with correct pronunciation, intonation, rhythm and actions/gestures. The course's rhymes lesson teaches the little poems themselves; the songs lesson teaches songs and the messages they carry; this lesson teaches the performing skill itself — verse done out loud, whole, as the speaking practice of the topic just learned.

## Sub lessons

1. **Listening to the poem and the song** — hear the poem and the song on the topic just taught, attentively, and join in.
2. **Reciting the poem** — say the poem whole, with correct pronunciation, intonation, rhythm and actions/gestures.
3. **Singing the song** — sing the song whole, keeping the rhythm with clapping or stamping, the words clear.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Teaching points

- Verse rides on a topic: in Gabon's curriculum a named poem and a named song accompany every topic in every stage — the verse is the topic said again in rhythm (Curriculum d'Anglais, 1re Année primaire, printed pp.25–26 and passim).
- Listen first: the pupil listens to the poem and the song before performing them.
- Reciting means whole and out loud: the poem said from start to finish, with correct pronunciation, intonation and rhythm, and the actions/gestures done with the words.
- Singing means whole and in rhythm: the song sung from start to finish, clapping or stamping to the rhythm.
- The aim of the performance is the speaking itself: clear sounds, a voice that rises and falls with the meaning, and the beat kept to the end.
