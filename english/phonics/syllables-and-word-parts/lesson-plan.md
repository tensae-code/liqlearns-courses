# Lesson plan — Syllables and Word Parts

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** Syllables and Word Parts
**Lesson:** syllables-and-word-parts — the beats inside a word — hear them, clap them, count them
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Sounds Together
**The one thing:** every word is made of beats called syllables, and your hands can count them
**This lesson's own level:** Phonics lesson: one beat words first, then two and three beats, then the long words.

## Why this lesson exists

No lesson taught the syllable itself. Sierra Leone's very first plans teach it with the hands: Primary 1 Term 1, Lesson 3 is "Clapping syllables" — clap the beats as you chant the word. The 2026-11-08 re-verification sweep marked it NEW LESSON NEEDED. Under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06) it gets its own lesson; its single source is the plan named in lesson/sources.md, teacher evidence cited as such.

## Sub lessons

1. **One beat, two beats** — short words fall into one beat or two — cat, mango — and the hands clap each beat
2. **Longer words, more beats** — three-beat and longer words — banana, Freetown, elephant — clapped and counted the same way

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is forced first — this lesson stands alone: it needs only words the learner can already say. It stands under blending-sounds, which joins the sounds inside each beat.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- Say what a syllable is: one beat of a word.
- Clap the syllables of a word as you say it, one clap per beat.
- Count the syllables in short words and in long words.
- Clap the syllables of your own name and your friends' names.

## What you need
Words — any words the learner knows. Hands to clap with.

## The run of the lesson
1. **Do it first** — the learner says their own name slowly and claps every time their voice jumps, before the word "syllable" is taught (lesson/lesson.md).
2. Work through sub lesson 1: one-beat and two-beat words.
3. Work through sub lesson 2: longer words.
4. ADD AUDIO: a voice chants words slowly with a clap on every beat — the student claps along, then leads.

## Add-ons
- Name game: the class claps the syllables of every learner's name; the longest name wins.
- Word hunt: find a four-beat word in today's reading and teach its claps to a partner.

## If the learner is stuck
If a learner claps the letters instead of the beats, slow the word down and sing it — beats are heard in the voice's jumps, not in spelling. If two beats blur into one, put a hand under the chin: the chin drops once per beat.

## Teacher note
The lesson stands on one Sierra Leone plan (Primary 1 Term 1, Lesson 3), stated plainly in lesson/sources.md: the plan names the method — clap the syllables as you chant — and the lesson works that method from short words to long ones, the plan's own direction.
