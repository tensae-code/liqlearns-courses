# Syllables and Word Parts

## Do it first

Say your name. Say it slowly. Clap your hands every time your
voice makes a jump.

How many claps? That number is what this short lesson is
about.

## Sub lesson 1 — One beat, two beats

A word is made of beats. One beat is called a **syllable**.

- **cat** — one beat. One clap. *Cat.*
- **dog** — one beat. One clap.
- **man-go** — two beats. Two claps. *Man-go.*
- **pen-cil** — two beats. Two claps.

Say it and clap it together: the voice jumps once per beat,
and the hands count the jumps. A hand under your chin helps:
your chin drops once for every beat.

Clap these: sun (1), river (2), fish (1), mother (2), book (1),
teacher (2).

*Difficulty: first steps — the hands lead; the word "syllable" comes after the clapping already works.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Longer words, more beats

Long words are only short words' beats, more of them:

- **ba-na-na** — three beats.
- **el-e-phant** — three beats.
- **Free-town** — two beats.
- **Sier-ra Le-one** — four beats.

The way never changes: say it slowly, clap the jumps, count
the claps. That count is the word's syllables.

Clap your friends' names. Clap the names of places you know.
Find the longest word you can clap without losing count.

*Difficulty: building — the same clap on longer words; losing count and starting again is part of the work.*

## Say it to your companion

Teach your companion the clap: one clap per beat. Clap three
words for them — a one-beat, a two-beat and a three-beat word
— and let them count. Then change places.

## Do It Yourself

1. Clap and count the syllables in: school, banana, water,
   elephant, Freetown, pencil.
2. Write three words with two syllables and two words with
   three syllables. Clap each one to check.
3. Clap the syllables of everyone in your family's names.
   Whose name has the most beats?
