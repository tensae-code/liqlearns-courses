# Stress and intonation — sub lessons and depth

This file is the lesson's depth record, re-expressed as its SUB
LESSONS (2026-11-08 restructure — README, "Units, Lessons and Sub
Lessons"). Practice is tied to the skill number, as before. The
questions live in the type files; this file is the by-sub-lesson
view. The sub lessons are the depths inside this one thing — the
music over the sounds — the word's one loud beat first (India;
Sierra Leone), then the sentence's lean and the schwa (Sierra
Leone; Ecuador; Vietnam), then the tunes that end a sentence
(Vietnam's Grade 11–12 system).

Mapping from the old depth layers: old Level 1 = Sub lesson 1;
old Level 2 = Sub lessons 2–3 (its pass mark splits at the same
jump: the sentence's lean, then the schwa the small words
shrink to); old Level 3 = Sub lesson 4 — its last clause, the
emphatic push that corrects a listener, is taught inside Sub
lesson 2's sentence stress and is noted there in its pass mark.

## Sub lesson 1 — Word stress
Sub lesson 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: count
a word's syllables (one per vowel sound), find the ONE
stressed syllable, and mark it with the ´ mark the way
India's book does — ´manner, and the stressed syllables
of camel, balloon, decent, opinion found without a miss.

## Sub lesson 2 — Sentence stress
Sub lesson 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark (its part
of old Level 2's): say a sentence leaning on its content words
(nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs, negative auxiliaries)
while the structure words shrink — and push one word with
emphatic stress to correct a listener, Sierra Leone's contrast
use (the clause old Level 3's pass mark also names).

## Sub lesson 3 — The schwa
Sub lesson 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark (its part
of old Level 2's): say the schwa where it lives — the
unstressed syllables of banana and sofa, and the small words
the, to, of (Ecuador; Sierra Leone, SSS3 L20).

## Sub lesson 4 — The tune at the end
Sub lesson 4 questions from the type files. Pass mark: end
each sentence kind with its tune from Vietnam's system —
falling (statements, commands, Wh- questions, lists),
rising (yes/no questions, unfinished speech), fall-rise
(invitations, suggestions, polite requests), the
choice-question up-down, and the tag-question choice.
