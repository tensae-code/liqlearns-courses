# Stress and intonation — questions by level

Practice by level, tied to the skill number. The questions live in
the type files; this file is the by-level view. The levels are the
depths inside this one thing — the music over the sounds — set by
the pattern across the countries' books: the word's one loud beat
first (India; Sierra Leone), then the sentence's lean and the
schwa (Sierra Leone; Ecuador; Vietnam), then the tunes that end a
sentence (Vietnam's Grade 11–12 system).

## Level 1 — word stress
Level 1 questions from the type files. Pass mark: count
a word's syllables (one per vowel sound), find the ONE
stressed syllable, and mark it with the ´ mark the way
India's book does — ´manner, and the stressed syllables
of camel, balloon, decent, opinion found without a miss.

## Level 2 — sentence stress and the schwa
Level 2 questions from the type files. Pass mark: say a
sentence leaning on its content words (nouns, verbs,
adjectives, adverbs, negative auxiliaries) while the
structure words shrink — and say the schwa where it
lives: the unstressed syllables of banana and sofa, and
the small words the, to, of (Ecuador; Sierra Leone,
SSS3 L20).

## Level 3 — the tunes
Level 3 questions from the type files. Pass mark: end
each sentence kind with its tune from Vietnam's system —
falling (statements, commands, Wh- questions, lists),
rising (yes/no questions, unfinished speech), fall-rise
(invitations, suggestions, polite requests), the
choice-question up-down, and the tag-question choice —
and push one word with emphatic stress to correct a
listener, Sierra Leone's contrast use.
