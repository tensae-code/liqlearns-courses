# Hands-on — Stress and intonation

You do it, we guide you. Everything here is done out
loud, with a companion or the Explain Companion.

1. **Mark and tap (India's drill).** Write these words:
   camel, balloon, decent, opinion, manner. Count each
   word's syllables on your fingers — one per vowel
   sound — then say the word slowly, find the beat that
   stands out, and write the ´ mark over it, the way
   India's Class 8 book marks **´manner**. Tap the table
   once, loud, on the marked beat as you say each word.
2. **The schwa hunt (Ecuador's cards).** Say Ecuador's
   practice cards slowly, then at talking speed: SOFA,
   ABOUT, AGO, PROBLEM, FOCUS, FAMILY, ELEVEN, CUSTOMER,
   GRAMMAR (Superior Worksheet 6, printed pp.40–41).
   Circle the syllable that shrinks to "uh" in each.
   Finish with the book's own star word: banana —
   "buh-NAH-nuh", two schwas in one word.
3. **Lean the sentence (Sierra Leone's split).** Take
   the sentence "The cat is sleeping under the table."
   Say it leaning only on the content words — CAT,
   SLEEPING, TABLE — and let the, is, under shrink.
   Then push ONE word harder to correct your companion:
   they say "The DOG is sleeping…", you answer "The
   CAT is sleeping…" (emphatic stress for contrast,
   SSS2 L95's own example shape: "It is not my sister
   that I am angry with; it is my aunt").
4. **The tune ladder (Vietnam's system).** Say these
   four with their tunes, drawing each tune in the air
   with your hand: "Open the window." (falling —
   command) · "Are you hungry?" (rising — yes/no) ·
   "What time is it?" (falling — Wh-) · "Would you like
   some tea?" (fall-rise — invitation). Then one tag,
   both ways: "It's cold, isn't it?" falling — I expect
   you agree; rising — I am really asking.
