# Lesson plan — Stress and intonation

**Course:** English · **Title:** Phonics · **Lesson:** Stress and intonation
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Sounds Together
— inside the unit with Blending sounds, Silent letters
and The three sounds of -ed (CURRICULUM.md;
CATCH-UP-PLAN.md places the pronunciation extensions
inside Unit 3).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the music over
the sounds — every long word has ONE syllable that
stands out (word stress); every sentence leans on its
content words while its small words shrink to the schwa
(sentence stress); and the voice rises or falls at the
end of a sentence in patterns a listener can read
(intonation).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Word stress** — one syllable in every long word stands out; India's way of marking it — the ´ mark: ´manner, camel, balloon — and counting syllables by vowel sounds. (Was depth Level 1 — word stress.)
2. **Sentence stress** — the sentence leans on its content words (nouns, verbs, adjectives, adverbs) while its structure words shrink; emphatic stress pushes one word harder to correct or contrast. (Was part of depth Level 2 — sentence stress and the schwa.)
3. **The schwa** — the quick, relaxed "uh" (ə) that lives in unstressed syllables and small words — banana, sofa, the, to, of — and its weak forms and elision. (Was part of depth Level 2 — sentence stress and the schwa.)
4. **The tune at the end** — intonation in Vietnam's system: falling for statements, commands, lists and Wh- questions; rising for yes/no questions; fall-rise for invitations, suggestions and polite requests; choice questions up then down; the tag-question choice. (Was depth Level 3 — the tunes.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Blending sounds and Two-letter
  sounds (the sounds this music rides on). Recommended,
  never forced.
- Reading punctuation as an end-mark is Capital and full
  stop's work; here punctuation is met again as a
  reading-aloud signal (Sierra Leone's lessons), a
  different thing.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Find the one stressed syllable in a word and mark it,
   India's way: the ´ mark over the syllable that stands
   out — **´manner**, ca**mel**, bal**loon** (Honeydew,
   Class 8, Unit 7).
2. Lean a sentence on its content words (nouns, verbs,
   adjectives, adverbs) and let the structure words
   shrink — Sierra Leone's emphatic/sentence stress
   lessons (SSS2 L50; SSS3 L20) — including pushing one
   word harder to correct or contrast.
3. Say the schwa: the quick, relaxed "uh" (ə) that lives
   in unstressed syllables and small words — Ecuador's
   Superior Worksheet 6 (printed pp.40–41): banana,
   "buh-NAH-nuh"; the, to, of.
4. End sentences with the right tune, from Vietnam's
   Grade 11–12 system: falling for statements, commands,
   lists and Wh- questions; rising for yes/no questions;
   fall-rise for invitations, suggestions and polite
   requests; the tag-question choice (falling = I expect
   you to agree; rising = I am really asking).

## What you need
Nothing but a voice. A hand to tap the beats on the
table.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner says "banana" flat,
   then hunts for the beat that stands out (BA-NA-NA,
   ba-NA-na, ba-na-NA) and hears which one English
   keeps (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD AUDIO at the top of the lesson: "banana" said
     three ways, the right way last and slowest — the
     student taps the table on the loud beat each time.
2. **The explanation** — word stress (India; Sierra
   Leone's word-stress lessons; Jordan's syllable
   stress and noun/verb stress); sentence stress and
   the content/structure split (Sierra Leone; Vietnam);
   the schwa (Ecuador; Vietnam's weak forms and
   elision; Jordan's weak form of of); the tunes
   (Vietnam's Grade 11–12 intonation system; Sierra
   Leone's rising/falling lessons; Jordan's intonation
   patterns) (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE under word stress: a picture of the words
     camel, balloon, opinion written big with the ´
     mark glowing over the stressed syllable — the
     student says each word and taps its glowing beat.
   - ADD GIF: a picture that moves — a voice line
     drawing itself over a sentence, rising at the end
     of a yes/no question and falling at the end of a
     statement, 1–2 seconds — the student traces the
     line in the air with a finger as they say it.
3. **Say it to your companion** — say one sentence three
   ways, pushing a different word each time; the
   companion says what changed (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed —
     beats and tunes in the super-simple voice (made
     later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — mark the stress in five words,
   shrink the small words in three sentences, and end
   four sentences with their right tunes
   (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Stresses every syllable equally → tap it out: one
  loud tap per word, the rest soft. A word has as many
  syllables as it has vowel sounds, and only ONE stands
  out (India's teaching).
- Says every word in a sentence at full weight → the
  shrinking IS the skill: the small words (the, to, of,
  a) collapse to the schwa. Ecuador's cards first (sofa,
  about, ago), then whole sentences.
- Mixes the tunes → punctuation is the first map (Sierra
  Leone's JSS lessons): a full stop falls, a yes/no
  question mark rises. Then Vietnam's finer system.
- This lesson's neighbours: The three sounds of -ed
  (before); Word families (next door in Level 4).
