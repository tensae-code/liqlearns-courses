# Stress and intonation — short answer

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Word stress · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 3 — The schwa · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 4 — The tune at the end, and Sub lesson 2 — Sentence stress (item 4's emphatic contrast is taught inside sentence stress).

Answer in a word or a short line.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** How many stressed syllables does one word have, and how do you count its syllables (India)?
**Answer: one stressed syllable per word; a word has as many syllables as it has vowel sounds** — Honeydew, Class 8, Unit 7; Sierra Leone's SSS2 L48 states the same one-stress rule.

**2. (Sub lesson 3)** In "the, to, of" said quickly inside a sentence, what sound do their vowels shrink to, and what is the sound called?
**Answer: the schwa — ə, said like a quick "uh"** — Ecuador names these very function words as schwa homes (Superior Worksheet 6, p.40); Jordan practises the weak form of of by name.

**3. (Sub lesson 4)** Name the tune for each: a finished statement; a yes/no question; a Wh- question.
**Answer: statement — falling; yes/no question — rising; Wh- question — falling** — Sierra Leone, SSS2 L71–72 and the JSS punctuation lessons; Vietnam, Grade 11, Units 6–7.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** Your listener thinks you are angry with your sister, but it is your aunt. Say the correction with emphatic stress and name the job the pushed word does.
**Answer: "It is not my SISTER that I am angry with; it is my AUNT" — the pushed words do the contrast job** — Sierra Leone, SSS2 L95's own example; L50 names the jobs: terminology, specific questions, contrast.
