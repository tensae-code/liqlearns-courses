# Stress and intonation — true or false

Say true or false. If it is false, say why.

**1. (Level 1)** Every syllable in a long word is stressed equally.
**Answer: False** — one syllable stands out as the more prominent one; the rest are unstressed (India, Honeydew, Class 8, Unit 7; Sierra Leone, SSS2 L48).

**2. (Level 2)** The schwa is a loud sound that appears in stressed syllables.
**Answer: False** — the schwa is the quick, quiet "uh" of UNstressed syllables and small function words (Ecuador, Superior Worksheet 6, p.40).

**3. (Level 3)** Yes/no questions fall at the end, and finished statements rise.
**Answer: False** — it is the other way: yes/no questions and unfinished speech rise; finished statements fall, the fall landing on the final stressed syllable (Sierra Leone, SSS2 L71–72, SSS3 L51).

**4. (Level 3)** Vietnam's Grade 11 book gives invitations and polite requests their own tune, the fall-rise.
**Answer: True** — Grade 11, Unit 8: intonation in invitations, suggestions, and polite requests (fall-rise).
