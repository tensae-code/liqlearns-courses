# Blends — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Phonics · Lesson: Blends
Built from: Egypt, English Primary 1 Term 2.
This lesson is ONE thing only: two letters at the start of a word,
where you can hear BOTH sounds, sliding together. It is not the
two-letter-sounds lesson — there, two letters say ONE sound. Here
they say two sounds, fast, holding hands without letting go.

## Do it first
Say s, then k. Now say them with no gap: sk — the start of "skip".
Again, slower: s-k, s-k, sk. Your mouth kept both sounds. That
keeping-both is the whole lesson.

## Then the explanation
So a word can open with two sounds pushed together. Egypt's book
teaches them in two sets, printed pink inside the whole words:

**The s-set** (Term 2, Unit 2):
- **sk** — skip, sky
- **sn** — snail, snake
- **st** — strong, star
- **sw** — swing, swim

**The l-set** (Term 2, Unit 5):
- **cl** — clock
- **bl** — black
- **fl** — flag (the book shows the flag of Egypt)
- **sl** — slide

The book blends each one and reads it aloud, then drops it inside
sentences — because a blend is not a trick for its own page; it is
how real words start.

Here is the test against the next-door lesson: in a blend, cover
the second letter and the first sound is still there in the word —
s is really in "skip". In a two-letter sound like ch, covering h
leaves a c that is not the word's sound at all.

## Say it to your companion
Say the eight blends, each with one book word. Then explain the
cover test: how do you know "sk" is a blend and "ch" is not?

## Do It Yourself
1. Say each blend word slowly, first sound by first sound, then
   smooth: s-k-ip, skip.
2. Write the s-set words in one line and the l-set words in
   another.
3. Find the blend at the start of "snake" and "slide" and say what
   the two sets share. (They are cousins: the second letter is
   where they differ.)
