# Media Studies: Magazines and Brochures — lesson plan

**Unit:** Unit 15, Reading and the Paragraph

Slots are written as described picture/video slots: the words say what the picture shows, then what the student does with it.

- **Sub lesson 1:** A picture of two magazine covers side by side: one showing a famous singer's face large, the other a harbour full of boats. *The learners say which magazine is aimed at everyone and which at one target audience, and what tells them.*
- **Sub lesson 1:** A picture of an old fable — a tortoise and a hare at a starting line — redrawn with the animals in modern clothes outside a city office. *The learners say what has been kept from the fable and what has been changed.*
- **Sub lesson 2:** A picture of a living room where a television glows while a small radio sits on a shelf, switched off. *The learners say one thing the television can do that the radio cannot, and one thing the radio can do that the television cannot.*
- **Sub lesson 2:** A picture of a farmer in a field with a small radio to his ear, far from any house or power line. *The learners say why the radio reaches this listener when television cannot.*

## Sub lessons
1. Magazines and Their Stories
2. Television and Radio

Gate pool for each sub lesson: the whole question set of the sub lesson before it — every question file of this lesson carries (Sub lesson N) markers, and the gate draws from the previous sub lesson's items, at most 5 questions, mixed with earlier parts.
