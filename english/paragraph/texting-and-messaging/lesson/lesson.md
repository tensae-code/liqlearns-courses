# Texting and Messaging

## Sub lesson 1 — The short forms — chatspeak

In fast messages, people shorten words. This shortened writing is often called **chatspeak** or text speak.

- Letters for sounds: **u** = you, **r** = are, **c** = see.
- Numbers for sounds: **2** = to/too, **4** = for, **8** = -ate (as in *l8r* = later).
- Shortened words: **pls** = please, **thx** = thanks, **tmr** = tomorrow, **msg** = message.
- First letters of a phrase: **lol** (laughing out loud), **brb** (be right back), **idk** (I don't know).

You must be able to READ these — messages to you will use them — and to write the full forms when the short ones don't fit.

Do it: read this message aloud in full words: "c u tmr, pls bring ur book, thx".

## Sub lesson 2 — Writing a note and a text message that still reads clearly

Short is fine. Unclear is not.

- A **note** (paper, left for someone) and a **text message** do the same job: say WHO it is for, WHAT it is about, and WHAT happens next — in very few lines.
- Keep the short forms for friends and family. For a teacher, an older person, or anything important, write the full words.
- Before sending, read it as the other person: could they misunderstand it? One more word is cheaper than one misunderstanding.

Do it: write the same message two ways — to your friend, and to your teacher: you will be ten minutes late tomorrow.
