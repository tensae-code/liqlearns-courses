# Short Answer — Cohesion and Coherence

(Every item is marked with the sub lesson it belongs to: (Sub lesson N). The gate pool for a sub lesson is all of that sub lesson's questions — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the whole set is the pool.)

1. Name three kinds of cohesive tie. (Sub lesson 1)
   — Pronouns pointing back to their nouns; words repeated or echoed; connectors signposting the road (first, however, because, in the end).

2. Can a text be cohesive but not coherent? Give the proof in one line. (Sub lesson 2)
   — Yes — the connectors can all be correctly used while the ideas wander off the point; surface links do not guarantee underlying sense.

3. What earns coherence? (Sub lesson 2)
   — Thinking: one governing idea, the parts in an order that builds, and nothing contradicting or wandering off the point.

4. Which of the pair can a teacher count in a text, and which must they judge? (Sub lesson 2)
   — Cohesion can be counted (the devices are on the surface); coherence must be judged (the logic is under the surface).
