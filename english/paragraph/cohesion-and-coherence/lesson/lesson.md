# Cohesion and Coherence

## Do it first

A text can have every linking word in place and still wander; it can have no linking words at all and still make perfect sense. Those are two different virtues, and the Philippines' Grade 10 book teaches them as a pair: **cohesion** versus **coherence** — what each means, and how linking devices differ from logical sense. This lesson teaches the pair.

## Sub lesson 1 — Cohesion: the links you can see

*Difficulty: first step — the ground of the lesson.*

**Cohesion** lives on the surface of the text: it is the set of visible ties from sentence to sentence. Pronouns point back (*the children… they*); words repeat or echo (*the leaf — that same leaf*); connectors signpost the road (*first, then, however, because, in the end*). These are the **linking devices**, and they can be listed, counted and taught one by one. Their job is to save the reader from jumping: each sentence arrives holding the hand of the one before.

Try it now: Take two sentences — "Johnsy counted the leaves. She was sure the last one mattered." — and name every cohesive tie between them. Then write a third sentence that holds both hands.

## Sub lesson 2 — Coherence: the sense that holds

*Difficulty: builds on Sub lesson 1.*

**Coherence** lives under the surface: it is the logical sense of the whole — ideas in an order that builds, each part answering to the text's one purpose, nothing contradicting or wandering. A text can be cohesive but incoherent: "Johnsy counted the leaves. *However*, soup is hot in winter. *Therefore*, my uncle drives a bus." Every connector is correctly used; nothing holds. And a text can be coherent with few connectors, when the order of ideas itself carries the sense. The book's point is the difference: linking devices are tools of cohesion; coherence is earned by thinking — by one governing idea, ideas in building order, and nothing off the point.

Try it now: Write two three-sentence texts: one cohesive but incoherent (correct connectors, wandering ideas), one coherent with no connectors at all. Read both to a partner — which failure do they feel first?

## Do it yourself

Take a page of your own writing. First pass (cohesion): circle every linking device and add any missing hand-holds. Second pass (coherence): write the governing idea in the margin; cross out or move anything that does not answer to it. Note which pass changed the page more.
