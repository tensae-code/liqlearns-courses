# Reading Signs and Notices

## Do it first

You are standing in front of a gate. On the gate is a board
with two words:

> **No Parking**

You cannot read the second word yet. But you know the
first word — **No**. A sign that begins with No is telling
you something is **not allowed** here. The second word
names the thing. By the end of this lesson you will read
the whole sign — and every sign like it.

Signs are the shortest texts in the world. They stand in
parks, on streets, in libraries and classrooms, and they
speak to everyone who passes. Saudi Arabia's Grade 3 book
teaches its learners to read them in its town unit. Here
they are.

## Sub lesson 1 — Signs that say no

Some signs forbid a thing. They begin with **No**, **Do
Not**, or **Keep Off** — and those first words are the
whole message's key: *this is not allowed here.*

Read the forbidding signs of the town:

> **No Parking** — cars may not stop and stay here.
>
> **No Eating** — you may not eat in this place.
>
> **No Drinking** — you may not drink in this place.
>
> **No Skateboards** — skateboards are not allowed here.
>
> **Keep Off Grass** — do not walk on the grass; keep off it.
>
> **Do Not Litter** — do not throw litter on the ground.

See how each sign is built: the forbidding words first,
then the thing itself. **No** + the thing. **Do Not** +
the action. **Keep Off** + the place. Once you can read
the first words, no forbidding sign can hide from you —
read the rest of it word by word and it gives up its
meaning.

*Difficulty: first steps — three forbidding openers (No, Do Not, Keep Off) and six signs that use them.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Signs that ask and remind

Not every sign forbids. Some signs **ask** — and the word
that asks is **Please**:

> **Please Switch Off All Cell Phones** — the sign asks
> you, politely, to turn your phone off. You will see it
> where people must be quiet: a cinema, a library, an
> examination room.

And some signs **remind** you of a good thing to do:

> **Think Green** — remember the trees, the grass and the
> clean ground; look after them.

Now the whole skill. A sign you have never seen is still
only words. Read its **first word** first:

- **No / Do Not / Keep Off** → it forbids a thing.
- **Please** → it asks you to do a thing.
- Any other first word → read on, word by word; the sign
  names its thing, and the place you are standing in
  helps you.

That is all a sign ever does: it names a thing, and it
tells you — forbids, asks, or reminds. Read the words,
and the town starts talking to you.

*Difficulty: building — the asking word Please and the reminder sign, then the first-word key turned on any sign at all.*

## Say it to your companion

Explain signs to your companion: the three forbidding
openers, the asking word, and how you read a sign you
have never seen. Then show them one sign — real or
written by you — and let them read it to you, first word
first.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the eight signs of this lesson from memory:
   the six that say no, the one that asks, the one that
   reminds. Check your spelling against the lesson.
2. For each sign, write one line: where does this sign
   stand? (A park gate? A street? A library door?)
3. Make two signs of your own for your classroom — one
   that says no, one that asks with Please — and put
   them where they belong.
