# Lesson plan — Reading Signs and Notices

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** Reading Signs and Notices
**Lesson:** reading-signs-and-notices — the short texts of the town: what a sign tells you not to do, and what a sign asks you to do
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 15 — Reading Whole Texts
**The one thing:** a sign is a short text that speaks to everyone who passes; its first words tell you its kind — No / Do Not / Keep Off forbid a thing, Please asks for a thing, and the rest of the sign names the thing itself
**This lesson's own level:** First real-world reading: eight printed town signs from one Grade 3 book page, read word by word, then the same reading skill turned on any sign.

## Why this lesson exists

No lesson taught reading signs. Saudi Arabia's Grade 3 book (*We Can! 3*, Unit 11 "Hanging Out in Town") teaches its learners to read the town's signs in English — Keep Off Grass, No Skateboards, No Parking, Please Switch Off All Cell Phones, Do Not Litter, No Eating, No Drinking, Think Green (printed p.89). The 2026-11-08 re-verification sweep marked it NEW LESSON NEEDED. Under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06) it gets its own lesson.

## Sub lessons

1. **Signs that say no** — the forbidding signs: No Parking, No Eating, No Drinking, No Skateboards, Keep Off Grass, Do Not Litter. No, Do Not and Keep Off at the front mean: this is not allowed here.
2. **Signs that ask and remind** — Please Switch Off All Cell Phones asks politely; Think Green reminds. Then the whole skill: read a sign you have never seen by its words, first word first.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is forced first — this lesson stands alone: it needs only words the learner can already read. The town lesson (the-town) helps but is not required first.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- Read the eight signs of the source page and say what each one tells people to do or not do.
- Tell a forbidding sign from an asking sign by its first words.
- Say where each kind of sign stands — park, street, library, cinema, classroom.
- Read a new sign slowly by its words and work out what it means.

## What you need
Nothing but eyes — the signs are all around the town. Paper helps for the sign hunt.

## The run of the lesson
1. **Do it first** — the learner meets a sign they cannot yet read ("No Parking") and works out, from the picture of the place and the word No, what it must say (lesson/lesson.md).
2. Work through sub lesson 1: signs that say no.
3. Work through sub lesson 2: signs that ask and remind, and reading any sign.
4. ADD AUDIO: the eight signs read aloud slowly, word by word — the learner hears that a sign is read the same way it is written, with a stop at the end.

## Add-ons
- Sign hunt: walk the school or the street and write down every English sign you pass; sort them into "says no" and "asks or reminds".
- Make a sign: each learner makes one sign for the classroom — a real one, in correct English, of either kind.

## If the learner is stuck
If a learner reads a sign but cannot say what it means, go back to the first word: No and Do Not forbid, Please asks, and the words after them name the thing. The first word is the key that opens every sign.

## Teacher note
The lesson stands on one Saudi Arabia book page (Grade 3, Unit 11, printed p.89), stated plainly in lesson/sources.md. The eight signs are the book's own signs, taught in the book's own town unit; the example sentences around them are the course's own.
