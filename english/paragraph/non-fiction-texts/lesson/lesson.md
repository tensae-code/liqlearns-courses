# Non-Fiction Texts

## Do it first

Stories are invented; poems are made of sound and image. Between them, every one of Bhutan's Reading & Literature anthologies carries a third kind of reading: **non-fiction** — true prose about the real world. An essay that informs, a diary that remembers, a speech that argues. This lesson walks the non-fiction sections of all nine anthologies, Class IV to Class XII, piece by piece. (The pieces are named and described here, never copied out.)

## Sub lesson 1 — Informational essays in the Classes IV–VI anthologies

*Difficulty: first step — information prose on near topics.*

**Class IV** — "Friendship" (a third-person essay on the value of friendship and choosing good friends); "Some Small Mammals in Bhutan" (bats and other small mammals — also used in the book as a model informative essay; its headnote also teaches subject–verb agreement, stocked in that lesson); "Artificial Intelligence" (what AI is, and the AI already in students' phones).

**Class V** — "How Young Animals are Protected" (Audrey Wilson — the clever ways animal parents protect their young); "The Great Mouse Plot" (Roald Dahl, from *Boy* — schoolboys put a dead mouse in the sweet-shop jar; a narrative essay with situational humour, also used for contractions and direct/indirect speech); "We Take Care of Each Other" (a divorced family whose parents and children still support each other; narrative-writing features taught from it); "Let's Talk Advertising" (Susan Hughes — how advertising works, why media depend on it, and the pros and cons consumers must weigh).

**Class VI** — "Homesickness" (Roald Dahl, from *Boy* — the boy fakes appendicitis to escape boarding school; first-person narration); "My Hero" (Katie Gill — a sixteen-year-old cancer patient learns the power of attitude from a laughing baby in the chemotherapy room); "Learn About the Environment" (Elizabeth Hogan — an informational essay on the environment and caring for it); "Artificial Intelligence" (the four kinds of AI: reactive machines, limited memory, theory of mind and self-awareness).

Try it now: name one informational essay from each of the three books, and say in one sentence what it informs the reader about.

## Sub lesson 2 — Personal non-fiction, memoir and speech in the Classes VII–VIII anthologies

*Difficulty: builds on Sub lesson 1 — the real world gets bigger: history, war, disaster, a child's speech to the world.*

**Class VII** — "Starfish" (an anecdote: a boy throws stranded starfish back into the sea one by one — "I made a difference to that one"); "Tell the World: A Young Environmentalist Speaks Out" (Severn Cullis-Suzuki — the twelve-year-old's 1992 Earth Summit speech blaming adults for the world's damage); "The Unfortunate Break" (Jeremiah I. Reyes — a fourteen-year-old's personal narrative of Covid-19 lockdown); "Childhood" (Flora Thompson, from *Lark Rise* — village childhood in early-1900s England); supplementary: "Anger" (Donna Douglas) and "A Writer's Education" (Jean Little).

**Class VIII** — "Anne Frank's Diary" (excerpt — Anne's life in hiding from Nazi persecution in Amsterdam); "Gandhi and the Salt March" (Gerald Gold — Gandhi's non-violent road to Indian independence and the 1930 salt march); "Weathering the Storm in Ersama" (Harsh Mander — Prashant leads his village's survival after the 1999 Orissa super-cyclone); "Prayer Flags Blowing in the Wind" (Gustasp Irani — what Bhutan's prayer flags are and what is really blowing in the wind); supplementary: "Fast Food Nation" (Eric Schlosser) and "Death of a Giant" (Bruce Hutchison).

Try it now: which of these pieces is a speech, which is a diary, and which is a report of a disaster? Say what makes each one non-fiction.

## Sub lesson 3 — Non-fiction in the senior anthologies, Classes IX–XII

*Difficulty: builds on Sub lesson 2 — senior prose, read critically.*

**Class IX** — the essay set opens with "The Dignity of Labour", and the book's non-fiction is studied with the back-of-book **"Menu of Critical Reading Questions"** apparatus (the book's front matter frames the whole senior run with its Key Stage IV Standards and twelve Learning Objectives for Reading & Literature — that frame is stocked as teacher evidence in answering-from-the-text).

**Class X** — the essay set follows the poetry in the book's contents, each piece with its About-the-Author note (printed pp.54–end).

**Class XI** — the essay set opens with "The Skier" (Nancy Dorey) (printed pp.58–end).

**Class XII** — the essay set opens with "English Zindabad versus Angrezi Hatao" (Khushwant Singh) — the case for English in India argued against the drive to remove it (printed pp.58–end).

Try it now: name the opening essay of the Class XI and Class XII sets, and say what question Khushwant Singh's title alone tells you his essay argues about.
