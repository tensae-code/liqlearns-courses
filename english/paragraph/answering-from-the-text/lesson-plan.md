# Lesson plan — Answering from the text

**Course:** English · **Title:** Paragraph · **Lesson:** Answering from the text
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 15 — Reading Whole Texts — the third lesson of the unit (CURRICULUM.md).
Building it completes Unit 15.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** answering questions
about a text FROM the text — find the sentence that carries
the answer, give it whole and in your own words, keep your
outside knowledge out of it, and say "the text doesn't say"
when the text truly doesn't.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Finding the idea
of a text and Words you don't know — this level's reader kit
completed. It is also the course's whole provenance law
worn by the learner: every claim points at its place.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown changing — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Finding the Carrier** — every text-answer rides on a
   sentence inside the text: read the question, name what
   it hunts, land a finger on the carrying sentence.
   (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Answering Whole, in Your Own Words** — the carrier
   gives the fact; the answer gives it a whole sentence's
   ride, facts the text's, wording the reader's. (Was
   depth Level 2.)
3. **The Two Buckets** — the text says / I know: India's
   Understanding the text against Talking about the text,
   never mixed inside one answer. (Was the bucket half of
   depth Level 3.)
4. **The Honest Silence** — when no carrier exists, "The
   text doesn't say" is the complete, correct answer.
   (Was the silence half of depth Level 3.)
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Finding the idea of a text; Words you
  don't know. Recommended, never forced.
- Opinion questions ("What do YOU think?") exist and are
  lawful — the books separate them ("Talking about the
  text" vs "Understanding the text," India). This lesson
  teaches telling the two apart, not banning opinions.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. For any factual question, locate the carrying sentence in
   the text and point at it before answering.
2. Answer whole, in own words (Ghana's summarising law again),
   with the text's facts — names, numbers, reasons as the
   text gives them.
3. Refuse imports: outside knowledge, however true, is not
   an answer FROM the text; and the learner can say what
   kind of question allows it (opinion/discussion).
4. Answer "The text doesn't say" — confidently, as a
   complete answer — when the text is silent, instead of
   guessing.

## What you need
Any short text with questions — a book page serves. Paper
and a pencil; a finger for pointing.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner answers three questions
   about a tiny text; one of them the text never answers,
   and the class of the trap is felt before it is named
   (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — find the carrier; answer whole and
   own-words; text-says vs I-know; the honest silence
   (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the carrier part: a picture of a question
     mark flying over a text like a hawk and landing on ONE
     sentence, which lights up, 1–2 seconds — the student
     lands the hawk on the carrier for five questions before
     answering any.
   - ADD IMAGE under the imports part: a picture of two
     buckets — FROM THE TEXT and FROM MY HEAD — over a pile
     of answer-cards → the student buckets six answers and
     defends each bucket by pointing (or not being able to).
   - ADD AUDIO in the silence part: a voice asking four
     questions, one unanswerable from its text — the student
     shouts "doesn't say!" on the silent one before the
     voice answers.
3. **Say it to your companion** — point-first pairwork: no
   answer may be spoken until a finger rests on its carrier
   sentence (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the
     hawk landing, in the super-simple voice (made later,
     when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — take one page with its book's own
   questions; answer all in writing, carrier line noted
   beside each; add one question the text canNOT answer and
   answer it honestly (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Answers from memory of the topic, not the page → the
  finger law: no pointing, no answering. Two rounds of
  point-first usually cure what lectures cannot.
- Copies the carrier sentence word for word → Ghana's own-
  words law is already theirs from two lessons back; ask
  for the same answer with the book closed. The facts stay;
  the dress becomes theirs.
- Guesses at the silent question rather than say "doesn't
  say" → name the fear kindly: in examinations and in life,
  the invented answer is the only truly wrong one here. The
  honest silence scores; the guess gambles.
