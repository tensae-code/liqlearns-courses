# Answering from the text — true or false

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Finding the Carrier (item 4), except item 1, which states the bucket law -> Sub lesson 3 — The Two Buckets · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Answering Whole, in Your Own Words (item 2), except item 6, Egypt's unseen passages -> Sub lesson 4 — The Honest Silence · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 (item 5) and Sub lesson 4 (item 3). Items are marked by what each item actually asks.

Say true or false. If it is false, say why.

**1. (Sub lesson 3)** If you know the true answer from your own
knowledge, it is a good answer to a question about a text
that says something else.
**False** — from-the-text questions bind you to the text.
Your knowledge has its own question kind (Talking about the
text).

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** Copying the carrier sentence proves
understanding completely.
**False** — it proves finding. Understanding shows when the
book closes and the answer keeps the facts in your words.

**3. (Sub lesson 4)** "The text doesn't say" can be a complete,
correct answer.
**True** — when no carrier exists. It is reading's proof,
not its failure.

**4. (Sub lesson 1)** India's "Think as you read" questions wait
until the end of the book.
**False** — they sit inside the text at each break, while
the carriers are warm.

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** An answer may mix one clause from the text
and one from your head if both are true in the world.
**False** — buckets don't mix inside a from-the-text
answer; the head's clause belongs to an opinion answer,
labelled as such.

**6. (Sub lesson 4)** Egypt's unseen-passage work gives you a
passage you have already studied, to be fair.
**False** — unseen means unseen: the fairness is that
everything needed is inside the passage.
