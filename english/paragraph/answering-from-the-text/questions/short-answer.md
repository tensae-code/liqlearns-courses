# Answering from the text — short answer

Use the Selam text (in multiple-choice.md) where a question
points at it.

**1. (Level 1)** Where does Selam sell coffee? Answer whole,
and name the carrier's first two words.
**Answer:** "Selam sells coffee at the market gate." —
carrier: "Selam sells…" (sentence 1).

**2. (Level 1)** Who stops at the stall, and on their way
where?
**Answer:** "Drivers stop at her stall on their way to the
capital." — sentence 3 carries both parts.

**3. (Level 3)** Does the text say Selam is rich? Answer as
the lesson rules.
**Answer:** "The text doesn't say." — fame is stated;
riches are not. No carrier exists.

**4. (Level 2)** Answer in your own words, book closed after
finding: why is the stall opened at six?
**Answer:** e.g. "She opens at six so she is ready before
the buses arrive." — fact kept (six, before the buses),
dress the reader's.

**5. (Level 3)** A classmate answers Q1 with "At the market
gate, and her injera is famous too." Find the fault.
**Answer:** the injera is imported — the text says her
COFFEE is famous. One clause from the text, one from the
head: buckets mixed inside a single answer.

**6. (Level 2)** What are India's two labelled kinds of
end-of-text work called?
**Answer:** Understanding the text (from it) and Talking
about the text (your experience/opinions) — with Thinking
about language beside them.
