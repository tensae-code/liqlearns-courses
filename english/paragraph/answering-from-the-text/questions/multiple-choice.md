# Answering from the text — multiple choice

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Finding the Carrier · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Answering Whole, in Your Own Words · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — The Two Buckets (item 5) and Sub lesson 4 — The Honest Silence (item 3).

Use this text for questions 1–4: "Selam sells coffee at the
market gate. She opens her stall at six, before the buses
come. Her coffee is famous in the town, and drivers stop
for it on their way to the capital."

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Why do drivers stop at Selam's stall?
a) Because she is their friend.  b) Because her coffee is
famous.  c) Because the buses are late.
**Answer: b)** — the carrier stands in the text; a) and c)
live in heads, not on the page.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** The carrier for "When does she open?" is —
a) sentence 1  b) sentence 2  c) sentence 3
**Answer: b)** — "She opens her stall at six."

**3. (Sub lesson 4)** "What does Selam sell at the bus station on
Sundays?" — the correct from-the-text answer is —
a) Coffee, probably.  b) The text doesn't say.  c) Coffee
and tea.
**Answer: b)** — the text gives a market gate, no station,
no Sundays; inventing is the one true failure.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** The whole, own-words answer to "When does
Selam open her stall?" is —
a) "At six."  b) "She opens her stall at six in the
morning." (check the text: it says at six, before the
buses — "in the morning" is a fair dress of it)  c) A copy
of the carrier, word for word, or nothing.
**Answer: b)** — whole sentence, own dress, the text's
fact. a) is the prey without the sentence.

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** "Do you think Selam works hard?" is which
kind of question?
a) Understanding the text  b) Talking about the text — an
opinion question, answered from the head, lawfully, when
labelled  c) a trick with no answer
**Answer: b)** — India's border, printed in its books.

**6. (Sub lesson 2)** Ghana's strands require personal responses
to be —
a) long  b) supported by textual evidence  c) copied from
the text
**Answer: b).**
