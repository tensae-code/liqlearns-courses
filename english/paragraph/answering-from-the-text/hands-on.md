# Hands-on — Answering from the text

The lesson played with fingers on real pages.

## Point-first rounds
As in the lesson, at a table with any text + questions:
finger on the carrier before mouth opens; hands flat on
the table for doesn't-say. Keep a tally of challenges
("Carrier?") and how they fell. Ten rounds make the habit
visible; the tally sheet is the score that matters.

## Carrier margin marks
Read a page with a pencil: every time a sentence answers a
question you can imagine ("when? why? how many?"), tick it
and write the question word in the margin. Pages read this
way become answerable at a glance — you have built the
index the questions will use.

## The bucket sort
Deal twelve answer-cards about a shared text (some from the
text, some from general knowledge, some invented). Players
sort them into FROM THE TEXT / FROM MY HEAD / NOT IN THE
TEXT AT ALL, defending each by pointing or by showing the
empty page. The third pile is the lesson's pride.

## Examination dress rehearsal
Egypt's way, at kitchen-table scale: a passage nobody at
the table has read, five questions, ten minutes, books of
the world closed. Mark each other's answers by one rule
only: could this answer point at its carrier? Then read the
passage again together and re-mark. The gap between the two
markings is the skill, measured.
