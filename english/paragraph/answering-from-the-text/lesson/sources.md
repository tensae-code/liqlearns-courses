**Acceptance count: taught in 13 countries (Egypt, Ghana,
India, China, Indonesia, Iran, Sierra Leone, Jordan, Greece, Kazakhstan, Iraq, Ethiopia, South Africa).** India builds the carrier hunt into the page and
prints the question-kind border; Ghana demands textual
evidence under personal responses; Egypt examines by the
unseen passage; China grades the answering shapes themselves
— postcard questions, a tick table, multiple choice, answers
that live only in the picture, true/false on a letter.
Indonesia answers a two-sided text in purpose, Yes/No,
reference and fact/opinion shapes, answers implicit
information only from evidence, and teaches answering
line-referenced questions in its Grade 10 guide; Iran
teaches scanning-for-the-answer and question generation —
making the question each main point answers; Sierra Leone
prints the factual/inferential border as an examination
system, states the questions-first routine, and faces the
unseen WASSCE text in two full lessons; Jordan adds the
true / false / doesn't-say shape with explanations at
Grade 6 and names skimming and scanning as its Grade 10
Active Reading strategy; Greece answers its long Lykeio
texts in graded shapes — true/false, short answers and
multiple choice on one feature, true/false/not-mentioned
with purpose, audience and reference questions on
another, and paragraph-location questions that send the
reader to the paragraph where the answer lives;
Kazakhstan examines by the unseen text in its Grade 9
Reading Practice — ten exam-format tasks answered from
their texts alone — and prints the third answer shape
at Grade 10, where its science book's statements are
judged True, False or Not Given;
Iraq answers from the text from primary school up —
the pond text's numbers worked in writing at Grade 3,
workbook-only texts questioned at Grade 8, an unseen
four-paragraph text examined at Grade 10, and at
Grade 12 true/false answers checked with the evidence
underlined in the text itself;
Ethiopia stocks the answering in part — its Grade 8
reading work runs on direct / inferential / referential
question triads over every text (the answer stated, the
answer deduced, the reference resolved), but no read
Ethiopia book names the honest "the text doesn't say"
answer, so that part rests on the other countries; South Africa answers in writing from the text through the whole set — its Grade 3 Home Language book's standing instruction is that answers are written in full sentences from the passage, its Grade 3 First Additional Language book answers seven written comprehension questions on a health article (printed pp.22–23), and its Grade 4 Home Language book answers comprehension and writes a 40–50-word summary of a story (Theme 1) — plainly literal-level comprehension, inference appearing only as occasional "Why do you think…?" questions.
