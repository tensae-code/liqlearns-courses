# Reading Comics and Visual Narratives

## Do it first

Look at a cartoon strip: three or four boxes, people talking in bubbles, no story words anywhere. Can you tell what happens first, what the people say, and how it ends — from the pictures alone? This lesson teaches how a visual narrative works: a story told in pictures and speech bubbles.

## Sub lesson 1 — Panels and speech bubbles

A cartoon strip tells its story in **panels** — the boxes, one after another, in reading order. What a person says sits in a **speech bubble** coming from their mouth; the bubble's tail points to the speaker. Fiji's Year 2 book has its learners turn their market role play into a cartoon strip with a speech bubble: the dialogue they acted becomes the words in the bubbles, and the boxes carry what happens.

To read a strip: go panel by panel, in order. In each panel ask: who is here, what are they doing, and what does the bubble say?

*Difficulty: A new page shape — boxes and bubbles instead of lines of text.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Following a whole comic story

A longer comic is a whole narrative in pictures. Fiji's Year 6 book gives a full unit to one: "The Adventures of Ben and Tom", read as a visual narrative. The story work is the same as any story — who the people are, where it happens, what goes wrong, how it ends — but the telling is in the panels: a face shows the feeling, a picture shows the action, and the bubbles carry the talk.

Read it like a story: follow the panels, name the people, watch for the problem, and tell the story back in words when you finish.

*Difficulty: Holding a whole story that is told in pictures — people, problem and ending — and retelling it in words.*

## Say it to your companion

Take one panel of a comic. Your companion says who is speaking and what the bubble means; you say what happens next in the story. Then swap panels.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw a cartoon strip of something you did today: four panels, in order.
2. Put one speech bubble in each panel — the words someone really said, or would say.
3. Show it to someone and ask them to tell the story back from your panels alone.
