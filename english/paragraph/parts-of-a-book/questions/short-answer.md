# Short Answer — Parts of a Book

(Every item is marked with the sub lesson it belongs to: (Sub lesson N). The gate pool for a sub lesson is all of that sub lesson's questions — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the whole set is the pool.)

1. Name the eight parts of a book this lesson teaches. (Sub lesson 1)
   — Spine, cover, title, author, illustrator, publisher, title page and call number.
2. You are standing at a shelf and can only see spines. What can you read from a spine? (Sub lesson 1)
   — The book's title (the spine shows the title as the book stands on the shelf).
3. A book has no pictures in it. Which named part does it not have? (Sub lesson 1)
   — An illustrator — there are no pictures for anyone to have drawn.
4. What two library labels work together on a borrowed book, and what does each say? (Sub lesson 2)
   — The call number, which says where the book lives in the library, and the bar code, which the library reads to know which book it is.
5. A magazine comes out every month. Name two things you would find inside it, using this lesson's words. (Sub lesson 2)
   — Articles (pieces of writing) — and any two of: new articles each time, pictures, questions and answers pages.
6. Your friend says a thesaurus is "a thin book that comes out every week." Correct your friend. (Sub lesson 2)
   — That is a magazine. A thesaurus is a book of words and their relations.
7. Why does a library keep its books in an order instead of in a pile? (Sub lesson 3)
   — So that every book can be found — by its call number and its place — and put back where it belongs; the order is the library's memory.
8. Say three rules of use you would follow with a borrowed book. (Sub lesson 3)
   — Any three of: borrow it properly; keep it only for the loan period; return it when the loan period ends; put it back in its place; handle it with care because the next reader needs it whole.
