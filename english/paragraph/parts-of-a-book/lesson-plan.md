# Lesson Plan — Parts of a Book

**One thing:** a book has parts, and each part has a name and a job — the spine, the cover, the title, the author, the illustrator, the publisher, the title page and the call number — and the library that keeps the book has its own words and its own order.

## Sub lessons

1. The Parts of a Book
2. The Library and Its Words
3. How a Library Is Organised

## Teaching points

- A book's parts: the **spine** (the strip that joins the pages and shows the title on a shelf), the **cover** (front and back), the **title**, the **author** (who wrote it), the **illustrator** (who drew the pictures), the **publisher** (who made the book) and the **title page** inside, which carries them all (Grade 7, Block II, Secuencia 4).
- The **call number** is the library's label for a book — the number that says where the book lives and how to find it (Grade 7, Block II, Secuencia 4).
- A library has its own words: an **article** is a piece of writing in a magazine or newspaper; the **bar code** is the striped label the library reads; the **loan period** is how long you may keep a borrowed book; a **magazine** is a thin book that comes out again and again; a **thesaurus** is a book of words and their relations (Grade 7, Block II, Secuencia 5).
- Libraries print their rules in English, and every library is organised — its books kept in an order, each book in its place, with rules of use for the people who borrow them (Grade 7, Block II, Secuencia 5; Grade 8, Block II, Secuencia 3).
