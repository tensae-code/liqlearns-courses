# Short Answer — Reading Graphs and Charts

(Every item is marked with the sub lesson it belongs to: (Sub lesson N). The gate pool for a sub lesson is all of that sub lesson's questions — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the whole set is the pool.)

Use this bar graph — "Books read this month": Ana 5, Ben 3, Ciku 7, Dan 5.

1. How many books did Ciku read? (Sub lesson 1)
   — 7 books.
2. Who read the fewest books, and how many? (Sub lesson 1)
   — Ben, 3 books.
3. Two children read the same number. Who are they, and what is the number? (Sub lesson 1)
   — Ana and Dan, 5 books each.

Use this graph, which sits inside a text about how children in one class get to school — "How we come to school": Walk 15, Bus 9, Bicycle 6.

4. How many children are in the class altogether, by the graph? (Sub lesson 2)
   — 30 children (15 + 9 + 6).
5. Say one fact from the graph as a sentence. (Sub lesson 2)
   — Any one of: Most children walk to school (15). / The fewest children come by bicycle (6). / Nine more children walk than come by bicycle (15 − 6).
6. Say in your own words what the whole graph shows. (Sub lesson 2)
   — Walking is the most common way to school in this class; buses and bicycles carry fewer children.
