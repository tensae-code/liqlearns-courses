# Lesson Plan — Reading Graphs and Charts

**One thing:** reading a graph — first a bar graph, then a graph inside a text — and pulling the facts out of it: what it shows, which is most, which is least, and the numbers it gives.

## Sub lessons

1. Reading a bar graph
2. Interpreting information on a graph

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Teaching points

- A bar graph shows numbers as bars: the longer or taller the bar, the bigger the number (Grade 3, Unit 28).
- Read a bar graph in order: the title, what each bar stands for, the numbers along the side or the bottom, then each bar against those numbers (Grade 3, Unit 28).
- A graph inside a text carries facts the words may not say: read the graph, pull its facts out, and use them with the text (Grade 7, Unit 21).
- Interpreting means saying what the graph shows in your own words: which is biggest, which is smallest, how two parts compare, and what changes (Grade 7, Unit 21).
