# Hands-On — Reading Graphs and Charts

Things you do with real things around you. You need paper, a pencil and a ruler.

1. **Count and draw.** Count something real — the shoes by the door, the books on a shelf, the people in each room. Draw one bar for each group, as tall as its number. Write the number on top of each bar.
2. **Read your own graph.** Point at your tallest bar and say what it is and its number. Point at your shortest bar and do the same. Say one sentence: "The most is ___. The least is ___."
3. **Ask the graph.** A partner looks at your graph and asks three questions: "How many ___?" "Which is most?" "How many more ___ than ___?" Answer every question from the graph alone, without counting again.
4. **Graph in a text.** Write three short sentences about your graph ("We counted the shoes in our house. There are more sandals than boots."). Put the graph under the sentences. Read the whole thing aloud: words first, then the facts the graph adds.
