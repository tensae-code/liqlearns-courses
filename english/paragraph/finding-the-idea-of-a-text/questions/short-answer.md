# Finding the idea of a text — short answer

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 4 — The Traps (items 3–4 name the traps) · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 1 — Gathering the Paragraph Ideas (item 1) and Sub lesson 2 — Weighing the Ideas (item 6) · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Saying the Idea in Your Own Words (items 2 and 5).

Answer in a word or a short line.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Gather: give the three paragraph ideas of
the Auntie Rose text in a few words each.
**Answer:** ¶1 she plants a garden (March planting). ¶2 the
village trusts her seedlings. ¶3 she waters by hand when
rains fail.

**2. (Sub lesson 3)** State the main idea in your own words, one
sentence, without copying the text's.
**Answer:** e.g. "Auntie Rose's garden succeeds because she
works hard at it." — any one-sentence centre, own words,
whole text inside.

**3. (Sub lesson 4)** Which trap is this: "The text is about
gardens."
**Answer:** the bare topic — a word (with its article) but
no claim the text proves.

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** Which trap is this: "The text is about the
seedlings the village buys."
**Answer:** the detail in idea's clothing — one part
offered as the whole.

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Why does copying the author's own best
sentence fail the lesson even when it is true?
**Answer:** copying runs on the eyes; the skill is the
weighing. Own words are the only proof the weighing
happened (Ghana's rule).

**6. (Sub lesson 2)** What do you ask of gathered paragraph ideas
to find the centre?
**Answer:** which one do the others SERVE? — the idea the
most paragraphs lean on.
