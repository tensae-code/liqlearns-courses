# Finding the idea of a text — multiple choice

Use this text for questions 1–3: "Auntie Rose keeps a
garden behind her house. In March she plants tomatoes and
peppers in neat rows. The whole village buys her seedlings,
because hers never fail. When the rains are late, she
carries water from the well, bucket by bucket, and the
garden drinks. 'A garden,' she says, 'is just work that you
can eat.'"

**1. (Level 1)** The main idea is —
a) She plants tomatoes and peppers in March.
b) Auntie Rose's garden does well because she works hard
at it.
c) Gardens.
**Answer: b)** — a) is one paragraph's detail; c) is a bare
word; b) is what every part serves.

**2. (Level 1)** "The whole village buys her seedlings" is
a —
a) main idea  b) supporting detail  c) stranger
**Answer: b)** — it serves the centre (her skill and work);
it is not a stranger — it belongs, underneath.

**3. (Level 2)** In the weighing, which paragraph carries
the work-centre strongest?
a) the planting  b) the watering by hand when rains fail
c) the seedlings' fame
**Answer: b)** — bucket by bucket: the labour itself,
undiluted.

**4. (Level 3)** Ghana's summarising rule forbids —
a) short sentences  b) writing the same words as the author
c) using paragraphs
**Answer: b)** — own words are the proof of understanding.

**5. (Level 3)** A main-idea sentence that fits ANY text
about gardens is faulty because it is —
a) too true  b) too bare — narrowed by nothing this text
weighs  c) too short
**Answer: b)** — length is not the law; the weighing's
specific centre is.

**6. (Level 2)** Bangladesh's task shape for this skill is —
a) copy the best sentence  b) choose the main idea from
three options  c) translate the text
**Answer: b)** — as its letter lesson sets it.
