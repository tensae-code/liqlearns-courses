# Lesson plan — Finding the idea of a text

**Course:** English · **Title:** Paragraph · **Lesson:** Finding the idea of a text
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 15 — Reading Whole Texts — the first lesson of the unit (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** finding what a WHOLE
TEXT is about — its main idea: gather each paragraph's
naming sentence, weigh which idea the most paragraphs
serve, and state that idea in your own words in one short
sentence.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The paragraph
(ideas live in paragraphs first) — the reader's side of
that same law. Writing put one idea in a paragraph; reading
finds the one idea a whole text serves.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown changing — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Gathering the Paragraph Ideas** — read the text
   paragraph by paragraph and collect each paragraph's
   naming sentence, short. (Was the gathering half of
   depth Level 2.)
2. **Weighing the Ideas** — ask which gathered idea the
   others serve: that centre is the main idea, and choosing
   it from printed options (Bangladesh's task) is taught
   here. (Was the weighing half of depth Level 2; depth
   Level 1's choosing is taught in this sub lesson.)
3. **Saying the Idea in Your Own Words** — state the
   centre in one short sentence of your own words,
   narrowed by what this text's weighing gave. (Was the
   first half of depth Level 3.)
4. **The Traps** — the detail in idea's clothing, the bare
   topic, the copied sentence and the outside idea, each
   named and tested. (Was the second half of depth
   Level 3; trap-spotting items also sat in depth
   Level 1.)
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The paragraph. Recommended, never
  forced.
- Words you don't know (next lesson) and Answering from the
  text (this level's third lesson) complete the reader's kit.
  This lesson assumes slow, whole reading is possible; it
  teaches what to DO with the whole once read.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Read a text through, then state its main idea in one
   short sentence of their own words — Ghana's summarising
   rule: "Do not write the same words as the author."
2. Show the weighing: name each paragraph's idea and say
   which idea the text as a whole serves.
3. Refuse the two traps: a DETAIL dressed as the idea (one
   paragraph's business, too small for the whole), and a
   bare TOPIC WORD ("rice") with no idea in it.
4. Pick the main idea from options the way the books ask it
   (Bangladesh's three-option task) — and say why the other
   two fail.

## What you need
Any short text — a letter, a page from a book, a notice.
Paper and a pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner reads a three-paragraph
   text about a boy and a garden, then must say what it is
   about in ONE sentence; the class of wrong answers (too
   small, too bare, copied) shows itself live
   (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the gathering (paragraph ideas);
   the weighing (what most paragraphs serve); the saying
   (own words, one sentence); the traps (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the weighing part: a picture of a balance —
     paragraph-blocks loading onto both pans, the idea most
     of them serve sinking its pan slowly, 1–2 seconds — the
     student names the sinking idea aloud before the pan
     lands.
   - ADD IMAGE under the traps part: a picture of three
     nets — one too small (a detail-fish escapes the idea),
     one with no mesh (a bare word), one right — over a
     river of text → the student casts the right net over
     three short texts.
   - ADD AUDIO in the saying part: a voice reading a short
     text, pausing, then giving its idea in own words — the
     student covers the voice's sentence and says their own
     first, then compares.
3. **Say it to your companion** — read the same text apart,
   state your ideas to each other, and settle ONE sentence
   you both sign (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — a
     text weighed on the balance, in the super-simple voice
     (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — take one page of any book: gather,
   weigh, say — in writing; then do the same for a letter or
   notice from real life (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Retells the whole text when asked its idea → the one-
  sentence law with a timer: say it before ten fingers
  fall. Retelling is Unit 17's story work; the idea
  is one sentence, always.
- Picks the FIRST paragraph's idea as the text's → the
  weighing drill: tally what each paragraph serves on
  paper. The first paragraph often only opens the door.
- Copies a sentence from the text as "the idea" → Ghana's
  rule is the cure, kindly: own words, or it is not yet
  understood. A copied idea that survives re-wording was
  understood; the re-wording is the proof.
- Next: Words you don't know — the reader's other problem,
  solved.
