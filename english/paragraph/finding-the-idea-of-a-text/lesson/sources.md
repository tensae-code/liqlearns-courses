Ghana, China, Indonesia, Iran, Sierra Leone, Jordan, Greece, Kazakhstan, Iraq, Ethiopia, South Africa, Syria, South Sudan, Nepal, Armenia).** Ghana names the weighing (main idea vs supporting
details) and the own-words law; Bangladesh sets the choice
as a task; China hunts the idea by title-choice and by the
one question a whole text answers, and names the
supporting-detail hunt outright in a strategy sidebar.
Indonesia names "main ideas and writer's purpose" as the
reading work of whole units, and its Grade 10 guide states
the whole-text law (a main idea represents the whole text,
not one or two paragraphs); Iran teaches the hunt as named
strategies — skimming with main-idea circling, question
generation from the main points, highlighting for main
ideas; Sierra Leone names the lesson itself (stated vs
implied main ideas), works it on a formal letter's frame,
and teaches the own-words paraphrase of a text's main
ideas from Primary 6 to the SSS3 summarising lesson;
Jordan names the hunt in its Active Reading boxes —
understanding the main idea at Grade 8, summarising
texts at Grade 10; Greece stocks the hunt in part — it
never names the main idea, but it works the same weighing
as gist questions and as heading and paragraph-title
matching, part by part at Grade 10 and paragraph by
paragraph at Grades 11–12; Kazakhstan stocks the hunt in
part — no read book names the main idea, but its Grade 6

paraphrasing, the idea carried in the reader's own words;
Iraq stocks the hunt whole — it names the main idea
