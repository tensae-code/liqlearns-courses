# Hands-on — Finding the idea of a text

The lesson done on real pages, with a pencil in the margin.

## Margin gathering
Take any page of any book. In the margin beside each
paragraph, write its idea in three or four words. At the
page's foot, write the weighing: arrows from the small
ideas to the one they serve. The foot sentence, in your own
words, is the page's main idea. Pages done this way stay
understood for weeks — the margin is the receipt.

## The three-net cast
On cards, write three candidate "ideas" for a text you both
know: the true centre, a too-small detail, a bare topic.
Trade cards and texts with a partner: they must cast away
two nets and defend the third by pointing at paragraphs.
Defence by pointing — evidence, not taste.

## Headline practice
News and notices are idea-compression for a living: take
any short report and write its headline as your main-idea
sentence. Then do the reverse — read a headline, predict
the text's weighing, read the text, grade your prediction.
Newspapers become practice fields.

## The copied-sentence cure
Find a text whose author names the idea in one clean
sentence. Copy it — then close the book and write the idea
from the weighing, in your words. Compare: if the two match
in meaning but differ in dress, the law is learned. If you
cannot write it closed-book, the gathering was skipped;
gather again.
