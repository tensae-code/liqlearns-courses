# Lesson plan — Translating well

**Course:** English · **Title:** Paragraph · **Lesson:** Translating well
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 44 (CATCH-UP-PLAN.md,
Levels 42–44 build list).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** carrying MEANING across languages, not words: word-for-word translation is rarely applicable; the translator matches meaning and structure, respects different word order and grammatical categories, and takes idioms across as wholes.
**What the learner can do at the end:** translate a short real text (a newspaper or magazine passage) meaning-first; spot and repair a word-for-word failure; and carry an idiom across by its whole meaning, not its words.

## Before this lesson
- Idioms and collocations (Level 43) is the neighbour lesson — if you know it, this one will be easier, because idioms are where word-for-word dies first. This lesson stands alone: its whole law is stated here.
- Recommended, never forced. This lesson stands alone for a
  learner who lands on it directly; anything earlier is a soft
  hint only ("if you know this one, this lesson will be easier").

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a bridge carrying a sealed box labelled MEANING, while loose single words fall off a broken plank bridge beside it.** Written slot: "a picture of
   two bridges over a river of language: a strong one carrying a box marked MEANING, a broken one dropping single words." What the student does: the student points at the bridge each of three spoken translations is crossing, and says why
2. **Slot 2 — two sentences as trains on different track layouts — the same carriages in a different order.** Written slot: "a picture of
   the same sentence-carriages arranged in two different orders on two tracks." What the student does: the student rearranges the carriages to match the second track without losing one

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first**: a sentence translated word-for-word comes out broken — the learner must say what broke.
2. **The honest base**: ONE source — Bolivia's Year 6 compendium, Topic 7.3 — stated to the learner plainly.
3. **The principle**: word-for-word translation is rarely applicable; it works only where the two languages share the structure (Bolivia, p.226).
4. **The mechanics**: grammatical categories and differing word order between English and Spanish — the lesson's own named cares.
5. **Idioms as wholes**: the bridge test — an idiom crosses by meaning or not at all.
6. **The production**: Bolivia's own assignment — find two newspaper/magazine articles, translate them following the guidelines, read in class.
7. **Do it yourself**: the closing tasks.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck word-for-word: say the sentence's MEANING in your own language first, in your own words, without looking at the English words. Then build that meaning fresh in the other language. That gap is where good translation lives.
- A word with no twin: carry its job, not its shape — explain it inside the sentence.
- Check by travelling back: translate your translation home. If the meaning that returns is a stranger, the crossing failed.
