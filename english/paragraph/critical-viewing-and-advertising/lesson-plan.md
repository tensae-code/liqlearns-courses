# Lesson Plan — Critical Viewing and Advertising

**One thing:** critical viewing — watching audio-visual media with the machinery visible: what the message is for, who made it and why, how its stories and characters are built, and how advertisements in particular are made to persuade — so the viewer judges instead of only absorbing.

## Sub lessons

1. Seeing what media messages are for
2. Judging stories on screen
3. Advertising under the lens

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Teaching points

- Recognising that electronic media messages are created to inform, persuade and other purposes; creator purpose in print and electronic media; central theme and main idea in audio-visual programmes; visual cues; verbal and nonverbal messages (Secondary Language Arts Curriculum Guidelines, objectives 9.10–9.24, printed pp.85–86).
- Judgements about characters, events and setting in movies; comparing stories in print with their filmed adaptations; similarities and differences in portrayal; factual vs fictional in audio-visual programmes; different points of view; effects on mood (objectives 9.10–9.24, printed pp.85–86).
- Interpreting advertisements; messages in advertisements; persuasive techniques in advertising; pros and cons of visual and auditory advertisements; pictorial graphs (objectives 9.10–9.24, printed pp.85–86).
