# Short Answer — Critical Viewing and Advertising

(Every item is marked with the sub lesson it belongs to: (Sub lesson N). The gate pool for a sub lesson is all of that sub lesson's questions — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the whole set is the pool.)

1. Why does the critical viewer ask who made a message before asking what it says? (Sub lesson 1)
   — Because the creator's purpose explains the choices — the music, faces, what is shown and what is never shown; knowing the purpose tells you how the message was built to work on you.
2. Name two things to compare when a story exists in print and on film. (Sub lesson 2)
   — Any two of: portrayal of characters, plot (whole vs cut), setting (imagined vs built).
3. An advertisement shows laughing friends drinking a soda on a beach. Read it in the four steps. (Sub lesson 3)
   — Product: the soda. Promise: friendship and happiness come with it. Feeling: joy, belonging. Missing fact: anything about the drink itself — its contents, price or health (any sound four-step reading).
4. Give one pro and one con of a visual advertisement against an auditory one. (Sub lesson 3)
   — Pro (visual): shows the thing and fires the wish at a glance. Con: it is missed when eyes are busy; sound rides along with work and driving and repeats its hook — with the matching con that sound cannot show the product (any sound pro/con pair).
