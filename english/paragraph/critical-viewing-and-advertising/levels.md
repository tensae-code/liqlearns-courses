# Levels — Critical Viewing and Advertising

One lesson, 3 sub lessons. A sub lesson opens by proving the previous one (at most 5 questions, mixed with earlier parts, drawn from the whole question set of the sub lesson just before — no hardness ranking is written in questions/).

| Sub lesson | What the learner can do at the end |
|---|---|
| 1. Seeing what media messages are for | Recognise that electronic media messages are created for purposes (to inform, persuade and others), name creator purpose in print and electronic media, find the central theme and main idea in audio-visual programmes, and read visual cues and verbal and nonverbal messages. |
| 2. Judging stories on screen | Make judgements about characters, events and setting in movies, compare stories in print with their filmed adaptations (portrayal of characters, plot and setting), and separate factual from fictional in audio-visual programmes. |
| 3. Advertising under the lens | Interpret advertisements and their messages, name the persuasive techniques in advertising, weigh the pros and cons of visual and auditory advertisements, and read pictorial graphs. |

A learner who finishes all 3 sub lessons has the whole of this lesson: critical viewing — watching audio-visual media with the machinery visible: what the message is for, who made it and why, how its stories and characters are built, and how advertisements in particular are made to persuade — so the viewer judges instead of only absorbing.
