# Levels — Analysing Speakers and Persuasion

One lesson, 3 sub lessons. A sub lesson opens by proving the previous one (at most 5 questions, mixed with earlier parts, drawn from the whole question set of the sub lesson just before — no hardness ranking is written in questions/).

| Sub lesson | What the learner can do at the end |
|---|---|
| 1. Reading a speaker | Name a speaker's tone and mood, point of view, purpose and what their manner shows of their character. |
| 2. Spotting the persuasion devices | Detect bias, name propaganda devices, and catch generalisations — including unsupported ones — and emotive language in speech. |
| 3. Judging the speaker's claims | Separate a speaker's inferences from their facts, weigh their reasons, intentions and standpoint, recognise rhetorical strategies, and judge and evaluate their sources. |

A learner who finishes all 3 sub lessons has the whole of this lesson: taking a speaker apart like a detective — reading the speaker's tone, mood, point of view, purpose and character; spotting the persuasion devices (bias, propaganda, generalisations, emotive language); and judging the speaker's inferences, reasons and sources before you are won over.
