# Lesson Plan — Analysing Speakers and Persuasion

**One thing:** taking a speaker apart like a detective — reading the speaker's tone, mood, point of view, purpose and character; spotting the persuasion devices (bias, propaganda, generalisations, emotive language); and judging the speaker's inferences, reasons and sources before you are won over.

## Sub lessons

1. Reading a speaker
2. Spotting the persuasion devices
3. Judging the speaker's claims

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Teaching points

- Analysing a speaker: tone, mood, point of view, purpose and character (Secondary Language Arts Curriculum Guidelines, Sub-goal 1, objectives 1.13–1.24, 1.30, printed pp.39–40).
- Detecting bias; propaganda devices in speech; generalisations and unsupported generalisations; emotive language (objectives 1.13–1.24, 1.30, printed pp.39–40).
- A speaker's inferences; judging and evaluating sources; rhetorical strategies; a speaker's reasons, intentions and standpoint (objectives 1.13–1.24, 1.30, printed pp.39–40).
