# Lesson plan — Other ways to communicate

**Course:** English · **Title:** Paragraph · **Lesson:** Other ways to communicate
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 44 (CATCH-UP-PLAN.md,
Levels 42–44 build list).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** communication beyond the spoken and written word: sign language and finger spelling, Braille — the script read by touch — and the gestures and nonverbal signals every body speaks, whose meanings change across cultures.
**What the learner can do at the end:** say what sign language is made of and finger-spell; name Braille and what it is for; read common nonverbal cues as a set, not a single signal; and name gestures whose meaning flips across cultures.

## Before this lesson
- Conversation lessons earlier in this course teach spoken exchange — if you know them, this one will be easier. This lesson is about the channels that run beside speech, and it stands alone.
- Recommended, never forced. This lesson stands alone for a
  learner who lands on it directly; anything earlier is a soft
  hint only ("if you know this one, this lesson will be easier").

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — two hands finger-spelling a word in the air, letter by letter, with the letters drawn beside them.** Written slot: "a picture of
   hands in the air shaping letters one by one." What the student does: the student copies three hand letters with their own hands before reading on
2. **Slot 2 — a page of raised Braille dots under a fingertip, the finger reading what eyes cannot.** Written slot: "a picture of
   a fingertip resting on a page of raised dots." What the student does: the student closes their eyes and feels any textured surface, and says what touch-reading must demand
3. **Slot 3 — the same thumbs-up gesture arriving at three borders with three different stamps — GOOD, RUDE, ONE.** Written slot: "a picture of
   one raised thumb meeting three border stamps with different verdicts." What the student does: the student says why a traveller must learn gestures like words, before reading on

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first**: a whole conversation with no voice and no writing — the learner must make hands and face carry it.
2. **Sign language**: India's Class 3 spread — signs, gestures, finger spelling, facial expressions; total communication with lipreading and writing (U9).
3. **Braille**: asked by name in India's Class 4 book — 'What is the script for children who cannot see called?'
4. **Afghanistan rides in**: the Grade 9 sign-language conversation — 'Have you ever seen anyone use sign language?' (U6 L3, pp.62–63) — taught here for its own content (its present-perfect grammar is stocked in that tense's lesson).
5. **Nonverbal communication**: Ecuador's Superior Worksheet 1 — cue types, the all-signals rule, and the cross-cultural gesture grids (printed pp.5–10).
6. **Do it yourself**: the closing tasks.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck reading a person: Ecuador's rule — never read too much into a single gesture; weigh ALL the signals together (eye contact, face, posture, tone).
- Sign language is not 'simple gestures': India's spread names its parts — signs, finger spelling, facial expressions — a full language with its own grammar.
- A gesture is a word: like a word, it can mean different things in different places. Learn it per place.
