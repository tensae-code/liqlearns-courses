# Short Answer — Reading Hypertexts and Digital Navigation

(Every item is marked with the sub lesson it belongs to: (Sub lesson N). The gate pool for a sub lesson is all of that sub lesson's questions — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the whole set is the pool.)

1. Name the three textual organisers of a hypertext. (Sub lesson 1)
   — The global structure of pages, symbols, and links.

2. How is a hypertext read differently from a printed page? (Sub lesson 1)
   — A printed page is read top to bottom; a hypertext is read by moving — following its structure, symbols and links from place to place.

3. What does a page's global structure tell the reader before any content is read? (Sub lesson 1)
   — What kind of place they have landed in and what the page holds — the map of the page itself.

4. Why can't a reader memorize one hypertext's map and use it for all hypertexts? (Sub lesson 2)
   — Because hypertexts vary: their organisers change from one text to another, so the organisers must be recognised afresh in each new text.

5. You followed two links and are lost. What does a good navigator do? (Sub lesson 2)
   — Stop clicking at random; go back to the page's global structure and read the organisers — structure, symbols, links — again, then move on purpose.
