# Multiple Choice — Reading Hypertexts and Digital Navigation

(Every item is marked with the sub lesson it belongs to: (Sub lesson N). The gate pool for a sub lesson is all of that sub lesson's questions — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the whole set is the pool.)

1. A hypertext is: (Sub lesson 1)
   a) A very long printed book
   b) A digital text read by moving through its structure, symbols and links ✓
   c) A text with no organisation at all
   d) A story read only aloud

2. The global structure of a hypertext page is: (Sub lesson 1)
   a) The whole arrangement of the page — its regions and where its parts sit ✓
   b) The first sentence of the text
   c) The reader's opinion of the page
   d) A list of every word on the page

3. In a hypertext, links are: (Sub lesson 1)
   a) The parts you can follow to another place in the text or beyond it ✓
   b) Mistakes in the text
   c) Pictures that cannot be used
   d) The title of the page

4. The symbols on a hypertext page: (Sub lesson 1)
   a) Are pure decoration
   b) Signal what a thing on the page does ✓
   c) Must never be looked at
   d) Appear only on the last page

5. Hypertexts vary, which means: (Sub lesson 2)
   a) All hypertexts are organised in exactly the same way
   b) Their organisers change from one hypertext to another, so they must be recognised afresh ✓
   c) Only one hypertext exists
   d) Navigation is impossible

6. You land on a hypertext you have never used. The first thing to do is: (Sub lesson 2)
   a) Click the first thing you see
   b) Close it — new texts cannot be read
   c) Read its organisers: its global structure, its symbols and its links ✓
   d) Read the last page first

7. A reader who navigates well: (Sub lesson 2)
   a) Clicks and hopes
   b) Knows which place they are in, and which link they followed to get there ✓
   c) Never follows a link
   d) Memorizes one page and uses it for every text
