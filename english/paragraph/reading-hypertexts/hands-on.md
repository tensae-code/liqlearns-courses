# Hands-On — Reading Hypertexts and Digital Navigation

Things you do with real screens. You need a phone, tablet or computer, and paper.

1. **Read the map first.** Open a page you use often. Do not read its words. Draw the page's global structure as boxes on paper: where the top is, where the main text sits, where you go to move on.
2. **Symbol hunt.** On the same page, find five symbols. For each one, say out loud what it signals — what the thing under it does. If you cannot say, that symbol is one to learn.
3. **Door check.** Find three links. Before following each one, say where you think it goes. Follow it. Were you right? Say where you are now, and how you could get back.
4. **A stranger page.** Open a hypertext you have never used and find one set fact (a date, a name, a price — your teacher or partner chooses). Say your organisers out loud as you move: structure, symbols, link, new place. If you get lost, name the structure again instead of clicking at random.
