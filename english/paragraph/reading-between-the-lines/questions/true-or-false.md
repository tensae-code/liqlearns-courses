# Reading between the lines — true or false

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to, split item by item by what each item actually asks. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: fact/opinion and inference items -> Sub lesson 1 — Inference and the Two Sorts · guessing-words-from-context items -> Sub lesson 2 — Guessing Words from Context · tone, viewpoint, purpose and mood items -> Sub lesson 3 — The Writer's Weather.

Say true or false. If it is false, say why.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Every answer in a text is printed in so many words. **Answer: False** — Libya's tip says it plainly: you won't always find the exact information — some answers must be worked out from clues

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** An opinion becomes a fact if many people agree with it. **Answer: False** — agreement does not make it checkable; it stays a belief

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** Tone and mood are the same thing. **Answer: False** — tone is the writer's attitude; mood is the feeling built in the reader — a flat tone can still build a frightening mood
