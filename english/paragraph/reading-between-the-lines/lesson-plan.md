# Lesson plan — Reading between the lines

**Course:** English · **Title:** Paragraph · **Lesson:** Reading between the lines
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 42 (CATCH-UP-PLAN.md,
Levels 42–44 build list).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the detective moves BEYOND finding the main idea and answering from the text: inference, fact vs opinion, guessing a word from its context, and reading tone, the author's viewpoint and purpose, and mood.
**What the learner can do at the end:** infer what a text means but does not say, and point at the clues; sort facts from opinions; guess an unknown word from the sentences around it; and name a text's tone, viewpoint, purpose and mood with evidence.

## Before this lesson
- Finding the idea of a text and Answering from the text are already taught earlier in this course — if you know them, this lesson will be easier for you; you might try them first. This lesson starts where they stop: what the text does NOT say outright.
- Recommended, never forced. This lesson stands alone for a
  learner who lands on it directly; anything earlier is a soft
  hint only ("if you know this one, this lesson will be easier").

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a magnifying glass over a page, with faint words glowing between the printed lines.** Written slot: "a picture of
   a magnifying glass held over a page, the hidden meaning glowing between the lines." What the student does: the student says one thing the page does NOT print but still tells them, before reading on
2. **Slot 2 — two labelled baskets, FACT and OPINION, with sentence cards falling toward them.** Written slot: "a picture of
   two baskets labelled FACT and OPINION and sentence cards in the air above them." What the student does: the student calls out which basket each card must fall into, and why
3. **Slot 3 — a face in three weathers — sun, cloud, storm — over the same short text.** Written slot: "a picture of
   the same reader's face under sun, cloud and storm, for warm, flat and angry tones." What the student does: the student points at the weather that matches a line read aloud, then finds the word that set it

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first** (lesson/lesson.md): a four-line text that never says its key fact — the learner must say it anyway, from clues.
2. **Inference**: the text plus what you know, worked out — Libya's drawing-conclusions tip: find where in the text the answer must live, read that part closely, work it out.
3. **Fact vs opinion**: Egypt's Reading Tips (Prep 8 T1, U5 L1, p.74) — a fact can be checked; an opinion is what someone believes.
4. **Context clues**: Egypt's Context Clues Tip (U6 L3, p.92) — guess an unknown word from the sentences before and after it.
5. **Tone, viewpoint, purpose, mood**: Pakistan Grade 5's Literary Analysis set (p.23) and Barbados's Class 4 comprehension row 3.4.10 (syllabus evidence).
6. **Do it yourself**: the three closing tasks in the lesson.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck on inference: use Libya's steps in order — where MUST the answer live? Read only that part closely. Guessing without a clue is not inference.
- Stuck on fact vs opinion: ask 'could we check this?' If nobody can check it, it is an opinion, however loudly it is said.
- Stuck on tone vs mood: tone is the WRITER's weather; mood is the weather the text makes in YOU.
