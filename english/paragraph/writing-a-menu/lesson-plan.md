# Lesson Plan — Writing a Menu

**One thing:** the menu — what it holds (the meals of the day and the dishes named under each) and how to build one (choosing a dish and listing its ingredients with the right markers).

## Sub lessons

1. What a menu holds — the three meals of the day, and the dishes named under each meal, from two countries side by side
2. Building the menu — choosing a dish for the menu and listing its ingredients with the right markers (countable and uncountable nouns, a spoonful of, two pounds of)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Teaching points

- A menu is the written list a meal is chosen from: it is organised by the meals of the day — breakfast, lunch, dinner.
- Under each meal, the menu names dishes. The book's closing project builds a **binational menu**: dishes of two countries side by side — Haitian dishes (joumou, griot, pikliz, tchaka, legim, poulet aux noix) and American dishes (buffalo wings, pecan pie, chocolate chip cookies, s'mores, corn dogs, New England lobster roll).
- Building a menu means more than naming dishes: under each chosen dish, its ingredients are listed.
- Ingredient lists need the right markers: countable ingredients are counted (three lemons), uncountable ones are measured (a spoonful of oil, two pounds of…, big chunks of…).
- A good menu is complete and clear: every meal of the day present, every dish named plainly, every ingredient list honest — a reader must be able to choose from it and cook from it.
