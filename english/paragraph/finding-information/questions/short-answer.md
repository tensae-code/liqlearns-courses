# Short Answer — Finding Information

Answer in a few words.

1. Name three kinds of non-text sources. (Sub lesson 1)
   **Answer:** Figures (charts/graphs), tables and diagrams.

2. Two bars look far apart on a chart. What must you check before saying the amounts differ greatly? (Sub lesson 1)
   **Answer:** The scale — they may differ by very little.

3. Write a true record of a fact, showing its parts. (Sub lesson 2)
   **Answer:** The fact exactly (number/name as in the source), the source it came from, and its units and date — e.g. "Enrolment this year: 246 children (school register table, office display, seen 10 November)."

4. Why must sources be named when you present findings? (Sub lesson 3)
   **Answer:** So a reader can trust and check the facts — each one is traceable.

5. Give one example of a focused question. (Sub lesson 3)
   **Answer:** Any narrow, answerable question, e.g. "Which month gets the most rain in our town?"

6. Name two kinds of sources a research project should hunt. (Sub lesson 3)
   **Answer:** Text sources and non-text sources (tables, figures, diagrams, records, people).
