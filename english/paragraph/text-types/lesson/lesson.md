# Text Types

## Do it first

Here are three short texts. "First we washed the dalo. Then we put it in the lovo." — "Wash the dalo well before you cook it." — "Dalo is a root crop grown in wet ground." All three are about the same food, but each one is doing a different job. This lesson teaches the eight kinds of texts — the text types — so you can say what job any text is doing before you read it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The eight text types

Fiji's Year 3 book names eight text types and says what each one is for:

- **Recount** — tells what happened, in the order it happened.
- **Poetry** — verses that play with sound and feeling.
- **Transaction** — a text that gets something done between people: a letter, an invitation, a form.
- **Report** — gives facts about a whole class of things: what a tornado is, how chickens live.
- **Procedure** — tells how to do something, step by step.
- **Persuasive** — argues for one side, to change what the reader thinks or does.
- **Narrative** — a story: people, a place, something goes wrong, and it ends somehow.
- **Exposition** — explains and argues a topic with reasons.

When you open a text, ask first: which of the eight is this? The answer tells you how to read it.

*Difficulty: Eight kinds, each with its own job — known by the job, not by the topic.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Knowing a text type by its shape

Each text type is built in its own way, and Fiji's later books teach you to spot the build. The Year 4 book puts a genre-feature box beside every unit's text. The Year 6 book opens with a genre map of all its units. The Year 8 book ends with a whole text-type library: annotated model texts that show each type's structure and its language features — reports use generalised nouns, technical terms and the timeless present; narratives build intricate noun groups for their people and places; recounts run on time connectives; procedures are built from action verbs.

So the shape gives the type away: steps in order with commands — a procedure. Facts about a whole class, in the timeless present — a report. A problem and an ending — a narrative.

*Difficulty: Reading the build of a text — its structure and its grammar — and naming the type from the evidence.*

## Say it to your companion

Your companion names a text you both know — a recipe, a news story, a poem, a letter of invitation. You say its text type and the job it is doing. Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Find three texts at home or at school: one from a book, one from a packet or a sign, one from a letter or a phone.
2. For each one, write its text type and one sentence: what job is this text doing?
3. For one of them, write the shape clue that gave it away — the steps, the facts, the story problem.
