# Multiple Choice — Evaluating Media for Accuracy and Effectiveness

(Every item is marked with the sub lesson it belongs to: (Sub lesson N). The gate pool for a sub lesson is all of that sub lesson's questions — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the whole set is the pool.)

1. Evaluating media asks two questions: (Sub lesson 1)
   a) Is it long? Is it loud?
   b) Is it accurate? Is it effective? ✓
   c) Is it new? Is it free?
   d) Who made it? Who paid?

2. For accuracy, the verdict is given: (Sub lesson 1)
   a) For the whole clip at once
   b) Per claim — a clip can be sound on one claim and shaky on another ✓
   c) By the viewer's feelings
   d) By counting its views

3. A claim that is only asserted, with no evidence shown, is: (Sub lesson 1)
   a) Proven
   b) Shaky until supported ✓
   c) The most important claim
   d) A summary

4. Effectiveness asks: (Sub lesson 2)
   a) Whether the claims are true
   b) Whether the material does its job for its purpose and audience ✓
   c) Whether it was expensive to make
   d) Whether the speaker is famous

5. A true clip can still fail because: (Sub lesson 2)
   a) Truth is not enough
   b) Its order is muddled, its images fight its words, or it dodges its audience's questions ✓
   c) It is too short
   d) It has no music

6. The reconciliation discipline is: (Sub lesson 2)
   a) Memorising the clip
   b) Comparing the questions you expected with the ones the material actually raised and answered ✓
   c) Counting the speakers
   d) Rewinding three times
