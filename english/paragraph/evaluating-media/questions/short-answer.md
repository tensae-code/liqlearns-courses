# Short Answer — Evaluating Media for Accuracy and Effectiveness

(Every item is marked with the sub lesson it belongs to: (Sub lesson N). The gate pool for a sub lesson is all of that sub lesson's questions — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the whole set is the pool.)

1. Why must accuracy be judged per claim, not per clip? (Sub lesson 1)
   — Because one production can be careful on one topic and careless on another; a single verdict would hide exactly where it can and cannot be trusted.

2. What is the difference between accuracy and effectiveness? (Sub lesson 2)
   — Accuracy: are its claims sound? Effectiveness: does the material achieve its purpose for its audience? A clip can pass one and fail the other.

3. Name three signs of an ineffective clip. (Sub lesson 2)
   — E.g. muddled order; images fighting the words; pace losing the audience; purpose never reached; the audience's real questions dodged.

4. How does writing expected questions before viewing change the viewing? (Sub lesson 2)
   — It makes the viewer an active judge: afterwards they can reconcile what was answered against what they came to learn, and spot what was dodged.
