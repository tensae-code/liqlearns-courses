# Levels — Viewing and Media Literacy

One lesson, 2 sub lessons. A sub lesson opens by proving the previous one (at most 5 questions, mixed with earlier parts, drawn from the whole question set of the sub lesson just before — no hardness ranking is written in questions/).

| Sub lesson | What the learner can do at the end |
|---|---|
| 1. Reading what you see | Use visual clues, interpret maps, charts, diagrams and graphs, say how illustrations and graphics support a text's meaning, and describe the sensory impressions a viewed thing gives you. |
| 2. Making and judging visual media | Create visual materials for a presentation, compare print and electronic media, and critique the persuasive techniques a piece of media is using. |

A learner who finishes all 2 sub lessons has the whole of this lesson: viewing as a skill in its own right — reading visual information on purpose (clues, maps, charts, diagrams, graphs and illustrations), making visual materials that carry your own meaning, and judging the media in front of you, including how it tries to persuade.
