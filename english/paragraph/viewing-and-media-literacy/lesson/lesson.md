# Viewing and Media Literacy

## Do it first

Look at the nearest screen or poster. It is showing you more than pictures — it is telling you where things are, which number is biggest, and how to feel about a product. Viewing is reading with the eyes, and it is a skill you can train. This lesson trains it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Reading what you see

*Difficulty: first step — the entry into this lesson.*

Pictures carry information, and a good viewer reads it on purpose. Start with **visual clues**: the colours, the arrows, the size of things, what stands in front and what behind. Then read the working pictures: a **map** shows where things are; a **chart** or **graph** shows numbers as shapes; a **diagram** shows how a thing is built or how it works.

Illustrations and graphics are not decoration. They **support the meaning** of the words beside them: the picture of the water cycle carries half the lesson. When you view, ask: what does this picture tell me that the words did not — and what do the words tell me that the picture cannot?

Try it now: Open any book or page with a picture and words. Cover the words and read only the picture: list three things it tells you. Then cover the picture and read the words. What did each side carry?

## Sub lesson 2 — Making and judging visual media

*Difficulty: builds on Sub lesson 1 — the same skill, taken further.*

Viewers also make. When you present, **create visual materials** that carry your point: one clear picture beats a page of small words. Choose the medium on purpose — a printed poster stays on the wall; an electronic slide can move, sound and change.

And viewers judge. Media tries things on you: bright colours for excitement, a sad face for sympathy, a famous face for trust. **Critique the persuasive techniques**: name the trick, and the feeling it is fishing for, before you decide what you think.

Try it now: Find one advertisement in print and one on a screen. For each: name the medium, one persuasive technique, and the feeling it wants. Which worked better on you — and why?

## Say it to your companion

Read this lesson aloud to your companion — a toy, a pet, a person. Then explain, in your own words: how to read visual information — maps, charts, diagrams, graphs and illustrations — and how to judge the media that carries it.

## Do It Yourself

1. Read one map, chart or diagram today. Write down three facts it gives you without a single sentence.
2. Take one page with a picture and words. Write what the picture carries and what only the words carry.
3. Make one visual for something you must present: one picture, few words, your point clear.
4. Find one piece of media persuading you. Name its technique and the feeling it is fishing for.
