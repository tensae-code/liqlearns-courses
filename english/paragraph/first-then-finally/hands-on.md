# Hands-on — First, then, finally

The lesson done with hands, in true order.

## Do it, say it
Make the real thing — tea, a paper boat, a washed face —
in silence, while a partner narrates your hands live in
signposted sentences: "First, you are filling the pot…"
Swap at the halfway step. If the narration runs ahead of
the hands or behind them, both stop and re-join at the true
step. The paragraph and the process must arrive together.

## Scramble cards
Write a five-step process on five cards, signposts and all.
Shuffle and hand them over: your partner lays the true
order and reads it aloud. Then they RE-scramble it with the
signposts left lying (Finally on step two) — and you must
fix the WORDS, not just the cards. Two different rescues,
two different skills.

## The trip retold on the floor
Five big papers on the floor, one per signpost word. Retell
a real trip walking the papers: one true sentence per stone,
past tense, no stone skipped. A listener may call "joint!"
when a step change went unsignposted — step back and dress
it before walking on.

## Recipe for a stranger
Write a family process (a dish, a remedy, a way of braiding)
as a signposted paragraph, and give it to someone who has
never done it. Their attempt is your grade: where their
hands hesitate, your paragraph missed a joint. Rewrite that
step, not the whole page.
