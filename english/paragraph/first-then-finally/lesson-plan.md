# Lesson plan — First, then, finally

**Course:** English · **Title:** Paragraph · **Lesson:** First, then, finally
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4, Unit 1 "The Paragraph"
— the second lesson of the unit (CURRICULUM.md). Building
it completes Unit 1.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** ordering the sentences
of a paragraph in TIME with the sequence words — first,
then, next, after that, finally — so a process can be
followed and a day can be retold, step by step, in the
order things truly happen.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The paragraph
(sentences belonging together) and Past actions (the recount
tense). Belonging told us WHICH sentences stand together;
this lesson stands them in their ORDER.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown changing — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The paragraph; Past actions.
  Recommended, never forced.
- The words also open stories (Unit 4 spends them there);
  this lesson keeps them inside the paragraph, on processes
  and recounts — their first home in every book here.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Write a process paragraph that a stranger could follow:
   making tea, washing hands, planting a seed — steps in
   true order under their signposts.
2. Retell a finished day or trip as a recount paragraph:
   first… then… after that… finally.
3. Unscramble a mixed paragraph back into its true order
   and defend the order by the meaning, not the signposts
   alone.
4. Use the comma dress of the openers ("First, …") and vary
   the middle words (then / next / after that) so the road
   does not stutter.

## What you need
A process you can do with your hands (tea, a paper boat,
washing). Paper and a pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner reads a tea-making
   paragraph with its steps scrambled and feels it break:
   you cannot drink the water before you boil it; the
   ORDER is meaning (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the signpost set and each word's
   seat; the two homes (process, recount); the comma after
   the opener; when the signposts may rest (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the set part: a picture of five stepping
     stones across a stream, each lighting as a foot lands —
     FIRST, THEN, NEXT, AFTER THAT, FINALLY printed on the
     stones in turn, 1–2 seconds — the student steps a real
     recount across the room on the words, one stone-word
     per step.
   - ADD IMAGE under the process part: a picture of a
     four-panel strip of hands making tea, panels unlabelled
     → the student writes the signposted sentence for each
     panel, in order.
   - ADD AUDIO in the recount part: a voice retelling a
     school trip with the signposts — the student holds up a
     finger at each signpost and afterwards retells the same
     trip back in their own order-words.
3. **Say it to your companion** — teach a process you know
   (a game, a recipe, a repair) in signposted sentences;
   your companion mimes each step only when its order is
   safe (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — tea
     made on camera under the signposts, in the super-simple
     voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, ordering, matching — served
   at the learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — write how to make something real
   (five steps, signposted), and yesterday retold as a
   recount paragraph (lesson/lesson.md).

## If the learner is stuck
- Every sentence opens with "Then" → the set has five
  words; First opens, Finally closes, the middle three
  alternate. A road of only one signpost-word stutters; the
  books vary them.
- Signposts without order ("First, I drank the tea. Then, I
  boiled the water.") → the words are promises, not magic:
  unscramble by the WORLD first (what must happen before
  what), then dress it with signposts.
- Order words on a non-time paragraph (describing a house:
  "First, the roof…") → honestly: place-order exists in
  description too (top to bottom) — named here as a cousin,
  and the time family stays this lesson's plant.
- This lesson completes Unit 1. Next: Unit 2 — Reading
  Whole Texts.
