# First, then, finally — true or false

Say true or false. If it is false, say why.

**1. (Level 1)** In a process, the order of steps is part of
the meaning.
**True** — boil-after-drinking is nonsense; the laugh in
the Do-it-first proves it.

**2. (Level 1)** Every sentence in a paragraph needs its own
signpost.
**False** — signposts stand at the joints; glued steps
travel without.

**3. (Level 2)** A recount wears the past tense under its
signposts.
**True** — finished time, past coats (Unit 3's own rule
riding here).

**4. (Level 3)** If the signposts are in order, the paragraph
is right.
**False** — the WORLD's order must be true first; First on
a last step is a dressed lie.

**5. (Level 2)** "After that" and "next" do the same middle
work as "then."
**True** — the middle three alternate so the road does not
stutter.

**6. (Level 3)** Egypt's Tip calls these words "linking
words."
**True** — "Linking words make the story clear and easy to
follow."
