# First, then, finally — ordering

Stand the sentences in their true order. (This type IS this
lesson: order is its one thing.)

**1. (Level 1)** Finally, eat the bread. / First, mix the
flour and water. / Then, bake the bread.
**Answer:** First, mix the flour and water. Then, bake the
bread. Finally, eat the bread.

**2. (Level 1)** Then, dry your hands. / First, wash your
hands. / Finally, hang the towel.
**Answer:** First, wash your hands. Then, dry your hands.
Finally, hang the towel.

**3. (Level 2)** After that, we ate under the tree. / First,
we walked to the river. / Finally, we went home. / Then, we
swam.
**Answer:** First, we walked to the river. Then, we swam.
After that, we ate under the tree. Finally, we went home.

**4. (Level 3)** The signposts lie — fix the words too:
"Finally, plant the seed. First, water the seed. Then, dig
the hole."
**Answer:** First, dig the hole. Then, plant the seed.
Finally, water it (the seed). — sentences re-ordered AND
signposts re-seated.

**5. (Level 3)** Join and order: he opened the door / he
went in / he knocked — one step needs no signpost; glue it.
**Answer:** "First, he knocked. Then, he opened the door
and went in." — the glued pair travels together, as the
lesson rules.
