# First, then, finally — short answer

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — The Process Home · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — The Recount Home · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Judgment at the Joints.
Answer in a word or a short line.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Signpost it: steps of washing your hands,
three sentences.
**Answer:** "First, wet your hands. Then, rub them with
soap. Finally, wash the soap away." (orders may vary in
words; the steps may not.)

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** Why does "First," wear a comma?
**Answer:** the signpost opens the sentence and the voice
pauses after it — the comma writes that pause.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Retell in two signposted sentences: you
went to the market; you bought rice.
**Answer:** "First, I went to the market. Then (After that),
I bought rice." — past coats on, middle word at the joint.

**4. (Sub lesson 3)** A paragraph reads: "Then, she slept. First,
she ate. Finally, she worked." Rescue it.
**Answer:** "First, she worked. Then, she ate. Finally, she
slept." — the world's order restored, signposts re-seated
to match (any true day-order earns it).

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** When may a step travel WITHOUT a signpost?
**Answer:** when the meaning glues it tight to the step
before ("He opened the door and went in") — signposts stand
at the joints, not on every stone.

**6. (Sub lesson 2)** Name the middle words of the set (three).
**Answer:** then, next, after that.
