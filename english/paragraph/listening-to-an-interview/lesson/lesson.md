# Listening to an Interview

## Do it first

Look at these two short talks. Which one is an interview?

- Talk 1: "Good morning. Today our guest is a singer.
  Welcome! — Thank you. — When did you start singing?
  — As a child, with my family."
- Talk 2: "Singing is an old art. Many families sing together.
  Songs carry the history of a place."

Talk 1 is the interview: one person asks, and the other
answers. This lesson teaches you to listen to an interview —
to hear its shape, and to hear what the speaker likes, does
not like, and is praised for.

## Sub lesson 1 — What an interview is

**Friendly opener.** An interview is a talk with a job: one
person's job is to ask, and the other person's job is to
answer. You hear interviews on the radio, on television and
on the internet, and you read them in newspapers.

**The shape of it.** The person who asks is the interviewer.
The person who answers is the guest. People give interviews
to share what they know, what they do and what they feel —
a singer talks about her music, a farmer about his fields,
a doctor about her work.

**Work it.**
- "Welcome! When did you start singing? — I started as a
  child." — the first voice is the interviewer; the second
  is the guest.
- "Our guest today is a maker of instruments." — a guest is
  coming, and the talk will be about his work.

**Watch out.** In an interview the questions lead. If nobody
asks anything, it is a talk or a speech, not an interview.

*Difficulty: first steps.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Listening for the shape

**Friendly opener.** Every interview has the same skeleton.
If you listen for the skeleton, the words are easier to catch.

**The shape of it.** An interview has four parts, in order:
the **opener** (a welcome, and the guest's name and work),
the **questions**, the **answers**, and the **closing** (a
thank-you and a goodbye). Listen to this short practice
interview and find the four parts:

- — Welcome to the programme. Today our guest is a singer of
  traditional music. Welcome! *(opener)*
- — Thank you. I am happy to be here.
- — When did you start singing? *(question)*
- — I started as a child, with my family. *(answer)*
- — What do you like most about your work? *(question)*
- — I love singing the songs of my region. They carry our
  history. *(answer)*
- — Your music keeps our heritage alive. Thank you for
  coming. *(closing)*
- — Thank you for inviting me.

This lesson follows Mexico's Grade 7 book, which listens to
an interview with the singer Susana Harp, under the heading
"Afro-México y patrimonio cultural". The book's work is the
same as yours here: hear the shape, then hear what the guest
likes and dislikes and what the interviewer praises.

**Work it.**
- The opener tells you who the guest is before the questions
  start — listen to it carefully; it names the topic too.
- The closing often holds a compliment — listen to the end.

**Watch out.** Do not stop listening after the first answer.
The shape runs to the closing, and the closing counts.

*Difficulty: building up.*

## The companion

Ask a partner three questions about their work, their music
or their family, in interview shape: welcome them first, ask,
listen, and close with a thank-you. Then change places.

## Sub lesson 3 — Listening for likes, dislikes and compliments

**Friendly opener.** Inside an interview, a speaker tells
you three personal things again and again: what they like,
what they do not like, and — from the interviewer's side —
compliments, the kind words that praise the guest.

**The shape of it.**
- A **like**: "I love singing the songs of my region."
  "I really enjoy working with children."
- A **dislike**: "I don't like it when people forget our
  traditions." "I don't enjoy long journeys."
- A **compliment**: "Your music keeps our heritage alive."
  "You sing beautifully."

Likes and dislikes come from the guest. Compliments usually
come from the interviewer, often in the opener or the
closing.

**Work it.**
- "I love the food of my town." — a like.
- "I don't like cold mornings." — a dislike.
- "Your work protects our heritage." — a compliment.

**Watch out.** A compliment is about the guest, not about
the speaker: "I sing well" is a fact about me; "You sing
beautifully" is a compliment to you.

*Difficulty: building up.*

## Sub lesson 4 — Listening again and answering from what you heard

**Friendly opener.** Listening once is rarely enough. Good
listeners listen again — and then answer from what they
heard, not from what they guess.

**The shape of it.** When a question asks about a listening,
the answer lives inside the listening. Listen once for the
shape, listen again for the details, and answer with what
the speaker said. If you missed a word, listen for the words
around it. Never invent a detail the speaker never said.

**Work it.** Answer from the practice interview in Sub
lesson 2:
- What does the guest like most about her work? — Singing
  the songs of her region.
- What compliment does the interviewer give? — That her
  music keeps the heritage alive.
- When did the guest start singing? — As a child, with her
  family.

**Watch out.** An answer that sounds clever but is not in
the listening is a wrong answer. Short and heard beats long
and invented.

*Difficulty: the full move.*

## Do It Yourself

Find a short interview — on the radio, on video or in a
newspaper — and write down: the guest's name and work, one
like, one dislike if there is one, and one compliment. You
have listened to an interview the way this lesson teaches.
