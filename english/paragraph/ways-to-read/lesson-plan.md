# Lesson plan — Ways to read

**Course:** English · **Title:** Paragraph · **Lesson:** Ways to read
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 42 (CATCH-UP-PLAN.md,
Levels 42–44 build list).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** reading ON PURPOSE, with named systems: SQ3R and KWL as whole routines, and skimming and scanning as deliberate fast strategies — choosing HOW to read before reading.
**What the learner can do at the end:** run SQ3R (Survey, Question, Read, Recite, Review) and KWL (Know, Want to know, Learned) on a real text; skim for the gist in minutes; and scan for one named fact without reading the rest.

## Before this lesson
- Finding the idea of a text is already taught — if you know it, this lesson will be easier. This lesson is about the road you take THROUGH a text, not the idea you carry out of it.
- Recommended, never forced. This lesson stands alone for a
  learner who lands on it directly; anything earlier is a soft
  hint only ("if you know this one, this lesson will be easier").

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a road map over an open book, with five named stops: Survey, Question, Read, Recite, Review.** Written slot: "a picture of
   a map laid over a book, its road stopping at five named towns." What the student does: the student traces the road with a finger and says the five stops aloud before reading on
2. **Slot 2 — two runners beside a slow walker on the same page — one flying over it, one hunting one word.** Written slot: "a picture of
   a page with a bird flying over it, a hunting dog nosing one line, and a slow careful walker." What the student does: the student points at the reader they need for 'find the date' and for 'get the gist', and says why

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first**: one page, three hunts — gist, one date, full understanding — and the discovery that one road does not fit all three.
2. **Skimming**: fast, for the gist — Ghana's skim/scan in 3–4 minutes; Rwanda's own skimming explanation (P.6 U6, p.76).
3. **Scanning**: fast, for ONE thing — Rwanda's scanning explanation (p.77); Libya's scanning tip — look for clues in the question first (Grade 7, p.89).
4. **SQ3R**: Survey, Question, Read, Recite, Review — Ghana's named system (curriculum evidence).
5. **KWL**: Know, Want to know, Learned — Ghana's routine (curriculum evidence).
6. **Choosing under pressure**: Vietnam's Exam Strategies appendix sorts reading moves by question type (G12, pp.145–146).
7. **Do it yourself**: the closing tasks.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck on skim vs scan: ask what you are hunting. The whole gist? Skim. One name, date or number? Scan.
- Stuck in SQ3R: the first two stops happen BEFORE reading — survey and question. Skipping them is the usual failure.
- Reading everything slowly is not a virtue; it is one strategy among several, for one kind of job.
