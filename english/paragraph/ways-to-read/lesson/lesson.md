# Ways to read — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Paragraph · Lesson: Ways to read
Built from: Ghana, the NaCCA curricula (SQ3R named in full and KWL — CURRICULUM evidence, worded so) — Rwanda, English for Rwandan Primary Schools, Pupil's Book 6, Unit 6 (printed pp.75–87) — Libya, English for Libya Grade 7 Course Book (Reading tip: Scanning a text for information, p.89) — Vietnam, Tiếng Anh 12 (Global Success), Appendix 2: Exam Strategies (printed pp.145–146).

## Do it first
Here is one page of a book (any page will do — use a real one near you). Three jobs: (1) tell me what the page is about; (2) find the one date on it; (3) learn it so well you could teach it. Time yourself on the first two. You will find you did NOT do the same thing three times — your eyes flew for the first, hunted for the second, and would have to slow right down for the third. Those different ways of reading have names, systems, and drills. This lesson teaches them as tools you pick up ON PURPOSE.

## Sub lesson 1 — Skimming and Scanning
**Skimming — the fast fly-over.** Skimming is reading for the GIST: titles, headings, first and last lines, pictures — the big shape, in minutes. Ghana's curriculum drills skim/scan as a timed act — in 3–4 minutes (that is curriculum evidence: the NaCCA curriculum names the drill; Ghana's held set is its curricula, not pupil's books). Rwanda's P.6 book explains skimming in its own pages, with a short extract to practise on (Unit 6, printed p.76). Skim when you must decide: is this text worth my slow reading? What is it roughly about?

**Scanning — the hunt for one thing.** Scanning is NOT reading: it is searching. Rwanda explains scanning beside skimming (P.6 U6, p.77), and Libya's Grade 7 book gives the hunter's tip in full — "Scanning a text for information: to find information quickly, look for clues in the questions" — what does the thing begin with? A name begins with a capital; an age is a number; a country is a name you can recognise (Libya, Grade 7, p.89). Your eyes run the page in jumps until the target's SHAPE appears. Scan for names, dates, prices, times, one fact.

## Sub lesson 2 — SQ3R and KWL
**SQ3R — the study system.** Ghana's curriculum names SQ3R in full: **Survey, Question, Read, Recite, Review** (curriculum evidence). Survey the whole text first — headings, pictures, ends of chapters. Turn each heading into a Question. Read to answer your questions. Recite: close the book and say the answers aloud, in your own words. Review later — the same day, the next day — or the reading evaporates. SQ3R is slow on purpose: it is for texts you must KEEP.

**KWL — the before-and-after frame.** Ghana's curricula open texts with KWL: what learners **Know**, what they **Want to know** — and, after reading, what they **Learned**. The first two columns are filled BEFORE reading: your own knowledge wakes up, and your wants become the questions your reading hunts. The third column is the receipt. KWL fits any topic text, in any subject.

## Sub lesson 3 — Choosing the Reading Road
**Choosing under pressure.** In an examination you cannot SQ3R everything. Vietnam's Grade 12 book ends with an Exam Strategies appendix (printed pp.145–146) that sorts its reading strategies BY QUESTION TYPE — main-idea questions, specific-information questions, inference questions, referent questions, vocabulary questions each get their own road. That is the grown-up form of this lesson: the question chooses the strategy. Slow reading is one tool; the skilled reader owns the whole box and picks before starting.

## Media in this lesson
[1] a picture of a map laid over a book, its road stopping at five named towns — the student traces the road with a finger and says the five stops aloud before reading on
[2] a picture of a page with a bird flying over it, a hunting dog nosing one line, and a slow careful walker — the student points at the reader they need for 'find the date' and for 'get the gist', and says why

## Say it to your companion
Be each other's timer and target-setter. One reads, the other holds the clock and the jobs: 'Skim — gist in three minutes.' 'Scan — find the captain's age.' Then swap, and compare: where did your eyes actually go? Naming your own eye-road is the skill becoming conscious.

## Do it yourself
1. Skim one chapter of a real book in four minutes; write its gist in one sentence; then scan the same chapter for one named fact and write how your eyes hunted it.
2. Run a full SQ3R on the next study text you meet — all five stops, on paper.
3. Start a KWL chart for a topic you know nothing about, and finish it after one reading.
