# Lesson plan — Exam technique

**Course:** English · **Title:** Paragraph · **Lesson:** Exam technique
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 44 (CATCH-UP-PLAN.md,
Levels 42–44 build list).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** winning the marks a question actually offers: reading the instruction verb (list, state, identify, discuss, evaluate, compare, summarise, compose) and giving exactly what it demands, choosing a strategy for each question type, and using a book's contents and index under time.
**What the learner can do at the end:** name what each instruction verb demands and refuse the extras it does not pay for; pick a strategy by question type from Vietnam's appendix; and find a topic in a book by contents and a word by index, against the clock.

## Before this lesson
- Ways to read (skimming, scanning, SQ3R) is this level's neighbour — if you know it, this lesson will be easier. Nothing earlier is required: the verbs are taught here whole.
- Recommended, never forced. This lesson stands alone for a
  learner who lands on it directly; anything earlier is a soft
  hint only ("if you know this one, this lesson will be easier").

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a toolbox of eight labelled tools — LIST, STATE, IDENTIFY, DISCUSS, EVALUATE, COMPARE, SUMMARISE, COMPOSE — one being lifted out.** Written slot: "a picture of
   eight tools in a box, each stamped with an instruction verb." What the student does: the student names the tool each spoken order needs: 'Tell me if it is good, with reasons' — which tool?
2. **Slot 2 — a race clock over an open book's contents page, a finger already on the right line.** Written slot: "a picture of
   a clock and a contents page, with a finger landing on the right line fast." What the student does: the student races the finger: find Unit 6 in a contents list in under ten seconds

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first**: one question, two answers — the long one earns LESS. The learner feels why the verb rules.
2. **The honest base**: this lesson stands on TWO books only — stated to the learner, not hidden.
3. **The instruction verbs**: Rwanda's P.6 examination list (U6, printed p.87) — what each verb demands and what it refuses.
4. **Strategies by question type**: Vietnam's Exam Strategies appendix (G12, pp.145–146).
5. **The book as a tool under time**: Rwanda's contents/index teaching inside the reading-skills unit (pp.76–77).
6. **Do it yourself**: the closing tasks.

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck on a verb: translate it into a delivery order — LIST = items only; EVALUATE = judgement WITH reasons; SUMMARISE = the same road, much shorter. If you cannot say the delivery, you have not read the question.
- Writing everything you know is the classic mark-loser: the verb sets the size of the answer.
- Contents answers 'which part of the book?'; index answers 'on which page is this word treated?' Do not read the book to find out — that is what they are FOR.
