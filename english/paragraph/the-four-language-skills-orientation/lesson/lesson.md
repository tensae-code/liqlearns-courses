# The Four Language Skills

## Do it first

Watch your teacher. They stand at the front and do four
things without a word: they cup an ear. They open their
mouth. They look at a book. They write on the board.

Four things. Every English lesson you will ever open trains
one of them — or two, or three. This short lesson names the
four, so you always know what you are training.

## Sub lesson 1 — The four skills

English comes to you and leaves you through four doors:

- **Listening** — language comes in through the ears.
- **Speaking** — language goes out through the mouth.
- **Reading** — language comes in through the eyes.
- **Writing** — language goes out through the hand.

Two skills **take in**: listening and reading. Two skills
**send out**: speaking and writing.

Nothing else in English is a fifth thing. A lesson on
question tags trains speaking and writing. A story trains
reading — and listening, when it is read aloud. Grammar,
words, sounds: all of them ride on these four.

*Difficulty: first steps — four names, two directions, and a channel for each (ear, mouth, eye, hand).*

## Sub lesson 2 — How the four work together

The four skills lean on each other:

- You **speak** better when you have **listened** much.
- You **write** better when you have **read** much.
- **Reading** grows your words for **speaking**; **listening**
  tunes your ear for **reading** aloud.

So the practical move is naming: open any lesson in this
course and ask — **which skill am I training right now?**
Then ask the harder question once a week: **which of my four
is weakest — and which lesson feeds it?**

A learner strong in reading but silent in speaking does not
need more reading. They need the conversation lessons. The
map tells you where to go.

*Difficulty: building — the naming habit, turned on the learner's own four squares.*

## Say it to your companion

Name the four skills for your companion, with each one's
channel and direction. Then name the skills trained by the
last lesson you studied — and the weakest of your own four.
Your companion does the same back to you.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw the four squares: ear, mouth, eye, hand. Label them
   listening, speaking, reading, writing.
2. Shade each square by your strength. The lightest square
   is your target — write its name under the map.
3. Open three lessons in this course. Write down which
   skills each one trains.
