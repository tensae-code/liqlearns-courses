# Reading a Map

## Do it first

Here is a map in words. It shows a country by the sea. On it there are cities with dots, a blue line running down from the hills, and triangles standing in a row. Before you read any teaching, try: What is the blue line? What are the triangles? How would you find out what they mean — and how would you say which city is farthest north? If those questions are hard, this lesson is for you: by the end you will read a map the way you read a page.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a map shows, and the map key

*Difficulty: first step — for the youngest learners, and for anyone opening a map for the first time.*

A **map** is a picture of a place, drawn as if you were looking down at it from the sky. A map of a country shows the whole country small enough to see at once: its **cities**, its **rivers**, its **mountain ranges**, its neighbours and the sea beside it. In Liberia's Grade 1 reader *Where Is Liberia?*, the map of Liberia shows **16 cities** — you can count them. That is the first thing a map does: it lets you see and count what is really there.

But a map cannot draw everything at its real size, so it uses **signs**: a dot for a city, a blue line for a river, triangles for mountains. How do you know what a sign means? The map tells you itself, in its **key**:

> "A key tells what things on the map mean."

So read every map in this order:

1. **Look at the whole map.** What place is it? What big things can you see — sea, rivers, mountains?
2. **Read the key.** Match each sign: this sign is a city, this one a river, this one a mountain range.
3. **Now read the map with the key in your head.** Find the rivers. Find the mountain ranges. Count the cities — on the book's map of Liberia the count comes to 16.

Try it now: on any map you can find, say the place it shows, read its key out loud, and count one thing it shows — cities, rivers or mountains.

## Sub lesson 2 — The compass — North, South, East, West

*Difficulty: builds on Sub lesson 1 — the map now also tells you directions.*

A map also tells you **which way** things are. For that it carries a **compass**, and the compass gives the four directions:

- **North** — the top of the map.
- **South** — the bottom, opposite North.
- **East** — to the right.
- **West** — to the left, opposite East.

North and South face each other; East and West face each other. With the compass you can say where a place is: "This city is in the **north** of the country." "The sea is to the **west**." And you can **find your way**: if the river you want is east of you, you walk towards the east.

The reader uses the compass on Liberia itself: the country sits on the west coast of Africa, with the Atlantic Ocean along its coast and its neighbour countries around it — Guinea, Sierra Leone and Côte d'Ivoire. Every one of those facts can be said with the compass once you know the four directions.

Try it now: point north in the room you are in (your teacher or the map can help you the first time). Now point south, east, west. Then say one sentence about a real place: "___ is north of us."
