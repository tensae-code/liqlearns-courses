# Reading Fluency

## Do it first

Read this line aloud: "Why did the old man laugh?" If your voice lifted at the end and paused nowhere, you were already reading fluently — the marks on the page told your voice what to do. Fluency is that smoothness, trained until it is habit. This lesson trains it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Reading so it sounds like talking

*Difficulty: first step — the entry into this lesson.*

Fluent reading **sounds like talking**. The reader is not spelling out word — by — word; the words come in **phrases**, the way speech comes. "The cat / sat / on the mat" read as three chunks with a lift in the voice is already more fluent than nine separate words hammered out one by one.

The first gear of fluency is **accuracy**: the words on the page, all of them, in their order. Speed comes later and means nothing without accuracy — a fast wrong reading is just fast and wrong. Grow in this order: right first, then smooth, then quick.

Try it now: Read one paragraph aloud twice. First time, read every word separately. Second time, read in phrases, like talking to a friend. Which sounded like speech? Read it a third time and beat your second reading.

## Sub lesson 2 — Expression, pace and the clues on the page

*Difficulty: builds on Sub lesson 1 — the same skill, taken further.*

The page gives the reader clues. A **full stop** says: land and breathe. A **comma** says: small pause, keep going. A **question mark** lifts the voice. **Italics** — slanted words — say: lean on this word, it matters. Fluent readers obey the clues without thinking about them.

Now put it together: **pacing** (not too fast, not dragging), **intonation** (the voice rising and falling with the meaning), and **expression** (the feeling in the voice matching the feeling in the text). Accuracy, speed and expression are not rivals — fluency is all three growing together, a little more every week of practice.

Try it now: Take one page with dialogue. Read it aloud giving each mark its due: pause at commas, land at full stops, lift at questions, lean on the italics. Then read it again a touch faster, keeping every clue.

## Say it to your companion

Read this lesson aloud to your companion — a toy, a pet, a person. Then explain, in your own words: what fluency is — reading that sounds like natural speech — and how accuracy, speed and expression grow together.

## Do It Yourself

1. Read one page aloud three times. Each time, make it sound more like talking.
2. Find a page with italics. Read it aloud, leaning on the slanted words.
3. Time yourself reading one page accurately. Read it daily for a week and watch the time fall while the reading stays right.
4. Read a story page to someone younger, with full expression. Ask them what they pictured.
