# Multiple Choice — Mediation: Transferring Information Between Texts and Languages

(Every item is marked with the sub lesson it belongs to: (Sub lesson N). The gate pool for a sub lesson is all of that sub lesson's questions — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the whole set is the pool.)

Use this source text: "The library trip is on Tuesday 4 May. The bus leaves school at 9.30. The trip costs 3 pounds. Bring a notebook." A note about the trip has gaps for the day, the time and the price.

1. Which fact fills the note's first gap — the day of the trip? (Sub lesson 1)
   a) a notebook
   b) Tuesday 4 May ✓
   c) 3 pounds
   d) 9.30

2. Which of these is an opinion, not a fact to carry? (Sub lesson 1)
   a) The bus leaves at 9.30
   b) The trip will be great fun ✓
   c) The trip costs 3 pounds
   d) The trip is on Tuesday 4 May

3. A learner's finished note says "The trip costs 30 pounds." What went wrong? (Sub lesson 1)
   a) The fact changed on the way ✓
   b) The words are too long
   c) The note is in the wrong language
   d) Nothing went wrong

4. The new text is in your own language. What must stay exactly the same? (Sub lesson 2)
   a) Every word
   b) The order of the sentences
   c) The facts — times, places, prices, names ✓
   d) Nothing at all

5. What is the safe way to fill a gap? (Sub lesson 2)
   a) Fill it from memory
   b) Guess the most likely fact
   c) Look back at the source text and take the fact exactly ✓
   d) Leave it empty

6. After you fill all the gaps, what do you do last? (Sub lesson 2)
   a) Hand it in at once
   b) Check the finished text against the sources, fact by fact ✓
   c) Add one interesting fact of your own
   d) Make the text longer
