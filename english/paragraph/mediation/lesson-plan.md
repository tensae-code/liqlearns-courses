# Lesson Plan — Mediation: Transferring Information Between Texts and Languages

Unit 15 — Reading Whole Texts.

**One thing:** mediation — reading a text (or two), taking the exact information out of it, and carrying that information into a different text, sometimes in a different language, with every fact still true and complete: nothing added, nothing lost, nothing changed.

## Sub lessons

1. **Finding the exact information** — reading source texts for the exact facts a new text needs: who, what, where, when, how much — and telling the facts from the opinions around them.
2. **Carrying the facts into a new text** — putting the exact information into the gaps of the text you are writing, in the other language when the new text asks for it, and checking the finished text back against the sources, fact by fact.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Teaching points

- Mediation is not word-by-word translation: you read for the *information*, then write the information in the new text's own words (Poland, CKE Grade 8 exam paper, Task 10).
- Work in two passes: first find and mark every fact the new text asks for; then fill the new text's gaps one by one, looking back at the source for each one (Task 10).
- A fact carried wrongly — a changed number, a swapped name, a dropped detail — is the one failure of mediation; exactness is the whole skill (Task 10).
- The new text's language decides the words, never the facts: the same information can land in a Polish e-mail or an English note, unchanged (Task 10).
