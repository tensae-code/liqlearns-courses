# Lesson Plan — Evaluating Sources

**Unit 42 — Reading Like a Detective.**

**One thing:** judging what to believe when studies disagree — reading an author's claims, and asking whether they are substantiated (backed with good evidence) or insubstantial.

## Sub lessons

1. **When studies disagree.** *Can-do: Read the lesson's contradictory set: studies whose claims about a food's properties fight each other; survey what the author is saying in each and name the conflict plainly.*
2. **Judging which claims to trust.** *Can-do: Evaluate: is the claim substantiated — backed by evidence strong enough to hold it — or insubstantial? Weigh the methods and the proof before you believe, cite or repeat a claim.*

(The gate pool for each sub lesson is every question marked with that sub lesson's number in this lesson's `questions/` files — the whole set, because no hardness ranking is written in questions/.)

## Teaching points

- Evaluating sources (item 429): conduct surveys; consider what the author is saying; deal with contradictory information across several studies on a food's properties; determine whether an author's claims are substantiated or insubstantial.
