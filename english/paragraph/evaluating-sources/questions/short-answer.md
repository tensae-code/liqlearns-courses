# Short Answer — Evaluating Sources

(Every item is marked with the sub lesson it belongs to: (Sub lesson N). The gate pool for a sub lesson is all of that sub lesson's questions — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the whole set is the pool.)

1. What does it mean to survey what an author is saying? (Sub lesson 1)
   — To state exactly what the author's claim is, in clean words, before judging it.
2. Write two contradictory claims about one food. (Sub lesson 1)
   — Example: "This juice protects the heart." / "This juice damages the heart."
3. Name the three judging questions. (Sub lesson 2)
   — Who is saying it — and what do they gain? How do they know — what did they actually test, and how many times? Who else agrees — do independent studies land in the same place?
4. "A famous singer says this tea cured her" — substantiated or insubstantial? Why? (Sub lesson 2)
   — Insubstantial: one person's story, from someone paid to say it, with no testable evidence.
