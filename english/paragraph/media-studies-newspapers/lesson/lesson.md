# Media Studies: Newspapers

## Do it first
Pick up any newspaper — paper or on a screen. Before reading a word of the stories, look at the whole front page for ten seconds. Which story does the page want you to read first? How can you tell? Keep your answer; it is the start of reading a newspaper as a made thing, not just a pile of news.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Mass Media
Newspapers belong to the **mass media**: the media that reach masses of people at the same time. Sāmoa's Year 9 book names the set plainly — **newspapers, radio and television** — and starts where a reader starts: with the words the texts use. A newspaper reader meets stories inside stories: a **parable** is a short story with a lesson hidden inside it, told so the lesson is remembered; a printed **joke** does different work, lightening the page and keeping the reader reading. Naming what each piece of a newspaper is *for* is the first media skill.

## Sub lesson 2 — Reading a Newspaper
The same book then does the honest thing: it copies a real issue of the *Samoa Observer* and studies it as the model. Reading a newspaper this way means asking of the whole page: what leads, what is played small, what sits beside what — the page is arranged, and the arrangement is a kind of writing too.

One caution the book itself teaches by accident: its reproduced story prints "struct" and "Vavafi" — slips inside the copied text. A careful reader notices them, reads past them for the meaning, and never copies them as model spellings. Real newspapers carry real errors; reading the medium includes spotting them.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the three mass media Sāmoa's book names, and one thing a reader should check when a copied text looks wrong.

## Do It Yourself
1. Take one front page and list its pieces in the order the page wants them read. Write one line on how you know.
2. Find a parable or a joke in any newspaper or magazine and write what work it is doing on the page.
3. Hunt one slip — a misspelling or a name spelled two ways — in a real printed text. Note it, and say whether it changes the meaning.
