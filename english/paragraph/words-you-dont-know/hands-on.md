# Hands-on — Words you don't know

The lesson worked on a real, slightly-too-hard page.

## The smudge page
Take any page and copy one paragraph by hand, smudging
(leave blank) three words. Trade pages: your partner must
read the paragraph whole, write each missing word's meaning
from the context, and name the clue for each. Then compare
with the true page. Score: meanings right (or near), clues
named — near-with-clue beats exact-by-luck.

## The word log, live
One hard page, read together at the table, the log open
between you in four columns: WORD · MY GUESS · THE CLUE ·
THE VERDICT. The dictionary stays CLOSED until a guess and
a clue both stand in their columns. Fill five rows; the
page will be finished before the log is — that is the
lesson felt in the hands.

## Parts auction
Cards with long words from real reading (unhappy, careless,
re-read, disagree, helpful, teacher…). Players bid how many
KNOWN parts they can find in a word; the highest bidder
breaks it down aloud — part, meaning, whole-word guess —
and the table judges. Parts are money; spend them before
the dictionary opens.

## Beep reading
As in the lesson, but standing and timed: one minute of
beeped sentences, companion scoring clue-names. Track the
week's scores; the ladder is learned when beeps stop
feeling like holes and start feeling like questions with
answers nearby.
