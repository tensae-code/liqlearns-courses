# Words you don't know — short answer

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 2 — Inferring from the Context (items 1 and 5) · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 (item 2, the clue) and Sub lesson 3 — Breaking the Word into Parts (item 3) · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 4 — Checking and Swap-Testing (items 4 and 6; item 6 states the whole ladder, whose last step is the check).

Answer in a word or a short line.

**1. (Sub lesson 2)** Infer it: "She was famished after the long
walk, so she ate three plates of rice." FAMISHED means —
**Answer:** very hungry — the cause (so she ate three
plates) carries it.

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** Name the clue: "He is a miser — that is, he
never spends a coin he can keep."
**Answer:** a restating neighbour — "that is" makes the text
gloss its own word in the line (Libya's habit).

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** Break and vote: UNHELPFUL.
**Answer:** un- (not) + help + -ful (full of) → "not full of
help; not helping." The parts agree; the word stands.

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** Your guess for a word was "fast"; the
dictionary offers "swift (fast), a small bird." The
sentence is about a river. Verdict?
**Answer:** the river sense is wanted — "swift" as fast-
flowing fits; the bird sense fails the swap test in THIS
sentence. The sentence chooses the sense.

**5. (Sub lesson 2)** Why must a guess be WRITTEN (margin,
pencil)?
**Answer:** a written guess can be checked against the
dictionary and the clue; a feeling cannot — the log is the
ladder's receipt.

**6. (Sub lesson 4)** State the ladder's four steps in order.
**Answer:** read on → infer from context → break the word →
check the dictionary (and swap-test).
