**Acceptance count: taught in 10 countries (Bangladesh,
Egypt, Ghana, Laos, Barbados, China, Indonesia, Iran,
Sierra Leone, Jordan).** Ghana names the process and prints the
unity test as a revise question; Bangladesh guides the
first paragraphs and models them; Egypt examines by the
paragraph; China adds a model-first writing ladder across
four units and a formal letter as connected prose;
Indonesia guides its first paragraph at Grade 4 and sets
a descriptive paragraph as a chapter outcome at Grade 7;
Iran names the paragraph outright — definition, format,
topic sentence as topic + controlling idea — and completes
it with supporting and concluding sentences; Sierra Leone
sizes the paragraph at 3–5 sentences on one topic in
JSS 1 and restates that standard at SSS 3; Jordan builds
a descriptive piece model-first at Grade 8, names topic
sentences as the Active Writing craft of its Grade 11
essay lesson, and has Grade 12 order a blog post's
paragraphs as the writing work itself. Other closed countries join as their books are
read (see READING-QUEUE.md).

**Laos Grade 6 added 2026-10-27** — *English for Secondary
Schools, Book One* (MoES/RIES, 2010; full read in
english/books/laos.md). Laos names the paragraph outright —
the first Laos book to do so — in the describing-people unit:
**"Write a paragraph about one of your family member,"** with
a model paragraph printed to write from (p.117).

**Laos Grade 8 added 2026-10-27** — *English for Secondary
Schools, Book Three* (MoES/RIES, 2012; full read in
english/books/laos.md). Writing grows a plan: a
**three-paragraph place description written under a printed
plan** (p.87), two-paragraph comparisons (p.78), and a 2–3
paragraph festival account written from sentence starters
(p.97).

**Laos Grade 10 added 2026-10-27** — *English 5 for Lao
Secondary Schools* (MoES/RIES, 2014; full read in
english/books/laos.md). Writing gets a size for the first
time in the series: **word-counted tasks — "a paragraph of
50 words," set three times** — and a prescribed two-paragraph
split: subject in the first, family in the second (p.5).
Transition signals are taught as a writing tool (p.134).

**Laos Grade 11 added 2026-10-27** — *English 6 for Lao
Secondary Schools* (MoES/RIES, 2015; full read in
english/books/laos.md). Laos turns paragraph writing into a
named craft: **"A tip for writing"** (p.132) names the parts —
**topic sentence** (the topic plus at most three key ideas),
**main body** with signal words, **concluding sentence** —
dissects a model paragraph, and the final revision makes
learners find those parts in a text and rearrange jumbled
sentences into one (pp.149–150).

**Barbados added 2026-10-27** — *Primary Language Arts
Syllabi, Classes 1–4* (Ministry of Education, Barbados; full
read in english/books/barbados.md). Barbados ladders the
paragraph across four classes in its shared Scope and
Sequence grid: topic sentences and transitions first, and
**"paragraph structure" itself a Class 4-only row** (grid
item 3.7.6 — begins and ends at Class 4), with Class 3's
qualitative bar that conventions should "aid rather than
interfere with reading" and paragraphs developed to hold the
reader's attention.
