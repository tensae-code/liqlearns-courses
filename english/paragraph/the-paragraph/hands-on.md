# Hands-on — The paragraph

The lesson built with real sentences on real paper.

## Sentence strips
Write eight sentences about your home on eight strips — and
write two strangers among them on purpose. Shuffle; a
partner must sort the strips into the home paragraph and
the strangers' pile, reading each aloud and defending every
call by the topic test. Then the partner writes the naming
sentence that promises the sorted paragraph, and lays it on
top.

## The question machine
Ghana's road, for real: pick "My School." Write four leading
questions down the page. Answer each in a whole sentence,
right under it. Cross out the questions, head the answers
with a naming sentence — a paragraph, built by answering.
Do it once more for a person you love.

## Stranger hunt in real books
Open any book you own at any page. Pencil-mark each
paragraph's first line; in the margin, name each paragraph's
idea in three words. Then hunt: is there a sentence
anywhere on the page that fails its paragraph's test?
(Finding none is also a result — real books are well kept;
report the hunt truly.)

## Two-paragraph home
Write "My Home" as TWO coherent paragraphs, the way Ghana's
composition asks: the home itself, then the people in it.
Each paragraph passes its own three-word test before the
pair is read aloud as one piece.
