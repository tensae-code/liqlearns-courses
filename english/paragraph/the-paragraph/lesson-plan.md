# Lesson plan — The paragraph

**Course:** English · **Title:** Paragraph · **Lesson:** The paragraph
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4, Unit 1 "The Paragraph"
— the first lesson of the unit and of Level 4
(CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** what a paragraph IS —
a group of sentences that all serve ONE idea, standing
together: the first sentence usually names the idea, every
other sentence works for it, and a new idea starts a new
paragraph.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on everything in
Level 3 (sentences whole, joined, dressed). A paragraph is
the sentence's bigger sibling — the first thing in the
course made of more than one whole thought.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown changing — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Level 3 whole (any of it — the
  paragraph spends sentences). Recommended, never forced.
- The ORDER of sentences inside the paragraph (first, then,
  finally) is the NEXT lesson. This lesson is about
  BELONGING: which sentences stand together at all.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a paragraph is and test any group of sentences
   with Ghana's own revise question: "Are all the sentences
   about the topic?"
2. Find and write the naming sentence (the topic sentence)
   that opens a paragraph and promises its idea.
3. Spot the stranger — the sentence that serves another
   idea — and move it out to its own paragraph.
4. Write a whole paragraph of their own on one idea,
   guided the way the books guide: leading questions first
   (Ghana's "My Home"), then the sentences that answer them.

## What you need
One idea you know well — your home, your village, your
school. Paper and a pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner reads a small text where
   one sentence about a cat hides inside a paragraph about
   a school; the wrongness is felt before it is named
   (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — one idea per paragraph; the naming
   sentence; every sentence serving; the stranger test; the
   paragraph's dress (a new paragraph starts a new line,
   its first line often stepped in) (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the stranger part: a picture of five
     sentences standing as blocks in a row about a SCHOOL,
     a sixth block about a CAT sliding in — the group shakes
     it out to start its own small row, 1–2 seconds — the
     student calls "belongs!" or "stranger!" for eight
     sliding blocks.
   - ADD IMAGE under the naming part: a picture of a market
     stall with its signboard over the goods — the paragraph
     beside it with its first sentence lit like the sign →
     the student writes signboard sentences for three
     pictured stalls.
   - ADD AUDIO in the guided part: a voice asking Ghana's
     leading questions for "My Home" one by one, pausing —
     the student answers each aloud in a whole sentence; the
     answers ARE their paragraph's draft.
3. **Say it to your companion** — read your paragraph aloud;
   your companion must name its ONE idea in three words or
   fewer, and point at any stranger (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — a
     paragraph built block by block, in the super-simple
     voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — write one paragraph on your home
   (Ghana's own topic) with a naming sentence; then one on
   your school; hunt one stranger in anything you wrote in
   Level 3 (lesson/lesson.md).

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- GIF — a picture of a stranger sentence being shaken out of
  a sentence-row → the student calls "belongs!" or
  "stranger!" eight times.
- IMAGE — a picture of a stall and its signboard beside a
  paragraph → the student writes signboard sentences for
  three stalls.
- AUDIO — leading questions asked one by one → the student
  answers whole; the answers are the draft.
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later) → the student
  builds along with it.

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the
plan is the build sheet, so nothing the lesson needs gets forgotten.

## If the learner is stuck
- One sentence per paragraph → that is a list, not a
  paragraph: an idea needs serving sentences (what, where,
  why, an example). Ghana's leading questions are the
  serving-sentence machine — ask them, answer them.
- Every sentence about a different thing → name the idea in
  ONE word first ("home"), write it at the top, and test
  each sentence against the word before it may stay.
- The naming sentence comes last in their drafts → neither
  wrong nor rare in real books, but the learner's control
  grows by writing it FIRST as a promise, then keeping the
  promise. Name it as a training wheel, honestly.
- Next in this unit: First, then, finally — the order of
  the sentences inside.
