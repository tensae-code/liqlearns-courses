# Hands-on — Inviting

You do it, we guide you. Everything here is done in the learner's
real day, with a companion or the Explain Companion.

1. **Syria's role-cards.** Syria's Starters 5 invitation unit
   works from pupil-A / pupil-B role-cards with reasons for
   refusing. Play them: A invites (shopping, the cinema, a
   football match); B's card secretly says YES or NO-with-a-
   reason. A must hear which answer it is and respond — gladly
   to the yes, kindly to the no. Then swap cards.
2. **The telephone invitation.** Malaysia's Form 1 book runs
   the invitation by telephone. Do the same, back to back so
   you cannot see each other: ring, invite to a real event,
   take the answer, and — if it is a no — ask kindly for the
   reason. Write the message down afterwards, the way a
   message is taken in the book's Chapter 5.
3. **Nepal's project: the birthday card.** Nepal Grade 8's
   invitation unit ends with a project: a birthday invitation
   card. Make a real one — who, what, when, where — for a real
   or imagined birthday, and deliver it.
4. **Two dresses, one invitation.** Write the same invitation
   twice: once as a formal card (Nepal's formal invitation
   card is the model), once as a short informal email to a
   friend (Nepal's informal invitation email is the model).
   Check: no fact may be lost between the dresses.
