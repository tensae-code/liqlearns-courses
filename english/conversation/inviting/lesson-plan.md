# Lesson plan — Inviting

**Course:** English · **Title:** Conversation · **Lesson:** Inviting
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Conversation (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** inviting — asking
someone to come with you or to your event ("Would you like
to come?"), and the two true answers: accepting gladly
("Yes, please. I'd love to.") and declining kindly ("I'm
sorry. I'd like to, but I'm afraid I can't.") — plus the
invitation carried on paper: a card, a message, an email.
**What the learner can do at the end:** invite a friend to
the cinema, a match, shopping or a party with the plan +
"Would you like to come?"; accept an invitation gladly;
decline one kindly, with a reason; and write a short
invitation message or birthday card.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The invitation, plan first** — what an invitation is, and its shape: the plan in "going to" first, then "Would you like to come?" (Was part of depth Level 1.)
2. **Accepting gladly, I'd love to** — the glad answer: "Yes, please. I'd love to" — with Malaysia's telephone invitation as its listening model. (Was part of depth Level 1.)
3. **Declining kindly, sorry, wish, can't** — the kind refusal in three parts — sorry, the wish ("I'd like to"), the can't, and a true reason. (Was depth Level 2.)
4. **The invitation on paper** — the invitation in its written dresses — informal email, formal card — always carrying its four facts: who, what, when, where. (Was depth Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- If you know Making offers, this will feel easier — an
  invitation is an offer of your company and a plan.
  Recommended, never forced.
- Suggesting ("Let's…") proposes the idea; inviting asks
  the person to YOUR plan. The line is thin and both
  lessons stand alone.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a girl holding two cinema
   tickets, holding one out to her friend.** Written slot:
   "a picture of an invitation to the cinema, two tickets,
   one offered." What the student does: the student says
   the invitation out loud — plan first, then "Would you
   like to come?" — before reading on.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of a formal invitation card beside
   a phone message, both inviting to the same party.**
   Written slot: "a picture of the same invitation on a
   card and in a phone message." What the student does:
   the student points at what stays the same in both —
   who, what, when, where — because an invitation is its
   facts, in any dress.
3. **Slot 3 — a short animation (3–4 seconds) of a friend
   shaking his head kindly while a calendar page shows a
   busy day.** Written slot: "a picture that moves: a kind
   refusal, with the busy day that explains it." What the
   student does: the student calls out the three parts —
   sorry, wish, reason — as each appears.
4. **Slot 4 — a picture of a birthday invitation card being
   written, pen in hand.** Written slot: "a picture of a
   birthday invitation card, half written." What the
   student does: the student finishes it out loud — Nepal's
   Grade 8 project is exactly this card.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first** (lesson/lesson.md): invite your companion
   to something real this week — and answer their
   invitation truly.
2. **The spoken invitation**: the plan in *going to* +
   "Would you like to come?" (Syria Starters 5, Unit 21
   Study Box).
3. **Accepting**: "Yes, please. I'd love to." (Syria);
   Malaysia Form 1's set expressions for accepting.
4. **Declining kindly**: "I'm sorry. I'd like to, but I'm
   afraid I can't" + a reason (Syria's role-cards of
   reasons); Malaysia's declining expressions; the telephone
   invitation as the whole exchange (Malaysia, Chapter 5).
5. **First meetings of the shape**: Bolivia Year 2's
   invitation dialogue (Dagmer invites Maydee) beside its
   offer dialogue — invitation and offer in one topic.
6. **The invitation on paper**: Nepal Grade 8's whole unit —
   a party invitation heard, an informal invitation email,
   a formal invitation card, an invitation message written,
   and a birthday invitation card as the project.
7. **Do it yourself**: one spoken invitation accepted, one
   declined kindly with a reason, and one written invitation.

## If the learner is stuck
- Declines with a bare "No, I can't" → the kind shape has
  three parts: sorry + wish ("I'd like to") + can't/reason.
  All three, every time.
- Invites without the plan → an invitation is plan first,
  question second: "I'm going to the cinema. Would you
  like to come?"
- This lesson's neighbours: Making suggestions (the idea
  proposed); Making offers (the invitation's parent shape).
