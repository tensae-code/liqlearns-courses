# Lesson plan — How are you?

**Course:** English · **Title:** Conversation · **Lesson:** How are you?
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Conversation
— after Hello and your name (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** asking how someone is, and
answering truly — "How are you?" with its three book answers.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Asking and answering how are you** — the question "How are you?" and its three answers — "I'm very well, thanks" (a good day), "I'm fine" (a normal day), "I'm not very well!" (a hard day). (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Asking how are you back** — "And how are you?" gives the question back, and Grade 3's shorter, smoother form of the same exchange. (Was depth Level 1; the real exchange of depth Level 2 is proved here.)
3. **Asking about feelings: How do you feel?** — the exchange deepened into feelings: "How are you feeling? — I am feeling…" over the seven feeling cards (Ecuador), "How do you feel?" and "What's wrong?" over the feelings set (China Grade 6), and "Are you happy? — Yes, I am. What's the matter? — I'm thirsty…" (Saudi Arabia). (Added 2026-11-08 under the whole-arc rule, from this lesson's own stocked sources — the feelings deepening was stocked and untaught.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Hello and your name: this question comes right after "Hello."
  Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Ask "How are you?"
2. Answer with the true one of the three: "I'm very well, thanks." /
   "I'm fine." / "I'm not very well."
3. Give the question back: "And how are you?"
4. Ask the deeper question — "How are you feeling?" / "How do
   you feel?" — and answer with a named feeling; ask "What's
   the matter?" and answer it.

## What you need
One other person, or the learner's explain companion. Nothing else.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — ask a real person (or the companion) "How are
   you?", listen to the answer, then answer it truly about yourself
   (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE at the top of the lesson: a picture of three faces —
     one bright and well, one normal, one tired and unwell — the
     student points at the face that matches their own today before
     they answer.
2. **The explanation** — the warm question after Hello; Libya Grade
   2's first answer, Grade 3's three answers side by side, and the
   polite move of giving the question back (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD AUDIO: the whole exchange read aloud — "How are you?" with
     each of the three answers, and "And how are you?" at the end —
     the student listens, then says each answer after it.
   - ADD GIF at the giving-back move: a picture that moves — a ball
     thrown from one child to another and back, with the question on
     it — the student passes an imaginary ball to their companion as
     they say "And how are you?"
3. **Say it to your companion** — ask; answer with each of the three
   answers one by one; explain why the question gets asked back.
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed —
     the exchange acted out in the super-simple voice (made later,
     when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — ask one real person today and listen to the
   whole answer; answer someone's question with the true answer;
   write the two lines: How are you? / I'm ___.

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- IMAGE — a picture of three faces: well, fine, not very well → the
  student points at today's face before answering.
- AUDIO — the exchange read aloud with all three answers → the
  student repeats each answer after it.
- GIF — a picture that moves: the question as a ball passed back →
  the student passes an imaginary ball on "And how are you?"
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later) → the student acts
  the exchange along with it.

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the
plan is the build sheet, so nothing the lesson needs gets forgotten.

## If the learner is stuck
- Always answers "I'm fine" by habit → point back at the three
  faces: the books allow all three; pick the true one.
- Forgets to give the question back → the ball picture: a question
  kept is a ball kept; the game is passing it.
- This lesson's neighbours: Hello and your name (before); Good
  morning and good afternoon (greeting by time of day — its own
  lesson).
