# Lesson plan — How old are you?

**Course:** English · **Title:** Conversation · **Lesson:** How old are you?
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 1, Unit 4 "Conversation"
— a Conversation lesson, taught inside the talk in the books
(CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** asking someone's age and
answering with your own — "How old are you?" → "I'm ___." where only
the number ever changes.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source).

## Before this lesson
- Hello and your name: the answer shape "I'm ___" is learned there
  first, with a name in the space; here a number goes in it.
  Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Ask "How old are you?"
2. Answer in the whole short sentence: "I'm ___."
3. Say what changes in the answer every year (only the number) and
   write next year's sentence.

## What you need
Someone it is polite to ask — a child or a friend — or the explain
companion. Paper and a pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — ask someone near you (or the companion) "How
   old are you?", listen to the number, then answer about yourself
   in a whole sentence (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE at the top of the lesson: a picture of a birthday
     cake with six candles, and beside it the same cake with seven,
     then eight — the student counts the candles on each cake and
     says "I'm ___" for each one.
2. **The explanation** — one fixed question; Libya's three grades
   answer it 6, 7, 8 as the children grow; the books' first answer
   is the short whole sentence "I'm 8," not "I'm 8 years old"
   (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD AUDIO: the question and the three book answers read aloud —
     "How old are you? — I'm 6." … "I'm 7." … "I'm 8." — the student
     repeats each answer, then answers with their own number.
   - ADD GIF at the growing numbers: a picture that moves — a
     child growing one step taller each year while the sentence
     "I'm ___" stays the same and only its number ticks up — the
     student says what changed and what stayed.
3. **Say it to your companion** — ask the question, answer it for
   yourself, explain the one thing that changes every year.
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed —
     the question asked across three birthdays, in the super-simple
     voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — ask one real person today; write your own
   answer as a whole sentence; write next year's sentence beside it.

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- IMAGE — a picture of the same birthday cake with 6, 7, then 8
  candles → the student counts and says "I'm ___" for each.
- AUDIO — the question with the three book answers → the student
  repeats each, then answers with their own number.
- GIF — a picture that moves: a child growing year by year while
  only the number changes → the student says what changed and what
  stayed.
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later) → the student answers
  along at each birthday.

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the
plan is the build sheet, so nothing the lesson needs gets forgotten.

## If the learner is stuck
- Answers with a bare number ("8") → the books teach the whole short
  sentence as one piece: "I'm 8."
- Wants to add "years old" → that's this lesson's deeper layer:
  Libya's first books keep the short shape ("I'm 8"), and Egypt's
  Primary 3 book teaches the full form ("I am 8 years old!").
  Both are the books' shapes; teach short first, full second.
