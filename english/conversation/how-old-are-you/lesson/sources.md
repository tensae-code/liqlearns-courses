**Acceptance count: taught in 7 countries (Egypt, Libya, Laos, China, North Macedonia, Indonesia, Iran) — Egypt joined 2026-10-25, Primary 3 Term 1; see the Egypt section below.**
China teaches the exchange in the full "years old" form with
candle counting (Grade 3 Volume 1) and later ties age to the
birthday date (Grade 5 Volume 2); North Macedonia practises it
through candle-count matching, the answer carried by listening.
Indonesia and Iran joined 2026-11-03: Indonesia teaches the
exchange in its first primary chapter, in the full "years old"
form; Iran gives age its own first-book lesson and widens the
question to the whole family a lesson later.
Egypt and Nicaragua's filed books ask names in their greeting
material, not age — so they are not counted here; Sierra
Leone's filed books likewise state a boy's age ("He is 10
years old," Primary 1 Term 3, Lesson 146) but never teach the
question, so Sierra Leone is not counted here. The count grows
as other closed countries' books are read.

### Egypt — Primary 3 Term 1 (read 2026-10-25)
Egypt now JOINS this lesson. Unit 1's "Meeting a New Friend" dialog
(p.7) asks it inside the talk — "How old are you?" — "I am 8 years
old! I am in Primary 3." — and the Introducing Yourself list carries
the frame "I am …. years old." (p.6); the fill-in dialog asks it
again (p.8). Egypt's form is the FULL form, "years old" said whole —
beside Libya's short classroom exchange. This changes the count:
**taught in 2 countries (Egypt, Libya).**

**Laos Grade 4 added 2026-10-27** — *English for Lao Primary
Schools, Student's Book 2, Grade 4* (MoES/RIES, 2012; full read
in english/books/laos.md). Age is its own lesson (Lesson 9):
**"Happy birthday, Nanthida! How old are you? — I'm sixteen
years old"** (p.66), a look-and-say set of ages 8–15 in numerals
(p.67), and a birthday song that counts the years up — "One,
two, three, four, five, six, / Seven years old! / Now I'm seven
years old" (p.69). The age answer rides on the numbers climb:
the same lesson's second half counts 31–50.

**Indonesia added 2026-11-03** — **Book:** *Bahasa Inggris:
English for Nusantara Kids untuk SD/MI Kelas III* — Indonesia,
Kementerian Pendidikan, Kebudayaan, Riset, dan Teknologi

**Iran added 2026-11-03** — **Book:** *English for Schools —
Prospect 1*, Grade 7 Student Book — Iran, Ministry of
Education — Organization for Educational Research and Planning
(OERP), ISBN 978-964-05-2207-3. Filed in english/books/iran.md
(Iran — English Grade 7 Student Book (Prospect 1) — FULLY READ
+ CITATION CHECK PASSED 2026-11-03). Age is a whole lesson —
Lesson 3, "My Age" (printed pp.16–21, the book's longest):
Practice 1 teaches "How old are you? — I'm 12 / I'm 12 years
old" in both the short and the full form, and Practice 2 joins
it to the birthday — "When is / When's your birthday? — It's
in Bahman" — over the twelve Persian-calendar month names and
the numbers 1–31 (the circle-your-birthday page is printed
p.19). Sounds and Letters turns the answer into spelling: a
misheard thirty corrected to thirteen, spelled letter by
letter. Lesson 4 then widens the question to the family
(Practice 1, printed pp.22–25): "How old is your
father/mother/brother/sister/uncle/aunt? — He's 38 / She's
35 …" — the exchange asked about other people, the step the
other countries in this file reach through birthdays.
