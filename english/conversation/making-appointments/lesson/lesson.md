# Making Appointments

## Do it first

You need to see the dentist. You are standing at the desk. The person at the desk looks up at you. What do you say first? If you say only "Tuesday", nothing has been asked for. If you say only a day, no time is fixed. An appointment is a small talk with four moves, and this lesson teaches the moves in their order — you do each one as you meet it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Asking for an appointment

*Difficulty: first step — opening the talk and proposing a time.*

An appointment talk opens politely, by saying what you want: **"I'd like to make an appointment."** That one sentence does the first job — now the other person knows why you are there. "I'd like…" is the polite shape; it asks without pushing.

Then you propose a time, and you propose it as a question, because the time must work for both sides: **"Is 3:00 o'clock O.K.?"** You are not announcing the time — you are offering it, and the other person can say yes or no.

Try it now: you need an appointment with your teacher after class. Say the opening, then propose a time of your own choosing as a question. If your time is a statement ("We meet at four"), say it again as a question — an appointment is agreed, not announced.

## Sub lesson 2 — Fixing the time

*Difficulty: builds on Sub lesson 1 — what to do when the first time does not work.*

Sometimes the answer to "Is 3:00 o'clock O.K.?" is no — 3:00 is taken. The talk does not end there. You offer another time, with the offering shape: **"How about 3:30?"** "How about…?" is how English proposes the next try. The two of you go back and forth — propose, answer, propose again — until one time is good for both.

Then you close the talk by fixing what was agreed, out loud: **"I'll see you then."** That last sentence is the lock on the appointment: the time is named, both sides heard it, and both sides know the meeting is real.

So the whole exchange, in its order: **ask politely → propose a time → offer another if needed → confirm**. Four moves, every time.

Try it now: with a partner, play the whole talk. One of you asks for an appointment and proposes a time. The other says no to the first time — kindly — and the first one offers another with "How about…?" When you agree, the asker closes: "I'll see you then." Then swap roles.

## Do it yourself

Make a real appointment this week — or set one up in play if none is needed. Use the four moves in order: open with "I'd like to make an appointment", propose your time as a question, keep "How about…?" ready in case the first time fails, and close by confirming the agreed time out loud. Afterwards, check yourself: did both sides leave knowing the same day and the same time? If yes, the appointment is made — the talk did its whole job.
