# Starting and Ending a Conversation

## Do it first

You meet a new person at school. What is your first line? And later — how do you finish the talk without being rude? Try both now, out loud. In this lesson you will learn openers that start a conversation, the answer-and-ask-back move that keeps it alive, and closers that end it kindly.

## Sub lesson 1 — Openers — starting a conversation

A conversation needs a first line. Friendly openers do three jobs — greet, notice, ask:

"Hello! Are you new here?" "Nice day, isn't it?" "I like your bag — where did you get it?"

Indonesia's Grade 11 English for Change teaches expressions for **starting a conversation** as their own set — because the first line is the hardest, and it can be learned.

Pick your favourite opener and say it to your partner now.

## Sub lesson 2 — Keeping it going — answers and follow-up questions

A conversation is a ball. A short answer drops it: "Yes." A good answer throws it back: "Yes, I love football — **do you play?**"

The move is: **answer + ask back**. "I'm from Surabaya. What about you?" "I read a lot. What do you like doing?"

One-word answers end talks. Answer-and-ask-back keeps them alive.

Practise: answer my question with more than one word, then ask me one back: "Do you like music?"

## Sub lesson 3 — Closers — ending a conversation politely

Every talk ends. A kind ending has three parts — a reason, a warm line, a goodbye:

"**I have to go now** — my class starts. **It was nice talking to you!** See you tomorrow!" "Well, **I should get going**. Let's talk again soon. Bye!"

Indonesia's book pairs its closing expressions with the openers — the two ends of the same skill. Never just turn and walk away mid-talk: give the reason, say the warm line, then go.

Close our practice talk now: reason, warm line, goodbye.

## Say it to your companion

Run a whole conversation together: open with a friendly first line, keep it going for four throws of the ball (answer + ask back), then close politely. Swap roles and run it again.

## Do It Yourself

Write a tiny conversation of six lines: two opener lines, two answer-and-ask-back lines, and two closing lines. Read it aloud in two voices, then use its opener on a real person today.
