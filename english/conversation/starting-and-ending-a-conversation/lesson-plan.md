# Lesson plan — Starting and Ending a Conversation

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** Starting and Ending a Conversation
**Lesson:** starting-and-ending-a-conversation — opening a talk, keeping it alive, and closing it kindly
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Conversation
**The one thing:** Conversation frames — openers that start a talk, answers that keep it going, and closers that end it without a slam.
**This lesson's own level:** Conversation lesson (frames): opening expressions, follow-up questions and interest answers, and polite closing expressions with a reason and a goodbye.

## Why this lesson exists

Indonesia's Grade 11 English for Change teaches expressions for starting and ending a conversation as its own named work, and no course lesson held it: the conversation lessons cover greetings, names and polite asking, but not the open-close skill that holds a whole talk together. This lesson is built from the placements under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06).

## Sub lessons

1. Openers — starting a conversation
2. Keeping it going — answers and follow-up questions
3. Closers — ending a conversation politely

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is needed before this lesson. Hello and Your Name covers the first hello; this lesson covers the whole talk from opening line to goodbye, and each stands alone.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- Open a conversation with a friendly first line.
- Keep a conversation going — answer, then ask back.
- Close a conversation politely, with a reason and a goodbye.

## What you need
Nothing special — a partner to talk with.

## The run of the lesson
1. ADD IMAGE: two people meeting — one opens their mouth happily, the other smiles. The learner guesses the first line before any frame appears.
2. Teach sub lesson 1's openers in pairs: greet, comment on something you share, ask a friendly question.
3. ADD GIF (1–2 seconds): a ball thrown back and forth — conversation is a ball: answer, ask back, keep it in the air.
4. Sub lesson 3 practises leaving kindly: a reason, a warm line, a goodbye — never just walking away mid-talk.

## Add-ons
- Opener roulette: draw a place card (bus stop, market, school gate) and open a conversation that fits it.
- Two-minute talks: pairs talk for two minutes, then each must close politely before the bell.

## If the learner is stuck
Keep to three openers and three closers, learned whole, until they come without thinking. Keeping-it-going is one step after.

## Teacher note
Indonesia's Grade 11 English for Change names the two ends of a conversation as teachable expressions; the middle — answer and ask back — is the discussion work of the same book's units, and the lesson keeps that order: open, keep, close.
