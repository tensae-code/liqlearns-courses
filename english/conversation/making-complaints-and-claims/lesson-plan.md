# Lesson Plan — Making Complaints and Claims

**Unit 5 — Conversation.**

**One thing:** how to complain politely and claim your rights in speech — naming the problem clearly (long waits, service problems, delivery issues, poor quality, high prices, hidden fees) and saying what you want done.

## Sub lessons

1. **Naming the problem.** *Can-do: The complaints vocabulary: long wait times, service problems, delivery issues, poor product quality, high prices, hidden fees — and the everyday scenes that carry them (traffic congestion, late public transportation, slow internet, phone batteries dying, apps crashing).*
2. **Complaining politely, claiming fairly.** *Can-do: Run the complaint as a conversation — state the problem, keep it polite and respectful, and ask for the fix; and know the claim behind it: consumer protection (PRO CONSUMIDOR defends Dominican consumers).*

(The gate pool for each sub lesson is every question marked with that sub lesson's number in this lesson's `questions/` files — the whole set, because no hardness ranking is written in questions/.)

## Teaching points

- Complaints vocabulary and scenes (item 317): bad customer service, long wait times, expensive prices, traffic congestion, late public transportation, slow internet connections, phone batteries dying quickly, apps crashing; the nouns: service problems, delivery issues, poor product quality, high prices, hidden fees.
- Complaints as conversation (item 318): a dialogue of everyday complaints, a listening (a 45-minute wait for customer service; a phone that sometimes can't make calls; food and transport getting more expensive), and PRO CONSUMIDOR, the body that protects Dominican consumers.
- Its polite spoken shape is practised in asking-politely (the shoe-return role-play).
