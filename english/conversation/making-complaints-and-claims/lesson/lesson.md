# Making Complaints and Claims

## Do it first

The bus is an hour late. The phone you bought yesterday will not charge. The bill has a fee nobody told you about. Life gives everyone reasons to complain — the skill is doing it well, in clear words, politely, so the problem gets fixed. That is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Naming the problem

*Very simple. Can-do: The complaints vocabulary: long wait times, service problems, delivery issues, poor product quality, high prices, hidden fees — and the everyday scenes that carry them (traffic congestion, late public transportation, slow internet, phone batteries dying, apps crashing).*

A good complaint starts by naming the problem exactly. The complaint words:

**long wait times** — you waited and waited (45 minutes for customer service!). **service problems** — the service does not work as it should. **delivery issues** — the thing you ordered came late, wrong, or never. **poor product quality** — the thing itself is badly made. **high prices** — it costs too much. **hidden fees** — extra charges nobody showed you at the start.

Around them, the everyday complaint scenes: traffic congestion, late public transportation, a slow internet connection that drops, a phone battery dying quickly, apps crashing.

Try it now: name the problem in each — "The internet fell three times in my class" (service problem). "The shoes cost more at the till than on the shelf" (hidden fee / price problem).

## Sub lesson 2 — Complaining politely, claiming fairly

*Simple. Can-do: Run the complaint as a conversation — state the problem, keep it polite and respectful, and ask for the fix; and know the claim behind it: consumer protection (PRO CONSUMIDOR defends Dominican consumers).*

Now say it to a person. The shape that works: **name the problem + say what happened + say what you want**. "I waited forty-five minutes for customer service. Nobody came. I would like to speak to the manager, please." Polite words are not weakness — they are what makes people help you.

And when polite talk is not enough, there is the **claim**: taking your problem to the body that protects buyers. In the Dominican Republic that body is **PRO CONSUMIDOR** — it protects consumers when a shop or company will not fix a problem.

Try it now: role-play the phone-shop complaint — a new phone whose battery dies quickly. Customer first (name it, politely), then the shop's answer.

## Say it to your companion

Read this lesson aloud to your companion. Then complain — politely — about three things: a late bus, a crashing app, a hidden fee. Your companion answers as the company.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the six complaint nouns with a one-line example of each.
2. Write a polite spoken complaint about a delivery that never came.
3. Say what PRO CONSUMIDOR does, in two sentences.
4. Turn an angry complaint ("This is terrible! I hate this shop!") into a polite one that names the problem.
