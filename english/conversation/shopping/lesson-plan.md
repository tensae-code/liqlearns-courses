# Lesson plan — Shopping

**Course:** English · **Title:** Conversation · **Lesson:** Shopping
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Conversation
(CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the whole shop
conversation — the assistant's offer, asking for a thing,
how many and how much, trying a size, and paying.
**What the learner can do at the end:** answer "Can I help
you?" with what they want; ask "How much is it?"; ask for
a size and try a thing on; say how they will pay — cash
or card — and take the receipt.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The assistant opens, Can I help you** — the frame every shop conversation starts with: "Can I help you?" — "I want to buy …, please" — "Anything else?" — "No, thanks." (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **How many, for counted things** — for things you can count: "How many … do you want to buy?" (Was part of depth Level 2.)
3. **How much, the price** — for the price: "How much is it?", "How much does an orange cost?" — and Greece's keeping of the pair apart at a real counter. (Was part of depth Level 2.)
4. **Sizes and trying on** — the clothes-shop step: "What size?" — "Can I try it on?" — the fitting room, and Mongolia's shop words. (Was part of depth Level 3.)
5. **Paying and the receipt** — the last step: "By card, please." / "Cash, please." — "Thank you." — and the receipt, the conversation's full stop. (Was part of depth Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Soft hint only: Numbers and money words help, in their
  own lessons. Recommended, never forced — this lesson
  stands alone.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a market stall: fish, garlic,
   honey, ice cream and jam on the counter.** Written
   slot: "a picture of a shop counter with food for sale."
   What the student does: the student names everything
   they can see, then answers the assistant's question
   about one of them — Cambodia's first shop words.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of a clothes rail with a T-shirt
   in three sizes, S, M and L.** Written slot: "a picture
   of the same shirt in three sizes." What the student
   does: the student points at their size and says "This
   one, please — medium" — Mongolia's shop dialogue runs
   on a shirt, sizes, and paying.
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of a checkout: cash, a debit card,
   a receipt and a fitting room sign.** Written slot: "a
   picture of the words of paying — cash, card, receipt,
   checkout, fitting room." What the student does: the
   student sorts the picture words into "in the shop" and
   "at the paying place" — Mongolia's shopping vocabulary
   set, learned as pictures.
4. **Slot 4 — a picture of a shopping list with prices in
   euros and a small purse.** Written slot: "a picture of
   a party shopping list with prices." What the student
   does: the student adds the prices and says if the
   money is enough — Greece's supermarket budgeting task.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first** (lesson/lesson.md): a pretend shop —
   one assistant, one customer, one thing bought.
2. **The assistant opens**: "Can I help you?" (Cambodia,
   Chapter 1, Unit 1).
3. **The customer answers**: "I want to buy …, please" —
   and "Anything else?" — "No, thanks."
4. **How many**: "How many … do you want to buy?"
   (Cambodia, Chapter 1, Unit 4).
5. **How much**: prices — "How much does an orange cost?"
   "How much is a …?" (Cambodia, Chapter 4; Greece's
   how much / how many at the supermarket).
6. **Sizes and trying on**: the shirt dialogue — sizes,
   the fitting room, try on (Mongolia, English X, Unit 5).
7. **Paying**: cash or card, the checkout, the receipt
   (Mongolia's shopping vocabulary; Saudi Arabia's
   Workbook shopping dialogue practises the same run).
8. **Do it yourself** (the three closing tasks).

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck on how much vs how many: things you can count
  take many; things you weigh or pour take much (Greece,
  Unit 9).
- Stuck at the till: two shapes cover paying — "By card,
  please" and "Cash, please" — then "Thank you" and take
  the receipt.
- This lesson's neighbours: Telephoning (booking the
  room) and Money and prices (the words of money, in
  Level 7).
