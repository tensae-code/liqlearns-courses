# Introducing a friend — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Conversation · Lesson: Introducing a friend
Built from: Libya, English for Libya Primary 1, Teacher's Book —
the "Sarah's Little Fish" dialog (Track 108).
This lesson is ONE thing only: bringing a new person into the talk —
showing your friend to someone who does not know them yet.

## Do it first
Stand with your explain companion and imagine a friend is with you.
Point to the friend and say: "This is my friend." Then turn to your
companion and ask about their friend: "Who's this?"

You just did the whole lesson. Now let's see how it works.

## Then the explanation
So two people know each other, and a third person is new. Someone
has to build the bridge. In Libya's book, Nadia does it. Her friend
Sarah is new to Zeinah, so the talk goes like this:

- Zeinah asks about the new person: **"Who's this?"**
- Nadia builds the bridge: **"This is my friend, Sarah."**
- Now Sarah and Zeinah can greet each other directly:
  "Hello, Sarah." — "Hello, Zeinah."

See the shape? Question about the new person → "This is my
friend, ___" → the two new people say hello. The introducer only
builds the bridge; then the talk belongs to them.

(The same dialog goes on to Sarah's age and her little toy fish —
those are other lessons, not this one. Age lives in How old are
you?; the fish is the story's own business.)

## Say it to your companion
Introduce an imaginary friend to your companion, with the whole
shape: let them ask "Who's this?", answer "This is my friend,
___," and then have the two greet each other.

## Do It Yourself
1. The next time you are with two people who do not know each
   other, introduce one of them for real, with the whole shape.
2. Write the three lines down:
   Who's this? / This is my friend, ___. / Hello, ___.
