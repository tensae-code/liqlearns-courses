# Lesson plan — Introducing a friend

**Course:** English · **Title:** Conversation · **Lesson:** Introducing
a friend
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 5, "Conversation"
(CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** bringing a new person into the
talk — "Who's this?" → "This is my friend, ___." → the two new people
say hello.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source).

## Before this lesson
- Hello and your name: the greeting itself comes first — you cannot
  hand two people a greeting they don't have yet. Recommended,
  never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Ask about a new person: "Who's this?"
2. Build the bridge: "This is my friend, ___."
3. Step back and let the two new people greet each other directly.

## What you need
Two other people, or one companion and one imagined friend.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — point to an imagined friend beside you and say
   "This is my friend," then turn and ask about the companion's
   friend: "Who's this?" (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE at the top of the lesson: a picture of three children
     — two who know each other talking, and a third standing new at
     the side — the student points and says who is new, and who has
     to build the bridge.
2. **The explanation** — Libya's dialog: Zeinah asks, Nadia builds
   the bridge with Sarah, then Sarah and Zeinah greet each other;
   the introducer only builds the bridge (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE under the shape: a picture of a small bridge over a
     gap, with the three moves written on it — Who's this? / This is
     my friend, ___ / Hello, ___ — the student walks a finger across
     the bridge and says each move as they cross it.
   - ADD AUDIO: the whole dialog read aloud (Zeinah, Nadia, Sarah)
     — the student listens, then takes one part and reads it.
3. **Say it to your companion** — introduce an imaginary friend with
   the whole shape: their question, your bridge, their hellos.
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed —
     three children acting the introduction in the super-simple
     voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the next time you are with two people who do
   not know each other, introduce one for real, with the whole shape;
   write the three lines down.

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- IMAGE — a picture of three children, one new at the side → the
  student says who is new and who builds the bridge.
- IMAGE — a picture of a bridge with the three moves on it → the
  student walks a finger across and says each move.
- AUDIO — the dialog read aloud in three parts → the student takes
  one part and reads it.
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later) → the student acts
  the introducer's part along with it.

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the
plan is the build sheet, so nothing the lesson needs gets forgotten.

## If the learner is stuck
- Introduces by pointing and saying only the name → the bridge needs
  its whole sentence: "This is my friend, ___."
- Keeps talking for the two new people → the introducer's job ends
  at the bridge; the hellos belong to them.
- Egypt Primary 2 Term 1 uses the same "This is my ___" shape for
  family (Unit 4, "My Family") — filed in this lesson's sources;
  the friend shape here stays Libya's dialog.
