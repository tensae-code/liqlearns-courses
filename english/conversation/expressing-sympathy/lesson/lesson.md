# Expressing Sympathy

## Do it first

A friend tells you bad news — an illness, a loss. What do you say? Many people freeze and say nothing. Try one sentence now: "I'm sorry to hear that." Go on — in this lesson you will learn the words for another person's hard time.

## Sub lesson 1 — I'm sorry to hear that — answering bad news

Bad news asks for a first answer, and the answer is sorrow:

- "**I'm sorry to hear that.**"
- "**I'm sad to hear that.**"
- Small and warm: "**Oh dear.**" "**Poor you.**"

Practise on small news first: "I lost my book." — "Oh dear. I'm sorry to hear that."

## Sub lesson 2 — I'm here for you — standing with the person

Sympathy is not only words about the news — it is standing with the person:

- "**I'm here for you.**"

Say it and mean staying: sit with them, help with the next thing, come back tomorrow. The sentence promises your presence.

## Sub lesson 3 — For a death — condolences

For a death, English has set forms. Learn them exactly:

- "**I'm sorry for your loss.**"
- "**My condolences.**" / "**My deepest condolences.**"
- "**My deepest sympathies.**"

Say them slowly and gently. These forms are not for small news — a lost pen does not take condolences. Fit the words to the weight.

## Sub lesson 4 — Thinking of you — sympathy that lasts

Sympathy can be written, and writing lasts:

- "**Thinking of you at this difficult time.**"

Over a disaster — an earthquake, a flood — whole communities write these lines to families who lost homes and people. A written sympathy can be read again on the worst day, and the next one.

## Say it to your companion

Draw an imagined news card each — one small, one heavy. Say the news; your companion answers with sympathy fitted to the weight: sorrow for the small one, presence for the middle, condolences only for the death. Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

Write three sympathy answers: one for a friend who failed an exam, one for a neighbour whose house burned, one for a family that lost their grandfather. Read them aloud, slowly.
