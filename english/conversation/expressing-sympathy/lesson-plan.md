# Lesson plan — Expressing Sympathy

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** Expressing Sympathy
**Lesson:** expressing-sympathy — words for another person's hard time: I'm sorry to hear that, I'm here for you, my condolences
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Conversation
**The one thing:** Sympathy talk — answering bad news with sorrow, standing with the person, the condolence forms for a death, and sympathy that lasts beyond the moment.
**This lesson's own level:** Conversation lesson (sympathy): I'm sorry/sad to hear that; oh dear; poor you; I'm here for you; for a death — I'm sorry for your loss, my (deepest) condolences/sympathies; thinking of you at this difficult time.

## Why this lesson exists

Mauritania teaches sympathy twice — the Grade 8 book's set for bad news and loss, and the Grade 10 book's disaster lesson with its condolence forms — and the course's conversation unit had no sympathy lesson beside its gratitude and congratulating lessons. This lesson is built from the placements under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06).

## Sub lessons

1. I'm sorry to hear that — answering bad news
2. I'm here for you — standing with the person
3. For a death — condolences
4. Thinking of you — sympathy that lasts

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is needed before this lesson. Expressing Gratitude answers kindness; this lesson answers sorrow, and each stands alone.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- Answer bad news: "I'm sorry to hear that."
- Offer your presence: "I'm here for you."
- Say the condolence forms for a death, correctly and gently.
- Write a sympathy line: "Thinking of you at this difficult time."

## What you need
Nothing special — a gentle voice; the class practises on imagined news.

## The run of the lesson
1. ADD IMAGE: a learner hearing that a friend's house burned, face fallen. The learner tries the first sympathy sentence before any form appears.
2. Teach sub lesson 1 with small bad news first — a lost book, a failed test — before the heavy forms.
3. ADD GIF (1–2 seconds): a hand resting on a friend's shoulder — I'm here for you is a staying word.
4. Sub lesson 4 writes the class sympathy card for an imagined family after the earthquake text.

## Add-ons
- News ladder: pairs draw news cards from small (lost pen) to heavy (a death) and must fit the sympathy to the weight.
- Card writing: one real sympathy card drafted, checked, and kept ready in the classroom.

## If the learner is stuck
Keep to "I'm sorry to hear that" until answering bad news feels possible at all — freezing is the common fault. Presence, condolences and writing come one step each after.

## Teacher note
The Grade 8 book teaches sympathy for news and loss generally; the Grade 10 book returns to it over an earthquake, where the condolence forms live. The lesson keeps that order: answer, stay, condole, write.
