# Interrupting Politely

## Do it first

A meeting is rolling on and your point cannot wait. Do you shout over the speaker — or is there a polite key? This lesson teaches the keys, from Costa Rica's Grade 10 book.

## Sub lesson 1 — Polite interrupting phrases

Sometimes you must speak while another person is speaking — a fact is wrong, a question cannot wait. Grade 10 teaches the polite keys that open the door: **“Sorry to interrupt…”**, **“Could I jump in?”**, **“May I interject?”**

The key is the softener first, then your point — short, clear, and done.

*Difficulty: Three short keys; the skill is in the timing.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Interrupting in conversations and meetings

The book sets the phrases **for conversations and meetings**: a question in a talk, a point in a meeting. Jump in to add, to correct, or to ask — then give the floor back: “…but please, go on.”

Interrupt to help the talk, never to steal it.

*Difficulty: The same keys, used live — in, point made, floor returned.*

## Say it to your companion

Your companion talks for thirty seconds without stopping. Break in politely with one of the three phrases and make a point. Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the three interrupting phrases.
2. Write a meeting moment for each: what is happening, and what you break in to say.
3. Practise the return: finish each interruption with “…but please, go on.”
