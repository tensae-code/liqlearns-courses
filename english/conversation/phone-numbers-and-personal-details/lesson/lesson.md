# Phone Numbers and Personal Details

## Do it first

A new teacher asks for your phone number so the school can call your family. Do you say it as one long number — "fifty-five million…"? No. This lesson teaches you to say a phone number the way English speakers do: digit by digit. And it teaches the three personal details that go together on a school card: your name, your age and your phone number.

## Sub lesson 1 — Saying a phone number

A phone number is said one digit at a time:

- **5 5 2 0 1 4** → "five — five — two — zero — one — four."
- The digit **0** has two spoken names in phone numbers: **zero**, or **oh** — "five five two, oh one four" says the same number.
- Say it slowly, in small groups, and pause between the groups. The listener writes each digit as you say it.

Listen first, then say yours. If the listener writes a wrong digit, say the number again — digit by digit.

*Difficulty: Ten digits, said one by one, with zero's two names.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Your personal details card

At school, your details are asked together:

- **Name** — "What's your name?" — "My name is …" (spell it if they ask: "A-M-I-N-A.")
- **Age** — "How old are you?" — "I'm twelve."
- **Phone number** — "What's your phone number?" — said digit by digit, as in Sub lesson 1.

These three things fill a personal details card. Learn to give all three clearly, in order, without rushing.

*Difficulty: Three questions, three clear answers, one card.*

## Say it to your companion

Your companion is the school secretary. They ask the three questions; you answer from your own card. Then swap — and this time your companion says the phone number with "oh" for zero.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write your own phone number (or a made-up one) as digits.
2. Under it, write how you say it, word by word — once with "zero", once with "oh".
3. Make your personal details card: name, age, phone number — and read it aloud to a grown-up.
