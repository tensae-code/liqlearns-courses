# Asking for Clarification and Repetition

## Do it first

Think of a talk that broke. The other person spoke too quietly, or used a word you did not know, and you said nothing. Try one sentence now: "Can you speak up, please?" Go on — in this lesson you will learn the sentences that repair a talk.

## Sub lesson 1 — I can't hear you — speak up, please

Sometimes the voice is too small. On the phone or across a room, say:

- "**I can't hear you.**"
- "**Can you speak up, please?**"

These are polite. The other person speaks louder, and the talk lives again.

Whisper game: I whisper a sentence. You ask me to speak up — then say the sentence back.

## Sub lesson 2 — Say it again — repetition and spelling

Sometimes you hear the words but cannot hold them. Ask for them again:

- "**Can you say it again, please?**"
- For a name or a hard word: "**How do you spell it?**" — and the other person spells it letter by letter: "D-i-e-n-g."

Spell your name aloud now. Then ask me to spell mine.

## Sub lesson 3 — What does it mean? — asking about words

Sometimes you hear every word but one word blocks the road:

- "**What does it mean?**" — point at the word: "What does 'double room' mean?"
- The polite softener opens the ask: "**I beg your pardon** — what does it mean?"

Ask me now: pick any word from this page and ask what it means.

## Say it to your companion

Call your companion on an pretend phone: book a double room for two nights. Your companion must whisper once (you repair with speak up), say one name fast (you repair with spelling), and use one hard word (you repair with what does it mean). Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

Write the repair set from memory: one speak-up sentence, one say-it-again sentence, one spelling question, one meaning question with I beg your pardon. Read them aloud as if the line were bad.
