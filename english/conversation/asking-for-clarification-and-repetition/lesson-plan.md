# Lesson plan — Asking for Clarification and Repetition

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** Asking for Clarification and Repetition
**Lesson:** asking-for-clarification-and-repetition — when you did not hear or did not understand: speak up, say it again, what does it mean?
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Conversation
**The one thing:** The repair set for a talk that breaks: asking the other person to speak up, to say it again or spell it, and to tell you what a word means.
**This lesson's own level:** Conversation lesson (repairing a talk): volume requests (speak up, I can't hear you), repetition and spelling requests, meaning requests (what does it mean), and the polite softener I beg your pardon.

## Why this lesson exists

Mauritania's Grade 7 book teaches the repair set as its own lesson, inside a phone call that books a hotel room — the learner must ask the other voice to speak up, to spell a name, and to explain a word, or the booking fails. The course's conversation unit had no lesson for a talk that breaks. This lesson is built from the placements under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06).

## Sub lessons

1. I can't hear you — speak up, please
2. Say it again — repetition and spelling
3. What does it mean? — asking about words

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is needed before this lesson. Telephoning teaches the phone call itself; this lesson teaches what to say when the call goes wrong, and each stands alone.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- Say "I can't hear you. Can you speak up, please?"
- Ask someone to say a word again and to spell it.
- Ask "What does it mean?" about a word you do not know, politely.

## What you need
Nothing special — two voices and one word nobody knows.

## The run of the lesson
1. ADD IMAGE: a learner on the phone, hand to ear, face puzzled. The learner tries one repair sentence before any form appears.
2. Teach sub lesson 1 on a real whisper: the teacher whispers; the learner must ask for volume.
3. ADD GIF (1–2 seconds): a name written letter by letter as it is spelled aloud — spelling is the second repair.
4. Sub lesson 3 runs the hotel call whole: book the room, repair twice on the way, finish the booking.

## Add-ons
- Bad-line game: pairs talk back to back in whispers; every repair sentence used scores a point.
- Word rescue: the teacher uses one hard word per sentence; learners must ask what it means before answering.

## If the learner is stuck
Keep to "Speak up, please" and "Say it again" until asking feels allowed — many learners stay silent instead. The meaning question is one step after.

## Teacher note
The Grade 7 book puts the whole repair set inside one phone call that books a hotel room, so the lesson keeps that call as its running scene: the repairs are taught as the tools that finish a real task.
