# Travel Dialogues

## Do it first

Stand at an imaginary desk and say it: "Hello! I would like a window seat, please." A journey is a chain of small conversations, and they run in the same order everywhere in the world. Learn them as whole dialogues and you can travel.

## Sub lesson 1 — At the airport

*Difficulty: first steps. One desk, five steps, always in the same order.*

The **check-in** conversation at the airport runs like this:

1. **Show your passport.** The worker asks for it; you hand it over.
2. **Choose your seat** — **a window seat** (by the window, for the view) or **an aisle seat** (by the walkway, for the leg room). Say which one you want.
3. **Hand over your luggage** — your bags go on the belt and travel under the plane.
4. **Take your boarding pass** — the card that is your ticket onto the plane.
5. **Find your gate** — the numbered door where your plane waits. The boarding pass tells you the number.

Then, outside, the **airport taxi**: tell the driver where you are going, and ask the price before you ride — in the book's dialogue the ride costs **£30**.

That is the whole airport: passport, seat, luggage, pass, gate — taxi. Five steps and a price.

## Sub lesson 2 — At the hotel

*Difficulty: a step up. The last conversation of the travelling day.*

You arrive, tired, at the **hotel reception**. The conversation runs like this:

1. **Ask for your room** — in the book's dialogue, **a single room** (a room for one person).
2. **Hear the price** — the room costs **£50**.
3. **Pay** — you can **pay by card**.
4. **Fill in the form** — your name and your details, in the hotel's book.
5. **Take the key** — your room is **room 101**, and it is **on the second floor**. Up the stairs or in the lift, turn the key, and the travelling day is done.

Room, price, card, form, key. Five steps again — the same shape as the airport. Journeys are kind to learners: the conversations repeat their shape everywhere.

## Say it to your companion

Run the whole day together. Your companion is the check-in worker, then the taxi driver, then the hotel receptionist; you are the traveller. Passport, seat, luggage, pass, gate — taxi, £30 — room, £50, card, form, key, room 101. Then swap: now you work all three desks.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the check-in dialogue: five lines for the worker and five for you, in order.
2. Write the hotel dialogue: ask for a single room, hear £50, pay by card, fill in the form, take the key to room 101.
3. Say both dialogues aloud from memory — no looking. The airport first, then the hotel.
