# Telephoning — multiple choice

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Answering the call · Sub lesson 2 — Asking for a person · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 4 — Taking and leaving messages · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 5 — Making an appointment by phone, except item 5 (the engaged line), which is Sub lesson 6 — Closing the call.

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** You answer the phone at home. You say —
a) Goodbye  b) Hello, Ana speaking  c) Hold the hotel
**Answer: b) Hello, Ana speaking** — a call opens with
hello and your name (Greece, Unit 2).

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** You want to talk to Mr Manou. You say —
a) Can I speak to Mr Manou, please?  b) Mr Manou is a
phone  c) Speak me Mr Manou
**Answer: a)** — the plain ask for a person (Greece,
Unit 2; Uzbekistan, Unit 8).

**3. (Sub lesson 4)** The person you want is out. The answerer
says —
a) He is a message  b) Can I take a message?  c) The line
is a message
**Answer: b) Can I take a message?** — taking messages is
one of Greece's Unit 2 telephone skills.

**4. (Sub lesson 5)** The most polite way to book by phone is —
a) Give me Tuesday  b) I want Tuesday now  c) Could I
make an appointment for Tuesday, please?
**Answer: c)** — Albania's polite ladder opens with
"Could I…?" (Chapter 34).

**5. (Sub lesson 6)** You hear short beeps and no voice. The
line is —
a) engaged  b) through  c) a directory
**Answer: a) engaged** — Greece's Unit 2 names engaged
lines among its telephone problems.
