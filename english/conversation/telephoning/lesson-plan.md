# Lesson plan — Telephoning

**Course:** English · **Title:** Conversation · **Lesson:** Telephoning
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 5, "Conversation"
(CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a whole phone call in
English — answering, asking for a person, waiting, taking
and leaving a message, and making an appointment by phone.
**What the learner can do at the end:** answer with their
name; ask "Can I speak to …?"; say "Hold on, please" and
"I'll put you through"; take a message with a name and a
number; leave a message; fix a day and time; and close the
call politely.

## Before this lesson
- Soft hint only: Hello and your name opens every call
  nicely. Recommended, never forced — this lesson stands
  alone.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a telephone with its parts
   labelled: receiver, mouthpiece, earpiece, dial, wire,
   ringing.** Written slot: "a picture of a telephone and
   its parts." What the student does: the student points
   at each part and says its name — Uzbekistan's book
   teaches the call by first naming the telephone itself.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of two people on phones, one
   holding up a finger: "Hold on, please."** Written slot:
   "a picture of a caller waiting while the other person
   fetches someone." What the student does: the student
   says the waiting lines aloud — "Hold on, please. I'll
   put you through."
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of a message pad with a name, a
   number and "Please call back" written on it.** Written
   slot: "a picture of a written telephone message." What
   the student does: the student copies the pad and fills
   it with a real name and number from the practice call.
4. **Slot 4 — a picture of a calendar with one day circled
   and a clock beside it.** Written slot: "a picture of an
   appointment day and time being agreed." What the
   student does: the student points at the day and says
   the appointment line: "I'd like to make an appointment
   for Tuesday at ten."

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first** (lesson/lesson.md): a pretend call —
   answer, ask for someone, take a message.
2. **Answering**: "Hello, … speaking." — Greece's hotel
   agent and Uzbekistan's telephone expressions open the
   call the same way.
3. **Asking for a person**: "Can I speak to …?" /
   "Could I speak to …?" — the polite ladder (Albania's
   "Could I…? / I'd like to… / Is it possible to…?").
4. **Waiting and problems**: put through, hold on, the
   line is engaged, cut off, call back (Greece, Unit 2).
5. **Messages**: taking one ("Can I take a message?") and
   leaving one ("Please tell him …"), with spelling a
   name and saying a number slowly (Malaysia's
   message-taking; Barbados's telephone directories).
6. **Appointments by phone**: Albania Chapter 34's three
   ways — in person, by phone, by text — with the polite
   appointment phrases.
7. **Closing**: "Thank you. Goodbye." and the call-back
   promise.
8. **Do it yourself** (the three closing tasks).

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck on numbers: say the number in small pieces, the
  way directory calls do it (Greece's Directory Enquiries
  listening).
- Stuck on the message: a message needs only three
  things — who called, the number, what to do (call back).
- This lesson's neighbours: How are you? (the warm
  opening) and Giving directions (the other half of
  Greece's hotel desk).
