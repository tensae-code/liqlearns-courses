# Giving directions — true or false

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 2 — The route verbs, go straight and turn (item 1 turns on the imperatives) · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 3 — Placing the end, opposite and next to · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 5 — Holding the steps in order, first, then, next.

Say true or false. If it is false, say why.

**1. (Sub lesson 2)** Directions are given with imperatives —
"Turn left", "Go straight" — with no "you" in them.
**True** — Kuwait names imperatives as the grammar for
commands, instructions and directions (Skyline 5B,
Unit 7).

**2. (Sub lesson 3)** "Opposite" means the same as "next to".
**False** — opposite is facing across; next to is
beside. Rwanda teaches both in one set, as different
words (P.4, Unit 3).

**3. (Sub lesson 5)** A good route gives every step at once
with no order words.
**False** — Malaysia's model route holds its steps in
order with sequence connectors and turn language; order
is what lets a stranger carry the route (Form 1,
Chapter 13).
