# Lesson plan — Giving directions

**Course:** English · **Title:** Conversation · **Lesson:** Giving directions
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Conversation
(CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** asking the way and
giving a route — straight, turn, past, opposite — until
the other person can walk it.
**What the learner can do at the end:** ask "Excuse me,
where's the …?"; give a route in order with imperatives
(Go straight, Turn left, Go past…); place a building
(opposite, next to, in front of, behind); and say how far
and how long ("It's just around the corner", "about five
minutes").

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Asking the way** — every route starts with a polite ask: "Excuse me. Where's the post office?" — and Libya's four asking shapes over a street map. (Was part of depth Level 1.)
2. **The route verbs, go straight and turn** — a route is a chain of imperatives: go straight, turn left, turn right, go along, go past, cross. (Was part of depth Level 1, with depth Level 2's route verbs seated here.)
3. **Placing the end, opposite and next to** — a route must land somewhere exact: "It's on your left / on the right", and the place words — opposite, next to, near, along, across, behind, in front of, in between. (Was depth Level 2.)
4. **How far, how long** — the distance answer: "It's far from here", "It's just around the corner", "How long does it take?" — "About five minutes." (Was part of depth Level 3.)
5. **Holding the steps in order, first, then, next** — long routes need glue — first, then, next: Malaysia's model routes hold three steps, a connector, three steps. (Was part of depth Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Soft hint only: The town (Unit 7) names the places.
  Recommended, never forced — this lesson stands alone.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a simple town map: a hotel, a
   bank, a post office, a station, a park, with two
   streets crossing.** Written slot: "a picture of a town
   map for directions." What the student does: the
   student puts a finger on "here" and traces the route
   as the directions are said — Libya's street map and
   Rwanda's Butare map, worked the same way.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of a person walking straight,
   then turning left at a corner, in three frames.**
   Written slot: "a picture of go straight, then turn
   left." What the student does: the student walks the
   three frames in the room, saying each step as they do
   it — Cambodia's Unit 24 lesson C is named for it:
   "Turn left, turn right."
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of a school with buildings
   opposite it, next to it, in front of it and behind
   it, each labelled.** Written slot: "a picture of
   opposite, next to, in front of, behind." What the
   student does: the student points at each label and
   says the place sentence — Rwanda's "adverbs of place"
   set, learned by pointing.
4. **Slot 4 — a picture of a signpost: post office, just
   around the corner.** Written slot: "a picture of a
   place just around the corner." What the student does:
   the student answers the how-far question both ways:
   "It's just around the corner" and "It's far from
   here" — Saudi Arabia's pair.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first** (lesson/lesson.md): direct your
   companion across the room to a hidden thing, voice
   only.
2. **Asking the way**: "Excuse me. Where's the post
   office?" (Saudi Arabia, Unit 11; Libya's four map
   questions).
3. **The route verbs**: go straight, turn left/right, go
   along, go past, cross (Cambodia Grade 7, Unit 24;
   Mongolia's direction dialogues).
4. **Placing the end**: on your left/right; opposite,
   next to, in front of, behind, in between (Rwanda's
   place set; Cambodia's position prepositions).
5. **How far, how long**: just around the corner, far
   from here, about five minutes, How long does it take?
   (Saudi Arabia; Libya's "about five minutes away").
6. **Imperatives do the work**: the route is a chain of
   orders with no you — Kuwait's grammar point, taught
   exactly for directions (Skyline 5B, Unit 7).
7. **A whole route**: Airil directs Howard in Melaka
   (Malaysia, Chapter 13) — sequence connectors holding
   the steps in order.
8. **Do it yourself** (the three closing tasks).

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck on left and right: hold up your hands — the left
  hand makes an L. Then say the route again.
- Stuck mid-route: a route is never more than three steps
  at a time: straight — turn — place. Give three, let
  them walk, give three more.
- This lesson's neighbours: Telephoning (Greece's hotel
  desk fields both calls and directions) and The town
  (the place words, in Unit 7).
