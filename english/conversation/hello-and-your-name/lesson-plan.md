# Lesson plan — Hello and your name

**Course:** English · **Title:** Conversation · **Lesson:** Hello and
your name
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Conversation —
the first lesson of that unit (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** how a greeting goes — hello
opens it, names go in the middle, goodbye closes it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written the same way:
**"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the
student does with it**. Written like that, the slot works twice:
while the course is being built (and while the AI learns from the
books — a picture book can only be learned from by describing its
pictures), the words ARE the picture; and when the lesson goes on the
website, the real image, GIF, or video is dropped into that slot,
with the little explanation staying under it as the student's guide.
Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with
its source).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Saying hello** — what a greeting is, and the word that opens it: "Hello!" (Was part of depth Level 1.)
2. **Names in the middle** — "What's your name?", the whole-sentence answer "My name is ___", "I'm" for "I am", and "Nice to meet you" answered back with "…, too", with the handshake. (Was part of depth Level 1.)
3. **Saying goodbye** — "Goodbye!" closes the greeting; the whole shape is told back (open — names — close), and the lesson text states its whole-sentence point about the name answer here, after the shape. (Was part of depth Level 1; old depth Level 2 — the whole greeting used with a real person — is proved here, at the shape's end.)
4. **The same greeting in other countries** — Libya opens the same door, and Nicaragua teaches the shape with two hands — right hand Hello, left hand Goodbye. The whole shape again, so this sub lesson overlaps Sub lessons 1–3. (Was part of depth Level 1 — the same basics, from other books.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the Pencil and Lines course (so the learner can
  do the writing step at the end). Recommended, never forced.
- Nothing else needed. This is a speaking lesson; it works before the
  learner can read the sentences, because the shapes are spoken first.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Start a greeting: say "Hello!"
2. Ask "What's your name?" and answer with a whole sentence:
   "My name is ___."
3. Say "Nice to meet you" — and answer it back with "…, too."
4. Close with "Goodbye!"
5. Tell the three parts of any greeting: open — names — close.

## What you need
One other person, or the learner's explain companion. Nothing else —
no book, no tools.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner stands up and does the whole greeting
   for real before any explaining (lesson/lesson.md, "Do it first").
   - ADD IMAGE at the top of the lesson: a picture of two children
     meeting at a school door, one waving hello — the student looks
     at it and says which child is starting the greeting.
2. **The explanation** — the greeting as a shape: open, middle, close;
   the Maya-and-Hana talk line by line; "I'm" = "I am"
   (lesson/lesson.md, "Then the explanation").
   - ADD IMAGE under the shape: a picture of three panels in a row —
     hello / names / goodbye — the student points at each panel and
     says what happens in it.
   - ADD GIF at the "Nice to meet you" line: a picture that moves —
     two hands shaking, again and again — the student copies the
     handshake with their companion on the same line.
   - ADD AUDIO: the whole Maya-and-Hana talk read aloud — the
     student listens once, then listens again and says each line
     after it.
3. **The same lesson from other countries** — Libya opens the same
   door; Nicaragua teaches it with two hands, right hand Hello, left
   hand Goodbye (lesson/lesson.md). Taught in 3 countries — the
   acceptance count lives in lesson/sources.md.
   - ADD IMAGE: a picture of two hands side by side, the right one
     marked Hello, the left one Goodbye (Nicaragua's way) — the
     student holds up their own hands the same way and says hello
     with the right, goodbye with the left.
4. **Say it to your companion** — read the talk aloud in both parts,
   then again with the learner's own name; explain the three parts
   back to the companion.
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed —
     two children acting the whole greeting in the super-simple
     voice — the student watches once, then acts it along with them.
5. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
6. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
7. **Do It Yourself** — greet a real person today, twice; then write
   the five sentences, one on each line (lesson/lesson.md, end).

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- IMAGE — a picture of two children meeting at a school door, one
  waving hello → the student says which child starts the greeting.
- IMAGE — a picture of three panels: hello / names / goodbye → the
  student points and says what happens in each.
- GIF — a picture that moves: two hands shaking → the student copies
  the handshake on "Nice to meet you."
- AUDIO — the Maya-and-Hana talk read aloud → the student listens and
  repeats each line.
- IMAGE — a picture of two hands, right marked Hello, left marked
  Goodbye → the student holds up their own hands the same way.
- VIDEO — the video-script acted out (made later, when videos are
  made) → the student watches, then acts along.

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the
plan is the build sheet, so nothing the lesson needs gets forgotten.

## If the learner is stuck
- Can't remember the order → go back to the shape: open, names, close.
- Answers with just a name ("Maya") → the books teach the whole
  sentence; practice "My name is ___" as one piece.
- This lesson's see-also: Good morning and good afternoon (greeting
  by the time of day — a different thing, its own lesson).
