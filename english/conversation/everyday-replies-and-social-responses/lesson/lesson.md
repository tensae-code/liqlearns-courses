# Everyday Replies and Social Responses

## Do it first

Imagine this. A girl walks up to you, smiling. "Hi! How are
you?" She knows you. You know her face. But her **name** — it is
gone. What do you say? Try. Say something out loud, anything.
Trying comes first here; then we learn what to say in this
moment, and in the other small awkward moments of everyday
life.

## Sub lesson 1 — When you forget a name

It happens to everyone. The name is gone — but the moment is
easy to fix, if you know the words.

**Say sorry, and ask kindly.** Do not pretend, and do not run
away. Smile and say it plainly:

- "I'm sorry — I've forgotten your name."
- "Sorry, tell me your name again?"

**Then use the name at once.** When she says "I'm Sara," answer
with her name in your next words: "Sara! Of course. How are
you, Sara?" Using the name straight away fixes it in your
memory — and it shows her you are glad to know it.

**If your own name is forgotten, help.** When someone looks at
you with that worried face, give your name with a smile before
they ask: "Hi, I'm Tomas — we met at school." It costs nothing
and it saves their moment.

Practise in pairs. One partner walks up, smiling, and greets
the other by name. The other has "forgotten" — say sorry, ask
kindly, and use the name the moment it comes back.

*Difficulty: first steps — three short wordings, each practised in the moment itself.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Awkward moments in public

**The escalator rule.** On an escalator, people **stand on the
right** and **walk on the left**. Stand on the right, hold the
rail, and leave the left side free for people in a hurry. If
you find yourself standing on the left with someone behind you,
the words are small and kind:

- "Sorry!" — and step to the right.
- "Excuse me." — when you pass on the left yourself.

**Small accidents.** You bump into someone in a corridor. You
stand in a doorway and block the way. You drop your books in
front of a stranger. Every one of these has the same small kit
of words:

- "Sorry!" — for the bump.
- "Excuse me." — for the block, as you move.
- "After you." — with a smile, letting the other person go
  first.

The rule under all of it: say the small word at once, with a
smile, and move. A quick "sorry" turns an awkward moment into
nothing at all.

Practise: walk a corridor in pairs. Bump (gently!). Block a
doorway. Pass on the stairs. Use the words every time, out
loud, until they come without thinking.

*Difficulty: building — the words are short; the skill is saying them at once, in the moment.*

## Say it to your companion

Explain to your companion, in your own words, what to do in the
two moments of this lesson: the forgotten name, and standing
the wrong way on an escalator. Then act them: your companion
plays the moment, and you must answer with the right words
before they count to three.

## Do It Yourself

1. Next time you forget a name — and it will happen — use the
   lesson's words: say sorry, ask kindly, and use the name at
   once when it comes back.
2. Ride an escalator or climb busy stairs. Stand on the right.
   Count how many people pass on the left.
3. Use "Excuse me" and "After you" once each today, on purpose,
   and notice how people answer.
