# At the Doctor's

## Do it first

You are sick. You walk into the clinic. The doctor asks: "What is the problem? How long have you felt this?" Could you answer — in English? After this lesson, yes: you will know the words of the visit and the shape of the talk, from the appointment to the dose.

## Sub lesson 1 — Words for the visit

*Very simple. Can-do: The visit words defined: appointment, diagnosis, prescription, dose, medical test — and the symptoms you name first (cold, allergy, food poisoning, cough, runny nose, stomachache, chills, sneeze, diarrhea, fever, headache, dizziness).*

Five words run every medical visit:

An **appointment** — the time fixed for you to see the doctor. A **medical test** — a check the doctor orders to find out more: blood, temperature, an X-ray. A **diagnosis** — the doctor's answer: the name of what is wrong with you. A **prescription** — the paper (or message) that says which medicine you must take. A **dose** — how much medicine, and how often: "one tablet, twice a day."

And before all five, you name your symptoms: a cold, an allergy, food poisoning, a cough, a runny nose, a stomachache, chills, sneezing, diarrhea, a fever, a headache, dizziness.

Try it now: match them — the doctor writes it (prescription); the doctor's answer (diagnosis); the amount you take (dose).

## Sub lesson 2 — In the consultation

*Simple. Can-do: Run the visit as a dialogue: say your problem and how long ("a bad cough and a fever since yesterday"), answer the doctor's questions (medicines taken, allergies), and take the advice — rest and take the medication.*

Now watch a whole visit. Mr Smith sees Dr Taylor:

— "I have had a bad cough and a fever **since yesterday**. I think I might have the flu." (Say the problem, and since when — *since* + the starting time.)
— The doctor asks what he has taken: "I took an aspirin last night." And about allergies: "I have no medication allergies." Answer honestly — the doctor's questions keep you safe.
— The doctor checks (a slight fever), gives the diagnosis, and the advice: rest, and take the medication in its dose.

Your turn to speak the patient's part: problem + since when + what you took + allergies. Try it now with a partner: one is the patient with a stomachache since Monday; one is the doctor who asks, checks and advises.

## Say it to your companion

Read this lesson aloud to your companion. Then act the consultation: you are the patient (a cough and fever since yesterday); your companion asks the doctor's questions.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the five visit words and their meanings from memory.
2. Practise the patient's four answers: problem, since when, medicine taken, allergies.
3. Make an appointment card: your name, the doctor, the day and the time.
4. Say the dose sentence for a medicine you know: "One tablet, twice a day."
