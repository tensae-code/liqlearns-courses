# Everyday Gesture Phrases

## Do it first

Your friend shows you a drawing. It is really good — what do you say? And what does your hand do while you say it?

Some English phrases are only half the message. The other half is a gesture: your hand, your shoulders, your face. In this lesson the words and the action always come together — say the phrase, do the gesture.

## Sub lesson 1 — Answering with words and actions

Four phrases you answer with — each one paired with its action:

**Good!** — thumbs up. You say it when something is done well: a good drawing, a good answer, a good game.

**Good idea!** — you say it when someone's plan sounds good, before anyone has done it. "Let's play outside." — "Good idea!"

**I don't know.** — shoulders up, hands open. You say it when the answer is not in you. The shrug IS part of the phrase: people read the shoulders before they hear the words.

**Me?** — point at your own chest. Someone calls a name or points your way, and you ask: is it me you mean? "Me?"

*Difficulty: first steps — six short phrases, each learned as words + gesture together.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Calling and celebrating

Two phrases that move people:

**Come here.** — beckon with your hand: fingers waving toward you. You say it when you want someone to come to where you are.

**Give me five!** — hold your open palm up high. Your friend slaps it with their open palm — five fingers against five fingers. You say it to celebrate: something went well, and the slap shares it.

*Difficulty: first steps — the same rule again: the words and the action are one piece; neither half works alone.*

## Say it to your companion

Teach your companion all six phrases. For each one, say what it means, show its gesture, and give one moment when you would use it. Then let your companion test you: they do a gesture, you say the phrase.

## Do It Yourself

1. Stand up. Say each phrase out loud WITH its gesture: Good! · Good idea! · I don't know. · Me? · Come here. · Give me five!
2. Draw the six gestures as six little pictures, and write the phrase under each one.
3. Teach the six phrases to someone at home — words and gestures together.
