# Making offers — multiple choice

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Offering a thing with Would you like · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Offering help with Shall I, except item 4 (the shop offer), which is Sub lesson 3 — The shop offer, Can I help you · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 (telling an offer from a request is taught there).

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Your guest looks thirsty. You offer a drink:
a) Would you like some juice?  b) Can I have some juice?  c) Give me juice.
**Answer: a) Would you like some juice?** — the offer shape is "Would you like…?" (Mongolia, English VI, p.90).

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** "Would you like some milk?" — the yes-answer is:
a) Yes, I do.  b) Yes, I would.  c) Yes, I am.
**Answer: b) Yes, I would.** — the short answers ride on *would* (Syria, Starters 5, Unit 2; Mongolia, English VI, p.90).

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Your friend is carrying two heavy bags. You offer help:
a) Shall I carry that?  b) Can you carry that?  c) You carry that.
**Answer: a) Shall I carry that?** — "Shall I…?" offers your own hands (Malaysia, Academy Stars Year 6, p.56; Mongolia, English VII, Unit 2).

**4. (Sub lesson 3)** A customer walks into your shop. Your first words are:
a) What do you want?  b) Can I help you?  c) Come here.
**Answer: b) Can I help you?** — the shop offer (Cambodia, Grade 10, Unit 16).

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Which one is an OFFER, not a request?
a) Could you pass me that knife, please?  b) Can I have some water?  c) Would you like some water?
**Answer: c) Would you like some water?** — in an offer the thing moves from you; in a request it moves to you (Cambodia, Grade 10, Unit 16).
