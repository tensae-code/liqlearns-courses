# Lesson plan — Making offers

**Course:** English · **Title:** Conversation · **Lesson:** Making offers
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 5, "Conversation" (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** making an offer — giving
someone a thing or your help before they have to ask — with
"Would you like…?", "Shall I…?" and "Can I help you?", and the
polite answers that go with them.
**What the learner can do at the end:** offer a drink, food or
help with "Would you like…?" and "Shall I…?"; offer help in a
shop or at a door with "Can I help you?"; and answer an offer
politely — "Yes, please," "Yes, I would," or "No, thank you."

## Before this lesson
- If you know How are you?, this will feel easier — offers often
  come right after the greeting. Recommended, never forced.
- Asking for things for yourself ("Can I have…?") is a different
  thing — it lives in Asking politely, next door in this level.
  Here you give; there you ask.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a host holding a tray with two
   glasses of juice, offering one to a guest.** Written slot:
   "a picture of someone offering a guest a glass of juice."
   What the student does: before reading on, the student says
   the offer out loud — "Would you like some juice?" — because
   an offer is said first and explained after.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of a shop worker smiling at a customer
   who has just walked in.** Written slot: "a picture of a
   shop worker greeting a customer." What the student does:
   the student says the worker's first words — "Can I help
   you?" — the offer that opens every shop talk.
3. **Slot 3 — a short animation (3–4 seconds) of a friend
   carrying two heavy bags, and a second friend reaching for
   one of them.** Written slot: "a picture that moves: a
   friend offering to carry one of two heavy bags." What the
   student does: the student calls out the helping offer —
   "Shall I carry that?" — at the moment the hand reaches out.
4. **Slot 4 — a picture of two answer cards, one green
   ("Yes, please") and one soft red ("No, thank you").**
   Written slot: "a picture of the two polite answers to an
   offer." What the student does: the student points at the
   true card each time the companion makes an offer.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first** (lesson/lesson.md): offer your companion a
   real thing — a drink, a seat, your help with a bag — before
   any rule is named.
2. **The offer of a thing**: "Would you like…?" + the thing —
   Syria's explorer question ("Would you like to be an
   explorer? — Yes, I would. / No, I wouldn't.") and Mongolia's
   food offer ("Would you like some milk?").
3. **The offer of help**: "Shall I…?" and "Would you like me
   to…?" — Malaysia's Year 6 offer pair; Mongolia Grade 7's
   "shall offers."
4. **The shop offer**: "Can I help you?" — Cambodia Grade 10's
   requests-and-offers unit, which teaches telling a request
   from an offer.
5. **The answers**: yes with thanks ("Yes, please. / Yes, I
   would. / Thank you."), no with thanks ("No, thank you.") —
   Cambodia's accepting and rejecting politely; Nepal Grade
   8's offers unit practises making and accepting offers and
   sends learners out to collect offer expressions.
6. **Do it yourself** (the three closing tasks in the lesson):
   three real offers today, one shop offer answered, and an
   offer collection like Nepal's project.

## If the learner is stuck
- Answers an offer with a bare "No" → the answer always
  carries its thanks: "No, thank you" is the whole answer.
- Mixes offer and request → Cambodia's own test: if the thing
  moves FROM you, it is an offer; if it moves TO you, it is a
  request (Asking politely's lesson).
- This lesson's neighbours: Making suggestions (offering an
  idea, not a thing); Asking politely (asking, not giving).
