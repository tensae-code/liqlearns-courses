# Lesson plan — Giving advice

**Course:** English · **Title:** Conversation · **Lesson:** Giving advice
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 5, "Conversation"
(CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** giving advice —
helping words for a problem — at the right strength, from
the gentle "How about…?" to the strong "You'd better…".
**What the learner can do at the end:** ask for advice
("What should I do?"); give it with should / How about…? /
Why don't you…? / If I were you, I would…; climb Libya's
advice strength scale on purpose; and use had better for
the advice that carries a warning.

## Before this lesson
- Soft hint only: How are you feeling work names the
  problems advice answers. Recommended, never forced —
  this lesson stands alone.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a ladder with five rungs,
   labelled with advice lines from soft (bottom) to
   strong (top).** Written slot: "a picture of the advice
   strength ladder." What the student does: the student
   points at a rung and says its line — Libya's
   strength box, learned as a climb.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of a boy with a toothache,
   holding his cheek.** Written slot: "a picture of a
   tooth that hurts." What the student does: the student
   gives the dentist advice aloud: "You should go to a
   dentist" — Albania's own sentence.
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of someone washing spinach under
   a tap.** Written slot: "a picture of washing food
   before cooking." What the student does: the student
   says the warning advice: "You'd better wash it first"
   — Libya's cooking dialogue.
4. **Slot 4 — a picture of two friends at a table, one
   telling a money problem, one answering.** Written
   slot: "a picture of a friend giving saving advice."
   What the student does: the student gives the advice
   three ways — should, How about…?, If I were in your
   shoes… — Malaysia's four problem situations, acted.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first** (lesson/lesson.md): one real small
   problem — advise your companion in your own language.
2. **Asking**: "What should I do?" (Albania's health
   advice shape).
3. **The gentle shapes**: How about…? / Why don't you…?
   (Nepal, Grade 7, Unit 5; Malaysia, Form 1, Chapter 12).
4. **should**: the middle rung — "You should go to a
   dentist if your tooth hurts" (Albania, Chapter 34).
5. **If I were you**: stepping into the other person's
   shoes — Nepal's forms; Malaysia's "If I were in your
   shoes…"; Libya's Lesson "If I were you, I wouldn't
   worry."
6. **The strength scale**: Libya Grade 12's box — Don't… /
   You should… / It's a good idea to… / It's important
   to… / It's best to….
7. **had better**: advice with a warning inside — Libya
   Grade 11's "You'd better wash it first"; India's
   Beehive sorting of had better into advice, order and
   threat; Malaysia Form 5's grammar entry.
8. **Do it yourself** (the three closing tasks).

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck on strength: ask "what happens if they don't?"
  Nothing much — stay low on the ladder. Something bad —
  climb to had better.
- Stuck on If I were you: the sentence finishes with
  would — "If I were you, I would rest." Jordan's books
  give advice with should only ("You should work
  carefully") — a safe rung to fall back to, as filed.
- This lesson's neighbours: Congratulating and wishing
  (answering news) and the health-words lesson (the
  problems themselves).
