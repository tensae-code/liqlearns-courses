# Lesson plan — Asking politely

**Course:** English · **Title:** Conversation · **Lesson:** Asking politely
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 5, "Conversation" (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** asking politely — the
request ladder: casual requests with "Can you…? / Will
you…?", formal requests with "Could you…? / Would you…? /
Would you mind…?", and *please* in its seat — choosing the
rung that fits the person and the moment.
**What the learner can do at the end:** ask a friend for
help casually; ask a teacher, an elder or a stranger
formally; soften any request with please; and sort a mixed
set of requests onto the right rungs, the way Cambodia's
books set the sorting task.

## Before this lesson
- If you know Making offers, this will feel easier — a
  request is the offer's mirror: the thing moves TO you.
  Recommended, never forced.
- Requests for permission ("Can I…?") wear the same ladder
  but ask a different thing; they live with permission's
  own lessons. Here the request asks someone to DO
  something for you.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a ladder with two wide rungs:
   the low rung says CAN YOU, the high rung says COULD
   YOU / WOULD YOU MIND.** Written slot: "a picture of the
   request ladder, casual below, formal above." What the
   student does: the student points at the rung that fits
   each person the companion names — friend low, stranger
   high.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of a wedding table, a guest asking
   for the vegetables to be passed.** Written slot: "a
   picture of a wedding guest asking politely for the
   vegetables" — Cambodia's own story. What the student
   does: the student says the request at the formal rung
   before reading on: "Would you mind passing the
   vegetables, please?"
3. **Slot 3 — a short animation (3–4 seconds) of the word
   PLEASE flying in and landing at the end of a bare
   request, softening its colour.** Written slot: "a
   picture that moves: please landing at the end of a
   request." What the student does: the student says the
   request twice — bare, then with please — and hears the
   difference land.
4. **Slot 4 — a picture of a teacher's desk and a friend's
   shoulder, side by side.** Written slot: "a picture of
   the two hearers: the teacher and the friend." What the
   student does: the student asks each picture for the
   same help, changing rung as the hearer changes.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first** (lesson/lesson.md): ask your companion
   for three real things — casually first, then climb the
   ladder and ask again.
2. **The casual rung**: "Can you…? / Will you…?" — for
   friends and family (Cambodia Grade 8's own notes name
   them casual).
3. **The formal rung**: "Could you…? / Would you…? / Would
   you mind…?" — for teachers, elders, strangers, and
   formal moments like Cambodia's wedding story; the
   *-ing* after Would you mind.
4. **Please, and where it sits**: inside or at the end
   ("Could you pass me that knife, please?" — Cambodia
   Grade 10); thank you when you accept (its teacher's
   book's "Focus on").
5. **The same ladder in other books**: Malaysia Form 1's
   polite formulae for requesting (Chapter 2); Syria
   Grade 6's free-time exponents ("Can/Could you…?").
6. **Do it yourself**: one request at each rung to the
   right hearer, a re-asked request climbed a rung, and
   the sorting game played with real requests from your
   day.

## If the learner is stuck
- Uses "Can you" to a stranger and feels it land rough →
  climb one rung: Could you…, and add please. The ladder
  is the fix, not an apology.
- Puts the plain verb after Would you mind ("mind pass")
  → mind carries -ing: "Would you mind passing…?"
- This lesson's neighbours: Making offers (the mirror
  lesson); Apologising (the other lesson where care for
  the hearer is the grammar).
