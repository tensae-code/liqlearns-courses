# Hands-on — Asking politely

You do it, we guide you. Everything here is done in the learner's
real day, with a companion or the Explain Companion.

1. **The sorting game (Cambodia's own task).** Cambodia's Grade 8
   book and its teacher's guide sort requests into casual and
   formal. Write eight requests on cards — four Can you / Will
   you, four Could you / Would you / Would you mind. Shuffle,
   deal, and sort them onto two piles. Then deal the SITUATIONS
   (a friend, a teacher, a grandmother, a stranger at a wedding)
   and match each pile to its people.
2. **The wedding table.** Recreate Cambodia's wedding story at
   any meal: everything sits just out of reach, so every thing
   must be requested — at the formal rung, with please —
   "Would you mind passing the vegetables, please?" A bare
   request means the dish does not move.
3. **Climb the ladder.** Take one real need — help with a heavy
   door. Ask it three times, climbing: to your friend ("Can
   you…?"), to your teacher ("Could you…, please?"), to an
   elder you respect ("Would you mind…?"). Notice what changes:
   the words, not the need.
4. **The day's requests.** For one day, write down every request
   you make or hear. At night, sort them by rung and mark any
   that landed on the wrong rung — and write how you would say
   that one again.
