**Acceptance count: taught in 20 countries (Egypt, Libya, China, Mongolia, Indonesia, Iran, Sierra Leone, Jordan, Greece, Iraq, Ethiopia, South Africa, Syria, South Sudan, Nepal, Armenia, Cambodia, Sudan, Rwanda, Malaysia).**
China teaches the pair inside its colours unit in the first
primary book; Mongolia stocks the day-part ground the greeting
stands on — one boy's whole day told in clock pictures — from a
book with no printed English at all. Indonesia, Iran and Sierra
Leone joined 2026-11-03: Indonesia teaches the greetings as a
set of four with evening and night; Iran teaches the pair with
a teacher's title and name; Sierra Leone matches the greetings
to the times of day in its first lesson and opens every lesson
with the pair. Jordan joined 2026-11-03 carrying the morning
half only — "Good morning!" in its Grade 1 Welcome, the same
morning-only shape Egypt's rows in this file carry; no
afternoon greeting is printed in its filed books.
Greece joined 2026-11-03 carrying the morning half only
too — a full-day story of good-morning, hello and good-night
exchanges in its Grade 2 workpack, and "good morning" in ten
languages in its Grade 5 appendix; no afternoon greeting is
printed in its filed books either.
Iraq joined 2026-11-03 carrying the morning half only
too — "Good morning" with a classroom conversation in
its Grade 1 Teacher's Book (Track 31); no afternoon
greeting is printed in its filed books either.
Ethiopia joined 2026-11-03 with the morning greeting
named — a "Good morning" song and greeting exchange in
Grade 1 Unit 1 — and with times of day taught with
their matching greetings in Unit 2; the Ethiopia filing
does not quote the words "Good afternoon" themselves,
so its stock is worded as morning named plus
times-of-day greetings.
Syria joined 2026-11-03 carrying the morning half only
too — "Good morning" stands among the greeting formulas
of its Grade 1 first unit and is practised in its
Workbook; no afternoon greeting is printed in its
filed books either.
South Sudan joined 2026-11-03 with the pair itself, in
full — unlike the morning-only countries above, its
Primary 1 greetings unit teaches "Good afternoon" as
its own exchange ("Good afternoon, John.", printed
p.3) beside "Good morning", and its Teacher's Guide
plans the two as consecutive sections.
Counts grow as other closed countries' first books are read.
Nepal joined 2026-11-03 with the pair itself, in full — its Grade 2 book opens on good morning / afternoon / evening and good night as one greeting set (Unit 1, printed pp.1–7), over Grade 1's "Good morning, Miss" (Unit 1, printed pp.1–6); its Grade 1 teacher's guide also practises good morning/afternoon in its own opening lessons, cited for its own teaching as a guide to a different, unheld workbook (Lessons 1–5, printed pp.50–96).
Armenia joined 2026-11-03 with guide-only partial stock, recorded plainly as such: its pupil books teach hello/hi/goodbye but print no "Good morning/Good afternoon" exchange; the stock is the Grade 7 Methodological Guide's "Classroom Language Exponents" list (printed p.48), which names "Good morning/ afternoon/ evening (everyone/ class)" as teacher greeting phrases.
