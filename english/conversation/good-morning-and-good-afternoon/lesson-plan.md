# Lesson plan — Good morning and good afternoon

**Course:** English · **Title:** Conversation · **Lesson:** Good morning
and good afternoon
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Conversation
(CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the greeting changes with the
time of day — "Good morning" before midday, "Good afternoon" after.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source).

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Saying good morning** — the greeting for the morning — from when you wake up until midday: "Good morning", with Egypt's picture story of it. (Was part of depth Level 1.)
2. **Saying good afternoon** — the greeting for the afternoon — after midday, while the day is still bright: "Good afternoon"; the closing line sums up both greetings (the time of day chooses), so it overlaps Sub lesson 1. (Was part of depth Level 1.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Hello and your name: plain "Hello" works all day; this lesson adds
  the greetings that know the time. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say "Good morning" at the right time — waking until midday.
2. Say "Good afternoon" at the right time — after midday while the
   day is bright.
3. Look at the time and choose the right greeting without help.

## What you need
A clock or the sky. One person to greet, or the explain companion.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — look at the time right now, before midday or
   after it, and say the greeting that fits this moment, out loud
   (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE at the top of the lesson: a picture of the sun's road
     across one day — rising on the left, high at midday in the
     middle, bright afternoon on the right — with "Good morning"
     written under the first part and "Good afternoon" under the
     second — the student points at where the sun is right now and
     says that part's greeting.
2. **The explanation** — Libya Grade 1 teaches the two greetings in
   its second lesson; Egypt's youngest book shows "Good morning" in
   a picture story (arriving at school) and KG2 puts "Hello / Good
   morning" side by side (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE under Egypt's picture story: a picture of a child
     arriving at school in the morning, saying "Good morning" — the
     student says what time of day the picture shows, and how they
     can tell.
   - ADD AUDIO: the two greetings read aloud, morning then
     afternoon, each twice — the student repeats each one and says
     its time.
3. **Say it to your companion** — it is 9 in the morning: what do you
   say? It is 3 in the afternoon: what do you say? Explain why the
   greeting changed and the door did not.
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed —
     the same door greeted at two times of day, in the super-simple
     voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — tomorrow, greet the first person you meet
   with the right greeting for that moment; after midday, greet
   someone with "Good afternoon"; write both down with their times.

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- IMAGE — a picture of the sun's road across the day, with the two
  greetings under their parts → the student points at "now" and says
  its greeting.
- IMAGE — a picture of a child arriving at school in the morning →
  the student says the time of day and how they can tell.
- AUDIO — the two greetings read aloud → the student repeats each
  and says its time.
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later) → the student greets
  along at both times.

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the
plan is the build sheet, so nothing the lesson needs gets forgotten.

## If the learner is stuck
- Says "Good morning" all day → the sun's road: the greeting follows
  the sun, not the habit. Midday is the fence.
- Wonders about "Goodbye" on Egypt's page → that is the closing
  word, a different lesson (Hello and your name); this lesson is
  only about which opening the time chooses.
