# Dialogue and Discussion

## Do it first

Turn to the person beside you. Talk together about your way to
school — what you see, who you meet, how long it takes. You
have one minute each. Go.

Did it work? One of you talked more. One of you listened more.
Maybe you both talked at once. This lesson is the moves that
make a talk work — the books teach them as their own thing,
from the first year of school to the last.

## Sub lesson 1 — Talking in pairs

A talk is built by two people, in turns.

- One speaks. The other listens.
- Then you change: the listener speaks, the speaker listens.
- When your partner finishes, answer **what they said** — not
  a new thing of your own. "You said the road is long. I think
  so too, because …"

Try it: "Tell me about your friend." Listen. Answer what they
said. Then change places.

*Difficulty: first steps — short turns, one topic, the answer-what-was-said rule held from the start.*

## Sub lesson 2 — The good listener and the good speaker

The books name these two people outright. Here is what each
one does.

**A good listener:**
- looks at the speaker;
- does not speak while the other is speaking;
- listens for the idea, not only the words;
- can say back what the speaker said.

**A good speaker:**
- speaks clearly, not too fast;
- says one idea at a time;
- looks at the listener;
- stops and lets the listener answer.

Practise in pairs: one speaks for half a minute. The listener
then says back what they heard. If the saying-back is wrong,
the speaker says it again, more clearly. Change places.

*Difficulty: building — the pair checks each other: the listener's saying-back proves the listening, not a teacher's mark.*

## Sub lesson 3 — Conversations and role play

A whole conversation has a shape:

1. **Opening** — a greeting or a first question.
2. **Middle** — questions and answers, each answer answering
   what was asked; statements answered by the other person.
3. **Closing** — a last line that ends the talk kindly.

**Role play** is a conversation acted out: each person takes
a part — a customer and a shopkeeper, a visitor and a guide —
and the talk is built between the parts. The JSS books even
read a play's dialogue aloud in parts this way: the words are
given, and the talking still has to work.

Practise: in pairs, act the customer and the shopkeeper. Open,
ask and answer in the middle, close kindly. Then change parts
and act it again.

*Difficulty: building — whole talks now, with an opening, a middle that answers itself, and a closing.*

## Sub lesson 4 — Discussion and debate

A **discussion** is a whole group talking one topic through.
Each person gives a view **with a reason**:

- "I think school should start later, **because** children
  sleep too little."
- "I agree with Ama, because …" / "I see it another way:
  … **because** …"

A **debate** is a discussion with sides. One side speaks for
the question, the other against it:

1. The first side gives its reasons.
2. The second side answers those reasons and gives its own.
3. Each side speaks again, answering what the other said.
4. The talk closes; the listeners judge which side answered
   better — not which side shouted louder.

Hold a class debate: "School uniforms: for or against?"
Split the class, prepare reasons with **because**, and debate
it the four steps above.

*Difficulty: deepest — views with reasons, answers to the other side's reasons, and a closing judgment on the answering, not the volume.*

## Say it to your companion

Explain the whole lesson to your companion: the pair rule,
the good listener, the good speaker, the shape of a
conversation, and the difference between a discussion and a
debate. Your companion asks one question; answer what they
asked. Then change places.

## Do It Yourself

1. With a partner, hold a two-minute conversation about your
   families. Open it, keep the middle answering itself, close
   it kindly.
2. Write down three views on "Homework: yes or no?" — yours,
   and two you do not hold — each with a **because**.
3. In a group of four, debate one question for ten minutes,
   the sub lesson 4 way. Afterwards, each person says back
   the strongest reason the other side gave.
