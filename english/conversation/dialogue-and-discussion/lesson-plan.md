# Lesson plan — Dialogue and Discussion

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** Dialogue and Discussion
**Lesson:** dialogue-and-discussion — talking with another person on purpose — taking turns, listening, answering, discussing and debating
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Conversation
**The one thing:** a talk is two people building one thing together: one speaks, the other truly listens and answers what was said
**This lesson's own level:** Conversation lesson: pair talk first, then the good listener and speaker, then whole conversations, then discussion and debate.

## Why this lesson exists

No lesson housed the talking itself. Sierra Leone's books teach it as its own strand from the first year of school to the last: Primary 1 turns children to a partner to talk (Term 1, Lesson 4); Primary 5 and 6 name the good listener and the good speaker outright; the JSS handbooks run Conversations lessons and read Shakespeare's dialogue aloud in parts; the SSS handbooks end in formal Debate. The 2026-11-08 re-verification sweep marked 41 such items NEW LESSON NEEDED. Under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06), a teaching item with no lesson gets a new lesson built; this is that lesson. Its sources are the Sierra Leone books named in lesson/sources.md.

## Sub lessons

1. **Talking in pairs** — turn to a partner, speak, listen, and answer what your partner said
2. **The good listener and the good speaker** — what each one does — ears and eyes on the speaker; a clear voice, one idea at a time
3. **Conversations and role play** — holding a whole conversation, and acting a talk in parts
4. **Discussion and debate** — talking a topic through as a class, then arguing one side of a question with reasons

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is forced first — this lesson stands alone: it starts from "two people can build one talk together" and teaches every move it uses. The greeting lessons (hello-and-your-name, how-are-you) help but are not required first.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- Turn to a partner and hold a short talk, taking turns.
- Say what a good listener does and what a good speaker does — and do both.
- Hold a whole conversation from an opening to a closing.
- Take part in a class discussion, giving a view with a reason ("I think … because …").
- Argue one side of a question in a debate, and answer the other side.

## What you need
A partner. Every part of this lesson is done with another person — that is the lesson.

## The run of the lesson
1. **Do it first** — the learner turns to a partner and talks for one minute about their way to school, before anyone teaches the moves (lesson/lesson.md).
2. Work through sub lesson 1: talking in pairs — turns, and answering what was said.
3. Work through sub lesson 2: the good listener and the good speaker.
4. Work through sub lesson 3: whole conversations and role play.
5. Work through sub lesson 4: discussion and debate.
6. ADD AUDIO: two voices holding the model conversation — the student listens, then plays one part against the recording.

## Add-ons
- "Talk dice": roll and talk — each number is a topic (my family, my school, my friend); partners talk for a minute each.
- Class debate day: the class splits into two sides on one question and debates it the sub lesson 4 way.

## If the learner is stuck
If turns collapse into one person talking, go back to the pair rule: one speaks, one listens, then they change — and the listener must first say back what the speaker said. If a learner cannot find words, give the frames on the board: "I think … because …", "I agree with … because …", "I see it another way: …".

## Teacher note
Sierra Leone's Primary books are teacher lesson plans — the talk they specify is cited as teacher evidence in lesson/sources.md; the JSS and SSS conversation and debate lessons are the pupils' own handbook lessons. The books return to talk every single year, which is why this lesson climbs from pair talk to debate instead of treating talk as one small skill.
