# Lesson plan — Congratulating and wishing

**Course:** English · **Title:** Conversation · **Lesson:** Congratulating and wishing
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 5, "Conversation"
(CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** answering good
news and bad news — congratulating, wishing well, and
saying the kind word when things go wrong.
**What the learner can do at the end:** say
"Congratulations!" and "Congratulations on …" for a win;
use the wishes bank (Well done! Good luck! Best wishes!
Happy birthday!); answer with thanks; and meet bad news
with sympathy — "I'm so sorry", "What a shame!", "You
poor thing!"

## Before this lesson
- Soft hint only: How are you? teaches reading a person's
  day. Recommended, never forced — this lesson stands
  alone.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a student holding a passed
   exam paper, smiling, friends around.** Written slot:
   "a picture of someone who has just passed an exam."
   What the student does: the student says the first
   congratulation that comes — then learns the book's
   three shapes for it (Mauritania's Omar, who passed
   with distinction).
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of six greeting cards in a row:
   Birthday, Valentine's, Christmas, Thank-you,
   Appreciation, Graduation.** Written slot: "a picture
   of the six card kinds." What the student does: the
   student points at the card for a named moment — a
   birthday, a graduation, a thank-you — Mongolia's card
   matching, done with the eyes.
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of two friends, one sad, one with
   a hand on her shoulder.** Written slot: "a picture of
   sympathy between friends." What the student does: the
   student says the sympathy line aloud — "You poor
   thing!" — Libya's Farah-and-Reema shape.
4. **Slot 4 — a picture of a birthday table with flowers
   and a card.** Written slot: "a picture of a birthday
   with flowers and a wishes card." What the student
   does: the student writes the card's two lines from the
   wishes bank (Mauritania's Fatou: birthday, flowers —
   and an exam passed too).

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first** (lesson/lesson.md): one good news, one
   bad news — react to both in your own language.
2. **Congratulations**: "Congratulations!" /
   "Congratulations on …" / "I congratulate you on …"
   (Mauritania, Lesson 17).
3. **The wishes bank**: Great! Well done! Good job! Best
   wishes! Happy …! Good luck! (Mauritania, Lesson 18).
4. **Cards and messages**: the six card kinds (Mongolia);
   the congratulations message and letter (Nepal).
5. **Answering**: "Thank you!" — and for kindness, "Thank
   you for your kind words" (Libya).
6. **Sympathy**: the bad-news half — "I'm so sorry",
   "What a shame!", "You poor thing!" (Libya's vibes;
   Syria's congratulating-and-sympathy pairing).
7. **Do it yourself** (the three closing tasks).

## If the learner is stuck
- Stuck between Well done and Good luck: Well done looks
  back at a finished win; Good luck looks forward to a
  thing not yet done.
- Stuck on sympathy: bad news never takes
  "Congratulations". The safe three are "I'm so sorry",
  "What a shame!", "You poor thing!"
- This lesson's neighbours: Telephoning (Jubeda
  congratulates Krishna by phone, in Nepal's guide) and
  the personal letters of Level 40.
