# Congratulating and wishing — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Conversation · Lesson: Congratulating and wishing
Built from: Mauritania, Study English Grade 12 (Unit Seven,
Lessons 17–18) — Nepal, English Grade 9 and its Teachers'
Guide (Unit 5) — Mongolia, English VIII (Unit 1) — Libya,
English for Libya Grade 11 Course Book (Unit 7, Lesson 6)
— Syria, Emar Student's Book 12 (Unit 2).
This lesson is ONE thing only: answering news — the good
kind and the bad kind. Exam English and letter writing
live in their own lessons next door.
Soft hint: if you have done How are you?, you already
read a person's day — a hint only, never a gate. This
lesson stands alone.

## Do it first
Your companion tells you two pieces of news. First:
"I passed my exam!" Answer them — with your face, your
voice, your words, in your own language. Second: "My car
was stolen." Answer that one too. Feel how different the
two answers are.
You just did the whole lesson. Now let's see how English
does it.
On the speaker button: the explanation follows YOUR
language. The congratulations, wishes and sympathy lines
themselves — the target phrases — are never translated.
You hear them in English, in an English voice, exactly as
a friend would say them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Congratulations, the three shapes
**Congratulations — the three shapes.** Mauritania's
Grade 12 book gives congratulating a whole unit — Unit
Seven, "congratulating and wishing." Its exam lesson
(Lesson 17, "I Have Got It!", p.52) prints the three
shapes on Omar, who passes with distinction:
"Congratulations!" — the shout on its own.
"Congratulations on …" — with the win named:
Congratulations on your exam!
"I congratulate you on …" — the full, formal shape.

## Sub lesson 2 — The wishes bank
**The wishes bank.** The same book's birthday lesson
(Lesson 18, "Happy Birthday to You!", p.55 — Fatou's
birthday, with flowers, and an exam passed too) banks
the short bright words: "Great! Lucky you! Marvelous!
Wonderful! Incredible! Well done! Good job! Best wishes!
Happy …! Good luck!" Two of them have directions:
Well done looks BACK at a finished win. Good luck looks
FORWARD, to the exam still to come.

## Sub lesson 3 — Cards and messages
**Cards and messages.** Mongolia's Grade 8 book sorts
greeting cards into their kinds — Birthday, Valentine's,
Christmas, Thank-you, Appreciation, and Graduation
congratulations — and asks for a card and a reply written
(English VIII, Unit 1, p.10). Nepal writes the
congratulation down properly: its Grade 9 book carries a
letter congratulating Mrs Rai on becoming Chief Editor
and messages of congratulations as the writing work
(Unit 13), and its Teachers' Guide gives congratulating
a unit of its own — "Expressing congratulation" — with a
message to Mahabir Pun for his award and a telephone
tapescript in which Jubeda congratulates Krishna on a
best-female-singer award (Teachers' Guide, Unit 5,
pp.42–48; the guide is a draft guide for an older
textbook generation, as filed, and is cited as guide
evidence).

## Sub lesson 4 — Sympathy, the bad-news half
**The bad-news half — sympathy.** News is not always
good, and English keeps a kind set for the other half.
Libya's Grade 11 book names the lesson for it: "Positive
and Negative Vibes" (Unit 7, Lesson 6, p.87). Farah hears
Reema's news — "My father's in hospital" — and answers:
"You must be really worried." "You poor thing!" Reema
closes: "Thank you for your kind words." The same lesson
sorts four more newses: a passed driving test —
"Congratulations!"; full marks in maths — "Well done!";
a car stolen — "I'm so sorry"; a fight with a best
friend — "What a shame!" Syria's Grade 12 book pairs the
two halves in one slot — its "Everyday English" for the
Success unit is congratulating AND expressing sympathy
(Student's Book 12, Unit 2, pp.19–26; the Workbook runs
the sympathy side in a hang-gliding accident dialogue,
pp.9–15). One rule holds both halves together: bad news
never takes "Congratulations."

## Sub lesson 5 — Answering with thanks
**Answering.** A congratulation is answered with thanks:
"Thank you!" For a kind word in a hard moment, Libya's
full answer is the warmer one: "Thank you for your kind
words."

## Do it yourself
1. **Three wins.** Congratulate your companion on three
   wins — an exam, a race, a new job — once with each
   shape: "Congratulations!", "Congratulations on …",
   "Well done!"
2. **The right card.** Name the card kind and write its
   first line for: a birthday, a graduation, and a
   thank-you (Mongolia's six kinds).
3. **The kind answer.** Your companion gives you one bad
   news. Answer with the sympathy set — "I'm so sorry",
   "What a shame!", or "You poor thing!" — and let them
   close with "Thank you for your kind words."
