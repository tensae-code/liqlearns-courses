# Friendships and Relationships

## Do it first

Your teacher asks the class: "Do you get on well with your friends?" This lesson gives you the words for how people get on together — the words a whole unit of Tunisia's Year 8 book is built on — and teaches you to talk about your own friendships with them.

## Sub lesson 1 — Words for getting on with people

- **get on well (with someone)** — to be friendly and happy together: "I get on well with my cousin."
- **keep company** — to spend time with someone: "She keeps company with her neighbours."
- **strike up a friendship** — to start a friendship: "They struck up a friendship at school."
- **fight** — to argue or quarrel: "Friends sometimes fight, then make up."

Learn the four as two pairs: starting and keeping a friendship (strike up, keep company, get on well) — and the hard moment inside one (fight).

*Difficulty: Four relationship words, each with its own picture in your mind.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Talking about your friendships

Put the words to work on your own life:

- "I **get on well** with my best friend. We **keep company** every day at break."
- "I **struck up a friendship** with the new girl in our class."
- "We sometimes **fight** about games, but we make up quickly."

Say who, say how you get on, say what you do together. That is a whole small talk about a friendship.

*Difficulty: The four words, used about your real friends.*

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion about one friendship using at least two of the lesson's words. Your companion must ask: "Do you ever fight?" Answer honestly — in English.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write the four words and one short sentence for each, about people you know.
2. Write three sentences about your best friendship.
3. Read them to a friend — and strike up the conversation in English.
