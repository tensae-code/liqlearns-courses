# Lesson plan — Making suggestions

**Course:** English · **Title:** Conversation · **Lesson:** Making suggestions
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Conversation (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** making a suggestion —
offering an IDEA for what to do together — with "Let's…",
"Why don't we…?" and "How about…?", and answering one with
"That's a good idea" or a polite "I don't feel like it."
**What the learner can do at the end:** suggest an activity
three ways; answer a suggestion with a yes that sounds glad
("That's a good idea / I'd love to") or a no that stays kind
("I don't feel like it / I don't think so"); and turn a bare
plan ("the river") into a full suggestion.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Let's, the shortest suggestion** — what a suggestion is, and its shortest shape: "Let's + the plain verb". The opening paragraph previews all three shapes, so it overlaps Sub lessons 2–3. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Why don't we, the question-shaped suggestion** — "Why don't we…?" — a suggestion in a question's coat, with Syria's third shape "We/You could…" standing beside it. (Was part of depth Level 2.)
3. **How about, the -ing suggestion** — "How about / What about + the -ing form" — after How about, the verb changes its coat. (Was part of depth Level 2.)
4. **Answering a suggestion, glad or kind** — the glad yes ("That's a good idea") and the kind no ("I don't feel like it", "I'd rather…") — a no that refuses the idea, never the person, often with a new suggestion of its own. (Was depth Level 3, with depth Level 1's glad answer banked here.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- If you know Making offers, this will feel easier — a
  suggestion is an offer whose gift is an idea. Recommended,
  never forced.
- Giving advice ("You should…") is a different thing, with
  its own lesson. Here nobody is told; everybody is invited.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of two friends looking at a grey,
   rainy window, with three empty speech bubbles over their
   heads.** Written slot: "a picture of two friends with
   nothing to do and three empty speech bubbles." What the
   student does: the student fills one bubble out loud with
   a suggestion before reading on.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of a compass and a weather map on a
   table, like Syria's walk-planning dialogue.** Written
   slot: "a picture of a compass and a weather map for
   planning a walk." What the student does: the student
   points at the map and suggests the walk — "Let's walk
   here" — the way Syria's class plans it.
3. **Slot 3 — a short animation (3–4 seconds) of the words
   LET'S growing the word GO behind them, like a train
   pulling a carriage.** Written slot: "a picture that
   moves: Let's pulling the plain verb behind it." What the
   student does: the student calls out the verb that rides
   after "Let's" each time a new carriage appears — go,
   swim, fish, cook.
4. **Slot 4 — a picture of two answer faces: one glad
   ("That's a good idea") and one sorry but kind ("I don't
   feel like it").** Written slot: "a picture of the glad
   yes and the kind no to a suggestion." What the student
   does: the student points at the true face before
   answering each suggestion the companion makes.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first** (lesson/lesson.md): the empty afternoon —
   suggest one real thing to do with your companion, now.
2. **Let's + the plain verb**: the shortest suggestion
   (Cambodia Grade 10's fishing suggestion; Mongolia Grade
   7's form list).
3. **Why don't we…?**: the question-shaped suggestion
   (Syria Starters 5, Unit 3 Study Box; Cambodia's Unit 17
   is named for it).
4. **How about / What about + -ing**: Mongolia Grade 7's
   third form (p.26), with Syria's "We/You could…" riding
   beside it.
5. **The answers**: glad yes — "That's a good idea," "It
   sounds great," "I'd love to"; kind no — "I don't feel
   like it," "I don't think so," "I'd rather…" (Cambodia
   and Mongolia's approving/rejecting phrases).
6. **Do it yourself**: three suggestions for this weekend,
   one answered no made kind, and a walk planned the Syria
   way — forecast, compass, suggestion.

## If the learner is stuck
- Puts -ing after Let's ("Let's going") → the train picture:
  Let's pulls the PLAIN verb; -ing rides only after
  How about.
- Answers every suggestion "Yes" by habit → the two faces:
  the books teach the kind no on purpose; use it truly.
- This lesson's neighbours: Making offers (giving a thing);
  Giving advice (telling, not inviting — its own lesson).
