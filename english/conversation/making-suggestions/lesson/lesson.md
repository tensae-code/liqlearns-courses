# Making suggestions — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Conversation · Lesson: Making suggestions
Built from: Syria, English for Starters 5 (York Press) and
English Pupil's Book 6 — Cambodia, English Grade 10 —
Mongolia, English VII.
This lesson is ONE thing only: making a suggestion — and
answering one. Giving advice ("You should…") lives in its
own lesson; here nobody is told what to do.
If you know Making offers, this will feel easier — but you
can start right here.

Press the speaker button and the explanations come in your own
language. The suggestion phrases themselves always stay in
English — they are the thing you are learning, so they are
never translated.

## Do it first
You and your companion have an empty hour. Do not wait for an
idea to arrive — offer one, in English: "Let's go for a walk."
Or "Why don't we play a game?" Let your companion answer
truly: glad, or not glad. Then change places.

You just did the whole lesson. Now let's see how it works.

## Sub lesson 1 — Let's, the shortest suggestion
A **suggestion** is an offer whose gift is an idea. The books
teach three shapes, and each shape carries its verb
differently — that is the whole care this lesson needs.

**Let's + the plain verb.** The shortest suggestion: "Let's
go." "Let's swim." Cambodia's Grade 10 book fishes with it —
"Let's go fishing near the river" is one of its citation
lines. After Let's, the verb stays plain: never "Let's
going," never "Let's to go."

## Sub lesson 2 — Why don't we, the question-shaped suggestion
**Why don't we…?** It wears a question's coat but asks
nothing — it suggests. Syria's Starters 5 book puts it in its
Study Box for planning a walk, beside Let's and "We/You
could…": the class reads a weather forecast, holds a compass,
and suggests the walk into being. Cambodia's Grade 10 unit
is named for this very shape: "Why don't we…?"

## Sub lesson 3 — How about, the -ing suggestion
**How about / What about + the -ing form.** Mongolia's Grade
7 book lists the suggestion forms together on one page
(p.26): "How about/What about + verb-ing; Let's + verb; Why
don't + verb." After How about, the verb changes its coat:
"How about swimming?" "How about fishing?" Syria's Grade 6
book carries the same exponents in its free-time unit —
"Let's…; Why don't we…?" — as the language of suggesting,
taught beside requests and apologies.

## Sub lesson 4 — Answering a suggestion, glad or kind
**The answers.** A suggestion deserves a true answer, and the
books teach both. The glad yes: Cambodia's "That's a good
idea"; Mongolia's "I think it sounds great," "It's perfect,"
"I'd love to." The kind no: Cambodia's "I don't feel like
it"; Mongolia's "I don't think so" and "I'd rather…" — a no
that refuses the idea, never the person. A kind no often
carries a new suggestion of its own: "I don't feel like
swimming. How about a walk?"

## Do it yourself
1. **Three shapes, one afternoon.** Suggest the same activity
   three times — once with Let's, once with Why don't we,
   once with How about. Feel how each shape carries its verb.
2. **Answer truly.** Have your companion make four
   suggestions. Answer two glad and two kind — and after one
   kind no, add a suggestion of your own.
3. **Plan it like Syria.** With a forecast and a direction,
   plan a real walk in suggestions only. Every plan sentence
   must be a suggestion — no bare "we will."
