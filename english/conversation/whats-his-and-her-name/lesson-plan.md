# Lesson plan — What's his and her name?

**Course:** English · **Title:** Conversation · **Lesson:** What's his
and her name?
**Place in the curriculum:** Year 1, Term 1, Unit 4 "Conversation"
(CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** asking the name of ANOTHER
person — "What's her name?" for a girl or woman, "What's his name?"
for a boy or man.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source).

## Before this lesson
- Hello and your name: "What's your name?" is the first door; this
  lesson is the third door — the person over there. Recommended,
  never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Ask about a girl or woman: "What's her name?"
2. Ask about a boy or man: "What's his name?"
3. Answer with just the name — "Muna." / "Kareem." — and know why it
   is not "My name is" (you cannot say "my" for someone else).

## What you need
People nearby to point at, or pictures of people. The companion.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — point at a person near you (or a photo) and ask
   the companion the right question for them: her or his
   (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE at the top of the lesson: a picture of two children
     standing apart from the talk — a girl on one side, a boy on the
     other, no names shown — the student points at each one and asks
     the question that fits them.
2. **The explanation** — names have three doors; her goes with a
   girl or woman, his with a boy or man; the answer is just the name
   carried across (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD AUDIO: both pairs read aloud — "What's her name? — Muna."
     and "What's his name? — Kareem." — the student repeats each
     pair, then says which word did the pointing work (her / his).
   - ADD IMAGE under the answer: a picture of a name being carried —
     one child holding a name card and handing it across to the
     asker — the student says why the card carries only the name,
     not "My name is."
3. **Say it to your companion** — two people, a girl and a boy, real
   or in a picture: ask about each and answer, until her and his
   come without thinking.
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in questions/video-script.md, filmed —
     the two questions asked about two people, in the super-simple
     voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (questions/levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — questions/hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — today, ask someone about a third person for
   real; write both question-and-answer pairs down, one for her, one
   for his.

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- IMAGE — a picture of a girl and a boy apart from the talk, unnamed
  → the student points and asks the fitting question for each.
- AUDIO — both question-and-answer pairs read aloud → the student
  repeats them and names the pointing word.
- IMAGE — a picture of a name card handed across → the student says
  why the answer is just the name.
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later) → the student asks
  both questions along with it.

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the
plan is the build sheet, so nothing the lesson needs gets forgotten.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes her and his → the small word points at the person: her →
  her (a girl, a woman); his → him (a boy, a man). Point first,
  then ask.
- Answers "My name is Muna" for someone else → you are not that
  person; you only carry their name across.
