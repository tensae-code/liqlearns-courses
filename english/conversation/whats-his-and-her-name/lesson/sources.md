**Correction note (2026-11-08, whole-arc audit):** the
lesson text previously stated the answer "is just the name."
That is one book's version — Libya's (and Iraq's opening
unit). The stocked full form is now taught in Sub lesson 3
beside it: Iraq Grade 2 Unit 7's "What's her name? — Her
name is Nada" (printed pp.58–67) and Jordan Grade 2's

pp.32–39). No existing row changed.

**Acceptance count: taught in 14 countries (Libya, China, North Macedonia, Iran, Indonesia, Jordan, Iraq, Ethiopia, South Africa, Nepal, Sudan, Rwanda, Malaysia, Ecuador).**
China names third people by role with he/she and identifies
family members one by one; North Macedonia works the naming
frame through a full set of named family portraits. Iran and
Indonesia joined 2026-11-03: both ask about the third person
with "Who is he/she / that …?" rather than Libya's name
question, and answer with he/she plus who the person is to
you — the same third-person naming work in the *who* form,
recorded here as the form each book actually prints. Jordan
joined 2026-11-03 in a third form of the same work: its
question is "Who's this?", and the third person's name is
stated with the exact his/her frame — "His name's… / Her
name's…" — as the answer.
Iraq joined 2026-11-03 with Libya's form itself, not a
*who* form: its Grade 2 book teaches "What's his/her
name?" in its opening lesson, and asks "What's her
name? — Her name is Nada." a unit from the end of the
year.
Ethiopia joined 2026-11-03 in the statement form, like
Jordan: it never prints the "What's his/her name?"
question, but its Grade 1 book states a third person's
name as its own frame — "My father's name is …" — and
its Grade 4 book names father's name among the items of
its personal-information exchange.
Counts grow as other closed countries' books are read.
Nepal joined 2026-11-03 with Libya's form itself: its Grade 1 book asks "What's her/his name?" inside its first unit's naming work (Unit 1, printed pp.1–6), and its Grade 2 book asks "What's his name?" inside its occupations unit (Unit 2, printed pp.8–12).
