**Acceptance count: taught in 5 countries (Libya, China, North Macedonia, Iran, Indonesia).**
China names third people by role with he/she and identifies
family members one by one; North Macedonia works the naming
frame through a full set of named family portraits. Iran and
Indonesia joined 2026-11-03: both ask about the third person
with "Who is he/she / that …?" rather than Libya's name
question, and answer with he/she plus who the person is to
you — the same third-person naming work in the *who* form,
recorded here as the form each book actually prints.
Counts grow as other closed countries' books are read.

states the frame in its objectives (printed p.34): "Who is
he/she? — He is my father," with "They are my little brother

teacher" (objectives, printed p.150). Like Iran, Indonesia
asks with *who* — the third person is identified by who he or
she is to you, the name carried by the story around the frame.
