# Lesson Plan — Keeping a Conversation Going

**One thing:** the listener's small answers — I see. Great! Really? Right. Yeah. Sure. Awesome. — and how they keep a short conversation going inside mini-dialogues.

## Sub lessons

1. The listening answers — the seven small responses and what each one tells the speaker
2. Keeping a mini-dialogue going — placing the answers inside short dialogues so the talk does not stop

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Teaching points

- A conversation dies when only one person makes sounds. The listener's job is to answer — briefly, warmly, and at the right moments.
- The book's set of listening answers: **I see. Great! Really? Right. Yeah. Sure. Awesome.**
- Each answer does a small job: *I see* = I follow you; *Really?* = that surprises me, tell me more; *Great! / Awesome.* = that is good news; *Right / Yeah* = I agree, go on; *Sure* = yes, willingly.
- The answers are short on purpose: the listener feeds the talk without stealing it.
- In a mini-dialogue the answers come after the speaker's lines, again and again — that rhythm is what "keeping a conversation going" means.
