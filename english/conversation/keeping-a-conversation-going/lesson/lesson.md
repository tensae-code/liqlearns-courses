# Keeping a Conversation Going

## Do it first

Two friends are talking. One tells a story. The other says nothing — no word, no sound, only a face. After three lines, the story stops. Why? Because talking to silence feels like talking to a wall. This lesson teaches the small answers that tell a speaker: *I am here, I am listening, go on.*

## Sub lesson 1 — The listening answers

*Difficulty: first steps. Seven short answers — learn them by heart.*

Here is the set, exactly as the book teaches it:

- **I see.**
- **Great!**
- **Really?**
- **Right.**
- **Yeah.**
- **Sure.**
- **Awesome.**

Each one does a small job:

- **I see.** — I follow what you are saying.
- **Really?** — That surprises me — tell me more.
- **Great!** / **Awesome.** — That is good news; I am glad.
- **Right.** / **Yeah.** — I agree; go on.
- **Sure.** — Yes, willingly.

Notice how short they are. A listening answer is not a speech. It is a door held open: the speaker walks through and keeps talking.

## Sub lesson 2 — Keeping a mini-dialogue going

*Difficulty: a step up. Now the answers must land in the right places.*

Watch the rhythm in a mini-dialogue:

— I passed my exam yesterday.
— **Great!**
— And my sister passed hers too.
— **Really?** That's wonderful.
— We are going to celebrate on Saturday.
— **Awesome.** Can I come?
— Of course!
— **Sure** — I mean… yes!

Every speaker line gets an answer. No line is left hanging in silence. That is the whole skill: **answer, answer, answer** — briefly, warmly, and at the right moment — and the conversation keeps going by itself.

Two dangers to avoid: answering before the speaker finishes (that is interrupting, not listening), and using the same answer for everything (a flat *Yeah. Yeah. Yeah.* tells the speaker you stopped listening long ago).

## Say it to your companion

Your companion tells you three true things about their week, one by one. After each one, answer with a different listening answer — the one that fits. Then change places: you talk, they keep you going.

## Do It Yourself

1. Write a mini-dialogue of eight lines: four speaker lines, four listening answers, no answer used twice.
2. Listen to any real conversation today (at home, at school, on the bus). Count the listening answers you hear. Which one did people use most?
3. Practise the set aloud until the seven answers come without thinking: I see. Great! Really? Right. Yeah. Sure. Awesome.
