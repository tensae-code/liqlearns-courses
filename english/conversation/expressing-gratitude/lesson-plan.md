# Lesson plan — Expressing Gratitude

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** Expressing Gratitude
**Lesson:** expressing-gratitude — saying thank you well: thanks for…, I'm grateful, and the thank-you letter
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Conversation
**The one thing:** Gratitude talk — thanking a person, naming what you thank them for, the stronger grateful forms, and thanks written as a letter.
**This lesson's own level:** Conversation lesson (gratitude): thank you / thanks for + noun or -ing; I'm grateful to you for…; that's really kind of you; the thank-you letter's shape.

## Why this lesson exists

Mauritania teaches gratitude twice — the Grade 8 book's forms (thanks for, thank you for, I'm grateful to you for) and the Grade 10 book's thank-you letter — and the course's conversation unit had apologising and congratulating but no gratitude lesson. This lesson is built from the placements under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06).

## Sub lessons

1. Thank you — the first forms
2. Thanks for… — naming the gift
3. I'm grateful — stronger thanks
4. The thank-you letter

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is needed before this lesson. Congratulating and Wishing answers good news; this lesson answers kindness, and each stands alone.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- Thank a person plainly: "Thank you." "Thanks."
- Name the gift: "Thank you for the book." "Thanks for helping me."
- Say the stronger thanks: "I'm grateful to you for…" "That's really kind of you."
- Write a short thank-you letter.

## What you need
Nothing special — one kindness to thank for, real or imagined.

## The run of the lesson
1. ADD IMAGE: a learner receiving a tablet as a gift, surprised and happy. The learner tries the first thank-you before any form appears.
2. Teach sub lesson 2 with real class kindnesses: hold a door, lend a pen — each gets its named thanks.
3. ADD GIF (1–2 seconds): a heart growing bigger — grateful is the bigger word for a bigger kindness.
4. Sub lesson 4 writes the class thank-you letter to someone who helped the class.

## Add-ons
- Thanks chain: each learner thanks the next for a real small thing, naming it.
- Letter box: real thank-you letters written and delivered inside the school.

## If the learner is stuck
Keep to "Thank you for…" with the gift named until the naming is automatic — bare thank-you is only the first step. Grateful and the letter come one step each after.

## Teacher note
The Grade 8 book builds gratitude as forms; the Grade 10 book returns to it as a letter to a whole family — the lesson keeps that climb: say it, name it, strengthen it, write it.
