# Expressing Gratitude

## Do it first

Someone helped you this week — held a door, lent a pen, explained a lesson. Did you thank them well? Try it now, out loud: "Thank you for…" and name the thing. Go on — in this lesson you will learn to thank people fully.

## Sub lesson 1 — Thank you — the first forms

The first forms are short and warm:

- "**Thank you.**" / "**Thanks.**"
- To the person, with a smile: "Thank you very much."

Say them now to someone near you — for anything, even a small thing.

## Sub lesson 2 — Thanks for… — naming the gift

Good thanks names the gift:

- "**Thanks for** the tablet!"
- "**Thank you for** helping me."
- "Thanks for **coming**." (for + -ing when it is a doing)

Thank three people now, and name each gift as you thank.

## Sub lesson 3 — I'm grateful — stronger thanks

For a big kindness, English has a bigger word — **grateful**:

- "**I'm grateful to you for** the tablet!"
- "**I am really grateful to you**, to your mum and to all your family."
- Answer the kindness itself: "**That's really kind of you.**"

Grateful is thank-you with weight. Save it for the kindnesses that deserve it.

## Sub lesson 4 — The thank-you letter

Thanks can be written. A thank-you letter:

1. Opens with the person: "Dear Aunty…"
2. Thanks and names the gift: "I am really grateful to you for…"
3. Says what the gift did for you.
4. Closes warmly with your name.

Write yours in the Do It Yourself — short is fine; named and warm is the whole art.

## Say it to your companion

Give your companion an imaginary gift — a tablet, a book, help with a lesson. They thank you three ways: plain, with the gift named, and with grateful. Judge which fits the gift's size. Then swap.

## Do It Yourself

Write a thank-you letter of five or six lines to a real person: name them, name the gift, say what it did for you, close warmly. Read it aloud before you send it.
