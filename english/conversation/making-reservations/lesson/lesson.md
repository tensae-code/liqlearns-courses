# Making Reservations

## Do it first

You arrive in a new town at night, tired, with your bag in your hand. Every hotel door you try says the same thing: full. That night is what a reservation prevents. A reservation is a booking made **before** you travel — the room or the seat is held for you, in your name, before you arrive. This lesson teaches the talk that makes one: you ask, you say what you want, and you hear it confirmed.

## Sub lesson 1 — Asking for a reservation

*Difficulty: first step — the ask, the trip you want, and the confirmation.*

The talk opens with one polite sentence that does the whole first job: **"I'd like to make a reservation, please."** Now the person at the desk — or on the phone — knows exactly why you are there.

Then you say what you are booking. For a flight, name the trip itself: **"I'd like a round trip flight"** — out and back — or **"I'd like a nonstop flight"** — one flight, no stops on the way. The words *round trip* and *nonstop* carry the shape of the whole journey in one phrase, so say them exactly.

The talk is not finished when you have asked. It is finished when you hear the booking confirmed: **"Your room has been booked"** — or **"Your flight has been booked."** That sentence is your proof: the room or seat now waits for you.

Try it now: book a flight in play. Open with "I'd like to make a reservation, please", then choose — round trip or nonstop — and say it. Your partner answers with the confirmation. If you never hear "has been booked", the reservation is not made yet: ask again until you do.

## Sub lesson 2 — The hotel words and the reservation talk

*Difficulty: builds on Sub lesson 1 — the words of the place you are booking into.*

A reservation talk needs the words of the place itself. A **hostel** is a simple, cheaper place to stay; a **three-star hotel** is a hotel of the middle kind. Inside, the **front desk** is where you ask, book and check in; the **lobby** is the big entrance room; the **elevator** carries you up to your floor; the **security box** keeps your important things safe; and the **swimming pool** is the water you swim in. Each word names a part of the stay your reservation is buying.

With the words in hand, the whole talk runs as one conversation: greet → "I'd like to make a reservation, please" → say the room or flight you want → ask about the place with its own words ("Is there a swimming pool?" "Where is the front desk?") → hear the confirmation: "Your room has been booked."

Try it now: with a partner at a pretend front desk, hold the full conversation. The guest books a room at a three-star hotel and asks two questions using the hotel words. The desk answers and closes with the confirmation. Then swap roles, and this time book into a hostel.

## Do it yourself

Before your next real trip — or in play, for a trip you dream of — make the reservation in English. Write the talk out first if you need to: the ask, the exact room or flight you want, one or two questions with the hotel words, and the confirmation you must hear before you stop. Afterwards, check: is something now held for you, in your name, that was not held before? If yes, you made a reservation — and no full hotel door can surprise you.
