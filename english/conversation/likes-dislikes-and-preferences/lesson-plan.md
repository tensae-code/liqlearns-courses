# Lesson plan — Likes, Dislikes and Preferences

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** Likes, Dislikes and Preferences
**Lesson:** likes-dislikes-and-preferences — what you like, what you want, and what you would rather do
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Conversation
**The one thing:** Taste talk in three climbs: like/dislike and your favourite; wanting — want, would like, feel like; and choosing — prefer and 'd rather.
**This lesson's own level:** Conversation lesson (tastes and choices): like/dislike + noun or -ing, favourite; want + noun/to-infinitive, would like, feel like + -ing; prefer X to Y, 'd rather X than Y, asking preferences (do you prefer…or…?).

## Why this lesson exists

Mauritania teaches the taste strand in three places — the Grade 7 book's likes and dislikes with favourite, and the Grade 13 book's desires (would like, feel like, want) and preferences (prefer, 'd rather) — and the course had no lesson that carries the wanting and choosing climbs. This lesson is built from the placements under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06).

## Sub lessons

1. Likes and dislikes — like, dislike, favourite
2. Wanting — want, would like, feel like
3. Preferences — prefer X to Y
4. I'd rather — choosing between two

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is needed before this lesson. Likes and Hobbies talks about free-time likes; this lesson carries dislikes, wanting and choosing, and each stands alone.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- Say what you like and dislike, with nouns and -ing forms, and name your favourite.
- Say what you want three ways: want, would like, feel like.
- Choose with prefer: "I prefer reading to watching."
- Choose with 'd rather: "I'd rather study at home." and ask "Would you rather…or…?"

## What you need
Nothing special — your own tastes are the material.

## The run of the lesson
1. ADD IMAGE: two plates — rice and fish; a learner points at one and pulls a face at the other. The learner tries "I like… / I don't like…" before any rule appears.
2. Teach sub lesson 1 on the class's real tastes: sports, foods, school subjects.
3. ADD GIF (1–2 seconds): a hand reaching past a near cup to a farther one — choosing is the lesson's climb.
4. Sub lesson 4 runs preference interviews: every learner asks three people "Would you rather…or…?" and reports the answers.

## Add-ons
- Taste line: learners stand on a line from love to hate for each named thing and say their sentence.
- Rather market: pairs offer two activities; the other must choose with 'd rather and say why.

## If the learner is stuck
Keep to "I like / I don't like + thing" until tastes are sayable at all. Wanting is one step up; prefer and 'd rather are the last two steps, one at a time.

## Teacher note
The Grade 7 book builds likes with favourite; Grade 13 returns years later with the desire and preference systems — the lesson keeps the books' order and treats them as one strand: taste, want, choose.
