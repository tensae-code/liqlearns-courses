# Lesson plan — Stating and Giving Opinions

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** Stating and Giving Opinions
**Lesson:** stating-and-giving-opinions — saying what you think, giving reasons, and hearing the other side
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Conversation
**The one thing:** Opinion talk — I think…, In my opinion…, because…, and answering another opinion without a fight.
**This lesson's own level:** Conversation lesson (opinions): stating frames (I think / In my opinion), reasons with because, for-and-against talk on a real question, and polite disagreement.

## Why this lesson exists

Indonesia teaches opinion work in four books and none had a lesson: Grade 8's stating and giving opinions, Grade 10's graffiti question (art or vandalism?), Grade 11 English for Change's opinion openings and discussion work, and Grade 12's pros-and-cons evaluation. The course had agreeing-and-disagreeing for reactions, but no lesson that builds an opinion from the ground. This lesson is built from the placements under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06).

## Sub lessons

1. Saying what you think — I think…
2. Giving reasons — because…
3. For and against — a real question, two sides
4. Answering another opinion — politely

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is needed before this lesson. Agreeing and Disagreeing gives the short reactions; this lesson builds the opinion itself, and each stands alone.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- State an opinion: "I think graffiti is art." "In my opinion, …"
- Give a reason with because.
- Talk about both sides of a question — the pros and the cons.
- Answer an opinion you don't share, politely.

## What you need
Nothing special — one real question the class can argue about kindly.

## The run of the lesson
1. ADD IMAGE: a wall painting — half the class says art, half says mess. The learner picks a side and says "I think…" before any rule appears.
2. Teach sub lesson 1 with the frames on the wall: I think / In my opinion / I believe.
3. ADD GIF (1–2 seconds): a scale tipping — reasons weigh. Every opinion needs its because.
4. Sub lesson 3 runs the graffiti question whole-class: for, against, then each learner's own landing.
5. Sub lesson 4 practises the soft landings: "I see your point, but I think…"

## Add-ons
- Two-minute debate: pairs argue opposite sides of "School should start later", then swap sides.
- Opinion line: learners stand on a line from strongly agree to strongly disagree and say why.

## If the learner is stuck
Keep to "I think… because…" until the pair is automatic. The two-sides work and polite disagreement come one step each after that.

## Teacher note
Indonesia's Grade 10 book carries its opinion work inside the graffiti exposition; Grade 11 English for Change opens with opinion and discussion work; Grade 12 weighs pros and cons. The lesson keeps that order: state, reason, weigh, answer.
