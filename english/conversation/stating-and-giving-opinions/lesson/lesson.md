# Stating and Giving Opinions

## Do it first

Look at a painting on a wall in the street. Is it art, or is it a mess? Pick your side and say "I think…" — go on, try before we start. In this lesson you will learn to say what you think, give your reasons, hear the other side, and answer politely.

## Sub lesson 1 — Saying what you think — I think…

An **opinion** is what you think — not a fact everyone can check. English has friendly frames for it:

"**I think** graffiti is art." "**In my opinion**, school starts too early." "**I believe** reading is fun."

Indonesia's Grade 11 English for Change opens its work with these frames — because a learner who can state an opinion can join any discussion. Grade 8 teaches stating and giving opinions by name.

Say one about your town now: "I think…"

## Sub lesson 2 — Giving reasons — because…

An opinion alone is light. A **reason** gives it weight: "I think graffiti is art **because** it makes grey walls beautiful." "In my opinion, homework helps **because** we practise again at home."

Indonesia's Grade 10 book builds its whole graffiti chapter this way: the question — is graffiti art or vandalism? — then reasons on both sides.

Never leave an opinion bare. Add its because.

Give a reason for your opinion from sub lesson 1.

## Sub lesson 3 — For and against — a real question, two sides

Most real questions have two sides. Indonesia's Grade 12 book teaches learners to weigh the **pros and cons** — the points for, and the points against:

Graffiti — for: it is art; it tells the city's stories. Against: walls are not ours to paint; cleaning costs money.

Saying both sides before your own landing makes your opinion strong, not weak: "Some say… Others say… I think… because…"

Take the homework question: say one point for, one point against, then your own landing.

## Sub lesson 4 — Answering another opinion — politely

Opinions meet other opinions. Answer the idea, not the person:

"I see your point, **but** I think…" "That's true, **and** I also think…" "I don't agree, because…"

Indonesia's discussion work trains exactly this: hold your side, hear theirs, answer with a reason. No shouting — the best reason wins.

Answer this one politely: "I think football is boring." Use "I see your point, but…" and give your reason.

## Say it to your companion

Pick a question together — "Phones in class: yes or no?" Each of you: state your opinion, give a reason, name one point from the other side, and answer your companion's opinion politely.

## Do It Yourself

Write four sentences: your opinion ("I think…"), your reason ("…because…"), one point for the other side ("Some say…"), and your landing ("I still think… because…"). Read them aloud to someone at home and ask for their opinion.
