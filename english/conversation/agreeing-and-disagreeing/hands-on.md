# Hands-on — Agreeing and disagreeing

You do it, we guide you. Everything here is done in the learner's
real day, with a companion or the Explain Companion.

1. **Mongolia's chain game.** Mongolia's Grade 6 book practises
   So do I / Nor do I in a chain game (p.80). Play it: one
   person says a routine truth — "I get up at six." The next
   must echo truly: "So do I" — or break the chain honestly:
   "I don't. I get up at seven." The chain grows until someone
   cannot echo; then a new chain starts with a no-sentence and
   Neither.
2. **The two-sides debate (Cambodia's way).** Cambodia's Grade
   10 debate unit argues the effects of tourism. Take its
   shape, any topic: two sides, each side must open with a
   frame ("I think… / In my opinion…") and must say "I agree,
   but…" at least once before it may disagree. Swap sides
   halfway — argue the side you did not choose.
3. **Letters for and against (Uzbekistan's way).** Uzbekistan's
   Grade 9 book teaches agreeing and disagreeing through an
   article and letters for and against a school system. Write
   two short letters about one school question — one for, one
   against — each with a claim and two reasons. Sign neither;
   your companion must guess which one is truly yours.
4. **Mauritania's frame, your topic.** Pick the book's own
   question or yours: should students bring phones to school?
   Build the full frame on paper — claim ("In my view…"),
   First…, Second…, Finally…, conclusion — then read it aloud
   and let your companion answer the OTHER side, as the book's
   writing task demands.
