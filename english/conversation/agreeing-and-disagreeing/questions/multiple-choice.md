# Agreeing and disagreeing — multiple choice

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — So do I, the yes-world echo · Sub lesson 2 — Neither do I, the no-world echo · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 3 — Stating a view, I think and In my opinion, except item 4 (agreeing with a door open), which is Sub lesson 4 — Agreeing and disagreeing with a view · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 (item 5 asks for the frame itself, boxed there).

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** "I like swimming." You like it too:
a) So do I.  b) Neither do I.  c) I don't.
**Answer: a) So do I.** — the yes-world echo (Mongolia, English VI, p.80; Cambodia, Grade 10, Unit 8).

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** "I don't like getting up early." You don't like it either:
a) So do I.  b) Neither do I.  c) Yes, I do.
**Answer: b) Neither do I.** — the no-world echo; Mongolia's dress for it is "Nor do I" (English VI, Unit 7).

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** Which sentence states a view in its frame?
a) In my opinion, the town needs a library.  b) The library is over there.  c) A library is a building.
**Answer: a) In my opinion, the town needs a library.** — Cambodia's opinion frame (Grade 10, Unit 29).

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** You agree with most of a view but not all. The door-open answer is:
a) I agree, but…  b) Nonsense!  c) You're wrong.
**Answer: a) I agree, but…** — Cambodia's debate set (Grade 10, Unit 29, pp.157–162).

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Mauritania's argument frame runs: claim → reasons → —
a) silence  b) conclusion  c) a new topic
**Answer: b) conclusion** — claim ("In my view / I think…"), reasons ("First… Second… Finally…"), conclusion (Study English Grade 12, Lesson 7, pp.23–24).
