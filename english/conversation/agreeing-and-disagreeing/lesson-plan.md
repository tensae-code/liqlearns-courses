# Lesson plan — Agreeing and disagreeing

**Course:** English · **Title:** Conversation · **Lesson:** Agreeing and disagreeing
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 5, "Conversation" (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** agreeing and
disagreeing — saying your view ("I think… / In my
opinion…"), agreeing in full ("I agree / You're right"),
agreeing with a *but* ("I agree, but…"), disagreeing
honestly ("I don't agree / I disagree"), and the short
echo answers for likes and habits: "So do I / Neither do
I / I don't."
**What the learner can do at the end:** answer a like or a
habit with the right echo (So do I for a yes-world,
Neither do I for a no-world, I don't when your truth
differs); state an opinion with its frame; and disagree
with a person while staying their friend — claim, reasons,
kind words.

## Before this lesson
- If you know Making suggestions, this will feel easier —
  its glad and kind answers ("That's a good idea / I don't
  think so") are this lesson's first cousins. Recommended,
  never forced.
- Question tags ("…, isn't it?") confirm and check; Nepal
  teaches them in the same unit as So/Neither, but they are
  their own lesson. Here: your answer, not the check.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of two friends at a table, both
   nodding at the same bowl of soup, with SO DO I over
   their heads.** Written slot: "a picture of two friends
   agreeing over the same bowl." What the student does:
   the student says the echo out loud — "I like soup."
   "So do I." — before reading on.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of a see-saw that will not balance:
   one side says NEITHER DO I, the other I DON'T.** Written
   slot: "a picture of the two different answers to a
   no-sentence." What the student does: the student points
   at the side that matches their own truth for each
   no-sentence the companion says.
3. **Slot 3 — a short animation (3–4 seconds) of a speech
   bubble growing a frame: I THINK… at its head and FIRST /
   SECOND / FINALLY steps under it.** Written slot: "a
   picture that moves: an opinion growing its frame and
   its reason steps" — Mauritania's argument frame. What
   the student does: the student climbs the steps out loud
   with their own opinion.
4. **Slot 4 — a picture of two debaters shaking hands over
   a table that says TOURISM — GOOD? BAD?** Written slot:
   "a picture of a friendly debate, like Cambodia's tourism
   debate." What the student does: the student gives one
   sentence for each side before choosing their own.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first** (lesson/lesson.md): the likes round —
   say three likes and one dislike; your companion must
   echo truly, never copy.
2. **The echoes for yes-worlds**: "So do I" (Mongolia
   Grade 6's box, p.80, with its chain game; Cambodia
   Grade 10's leisure unit).
3. **The echoes for no-worlds**: "Neither do I / Nor do I"
   when you share the no; "I don't" when you do not
   (Cambodia); Nepal's So/Neither + auxiliary (Grade 10,
   Unit 16 — the helper verb changes with the sentence).
4. **Stating a view**: "I think… / In my opinion…" and
   Mauritania's argument frame — claim, reasons (First…
   Second… Finally…), conclusion.
5. **Agreeing and disagreeing with a view**: "I agree /
   I agree, but… / I don't agree / I disagree / You're
   right / You're wrong" (Cambodia's debate unit;
   Mauritania's phrase bank; Uzbekistan's letters for and
   against a school system).
6. **Do it yourself**: an echo round of six, one framed
   opinion with two reasons, and one kind disagreement
   written as a short for/against letter, Uzbekistan's way.

## If the learner is stuck
- Says "So do I" to a no-sentence ("I don't like tea —
  So do I") → the see-saw picture: no-worlds echo with
  Neither; So belongs to yes-worlds only.
- Disagrees by attacking ("You're wrong! Nonsense!") →
  Mauritania files those words in its phrase bank, but the
  lesson's working shape is Cambodia's: "I agree, but…" and
  "I don't agree" + a reason. Strong words exist; reasons
  persuade.
- This lesson's neighbours: Making suggestions (answers to
  ideas); Giving advice (opinions with should — its own
  lesson).
