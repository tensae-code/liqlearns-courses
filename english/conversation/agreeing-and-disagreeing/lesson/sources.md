**Acceptance count: taught in 12 countries (Cambodia, Mauritania, Mongolia, Nepal, Uzbekistan,
China, Mexico, Nicaragua, Rwanda, Sierra Leone,
South Sudan, Sudan).** Rwanda joins with agreeing inside Primary 5's quiz dialogue and Primary 6's two class debates (stocked 2026-11-11 by the re-verification sweep merge). Mongolia
teaches the short echoes first, in Grade 6, with a box and
a chain game; Cambodia works the echoes over leisure likes
in Grade 10 and builds the opinion frames into a full
debate unit; Nepal names a Grade 10 unit for agreeing and
disagreeing expressions and teaches So/Neither +
auxiliary in the unit before it; Uzbekistan teaches
agreeing and disagreeing through for-and-against letters
in Grade 9; Mauritania's Grade 12 book supplies the
argument frame — claim, reasons, conclusion — and the
full phrase bank from its argument lessons. China carries
the smallest first step of the strand: agreeing with a
negative — "Me, neither" — inside its Grade 3 likes unit
(stocked 2026-11-08 by the re-verification sweep).
The sweep also stocked Mauritania's other agreement teaching:
the Grade 9 forms, the Grade 10 polite/informal set, and Grade
13's full and partial agreement with clarification (stocked
2026-11-09 by the Mauritania re-verification sweep merge). Mexico's Grade 8 book practises agreement as a civic skill: tombola games designed for democratic decision-making and assertive attitudes (stocked 2026-11-09 by the Mexico re-verification sweep merge).
