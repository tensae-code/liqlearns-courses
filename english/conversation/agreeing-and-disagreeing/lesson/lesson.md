the echoes, the opinion frames, and the friendly argument.

## Do it first
Say three things you like and one thing you do not like, to
your companion: "I like fishing. I don't like getting up
early." Your companion must answer each one TRULY — same as
you, or different — never just copying. Then change places.

You just did the whole lesson. Now let's see how it works.

## Then the explanation
Agreeing has two sizes: the **echo**, for likes and habits,
and the **argument**, for views. The books teach the echo
first.

**The echo in a yes-world — "So do I."** "I like swimming."
— "So do I." Mongolia's Grade 6 book boxes it beside telling
the time (the SO DO I / NOR DO I box, printed p.80) and
practises it in a chain game; its Grammar Reference sums the
unit as "So do I / Nor do I ('me too')." Cambodia's Grade 10
leisure unit teaches the same agreement over free-time
likes: "So do I," "Neither do I," "I don't."

**The echo in a no-world — "Neither do I."** "I don't like
tea." — "Neither do I." (Mongolia writes it "Nor do I" —
same echo, second dress.) And when your truth differs, say
so plainly: "I don't like tea." — "I do." Cambodia's set
holds all three answers side by side, because agreeing
honestly sometimes means not agreeing. Nepal's Grade 10
book teaches "So / Neither + auxiliary" as its confirming
grammar (Unit 16): the echo carries the sentence's own
helper — So do I, Neither did she, So has he.

**Stating a view — the frame.** For bigger things than
likes, English frames the view: **"I think…" "In my
opinion…"** Cambodia's debate unit ("There are always two
sides") builds on exactly these frames. Mauritania's Grade
12 book gives the frame its full skeleton — the argument
frame box: claim ("In my view / I think…") → 2–3 reasons
("First… Second… Finally…") → conclusion — and its writing
task then argues the OTHER side.

**Agreeing and disagreeing with a view.** Full agreement:
**"I agree." "You're right."** Agreement with a door open:
**"I agree, but…"** — Cambodia's debate set holds it beside
"I don't agree." Honest disagreement: **"I don't agree."
"I disagree."** Mauritania's phrase bank, from a
grandmother-and-granddaughter argument, runs hotter —
"I agree / I disagree / you're right / you're wrong /
Nonsense!" — the words exist, and this lesson files them;
but the working shape the books build is disagreement with
reasons. Uzbekistan's Grade 9 book teaches the whole move
in writing: ways of agreeing and disagreeing through an
article on British schools and letters for and against
them — claim, reasons, the other side answered.

## Do it yourself
1. **The echo round.** Six sentences from your companion —
   three yes-world, three no-world. Echo each one truly:
   So do I, Neither do I, or your differing truth.
2. **One framed view.** State one real opinion in the full
   frame: "In my opinion… First… Second… Finally…" Let your
   companion answer: agree, agree-but, or disagree — with a
   reason.
3. **Write both sides.** Like Uzbekistan's letters: write
   four lines FOR one school question and four lines
   AGAINST it. The against lines must be as honest as the
   for lines.
