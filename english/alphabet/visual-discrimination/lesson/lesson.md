# Visual Discrimination

## Do it first

Look at these two letters: **b** and **d**. They are almost the same — but not the same. Reading starts with eyes that can catch small differences like that. This lesson trains those eyes, the way the books train them before letters begin: telling alike from different, matching, and sorting by what you see.

## Sub lesson 1 — Alike and different

*Difficulty: first step — for the youngest learners, and for anyone whose eyes are new to close looking.*

Put two things in front of you — two cups, two leaves, two stones. Look slowly. Ask two questions:

- **How are they the same?** Same colour? Same shape? Same size?
- **How are they different?** One bigger? One darker? One with a spot the other does not have?

That is the whole skill: **telling alike from different**. The books practise it with the odd one out: a row of things where all are the same but one. Look at each one. Find the one that is not like the others, and say why.

Try it now: make a row of four things — three the same, one different. Ask someone to find the odd one out and say why it is different. Then swap: they make the row, you find it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Matching and sorting by what you see

*Difficulty: builds on Sub lesson 1 — from spotting differences to using them.*

Eyes that can tell alike from different can also **match**: find the two that belong together. The books match adult animals with their babies — the cow with the calf, the hen with the chick. Look at the shape, the colour, the marks: the baby carries signs of its mother.

And they can **sort**: put things into groups by what you see —

- by **colour**: all the red ones here, all the blue ones there;
- by **shape**: the round ones, the long ones, the ones with corners;
- by **size**: and then arrange one group in order, biggest to smallest, left to right.

Try it now: gather a handful of mixed things — buttons, stones, leaves. Sort them by colour. Then sort the same things again by size, and lay one group out in order from smallest to biggest.

## Say it to your companion

Read this lesson aloud to your companion — a toy, a pet, a person. Then show them two things and explain, in your own words: how are they alike, and how are they different? Use the words: same, different, match, sort, colour, shape, size.

## Do It Yourself

1. Find the odd one out in a row someone makes for you — and say why it is the odd one.
2. Match five adult animals with their babies (in pictures or in real life) and say what signs told you.
3. Sort a mixed handful of things by colour, then by shape, then by size.
4. Lay five things in a line by size, smallest to biggest, left to right.
