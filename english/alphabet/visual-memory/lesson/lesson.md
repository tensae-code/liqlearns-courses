# Visual Memory

## Do it first

Look around the room for ten seconds. Now close your eyes. How many things can you name that you just saw? Where was each one? That holding-of-a-picture in your mind is **visual memory** — and like everything else, it grows when you practise it. This lesson is that practice, in the shape the books give it: remembering things and patterns, and remembering experiences.

## Sub lesson 1 — Remembering things and patterns

*Difficulty: first step — for the youngest learners, and for anyone new to memory games.*

The game is simple. Put three things in front of you — say a cup, a stone and a leaf. Look at them well. Now cover them, or turn away. Say what was there: “A cup, a stone and a leaf.” Uncover and check. Did you miss one? Look again, and try again.

Patterns work the same way. Look at a pattern — red, blue, red, blue — cover it, and say it back. Then build it again from memory and check it against the first one.

The trick is to look *on purpose*: name each thing quietly in your head as you look. A thing you named is a thing you remember.

Try it now: start with three things. When three is easy, try four, then five.

## Sub lesson 2 — Remembering and describing experiences

*Difficulty: builds on Sub lesson 1 — from things on a table to things that happened.*

Memory holds more than objects. It holds **experiences** — things that happened. Think of something you did today: walking to school, eating, playing. Close your eyes and see it again. Where were you? Who was there? What happened first, and what happened next?

Now describe it — out loud, from memory, in order. Describing is what turns a picture in your mind into something you can share. If you get stuck, close your eyes and look at the picture again.

Try it now: describe yesterday's journey to school or to the market — every place you passed, in order — without looking at anything.

## Say it to your companion

Read this lesson aloud to your companion — a toy, a pet, a person. Then play the covering game with them: show them three things, cover them, and let them say what was there. Explain, in your own words: what is visual memory?

## Do It Yourself

1. Play the covering game with three things, then four, then five.
2. Look at a pattern for ten seconds, cover it, build it again from memory, and check.
3. Describe one thing that happened yesterday, in order, from memory.
4. Name everything on one shelf or one table from memory — then look, and count what you missed.
