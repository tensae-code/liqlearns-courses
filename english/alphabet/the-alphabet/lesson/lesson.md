# The alphabet — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Alphabet · Lesson: The alphabet
Built from: Libya, English for Libya Pupils Book Grade 1, Unit 1
"The Alphabet" — and Egypt, English KG1, KG2 and Primary 1
(the letters and their sounds).

## Do it first
Say the letters you already know, out loud, in order, as far as you can
go. Stop where you stop. That stopping place is where this lesson
starts working.

## Sub lesson 1 — Alphabet A to Z

So the alphabet is the whole tool box of English reading. Every word
you will ever read is built from these 26 letters — nothing else. Learn
the 26, and no word can hide from you; it can only be new, never
locked.

Each letter has three things:
- a NAME — what we call it when we say the alphabet: A, B, C…
- a SHAPE — a big one (capital) and a small one: A and a, B and b.
  Same letter, two coats.
- a SOUND — the noise it makes inside a word. A says "a" in apple.

Here they are, the way Libya's book lines them up, one word for each
letter — the word starts with that letter's sound:

A apple · B ball · C car · D duck · E elephant · F flower · G gate ·
H hand · I insect · J jam · K kite · L lemon · M monkey · N nose ·
O orange · P plane · Q queen · R rocket · S sun · T tomato ·
U umbrella · V violin · W watch · X x-ray · Y yo-yo · Z zebra

Picture them as a train of 26 cars. A is the engine. Z is the last
car. When you sing them in order, the train moves; when one car is
missing, you feel the gap. That feeling is the alphabet working.

## Sub lesson 2 — Alphabet by sounds

Now, a second way books teach them — Egypt does not start at A. Egypt's
first letters are T and I, then S, A, and N: a few letters at a time,
grouped by their sounds. Libya's second unit uses that same group —
s, a, t, p, i, n — right after the alphabet. Both ways are ways of
teaching the same 26 letters, so both stay in this lesson:
- The ORDER way (Libya Unit 1): the whole train, A to Z.
- The SOUNDS way (Egypt KG1, Libya Unit 2): a few letters at a time,
  grouped by sound.

(Joining those sounds together to read whole words is a different
thing from knowing the letters — it is the next lesson:
Blending sounds.)

## Say it to your companion
Read the 26 letters out loud to your companion, big name by big name.
Then cover the list and explain: what are the three things every
letter has? (A name, two shapes, a sound.)

## Do It Yourself
1. Sing or say the whole alphabet, A to Z, three times today. Mark
   the letter where you stop or stumble — that letter is tomorrow's
   first work.
2. Write the alphabet in capitals. Under each capital, write its small
   letter. A a, B b, C c…
3. Pick five letters and say each one's word from the list:
   "A — apple." Your mouth saying the sound is the lesson landing.
