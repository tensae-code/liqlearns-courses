# Labelling

## Do it first

Look at a drawing of a mango with the word **mango** written
under it. Now look at the same drawing with no word at all.

The first drawing speaks: it tells anyone who sees it what
the thing is. The word under the picture is its label, and
labelling — drawing a thing and writing its true name beside
it — is some of the first real writing you will ever do.

## Sub lesson 1 — Drawing and labelling objects

**Friendly opener.** A label is a name tag for a picture.
You draw the thing; you write its name; the two together do
what neither does alone.

**The shape of it.** Ghana's Basic 1 standard sets the work:
draw simple pictures of peers or various objects and label
them. A good label is:
- The thing's **true name** — a drawing of a cup is labelled
  *cup*, not *drink*.
- **Readable** — written clearly enough for another person
  to read; labels are for readers, not only for the writer.
- **Rightly placed** — beside or under the thing it names,
  close enough that nobody can mistake which thing it
  belongs to.
- **One name for one thing** — if the drawing has three
  things, it needs three labels.

**Work it.**
- Draw a hen. Labels: *hen* under the whole bird; then, as
  your drawing grows, *beak*, *wing*, *leg* beside their
  parts.
- Draw your school bag. Label: *bag*, *strap*, *book* (for
  the book inside, if you drew it).

**Watch out.** A label with the wrong name is worse than no
label: it teaches the reader a falsehood. If you do not know
a thing's name, ask — do not guess a label.

*Difficulty: first steps.*

## The companion

Draw something for your companion without labelling it.
They must guess the thing and write its label. Check
together: is the label the true name, readable, and rightly
placed? Then change places.

## Sub lesson 2 — Labelling the world around you

**Friendly opener.** Labelling leaves the single object and
walks outside. Ghana's Basic 2 standard widens the work:
draw and label objects found in their environment.

**The shape of it.**
- Draw a place — your classroom, your home, the farm — and
  label everything in it: *door, window, table, tree,
  well, path*.
- Draw people too: Ghana's Basic 1 wording includes peers —
  a drawing of your friend, labelled with their name, is a
  label at work.
- Labels can grow into labelled diagrams: a plant with
  *root, stem, leaf, flower* is labelling carrying a whole
  science lesson.
- Keep the three rules from Sub lesson 1: true name,
  readable, rightly placed.

**Work it.**
- A classroom drawing, labelled: *blackboard, desk, chair,
  window, door, teacher*.
- A farm drawing, labelled: *yam, hoe, fence, goat, tree*.

**Watch out.** Do not crowd. Ten clear labels teach more
than thirty squeezed ones nobody can read. If a drawing
fills up, start a second drawing.

*Difficulty: the full move.*

## Do It Yourself

Draw your own room or your walk to school, and label
everything you drew — at least eight labels, each one a true
name, readable and rightly placed. You have labelled the way
this lesson teaches.
