# Lesson Plan — Early Writing Stages

**One thing:** how writing grows before it is real spelling — a child's marks climb six stages, from a drawing that means something, through scribbles and letter-like shapes and strings of letters, to invented spelling, and at last to conventional spelling; and the body gets ready alongside, with posture and grip.

## Sub lessons

1. Drawing and scribbling — the first writing
2. Letter-like forms and random letters
3. Invented spelling — writing the sounds you hear
4. Conventional spelling — and a body ready to write

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Teaching points

- Early writing climbs six categories in order: Drawing (the drawing carries meaning) → Scribbling (writing distinguished from drawing) → Letter-like forms and symbols (lines, zigzags, loops) → Random letters or letter strings (mostly upper case; letters may be reversed while directionality is learned) → Writing with invented spelling → Writing with conventional spelling (NEL Guide, Ch.2 Learning Goal 4 support, pp.20–22).
- Invented spelling means writing the letters for the sounds you hear — often only the first sound at first — and spaces begin to appear between words (pp.20–22).
- Even at conventional spelling, irregular spellings such as *blue* and *shoe* are still invented for a while (pp.20–22).
- Handwriting readiness is taught alongside: good posture, shoulder stability, visual perception and pre-writing mark-making; children are NOT expected to perfect handwriting by the end of preschool (p.23).
- The hand's grip grows too: from a cylindrical grasp to the recommended tripod grasp, and letters are written "with proper posture and the recommended pencil grip" (pp.52, 78–79).
- A child's writing development is tracked against the six categories, using dated samples of the child's drawing and writing with anecdotal notes (pp.113, 117–118).
