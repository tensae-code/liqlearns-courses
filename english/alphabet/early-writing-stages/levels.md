# Levels — Early Writing Stages

One lesson, four sub lessons. A sub lesson opens by proving the previous one (at most 5 questions, mixed with earlier parts, drawn from the whole question set of the sub lesson just before — no hardness ranking is written in questions/).

| Sub lesson | What the learner can do at the end |
|---|---|
| 1. Drawing and scribbling — the first writing | Say how writing begins: a drawing that carries meaning, then scribbling that the child already treats as different from drawing. |
| 2. Letter-like forms and random letters | Recognise the next two stages: marks that look like letters (lines, zigzags, loops), then real letters strung together at random, mostly upper case, sometimes reversed. |
| 3. Invented spelling — writing the sounds you hear | Explain invented spelling — letters written for the sounds heard, often only the first sound — and read simple invented spellings; know that spaces appear at this stage. |
| 4. Conventional spelling — and a body ready to write | Say what conventional spelling is (most sounds represented, some irregular spellings still invented), and get the body ready to write: posture, grip growing from cylindrical grasp to tripod grasp, and writing tracked against the six stages with dated samples. |

A learner who finishes all four sub lessons can look at any young child's page and say which stage of writing it shows — and sit ready to write their own.
