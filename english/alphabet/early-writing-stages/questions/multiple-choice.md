# Multiple Choice — Early Writing Stages

(Every item is marked with the sub lesson it belongs to: (Sub lesson N). The gate pool for a sub lesson is all of that sub lesson's questions — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the whole set is the pool.)

1. What is the very first stage of early writing? (Sub lesson 1)
   a) Invented spelling
   b) Drawing — the drawing carries meaning ✓
   c) Random letters
   d) Conventional spelling

2. In the scribbling stage, what has changed from the drawing stage? (Sub lesson 1)
   a) The child writes real letters
   b) The child treats the scribble as different from a drawing — as writing ✓
   c) Spaces appear between words
   d) The child spells words the way books do

3. Which marks belong to the letter-like forms stage? (Sub lesson 2)
   a) Whole sentences
   b) Lines, zigzags and loops that look like letters ✓
   c) Only numbers
   d) Printed book text

4. In the random-letters stage, what is true? (Sub lesson 2)
   a) The letters spell real words
   b) The letters are lower case only
   c) The letters are mostly upper case, strung together, and some may be reversed ✓
   d) Spaces separate every word

5. What is invented spelling? (Sub lesson 3)
   a) Copying words from a book
   b) Writing letters for the sounds you hear, often only the first sound at first ✓
   c) Guessing letters at random
   d) Tracing letters on dotted lines

6. A child writes DG for *dog*. This shows: (Sub lesson 3)
   a) Random letters
   b) Scribbling
   c) Invented spelling — letters for the sounds heard ✓
   d) Conventional spelling

7. What is conventional spelling? (Sub lesson 4)
   a) The first stage of writing
   b) Most sounds represented and words spelled the way books spell them, though irregular spellings like *blue* and *shoe* may still be invented ✓
   c) Writing with no letters at all
   d) Copying a teacher's writing exactly

8. Which grip is the recommended one for writing letters? (Sub lesson 4)
   a) The cylindrical grasp — the whole fist around the pencil
   b) Holding the pencil between two hands
   c) The tripod grasp — thumb and two fingers ✓
   d) Pinching the very end of the pencil with all five fingers
