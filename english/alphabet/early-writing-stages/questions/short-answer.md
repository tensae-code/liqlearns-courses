# Short Answer — Early Writing Stages

(Every item is marked with the sub lesson it belongs to: (Sub lesson N). The gate pool for a sub lesson is all of that sub lesson's questions — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the whole set is the pool.)

1. Name the six stages of early writing, in order. (Sub lesson 1)
   — Drawing; Scribbling; Letter-like forms and symbols; Random letters or letter strings; Writing with invented spelling; Writing with conventional spelling.

2. A child draws a dog and says, "This says *dog*." Which stage is this, and why? (Sub lesson 1)
   — The drawing stage: the drawing carries the meaning.

3. A page shows zigzags, loops and a shape like the letter O, but no real letters. Which stage is it? (Sub lesson 2)
   — Letter-like forms and symbols.

4. A child writes the letter B backwards inside a string of letters. Should the child be corrected as careless? Why or why not? (Sub lesson 2)
   — No. In the random-letters stage, letters may be reversed while the child is still learning which direction letters face; it is part of the stage.

5. A child writes SN for *sun*. What stage is this, and what is the child doing? (Sub lesson 3)
   — Invented spelling: writing letters for the sounds heard.

6. What new thing appears between words at the invented-spelling stage? (Sub lesson 3)
   — Spaces.

7. Name two things that get the body ready to write. (Sub lesson 4)
   — Any two of: good posture; shoulder stability; visual perception; pre-writing mark-making practice.

8. How is a child's writing development tracked against the stages? (Sub lesson 4)
   — By keeping dated samples of the child's drawing and writing, with anecdotal notes, and reading them against the six categories.
