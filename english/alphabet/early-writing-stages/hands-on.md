# Hands-On — Early Writing Stages

Things you do with real things around you. You need paper, crayons or pencils of different thicknesses, and a table.

1. **Climb the ladder yourself.** On one page, make the six stages in order: a drawing that means something; a line of scribble-writing; letter-like forms (a zigzag, a loop, a line and a circle); a string of capital letters in any order; three words in invented spelling; the same three words in conventional spelling. Say each stage's name as you do it.
2. **Read invented spelling.** A partner writes five words the invented way (first sounds only, then more sounds). Read each one aloud. Then write the conventional spelling beside it.
3. **Grip check.** Hold a fat crayon in a whole-fist cylindrical grasp and make marks. Then hold a pencil in a tripod grasp — thumb and two fingers — and write your name. Feel how the tripod grip lets the fingers move the pencil.
4. **Posture check.** Sit slumped and write a line. Then sit with feet flat, back straight, paper square in front of you, and write the same line. Compare the two lines.
5. **Date and keep.** Write today's date on your ladder page and keep it. In a month, make the page again and compare — that is tracking writing against the stages with dated samples.
