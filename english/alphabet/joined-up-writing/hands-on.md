# Hands-on — Joined-up writing

You do it, we guide you. Everything here is done on real
paper, with a companion or the Explain Companion.

1. **The four joins, by hand.** Write each of Barbados's
   example pairs five times, saying the join as you go:
   ai, ar, un (diagonal) · ou, vi, wi (horizontal) ·
   ab, ul, it (to a letter with an ascender) · ol, wh, ot
   (horizontal, to a letter without an ascender). The
   pencil may not lift inside a pair.
2. **Trace, then copy (the Kuwait routine).** Take one
   short sentence. Write it once lightly in grey pencil
   as the model, trace over it, then copy it twice on
   fresh lines with no model showing. Forty pages of
   Kuwait's Handwriting Book 3A run on exactly this
   routine — two model sentences a page (printed
   pp.11–50).
3. **The Bangladesh steps.** Day one: cursive capitals
   A–Z on ruled lines. Day two: cursive small letters
   a–p, with a picture word in cursive under each group.
   Day three: q–z. Day four: the days of the week in
   cursive, in the right order — Saturday first, the way
   Bangladesh's Class 4 book lists them (Units 9–12).
4. **The one-run name.** Write your own name, your
   companion's name, and your street's name, each in one
   run. Count the lifts. Tomorrow's warm-up is the name
   with the most lifts today.
