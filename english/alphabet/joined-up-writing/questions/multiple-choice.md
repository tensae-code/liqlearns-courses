# Joined-up writing — multiple choice

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — The exit flick, and Sub lesson 2 — The four basic joins (split item by item) · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 3 — Steps into whole words, except its item 2 (what an ascender is — taught inside the four joins), which is Sub lesson 2 · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 4 — Trace then copy.

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** In cursive, what joins one letter to the next?
a) a space  b) the first letter's exit flick  c) a full stop
**Answer: b) the first letter's exit flick** — the flick is the bridge the next letter begins on.

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** The pair "ou" is one of Barbados's examples of —
a) a diagonal join  b) a horizontal join  c) a capital join
**Answer: b) a horizontal join** — ou, vi, wi run flat along the line (Barbados, Scope and Sequence 4.6.5).

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** In Bangladesh's Class 4 book, what is taught FIRST?
a) whole sentences  b) cursive small letters q–z  c) cursive capital letters A–Z
**Answer: c) cursive capital letters A–Z** — Unit 9, printed p.19; the small letters follow in Units 10–11.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** An ascender is —
a) the tall part of a letter that reaches up  b) the bridge between letters  c) the grey model line
**Answer: a) the tall part of a letter that reaches up** — Barbados's joins are sorted by whether the next letter has one (ab, ul, it) or not (ol, wh, ot).

**5. (Sub lesson 4)** In Kuwait's Handwriting Book, the learner —
a) traces the grey model, then copies the solid model unaided  b) copies once and stops  c) learns the joins from diagrams
**Answer: a) traces the grey model, then copies the solid model unaided** — the routine on all 40 practice pages, printed pp.11–50; the book teaches no joins.
