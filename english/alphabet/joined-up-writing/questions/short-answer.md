# Joined-up writing — short answer

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 2 — The four basic joins, and Sub lesson 5 — Manuscript or cursive (split item by item) · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 3 — Steps into whole words · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 4 — Trace then copy.

Answer in a word or a short line.

**1. (Sub lesson 2)** Name the four kinds of join in Barbados's syllabus list.
**Answer: diagonal joins; horizontal joins; joins to a letter with an ascender; horizontal joins to a letter without an ascender** — Scope and Sequence 4.6.5, with examples ai/ar/un, ou/vi/wi, ab/ul/it, ol/wh/ot.

**2. (Sub lesson 5)** What is print-style, unjoined writing called, and what is joined writing called?
**Answer: manuscript; cursive** — Barbados's Class 3 syllabus sets the two side by side as the writer's choice.

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** Write the word "sun" in cursive, in one run, and say how many times the pencil lifted inside the word.
**Answer: sun written joined, 0 lifts** — inside a word the pencil stays down; lifts happen between words, not inside them.

**4. (Sub lesson 4)** Your companion's joined writing climbs off the line. What do the four-line guides do, and which country's book writes on them?
**Answer: the letter's body sits between the middle lines and ascenders reach to the top line, so every letter keeps its size and seat; Kuwait's Handwriting Book 3A** — its practice pages, printed pp.11–50, are ruled with four-line guides throughout.
