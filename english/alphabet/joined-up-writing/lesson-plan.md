# Lesson plan — Joined-up writing

**Course:** English · **Title:** Alphabet · **Lesson:** Joined-up writing
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — The Alphabet —
the second lesson of the unit (CURRICULUM.md;
CATCH-UP-PLAN.md places joined-up writing inside Unit 2).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** joining letters
together in cursive — the letter does not lift the pencil
at the end of its shape; it flicks out and runs straight
into the next letter, so a whole word is written in one
run.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown changing — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The exit flick** — a cursive letter does not end flat — it ends with a little flick, and that flick is the bridge the next letter begins on. (Was part of depth Level 1 — one pair, one join.)
2. **The four basic joins** — Barbados's four kinds of bridge: diagonal joins (ai, ar, un); horizontal joins (ou, vi, wi); joins to a letter with an ascender (ab, ul, it); horizontal joins to a letter without an ascender (ol, wh, ot). (Was part of depth Level 1 — one pair, one join.)
3. **Steps into whole words** — Bangladesh's climb: cursive capitals A–Z, then cursive small letters a–p, then q–z, with picture words, then the days of the week in cursive. (Was depth Level 2 — whole words in one run.)
4. **Trace then copy** — Kuwait's routine on four-line guides: trace the grey model first, then copy the solid model unaided. (Was part of depth Level 3 — whole lines, traced then copied.)
5. **Manuscript or cursive** — print-style writing is manuscript, joined writing is cursive — both hands are kept, and the writer chooses the one that fits the job. (Was part of depth Level 3 — whole lines, traced then copied.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The alphabet (the letter shapes this
  lesson joins) and the Pencil and Lines course (the hand).
  Recommended, never forced.
- Forming single letters on their own — print-style
  (manuscript) writing — is the Alphabet lesson's work.
  This lesson is the JOINS only: how letters hold hands.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name and show the four basic joins Barbados's syllabus
   lists: diagonal joins and horizontal joins, to letters
   with ascenders and to letters without (ai, ar, un;
   ou, vi, wi; ab, ul, it; ol, wh, ot).
2. Join any two letters without lifting the pencil, using
   the first letter's exit flick as the bridge.
3. Write whole words and short sentences in cursive, the
   way Bangladesh's Class 4 book moves from cursive
   capitals (A–Z) to cursive small letters (a–p, then
   q–z) to picture words and the days of the week in
   cursive.
4. Keep the hand steady with Kuwait's routine: trace the
   grey model first, then copy the solid model unaided,
   on four-line guides.

## What you need
Paper with four-line guides if you can get it (the lines
Kuwait's Handwriting Book writes on), and a pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner writes "cat" in separate
   letters, then tries to write it without lifting the
   pencil once (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the top of the lesson: a picture that
     moves — a pencil writing c-a-t in one run, the joins
     glowing as the pencil crosses them, 1–2 seconds —
     the student watches where the pencil does NOT lift,
     then tries the same run.
2. **The explanation** — the exit flick; the four basic
   joins (Barbados, syllabus evidence); cursive capitals
   then small letters then whole words (Bangladesh);
   trace-then-copy (Kuwait) (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD IMAGE under the four joins: a picture of four
     joined pairs standing in a row — ai, ou, ab, ol —
     each join drawn thick — the student names each pair's
     join kind before writing it.
   - ADD IMAGE under the Bangladesh steps: a picture of
     a cursive capital A beside the cursive small letters
     a to p on ruled lines — the student points at the
     step they are on today.
3. **Say it to your companion** — write a joined word for
   your companion to read back; then swap
   (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed —
     one word joined letter by letter in the super-simple
     voice (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — the four joins as pairs, three
   whole words in one run each, and the days of the week
   in cursive (lesson/lesson.md).

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)

- IMAGE — a picture of the four join pairs (ai, ou, ab, ol)
  → the student names each join kind.

  writes along, one run per word.

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the

## If the learner is stuck
- Lifts the pencil between every letter → the join IS the
  exit flick: practise one pair (a→i, o→u) ten times
  before any whole word. The flick comes first; speed
  comes by itself.
- Joins climb off the line → go back to four-line guides
  (Kuwait's page furniture): body of the letter between
  the middle lines, ascenders reaching up, then re-enter.
- This lesson's neighbours: The alphabet (before — the
  shapes); Blending sounds (next door in Unit 3 — the
  sounds, a different thing).
