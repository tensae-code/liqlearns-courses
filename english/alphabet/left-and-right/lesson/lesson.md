# Left and Right

## Do it first

Hold both hands up in front of you, palms facing away. Wiggle the fingers of one hand, then the other. One of these hands is your **left** hand. The other is your **right** hand. This lesson teaches you to know them apart — every time, without guessing.

## Sub lesson 1 — Your left hand and your right hand

Put your left hand flat on the page and draw around it. Do the same with your right hand. Count the fingers on each drawing: five and five — the hands look the same, and that is why they are easy to mix up.

Here is a way that never changes: hold both hands up with the backs facing you and your thumbs pointing at each other. The hand on the left side of your body is your left hand. The hand on the right side of your body is your right hand. Left and right belong to YOUR body — when you turn around, your left hand turns with you.

*Difficulty: Two hands that look the same, told apart by the side of your own body.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Your writing side and your kicking side

Most people write with one hand and kick a ball with one foot — and it is fine for that to be the left side or the right side. Show which hand you write with: that is your writing hand. Show which foot you kick with: that is your kicking foot. For most people the writing hand and the kicking foot are on the same side — your strong side.

Knowing your left from your right is the start of directions: turn left, look right, the page that comes next.

*Difficulty: Your own strong side, named.*

## Say it to your companion

Hold up your left hand and say "left". Hold up your right hand and say "right". Then tell your companion which hand you write with and which foot you kick with.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw around both hands and write L on the left drawing and R on the right drawing.
2. Stand up. Touch your left ear with your left hand, then your right knee with your right hand.
3. Ask someone at home: which hand do you write with? Show them your own writing hand.
