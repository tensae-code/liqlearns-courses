# The Odd One Out

## Do it first

Look at this row: 🍎 🍎 🍌 🍎. Four fruits — but one of them is not like the others. Did you spot it? Finding the one that does not belong is called finding the **odd one out**, and it is one of the first reading skills: your eye must catch what is different before your eye can learn letters.

## Sub lesson 1 — Spot the one that is different

Look at the whole row first. Then look at each thing one by one. Three of them belong together — they are the same, or the same kind. One is different. That one is the odd one out.

Try it: 🐶 🐶 🐱 🐶 — the cat is the odd one out. And here: ⭕ ⭕ ⭕ ⬜ — the square is the odd one out.

The difference can be the shape, the kind, the colour, or which way the thing faces. Look slowly; the odd one is always there.

*Difficulty: A row of things that look almost the same — and one that is not.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Say why it is different

Spotting is half the skill. The other half is saying WHY: "The banana is the odd one out because the others are apples." "The square is the odd one out because the others are round."

When you can say why, your eye truly saw the difference — and that same eye will soon tell b from d and was from saw.

*Difficulty: The difference, put into words.*

## Say it to your companion

Make a row of four things for your companion — three the same, one different. Ask: "Which is the odd one out? Why?" Then swap: your companion makes a row for you.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw a row of four shapes with one odd one out. Circle the odd one.
2. Under it, write one line: "The ___ is the odd one out because ___."
3. Do it once more — this time make the odd one out harder to spot.
