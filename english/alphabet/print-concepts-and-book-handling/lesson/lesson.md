# Print Concepts and Book Handling

## Do it first

Take a book. It is closed, and it is upside down. Set it
right. Open it at its beginning. Find the first line of print
on the first page and put your finger under its first word.

You used four pieces of knowledge just now — what a book's
parts are, how to hold it, where it starts, and which way its
print runs. This lesson teaches those four on purpose, the
way the first school books teach them before any letters.

## Sub lesson 1 — The parts of a book

A book has parts, and each part has a name:

- The **front cover** — the face of the book. It carries the
  book's name.
- The **back cover** — the book's back.
- The **beginning** — the first page, right after the front
  cover.
- The **end** — the last page, right before the back cover.

On a page there are two kinds of marks: **pictures** and
**print**. Pictures show you things. Print is the part you
read — the letters and words. Point at a page: "Picture.
Print. Picture. Print."

*Difficulty: first steps — naming by pointing, on a real book in the learner's own hands.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Holding and handling a book

Hold the book the right way up — the print stands straight,
not upside down and not on its side.

- Open it at the **front**, never the back.
- Turn **one page at a time**, from the top corner.
- Close it gently when you finish, front cover on top.

Practise: close the book. Open it at the beginning. Turn
three pages, one at a time. Close it. Do it again until your
hands know it without thinking.

*Difficulty: building — the hands repeat until the handling is quiet and sure.*

## Sub lesson 3 — Following the print

Print runs one way, in every book of this course:

- A line runs **left to right**.
- Lines run **top to bottom** down the page.
- When a page ends, the story goes on at the **next page**.

**Tracking** is following the print with your eyes — and at
first with a finger under the line — from the line's start to
its end, then down to the next line, never jumping.

And if a word is **missing** from a line, the line limps.
Read: "The cat sat on the ___." Your eye felt the hole.
Noticing the hole is part of reading.

Practise: track three lines with a finger, slowly. Then read
a line with one word covered. Say the missing place out loud:
"A word is missing here."

*Difficulty: building — eyes and finger move together; the missing-word check makes the tracking honest.*

## Say it to your companion

Show your companion a closed book. Name its parts as you
pick it up, open it at the beginning, and track one line with
your finger, saying which way the print runs. Your companion
checks each move. Then change places.

## Do It Yourself

1. Take any book. Name its front cover, back cover, beginning
   and end, out loud, pointing at each.
2. Close it, put it down upside down, then set it right and
   open it at the beginning three times, until it is easy.
3. Track one whole page with a finger, left to right, top to
   bottom. Then cover one word with a finger and say: "A
   word is missing here."
