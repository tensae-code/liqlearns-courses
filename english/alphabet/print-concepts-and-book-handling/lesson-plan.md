# Lesson plan — Print Concepts and Book Handling

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** Print Concepts and Book Handling
**Lesson:** print-concepts-and-book-handling — the book itself, before the letters — its parts, how to hold it, and which way print runs
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 2 — The Alphabet
**The one thing:** a book is a thing you hold the right way up and open at the front, and its print runs one way — left to right, top to bottom, page after page
**This lesson's own level:** First-words lesson: the groundwork under the alphabet — book parts, handling, then following print.

## Why this lesson exists

No lesson housed what a book is. Sierra Leone's first-year plans teach it before letters: print told apart from pictures, the front cover from the back, how to open and close the book, how to hold it and turn its pages, and the direction print runs. The 2026-11-08 re-verification sweep marked 14 such items NEW LESSON NEEDED. Under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06), a teaching item with no lesson gets a new lesson built; this is that lesson. Its sources are the Sierra Leone plans named in lesson/sources.md — teacher evidence, cited as such.

## Sub lessons

1. **The parts of a book** — front cover, back cover, beginning and end — and print told apart from pictures
2. **Holding and handling a book** — the right way up, opening and closing, turning pages one at a time
3. **Following the print** — directionality — left to right, top to bottom — tracking a line, and noticing a missing word

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is forced first — this lesson stands alone and stands under everything else: it needs no letters at all. It belongs beside holding-a-pencil, which teaches the other half of the same groundwork.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- Name a book's parts: front cover, back cover, beginning, end.
- Hold a book the right way up, open it at the front, and turn its pages one at a time.
- Point to the print and say how it runs: left to right, top to bottom.
- Follow a line of print with a finger from its start to its end.
- Notice when a word is missing from a line.

## What you need
Any real book — the learner's own exercise book or story book is best.

## The run of the lesson
1. **Do it first** — hand the learner a book upside down and closed; they set it right and open it, before anyone teaches the parts (lesson/lesson.md).
2. Work through sub lesson 1: the parts of a book.
3. Work through sub lesson 2: holding and handling.
4. Work through sub lesson 3: following the print — direction and tracking.
5. ADD IMAGE: a book lying open with its parts named — front cover, back cover, first page, last page — the student points and names each on their own book.

## Add-ons
- "Book hunt": the teacher hides a word on one page; the learner opens the book at the front and turns pages one at a time until they find it.
- Upside-down start: every reading time this week begins with the book handed over upside down, and the learner sets it right without a word.

## If the learner is stuck
If a learner turns two pages at once or starts at the back, do not correct with words first — hand the book back closed and let them open it again; the hands learn this, not the ears. If direction is lost mid-line, a finger under the words, moving slowly, is the fix the plans use.
