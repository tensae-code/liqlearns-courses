# Lesson plan — Article writing

**Course:** English · **Title:** Essay · **Lesson:** Article writing
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 37 — "Writing Your View"
(CURRICULUM.md; CATCH-UP-PLAN.md Levels 36–37).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the magazine/newspaper article — a headline, a hook that pulls the reader in (a rhetorical question among its tools), and an ordered body that informs in a voice a stranger enjoys; with its small factual brother, the fact file.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on News writing (Level 36 — the article is news slowed down and given a voice) and on The essay. It is the third lesson of Level 37.
**What the learner can do at the end:**
- write an article whole: headline, hook, ordered body, closing line that lands
- use a rhetorical question as a hook or a turn, and keep the article's tone direct and alive
- analyse and imitate a model article paragraph by paragraph, the way the books teach it
- build a fact file: title, headed sections, short notes, interesting facts, simple language, a picture

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: News writing; The essay. Recommended, never forced.
- An article is not a news report (no five-W lead, no biggest-first body) and not an essay (no thesis to prove): it informs a stranger who is free to stop reading — every paragraph must re-earn them.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a magazine page: a bold headline, a first line ending in a question mark circled, three body columns under it.** Written slot: "a picture of an article with its headline and hook showing." What the student does: the student reads the headline and the hook only, then says whether they would keep reading — and why. That yes is the hook's whole job.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of an animal fact file: a title, four headed boxes (Description / Habitat / Food / Babies), short notes inside each.** Written slot: "a picture of a fact file with headed sections." What the student does: the student points at the box that would answer 'where does it live?' — headed sections are how a fact file is searched, not read.
3. **Slot 3 — a GIF (1–2 seconds) of a fishing line cast over a page: the hook lands in the first line, the page's reader is pulled gently in.** Written slot: "a picture of a hook in an article's first line pulling a reader in." What the student does: the student names the hook in the article in front of them — question, surprise, or promise — before reading on.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first** (lesson/lesson.md): two openings on the same subject — one that informs and bores, one that hooks; the reader's feet decide.
2. **The short article defined**: Egypt's Helpful Hints (Preparatory 9 Term 2) — a piece giving information or ideas about a topic in a clear and simple way; title, introduction, body, conclusion.
3. **The hook**: rhetorical questions and a direct, less formal tone to win attention (Malaysia, Form 5, Unit 4 — an article analysed by paragraph).
4. **The magazine article at length**: Kuwait's 150–170-word magazine article (Over to You 10B, Unit 7); Malaysia's Form 4 magazine articles read (Module 7).
5. **Articles that argue gently**: Vietnam's Grade 11 articles — advantages/disadvantages (Unit 3), pros and cons of self-study (Unit 8) — the body ordered by its points.
6. **The fact file**: Egypt's animal fact file and its Writing Tip (Preparatory 9 Term 2, Unit 9).
7. **Do it yourself**: one article with a hooked opening, one fact file with headed sections.

## If the learner is stuck
- An opening that warms up for a page → the hook must land in the first two lines. Move the most surprising true sentence to the front and cut the warm-up.
- A body that wanders → one point per paragraph, points in a felt order (best first, or the thing's own order). The article borrows the essay's skeleton and the blog's voice.
- Rhetorical questions everywhere → one hook question earns its place; three in a row become a quiz. Use one to open, perhaps one to turn.
- This lesson's next door: Advertisements and posters (same level) — the article's loud little brother: all hook, no body.
