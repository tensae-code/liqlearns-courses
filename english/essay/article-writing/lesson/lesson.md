# Article writing — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Essay · Lesson: Article writing
Built from: Kuwait, Over to You, Student's Book 10B (Unit 7 — a magazine article, 150–170 words) — Malaysia, Form 4 (Module 7 — magazine articles read), Form 5 (Unit 4 — writing an article, analysed by paragraph) — Vietnam, Tiếng Anh 11 (Global Success), Units 3 and 8 (writing articles) — Egypt, Preparatory 9 Term 2 (the short article defined; the animal fact file).

*How this page talks: the explanation is given in the learner's
own language by the speaker button. The TARGET text — the model
wording itself, the lines set as models — is never translated
and is read in English only. This lesson stands alone: you can
start here. Soft hint only: if you know News writing, this course will be easier for you — you might try it first. The article keeps the news's clarity and adds a voice.*

## Do it first
Two openings, same subject — saving water. First: "Water
is an important resource which is used by human beings
for many purposes since ancient times." Accurate; nobody
alive continues. Second: "Do you really have to drive?"
— no wait, that is Kuwait's energy article's model, and
it works on the same law: a question you cannot answer
yet, asked straight at YOU. The first opening informs a
reader who is already trapped. The second catches a
reader who is free to leave. Articles are written for
free readers. Here is how the books catch them.

## Then the explanation
**The kind defined.** Egypt's Preparatory 9 book (Term 2)
defines the short article in its Helpful Hints: a piece
of writing that gives information or ideas about a topic
in a clear and simple way — with a structure of Title
(choose a catchy title), Introduction (one or two
sentences to introduce the main idea), Body (the main
topic, with examples), Conclusion (a simple sentence to
summarize). 100–110 words at that age; the shape does not
change as the size grows.

**The headline.** Short, catchy, carrying the topic and a
reason to look — the news headline's cousin, allowed more
music: where news says "Storm destroys school roof", the
article may say "The Night the Sky Fell In" — but the
topic must still be findable inside it.

**The hook.** Malaysia's Form 5 book (Unit 4, Writing,
pp.52–53) teaches the article's opening by name: getting
the readers' attention with a direct, less formal tone
and RHETORICAL QUESTIONS — questions asked for effect,
not for answer: "Do you really have to drive?" (the
question is Kuwait's model energy article's own title —
the hook wearing the headline's coat). A surprising true
fact and a promise are the hook's other two shapes. The
hook's seat is the first two lines; a hook on line nine
is a rescue, not an opening.

**The body — ordered points.** The article's body is
paragraphs of points, in an order the reader can feel.
Vietnam's Grade 11 book writes the kind twice: an article
of 120–150 words about the advantages and disadvantages
of living in a smart city (Unit 3 — the body ordered by
its two sides), and an article of 150–180 words about the
pros and cons of self-study (Unit 8 — its model itself
phrased as a question, "Living in a smart city: Good or
bad?" is Unit 3's model). Malaysia's Form 4 book (Module
7) reads magazine articles as the kind at full colour —
animal intelligence; eco-friendly products — articles
whose bodies are carried by chosen examples.

**Kuwait's measure.** Over to You 10B sets its compositions
at 150–170 words, the magazine article among them (Unit
7: an article suggesting practical ways to save energy,
modelled on "Do you really have to drive?"). The size
teaches the discipline: at 150 words an article holds a
hook, three points and a landing — nothing else fits,
and nothing else is missed.

**The closing.** Egypt's short article closes with a
simple sentence that summarizes; the grown article closes
with a line that LANDS — the thought the reader carries
out of the page. Summary closes the information; landing
closes the experience. Aim for both in one sentence when
you can.

**The fact file.** Egypt's same book (Unit 9) defines the
article's small factual brother in its Writing Tip: a
fact file is a short report that gives important
information about a topic — an animal, a place, a person,
a machine — in a clear and organized way. Its model is an
animal fact file with headed sections (Physical
Description / Habitat / Adaptations / Diet / Conservation
Status), and its rules: start with a title; use sections;
write short notes; include interesting facts; keep the
language simple; add a picture. A fact file is not read
top to bottom — it is SEARCHED, and its headings are its
index.

## Do it yourself
1. **An article.** Choose a topic you know and love.
   Headline, hook (a rhetorical question or a surprising
   fact), three body paragraphs in a felt order, one
   closing line that lands. About 150 words.
2. **A fact file.** Build a fact file on an animal, a
   place, a person or a machine: title, four or five
   headed sections, short notes, one interesting fact
   each, simple language, a picture or its space.
3. **The surgery.** Take any magazine article and label
   each paragraph's job in the margin, Malaysia-style.
   Where a paragraph has no job, say what the article
   loses by it.
