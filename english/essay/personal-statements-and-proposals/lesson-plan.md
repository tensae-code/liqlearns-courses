# Lesson plan — Personal statements and proposals

**Course:** English · **Title:** Essay · **Lesson:** Personal statements and proposals
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 35, "Writing for a Job".
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the two short
formal pieces that present a person and a plan — the
personal statement (who I am, in prose, for an
application) and the formal proposal (what should be
done, laid out for a decider).
**This lesson's own level:** the personal statement grows
out of the CV's two-sentence profile and becomes a whole
piece; the proposal is the authority letter's sibling —
facts, need, plan, ask — at document length.
**What the learner can do at the end:** write a personal
statement from its model's moves and a short formal
proposal in the books' section shape.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: CV and cover letter. Recommended,
  never forced.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a one-page statement beside a
   CV, the CV's profile line growing into paragraphs.**
   What the student does: the student points at what the
   statement may say that the CV cannot.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of a proposal as four stacked
   cards: NOW, NEED, PLAN, ASK.** What the student does:
   the student deals the cards for a change they want.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first**: a CV line vs a statement paragraph —
   what grew?
2. **The personal statement** (Jordan's model + tips).
3. **The proposal's shape** (Jordan's model for a
   headteacher; Greece's word-counted formal proposal).
4. **Do it yourself**: statement and proposal written.

## If the learner is stuck
- Statement reading like a CV in sentences: add the WHY —
  choices, turns, aims — the CV lists, the statement joins.
- Proposal all wish, no plan: force the four cards; a
  card with nothing on it is the missing work.
- Next in this level: Job interviews.
