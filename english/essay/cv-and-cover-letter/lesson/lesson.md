# CV and cover letter — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Essay · Lesson: CV and cover letter
Built from: Syria, Grade 12, Project 2, "Curriculum
Vitae" (pp.84–86) — Cambodia, Grade 12, Unit 30
"Applying for a job" (printed pp.163–168) and Unit 29's
job adverts — Egypt, Secondary 10 Term 1, Unit 6 (the CV,
Lesson 5; the model resume, Lesson 4) — Nepal, Grade 10,
Unit 11 (the model CV) — India, Hornbill Class XI,
Writing Skills 5 (the CV format, pp.107–115) — Laos,
Book Seven, Grade 12, Unit 10, Lesson 1 — Armenia,
English 11, §1.7 (CV writing, pp.25–27).

*Speaker note: the explanation is read in your own
language. The CV's and letter's own wording — headings,
profile lines, closings — is the TARGET text: never
translated, always read in English.*

*If you have written an application letter, this lesson
builds the paper it encloses — but it stands alone: the
chain is walked whole here.*

## Do it first
A manager opens two envelopes. In the first: a neat
letter — and no CV. In the second: a CV whose sections
run hobbies, a job from six years ago, primary school,
then (on page three) a name. Both writers lose the job
before a word is judged. The pair must arrive together,
and the CV must read in the order a busy stranger needs.
That order is this lesson.

[Media slot: a picture of four cards in a chain — ADVERT,
CV, COVER LETTER, INTERVIEW. What you do: walk the chain
aloud and say what each card carries.]

## Then the explanation

## Sub lesson 1 — CV and cover letter — the chain and the sections

**The chain.** Cambodia's Grade 12 book teaches job
seeking as one arc: job adverts (Unit 29), "Applying for
a job" (Unit 30) and "At a job interview" (Unit 31). Hold
the arc whole: **advert → CV → cover letter →
interview**. Nothing is written in a vacuum — the advert
is read first, and both papers are built from it.

**The CV's sections and order.** Syria's Grade 12 Project
2 (pp.84–86) defines the CV against the one-page resume
and gives NINE steps for writing one: personal details,
profile statement, achievements, education, employment,
qualifications, skills, hobbies, references. Egypt's book
gives the same body in SIX sections (Unit 6, Lesson 5):
personal information; a personal statement/objective of
2–3 sentences; education in REVERSE chronological order —
most recent first; work experience (dates, company, title,
responsibilities and achievements); skills with levels;
and additional sections (certificates, projects,
interests, references). India's Hornbill prints its CV
format in full (W5): name, address, telephone, email,
date of birth, a qualifications table, experience from
the present backwards, skills, languages, hobbies,
achievements, references. Three countries, one skeleton:
**who you are and how to reach you → the worker in one
breath → study, newest first → work, newest first →
skills → the rest → who speaks for you.** Armenia's
Grade 11 §1.7 teaches the same craft as techniques,
formatting rules and the proper order of sections, then
critically reviews a sample student's CV — the order is
checkable, and checked.

## Sub lesson 2 — CV and cover letter — the CV's laws

**The CV's laws.** Egypt states them plainly: keep it
short (1–2 pages maximum), clear simple language, ONLY
relevant information, easy to read, updated regularly —
and it teaches the action verbs that carry experience
lines (managed, developed, assisted, organised…). Nepal's
Grade 10 book teaches by a model CV printed whole in
Unit 11 — the pupil reads a real-shaped life before
writing their own. Laos's Grade 12 job unit (Unit 10,
Lesson 1) does the same with a European-style CV: read
it, answer, then "create your own CV". Cambodia's Unit 30
adds the matching drill: what a CV contains (full name,
date of birth, contact details, work experience,
education, achievements, hobbies), a guide to writing
one, and four missing items to find in a flawed CV —
the CV is learned by repairing one.

## Sub lesson 3 — CV and cover letter — the pair, matched

**The cover letter, matched.** Cambodia coaches its
Unit 30 cover letter paragraph by paragraph to a named
advert (a café waiter post), then sets the learner's own
letter to a second advert and a CV written TO MATCH the
letter already written. That matching is the lesson's
law: letter and CV are one act in two papers — every
claim in the letter stands on a line in the CV, and the
CV carries nothing this advert never asked for.

## Do it yourself
1. Build the hands-on pair: CV and cover letter to the
   Bright Goods advert.
2. The repair drill (Cambodia's way): take your CV, remove
   four items, swap with a companion — each finds the
   other's four missing pieces.
3. Order test: write your CV's section headings on slips,
   shuffle, re-stack in the books' order from memory.
