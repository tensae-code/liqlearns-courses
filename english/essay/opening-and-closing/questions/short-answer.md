# Opening and closing — short answer

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — The Opening — Catch and Name · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — The Closing — Return and Land (item 3), except item 5, writing the opening last -> Sub lesson 3 — The Ends Judged · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3.

Answer in a word or a short line.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Name three catching moves for an opening.
**Answer:** a question the reader wants answered; a small
scene before the eyes; a striking fact (a story's first
breath serves too).

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** After the catch, what must the opening
paragraph still do before it ends?
**Answer:** name — Bolivia's duty: tell what the topic and
the main ideas are, so the reader holds the map.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Write the closing's return for this
opening: "Three reasons make the bicycle the best machine
in our town: it is cheap, it is quick, and it keeps us
strong."
**Answer:** e.g. "In conclusion, the bicycle asks little,
gives speed, and builds the body — no other machine in our
town pays its owner back three ways." — same information,
new words and perspective, signal on, no new point.

**4. (Sub lesson 3)** A writer says their closing "feels flat"
though its facts are right. Diagnose in one line.
**Answer:** flat closings are usually echo closings — the
perspective never changed; write it from what the end
KNOWS that the opening only promised.

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** Why may a writer lawfully write the opening
LAST?
**Answer:** the ends are revision-stage crafts (Ghana's
process: draft, then revise); once the body exists, the
writer knows exactly what must be named and can aim the
catch precisely.

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** State the five faults from memory.
**Answer:** the form opening (names, never catches); the
trick opening (catches, never names); the echo closing
(copied words); the smuggler closing (a new point); the
cliff stop (no return).
