# Lesson plan — Opening and closing

**Course:** English · **Title:** Essay · **Lesson:** Opening and closing
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 4, Unit 3 "The Essay" —
the second lesson of the unit (CURRICULUM.md). Building it
completes Unit 3.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** writing the essay's
two ends well — the opening paragraph, which first catches
the reader's attention and then names the topic and its
main ideas; and the closing paragraph, which returns to
the opening's information from a different perspective, in
fresh words, with no new points.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The essay (the
places exist; this lesson is their craft) and on Ghana's
revise questions, whose first question is this lesson's
opening law.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the
exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an
image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture
of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does
with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and
while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is
dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the
student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph,
or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be
shown changing — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The essay. Recommended, never forced.
- Order of work inside the lesson matters and is stated:
  many writers write the opening LAST (after the body
  exists) — the books' process order (draft, then revise)
  allows it; the plan teaches both ends as revision-stage
  crafts, not as gates to beginning.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Open with a catching first move (a question, a scene, a
   striking fact, a short story-start) and land, within the
   same paragraph, on Bolivia's duty: the topic and main
   ideas named.
2. Close by returning: the introduction's information
   restated from a different perspective — new words, the
   weight of the body behind them — signalled ("In
   conclusion…") and ended on a sentence that lands.
3. Refuse the closing faults: a copied opening (same words
   = no perspective gained), a new point smuggled in, a
   bare "The End" stop.
4. Judge ends by Ghana's questions: does the beginning
   catch attention? Does the ending round the piece off?

## What you need
An essay already drafted (The essay lesson's Do It Yourself
serves). Paper and a pencil.

## The run of the lesson (in order)
1. **Do it first** — the learner reads two openings of the
   same essay and two closings, and ranks them; the catching
   one and the returning one win before any rule is stated
   (lesson/lesson.md).
2. **The explanation** — the opening's two jobs in order
   (catch, then name); the catching moves; the closing's
   return (same information, different perspective); the
   signals; the faults (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD GIF at the opening part: a picture of a market
     caller — first a shout that turns heads, then a clear
     voice naming his goods and prices, 1–2 seconds — the
     student shouts and names for a topic of their own, in
     that order.
   - ADD IMAGE under the closing part: a picture of a river
     bending in a great loop back near its start — same
     water, far bank — the student traces the loop and says
     where their closing stands on it.
   - ADD AUDIO in the faults part: a voice reading an
     opening and then a closing that COPIES it word for
     word — the student calls the fault aloud and supplies
     the changed-perspective version.
3. **Say it to your companion** — ends-only exchange: read
   your partner just your opening and closing; they must
   reconstruct your essay's points from the two ends alone
   (lesson/lesson.md).
   - ADD VIDEO: the script in video-script.md, filmed — the
     caller and the river loop, in the super-simple voice
     (made later, when videos are made).
4. **Questions** — from questions/: multiple-choice, short-answer,
   fill-in-the-blank, true-or-false, matching — served at the
   learner's level and teaching-mix (levels.md).
5. **Hands-on** — hands-on.md.
6. **Do It Yourself** — rewrite both ends of your essay from
   The essay lesson: a catching opening that names, a
   returning closing that lands (lesson/lesson.md).

## Add-ons for this lesson (the full list)
- GIF — a picture of a market caller shouting, then naming
  his goods → the student shouts and names for their topic.
- IMAGE — a picture of a river looping back near its start
  → the student traces it and places their closing.
- AUDIO — a closing that copies its opening word for word →
  the student names the fault and re-says it changed.
- VIDEO — the video-script filmed (made later) → the student
  works the ends along with it.

A slot the builder cannot fill yet stays in the plan as a slot — the
plan is the build sheet, so nothing the lesson needs gets forgotten.

## If the learner is stuck
- Opening names everything but catches nothing → Ghana's
  first revise question is the diagnosis. Move the naming
  down two sentences; let a question or a scene stand first.
  The duty remains — it just stops being the doormat.
- Closing repeats the opening in the same words → Bolivia's
  phrase is the cure: same information, DIFFERENT
  perspective. Ask: "What do you know at the end that the
  opening only promised?" — the closing is written from
  THAT knowledge.
- Closing smuggles a new point → move it to the body plan
  (The essay's rule) or cut it; the closing spends earned
  weight, it does not mint new.
- This lesson completes Unit 3. Next: Unit 4 — Stories,
  the last unit of Level 4.
