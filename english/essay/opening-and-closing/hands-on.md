# Hands-on — Opening and closing

The lesson worked on real ends, cut out and handled.

## Ends on cards
Collect openings and closings (your class's essays, books,
papers) on separate cards. Shuffle the closings away from
their openings: match them back by topic and points alone —
ends that cannot be matched have failed their jobs, and the
table says which fault.

## Three openings for one essay
For one real drafted essay, write three openings: question,
scene, fact. Read them to listeners who vote with their
feet (stand behind the writer of the one they would keep
reading). The naming duty is then checked on the winner:
topic and points present, or the catch was a trick.

## The loop trace
Two colours of string on a printed essay: one from each
promise in the opening to where the closing returns it.
Promises with no string home are openings that over-
promised; closing lines with no string back are smuggled
goods. Repair by string-light first, prose second.

## Sixty-second closings
Given any opening read aloud, improvise a closing in sixty
seconds: signal, return in new words, landing line. The
table scores only two things — perspective CHANGED? line
LANDED? Speed drills make the shape a reflex.
