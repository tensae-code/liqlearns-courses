# Short Answer — Making a Short Documentary Film

(Every item is marked with the sub lesson it belongs to: (Sub lesson N). The gate pool for a sub lesson is all of that sub lesson's questions — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the whole set is the pool.)

You are making a short documentary about the old well in your village.

1. Is your film a documentary if you invent a story about the well? Why? (Sub lesson 1)
   — No. A documentary tells a true story — real people, places and events; an invented story is not a documentary.
2. Name the stages of planning, in order. (Sub lesson 1)
   — Choose the subject; find out about it; decide the story (what the viewer will learn, in one sentence); write the shot list and the interview questions.
3. Write two pieces for your shot list. (Sub lesson 1)
   — Any two filmable pieces, e.g.: the well itself in the early morning; a person drawing water and saying how long the well has been used.
4. Give two rules for shooting well on a phone. (Sub lesson 2)
   — Any two of: hold it steady (two hands or resting on something); get close enough to see faces and hear words; film more pieces than you need; follow the shot list.
5. After filming, what do you do with your pieces? (Sub lesson 2)
   — Watch them all, choose the best pieces, and put them in the order that tells the story clearly.
6. What can your own voice, or words on the screen, do in the finished film? (Sub lesson 2)
   — Explain what the viewer is seeing where a piece cannot explain itself.
