# Lesson plan — Complaint letters

**Course:** English · **Title:** Essay · **Lesson:** Complaint letters
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 34, "Letters That Work".
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the complaint letter
and its other half — the apologetic reply — as one pair:
state the facts, name the trouble, ask for the fix; then
answer with apology, explanation and action.
**This lesson's own level:** it wears The formal letter's
frame; its new work is TONE — firm facts, no rage — and
the reply's three moves.
**What the learner can do at the end:** write a complaint
that gets fixed, and reply to one so the writer feels heard.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The formal letter. Recommended, never
  forced.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of two letters facing each other
   across a desk, arrows showing question and answer.**
   What the student does: the student points at each fact
   in the complaint and finds its answer in the reply.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of a hotel room with three faults
   circled in red.** What the student does: the student
   turns each circled fault into one factual sentence.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first**: a raging complaint vs a factual one —
   which gets the refund?
2. **The complaint's moves** (Greece's hotel pair).
3. **The reply's moves**: apologise, explain, act.
4. **Register under pressure** (Uzbekistan's mixed-style
   complaint, corrected).
5. **Do it yourself**: the hands-on pair, both letters.

## If the learner is stuck
- Rage creeping in: facts only — every sentence must pass
  the "could I prove this?" test.
- Reply defensive: run its three moves as a checklist.
- Next in this level: Letters that ask and advise.
