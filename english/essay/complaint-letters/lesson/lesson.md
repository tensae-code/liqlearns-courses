# Complaint letters — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Essay · Lesson: Complaint letters
Built from: Greece, English for the Hotel and Tourist
Trade, Grade 12 EPAL, Unit 6, "Dealing with Complaints"
(printed pp.99–112) — Uzbekistan, English Matters 9,
Unit 7 (the formal letter of complaint, p.80; the
wrongly-mixed complaint corrected in the Review, p.85) —
Sierra Leone (the letter of complaint; formal letters for
a range of purposes) — Jordan, High Note Grade 12 (the
email of complaint among its taught genres) — Ghana, the
NaCCA curricula (formal writing kinds — curriculum
evidence only; no Ghana pupil textbook exists).

*Speaker note: the explanation is read in your own
language. The letters' own wording is the TARGET text:
never translated, always read in English.*

*If you know the formal letter's frame, this is that
frame under pressure — but it stands alone.*

## Do it first
Two complaints about the same broken shower. A: "Your
hotel is a disgrace!!! Nobody there knows anything, I was
furious all night, worst hotel in the world!!!" B: "I
stayed in Room 12 on 4 May. The shower gave cold water
only. I reported it at 9 p.m.; nobody came. I would be
grateful if you could refund one night's charge." Which
letter gets the money back? B — every time. Anger makes
the reader defend; facts make the reader act. This lesson
writes B — and then answers it.

[Media slot: a picture of a hotel room with three faults
circled in red. What you do: turn each circled fault into
one factual sentence — who, what, when.]

## Then the explanation
**The complaint's moves.** Greece's Hotel and Tourist
Trade book gives a whole unit to complaints (Unit 6,
pp.99–112): two guests complain at the desk — a noisy room,
a hot room with a blurred TV, no soap, too few hangers —
and its writing work is the pair this lesson teaches: a
guest's letter of complaint (about slow breakfast service,
unhelpful receptionists and raised prices) and the
manager's reply. The complaint's order, in our words:
**(1)** the booking — date, room, what was promised;
**(2)** the facts — what went wrong, in time order, one
fault per sentence, each provable; **(3)** the cost — one
sentence on what the trouble did to you; **(4)** the ask —
ONE fix, named exactly (refund, repair, replacement), in
the formal request dress: "I would be grateful if you
could…".

**The reply's moves.** The Greek manager's reply explains
staff illness and untrained replacements — and that is
its middle, not its whole. The reply's order: **(1)
apologise** — first, plainly, naming the trouble back so
the writer knows they were read; **(2) explain** — the
honest cause, briefly, without blaming the writer;
**(3) act** — what has been done or will be done, with
the refund or repair named. The unit's spoken tables
teach the same pair at the desk: apologising, and
promising to take action. A reply missing any move
leaves the complaint standing.

**Tone — firm facts, formal dress, under pressure.**
Uzbekistan's letters unit reads a formal letter of
complaint about a restaurant meal (p.80) and its Review
sets the trap this lesson exists to disarm: a complaint
letter with formal and informal styles wrongly mixed, to
spot and correct (p.85). The rule: the worse the trouble,
the steadier the register. No contractions, no slang, no
exclamation marks — and no threats you cannot carry out.
Sierra Leone teaches the letter of complaint by name, and
its formal-letters lessons list complaint among the formal
purposes (complaint, request, business, application);
Jordan teaches the email of complaint as a full genre at
Grade 12; Ghana's NaCCA curricula name formal writing
kinds — applications, invitations and letters to
authorities — as curriculum evidence (no Ghana pupil
textbook exists, and none is claimed here).

## Do it yourself
1. Write the hands-on pair — complaint first, then the
   manager's reply to your own letter.
2. Fact-or-feeling sort: take ten sentences from your
   complaint draft; mark each F (provable fact) or E
   (emotion). Rewrite every E as an F or cut it.
3. Reply rescue: write a bad reply (defensive, no action)
   on purpose; swap with a companion; repair each other's
   with the three moves.
