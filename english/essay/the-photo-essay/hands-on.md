# Hands-On — The Photo Essay

Things you do with real things around you. You need a camera or phone — or paper to draw the photographs you would take — and a pencil.

1. **Walk your subject.** Choose one subject in your community and walk around it slowly. List six to eight scenes that each show something different about it.
2. **Caption on the spot.** For the first scene, say its caption out loud in two sentences: what the scene is, and why it matters. If the second sentence is hard, choose a scene that matters more.
3. **Lay it out.** Put your photographs (or drawings) in a row on a table. Move them until the order feels like a story: the place first, the building scenes in the middle, the strongest last.
4. **Test it on a visitor.** Show the row to someone who does not know the place. Ask: "What is this place like? What matters here?" Where their answer is wrong or empty, fix the caption — or change the order.
