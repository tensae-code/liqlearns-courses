# Short Answer — The Photo Essay

(Every item is marked with the sub lesson it belongs to: (Sub lesson N). The gate pool for a sub lesson is all of that sub lesson's questions — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the whole set is the pool.)

You are making a photo essay about the well your street uses.

1. What are the two jobs of a caption? (Sub lesson 1)
   — Describe the scene the photograph shows, and say its importance — why it matters.
2. Where do the photographs of a photo essay come from? (Sub lesson 1)
   — From your own community — its places, work and people.
3. Write a caption for a photograph of people drawing water at the well in the early morning. (Sub lesson 1)
   — Any caption doing both jobs, e.g.: "Women and children draw the day's water at the old well before the sun is hot. The well is the street's first meeting place, and its water feeds every house and garden here."
4. Why does the order of the photographs matter? (Sub lesson 2)
   — Because the essay is read in that order: the first photograph sets the place, the middle ones build, and the last one lands the whole message.
5. How is a photo essay like a written essay? (Sub lesson 2)
   — Its parts are chosen and ordered to build one whole message, as an essay's paragraphs are.
6. Say the whole message of your photo essay in one sentence. (Sub lesson 2)
   — Any one sentence that says what the photographs together show and why it matters, e.g.: "The old well gives our street its water, its work and its morning meeting — it is small, and everything here depends on it."
