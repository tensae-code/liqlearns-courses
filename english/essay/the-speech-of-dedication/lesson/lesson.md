# The Speech of Dedication

## Do it first

When a new library opens, a bridge is named, a park is given to a hero — someone must say the words that hand the place over to its purpose. That speech is the dedication, and the Philippines' Grade 10 book teaches it directly: what it is, what it should say, and a five-step method for writing one. This lesson teaches the speech and the method.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a dedication speech is

*Difficulty: first step — the ground of the lesson.*

A **speech of dedication** is spoken at the moment a building, monument, park or work is formally given to the **cause, person or event it is dedicated to**. Its work: **name** the dedication — whose name, which cause, this place now carries; **honour** — tell why that name belongs here, through the story of the person or cause and the story of the place; and **hand over** — declare the place open to its purpose and to the people it will serve. What it should say follows from the work: the name and its reason, gratitude to those who built and gave, the hope for what will happen here, and the formal act of dedication itself. The book prints its source for the speech's text plainly — one printing drops the colon from the web address ("Source:http/www.ehow.com/…", taught corrected): small print, but a dedication is an occasion where names must be exactly right.

Try it now: List three dedications you could witness (a school hall, a bridge, a garden). For one, write in three lines: the name it carries, the reason, and the words of handing over.

## Sub lesson 2 — Writing one in five steps

*Difficulty: builds on Sub lesson 1.*

The book's method builds the speech in five steps. **1. Open with the occasion** — gather the company on this day, at this place, for this act. **2. Tell the story of the name** — the person, cause or event, and why this place carries it. **3. Tell the story of the place** — how it came to be: the need, the builders, the givers. **4. Say what the place is for** — the hopes: who will use it, what will happen here, what it must mean in fifty years. **5. Dedicate** — speak the formal words of handing over, plainly and once: the name is given, the doors are opened, the purpose begins. Practised whole, the speech is short, grave and glad at once — it buries nothing, it begins something.

Try it now: Draft a dedication speech (two minutes) for a chosen place by the five steps. Head each paragraph with its step. Read it aloud: does step 5 sound like an act, not a summary?

## Do it yourself

Write a full speech of dedication (about two minutes) for a real or imagined place — a school library, a community garden, a bridge, a clinic — following the five steps, with the steps headed in your draft. Deliver it aloud, then remove the headings and write one line on whether step 5 still sounds like an act.
