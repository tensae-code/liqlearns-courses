# Lesson plan — Job application letters

**Course:** English · **Title:** Essay · **Lesson:** Job application letters
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 34, "Letters That Work".
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the letter that asks
for a job — the application (covering) letter — and its
paragraph order: where you saw the job, who you are, why
you fit, what you enclose, and the polite push for an
interview.
**This lesson's own level:** it wears The formal letter's
frame whole; the CV gets its own lesson (Level 35) — here
the letter only points at it ("I enclose my CV").
**What the learner can do at the end:** write a complete
application letter matched to one real advert, in the
books' paragraph order, with the sincerely/faithfully rule
held.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The formal letter. Recommended, never
  forced.
- Application FORMS are a neighbour skill (Laos and Iraq
  drill them); this lesson is the letter, not the form.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a job advert pinned above a
   blank letter, with five paragraph blocks waiting.**
   What the student does: the student matches each block
   to the advert line it answers.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of an envelope holding two papers,
   letter on top, CV beneath.** What the student does: the
   student says which paper is read first and why.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first**: two application openings — one generic,
   one matched to its advert — ranked.
2. **The paragraph order** (Cambodia's coaching order;
   India's four content steps beside it).
3. **The formal-email twin**: Egypt teaches the same ask
   as a formal email whose purposes include job application.
4. **Forms ride along**: Laos's application forms and
   Iraq's application form — the letter's paper cousins.
5. **Do it yourself**: the hands-on letter, written to a
   given advert.

## If the learner is stuck
- Letter praises the writer, ignores the job: re-read the
  advert and underline its three asks; one paragraph must
  answer each.
- Paragraphs shuffled: the order chant — saw it, am it,
  fit it, enclose it, meet me.
- Next in this level: Complaint letters.
