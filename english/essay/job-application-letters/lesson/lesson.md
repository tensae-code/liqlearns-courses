*Speaker note: the explanation is read in your own
language. The letter's own wording — its openings,
closings and formal phrases — is the TARGET text: never

*If you know the formal letter's frame, this lesson is
that frame aimed at one job — but it stands alone: the
order below is complete.*

## Do it first
Two first paragraphs for the same library job. A: "Dear
Sir, I am a very good and hardworking person and I need a
job. Please give me work." B: "Dear Sir or Madam, I am
writing to apply for the post of Library Assistant,
advertised in the Daily Post on 12 March." One letter
could be sent for ANY job; the other was written for THIS
job. Employers can smell the difference in one line. This
lesson writes letter B.

## Sub lesson 1 — Job application letters — the paragraph order

**The paragraph order.** Cambodia's Grade 12 book coaches
the covering letter paragraph by paragraph (Unit 30,
"Applying for a job", printed pp.163–168), and India's
Hornbill states the formal letter's content steps the same
way (introduce/refer back → purpose → action or
information → close with an offer). In our words, the
order is five moves: **(1) the post** — name the job, the
advert, the date you saw it; **(2) the person** — who you
are, your school and training, briefly; **(3) the fit** —
the heart: take the advert's asks in its order and answer
each with a fact about you; **(4) the enclosure** — "I
enclose my CV" (the CV itself is the next level's lesson);
**(5) the meeting** — thank the reader and say you can
attend an interview. Uzbekistan's "Letters of
application" lessons (English Matters 9, Unit 9, p.102)
drill the same letter kind at school-leaving age.

## Sub lesson 2 — Job application letters — matched to the advert

**Matched, never generic.** India's unit prints the whole
chain on the page: a sample advertisement, the CV format,
and the model covering letter written TO that advert — the
letter's facts are the advert's asks, answered. Egypt's
employment unit teaches the same law from the employer's
side: personalised applications over generic resumes.
Write the letter last, advert open beside you.

## Sub lesson 3 — Job application letters — the letter judged whole

**The email twin.** Egypt's Secondary 10 book teaches the
application as a formal email too (Unit 4, Lesson 5): the
template keeps the same skeleton — subject line, "Dear
[Title + Last Name],", "I am writing to [state your
purpose clearly]", polite full sentences, "Thank you for
your time and consideration", "Yours sincerely" — and its
box names job application first among the formal email's
purposes. Letter or email, the order does not change.

**The form is a cousin, not the letter.** Laos's Grade 12
book pairs its scholarship application letter (Unit 1)
with vacancy application FORMS (Unit 10: complete a form
from a word box; read a filled model form) — boxes filled,
not paragraphs written. Iraq's Grade 12 Activity Book
drills the same form-filling. South Sudan teaches the job
application LETTER itself at primary level (Unit 6,
"Letter Writing": "A JOB APPLICATION LETTER", p.74, and
"RE: APPLICATION FOR A VACANCY AS A TEACHER", p.76) —
proof the kind is one thing at every age: the frame, the
order, the match.

## Do it yourself
1. Write the hands-on letter to the library advert, in
   the five-move order.
2. Swap adverts: take a second invented advert (cook,
   driver, shop assistant) and list, before writing, the
   three asks your paragraph 3 must answer.
3. The generic-letter repair: take paragraph A from Do it
   first and rewrite it as a matched opening for the
   library job.
