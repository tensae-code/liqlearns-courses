# Short Answer — Citing Sources and Bibliography

(Every item is marked with the sub lesson it belongs to: (Sub lesson N). The gate pool for a sub lesson is all of that sub lesson's questions — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the whole set is the pool.)

1. What three kinds of thing must be cited when you take them from another text? (Sub lesson 1)
   — A fact, an idea, and a person's exact words.

2. Why is a citation called a pointer, not a copy? (Sub lesson 1)
   — It does not bring the source into your work; it names the source at the place of use so the reader can go and find it.

3. Why does citation follow set procedures instead of everyone naming sources their own way? (Sub lesson 1)
   — So that every citation carries what a reader needs to find the source — the same information, in the same shape, every time.

4. What is the difference between the citations inside a work and its bibliography? (Sub lesson 2)
   — The citations stand one by one at the places where sources are used; the bibliography gathers all the work's sources together, named in full, at the end.

5. A work cites two books inside but its bibliography lists only one. What is wrong? (Sub lesson 2)
   — The bibliography must hold every source the work used; a cited source missing from it leaves the reader unable to find that source in full.
