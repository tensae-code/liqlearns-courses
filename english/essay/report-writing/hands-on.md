# Hands-on — Report writing

You do it, we guide you. A real survey, a real report — paper first, prose second.

1. **The ten-person survey.** Choose ONE question with a countable answer (How do you come to school? What do you eat for breakfast?). Write it as a questionnaire line with tick boxes. Ask ten people. Tally every answer where they can see you do it — the method must be visible, because the report will state it.
2. **Numbers into sentences.** Turn the tally into three sentences: one majority sentence (Most… / The majority…), one minority sentence (A few… / Only two…), one surprise sentence. Then draw the pie chart or bar chart the sentences describe — Sudan's survey report carries its statistics in a pie chart; yours must match your sentences exactly.
3. **The model frame.** Write the survey report on this frame (the TARGET frame — copy the headings, never translate them): **Title** / **Aim** (one sentence) / **Method** (who, how many, how asked) / **Findings** (your three sentences + the chart) / **Conclusion** (one or two sentences the numbers earn) / **Recommendation** (one thing that should be done). 120–180 words is a full report at this size.
4. **The formal dress check.** Read your draft aloud hunting contractions and chat-words. Every "don't" becomes "do not"; every "lots of" becomes a number or "many". Egypt's rule: full forms, polite neutral tone, headings standing clear.
