# Lesson plan — News writing

**Course:** English · **Title:** Essay · **Lesson:** News writing
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 36, "Writing the Facts"
(CURRICULUM.md; CATCH-UP-PLAN.md Levels 36–37).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the news report — a headline that carries the story in a few words, and a lead that answers who, what, where and when at once, with the tenses news uses: the short present of headlines, the past of finished events, the present perfect of fresh news.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Report writing (same level — the same checked facts, told faster) and on the tense lessons (past actions; the present perfect). It is the second lesson of Level 36.
**What the learner can do at the end:**
- write a headline that carries one whole news event in a few words
- write a lead sentence answering who, what, where and when before anything else
- choose news tenses on purpose: headline present, past simple for finished events, present perfect for news still warm
- build a full news report from bare notes, the way Egypt's book builds one from its note list

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Report writing; Past actions; The present perfect. Recommended, never forced.
- A news report is not a story and not an opinion: events first, in the order a reader needs them — most important first, not first-happened first.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a newspaper front page with one huge headline and the first line of the story circled.** Written slot: "a picture of a front page, its headline and lead showing." What the student does: the student reads ONLY the headline and the first line, then says the whole event back — if they can, the headline and lead have done their jobs.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of five question cards fanned out: WHO? WHAT? WHERE? WHEN? WHY?** Written slot: "a picture of the five W cards." What the student does: the student holds each card against the lead they wrote and ticks the ones it answers.
3. **Slot 3 — a GIF (1–2 seconds) of a clock's hands spinning while a headline shrinks from a long sentence to four words.** Written slot: "a picture of a long sentence shrinking into a short headline." What the student does: the student counts the words before and after — news is the same facts with the fat cut off.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first** (lesson/lesson.md): one event told twice — once as a slow story that buries the news, once as headline + lead; the second tells MORE in fewer words.
2. **The headline**: short, present-tense, small words dropped (Cambodia Grade 12, Unit 15 'In the news').
3. **The lead and the five W's**: who, what, where, when (and why when it is known) in the first sentence or two (Egypt's five W's reminder box).
4. **Tense choice for news**: verb tenses in reporting news items (Uzbekistan, English Matters 8, Unit 5 'News Matters'); past simple for the finished event, present perfect for the just-happened, still-open one.
5. **News from notes**: Nepal's news report written from clues, and its model report read first (Grade 10, Unit 19); designing the front page the report will sit on.
6. **Saying it**: presenting a news report — tone, speed, pronunciation (Egypt, Unit 10, Lesson 4).
7. **Do it yourself**: one real event from your week, written as headline + lead + short report.

## If the learner is stuck
- The lead that rambles → hold the five W cards against it. A W with no answer is a hole; a sentence with no W is fat. Cut or fill.
- Headline too long → drop the small words first (a, the), then turn the verb to its short present ("Fire destroys market"), then cut any fact the lead can carry instead.
- Tense wobble → ask: is it finished and dated? Past simple. Has it just happened, with its result standing now? Present perfect. Is it the headline? The headline's own short present.
- This lesson's next door: Process writing (same level) — facts again, but facts in an order you must DO, not just know.
