# News writing — Multiple choice

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — News writing — headline and lead · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — News writing — the body and tense choice · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 2 — News writing — the body and tense choice.

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** A headline's verb usually stands in —
a) the past perfect  b) a short present ('Fire destroys market')  c) the future **Answer: b)** — headlines drop small words and use the short present, even for finished events (Cambodia, Unit 15).

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** The lead of a news report must answer at once —
a) who, what, where, when  b) the writer's feelings  c) every detail in time order **Answer: a)** — Egypt's five W's reminder box; the lead carries the four at once, why when known.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** "The President has just left" in a news text uses the present perfect because —
a) the event is ancient  b) the news is still warm — just happened, result standing now  c) headlines demand it **Answer: b)** — Uzbekistan's news gap-fills use the form for just-happened news (English Matters 8, Unit 5).

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** The body of a news report orders its facts —
a) in strict time order  b) most important first  c) alphabetically **Answer: b)** — news is not a story: a reader who stops early still holds the biggest facts.
