# News writing — Short answer

Answer in whole sentences.

**1. (Level 1)** Write a headline (six words at most) for this event: last night, high winds blew the roof off a primary school in your town. **Answer: Winds destroy school roof (any six-word headline in the short present earns it).** — headline rules: few words, small words dropped, short present.

**2. (Level 2)** Write the lead for the same event, answering who, what, where and when. **Answer: High winds blew the roof off [the named school] in [the town] last night (all four W's in one or two sentences earns it).** — the lead answers the four W's at once (Egypt's box).

**3. (Level 3)** Your report's third paragraph gives the mayor's promise to rebuild. Why does it stand third, not first? **Answer: Because the event itself is bigger news than the promise; the body runs most important first.** — order by importance, not by time.
