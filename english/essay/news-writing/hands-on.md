# Hands-on — News writing

You do it, we guide you. Real events only — a report built from notes, the way the books build theirs.

1. **Notes first.** Take one real event from your school or street this week. Write ONLY notes, Egypt-style: who, what, where, when, plus two details — no sentences yet. (Egypt's book builds its news report from a note list: 15 teenagers, 10 hours, volunteers collecting rubbish from beaches.)
2. **Headline cuts.** Write the headline three times: first as a full sentence, then with the small words dropped, then in the headline's short present. Keep the shortest one that still carries the whole event.
3. **The lead on the frame.** Write the lead on this TARGET frame — one or two sentences answering, in order: **Who** did / **what** happened / **where** / **when**. Read it to a companion: they must be able to retell the event from the lead alone.
4. **The front page.** Lay your report out as a newspaper front page (Nepal's Grade 10 project): headline at the top, lead under it, body in most-important-first order, your name and the date on it. Read it aloud once at newsreader speed — steady tone, no rushing (Egypt's presenting lesson).
