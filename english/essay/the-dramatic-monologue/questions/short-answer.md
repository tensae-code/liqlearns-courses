# Short Answer — The Dramatic Monologue

(Every item is marked with the sub lesson it belongs to: (Sub lesson N). The gate pool for a sub lesson is all of that sub lesson's questions — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the whole set is the pool.)

1. What three things must come through the single voice in a monologue? (Sub lesson 1)
   — The situation, the story behind it, and the character — who is revealed, often beyond what they intend to show.

2. Why must the speaker know who they are speaking to, even though that person never answers? (Sub lesson 1)
   — Because speech is shaped by its listener: what is said, hidden and leaked all depend on who is being addressed in the dramatic moment.

3. Name the four prosodic features and one feeling each can carry. (Sub lesson 2)
   — Pitch (fear rises, grief falls); loudness (what can no longer be held in); tempo (excitement runs, resolve slows); rhythm (control steady, breakdown broken).

4. How does a performer prepare the voice work? (Sub lesson 2)
   — Mark the script first — pitch, loudness, tempo per line, matched to the feeling — then perform what the marks say.
