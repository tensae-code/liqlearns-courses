# Short Answer — The Toast

(Every item is marked with the sub lesson it belongs to: (Sub lesson N). The gate pool for a sub lesson is all of that sub lesson's questions — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the whole set is the pool.)

1. Name three ways a toast differs from a full speech. (Sub lesson 1)
   — Length (seconds/minutes vs an argument's arc); shape (one tribute thought vs developed parts); occasion and ending (spoken standing at a gathering, ending in the shared drink rather than a conclusion).

2. Why must the tribute be one stroke, not a biography? (Sub lesson 2)
   — Because the toast's craft is smallness: one concrete, true thing makes the room see the desert at once; a biography spends the moment the toast exists to crown.

3. What are the toast's four beats in composing? (Sub lesson 2)
   — Name the honoured one; tribute in one vivid stroke; the wish; the lift (closing call to drink together).

4. What does a toast's delivery ask of the speaker? (Sub lesson 2)
   — Stand, smile, glass raised, voice to the back of the room — and sit down on the applause without explaining afterwards.
