# The Toast

## Do it first

At the feast, someone stands, glass in hand, and speaks for thirty seconds — and the room is changed. The Philippines' Grade 10 book gives that small speech its own teaching: what a toast is, how it differs from a full speech, model toasts, and composing one for an occasion (its class toasts Mother Earth). This lesson teaches the toast. (Its big ceremonial cousins — the eulogy, the dedication, the goodwill speech — have their own lessons.)

## Sub lesson 1 — What a toast is — and is not

*Difficulty: first step — the ground of the lesson.*

A **toast** is a short tribute speech given at a gathering — a meal, a wedding, a farewell, a feast — ending with the company drinking to the person or cause named. It differs from a full speech in every measure: **length** — a toast is measured in seconds or a few minutes, never in an argument's arc; **shape** — one thought only: whom we honour, why, and the wish we drink to; **occasion** — it belongs to the table and the moment, spoken standing, glass raised; **ending** — it does not conclude, it *lifts*: the last line is the invitation to drink ("To Mother Earth!"). The book teaches it against the full speech precisely so the learner feels the smallness as a craft: everything unnecessary must go.

Try it now: Take a full short speech you know (or one paragraph of one). Cut it to a toast: keep one honoured name, one reason, one wish. Read both versions and time them.

## Sub lesson 2 — Composing a toast for an occasion

*Difficulty: builds on Sub lesson 1.*

The book's model toasts share a skeleton. **Name** the honoured one at once — the toast has no time for approach. **Tribute in one vivid stroke:** not a biography — one true, concrete thing that makes the room see why this person or cause deserves the glass (the book's class composes toasts *for Mother Earth*, where the stroke is a gift of nature named plainly). **Wish:** the future you drink to, stated as a hope for the honoured one. **Lift:** the closing call — short, repeatable, said together ("To…!"). Delivery matches the smallness: stand, smile, glass up, voice to the back of the room, sit down on the applause — a toast that explains itself afterwards has already failed.

Try it now: Compose a toast for one occasion (a classmate's win, a teacher's farewell, a family feast, or the book's Mother Earth). Write it to be spoken in under a minute. Mark its four beats: name / stroke / wish / lift.

## Do it yourself

Compose toasts for three different occasions (one of them the book's own: Mother Earth). Keep each under a minute spoken, mark the four beats in each, deliver the best one aloud, and write two lines on what you cut from your first draft and why the toast is better smaller.
