# Blog and online posts — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Essay · Lesson: Blog and online posts
Built from: Cambodia, ENGLISH Grade 10 (Unit 28 — writing a blog about a tourist spot) — Malaysia, Academy Stars, Year 6 (Unit 3 — a blog, with a mind-map plan) — Egypt (the blog post and its tips, Primary 6 Term 2 and Secondary 11 Term 1; the social-media post and its Tip, Preparatory 8 Term 1) — Greece (online comments met in the books; reacting to good and bad news as a taught function).

## Do it first
The same school trip, twice. First: "The excursion was
undertaken by the class on Tuesday. Various locations
were visited. The experience was educational in nature."
True — and dead. Second: a heading, "The Day the Bus
Left Without Us"; a date; then short paragraphs that tell
it like a story to a friend. A stranger reads the second
one to the end and leaves a kind comment. Online writing
wins or loses in its voice. Here is its shape.

## Then the explanation

## Sub lesson 1 — Blog and online posts — the blog's shape

**The blog's shape.** Egypt's books teach the blog post
twice, at two ages, and the shape holds: a catchy
title/hook, an introduction, body topics, a conclusion —
and the voice rule stated in the Secondary 11 book's
structure box (Unit 3, Lesson 5, pp.39–40): write it like
telling a story to a friend. The younger book (Primary 6
Term 2) models a blog with SIX BLOG TIPS, opening with a
title that catches attention, and carrying the writer's
name and date as part of the kind: a blog is a PERSON
writing, on a DAY, and the page shows both.

**Planning — the mind map.** Malaysia's Year 6 book
(Academy Stars, Unit 3, "Adventure time") writes its blog
from a plan: Emma's Canada adventure blog is read
(pp.34–35), then blog writing is taught with a mind-map
plan (pp.40–41) — the topic in the centre, the post's
ideas on lines running out, each line becoming a
paragraph. The plan is what keeps a friendly voice from
wandering: the voice may roam inside a paragraph, never
across the whole post.

**Cambodia's blog.** Cambodia's Grade 10 book (Unit 28,
"Jobs", printed pp.151–156) sets writing a blog about a
tourist spot — proof that the blog's home ground is
experience: a place you have been, told so a reader can
stand there too.

**Short paragraphs.** The blog's paragraphs are shorter
than the essay's — two to four lines is a room online.
White space is not emptiness; it is the stairs the
reader climbs. One idea per paragraph, as always; only
the rooms are smaller.

## Sub lesson 2 — Blog and online posts — the small rooms

**The social-media post.** Egypt's Preparatory 8 book
(Unit 5) sets a social-media post of 90–100 words — about
a country you would like to visit — with a Social Media
Post Tip: a small room with the same laws. One point.
Warmth. Completeness — a post that needs a second post to
make sense has failed — and an ending that invites the
reader in: a question, a wish, a door.

**Commenting online.** Greece's books meet online comments
as reading first: an e-project invites teenagers to send
in work, and learners match three pupils' COMMENTS to
what they comment on — the comment is a small public
writing with a shape: it answers the thing itself, it is
specific ("the light on the water in your second photo"),
and it signs its writer's manners. The course's rule for
writing them: praise what is really there, answer ideas
rather than people, and let a disagreement carry its
reason without carrying a wound.

## Sub lesson 3 — Blog and online posts — the hard messages

**Giving bad news politely.** Greece's books teach
reacting to good and bad news as a function — the spoken
half of this lesson's written skill. In writing, the
polite bad-news message runs in four moves: the CUSHION
(a kind true sentence first), the FACT (plain, once, no
hiding), the KINDNESS (what you wish, what you are sorry
for), and the DOOR (what can still happen — next time,
another way, your help). "I am so sorry — the trip list
closed yesterday, and your name came after the last seat.
I have kept your name first for the next trip." Bad news
delivered whole and kindly is remembered kindly.

## Do it yourself
1. **A blog post.** Mind-map one real experience; write
   the post on the frame: catchy heading, name and date,
   short paragraphs, a closing feeling or question.
   120–180 words.
2. **A social-media post.** One point, 90–100 words, about
   a place you would like to visit — ending with a
   question or wish that invites an answer.
3. **Two comments and one hard message.** Comment politely
   on two pieces of online writing (praise specific;
   disagree with an idea, with its reason). Then write
   one polite bad-news message in the four moves:
   cushion, fact, kindness, door.
