# Lesson plan — Blog and online posts

**Course:** English · **Title:** Essay · **Lesson:** Blog and online posts
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 37 — "Writing Your View"
(CURRICULUM.md; CATCH-UP-PLAN.md Levels 36–37).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** writing online in your own voice — the blog post (a heading that catches, a dated personal voice, short paragraphs, a mind-map plan), the social-media post, and the manners of being online: commenting politely, and giving bad news politely.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The paragraph (short paragraphs are the blog's rooms) and on Review writing in the same level — the blog is judgement and experience in a friendlier voice. It is the second lesson of Unit 37.
**What the learner can do at the end:**
- plan a blog post with a mind map and write it: catchy heading, dated opening, short paragraphs, a story told like to a friend
- write a social-media post that fits its small room: one point, warm, complete
- comment online politely — answer the idea, praise specifically, disagree without wounding
- give bad news politely online: cushion, fact, kindness, door left open

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The paragraph; Review writing. Recommended, never forced.
- Online writing is public writing: the voice is friendly, but every word can be copied forever. Friendly and careful are not enemies.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a blog page: a big heading, a date under it, then short paragraphs with white space between them like steps.** Written slot: "a picture of a blog post with its heading, date and short paragraphs showing." What the student does: the student counts the paragraphs and measures the longest with a finger — blog paragraphs stay short enough to breathe on a phone screen.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of a mind map: a topic in a centre circle, five lines running out to five small circles.** Written slot: "a picture of a mind-map plan for a blog post." What the student does: the student points from the centre out along one line and says that paragraph's job — Malaysia plans its blog exactly this way before writing.
3. **Slot 3 — a GIF (1–2 seconds) of a comment being typed angrily, deleted, and retyped kindly, the words softening as they land.** Written slot: "a picture of a sharp comment being rewritten politely." What the student does: the student reads both versions aloud — the facts are the same; only the temperature changed.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first** (lesson/lesson.md): the same trip told as a stiff school paragraph and as a blog post; the second is the one a stranger reads to the end.
2. **The blog's shape**: catchy title/hook, introduction, body topics, conclusion — written like telling a story to a friend (Egypt, Secondary 11 Term 1, Unit 3); Egypt's younger book's six blog tips (Primary 6 Term 2).
3. **Planning**: the mind-map plan before the post (Malaysia, Year 6, Unit 3 — blog writing with a mind-map plan, printed pp.40–41); Cambodia's blog about a tourist spot (Grade 10, Unit 28).
4. **The social-media post**: Egypt's social-media post and its Tip (90–100 words) — one country, one point, complete.
5. **Online manners**: commenting — Greece's online comments met and matched (the e-project comments), reacting to good and bad news as a taught function, practised here for the comment box: praise specifically, disagree with the idea, give bad news with its cushion.
6. **Do it yourself**: one blog post planned on a mind map, one post, one polite hard comment.

## If the learner is stuck
- Paragraphs like bricks → cut at every breath. A blog paragraph that fills a phone screen is two paragraphs wearing one coat.
- A title that names but does not catch → the title is a hand waved at a passing stranger: give it the strangest true thing in the post ('The night the bus left without us').
- A comment you want to send hot → write it, wait, rewrite it answering the IDEA. If your name signed under it would shame you tomorrow, it is not ready today.
- This lesson's next door: Article writing (same level) — the blog's public cousin: same catching opening, but built for strangers in print.
