# Hands-on — Blog and online posts

You do it, we guide you. Real topics from your real week — planned on paper, written for a screen.

1. **The mind-map plan (Malaysia's way).** Centre circle: your topic (a trip, a match, a market day, a tourist spot near you — Cambodia's Grade 10 blog is about a tourist spot). Five lines out, one idea per line, in the order a friend would enjoy hearing them. The map is finished when every line can be said as one sentence.
2. **Write the post on the frame.** Build the blog post on this TARGET frame — copy the parts, never translate them: **Heading** (the strangest true thing, catching) / **Name + date line** / **Opening** (where, when, who — in two or three lines) / **Body** (one mind-map line per paragraph; no paragraph over four lines) / **Closing** (your feeling or your question to the reader). Egypt's measure for its school blog posts is 150–180 words; a first full post at 120–180 words is complete.
3. **The 100-word post.** Take ONE line of your mind map and write it as a social-media post of 90–100 words, Egypt's size: one point, warm, complete, ending with a question or a wish that invites an answer.
4. **The polite rewrite drill.** You are given (or invent) three hot comments. Rewrite each: keep every fact, remove every wound, add one specific praise or one open door. Then write the hardest one for real: give a friend bad news (you cannot come; the plan is cancelled) in four moves — cushion, fact, kindness, door.
