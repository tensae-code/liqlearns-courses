# Hands-on — Summarising

A real summarising build — the ladder, rung by rung.

## The passage (invented for this lesson)
"The bicycle reached our town slowly. First came one
trader's bicycle, carried in parts on a lorry and put
together in his shop, and people stood in a crowd to
watch him ride it, wobbling, down the market street.
Within five years every teacher owned one, because a
bicycle cost less than a month's pay and cut the walk to
school from two hours to twenty minutes. Repairers
learned the bicycle's secrets — the chain, the brake,
the punctured tube — and a boy with a pump and a patch
could earn money at the roadside. When the first bus
came, people said the bicycle would die. It did not die.
Buses carried crowds between towns, but inside the town
the bicycle stayed king: cheap, silent, and always
ready." (about 140 words)

## Task 1 — the ladder
1. Underline the main points only (not the crowd, not the
   wobbling). Note them in note form first.
2. Write the summary at about 45–50 words (the one-third
   rule on 140) — full sentences, your own words.
3. Shrink it to 30 words (Sudan's rung), then to 20.
   At each rung, read back: are ALL main points still
   standing? Any rung that drops one has failed, however
   short it is.

## Task 2 — the story frame
Take any short story you know. Summarise it TWICE:
(a) on Malaysia's frame — Someone ___ wanted ___, so
___, but ___, then ___ — one sentence or a short
paragraph; (b) one sentence per heading — character /
want / problem / solution / resolution — joined into
one paragraph (South Sudan's way). Compare: what did
each frame force you to keep?
