# Lesson plan — Summarising

**Course:** English · **Title:** Essay · **Lesson:** Summarising
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 39 — Essays and Shortening.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the summary — a
text's main points, in YOUR OWN WORDS, rebuilt as full
sentences, to a SET LENGTH: the one-third rule, a worked
30-word model, and the shrinking ladder; plus the
Someone–Wanted–So–But–Then frame for story summaries.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Note-making —
notes are phrases for yourself; a summary is sentences
for a reader — and on Finding the idea of a text.
**What the learner can do at the end:** summarise any
passage to a word limit without copying its sentences,
shrink a summary down a ladder of lengths, and summarise
a story on the five-beat frame.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Selecting and Paraphrasing** — the definition — selection plus
   paraphrase: main points kept, written in your own words as full
   sentences. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Summarising to a Set Length** — the set length — the one-third
   rule and the books' set lengths, hit without copying a sentence.
   (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **The Shrinking Ladder and the Story Frame** — the shrinking
   ladder, run down keeping every main point, and the story frame
   Someone–Wanted–So–But–Then. (Was depth Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is
written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the
gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Note-making. Recommended, never
  forced.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of three sieves stacked, coarse
   over fine, a bucket of text pouring through and a
   small cup of gold points under the last sieve.** What
   the student does: the student names what each sieve
   removes — examples, details, then the writer's words
   themselves.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of a ladder with four rungs
   labelled 80, 50, 30, 20.** What the student does: the
   student points at the rung their summary must reach
   and says what may still be cut to get there.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first**: a copied "summary" vs a true one —
   which writer read the text, and which only carried it?
2. **The definition**: selection + paraphrase (India,
   Hornbill W2).
3. **The length law**: the one-third rule; Sudan's
   30-word worked model; set lengths in South Sudan.
4. **The shrinking ladder**: 80 → 50 → 30 → 20 words,
   rung by rung.
5. **The story frame**: Someone–Wanted–So–But–Then
   (Malaysia, Academy Stars Year 6); South Sudan's
   one-sentence-per-heading story summary.
6. **Do it yourself**: the hands-on ladder build.

## If the learner is stuck
- Copying the text's sentences: close the book. Say the
  point aloud in your own words first; write what you
  SAID, never what you saw.
- Too long: you are carrying examples. The main points
  ride; examples walk.
- Next in this level: this level closes; diaries and
  life writing open Unit 40.
