# Summarising — multiple choice

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Selecting and Paraphrasing · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Summarising to a Set Length · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — The Shrinking Ladder and the Story Frame.

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** A summary is (India's definition) —
a) the best sentences copied and joined b) selection +
paraphrase: main points kept, written in your own words
as full sentences c) notes in phrase form
**Answer: b)** — notes are phrases for yourself; a
summary is sentences for a reader (Hornbill W2).

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** In a passage, the first things a
summary drops are — a) the main points b) examples and
details c) the verbs **Answer: b)** — Sudan's rules:
main idea kept, details left out.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** The rule of thumb for length is — a) a
summary is usually one-third the length of the original
b) always exactly 30 words c) half, never less
**Answer: a)** — Hornbill's stated rule; set lengths in
examinations are stricter versions of the same law.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** Sudan's worked model summarises an
advertising passage in — a) not more than 100 words
b) not more than 30 words c) exactly three sentences
**Answer: b)** — SPINE 5, Chapter Seven, with the worked
answer showing what is kept and left out.

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** The SWBST frame runs — a) Someone –
Wanted – So – But – Then b) Setting – War – Battle –
Songs – Tomb c) Start – Wander – Sleep – Breakfast –
Train **Answer: a)** — Malaysia, Academy Stars Year 6,
Unit 8's story-summary frame.
