# The Writing Process

## Do it first
Take any short piece you have written — a paragraph is enough. Read it once, slowly. Find one sentence you would now write differently. Change it. You have just used the process this lesson names: you drafted, then you revised. Now learn the whole road.

## Sub lesson 1 — Plan, Draft, Revise, Publish
Writing is not one act; it is a road with stages, and Sāmoa's curriculum statement names them for its youngest writers: **plan, draft, revise, edit, publish**.

- **Plan** — decide what you are writing, what kind of text it is, who it is for, and what it must do. A text is always written in a type — a story, a report, an explanation — for a specific purpose and audience.
- **Draft** — get the ideas down in order. The draft is allowed to be rough; its job is to exist.
- **Revise** — read the draft as a reader. Move things, cut things, add what is missing, make sentences say what you mean.
- **Edit** — fix the surface: spelling, punctuation, grammar.
- **Publish** — present the finished text cleanly for its audience.

Publishing on a computer has its own conventions: text is often **justified** (spread so both margins run straight) and long words **hyphenated** at line ends when the line must break.

## Sub lesson 2 — Drafting Craft — the Writer's Tools
Writers are built with tools, and Sāmoa's statement lists the ones its teachers use. **Shared writing**: the class composes together while the teacher writes, so learners watch decisions being made. **Modelling**: the teacher writes in front of learners and thinks aloud. **Guided writing**: a small group writes with the teacher's help aimed at their next step. A **writer's notebook** collects ideas, starts and fragments before they are needed. A **writing frame** gives a beginner the skeleton of a text type to lean on. **Talking** is a planning tool — saying a text aloud before writing it. And **word-processing** makes redrafting cheap: moving and rewriting cost nothing, so revise bravely.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the five stages of the writing process in order, and which stage you usually skip.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write one paragraph twice: first a fast draft, then a revised version. Keep both and mark every change you made.
2. Start a writer's notebook page: five starts for pieces you might write this month.
3. Take a printed page and find one justified margin and one hyphenated word. Say what each is doing.
