# Lesson plan — Job interviews

**Course:** English · **Title:** Essay · **Lesson:** Job interviews
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 35, "Writing for a Job" —
the last lesson of the level.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the job interview as
a prepared performance — before (research, dress,
punctuality), during (experience answers built on STAR:
Situation–Task–Action–Result), and the mock interview that
makes it real.
**This lesson's own level:** the chain's last link (advert
→ CV → cover letter → interview); its writing is spoken,
but its answers are composed like paragraphs — built,
ordered, practised.
**What the learner can do at the end:** prepare for a named
interview, answer "Tell me about a time when…" in a full
STAR shape, and survive a mock interview with a companion.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: CV and cover letter. Recommended,
  never forced.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a candidate at a door, clock
   showing ten minutes early, phone silent in a bag.**
   What the student does: the student names the three
   preparations the picture shows.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of four stepping stones labelled
   S, T, A, R across a stream.** What the student does:
   the student crosses aloud, one stone per part of an
   answer.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first**: two answers to one question — rambling
   vs built — ranked.
2. **Preparation** (Cambodia's advice; Laos's tips;
   Armenia's etiquette).
3. **STAR** (Egypt's method, stated whole).
4. **The interviewer's other door**: Libya's interview
   questions retold as reported questions.
5. **The mock** (Cambodia's unit's close; Armenia's pair
   practice).
6. **Do it yourself**: a full mock, recorded or witnessed.

## If the learner is stuck
- Answers wandering: shrink to one story per question and
  walk S–T–A–R on fingers.
- Silence after questions: Armenia's thinking-time phrases
  buy the seconds honestly.
- This level closes here; the course continues to Level 36,
  Writing the Facts.
