# The Symposium

## Do it first

The Philippines' Grade 10 module ends by staging a whole event: a mini-symposium for Mother Nature, with assigned roles, a written speech and script, slides, and a judging rubric. The symposium is the course's largest speaking form — several speeches inside one ceremony. This lesson teaches how one runs. (Single speeches — toast, eulogy, dedication, goodwill — have their own lessons; presenting skills in general are *Presentation Skills*.)

## Sub lesson 1 — The people and parts of a symposium

*Difficulty: first step — the ground of the lesson.*

A **symposium** is a formal programme in which several speakers present on one theme before an audience, held together by officers. The book assigns the roles by name. The **resource speaker** is the expert voice: the one who brings the knowledge the event exists for. The **emcee** (master of ceremonies) hosts the whole: opens, names the parts, keeps the room oriented. The **introduction officer** presents each speaker — who they are, why they speak — so every speech lands with its authority stated. The **moderator/facilitator** runs the open forum: takes the audience's questions, gives them in fair order, keeps time and temper. A symposium fails when a role is missing: no emcee, and the parts fall apart; no moderator, and the questions eat the programme.

Try it now: Cast a symposium for your class on one theme: name who takes each role and write each officer's first line. If one person had to double two roles, which pairing breaks least — and why?

## Sub lesson 2 — Running one: script, slides, questions, rubric

*Difficulty: builds on Sub lesson 1.*

The book's symposium is *made*, not improvised: each speaker prepares a **written speech/script** and **slides** that carry the visuals, and the event is judged by a **rubric** whose lines are the real syllabus of the form: **organisation and time management** (the programme shaped, the clock kept), **delivery and elocution** (the speeches spoken well), **preparedness and fielding questions** (the open forum survived), **collaboration and originality** (a team event, freshly made, not copied), and **content** (the theme truly served). Plan backwards from the rubric: script the officers' lines as carefully as the speeches, rehearse the handoffs (emcee → introduction → speech → applause → next), and appoint the timekeeper before the day.

Try it now: Draft your symposium's running order on one page: roles, order of speakers, minutes per part, who fields questions. Mark beside each rubric line where in your plan it is earned.

## Do it yourself

Plan a complete mini-symposium for your class (theme of your choice; the book's class chose Mother Nature): cast the four roles, write the running order with minutes, draft the emcee's and introducer's lines in full, and state how your plan earns each of the rubric's five lines. If you stage it, attach the class's rubric scores and one lesson learned.
