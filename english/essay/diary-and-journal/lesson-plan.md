# Lesson plan — Diary and journal

**Course:** English · **Title:** Essay · **Lesson:** Diary and journal
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 40 — "Writing Your Own Life" —
the first lesson of the level.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the diary entry — the
date at the top, the day told in the first person in the past,
and a personal closing remark — and its wider cousin, the
journal, which also keeps goals, lessons and reflections.
**This lesson's own level:** it opens the own-life level;
Biography writing and Postcards and personal letters follow it.
**What the learner can do at the end:** write a dated diary
entry that runs greeting → the day in order → feelings → a
closing thought, and keep a journal entry that adds what was
learned or planned.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Diary and journal — the parts** — the diary's parts in order —
   date, greeting, the day's details, closing — and its voice: first
   person, past. (Was depth Level 1.)

2. **Diary and journal — the day in order** — a day run through its
   morning, daytime and evening sections with its feelings carried;
   the diary's voice paragraph rides in this sub lesson. (Was depth
   Level 2.)

3. **Diary and journal — diary into journal** — a diary entry
   widened into a journal entry — goals, lessons and reflection
   added — and the travel journal's five steps. (Was depth Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Past actions (the diary's tense-home).
  Recommended, never forced.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a diary page: the date written
   large at the top, then short lines running down the day.**
   What the student does: the student points at the date
   first, then reads the day downwards — date first, day after.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of one day split into three boxes:
   morning, daytime, evening.** What the student does: the
   student sorts three loose event-cards into the three boxes.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first**: two openings — which one is a diary?
2. **The four printed parts** (Egypt Primary 4: date,
   greeting, details, closing).
3. **The day in its sections** (Egypt Preparatory 8's
   Structure Tip: morning / daytime / evening, feelings,
   closing with future plans).
4. **The dated model read whole** (Nepal Grade 8: Neil's
   entry, Saturday, 5 March 2022) and Malaysia's genre
   lesson (the diary entry with informal features, beside
   Pepys's 1666 extracts).
5. **The journal widens it** (Egypt Secondary 10: private,
   informal, honest — experiences, feelings, goals, lessons;
   the travel journal's five steps).
6. **Do it yourself**: hands-on entry + three-entry mini journal.

## If the learner is stuck
- Entry reads like a report: Egypt Secondary 12's Tip Box —
  opening line, body of events AND feelings, closing
  thought/wish. Feelings are a required part, not decoration.
- No order inside the day: use the morning / daytime /
  evening sections until the order holds by itself.
- Next in this level: Biography writing.
