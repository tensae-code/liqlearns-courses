# Hands-on — Diary and journal

A real writing task — yesterday, kept properly.

## Task 1 — your diary entry (frame; your day inside)
[Day, date — e.g. Saturday, 5 March]
Dear Diary,
[Opening line: the day in one breath — the trip, the match,
the visitor, the surprise.]
Morning — [what happened first, in the past, first person.]
Daytime — [the middle of the day; one thing you saw, heard
or smelled.]
Evening — [how the day ended.]
[Feelings line: the day's strongest feeling, named plainly.]
[Closing remark: a thought, a wish, or tomorrow's plan.]

## Task 2 — the three-entry mini journal (Egypt's extension)
Three dated entries: one real day at home, one day of travel
(or a dreamed-of place), one imaginary journey. In each,
after the day, add one line the diary does not need:
*What I learned:* … / *My goal now:* …

## Check
- Is the date the very first thing on the page?
- Is every event in the past, in the first person?
- Is there a feelings line AND a closing remark?
  A list of events with neither is a timetable, not a diary.
