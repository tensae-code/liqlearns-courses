# Hands-on — Advertisements and posters

You do it, we guide you. Advertise something real — a gadget, a stall, an event, a cause — and test the result on a real wall and real feet.

1. **The twenty-word advert.** Take one real thing (Sudan's gadget, your school's play, a volunteer's cause). Write its advert on this TARGET frame — copy the parts, never translate them: **Claim** (one line, the big true promise) / **Benefits** (three short lines, each a benefit with its adjective) / **Call to action** (one imperative line: what to do, where, by when). The whole advert must fit in about twenty words outside the frame's labels — cutting is the task.
2. **The wall test.** Make the poster version: claim at the top in the biggest letters, ONE picture (drawn or pasted), the three benefits mid-size, the call and a contact at the bottom. Pin it up. Read it from three steps back, then from across the room. Whatever fails at three steps gets bigger or gets cut. Egypt's poster boxes are your checklist: plan, organize, language, design — then its own order: plan, draft, improve, check.
3. **The flyer in the hand.** Shrink the poster to a flyer on Ghana's anatomy (curriculum evidence): A5 size, eye-catching headline, a slogan or logo, a brief description, a list of benefits or offers, contact information — and a visual. Hand it to someone for five seconds, take it back, and ask what it was for: their answer is your flyer, graded.
4. **The infographic conversion.** Take five true facts about one topic (water, reading, your school's sports day). Build an infographic: a title, one big number in the centre, the other facts as small picture-and-caption pairs, almost no sentences. Indonesia's infographic tasks are the model: if a fact needs a paragraph, it belongs in an article, not here.
