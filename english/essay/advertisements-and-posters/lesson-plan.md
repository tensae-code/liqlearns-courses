# Lesson plan — Advertisements and posters

**Course:** English · **Title:** Essay · **Lesson:** Advertisements and posters
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 37 — "Writing Your View"
(CURRICULUM.md; CATCH-UP-PLAN.md Levels 36–37).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** short persuasive copy — an advertisement's claim, its benefit adjectives and its call to action, in the fewest words that can work; and its wall-sized family: posters, flyers and infographics.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Review writing and Article writing in the same level — persuasion with the review's facts kept honest and the article's hook sharpened to a point. It closes Level 37.
**What the learner can do at the end:**
- write an advertisement whole: a claim, benefit adjectives tied to real benefits, a call to action
- name the advertising methods and audiences and choose between them (Cambodia's set)
- build a poster or flyer that passes the wall test: readable at three steps, one message, a visual, contact or next step
- turn facts into an infographic: few words, big numbers, honest pictures

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Review writing; Article writing. Recommended, never forced.
- Persuasion is not licence: a claim the thing cannot keep is a lie in big letters. The review lesson's honesty law binds this lesson hardest of all.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a market stall poster: one big word at the top, a picture of one shining gadget, three short benefit lines, an arrow pointing at the stall.** Written slot: "a picture of a poster with its claim, picture, benefits and call to action showing." What the student does: the student reads the poster from three steps back — whatever cannot be read from there does not exist on a wall.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of four small doors labelled SOCIAL MEDIA, POSTER, RADIO, PUBLIC DISPLAY, each with a different crowd walking through.** Written slot: "a picture of the four advertising methods with their audiences." What the student does: the student chooses the door for advertising a school play to parents, and says why — method follows audience (Cambodia's lesson).
3. **Slot 3 — a GIF (1–2 seconds) of a long paragraph shrinking line by line into five big words on a wall.** Written slot: "a picture of many words shrinking into a few big ones." What the student does: the student counts the words before and after — poster writing is cutting until only the working words remain.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first** (lesson/lesson.md): a long honest paragraph about a gadget vs its advert; the advert must do in twenty words what the paragraph does in a hundred.
2. **The advertisement's anatomy**: Ghana's curriculum checklist — title, body, target market, selling points; simple present for timeless facts, modals/imperatives and rhetorical questions and repetition for persuasion (CURRICULUM evidence).
3. **Kinds and methods**: Cambodia's advertising unit (Grade 12, Unit 17) — product types; methods (social media, posters, radio, public display); audiences; the advertising speech performed.
4. **The gadget advert**: Sudan's Grade 7 writing (Unit 4, Lesson 3) — one product, its benefits, written to sell.
5. **Compositions that advertise**: Kuwait's Over to You composition set — the advertisement among the 150–170-word kinds; the workbook's advert for volunteers.
6. **The poster and flyer**: Egypt's poster as a taught kind (Secondary 10 Term 2, pp.62–63 — plan, organize, language, design; the checklist) and its flyer anatomy from Ghana's curriculum (A5, headline/slogan, benefits, contact).
7. **The infographic**: Indonesia's infographic writing — facts carried by picture and few words.
8. **Do it yourself**: one advert, one poster, tested at three steps.

## If the learner is stuck
- A claim with no benefit → ask 'what does it DO for the buyer?' until a benefit answers. Adjectives without benefits are paint without a wall.
- A poster nobody can read → the three-step test. Enlarge the message, cut the words, one picture only. A poster is read by feet that are still moving.
- Persuasion sliding into lies → the review law: claim only what the thing can keep. A burned reader never returns, and tells ten friends.
- This lesson's next door: Speech writing (Level 38) — persuasion leaves the page and stands up: the same claim and call, carried by a voice.
