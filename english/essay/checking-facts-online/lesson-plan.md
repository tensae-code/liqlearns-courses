# Lesson plan — Checking Facts Online

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** Checking Facts Online
**Lesson:** checking-facts-online — spotting fake news and checking a claim before you believe or share it
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 42 — Reading Like a Detective
**The one thing:** The fake-news check — a procedure for testing an online claim before believing it or passing it on.
**This lesson's own level:** Reading lesson (critical reading): what fake news is, the checking procedure (source, other reports, date, evidence), and the share/don't-share decision.

## Why this lesson exists

Indonesia's Grade 9 book teaches a procedure text for identifying fake news — how to spot a false claim online and check it — and no course lesson held it: Reading Between the Lines teaches inference from honest texts, but not testing a text that may be lying. This lesson is built from the placements under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06).

## Sub lessons

1. Not everything online is true — fake news
2. The check — testing a claim step by step
3. Decide before you share

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is needed before this lesson. Process Writing gives the procedure shape this check is written in; Reading Between the Lines gives the reading-under-the-words skill; each of them also stands alone, and so does this one.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- Say what fake news is and why it spreads.
- Run the check on a claim: who says it, who else says it, when, and what evidence.
- Decide — believe, doubt, or don't share — and say why.

## What you need
Nothing special — one real online claim to test together (a headline or a forwarded message).

## The run of the lesson
1. ADD IMAGE: two headlines side by side — one true, one invented, looking exactly alike. The learner guesses which is which; most classes split. That is the lesson's point.
2. Teach sub lesson 2 as a procedure, the book's own kind: numbered steps, imperatives — Check the source. Search for other reports. Look at the date.
3. ADD GIF (1–2 seconds): a magnifying glass over a phone screen — checking is looking closer, not looking away.
4. Sub lesson 3 runs the check live on a forwarded message someone in class has really received.

## Add-ons
- Headline court: pairs defend "true" or "fake" for a headline, using only the check's steps as evidence.
- Family shield: each learner teaches the check to one person at home and reports what claim they tested.

## If the learner is stuck
Keep to two steps — who says it? who else says it? — until they are habit. The full procedure is one step after.

## Teacher note
Indonesia's Grade 9 book writes this teaching AS a procedure text — the fake-news check is both a reading skill and an example of the procedure kind; Process Writing holds the kind, this lesson holds the check itself.
