# Checking Facts Online

## Do it first

Someone forwards you a message: "A giant snake was found in the river near our town!!" Do you believe it? Do you send it to your friends? Hold your answer. In this lesson you will learn what fake news is, a step-by-step check for online claims, and how to decide before you share.

## Sub lesson 1 — Not everything online is true — fake news

**Fake news** is a false story made to look like real news. It spreads because it is exciting, scary or funny — feelings travel faster than facts.

Indonesia's Grade 9 book teaches learners to identify fake news as its own skill — because a reader who cannot test a claim believes whatever shouts loudest.

Signs to notice: no named source, a headline that screams, a story only one place tells, photos that feel wrong.

Look at the snake message again: which signs can you already spot?

## Sub lesson 2 — The check — testing a claim step by step

The book teaches the check as a **procedure** — steps in order:

1. **Check the source.** Who published it? A known newspaper or site, or a page with no name?
2. **Search for other reports.** Do other trusted places tell the same story? A real big event is reported in many places.
3. **Check the date.** Is it new — or an old story wearing a new headline?
4. **Look for evidence.** Names, places, photos that can be verified — or only feelings and capital letters?

Run the four steps on the snake message now, one by one. What survives?

## Sub lesson 3 — Decide before you share

After the check, decide — out loud, with a reason:

- **It checks out** → you may believe it and share it.
- **It doesn't check out** → don't share it. Sharing spreads the lie further — and your name travels with it.
- **You can't tell** → wait. Not sharing yet is always safe.

The click takes one second; the check takes one minute. Indonesia's book puts this decision in school because the phone is already in every learner's hand.

Decide on the snake message: believe, doubt, or don't share — and say which step decided you.

## Say it to your companion

Bring (or invent) one online claim. Your companion runs the four steps aloud, then decides and defends the decision. Swap: this time, you check.

## Do It Yourself

Write the check as your own procedure: four numbered steps, imperative verbs — "Check the source." Then write one real claim you have seen online and your decision about it, with the step that decided you.
