# Hands-on — The essay

The lesson built with paper before it is built with prose.

## Piles and string
Literal piles: write every idea about a topic on its own
slip of paper. On the table, group the slips into piles;
tie each pile with string; the stringed piles are your main
points, and a pile of one slip is a point not yet earned —
grow it or drop it. Photograph the table: that photograph
IS your outline's first draft.

## The skeleton read-aloud
Take essays the class already wrote (or any book's essay).
One reader reads ONLY the topic sentences, in order, standing;
the table must then pass a five-question quiz on the essay
they never read whole. Skeletons that fail the quiz get
repaired at the paragraph whose opener failed — live, in
company.

## Shuffle and restore
Cut a short printed essay into its paragraphs. Shuffle.
In pairs, restore the true order — introduction and
conclusion betray themselves (one names, one returns),
and the body falls between by its points' logic. Then
defend the order: what clue carried each placement?

## The five-paragraph build
The full build, on the clock of an afternoon: outline on
one page (30 minutes, no prose allowed), draft (the house
framed), skeleton test with a partner, revise ONCE against
the test's findings, proofread with CUPS. Five paragraphs,
one topic, several points — an essay, start to finish.
