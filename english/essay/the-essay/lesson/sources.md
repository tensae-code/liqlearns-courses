**Acceptance count: taught in 12 countries (Bolivia, Ghana, Indonesia, Sierra Leone, Jordan, Greece, Kazakhstan, Iraq, Ethiopia, South Africa, Syria, South Sudan).**
Bolivia defines the essay and tables its general structure
whole; Ghana grows the writing process to the essay's
scale with the outline as its planning tool. Indonesia
teaches the multi-paragraph argumentative piece as named
genres with the whole structure labelled — exposition
(thesis – arguments – reiteration) and the two-sided
discussion text (issue – arguments for – arguments against
– conclusion/recommendation); Sierra Leone builds the
five-paragraph, ~450-word essay outright from Primary 5's
Parts of an Essay to the SSS1–SSS2 model lessons; Jordan
teaches the essay fully by Grade 12 — for-and-against,
analytical, general and opinion essays, each with a full
model, planned on printed Graphic Organisers and trained
in formal register; Greece teaches the essay as
word-counted, fully scaffolded Lykeio writing — an
opinion essay and a for-and-against essay at Grade 10,
each with a paragraph plan, a function-organised
linking bank and a checklist, and a problem–solutions
essay at Grades 11–12 with its own five-paragraph plan;
Kazakhstan teaches the essay as a modelled, planned
genre across its parallel series — a for-and-against
essay and a problem-and-solutions essay at Grade 7,
discussion and opinion essays at Grade 9, and two
opposed model opinion essays with a full PLAN closing
its other Grade 9 course; Iraq teaches the essay fully
across the Preparatory years — a modelled school essay
and essay planning at Grade 10, model essays reordered,
planned and written at Grade 11 (a festival essay, a
problem–solution essay mined for linking phrases, a
for-and-against essay whose paragraph functions are
identified before the pupil's own is written), and at
Grade 12 a for-and-against model teaching opinion-essay
linking, an opinion essay analysed by its body
paragraphs, brainstorming taught as the exam technique,
and staged 100–120-word opinion-essay building;
Ethiopia models and structures the essay — Grade 11
teaches essay structure by name (thesis statement,
topic sentences) and sets the argumentative and
narrative essays, Grade 10 sets the argumentative
essay as a named lesson, Grade 9 builds an internet
essay from a diagram beside its printed sample essay
and closes the unit with an opinion essay, and
Grade 12 practises the narrative and argumentative
essays through their jumbled paragraphs; South Africa builds the essay at primary scale — a planned four-paragraph essay about an animal at Grade 5 (Home Language Workbook 1, printed pp.32–33), a three-paragraph myth in Workbook 2 (printed pp.134–136), a persuasive argument with two supporting arguments and a concluding recommendation at Grade 6 (printed pp.48–49), a three-paragraph hobby essay closing its Grade 5 First Additional Language Workbook 2, and a descriptive essay with the past progressive in its Grade 6 First Additional Language Workbook 2 (worksheet 104); Syria teaches the essay at the top of the school — its Grade 12 literary book names and anatomises the essay itself ("What is an Essay": introduction, body, conclusion, the essay types and a three-stage method) and sets the pupil's own essay under a linking-words box, its Grade 12 scientific workbook builds essays unit by unit with Writing Skill boxes (introduction/body/conclusion, a three-step guide, the descriptive essay, balanced argument for a for/against essay), its Grade 7 workbook sets an advantages-and-disadvantages essay, and its Grade 9 book reads optimistic and argument essays as models — all of it short, most tasks capped at 100–120 words; South Sudan joined 2026-11-03: it builds extended writing at primary scale — its Primary 4 book teaches how to structure a persuasive piece with Revise, Edit and Publishing steps (printed pp.80–81) and reads an essay on the benefits of sports as a model (printed pp.141–142), its Primary 5 book sets an advantages-and-disadvantages essay and an essay on staying healthy as unit outcomes, and its Primary 8 book plans a five-paragraph essay by name (Unit 2) — modelled, planned extended writing, with no essay anatomy or thesis-statement teaching of the kind Ethiopia's senior books carry.
