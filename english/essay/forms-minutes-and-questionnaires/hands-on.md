# Hands-on — Forms, minutes and questionnaires

You do it, we guide you. Real instruments, used for real — a form about you, a meeting you hold, questions you really ask.

1. **Your own application form.** Complete a blank application form for yourself, Greece-style (the Grade 9 book ends its jobs unit by having pupils complete a blank application form and CV for themselves): name, date of birth, address, school, languages, skills. Rule of the task: every line true, every box filled or honestly empty, handwriting a stranger can read at arm's length.
2. **A real meeting, both papers.** Hold a ten-minute meeting (family, class, club) with a real decision in it. BEFORE: write the agenda on this TARGET frame — **Meeting of … / Date / Present / Items 1–3 (each with the decision wanted)**. AFTER: write the minutes on this frame — **Decisions** (one line each) / **Actions** (who — what — by when) / **Next meeting**. The chair signs nothing until the action owners have read their own lines and nodded.
3. **A five-question questionnaire.** Design five questions on one topic for a survey report: each question short, single, with three or four tick-box answers (plus 'other'). Test it on two people first: if either asks 'what do you mean?', rewrite that question before the survey runs.
4. **The tally test.** Run the questionnaire on ten people and tally the answers on the spot. Any answer that fits no box gets its own tally line and a note — that note is the questionnaire's first repair list.
