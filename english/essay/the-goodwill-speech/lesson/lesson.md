# The Goodwill Speech

## Do it first

Some speeches argue; this one befriends. The Philippines' Grade 10 book teaches the goodwill speech among its speech kinds: a speech whose whole purpose is to create goodwill — warmth, trust and friendship — between the speaker's group and the listeners. This lesson teaches what it is and the book's four pointers for writing one. (Its ceremonial neighbours — the toast, the roast, the dedication — have their own lessons; speech planning in general is *Speech Writing*.)

## Sub lesson 1 — What a goodwill speech is

*Difficulty: first step — the ground of the lesson.*

A **goodwill speech** is given by a representative — of a school, a company, a town, a country — to an audience whose friendship is wanted. Its key points: the purpose is **relationship, not victory** — nothing is attacked, nothing is demanded; the speaker **finds shared ground** (common history, common values, common hopes); the speaker **speaks appreciation aloud** — thanks for welcome, praise for the hosts' real achievements; and the speech **looks forward together** — to visits, work and friendship to come. It is the speech of the visiting team captain, the exchange student, the new ambassador: its success is measured in warmth left behind.

Try it now: Name three occasions where a goodwill speech fits (a visiting school, a sister-city visit, a company's anniversary guests). For one of them, list the shared ground a speaker could honestly name.

## Sub lesson 2 — Four pointers for writing one

*Difficulty: builds on Sub lesson 1.*

The book gives four pointers for composing. **1. Know the audience** — their names, their pride, their customs: goodwill dies on a wrong name. **2. Begin with thanks and a true compliment** — specific, not flattery: name the real thing you admire. **3. Build on shared ground** — tell the small story or name the value both sides hold, so the audience hears *we* more than *I* and *you*. **4. End by looking forward together** — an invitation, a hope, a next meeting: the door left open. Under all four runs the tone rule: warm, brief and sincere — a goodwill speech that runs long or boasts spends the very goodwill it came to build.

Try it now: Draft a one-minute goodwill speech for a chosen occasion: mark the four pointers in the margin as you write (thanks → compliment → shared ground → forward look). Read it to a partner, who checks each pointer is really there.

## Do it yourself

Write a goodwill speech of about two minutes for a real occasion you can imagine taking part in (a school visit, a sports exchange, a community celebration). Mark the four pointers in the margin, deliver it aloud, and write one line on which pointer was hardest to make sincere — and how you fixed it.
