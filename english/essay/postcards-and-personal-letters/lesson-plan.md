# Lesson plan — Postcards and personal letters

**Course:** English · **Title:** Essay · **Lesson:** Postcards and personal letters
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 40 — "Writing Your Own Life" —
the third lesson of the level.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** writing to one known
person — the postcard, short because its space is short
(greeting, place, weather, food, closing), and the personal
letter, the postcard's big warm room (greeting, why I am
writing, the news in its body, a polite warm closing, name).
**This lesson's own level:** it closes the own-life level —
the diary kept the day for yourself; this writes it to someone.
**What the learner can do at the end:** fit a whole holiday
onto a postcard without spilling, and build a personal
letter on the five-part friendly-letter frame.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Postcards and personal letters — the postcard's parts** — the
   postcard's moves in order — greeting, the place and its news,
   'Wish you were here!', closing and name — and what stands on its
   other side. (Was depth Level 1.)

2. **Postcards and personal letters — the space limit** — the
   correct amount — place, weather, food, one happening, one feeling
   — fitted inside a card's few lines, held together with linking
   words. (Was depth Level 2.)

3. **Postcards and personal letters — the personal letter** — a
   personal letter built on the five-part frame — greeting, why I am
   writing, body, polite closing, signature — in the postcard's warm
   voice, at letter length. (Was depth Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Diary and journal (the postcard is a
  diary entry addressed and shortened). Recommended, never forced.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a postcard, both sides: a photo
   of a place on the front; on the back, a few short lines,
   a name and address, a stamp.** What the student does: the
   student counts the lines the back can hold — the space
   limit is the lesson.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of a friendly letter opened flat,
   its five parts shaded in five colours.** What the student
   does: the student names each colour's job before reading on.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first**: a holiday told twice — letter-length and
   card-length. What survived the shrinking?
2. **The postcard modelled whole** (Egypt Primary 5: greeting,
   holiday news, "Wish you were here!", closing + name,
   address side).
3. **Two more cards read as shapes** (Saudi's model card
   from Jeddah; Mongolia's Istanbul card and its adjective
   upgrade) and Malaysia's rule: the correct amount.
4. **The personal letter's five parts** (Egypt's friendly-
   letter checklist).
5. **Do it yourself**: hands-on card + letter to one person.

## If the learner is stuck
- Card overflowing: Malaysia Form 3's law — write the
  correct amount; cut happenings, keep place, weather,
  food and feeling.
- Letter cold or shapeless: walk Egypt's five parts in
  order; warmth lives in the introduction and the closing.
- This level's next door: Unit 41 — Stories and poems in detail.
