# Short Answer — Critiquing a Speech

(Every item is marked with the sub lesson it belongs to: (Sub lesson N). The gate pool for a sub lesson is all of that sub lesson's questions — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the whole set is the pool.)

1. List the six written-half points of the guided checklist. (Sub lesson 1)
   — Content, relevance, practical application, timeliness, values integration, organisation.

2. Why must free-style criteria be stated before the verdict? (Sub lesson 2)
   — So the judgement is a measurement, not a mood: criteria invented afterwards can be shaped to fit any verdict and judge nothing.

3. What is the focal point assessment? (Sub lesson 2)
   — Judging whether the speech knew its one centre and whether its parts served that centre — the test that separates a shaped speech from a heap of good parts.

4. How is critiquing a speech different from judging its argument's soundness? (Sub lesson 1)
   — Soundness tests claims and evidence; the speech critique judges the whole act — written quality (content to organisation) and delivery (the voice) — for its occasion and audience.
