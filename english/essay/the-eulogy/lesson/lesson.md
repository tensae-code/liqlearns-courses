# The Eulogy

## Do it first

Some speeches are owed to a person. The Philippines' Grade 10 book teaches the eulogy inside its speech work: what a eulogy is, what it must include, and how to deliver one — practised on a short model eulogy for a pet. This lesson teaches the writing and the delivering. (Planning a speech in general is *Speech Writing*; how a speaker looks and sounds on any stage is *Presentation Skills*.)

## Sub lesson 1 — What a eulogy is and what it must include

*Difficulty: first step — the ground of the lesson.*

A **eulogy** is a speech of praise for a person who has died, spoken at their funeral or memorial, usually by someone who knew and loved them. Its work is threefold: to **remember** (the person's life, character and story), to **thank** (what they gave — love, teaching, example), and to **say farewell** (to let the listeners grieve together and begin to let go). What it must include, as the book sets out: who the person was; memories and moments that show their character better than claims do; their place in the lives of the people present; honest feeling, held with dignity; and a closing farewell. The book's model — a short eulogy for a pet — shows the same shape at small scale: a life remembered, a gift thanked for, a goodbye said.

Try it now: Outline a eulogy for a person (real, historical, or the book's pet): one memory that shows character, one gift to thank for, one farewell line. Say the outline aloud to a partner: does each part do its work?

## Sub lesson 2 — Delivering a eulogy

*Difficulty: builds on Sub lesson 1.*

A eulogy is delivered to grieving people, and the delivery serves them. **Word choice:** plain, warm and true — the person's own name, concrete memories, no showing off. **Stance and body language:** stand steady and still, eyes moving gently over the listeners, hands quiet; the body says *I am holding this with you*. **Vocal variety:** slower than ordinary speech, with pauses that let feeling land; softer at the farewell; a voice that may shake is allowed — a voice that performs is not. The book's guidance (its source is a speaking-tips page — the book prints the address as "speaming-tips.com", taught corrected) is practical: practise aloud, know the opening and closing by heart, and leave silence where the listeners need it.

Try it now: Take your outline from Sub lesson 1 and speak its first minute aloud. Record or perform it for a partner, who marks: one place the pace served the listeners, one place the words could be plainer.

## Do it yourself

Write a full short eulogy (about two minutes spoken) for a person you choose — a grandparent, a teacher, a public figure, or a beloved pet, as the book models. Build it in the three works, deliver it aloud twice, and note one delivery change you made between the two readings and why.
