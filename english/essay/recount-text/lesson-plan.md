# Lesson plan — Recount Text

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** Recount Text
**Lesson:** recount-text — telling what happened: the recount's shape and how to write one
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 36 — Writing the Facts
**The one thing:** The recount — a text that retells past events in the order they happened, in three parts: orientation, events, closing.
**This lesson's own level:** Writing lesson (text kind): recount purpose, the three-part structure, sequence words and past verbs, and writing a recount of a real event.

## Why this lesson exists

Indonesia teaches the recount as a named text kind in three books and it had no lesson of its own: the Grade 6 personal recount, the Grade 9 recount structure and writing work, and the Grade 10 sports-event recount. Process Writing carried the recount named inside Indonesia's genre spine, but no lesson taught the kind itself. This lesson is built from the placements under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06).

## Sub lessons

1. What a recount is — telling what happened
2. The recount shape — orientation, events, closing
3. Writing your own recount

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is needed before this lesson. Past Actions gives the past-tense verbs a recount runs on; First, Then, Finally gives the sequence words; each of them also stands alone, and so does this one.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- Say what a recount is and what it is for.
- Name and use its three parts: orientation, events in order, closing.
- Write a recount of something that really happened to you.

## What you need
Nothing special — one true memory: a trip, a match, a day that went wrong or right.

## The run of the lesson
1. ADD IMAGE: three photos in a row — leaving home, the match, coming back tired and happy. The learner tells the day in three sentences before the shape is named.
2. Teach sub lesson 2 on that telling: the first photo orients, the middle events run in order, the last closes.
3. ADD GIF (1–2 seconds): beads sliding onto a string in order — events in a recount cannot jump around; the string is time.
4. Sub lesson 3 writes the class's shared day first (a real school event), then each learner's own.

## Add-ons
- Photo recount: bring three photos of one day; write the recount they tell.
- Wrong-order fix: shuffle a recount's event sentences; a partner restores the order and says which words helped (first, then, after that, finally).

## If the learner is stuck
Keep to three events, in order, past tense, with first/then/finally. The shape is the lesson; length is not.

## Teacher note
Indonesia's books teach the recount as one of the named genre kinds, beside procedure and report; the lesson keeps it inside that family — Process Writing holds the procedure, this lesson holds the recount, each kind on its own feet.
