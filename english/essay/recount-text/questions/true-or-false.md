## True or false

1. A recount can jump backwards and forwards in time as it likes. (Sub lesson 1)
   **Answer: False** — events stand in the order they happened.

2. A recount of a football match is a real recount kind. (Sub lesson 1)
   **Answer: True** — the sports-event recount is taught in the books.

3. The orientation comes at the end of a recount. (Sub lesson 2)
   **Answer: False** — orientation opens it: who, where, when.

4. The closing tells how the events ended and how the writer felt. (Sub lesson 2)
   **Answer: True** — ending + feeling close a recount.

5. A recount uses present-tense verbs because it is read now. (Sub lesson 3)
   **Answer: False** — past events take the past tense.

6. Sequence words like first, then and finally help the reader follow the order. (Sub lesson 3)
   **Answer: True** — they are the string the events hang on.
