# Advantages and Disadvantages

## Sub lesson 1 — The words for both sides

To weigh a thing up, you need words for the two sides.

- Good side: **advantage, benefit, good point, plus** — *One advantage of cycling is that it is cheap.*
- Bad side: **disadvantage, drawback, bad point, minus** — *A drawback is that it is slow in the rain.*
- Turning words: **on the one hand … on the other hand**, **however**, **but**, **although**.
- Adding points: **firstly, secondly, also, finally**.

Do it: take "living in a big city" and say one advantage and one disadvantage out loud, using the words above.

## Sub lesson 2 — Building the balanced piece

A balanced piece has four moves.

1. **Opening** — name the topic and say you will look at both sides.
2. **Good points paragraph** — your advantages, each with a reason or example.
3. **Bad points paragraph** — your disadvantages, honestly given.
4. **Closing view** — weigh them: which side is stronger FOR YOU, and why.

Keep the two sides fair — a piece that hides the bad points is an advertisement, not a discussion. The source books practise exactly this shape, for example on the topic of team sports.

Do it: write the four moves for "homework" — one sentence for each move.

## Say it to your companion

Read the key sentences aloud and explain them to your companion in your own words — what the thing is, and one example of your own.

## Do It Yourself

Close the lesson and use the thing once in real life today — say it, write it, or notice it — then say how it went in one sentence.
