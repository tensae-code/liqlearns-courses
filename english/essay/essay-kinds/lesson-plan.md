# Lesson plan — Essay kinds

**Course:** English · **Title:** Essay · **Lesson:** Essay kinds
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 39, "Essays and Shortening".
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the kinds of essay
as ONE MAP — argumentative, opinion, persuasive,
descriptive, expository, compare-contrast, cause-effect,
problem-solution, advantages-disadvantages — what each
kind is FOR, and the two skills every kind shares:
introductions and conclusions, and the counter-argument.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The essay —
the basic shape is NOT retaught here; this lesson is the
map of what the shape can be aimed at.
**What the learner can do at the end:** name any set
essay's kind from its task, choose the structure that
kind demands, open and close it properly, and plant a
counter-argument and answer it.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The essay (the basic shape).
  Recommended, never forced — this lesson names the
  shape's parts without rebuilding them.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of nine signposted roads leaving
   one town square, each sign naming a kind of essay.**
   What the student does: the student reads a task aloud
   and points at its road.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of two scales balancing, one
   labelled FOR, one AGAINST.** What the student does:
   the student says which kinds must keep both pans
   loaded and which may tip the scale on purpose.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first**: three exam tasks — name each kind
   before any teaching.
2. **The map**: the nine kinds by their job (India's
   four checks ride over all of them).
3. **Indonesia's named frames**: analytical exposition,
   hortatory exposition, discussion text.
4. **Introductions and conclusions as skills**
   (Malaysia F5's two units).
5. **The counter-argument**: the other side, planted
   and answered.
6. **Do it yourself**: the hands-on kind-pick and build.

## If the learner is stuck
- Kinds blur: ask the task one question — "what must the
  reader DO at the end: believe, feel, understand, or
  decide?" The answer names the kind.
- Counter-argument missing: an argument without the
  other side is a speech to the converted; plant it in
  the plan, not as an afterthought.
