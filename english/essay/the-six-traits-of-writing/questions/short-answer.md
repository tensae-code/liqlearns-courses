# Short Answer — The Six Traits of Writing

(Every item is marked with the sub lesson it belongs to: (Sub lesson N). The gate pool for a sub lesson is all of that sub lesson's questions — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the whole set is the pool.)

1. Name all six traits in order. (Sub lesson 1)
   — Idea development, organisation, voice, word choice, sentence fluency, conventions.

2. A paragraph is clear and correct but sounds like a form being filled. Which trait is weak? (Sub lesson 1)
   — Voice — the writer is absent from the words; no living person speaks to the reader.

3. How do you test sentence fluency, and what are its two failure shapes? (Sub lesson 2)
   — Read aloud. Failures: choppy sameness (every sentence the same short shape) and endless tangles (sentences that never land).

4. Why revise trait by trait instead of 'making it better'? (Sub lesson 2)
   — Because the framework names where the weakness lives; a named trait gives the fix a target, while a general feeling gives none.
