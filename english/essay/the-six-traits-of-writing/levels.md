# Levels — The Six Traits of Writing

One lesson, 2 sub lessons. A sub lesson opens by proving the previous one (at most 5 questions, mixed with earlier parts, drawn from the whole question set of the sub lesson just before — no hardness ranking is written in questions/).

| Sub lesson | What the learner can do at the end |
|---|---|
| 1. Ideas, organisation, voice | Judge a piece by its first three traits: the development of its ideas, its organisation, and its voice. |
| 2. Word choice, sentence fluency, conventions | Judge a piece by its last three traits: word choice, sentence fluency and conventions — and revise by the trait, not by the feeling. |

A learner who finishes both sub lessons owns this lesson whole: the six traits that make writing good — idea development, organisation, voice, word choice, sentence fluency and conventions — used as a framework for judging and improving a piece of writing.
