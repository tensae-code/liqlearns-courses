# Lesson plan — Letters that ask and advise

**Course:** English · **Title:** Essay · **Lesson:** Letters that ask and advise
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 34 — "Letters That Work" —
the last lesson of the level.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the formal letter's
four asking kinds — the enquiry (ask for information), the
advice letter (give counsel), the letter to the editor
(ask the public), the letter to an authority (ask someone
with power) — and the primary set they grow from:
invitation, praise, request, sympathy, apology.
**This lesson's own level:** one frame, four addressees;
the ask changes its dress with who holds the power.
**What the learner can do at the end:** name which kind a
situation needs and write it, with the ask built for its
reader.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Letters that ask and advise — the primary five and the
   enquiry** — where asking letters begin — invitation, praise,
   request, sympathy, apology — and the enquiry, the ask for
   information, built as a list of questions. (The kinds of depth
   Level 1; the enquiry's ask was depth Level 2's.)

2. **Letters that ask and advise — the authority and the editor** —
   facts plus one request for someone with power, and a hedged
   opinion for the public — the door chosen by who holds the power.
   (Was depth Level 2's asks, with depth Level 3's door-choosing.)

3. **Letters that ask and advise — the advice letter** — counsel
   softened for its reader — restate the trouble, then advise
   without commanding. (Was depth Level 2's advice ask.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The formal letter. Recommended, never
  forced.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of one envelope travelling to four
   doors: an office desk, a friend's house, a newspaper,
   a chief's office.** What the student does: the student
   says what the letter asks at each door.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of five small cards: invitation,
   praise, request, sympathy, apology.** What the student
   does: the student writes one line for the card drawn.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first**: one problem, four possible letters —
   which door?
2. **The primary five** (Barbados's syllabus set).
3. **The enquiry** and the letter to an authority (Sierra
   Leone's formal purposes; Ghana's authority list).
4. **The editor letter** (Jordan's model + hedging).
5. **The advice letter**.
6. **Do it yourself**: two kinds written from one event.

## If the learner is stuck
- Wrong door chosen: ask "who can actually do this?" —
  the kind follows the power.
- Advice sounding like orders: soften with the counsel
  dress (If I were you…, Have you thought of…?).
- Next level: Writing for a Job — CV and cover letter.
