# Hands-on — Letters that ask and advise

A real writing task, with model frames — one event, two
letters.

## The event (invented for this lesson)
The road past your school has no crossing, and two
children were nearly hit this month. Something must change.

## Task 1 — the letter to an authority
Write to the head of the district roads office. Frame:
[Formal layout] Dear Sir or Madam,
P1 — I am writing to ask for … (a crossing, named, on the
named road, outside the named school).
P2 — the facts: the road, the traffic, the two near
misses with their dates.
P3 — I would be grateful if you could … (the one action,
and by when you hope for an answer).
Yours faithfully, [name]

## Task 2 — the letter to the editor
Same facts, public door. Frame:
Dear Editor,
P1 — I read your report on road safety (date) and I am
writing about the road outside … School.
P2 — the facts, shorter — a newspaper reader gets three.
P3 — your view, hedged and firm: "It seems to me that…
until a crossing is built, children remain at risk."
[name, town]

## Check
- Could Task 1's reader act tomorrow? Could Task 2's
  reader repeat your view in one sentence? Both must pass.
