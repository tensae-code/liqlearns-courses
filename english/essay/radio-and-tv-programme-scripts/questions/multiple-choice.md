## Multiple choice

1. Why must a radio script carry every sound in writing? (Sub lesson 1)
   a) The listener cannot see anything  b) Radios have no speakers  c) Programmes are very short
   **Answer: a) The listener cannot see anything** — every thing the listener must know is a word or a sound in the script.

2. What is the technical script also called? (Sub lesson 1)
   a) The cue  b) The production code  c) The wild track
   **Answer: b) The production code** — the instructions that run the programme beside the spoken words.

3. In radio terms, what is a sting? (Sub lesson 1)
   a) A long interview  b) A very short burst of music or sound that marks a join  c) The station's name
   **Answer: b) A very short burst of music or sound that marks a join.**

4. What is a wild track? (Sub lesson 1)
   a) Background sound recorded on location  b) A song played at the end  c) A mistake in the script
   **Answer: a) Background sound recorded on location** — it gives a scene its real air.

5. In the TV script format, which column holds everything the audience hears? (Sub lesson 2)
   a) WHO  b) VIDEO  c) AUDIO
   **Answer: c) AUDIO** — words, music and sounds.

6. What does one row of a TV script give you? (Sub lesson 2)
   a) One whole programme  b) One moment: who is there, what is heard, what is seen  c) Only the words
   **Answer: b) One moment: who is there, what is heard, what is seen.**
