# Lesson plan — Radio and TV Programme Scripts

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** Radio and TV Programme Scripts
**Lesson:** radio-and-tv-programme-scripts — how radio and TV programmes are planned and written: the radio script and its terms, production techniques, and the TV script's three columns
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 38 — Speeches and Shows
**The one thing:** a broadcast is written before it is heard or seen — the script is the plan that tells every voice, sound and picture where it goes
**This lesson's own level:** Writing lesson: radio first (a programme is defined, the script's parts named, a sample script read, production techniques taught), then TV (how programmes are written and produced, and the WHO / AUDIO / VIDEO script format).

## Why this lesson exists

No lesson housed broadcast writing. South Sudan's Secondary 2 Student's Book gives a whole unit stretch to it (Unit 7, printed pp.112–124): radio and TV programmes defined, the parts of a radio script (the technical script / production code), the radio terms (cue, sting, station), a sample radio programme script, radio production techniques (sounds, wild tracks, interviews, research), and how TV programmes are written and produced with the TV script format (WHO / AUDIO / VIDEO). The 2026-11-08 re-verification sweep marked all four parts NEW LESSON NEEDED. play-scripts in the same unit houses the stage play (and stocks South Sudan's TV drama-script item); the broadcast script is its own craft and gets its own lesson under the everything-goes-in ruling (2026-11-06). Its sources are named in lesson/sources.md.

## Sub lessons

1. **Radio programmes and the radio script** — what a radio programme is; the parts of a radio script (technical script / production code); radio terms (cue, sting, station); a sample radio programme script read whole; how a radio programme is produced (sounds, wild tracks, interviews, research)
2. **The TV script** — how TV programmes are written and produced; the TV script format with its WHO / AUDIO / VIDEO columns

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is forced first — this lesson stands alone. It stands beside play-scripts in the same unit: that lesson writes for the stage, where the audience sees the actors; here the radio audience sees nothing, so the script must carry every sound, and the TV script must plan picture and sound together. A learner who knows speech-writing will find the spoken-word care familiar; everything broadcast-specific is built inside the lesson.

## What the learner can do when it ends
- Say what a radio programme and a TV programme are, as the book defines them.
- Name the parts of a radio script and use the radio terms cue, sting and station rightly.
- Read a sample radio programme script and say how it is built.
- Name the radio production techniques: sounds, wild tracks, interviews, research.
- Lay out a TV script in its three columns — WHO, AUDIO, VIDEO — and say what goes in each.

## What you need
A phone or any recorder helps: the lesson's proof is a script the learner can record and play back.

## The run of the lesson
1. **Do it first** — the learner plans thirty seconds of radio about their own morning, in words only, before any script part is named (lesson/lesson.md).
2. Work through sub lesson 1: the radio programme, the script's parts and terms, the sample script, the production techniques.
3. Work through sub lesson 2: how TV programmes are written and produced; the three-column TV script.
4. ADD IMAGE: a radio script page with its parts labelled, beside a TV script page with its three columns — the student labels the same parts on a blank page.

## Add-ons
- Station minute: write and record a one-minute programme for your class "station" — opening cue, one interview, one wild track.
- Column swap: take the radio script from the Do It Yourself and re-lay it as a TV script, deciding what the VIDEO column shows while each line is heard.

## If the learner is stuck
If the radio script comes out as an essay, use the test: nobody can see anything — every thing the listener must know has to be a sound or a word in the script. If the TV columns blur, remember WHO speaks, AUDIO is everything heard (words and sounds), VIDEO is everything seen.

## Teacher note
South Sudan teaches this in Secondary 2 inside its communication unit, beside the critical review and The Time Machine — the lesson keeps the book's order: radio whole first, then television, because the radio script teaches the ear-only discipline the TV script then builds on.
