# Hands-on — Review writing

You do it, we guide you. Review something real that you have really seen, read or eaten — no reviewing from memory of other people's opinions.

1. **Notes before verdict.** While the experience is fresh (a film's end, a meal's last bite), write five specific details — things seen, heard, tasted, timed — before you write a single judgement word. Details first is the review's honesty machine.
2. **The weighing page.** Draw Egypt's balance: pros on the left, cons on the right, each entry one detail, not one adjective. Count the weight, not the entries — one heavy con can outweigh three light pros, and the recommendation must follow the weight.
3. **Write on the model frame.** Build the review on this TARGET frame — copy the part names, never translate them: **Introduction** (what it is + your overall impression in one sentence) / **Details** (the ordered specifics) / **Pros and cons** (weighed, fairly) / **Conclusion and recommendation** (who should go/read/eat, and who should not). 150–200 words is a full review at this size.
4. **The trust test.** Swap reviews with a companion. Each marks: one place where a strong word has no detail paying for it, and the sentence where the verdict stops matching the weighing. Repair both before the review is 'published' to the class wall.
