# Review writing — Short answer

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Review writing — the four parts · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Review writing — judgement with reasons · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Review writing — the trusted verdict.

Answer in whole sentences.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Write an overall-impression sentence for a café whose coffee is superb but whose chairs are broken. **Answer: The café serves superb coffee in a room that fights you (any one sentence naming the thing + a mixed verdict earns it).** — part one: the verdict in seed form, praise and blame already sharing it.

**2. (Sub lesson 2)** Pay for this judgement with a detail: 'The acting was wooden.' **Answer: The hero said his lines like a shopping list (any concrete detail that shows the fault earns it).** — a strong word without a detail is an unpaid claim.

**3. (Sub lesson 3)** Your review's pros are light (nice poster, cheap ticket) and one con is heavy (you could not hear the actors). What must the recommendation do? **Answer: It must follow the heavy side — recommend with a warning, or not at all; the verdict matches the weight, not the count.** — weigh, do not count: one heavy con can outweigh three light pros.
