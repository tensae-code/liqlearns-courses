# Lesson plan — Review writing

**Course:** English · **Title:** Essay · **Lesson:** Review writing
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 37 — "Writing Your View"
(CURRICULUM.md; CATCH-UP-PLAN.md Levels 36–37).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the review — a piece that informs AND persuades: a fair summary of the thing, your judgements in order with reasons, and a recommendation a reader can trust.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The essay and on Report writing — the review keeps the report's honesty and adds a judgement that must be earned. It opens Unit 37, Writing Your View.
**What the learner can do at the end:**
- build a review on Egypt's four parts: introduction + overall impression, specific details, pros and cons, conclusion + recommendation
- judge in order with reasons — every strong word paid for by a detail
- review different things in the same honest shape: a film, a book, a restaurant, a place
- describe richly without over-listing, and end with a recommendation a named reader can use

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The essay; Report writing. Recommended, never forced.
- A review is not an advertisement and not a summary: the summary serves the judgement, and the judgement must be fair to be believed.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a balance scale: on one side a plate of good points, on the other the bad points, a hand writing the verdict beneath.** Written slot: "a picture of a review weighing pros and cons." What the student does: the student points at the heavier side and says the verdict it earns — the recommendation must match the weight.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of a restaurant table with two dishes, one steaming and praised, one cold, a notebook open beside them.** Written slot: "a picture of a meal being reviewed, notes open." What the student does: the student writes one specific detail for each dish before reading on — reviews are built from details, not adjectives alone.
3. **Slot 3 — a GIF (1–2 seconds) of a cinema seat row: a film ends, lights rise, a hand writes five stars, then rubs one out and writes four.** Written slot: "a picture of a star rating being corrected from five to four." What the student does: the student says what the rubbed-out star teaches — an honest review can praise and still take one star away.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first** (lesson/lesson.md): two opinions of the same meal — one all praise with no detail, one weighed; only one can be trusted with your money.
2. **The four parts**: Egypt's REVIEW Tip Box (Secondary 12 Term 1, pp.93–95) — a review informs and persuades.
3. **The film review**: Egypt's film-review writing (New Hello! Secondary 11 Term 2, Unit 11) on two read models; Nepal's Grade 11 film and book reviews (Unit 18).
4. **The restaurant review**: Malaysia's Form 3 restaurant review (Unit 2) — ordering adjectives, recommending; Egypt's model restaurant review weighed.
5. **Description without over-listing**: Malaysia's Form 5 review writing (Unit 9).
6. **The short review commission**: Yemen's Book 6 workbook task — write a short review of a book you have just read or a film you have just seen (corrected: seen).
7. **Do it yourself**: one real review, weighed and recommended.

## If the learner is stuck
- All praise, no trust → add the cons honestly. One real fault, fairly stated, makes every praise believable.
- Adjectives without details → every strong word must be paid for: not 'the acting was bad' but what the actor DID that failed.
- A summary that eats the review → the thing itself gets a quarter of the space; your weighed judgement is the rest.
- This lesson's next door: Blog and online posts (same level) — the review's informal cousin: judgement again, in a dated, friendly voice.
