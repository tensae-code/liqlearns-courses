# Lesson plan — Note-making

**Course:** English · **Title:** Essay · **Lesson:** Note-making
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 39 — Essays and Shortening.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** cutting a text
down to NOTE FORM — phrases, not sentences; numbered and
grouped points; standard abbreviations — and heading a
text's sections with sub-titles, so the notes still make
sense when re-read cold.
**This lesson's own level:** it is the first shortening
craft; Summarising (next lesson) rebuilds sentences from
such notes, so the pair are taught in this order.
**What the learner can do at the end:** work a passage
through India's five steps, use the standard
abbreviations (ASAP, N.B., V.I.P., RSVP, c/o, e.g.)
without error, and divide a long text under sub-titles.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Cutting to Note Form** — the cutting — India's five steps and
   cutting sentences to note form, phrases not sentences; the steps
   also carry the numbering and grouping that the next sub lesson
   sets out as a system. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **The Note-Making System** — the system — the numbering systems
   (1 / (i) / (a), or decimal 1.1.1) and the standard abbreviations
   (ASAP, N.B., V.I.P., RSVP, c/o, e.g.). (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **Sub-Titling** — sub-titling — dividing a long text into
   sections and heading each with a sub-title that states its main
   idea at a glance. (Was depth Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is
written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the
gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Finding the idea of a text.
  Recommended, never forced.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a full page of text on the
   left shrinking into a small numbered card on the
   right, same meaning, a fifth of the words.** What the
   student does: the student points at one vanished word
   and says why the note does not miss it.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of a long newspaper page cut by
   three bold sub-titles into four short sections.** What
   the student does: the student reads only the
   sub-titles and says what the whole page is about.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first**: a sentence and its note twin — what
   exactly was cut?
2. **India's five steps** (Hornbill W1, pp.89–93).
3. **The numbering systems**: 1, 2, 3 / (i) (ii) (iii) /
   (a) (b) (c), and the decimal 1.1, 1.1.1.
4. **Abbreviations**: Uzbekistan's standard set for
   notes; Kuwait's elliptical notes and messages.
5. **Sub-titling** (Hornbill W3, pp.99–101).
6. **Do it yourself**: the hands-on note-making build.

## If the learner is stuck
- Notes are just short sentences: cut the verbs and the
  little words — a note is a label for an idea, not a
  small copy of the text.
- Notes unreadable a day later: the re-read test is the
  law — if cold-you cannot rebuild the meaning, the note
  failed, however short it is.
- Next in this level: Summarising.
