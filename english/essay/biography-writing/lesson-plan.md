# Lesson plan — Biography writing

**Course:** English · **Title:** Essay · **Lesson:** Biography writing
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 40, "Writing Your Own Life" —
the second lesson of the level.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** writing a life —
the biography (another person's life, third person) and
the autobiography (your own life, first person) — told in
life order, carried by key dates, in the past tense.
**This lesson's own level:** it follows Diary and journal
(the day) with the whole life; Postcards and personal
letters close the level.
**What the learner can do at the end:** plan and write a
biography in the books' order — who, when and where, early
life, major achievements, later life, why they matter —
and turn the same life into an autobiography by changing
the person, not the facts.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Diary and journal; Past actions.
  Recommended, never forced.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a life as a road with date-signs
   along it: born, school, first work, great deed, late
   years.** What the student does: the student walks the
   road aloud, saying what each sign must carry.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of two tellers of the same life:
   one stands beside the person (third person), one IS
   the person (first person).** What the student does: the
   student says one fact both ways — "He was born in…" /
   "I was born in…".

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first**: the same life in two voices — what changed?
2. **The pair defined** (South Sudan Primary 7: biography
   written by another; autobiography by the person).
3. **The kind defined short** (Egypt Preparatory 8's Tip:
   who they are, what they did, why they are important).
4. **The full structure** (Egypt Secondary 12: introduction,
   early life, major achievements, later life, conclusion;
   past tense, third person, dates, linking words).
5. **The turn** (South Sudan's own drill: rewrite a
   biography as an autobiography).
6. **Do it yourself**: hands-on biography + the turn.

## If the learner is stuck
- Facts in a heap: lay them on the road — Egypt's order is
  the cure; a life is told in the order it was lived.
- Voice slipping mid-piece: pick the teller and hold them —
  he/she for another's life, I for your own.
- Next in this level: Postcards and personal letters.
