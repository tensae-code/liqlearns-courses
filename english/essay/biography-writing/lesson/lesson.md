# Biography writing — the lesson

Course: English · Title: Essay · Lesson: Biography writing
Built from: Egypt, Secondary 12 Term 2, Unit 12, Lesson 5
(the biography structure; the model biography of Naguib
Mahfouz) — South Sudan, Primary English 7 Pupil's Book,
Unit 6 "Biographies and Autobiographies" (pp.67–82) —
Armenia, English 7, Unit 5 (writing the biography of a
famous person, p.119) — Ghana, the NaCCA curriculum
(curriculum evidence only: biography and autobiography
named among the kinds whose sequence of events is
analysed, Basic 9).

*Speaker note: the explanation is read in your own
language. The life-writing's own wording — dates, names,
"He was born…", "I was born…" — is the TARGET text: never
translated, always read in English.*

*If you have written a diary, you have written one day of
a life; this lesson writes the whole life — but it stands
alone: both kinds are defined and built here.*

## Do it first
Two openings about the same woman. (1) "Amina was born in
1960. She taught for forty years. She opened three
schools." (2) "I was born in 1960. I taught for forty
years. I opened three schools." Same facts, same order —
what changed? Only the teller. Everything in this lesson
grows from that one change.

[Media slot: a picture of two tellers of the same life —
one standing beside the person, one being the person.
What you do: say one fact both ways, "She was born in…"
and "I was born in…".]

## Then the explanation
**The pair.** South Sudan's Primary 7 book gives a whole
unit to the pair and defines them in one breath: a
**biography** is a life **written by another person**; an
**autobiography** is a life **written by the person
themselves**. The unit works the pair on real lives — a
printed biography of Dr John Garang de Mabior (born 1945;
died 30 July 2005) against a three-page autobiography
extract from Malala Yousafzai's own book — compares the
two, then sets the turn this lesson's hands-on copies:
**rewrite a biography as an autobiography**, and write
your own autobiography and a community biography.

**The kind, defined short.** Egypt's Preparatory 8 book
carries a Biography Tip beside its model (a cloze
biography of Ahmed Idris, the Nubian soldier honoured in
2017): a biography is **the story of a person's life —
who they are, what they did, and why they are important**.
Its task is a biography of 90–100 words about your role
model. Preparatory 7's three tips agree: say who and why
you chose them; what they did, in the past simple; the
qualities that make them known.

**The full structure.** Egypt's Secondary 12 book states
the whole frame: the questions **who / when and where /
what / why important**, answered in the order
**introduction, early life, major achievements, later
life, conclusion** — in the **past tense, third person**,
carried by **dates and linking words**. Its model is the
biography of Naguib Mahfouz (1911–2006; the Cairo Trilogy;
the Nobel Prize, 1988), and its task is a biography of
180–200 words about a person of your choice. Armenia's
Grade 7 book sets the same work inside its Olympics unit:
**writing the biography of a famous person** (p.119) —
the life of someone whose deeds are on record, so the
facts can be checked. Ghana's curriculum — curriculum
evidence only, as Ghana holds no pupil textbook — names
the pair from the analysis side: at Basic 9, learners
analyse the **sequence of events across autobiography,
biography, narrative and drama**. The order is the thing
every country agrees on: a life is told in the order it
was lived.

**Key dates do the carrying.** Born, schooled, first work,
the great deed, the honour, the end — a biography without
dates is a heap of facts; with dates, it is a road. Lay
the dates down first, as the hands-on frame does, and the
paragraphs almost write themselves.

## Do it yourself
1. Build the hands-on biography: dates first, then the
   five parts in order.
2. Do the turn: rewrite two paragraphs as autobiography.
3. Test a life-piece from any book against the short
   definition: who — what they did — why they matter.
   Mark any leg that is missing.
