# Lesson plan — Speech writing

**Course:** English · **Title:** Essay · **Lesson:** Speech writing
**Place in the curriculum:** Level 38, "Speeches and Shows".
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the speech — a piece
of writing made to be HEARD: address the audience, walk
your points in order, close with a line that stays; and
the tools that make listeners stay with you — the rule of
three and ethos, logos, pathos.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The essay (the
same ordered points, now spoken aloud) and gives the
for/against speech its own shape.
**What the learner can do at the end:** plan and write a
short speech with a vocative address, ordered points and
a closing line; build a for/against speech; and use — and
spot — tripling, repetition and the three appeals.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: The essay. Recommended, never forced.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a speaker at a school assembly,
   the audience's faces turned up to her.** What the
   student does: the student says who the audience is and
   what the opening address must call them.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of three stepping stones across a
   stream, labelled 1, 2, 3.** What the student does: the
   student names the speech's three parts as they step —
   tell what you will tell, tell it, tell what you told.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first**: the same news told as a report and as
   a speech opening — which one makes you look up?
2. **The three parts** (Ghana B8's printed rule).
3. **Address and order**: the vocative address; points in
   a felt order (Ghana curriculum; Nepal's speech work).
4. **The for/against speech** (Egypt's set writing kind).
5. **Rhetoric**: rule of three / tripling, repetition,
   parallel structure, emotive words, inclusive "we"
   (Ghana B9; Greece's rhetoric table) and ethos / logos /
   pathos (Greece).
6. **Do it yourself**: the hands-on speech, both kinds.

## If the learner is stuck
- Speech reads like an essay: read it aloud — every
  sentence the ear trips on gets shortened.
- Points wander: number them on the plan before drafting;
  a speech is heard once, so its order must be hearable.
- Next in this level: Presentation skills.
