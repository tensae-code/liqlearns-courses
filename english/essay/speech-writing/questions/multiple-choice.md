# Speech writing — multiple choice

Sub-lesson markers (2026-11-08 restructure): every item's marker names the sub lesson it belongs to. The files' old depth groups map onto the sub lessons as: (Level 1) -> Sub lesson 1 — Speech in Three Parts · (Level 2) -> Sub lesson 2 — Ordered Points and the For/Against Speech · (Level 3) -> Sub lesson 3 — Speech Rhetoric.

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** Ghana's three-part rule for a speech is —
a) open, argue, sit down b) tell what you are going to
tell them; tell them; tell them what you told them
c) read the whole essay aloud **Answer: b)** — the
listener cannot re-read, so the speech carries its map.

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** The vocative address is — a) the closing
line b) naming the audience at the start: "Teachers,
parents, my friends — good morning" c) the title
**Answer: b)** — Ghana's named standing part of a speech.

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** In a for/against speech, the honest build
is — a) hide the other side b) give both sides fairly,
then take yours and answer the strongest point against
you c) give only jokes **Answer: b)** — Egypt's kind,
argued the way its debate unit works both sides.

**4. (Sub lesson 3)** "It's your country, it's my country,
it's our future!" is — a) logos only b) tripling — three
parallel beats c) a vocative address **Answer: b)** —
Ghana B9's printed example of the device.

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** In Greece's table, an appeal to the
audience's feelings is — a) ethos b) logos c) pathos
**Answer: c)** — pathos is the heart's rope; ethos is
trust in the speaker, logos the true, ordered argument.
