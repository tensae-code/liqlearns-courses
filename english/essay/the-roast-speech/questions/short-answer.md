# Short Answer — The Roast Speech

(Every item is marked with the sub lesson it belongs to: (Sub lesson N). The gate pool for a sub lesson is all of that sub lesson's questions — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the whole set is the pool.)

1. What separates a roast from an insult speech? (Sub lesson 1)
   — Purpose and landing: a roast is researched affection — it teases what the guest can laugh about, inside a courteous frame, and ends in genuine praise; an insult speech aims to wound.

2. Why does the speech end in praise rather than in the biggest joke? (Sub lesson 1)
   — Because the occasion is an honour: the audience must leave knowing why the guest deserves the room; the praise is the roast's point, the jokes its road.

3. Give the book's five effectiveness ingredients. (Sub lesson 2)
   — Personal pronouns, direct quotations, polite expressions, humour, vivid description.

4. Why are the guest's own quoted words 'fairer' than paraphrase? (Sub lesson 2)
   — Because the teasing then rests on what the guest truly said — the room laughs at the real record, not at the speaker's invention.
