# Debating

## Do it first
Argue for one minute — out loud, to anyone — that school should start an hour later. Then argue for one minute that it should not. You have just felt the shape of a debate: two sides, one question, arguments in order. This lesson teaches the real contest.

## Sub lesson 1 — How a Debate Runs
A debate is a contest over a **moot** — a statement one team defends and the other attacks. The team defending it is the **affirmative**; the team against it is the **negative**. Sāmoa's books teach the speaking order the same way at two levels, because the order is the skeleton of the whole contest:

1. **First affirmative** — opens the case for the moot.
2. **First negative** — opens the case against it.
3. **Second affirmative** — builds the case further and answers the other side.
4. **Second negative** — the same, against.
5. **Third affirmative** — **rebuts** the negative team's arguments and **sums up** the affirmative case.
6. **Third negative** — rebuts and sums up last.

Hold the third speaker's job carefully: rebuttal means answering the *other* team's arguments. Sāmoa's Year 10 book prints this instruction with a slip — it tells the third affirmative speaker to rebut the affirmative team's own arguments — and the slip is worth noticing, because the corrected version is the whole job: you rebut your opponents, then you sum up your own side.

## Sub lesson 2 — Speaking in a Debate
Inside the order, the language does the work. Debaters **state opinions** as positions and must **recognise** the other side's opinions accurately enough to answer them — rebuttal fails when it attacks an argument nobody made. Sāmoa's Year 11 book gives juniors a starter exercise to build the habit at small scale before the full contest. And its Year 12 book teaches the reader's side of the skill: following a whole debate through its **quotations**, speaker by speaker — who said this, for which side, answering what — on the moot "That sport leads to violence". A debate you can follow on paper is a debate you can judge.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the speaking order of a debate and the third speaker's two jobs — rebuttal aimed at the right team.

## Do It Yourself
1. Take the moot "That sport leads to violence". Write one argument for each side.
2. In a group of six, run a three-minute mini-debate in the full speaking order, thirty seconds each.
3. Read any debate transcript and label each quotation: speaker, side, and what it answers.
