# Problem-Solving Strategies and Games

## Do it first
Here is a problem: your boat is sinking, and ten things can be saved — but only five fit in the lifeboat. Rank them. Most people grab and argue; problem solvers do something else first. Before you read on, write down what you think a *problem* actually is — your own definition, in one line. The book is about to replace it.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a problem is — and five tips
Uruguay's Grade 11 book begins by giving the word **problem** a new definition — not a disaster, not a wall: a situation that stands between where you are and where you want to be, which your own actions can change. Then it teaches **five tips for successful problem solving**:

1. **Ask: can you change it?** Some problems are yours to move; some are weather. Spend yourself on the first kind.
2. **Be calm.** A racing mind sees one door; a calm mind sees the room.
3. **See the big picture.** The whole situation, not only the corner that hurts.
4. **Ask for help wisely.** The right person, the right question.
5. **Try many solutions.** Not one way ten times — ten ways once each, then judge.

[Picture 0002]
*Difficulty: One definition and five tips; each tip is a habit, not a fact.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Problem-solving games
Then the book trains the strategies through **eight games**: **describing a picture** for others to draw, sight unseen; **guiding a blindfolded partner** with words alone; a **circle thinking map** that grows a problem's ideas outward; **storytelling from a photo**; the **sinking-boat survival ranking** — the game from this lesson's opening; the **Switcheroo** solution chain, where each new problem is handed on; a **pyramid of importance** for ranking what matters; and a **hot-air-balloon compass** for choosing a direction. Each game is a strategy wearing a costume: communication, guidance, mapping, ranking, chaining.

[Picture 0003]
*Difficulty: Eight games to know by name and purpose; playing them is where the strategies stick.*

## Say it to your companion
Say the five tips in order: can you change it — be calm — see the big picture — ask for help wisely — try many solutions.

## Do It Yourself
1. Apply the five tips to the sinking-boat ranking from the opening: rank the ten things again, tip by tip, and notice what changes.
2. Play two of the eight games with a partner and write one line on the strategy each game trained.
