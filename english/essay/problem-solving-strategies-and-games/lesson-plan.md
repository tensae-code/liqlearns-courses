# Lesson plan — Problem-Solving Strategies and Games

**Course:** English (Zero to Story)
**Title:** Problem-Solving Strategies and Games
**Lesson:** problem-solving-strategies-and-games — a new definition of a problem, five tips for solving one, and the eight games that train them — from Uruguay's Grade 11 book
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 42 — Reading the World and Thinking (International Baccalaureate)

**The one thing:** define a problem the book's way, use the five tips on a real problem, and play the games that train each strategy.

**This lesson's own level:** built from two sub lessons that open in order.

## Why this lesson exists
Uruguay's Grade 11 English book devotes a full reading to problem solving itself: a new definition of the word, five tips, and eight games that train the strategies. The course held thinking lessons around it — checking facts, making decisions — but nothing on solving problems as a skill of its own — so this lesson is built, from Uruguay's book alone.

## Sub lessons
1. **Sub lesson 1 — What a problem is — and five tips.** Uruguay's Grade 11 book gives the word problem a new definition and teaches five tips for solving one well: ask if you can change it, be calm, see the big picture, ask for help wisely, and try many solutions.
2. **Sub lesson 2 — Problem-solving games.** Eight games train the strategies: describing a picture for others to draw, guiding a blindfolded partner, a circle thinking map, storytelling from a photo, a sinking-boat survival ranking, the Switcheroo solution chain, a pyramid of importance, and a hot-air-balloon compass.

Sub lesson 2 opens only when the prove-it check on sub lesson 1 is passed.
Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
Nothing is needed first. Making decisions sits nearby — a solved problem often ends in a decision — and can be tried first as a soft hint, never a gate.

## What the learner can do when it ends
Define a problem in the book's terms, apply the five tips to a real problem in order, and play at least three of the eight games knowing which strategy each one trains.

## What you need
A partner or a group for the games; alone, the describing and ranking games still work against the clock.
