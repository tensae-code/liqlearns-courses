# Asking for Advice

## Sub lesson 1 — Asking — the letter that asks

An advice-asking letter has a clear shape.

1. Say who you are, briefly.
2. Tell the problem — what happened, and how it makes you feel. Keep to the facts a helper needs.
3. Ask for the advice directly: *What should I do? Can you help me?*
4. Thank the reader and sign off.

You can write it to an advice column (a magazine or website page that answers readers' problems) or to a person you trust. Short and clear beats long and tangled: the helper must understand the problem from your letter alone.

Do it: plan a letter about this problem: "My best friend is angry with me and I don't know why." Write the sentence that tells the problem and the sentence that asks.

## Sub lesson 2 — Giving advice back

The answering letter gives advice that is kind first and useful second.

- Open with understanding: *I am sorry you feel this way. Many people have this problem.*
- Give the advice with the advice words: **You should…**, **Why don't you…?**, **If I were you, I would…**, **Try to…**
- Give reasons, not orders — one or two clear steps beat ten.
- Close with hope: *I hope this helps. Good luck!*

Do it: answer the letter from sub lesson 1 in four sentences — understanding, two advice steps, and a hopeful close.

## Say it to your companion

Read the key sentences aloud and explain them to your companion in your own words — what the thing is, and one example of your own.

## Do It Yourself

Close the lesson and use the thing once in real life today — say it, write it, or notice it — then say how it went in one sentence.
