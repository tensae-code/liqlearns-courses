# Hands-on — Process writing

You do it, we guide you. The test of this writing is not reading — it is DOING.

1. **The stranger test (the books' own discipline).** Write a procedure for a task you know cold — making tea, washing hands well, planting a seed. Then give the page, and only the page, to a companion. They may do ONLY what the page says, exactly when it says it. Every place they stop, guess or ask is a fault in the writing, not in them. Mark each stop on the page.
2. **Repair on the frame.** Rewrite the procedure on this TARGET frame — copy the headings, never translate them: **Goal** (one line: what will be made or done) / **You need** (every material or ingredient, with amounts) / **Steps** (numbered; one action each; the verb bare at the front; a sequence word opening at least the first, middle and last steps).
3. **From pictures to process.** Take any ordered picture set or infographic (a jeans-recycling diagram, a hand-washing poster). Write the process it shows, Malaysia-style: one sentence per picture, ordering words carrying the turns, present passive where the thing — not the person — does the work ("the jeans are cut…").
4. **Directions in prose.** Barbados' task, at home: write the way from your home to one real place (school, market, a friend's house) as directions in prose — no map, words only — then walk a companion through it from the page.
