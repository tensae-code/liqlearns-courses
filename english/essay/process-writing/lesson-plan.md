# Lesson plan — Process writing

**Course:** English · **Title:** Essay · **Lesson:** Process writing
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 36 — "Writing the Facts"
(CURRICULUM.md; CATCH-UP-PLAN.md Levels 36–37).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the procedure/process text — writing that makes something happen in the right order: a goal, the things needed, and steps in imperatives joined by sequence words; and a map of the text kinds it belongs to (procedure, descriptive, recount, report).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on ordering words (First/Then/Finally) and on Report writing in the same level — the report says what was found; the procedure says what to DO. It is the third lesson of Unit 36.
**What the learner can do at the end:**
- write a procedure whole: goal, materials/ingredients, ordered steps
- write steps as imperatives a stranger can follow without you standing there
- join steps with sequence words (first, then, next, after that, finally) and keep one action per step
- name the four text kinds — procedure, descriptive, recount, report — and say what each one is FOR

   goal, materials, steps — with steps written as bare imperatives.
   (Was depth Level 1.)

2. **Process writing — the hinges** — steps joined with sequence
   words, one action per step, and instructions a stranger can
   follow. (Was depth Level 2.)

3. **Process writing — the four doors** — procedure, descriptive,
   recount and report named, each with its job, and a procedure that
   passes the stranger test. (Was depth Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: First, then, finally; Report writing. Recommended, never forced.
- A recount tells what DID happen; a procedure tells what TO DO so it happens. Same kitchen, different tense of the mind — keep them apart.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a recipe card split into three stacked parts labelled GOAL, INGREDIENTS, STEPS.** Written slot: "a picture of a procedure card with its three parts showing." What the student does: the student points at each part and says what would go wrong without it — no steps without a goal is a walk without a door.
2. **Slot 2 — a GIF (1–2 seconds) of hands washing step by step, a word flashing over each step: FIRST — THEN — NEXT — FINALLY.** Written slot: "a picture of hands being washed, one sequence word per step." What the student does: the student says the sequence words aloud with the hands — the words are the steps' hinges.
3. **Slot 3 — a picture of four doors in a row, labelled PROCEDURE, DESCRIPTIVE, RECOUNT, REPORT.** Written slot: "a picture of four doors, one for each text kind." What the student does: the student picks the door for 'how to plant beans' and the door for 'what I did yesterday' — kind first, writing second.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first** (lesson/lesson.md): instructions for making tea written as one tangled paragraph, then the same instructions as a procedure; only one of them can be cooked from.
2. **The shape**: goal — materials/ingredients — steps (Indonesia, Grade 7, Ch.2 procedure texts).
3. **The step voice**: imperatives — the verb bare at the front (Place…, Soak…, Drain…); one action per step.
4. **The hinges**: sequence words and ordering words; writing a process from an infographic with ordering words (Malaysia, Year 6, Unit 6).
5. **Instructions that work**: Nepal's instruction writing — hand-washing matched, teeth-brushing written (Grade 7, Unit 19); Barbados' directions in prose — home to school, how to make a cake (syllabus evidence).
6. **The four doors**: Indonesia's named genres — procedure, descriptive, recount, report — each kind with its own skeleton and job.
7. **Do it yourself**: a procedure for a real task, tested by a reader who may only do what the page says.

## If the learner is stuck
- Steps a reader cannot follow → the stranger test: hand the page to someone who has never done the task. Every question they ask is a missing step or a missing word. Add it; never explain aloud instead.
- Two actions hiding in one step → split at 'and'. One step, one action, one verb at the front.
- Steps in the wrong order → number them before writing sentences. The numbers are the skeleton; the prose is only the skin.
- This lesson's next door: Forms, minutes and questionnaires (same level) — the shortest factual writings: boxes, records and questions that gather facts.
