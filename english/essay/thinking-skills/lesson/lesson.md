# Thinking Skills

## Do it first

Your class must plan a building that does not harm the earth. Everyone shouts different ideas at once — nothing gets decided. This lesson teaches the tools that slow thinking down and point it one way at a time.

## Sub lesson 1 — The Six Thinking Hats

Grade 10 teaches thinking as a skill with tools. The first tool is the **Six Thinking Hats**: six hats for six ways of thinking. A group wears the **same hat at the same time**, so all minds pull in one direction — facts together, feelings together, dangers together, ideas together — instead of arguing in six directions at once.

The book works the tool on a real problem: **green building** — how to build without harming the earth.

*Difficulty: One problem, six directions — taken one at a time, on purpose.*

## Sub lesson 2 — Thinking Maps

The second tool draws thinking on paper. **Thinking Maps**: a **circle map** collects everything you know around a centre word; a **bubble map** describes a thing with describing words in bubbles around it; a **double-bubble map** sets two things side by side to compare them — alike in the middle, different at the edges.

The book applies the maps to the same green-building work: see the problem whole before you solve it.

*Difficulty: Three map shapes — gather, describe, compare.*

## Say it to your companion

Your companion names a problem. You say which hat the group should wear first, and why. Then swap with a new problem.

## Do It Yourself

1. Draw a circle map around “green building” with everything you know about it.
2. Draw a double-bubble map comparing a green building and an ordinary building.
3. Write which hat you would wear to list the dangers of a new idea, and which to invent ideas.
