# Making Decisions

## Do it first
Think of the last big decision you made — the one that changed where you are now. Did you really *decide* it, step by step? Or did it just happen to you while you weren't looking? Uruguay's Grade 12 book starts its whole course with exactly this question. Sort your decision before you read on: did you choose it, grab it, or drift into it?

[Picture 0001]

## Sub lesson 1 — Three kinds of decision
The book reads the account of a Fulbright teaching assistant whose chain of decisions brought her to Uruguay — every step a choice that opened the next. Then it names the three kinds of decision every person makes.

**No decision** — you let the moment pass, and the choice is made for you by time, by others, by accident. **Snap decision** — you choose in a flash, without weighing anything; sometimes right, sometimes wreckage. **Responsible decision** — you stop, think, weigh, and choose on purpose.

Most lives hold all three every week. The book's work is learning to see which kind you are making *while* you make it.

[Picture 0002]
*Difficulty: Three kinds, told apart by one measure: how much thought went in before the choice.*

## Sub lesson 2 — The five steps of a responsible decision
A learner in the book named Bryn lays out **five steps of responsible decision-making** — a path from seeing the decision in front of you, through gathering what you know and weighing where each choice leads, to choosing and standing by the choice. The class takes the steps one by one and then does the real work: each learner applies all five to **the last big decision they made**, and checks where their own decision followed the steps — and where it skipped.

[Picture 0003]
*Difficulty: The steps are easy to say; walking a real, personal decision through all five is the test.*

## Say it to your companion
Name the three kinds of decision aloud: no decision, snap decision, responsible decision. Then say which kind your last big decision was — and be honest.

## Do It Yourself
1. Sort five decisions from your week into the three kinds, in writing.
2. Write your last big decision at the top of a page and walk it through Bryn's five steps, one line per step. Mark the step you skipped.
