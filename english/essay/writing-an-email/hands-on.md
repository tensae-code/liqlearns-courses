# Hands-On — Writing an Email

Things you do with a real email frame. You need paper or a computer, and a partner.

1. **Build the frame.** Draw an empty email on paper: a line for the address, a line for the subject, a big space for the body, a line for the closing, a line for the signature. Say the five parts out loud, in order, as you draw each one.
2. **Fix the floating words.** Your partner writes a message body alone, with no frame. Read it and say what is missing: who is it for? What is it about? Who sent it? Now build the missing four parts together.
3. **Subject swap.** Take one body — "I cannot come to practice on Saturday because my grandmother is visiting." Write three different subjects for it: one clear, one vague ("Hello"), one wrong. Your partner reads each subject only, and says which email they would open first, and why.
4. **The cover test.** Write one full email. Cover the body: can your partner tell from the address and subject who it is for and what it is about? Cover the top: can they tell from the closing and signature who sent it? Fix any part that fails its test.
