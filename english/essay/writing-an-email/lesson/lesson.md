# Writing an Email

## Do it first

Look at a message with nothing around it: just the words "The meeting is moved to Friday." Who is it for? What is it about, in one line? Who sent it? You cannot tell — the words float alone. An email never floats: it is built inside a fixed frame of five parts, and the frame does half the work before the message even starts. This lesson teaches that frame.

## Sub lesson 1 — The address and the subject

*Difficulty: first step — where the email goes, and what it says it is.*

Every email begins with the **recipient address**: the address of the person the email is going to. It stands at the very top, because nothing else can happen until the email knows where it is going. One wrong letter in the address and the email goes to the wrong person — or nowhere.

Under the address comes the **subject**: one short line that says what the email is about. The subject is read before the email is opened — it is how the reader decides what the email is, and often whether to open it now or later. A clear subject names the thing itself: "Football practice on Saturday", not "Hello". The address says **who**; the subject says **what**.

Try it now: you must email your teacher that you will be absent tomorrow. Write only the first two parts — the address (use a made-up one) and the subject. If your subject could belong to any email in the world, write it again until it could belong only to yours.

## Sub lesson 2 — The body, the closing and the signature

*Difficulty: builds on Sub lesson 1 — the message itself, and how an email ends.*

The middle of the email is the **message body**: the message itself, the words you actually came to say. The body sits between the subject above it and the closing below it — the subject promises, the body delivers. Keep the body to the thing the subject named.

An email does not stop dead at the end of the body. It closes: the **closing** is the short ending line that rounds the message off, and under it stands the **signature** — the sender's name, signing the email the way a letter is signed. The closing and the signature answer the question the floating words at the start of this lesson could not: **who sent this**.

So the whole frame, in its fixed order: **recipient address, subject, message body, closing, signature**. Five parts, every time.

Try it now: take the email you started in Sub lesson 1 and finish it — a body of two or three sentences saying you will be absent and why, then a closing, then your name as the signature. Read the five parts top to bottom and check each one is doing its own job.

## Do it yourself

Write a complete email of your own, for a real reason you choose: asking a friend for something, telling a teacher something, writing to a club or a shop. Build it in the fixed order — the recipient address, a subject that names the thing itself, a body that keeps to the subject, a closing, and your signature. Before you finish, cover the body with your hand: the address and subject alone should tell the reader who the email is for and what it is about. Then cover the top: the closing and signature alone should tell the reader who sent it. If both tests pass, your email stands on its own frame.
