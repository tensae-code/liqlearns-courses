# Hands-on — Play scripts

A real conversion task — story in, script out.

## The scene (invented for this lesson)
"The market was closing when old Baba found the purse
under his stall. He picked it up slowly and called to
the girl who had bought tomatoes. 'You left something!'
he shouted. The girl ran back, afraid. 'It is not mine,'
she said. 'Mine has a blue string.' Baba laughed and
opened his hand. Inside the purse lay a small key."

## Task — turn it into a script
First sketch the scenario (Ghana's order): place, people,
the one problem. Then write the script on this frame:
SETTING LINE: (a market stall, evening — one line)
BABA: [stage direction for picking up the purse] …
GIRL: …
Rules:
- Every sentence of the story must land somewhere: a
  spoken line, a stage direction, or the setting line.
  Check them off one by one — nothing may vanish.
- "He called / she said / Baba laughed" are NOT lines:
  the calling and laughing become directions or disappear
  into how the line is marked.
- End with one direction that shows the key — the scene's
  last picture belongs to the bodies, not the words.
Then swap scripts with a companion and READ them aloud,
one voice each. Any line that cannot be spoken, or any
moment the reader must guess, is a repair waiting.
