# Lesson plan — Play scripts

**Course:** English · **Title:** Essay · **Lesson:** Play scripts
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 38 — Speeches and Shows.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the play script's
form — speaker names, one dialogue line per turn, stage
directions that tell bodies what words cannot — and how
to turn a story scene into that form.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on dialogue
punctuation and the story's scene; its new work is the
LAYOUT that lets other people perform your words.
**What the learner can do at the end:** lay out a script
page correctly, write stage directions in brackets, and
convert a prose scene into a performable script without
losing anything the scene means.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees
from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before
it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons
(ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Play Script Layout** — the script's layout — speaker names at
   the left, a colon, a new line for each speaker, directions in
   brackets. (Was depth Level 1.)
2. **Stage Directions** — stage directions — instructions an actor
   can obey: entrances, movements, feelings shown by the body, kept
   out of the spoken lines. (Was depth Level 2.)
3. **Turning a Story into a Script** — the conversion — turning a
   story scene into a script, and reading a whole play, losing
   nothing: narration becomes direction or dialogue. (Was depth
   Level 3.)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is
written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the
gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Speech marks (direct speech).
  Recommended, never forced.

## Media slots
1. **Slot 1 — a picture of a script page beside the same
   scene written as a story paragraph.** What the student
   does: the student points at where the narrator's
   sentences went in the script version.
2. **Slot 2 — a picture of two actors on a bare stage,
   one entering with a basket.** What the student does:
   the student writes the stage direction for that
   entrance in brackets.

## The run of the lesson, in order
1. **Do it first**: a story scene and its script twin —
   what did the prose lose, and where did it go?
2. **The layout** (Ghana B8's dialogue rules; Malaysia's
   pupil's-book script lessons).
3. **Stage directions**: who moves, where, how — in
   brackets, in the present tense.
4. **The conversion**: narrator sentences become
   directions or dialogue; nothing else may vanish.
5. **Do it yourself**: the hands-on scene conversion.

## If the learner is stuck
- Script reads like a story: every "he said angrily"
  must split — the words into the line, the angrily into
  a direction.
- Directions swallowing the play: a direction earns its
  place only if an actor must DO it to be understood.
