# Lesson plan — Energy — sources, and using energy wisely

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Motion, Force and Energy · **Lesson:** Energy — sources, and using energy wisely
**Place in the curriculum:** Motion, Force and Energy — Unit 7, the energy strand closes (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** energy sources split into non-renewable (finite) and renewable (endless) — the guide's keyed list runs to ten sources (teacher evidence) — and because the finite kind carries our dependence, conserving energy is a household skill with a concrete strategy list (teacher evidence)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Energy — forms and conversions: forms convert; now where energy comes from, and how not to waste it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **Sources — finite and endless** — the student book's renewable/non-renewable split, widened by the guide's ten-source list (teacher evidence)
2. **Conserving energy at home** — the student book's saving examples widened by the guide's concrete home and office strategy list (teacher evidence)

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Energy — forms and conversions (this title). Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Sort sources into renewable and non-renewable and defend the sort, including the book's own listing of wood among the finite.
2. Name the guide's ten sources and its ocean-energy family (teacher evidence).
3. Apply the guide's home conservation strategies: lids, taps, lights, soaking, right-sized appliances, shared rides.

## What you need
The home itself as the audit ground: its lights, taps, pots and appliances.

## If the learner is stuck
- Calls wood plainly renewable because trees regrow → the student book lists wood among its non-renewable examples; teach the book's sort and name the tension honestly in talk, not by rewriting the book.
- Lists 'saving money' as the strategy → the strategies are actions: lid on, tap off, soak first, ride together.
