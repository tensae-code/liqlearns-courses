# Energy — sources, and using energy wisely

## Do it first
Think of this morning's breakfast. What burned, what flowed, what shone to make it? List every helper. Now sort your list into two piles: helpers that will one day run out, and helpers that will not. You have just built this lesson's first idea with your own kitchen.

## Sub lesson 1 — Sources: finite and endless
An **energy source** stores energy we can use — petrol in a tank, food in a body. **Non-renewable sources are finite**: fossil fuels and nuclear fuels — and wood, as the book lists it — are used up and gone. **Renewable sources are endless**: the **sun, wind and water** return every day, quickly and dependably replacing themselves. Teachers' materials widen the list to **ten sources** (teacher guide — evidence): solar, wind, geothermal, hydrogen, tidal, wave, hydroelectric, biomass, nuclear and fossil fuels — with **ocean energy** named as its own family: wave, tidal, current and thermal.

## Sub lesson 2 — Conserving energy at home
Our dependence on the finite kind is heavy, so **conservation** — using energy wisely — matters at home and at school. The student book's examples: soak uniforms overnight to save washing effort; choose efficient bulbs and heaters. The teachers' strategy list (teacher guide — evidence) goes further, room by room: switch off lights, fans and appliances when unused; never leave taps running; cook vegetables covered, with only the water needed; soak pulses before cooking; choose efficient appliances sized to the need, insulate the hot-water boiler, clean furnace filters; and travel together — public transport and shared rides instead of driving alone.

## Say it to your companion
Sort the ten sources into the two columns, name three conservation strategies, and say why conservation matters more the heavier our dependence on the finite kind grows.

## Do It Yourself
1. Sort: coal, river water, sunlight, firewood (as the book lists it), wind, natural gas. Name each column.
2. Audit one evening at home: count lights left on in empty rooms and taps left running. Write the waste and the fix.
3. A family cooks pulses three times a week unsoaked. Which strategy applies, and what does it save?
