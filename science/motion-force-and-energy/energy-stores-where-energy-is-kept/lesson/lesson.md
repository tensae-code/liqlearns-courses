# Energy stores — where energy is kept

## Do it first
Think of a torch that has sat dark in a drawer for a month. The moment you switch it on, light pours out. Where was that light's energy all that month — and where is the energy in a lump of coal, or in your lunch, before it is used? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Greece's way in — its Grade 5 book gives the keeping-place of energy a chapter of its own.

## Sub lesson 1 — Where energy is kept — fuels, foods, batteries and other stores

Energy is not always on the move. Very often it is **kept** — held in a **store** until the moment it is used. Greece's Grade 5 book names the stores plainly: **fuels** keep energy until they burn, **foods** keep energy until the body draws on them, and **batteries** and other stores keep energy until a machine calls for it. A store is a keeping-place: the energy sits there, unchanged in amount, waiting to be released into work, heat, light or movement.

## Sub lesson 2 — Stored energy waiting to be released — the experiments

The workbook makes the store something you can test: its experiments work on **stored energy waiting to be released** — energy held in a store, then let out and put to use. That is the store's whole character: what is kept can be released, and what is released was first kept. First the keeping, then the release — Greece teaches the two in that order, the pupil book's chapter and then the workbook's experiments on it.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Where is a battery's energy before you switch the torch on?" Answer: Kept in the battery — the battery is an energy store: the energy sits there until it is used, like the energy kept in fuels and in foods.
