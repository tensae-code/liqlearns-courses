# Energy — kinetic and potential energy

## Do it first
Roll a ball slowly into a stack of light boxes — it nudges one. Roll it fast — boxes scatter. Same ball. Then lift the ball onto a low shelf, and onto a high one. From which shelf could its fall do more? You are already judging the thing this lesson measures: the ability to cause change.

## Energy
**Energy is the ability to cause change** — to light a room, move a bicycle, lift a book. We know it by the changes it makes, it converts between forms (the next lesson), and it is measured in **joules (J)**.

## Sub lesson 1 — Kinetic energy: the energy of moving
A moving body carries **kinetic energy**: **KE = ½mv²** — half the mass times the speed squared. Worked: a **4 kg** body at **4 m/s** holds KE = ½ × 4 × 4² = ½ × 4 × 16 = **32 J**. Read the formula's warning: the speed is **squared**. Double the mass and KE doubles; double the speed and KE grows **four** times. Speed is the dangerous factor, and the formula says so.

## Sub lesson 2 — Potential energy: the energy of position
A raised body stores **potential energy**: **PE = mgh** — mass times g times the height. The formula runs both ways. Worked backwards: a body holding **40 J** with a mass of **2 kg** — h = PE ÷ (mg) = 40 ÷ (2 × 9.8) ≈ **2 m**. It was raised about two metres; from there, its fall can give the 40 J back as change.

## Say it to your companion
Define energy and its unit, state both formulas, work the 32 J example, and run the 40 J example backwards to the height.

## Do It Yourself
1. Compute the KE of a 2 kg ball at 3 m/s. (Answer to check: 9 J.)
2. Compute the PE of a 5 kg mass raised 3 m. (Answer to check: 147 J.)
3. A ball's speed doubles while its mass halves. What happens to its KE? Work it with numbers and say the rule.
