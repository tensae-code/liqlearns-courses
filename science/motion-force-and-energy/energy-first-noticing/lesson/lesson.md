# Energy — first noticing

## Do it first
Before you read: look around the room you are in. Name one thing that shines, one thing that is warm, one thing that makes a sound, and one thing that moves. Say your answers out loud. Then read on: this is Zambia's way in — its Grade 1 Mathematics and Science module meets energy first as a noticing topic, inside its unit on materials.

## Sub lesson 1 — Noticing energy around us

Stand still and notice.

The **sun warms** your skin. A **lamp shines**. A **drum sounds** when it is hit. A **ball rolls** across the floor. In every one of these, something is at work — making light, making warmth, making sound, making movement.

That something has a name: **energy**.

Energy is not a thing you can hold in your hand like a stone. You **notice it by what it does**: where something shines, warms, sounds or moves, energy is at work. That noticing — before any lists of kinds, before any measuring — is the first step, and it is the whole of this lesson.

## Sub lesson 2 — Energy in the materials around us

Zambia's module meets energy inside its **materials** unit — among the things learners handle every day. So turn the noticing onto the materials themselves.

Some things around us **show energy at work**: a torch gives light, a cooking fire gives warmth, a bell gives sound, a bicycle moves. Other things **sit still and quiet** — a cup, a stone, a spoon — until something acts on them: hands lift the cup, a foot kicks the stone.

Sorting the things around you this way — *showing energy at work* or *waiting still and quiet* — is the Grade 1 way into the idea. The kinds of energy, where energy comes from, and how it changes from one kind to another are later lessons; they all stand on this first noticing.

## The key ideas

- **Energy** is first noticed by what it does: shining, warming, sounding, moving.
- Energy is not held in the hand like a thing — it is **noticed at work** in the things around us.
- The things around us sort into those **showing energy at work** and those **sitting still and quiet** until something acts on them.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — noticing energy first, then the sorting of the things around us. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "How do you know energy is there if you cannot hold it?" Answer with the noticing, not by repeating.

## Do It Yourself
1. Notice and list: write four things you can see right now — one that shines, one that warms, one that sounds, one that moves.
2. Sort five things at home into the two piles: showing energy at work, or sitting still and quiet. Write the two lists.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences — what energy is, as a first noticing, and how you know it is there.
