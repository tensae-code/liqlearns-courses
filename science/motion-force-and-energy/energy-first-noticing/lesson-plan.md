# Lesson plan — Energy — first noticing

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Motion, Force and Energy · **Lesson:** Energy — first noticing
**Place in the curriculum:** Motion, Force and Energy — appended to Unit 20 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** energy, noticed for the first time — before forms, sources or any measuring, the young learner simply notices energy at work in the things around them: things that shine, things that warm, things that sound, things that move — Zambia's own way, in its Grade 1 Mathematics and Science module, is to meet energy as a first noticing topic inside a materials unit
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Noticing energy around us** — Energy is first met by noticing: the sun warming, a lamp shining, a drum sounding, a ball rolling — something is at work in each one, and that something is what the word energy names.
2. **Energy in the materials around us** — Inside a materials unit, the noticing turns to the things in the learner's hands and room: which of them give light, warmth, sound or movement, and which sit still and quiet until something acts on them.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: see the lesson's own opening. Recommended, never forced.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Thinks energy is only electricity → return to the noticing: the sun's warmth, a drum's sound and a rolling ball are energy at work too — electricity is only one of the ways energy shows itself, and the forms themselves are a later lesson.
