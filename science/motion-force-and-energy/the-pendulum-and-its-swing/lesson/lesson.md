# The pendulum and its swing

## Do it first
Before you read: tie a small stone to a string and let it hang still. Now pull it to one side and let go — do not push it. What does it do? Does it stop at the bottom, or does it climb the other side? And does each swing take the same time as the one before? Say your answers out loud. Then read on: this is Cabo Verde's way in — its Grade 3 Ciências Integradas book puts a pendulum experiment among the closing experiences of its science unit, for the child to build and try.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a pendulum is

A **pendulum** is a weight — the **bob** — hanging from a string or rod, free to swing.

Pull the bob to one side and let go. It does not fall straight down and stop. It swings **down through the lowest point, up the other side, back down, and up the first side again** — back and forth, over and over, passing its hanging-still place in the middle of every swing.

One complete journey — from one side, across, and back to where it started — is **one swing** (one oscillation). The swing is the pendulum's own motion: the same road travelled again and again.

## Sub lesson 2 — The pendulum experiment — swinging, counting, timing

The book's experiment is the real test of the idea:

1. **Build it** — tie the bob firmly to the string and fix the string's top end so only the bob can move.
2. **Start it fairly** — pull the bob to one side and **let go without pushing**: a push would add your own force and spoil the test.
3. **Count the swings** — count one each time the bob comes back to the side you released it from.
4. **Time them** — time how long 10 swings take. Swing after swing, the pendulum keeps a steady beat — that is why pendulums were used to keep time in clocks.
5. **Watch the end** — the swings slowly get **smaller and smaller** until the bob hangs still again: the swing is not endless, it fades.

## The key ideas

- A **pendulum** is a weight (the **bob**) hanging from a string, free to swing back and forth through its lowest point.
- One complete back-and-forth journey is **one swing**.
- Start a pendulum fairly: pull it aside and **let go without pushing**.
- Counted and timed, the pendulum swings with a **steady beat** — and its swings slowly **shrink** until it stops.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — what a pendulum is first, then the experiment. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Why must you let go without pushing?" Answer: a push adds your own force and spoils the test — the swing must be the pendulum's own.
