# Lesson plan — The pendulum and its swing

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Motion, Force and Energy · **Lesson:** The pendulum and its swing
**Place in the curriculum:** Motion, Force and Energy — appended to Unit 20 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a pendulum — a weight hanging from a string — swings back and forth through its lowest point in a steady beat, and the swing is tested by building one, letting go without pushing, and counting and timing the swings — Cabo Verde's own way, in its Grade 3 Ciências Integradas book, is to meet the pendulum as a hands-on experiment among the closing experiences of the science unit
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **What a pendulum is** — A weight (the bob) hanging from a string, swinging down through the lowest point and up the other side, back and forth; one complete back-and-forth journey is one swing.
2. **The pendulum experiment — swinging, counting, timing** — Build it, let go without pushing, count the swings and time them; the beat is steady, and the swings slowly shrink until the bob stops.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: `motion-force-and-energy/motion-four-kinds-of-motion` — the kinds of motion by path; the pendulum's swing is the back-and-forth kind. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain sub lesson 1 in their own words: What a pendulum is.

3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. For any practical part: string and a small heavy object (a stone or a metal washer) to tie as the bob, and a clock or phone timer, where the hands-on says so.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Counts a half-journey as a swing → walk one full swing with a finger: from the release side, across, and back to the release side — only then is one swing done.
