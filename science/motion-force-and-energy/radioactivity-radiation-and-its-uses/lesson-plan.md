# Lesson plan — Radioactivity — radiation and its uses

**Course:** Science · **Title:** motion-force-and-energy · **Lesson:** Radioactivity — radiation and its uses
**Place in the curriculum:** Motion, Force and Energy — appended to Unit 20 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** radioactivity and nuclear energy — how nuclear energy is produced by fission and fusion, where it is used, Malaysia's own research reactor, and the impacts weighed in the debate over it
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

## Sub lessons
1. **Nuclear energy — fission and fusion** — Malaysia's Form 4 book teaches that **more than 30 countries use nuclear energy, mainly to generate electricity**, its benefits weighed against other sources. The energy is produced two ways: **nuclear fission** — a heavy nucleus bombarded and splitting into two smaller, more stable nuclei — and **nuclear fusion** — two light nuclei (deuterium and tritium) combining into a heavier nucleus, releasing energy.
2. **Malaysia's reactor, the impacts, and the debate** — Malaysia's own reactor is the **1 MW TRIGA PUSPATI Reactor** of the Malaysian Nuclear Agency, operating since **28 June 1982, for research only**. The book also carries the impacts: the **Hiroshima and Nagasaki bombings (6 and 9 August 1945)**, nuclear tests and their effects. It closes with a **debate: justifications for and against a nuclear power station in Malaysia**.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Mixes fission and fusion up → return to sub lesson 1: fission splits a heavy nucleus into two smaller ones; fusion joins two light nuclei into a heavier one.
- Thinks Malaysia runs a nuclear power station → return to sub lesson 2: the TRIGA PUSPATI reactor is 1 MW and for research only, since 1982.
