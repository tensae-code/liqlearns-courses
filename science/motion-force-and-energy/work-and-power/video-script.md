Today: **Work — and power** — Papua New Guinea's way, from its Department of Education science books (see lesson/sources.md for the exact books and pages).

Papua New Guinea's Standards Based Grade 8 guide opens its Work and Power lessons with the physics meaning of **work**: work is done when a force moves something through a distance — the relationship between **force, work and distance** is the lesson's core, and pushing without moving does no work in this sense, however tiring it feels.

The guide names the **units of force and of work**, then calculates: work is found from the force and the distance it moves through, in the force's direction. Force and distance are the two numbers every work calculation needs.

The run closes with **power**: what power is — how fast work is done — and the **units of power**. Two people can do the same work; the one who does it faster has the greater power. Work prices the job; power prices the pace.
