FULL SCRIPT — the cart experiment and its graph, as placed (walkthrough).

[Opening — the set-up on the bench]
Here is the bench. A cart on a smooth track. A string tied to the cart, over a pulley at the track's end, and hanging weights below. The hanging weights pull. That pull is our force.

[Step 1 — one weight]
Hang one weight. Let go. The cart starts slow and speeds up along the track. We time it over the measured length and work out its acceleration. Write it down: this force, this acceleration.

[Step 2 — two weights, then three]
Hang two weights. The pull is doubled. Let go — the cart runs away faster. Its acceleration has grown. Three weights: faster still. Each time, the same cart, the same mass — only the force changed.

[Step 3 — the graph]
Now plot it. Force along the bottom, acceleration up the side. The points climb in a straight line through the start. A straight rising line is proportion speaking: double the force, double the acceleration.

[Step 4 — add mass to the cart]
Load the cart heavier and pull with the first force again. The cart answers more slowly. Same force, more mass, less acceleration. Mass divides what force builds.

[Close — the law]
Newton's second law: acceleration is proportional to the net force, inversely proportional to the mass, and in the force's direction. a = F ÷ m. Twenty newtons on five kilograms gives four metres per second squared. The graph said it first — the law just writes it down.
