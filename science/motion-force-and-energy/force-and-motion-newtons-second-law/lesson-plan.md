# Lesson plan — Force and motion — Newton's second law

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Motion, Force and Energy · **Lesson:** Force and motion — Newton's second law
**Place in the curriculum:** Motion, Force and Energy — Unit 7, the laws of motion (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Newton's second law, read off the cart experiment — acceleration is proportional to the net force, inversely proportional to the mass, and in the force's direction: a = F/m
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Newton's first law: the net-force condition is stated; now the net force's effect is measured.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved:

1. **The cart experiment** — hanging weights pull the cart; acceleration is measured as the force grows, and force is graphed against acceleration
2. **The law — a = F/m** — the law read off the graph, stated exactly, and worked both ways

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Run the cart-and-hanging-mass experiment and graph force against acceleration.
2. Read the law off the graph: double the force, double the acceleration; double the mass, halve it.
3. Work F = ma both ways: 20 N on 5 kg gives 4 m/s²; and use it to back out an unknown starting speed.

## What you need
A cart, string, pulley, hanging masses, a measured track and a timer — the experiment's own kit; squared paper.

## If the learner is stuck
- Reads the graph's straight line as 'force equals acceleration' → the line's message is proportion: a grows as F grows, at fixed mass.
- Forgets the mass's direction of effect → more mass at the same force means LESS acceleration; the law divides by m.
