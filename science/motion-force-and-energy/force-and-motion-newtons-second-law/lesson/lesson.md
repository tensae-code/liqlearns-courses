# Force and motion — Newton's second law

## Do it first
A cart stands on a track, tied by a string over a pulley to a hanging weight. Let the weight fall: the cart rolls, speeding up. Now hang two weights. The cart runs away faster. Nothing changed but the pull. How much faster — twice as fast? That question is an experiment, and the experiment is this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — The cart experiment
Hang weights one by one and measure the cart's acceleration each time — same cart, same track, growing force. Then graph it: force along, acceleration up. The points climb in a **straight rising line**: as the force grows, the acceleration grows in the same proportion. Double the force, double the acceleration.

## Sub lesson 2 — The law: a = F/m
**Newton's second law:** the acceleration of a body is **proportional to the net force** on it, **inversely proportional to its mass**, and **in the direction of the force** — **a = F/m**. Worked: a force of **20 N on a mass of 5 kg** gives a = 20 ÷ 5 = **4 m/s²**. The formula also runs backward: from a measured acceleration and a known time, a body's starting speed can be backed out — the law is a tool in both directions.

## Say it to your companion
Tell the cart story, read the graph's message aloud, state the law exactly, and work the 20 N example.

## Do It Yourself
1. A force of 30 N acts on a 6 kg mass. Compute the acceleration. (Answer to check: 5 m/s².)
2. The same 30 N now acts on a 10 kg mass. Compute the new acceleration and say what changed it. (Answer to check: 3 m/s².)
3. A 4 kg body accelerates at 3 m/s². What net force acts on it? (Answer to check: 12 N.)
