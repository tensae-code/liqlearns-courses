# Motion — acceleration

## Do it first
Watch three starts. One runner bursts away faster and faster. One starts fast and eases down to a walk. One jogs at one steady pace the whole way. Three different stories — and all three are stories about velocity changing, or refusing to change. Give each story a sign before you know the word for it.

## Acceleration
**Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity**: **a = Δv ÷ Δt** — the change of velocity divided by the time it took. Its unit is the **metre per second squared (m/s²)**. Worked example: a body goes from rest to **20 m/s in 8 s** — a = 20 ÷ 8 = **2.5 m/s²**.

## The three signs
Speeding up gives **positive** acceleration — its speed–time graph rises. Slowing down (braking) gives **negative** acceleration — the graph falls. Steady velocity gives **zero** acceleration — the graph runs flat. Three runners' tables show exactly these three cases, each with its graph.

## The cornering corner
A car corners at a perfectly steady speed — is it accelerating? Yes. Velocity carries direction, and the direction is changing every moment of the turn. Starting, braking and cornering are all acceleration, because in each of them velocity — size, direction, or both — changes.

## Say it to your companion
State the formula and unit, work the 2.5 m/s² example aloud, and explain the cornering case.

## Do It Yourself
1. A cyclist goes from 5 m/s to 17 m/s in 4 s. Compute the acceleration. (Answer to check: 3 m/s².)
2. A runner's speed–time graph falls steadily to zero. Name the acceleration's sign and what the runner is doing.
3. A bus holds exactly 10 m/s around a roundabout. Is it accelerating? Say why, in one sentence.
