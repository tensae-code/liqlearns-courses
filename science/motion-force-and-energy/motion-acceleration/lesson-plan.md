# Lesson plan — Motion — acceleration

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Motion, Force and Energy · **Lesson:** Motion — acceleration
**Place in the curriculum:** Motion, Force and Energy — Unit 7, closing the motion strand (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** acceleration is the rate of change of velocity — a = Δv/Δt — and it is positive when speeding up, negative when slowing down, and zero at steady velocity, even when the 'change' is only a change of direction
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Motion — displacement and velocity: velocity is defined; now velocity itself is allowed to change.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Compute acceleration from a change of velocity and a time: 0 → 20 m/s in 8 s = 2.5 m/s².
2. Sort three runners' tables into positive, negative and zero acceleration, and match each to its graph.
3. Explain why steady-speed cornering is still acceleration.

## What you need
Squared paper; three runners or three toy cars for the starting-line test.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks acceleration needs a speed-up only → braking is acceleration too, with the sign flipped; cornering at steady speed changes direction, and velocity includes direction.
- Divides by the final velocity instead of the time → the denominator is always the time the change took.
