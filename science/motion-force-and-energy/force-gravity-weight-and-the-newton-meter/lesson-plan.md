# Lesson plan — Force — gravity, weight and the newton meter

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Motion, Force and Energy · **Lesson:** Force — gravity, weight and the newton meter
**Place in the curriculum:** Motion, Force and Energy — Unit 7, the force strand measured (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** weight is gravity's force on a mass — W = mg with g = 9.8 — and force is measured with the newton meter, whose spring stretches in proportion to the force hung on it
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Force — what force is and what it does: forces are sorted; now the greatest non-contact force is weighed and measured.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain gravity pulling at every position of a falling ball, from a low building or a high one alike.
2. Compute weights with W = mg for the practical's masses: 100 g, 200 g, 300 g.
3. Read a newton meter and say why its spring's stretch measures force.

## What you need
A newton meter (spring balance), 100 g, 200 g and 300 g masses, and a notebook for the measure-and-calculate table.

## If the learner is stuck
- Mixes mass and weight → mass (kg) is the body's matter and stays; weight (N) is gravity's pull on that mass and changes where gravity changes.
- Reads the meter while it still bounces → the stretch measures force only at rest; wait for the settle.
