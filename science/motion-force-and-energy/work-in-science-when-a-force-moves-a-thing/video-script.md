Today: **Work in science — when a force moves a thing** — Syria's way, from its Ministry of Education science books.

Syria's Grade 6 book opens with a question from the farm: the farmer's cart is loaded and moving — who does the moving, the farmer or the horse? The lesson's title plays on the everyday word: "I am sincere in my 'work'" — and asks what work means when science uses the word. In science, work is done when a force moves a thing. The horse pulls the cart and the cart moves: the pull does work. The size of the work hangs on the force and on how far the thing moves — a stronger pull, or a longer move, means more work. A force that moves nothing does no work in the science sense, however hard it pushes. Pushing against a cart that will not budge is tiring — the farmer feels it — but science counts work only when the force actually moves the thing. That is the lesson's test for every case: did the thing move?
