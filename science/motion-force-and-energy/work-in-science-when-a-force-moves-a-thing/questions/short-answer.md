# Work in science — when a force moves a thing — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** What does work mean in science, as Syria's book teaches it?
**Answer:** Work is done when a force moves a thing; its size hangs on the force and on how far the thing moves.

**2.** Why does the book open with the farmer's cart and the horse?
**Answer:** To separate the everyday word 'work' from the science idea: the question 'who does the moving?' points straight at the force that moves the thing — the horse's pull.

**3.** A learner pushes a wall all afternoon and is exhausted. Has work been done in the science sense? Why?
**Answer:** No — the wall did not move. Science counts work only when a force actually moves a thing, however tiring the push felt.

**4.** Name one everyday job and say where the work, in the science sense, happens in it.
**Answer:** Any job where a force moves a thing — e.g. carrying water: the lift and carry move the bucket; the work is in the moving, not in standing still holding it.
