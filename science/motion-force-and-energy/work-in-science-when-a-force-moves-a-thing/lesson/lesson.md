# Work in science — when a force moves a thing

## Do it first
Before you read: say out loud what you already think this lesson's one thing is — work as a science idea. Then read on: this is Syria's way in — its Ministry of Education science books teach this thing at the places named in the sources, and this lesson follows their order.

## Sub lesson 1 — Who does the moving? — the farmer's cart

Syria's Grade 6 book opens with a question from the farm: the farmer's cart is loaded and moving — who does the moving, the farmer or the horse? The lesson's title plays on the everyday word: "I am sincere in my 'work'" — and asks what work means when science uses the word.

## Sub lesson 2 — Work — a force that moves a thing

In science, **work** is done when a force moves a thing. The horse pulls the cart and the cart moves: the pull does work. The size of the work hangs on the force and on how far the thing moves — a stronger pull, or a longer move, means more work.

## Sub lesson 3 — No movement, no work

A force that moves nothing does no work in the science sense, however hard it pushes. Pushing against a cart that will not budge is tiring — the farmer feels it — but science counts work only when the force actually moves the thing. That is the lesson's test for every case: did the thing move?
