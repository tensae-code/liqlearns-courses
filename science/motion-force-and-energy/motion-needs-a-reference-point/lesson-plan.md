# Lesson plan — Motion — motion needs a reference point

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Motion, Force and Energy · **Lesson:** Motion — motion needs a reference point
**Place in the curriculum:** Motion, Force and Energy — Unit 7 (Force, Motion and Energy) opens here, Grade 7 depth (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** motion is a change of position against a chosen reference point or frame — the same body can be moving and at rest at once, depending on the frame chosen
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Materials lesson Motion, distance and speed, which first named relative motion at primary depth; here the reference frame is worked properly, with the two-station and bus puzzles.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Motion, distance and speed (Materials). Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what motion and rest mean — change, or no change, of position against a chosen reference point.
2. Judge one passenger four ways — against the ground, the bus, themselves and two traffic lights — and defend each answer.
3. Name the reference frame before judging any motion.

## What you need
Nothing to buy: two chairs as a 'bus', a doorway as a reference point, and the road outside.

## If the learner is stuck
- Says 'he is not moving, he is sitting' → sitting in what, passing what? Against the bus he rests; against the ground he flies. Both answers need their frame named.
- Thinks one answer must be the true one → the puzzle's four answers are all defensible; the frame is what changes, not the facts.
