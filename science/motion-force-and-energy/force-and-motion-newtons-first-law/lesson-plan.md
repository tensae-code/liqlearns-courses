# Lesson plan — Force and motion — Newton's first law

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Motion, Force and Energy · **Lesson:** Force and motion — Newton's first law
**Place in the curriculum:** Motion, Force and Energy — Unit 7, the laws of motion begin (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Newton's first law — a body at rest stays at rest, and a body in motion keeps constant velocity, unless a net external force acts on it; inertia is the name of that keeping-on
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Force — gravity, weight and the newton meter: forces are measured; now their rule over motion is stated, one law at a time.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Force — gravity, weight and the newton meter (this title). Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Perform the cup-and-paper test both ways — slow pull, fast pull — and explain the difference by inertia.
2. State the first law exactly, including its condition: a net external force.
3. Explain the bus lurches — at sudden stops, starts and turns — as passengers' bodies keeping their state.

## What you need
A cup of water and a sheet of hard paper — the experiment's whole kit; a bus ride remembered or imagined for the lurches.

## If the learner is stuck
- Says the fast pull 'left no time for force' loosely → tighten it: friction had too little time to drag the cup; with no net force acting long enough, the cup keeps its state — rest.
- Recites 'no force' instead of 'net force' → balanced forces act on resting books all day; the law's condition is a NET force.
