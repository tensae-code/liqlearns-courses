# Force and motion — Newton's first law

## Do it first
Stand a cup of water on a sheet of hard paper. Pull the paper slowly across the table — the cup rides along. Set it up again. This time whip the paper out fast. The cup stays standing where it was, water and all. Same cup, same paper, same table. What changed?

## Sub lesson 1 — The cup trick and inertia
The slow pull gives friction time to drag the cup along. The fast pull is gone before friction can drag it — so the cup simply **keeps its state**: it was at rest, and at rest it stays. This keeping-on has a name: **inertia**. A body does not start, stop or turn by itself; it holds what it is doing.

## Sub lesson 2 — The law, and the bus
**Newton's first law:** a body at rest stays at rest, and a body in motion keeps moving at constant velocity, **unless a net external force acts on it**. Note the condition carefully — net zero force, not no force: balanced forces act on resting bodies constantly, and the state still holds. Now ride the bus in your mind. The bus brakes suddenly: your body lurches forward — it kept moving while the bus stopped. The bus starts: you press back — your body kept resting while the bus moved. The bus turns: you lean outward — your body kept its straight line. Every lurch is the law, felt in your own body.

## Say it to your companion
Tell the cup story both ways, state the law with its net-force condition, and explain one bus lurch by inertia.

## Do It Yourself
1. Do the cup test with a coin on a card over a cup: flick the card away. Where does the coin land, and why?
2. A book rests on a table. Name two forces acting on it, and say why the first law is not broken.
3. Why do passengers wear seatbelts? Answer with the first law, naming the lurch the belt protects against.
