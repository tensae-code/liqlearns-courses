## Sub lesson 1 — Pressure in fluids at rest — hydrostatics

**Hydrostatics** is the study of fluids at rest — and of the **pressure** inside them. A fluid presses on everything it touches: on the sides of its vessel, on the bottom, and on anything dipped into it.

The deeper you go in a fluid, the greater the pressure, because the fluid above weighs down on the fluid below. That one fact — pressure growing with depth — is the ground both named principles stand on.

## Sub lesson 2 — Pascal's and Archimedes' principles

**Pascal's principle**: pressure applied to a fluid in a closed vessel is passed through the fluid, undiminished, to every part of it. Squeeze one end of a filled, closed tube and the pressure arrives at the other end whole — that is how fluid pressure can carry a push from one place to another.

**Archimedes' principle**: a body dipped in a fluid is pushed up by an **upthrust** equal to the weight of the fluid it pushes aside — the fluid it **displaces**. If the upthrust matches the body's weight, it floats; if the body is heavier than the fluid displaced, it sinks. Between them, the two principles answer the two everyday questions: how pressure travels in a fluid, and why things float or sink.

## The key ideas

- **Hydrostatics** studies fluids at rest and the **pressure** inside them.
- Pressure in a fluid **grows with depth**.
- **Pascal's principle**: pressure applied to a closed fluid is passed through it undiminished.
- **Archimedes' principle**: upthrust on a body equals the weight of the fluid it **displaces**.
