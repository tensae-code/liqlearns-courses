# Motion — four kinds of motion

## Do it first
Look around for two minutes and collect moving things in your mind: a ball rolling, a rope swinging, a wheel turning, a stone falling. Now try to sort them into groups by one rule only — the shape of the path each one travels. Then read the four names science gives those groups.

## The four kinds
**Linear (rectilinear) motion** travels a straight path — a ball rolling along a straight road, an object falling straight down. **Oscillatory (vibratory) motion** swings to and fro along the same path — a pendulum, a mass bouncing on a spring. **Curvilinear motion** follows a curved path — a car on a curved track, the Moon travelling around the Earth. **Rotational motion** spins around an axis — a wheel turning, the hands of a clock going round.

## Two more names (teacher guide — evidence)
Teachers' materials name two sub-kinds inside this family: **uniform circular motion** — going round a circle at steady speed — and **periodic motion** — a motion that repeats itself in equal times, as a pendulum's swing does. They sharpen the four kinds; they do not replace them.

## Sort by the path, not the thing
One trip can hold several kinds: a car runs linear on the straight, curvilinear on the bend, while its wheels rotate the whole way. Always ask: what shape does the path draw?

## Say it to your companion
Name the four kinds with one example each, then name the two teacher-guide sub-kinds and say what each adds.

## Do It Yourself
1. Sort these by path: a falling leaf in still air (watch it — is the path straight?), a swing, a bicycle wheel, a runner on a straight track, the Moon.
2. Find one motion at home that changes kind in the middle of its travel. Say where the change happens.
3. A pendulum's swing is both oscillatory and periodic. Explain in one sentence how both names fit.
