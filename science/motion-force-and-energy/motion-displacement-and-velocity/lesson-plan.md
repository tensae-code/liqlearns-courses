# Lesson plan — Motion — displacement and velocity

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Motion, Force and Energy · **Lesson:** Motion — displacement and velocity
**Place in the curriculum:** Motion, Force and Energy — Unit 7, after distance and speed (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** displacement is distance travelled in a specified direction — the straight change from start to finish — and velocity = change of displacement ÷ time; the slope of a distance–time graph is the speed
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Motion — distance and speed: distance and speed are measured; now direction is added to both.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Separate distance from displacement on the same journey — the winding path's full length against the 20 m straight shortcut.
2. Compute velocity from a change of displacement and a time.
3. Read a distance–time graph: its slope is the speed, and compare two bodies' graphs.

## What you need
A drawn map of two points joined by a winding path and a straight line; squared paper for the graphs.

## If the learner is stuck
- Uses distance and displacement as one word → distance walks the winding road; displacement takes the straight arrow, direction included.
- Reads a flat distance–time graph as 'fast' → flat means no distance is being added: the body is at rest. Steep means fast.
