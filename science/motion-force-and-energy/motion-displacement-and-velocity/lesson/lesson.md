# Motion — displacement and velocity

## Do it first
Two marks stand on the ground. Walk from the first to the second by a winding route. Now walk straight back. Your legs travelled two different lengths — but your start and finish swapped exactly. Which length describes where you ended up? Keep both answers; science keeps both too, under two different names.

## Displacement
**Displacement** is distance travelled **in a specified direction** — the straight change from start to finish, direction included. Between the same two points, a winding path may run far, while the displacement is the one straight arrow: in the worked picture, a path that wanders still ends only **20 m** from its start, so the displacement is 20 m in that direction. Distance has size only; displacement has size **and** direction.

## Velocity
**Velocity = change of displacement ÷ time.** It is speed with the direction kept: two bodies can share a speed and hold different velocities if they travel different directions. Its unit is still m/s — stated with the direction.

## Reading the graphs
Plot distance against time for two bodies and the graph itself speaks: the **slope** of a distance–time graph is the speed. A steep line is a fast body; a gentle line is slow; a flat line is a body at rest. Speed–time graphs are read the same careful way — shape first, numbers after.

## Say it to your companion
Explain distance against displacement with the two walks, state the velocity formula, and say what a flat distance–time graph means.

## Do It Yourself
1. A learner walks 60 m east around a field and ends 20 m east of the start. State the distance and the displacement.
2. A body's displacement changes by 100 m in 25 s. Compute its velocity. (Answer to check: 4 m/s in the direction of travel.)
3. Two lines on one distance–time graph: one steep, one gentle, both starting together. Which body is faster, and how does the graph tell you?
