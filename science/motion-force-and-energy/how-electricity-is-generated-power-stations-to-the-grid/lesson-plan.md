# Lesson plan — How electricity is generated — power stations from source to grid

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Motion, Force and Energy · **Lesson:** How electricity is generated — power stations from source to grid
**Place in the curriculum:** Motion, Force and Energy — Unit 7, the energy strand (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** almost all the world's electricity is made one way — an energy source turns a turbine, the turbine spins a generator, and the grid carries the result to your home — so every power station is an energy-conversion chain with the same middle
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Motors and generators — electricity to motion, motion to electricity (Electricity and Magnetism): one generator understood there; here the whole road from fuel and river to the socket on the wall.

## Sub lessons
1. **From source to spin** — something must turn: burning fuel or nuclear heat boils water to steam, falling water pushes directly, wind pushes blades — three roads, one spinning turbine.
2. **From spin to socket** — the turbine spins the generator, the generator makes electricity, a transformer steps it up for the long road of the grid, and a second transformer steps it down again for the home.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Motors and generators — electricity to motion, motion to electricity (Electricity and Magnetism title), and Energy — sources, and using energy wisely (this title). Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Tell the full chain for a fuel-fired station: chemical → thermal → kinetic (steam) → kinetic (turbine) → electrical.
2. Tell how hydro and wind stations reach the same spinning turbine by a shorter road.
3. Say what the generator does, in one sentence, and what the turbine does.
4. Explain why electricity travels at high voltage and arrives at the home stepped down.

## What you need
A bicycle with a dynamo if you can find one (or a hand-crank torch), and a look out of the window at the wires that reach your home.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks electricity is made inside the socket or the battery of the house → trace the wire backwards: socket → street → pylons → station → generator → turbine → source. The socket is a delivery point, not a birthplace.
- Mixes up turbine and generator → the turbine only spins; the generator is the part the spinning turns into electricity. One shaft, two jobs in a row.
- Thinks a hydro station has no turbine because it has no steam → falling water pushes the turbine directly; the steam step is skipped, the spin is the same.
