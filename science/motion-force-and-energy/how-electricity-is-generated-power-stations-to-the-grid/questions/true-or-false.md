# How electricity is generated — power stations from source to grid — true or false

Say true or false.

**8.** (Sub lesson 1) The turbine is the part of the station that makes the electric current.
**Answer: False** — the turbine only spins; the generator it drives makes the current

**9.** (Sub lesson 1) A hydro station has no steam stage, but it still has a turbine.
**Answer: True** — falling water pushes the turbine directly

**10.** (Sub lesson 2) The socket in your wall is where electricity is made.
**Answer: False** — the socket is a delivery point; the electricity was made at the station, at the moment of use

**11.** (Sub lesson 2) A transformer near your street steps the voltage down before the electricity enters homes.
**Answer: True** — stepped up for the journey, stepped down for the home

**12.** (Sub lesson 2) When a blackout ends and the lights return, generation somewhere has started again.
**Answer: True** — the grid stores almost nothing, so light means a station is spinning
