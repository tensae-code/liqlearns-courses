# Hands-on — How electricity is generated — power stations from source to grid

The lesson with your hands, not only your eyes.

## Be the turbine
Blow hard through a straw at a paper windmill or a fan's blades and watch it spin: your breath is the wind station's wind, and the spinning is the turbine's one job. Spin it faster and slower and feel how the push sets the speed. If you can find a bicycle dynamo or a hand-crank torch, turn it: the resistance you feel in your hand is the generator taking kinetic energy from you and sending it out as electrical energy — the whole power station, in your palm.

## Follow the wire home
From your front door, follow the wires with your eyes as far as you can — to the pole, the transformer box if there is one, and the line running out of your street. Sketch the road from that pole backwards: what you cannot see, draw anyway — pylons, a station, a spinning turbine — until your sketch reaches an energy source. Label every stage with its form of energy.
