**Course:** Science · **Title:** Motion, Force and Energy · **Lesson:** Trajectory — the trail a moving body leaves

**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Trajectory — the trail a moving body leaves** — 15: trajectory defined as the trail a moving body leaves; straight and curved trails (jet trails, boat wake, tyre tracks), some trajectories invisible; drawing your home-to-school route; Almaty–Astana transport comparison map task
2. **What a movement trajectory is** — The books teach this part as a trajectory can sometimes be seen (jet trail, boat wake) and sometimes not (car on a highway) — worked through in the books' own presentation, at the printed pages named in Sources.
3. **What a trajectory is** — the line a body describes as it moves, straight (rectilinear) or curved (curvilinear); the path travelled is the length of the trajectory; toy-car path measured on flat and sloped surfaces; the marsh harrier's flight trajectory looks like a kite frozen in the air
