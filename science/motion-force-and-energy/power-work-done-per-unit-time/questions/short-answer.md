# Power — work done per unit time — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Explain, in your own words: Same work, different time.
**Answer:** Eritrea's Grade 7 book closes its work-and-energy run with **power** (printed p. 71), after work (printed p. 57) and energy (printed p. 61) have been taught as their own topics. Power answers the question work alone cannot: not how much work is done, but **how fast** it is done. Two workers lift the same load to the same height — the same work — but the one who finishes sooner is the more powerful: the work is equal, the time is not, and power is exactly the measure that notices the difference.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Explain, in your own words: Counting power — work divided by time.
**Answer:** Power is counted as **work divided by the time the work takes**: power = work ÷ time. Its unit is the **watt** — one watt is one joule of work done per second. So a bigger power means either more work in the same time or the same work in less time; machines and muscles alike are rated by it, because what they can do in a second is what power names. Work and energy say how much; power says how fast.

**Answer:** Same work, different time → Counting power — work divided by time — in that order, as taught in lesson/lesson.md.
