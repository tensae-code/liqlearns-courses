Today: **Power — work done per unit time** — Eritrea's way, from its Ministry of Education Science books (see lesson/sources.md for the exact book and page).

Eritrea's Grade 7 book closes its work-and-energy run with **power** (printed p. 71), after work (printed p. 57) and energy (printed p. 61) have been taught as their own topics. Power answers the question work alone cannot: not how much work is done, but **how fast** it is done. Two workers lift the same load to the same height — the same work — but the one who finishes sooner is the more powerful: the work is equal, the time is not, and power is exactly the measure that notices the difference.

Power is counted as **work divided by the time the work takes**: power = work ÷ time. Its unit is the **watt** — one watt is one joule of work done per second. So a bigger power means either more work in the same time or the same work in less time; machines and muscles alike are rated by it, because what they can do in a second is what power names. Work and energy say how much; power says how fast.
