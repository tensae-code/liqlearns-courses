# Conservation of energy and matter

## Do it first
Burn a piece of wood down to ash and smoke: has the matter in it been destroyed? Run a lamp until the fuel or the battery is spent: has the energy been destroyed? Say your answers out loud. Then read on: this is Costa Rica's way in — its Grade 8 Ciencias states one law that answers both questions at once.

## Sub lesson 1 — The law for matter — nothing is lost

The **law of conservation of matter** says that in any change, matter is **not created and not destroyed**: it is rearranged. The wood, the ash, the smoke and the gases together account for all the matter that was there at the start.

That is why a careful weighing before and after a change balances: what seems to vanish has only changed its form or its place.

## Sub lesson 2 — The law for energy — nothing is lost

The **law of conservation of energy** says the same of energy: energy is **not created and not destroyed** — it is **converted**, passed from form to form. The lamp's electrical energy becomes light and heat; nothing of it is annihilated, and nothing appears from nowhere.

Every energy conversion in the neighbouring lessons obeys this law: forms change, the total stands.

## Sub lesson 3 — One law held together

Costa Rica's book teaches the two conservations as **one law of conservation of matter and energy**: matter and energy alike are conserved — rearranged and converted, never created from nothing and never destroyed into nothing.

Hold the pair as the book holds it: one universe, two accounts, and both accounts always balance.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — The law for matter — nothing is lost first, then The law for energy — nothing is lost first, then One law held together. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "A change seems to make something vanish. What does the law say really happened?" Answer: Nothing was destroyed: the matter was rearranged and the energy was converted — both are conserved, only their forms changed.
