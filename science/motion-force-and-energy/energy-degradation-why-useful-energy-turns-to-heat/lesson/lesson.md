# Energy — degradation: why useful energy is lost as heat

## Do it first
Rub your palms together, fast, twenty times. Feel them? They are warm now. Your muscles spent energy to move them — so where did the movement go? It did not vanish. It turned into that warmth in your skin. Hold on to that, because it is the whole lesson in your hands: energy never disappears, but it does slip into a form that can no longer do the job you wanted.

## Nothing is ever destroyed
You already know the great law: **energy is not created and not destroyed** — it only changes form and moves from place to place. Count all the energy before a change and all the energy after, and the two counts match exactly. That law never breaks. So when people say a car "uses up" its fuel, or a bulb "wastes" energy, they cannot mean the energy stopped existing. Something subtler is happening — and Argentina's physics booklet gives it its own name.

## Degradation — the name of the leak
In **every** real conversion, the energy splits. Part of it arrives where you wanted it: the bulb's light, the car's motion, the drill's turning. And part of it — always some part — slips away as **heat spread into the surroundings**: warm brake pads, a warm phone, a humming motor heating the air, sound that thins out and ends as the tiniest warming of the walls. That slipping-away is called **degradation** — in the booklet's Spanish, *degradación de la energía*. The energy is degraded because it has been demoted: from a form concentrated enough to do work, down to warmth scattered thin through air and metal, which can do almost nothing.

## Why the warmth cannot be gathered back
Here is the hard part, so take it slowly. A litre of petrol holds its energy packed tight — one small store that can push a car for kilometres. After the trip, that same amount of energy still exists, but now it is a faint warmth spread through the engine, the road, the tyres and a whole street of air. To run the car again you would have to collect every wisp of that warmth from everywhere it spread — and gathering spread-out warmth itself takes more energy than you would get back. So the count balances, and yet the useful energy is truly lost. That is why the world needs fresh fuel, fresh food and fresh charge every day, even though not one joule of energy has ever been destroyed.

## Efficiency — measuring the leak
Because every conversion leaks, we measure how much of the energy reaches the job. That share is the **efficiency**: useful energy out, divided by total energy in. An old glowing bulb turns only about a twentieth of its electrical energy into light — the rest degrades straight to heat, which is why it burns your fingers. A car engine sends roughly a quarter of its fuel's energy to the wheels; three quarters degrade as heat and sound. Better machines leak less — a modern LED bulb keeps most of its energy as light — but **no real machine leaks nothing**. Friction, electrical resistance and sound always take their share.

## Say it to your companion
Explain, in your own words: the law says energy is never destroyed — so what exactly is lost when a car finishes a trip? Use the word degradation, and say where the lost part is now.

1. A phone charging on a table gets warm. Trace its conversion chain, and point to exactly where degradation happens.
2. A bulb takes in 100 units of electrical energy and gives out 5 units of light. How many units degraded as heat, and what is the bulb's efficiency?
3. A cyclist brakes hard at the bottom of a hill. Where is the bicycle's kinetic energy one minute later — all of it? Name every place some of it sits.
