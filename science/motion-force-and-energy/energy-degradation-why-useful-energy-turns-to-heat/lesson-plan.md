# Lesson plan — Energy — degradation: why useful energy is lost as heat

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Motion, Force and Energy · **Lesson:** Energy — degradation: why useful energy is lost as heat
**Place in the curriculum:** Motion, Force and Energy — Unit 7, the energy strand (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** energy is never destroyed, but in every conversion some of it degrades — it spreads out as heat into the surroundings, where it can no longer do the job — so useful energy is lost even though energy itself is not
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Energy — forms and conversions: forms change into forms there; here the learner finds out why no conversion is ever complete, and why the missing part is always warmth.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the conservation law and explain why it does not mean energy is never lost to us.
2. Name degradation, and say where the degraded part of a conversion goes: heat, spread thin into the surroundings.
3. Explain why spread-out warmth cannot be gathered back to do the job again.
4. Work out a simple efficiency — useful energy out over total energy in — for a bulb and an engine.

## What you need
Your two hands, a bicycle with brakes if you can get one (or any wheel that can be spun and stopped), and a phone or torch that warms while it works.

## If the learner is stuck
- Says "the energy is gone" → nothing is gone; ask where it is now. It is warmth in the palms, the pad, the air — still energy, just spread too thin to use.
- Thinks degradation breaks the conservation law → the law counts ALL the energy, useful and degraded together. Degradation is about usefulness, not about amount.
- Thinks a perfect machine could avoid it → every real conversion leaks some warmth through friction, resistance and sound. Better machines leak less; none leak nothing.
