# Force and motion — Newton's third law

## Do it first
Blow up a balloon. Hold it shut, then — let go. It jets around the room, wild and loud, until its air is spent. Nothing pulled it. Nothing pushed it from outside. What threw it across the room? Its own air, escaping the other way. Hold that picture.

## The law
**Newton's third law:** for every **action** there is an equal and opposite **reaction**. Forces come in pairs: when body A pushes body B, body B pushes body A back, with the same size of force in the opposite direction. The released balloon pushes its air backward; the air pushes the balloon forward. Birds push air **down** with their wings; the air pushes the birds **up**. To jump and to walk, you push the ground **backward and down**; the ground pushes you **forward and up**.

## Why the pair never cancels
If action and reaction are equal and opposite, why does anything move at all? Because the two forces act on **different bodies**. The balloon feels only the air's push on it; the air feels only the balloon's push on it. Each body answers the force on itself — and so both move, in opposite directions. Cancellation belongs to forces on the same body; a third-law pair never shares a body.

## Say it to your companion
State the law, walk the balloon and the foot through it arrow by arrow, and give the different-bodies reason the pair never cancels.

## Do It Yourself
1. A swimmer pushes water backward with her hands. Name the reaction and what it does.
2. A rocket throws hot gas downward. Name the pair and explain the rocket's rise.
3. Two skaters face each other; one pushes the other. What happens to BOTH, and what does that prove about the pair?
