# Lesson plan — Force and motion — Newton's third law

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Motion, Force and Energy · **Lesson:** Force and motion — Newton's third law
**Place in the curriculum:** Motion, Force and Energy — Unit 7, the laws of motion close (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Newton's third law — every action has an equal and opposite reaction, and the pair never cancels because the two forces act on two different bodies
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Newton's second law: one body's acceleration is explained; now the force's partner is accounted for.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the third law and identify both forces of a pair in the balloon, the bird and the jump.
2. Explain why action and reaction never cancel: they act on different bodies.
3. Read a contact — foot on ground — into its two arrows.

## What you need
A balloon for the release test; the ground under your own feet for the jump test.

## If the learner is stuck
- Asks 'if they are equal, why does anything move?' → they act on different bodies; each body answers only the force on itself.
- Pairs gravity on the book with the table's push on the book and calls it third-law → those are balanced forces on ONE body (first-law ground), not an action–reaction pair.
