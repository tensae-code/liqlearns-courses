# Hands-on — Air resistance and the parachute

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Drop one, drop two
Tie four equal strings to the corners of a square of tissue paper and join them to a small load. Drop the load alone from your drop point and time the fall. Drop it again under the canopy and time that fall. Write both times down — the difference is drag, measured.

## The bigger-canopy test
Make a second canopy twice as wide. Drop the same load under both canopies, three times each, and average the times. Say aloud: the bigger canopy presents more area to the air, makes more drag, and so it balances the same weight at a lower speed — a gentler descent.

## Feel the force
Hold a flat sheet of card edge-on and walk fast; then hold it flat-facing and walk at the same pace. The flat-facing sheet pulls against your hand far harder. Say aloud: same speed, different area facing the air — drag followed the area.
