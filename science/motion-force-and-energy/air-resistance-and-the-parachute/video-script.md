Today: **Air resistance and the parachute** — how a sheet of tissue paper can catch a falling thing.

Drop a stone and it races down. But the air is not nothing: anything falling must push the air aside, and the air pushes back. That push-back is drag.
A parachute is a drag machine: a wide canopy that presents a huge area to the air. Falling, its drag grows until it exactly balances the weight — and then the fall stops speeding up and settles, slow and steady, all the way down.
Bigger canopy, more drag, gentler landing. The youngest physicists learn it by building one from tissue paper — and now so have you.

Now you. Say it back — in your own words.
