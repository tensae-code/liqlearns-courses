# Air resistance and the parachute — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Air resistance (drag) is…
a) Gravity pulling down  b) The air's push-back on a body moving through it, pointing against the motion  c) The weight of the air  d) A kind of friction between two solid surfaces
**Answer: b) The air's push-back on a body moving through it, pointing against the motion**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) Drag grows when…
a) Speed falls and area shrinks  b) The body gets heavier only  c) Speed rises, and when the area facing the air grows  d) The air is removed
**Answer: c) Speed rises, and when the area facing the air grows**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) In the tissue-paper parachute, the strings are kept the same length so that…
a) The canopy hangs level and fills evenly, presenting its full area to the air  b) The parachute looks tidy  c) The load spins less for fun  d) The tissue does not tear
**Answer: a) The canopy hangs level and fills evenly, presenting its full area to the air**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) A bigger canopy lowers the same load more gently because…
a) It is heavier  b) It catches more air — more area facing the air makes more drag  c) It falls in shadow  d) The strings stretch more
**Answer: b) It catches more air — more area facing the air makes more drag**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) The steady part of a parachute's fall begins when…
a) The load is dropped  b) Weight disappears  c) The canopy opens  d) Drag rises to balance weight, so there is no net force and no more speeding up
**Answer: d) Drag rises to balance weight, so there is no net force and no more speeding up**

**6.** (Sub lesson 3) If a canopy tears mid-fall, the load will…
a) Speed up — less area means less drag, so weight wins again until drag re-balances at a faster speed  b) Stop in the air  c) Fall more slowly  d) Rise
**Answer: a) Speed up — less area means less drag, so weight wins again until drag re-balances at a faster speed**
