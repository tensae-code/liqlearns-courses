Today: **Pressure in solids, liquids and gases** — Fiji's way, from its book (Fiji, Year 7 — *Year 7 Basic Science*, printed pp. 106-125).

Fiji's Year 7 Basic Science book teaches pressure together with force: a force pressing on something is felt as pressure, and the book's strand runs from that pairing outward — printed pp. 106–125 carry the whole treatment, so this lesson follows its frame.
Pressure is not only a liquid's business. The book teaches it across all three states: solids pressing where they rest, liquids pressing in their containers, and gases pressing as they are squeezed and heated. One idea, three states of matter.
Fiji's book enters the topic at home: the primus stove and kerosene accidents are its starting example. A primus stove works by pressure — and when pressure and kerosene are mishandled, the same pressure that cooks the food causes the accident. The danger is the science, met first.
