| §4-5, Pressure (الضغط): which way of standing in Fig. 4-5 is more comfortable — standing on tiptoe or on the soles of the feet as normal, and why? The answer lies in the concept of pressure: pressure is the ratio of the force applied to the area perpendicular to the force, الضغط (ض) = القوة (ق) ÷ المساحة (م); standing on tiptoe, the whole weight of the body rests on a smaller area than in normal standing, so there is greater pressure on the toes and the position is less comfortable; heavy vehicles that travel on soft ground need very wide wheels (Fig. 4-6): the wide wheels give a larger contact area and less pressure on the ground, preventing the wheels from sinking; the SI unit of pressure is the newton per square metre (ن/م²), and another common equivalent unit is the pascal (با): one pascal = one newton per square metre; the atmosphere at sea level exerts a pressure of about 100 kilonewtons per square metre on us; transmission of pressure through a liquid: in the demonstration apparatus (Fig. 4-7) pressure is applied to water by pushing a piston, and the water spurts out through the holes in all directions including backwards, showing that when pressure is applied to a liquid the pressure is transmitted equally in all directions; this principle has important applications in hydraulic machines — in a car service centre a car weighing 1600 kg can be raised or lowered easily with a force smaller than the car's weight, using the hydraulic lift (Fig. 4-8: pump piston, valves أ and ب opening and closing as the pump is pressed down and raised, a fluid reservoir and a control valve returning the fluid); chapter summary: force is an effort consisting of a push or a pull; a force acting on a body can cause a change in its speed and direction of movement, and can also cause a change in its size and shape;
