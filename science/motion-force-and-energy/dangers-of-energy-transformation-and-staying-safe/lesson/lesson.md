# Dangers of energy transformation — and staying safe

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Dangers of energy transformation — and staying safe. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Kenya's Grade 8 Integrated Science book spends a whole strand turning one form of energy into another — electrical to heat, light and sound, chemical to electrical, potential to kinetic — and then closes the strand with the dangers that travel with those transformations, and how to stay safe from them.

In your mind's eye: every energy transformation that serves us can also harm us — burns from heat, shock from electricity, damage from loud sound — and each danger has its own safety measures, which are part of using the energy at all. — picture it happening step by step, in order, and say each step as you see it.

## The key facts
**Heat transforms — and burns.** Appliances that turn electricity into heat — cookers, irons, heaters — can burn skin, scorch materials and start fires. The safety measures match the danger: keep flammables away, never leave heating appliances unwatched, switch off after use, and treat every hot surface as able to burn until it has cooled.

**Electricity transforms — and shocks.** Electrical energy on its way to light, heat, sound and movement can pass through a body as an electric shock. Dry hands, undamaged insulation, no water near sockets, and never opening or repairing a live appliance are the measures that keep the transformation in the wires where it belongs.

**Sound and movement transform — and injure.** Very loud sound damages hearing over time and at once; moving machinery and falling loads carry kinetic energy that injures on contact. Distance, guards, ear protection and attention are the safety measures. The rule for all of them: name the transformation, and you can name its danger and its guard.
