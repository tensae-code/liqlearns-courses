Today: Dangers of energy transformation — and staying safe.

Remember this one thing: every energy transformation that serves us can also harm us — burns from heat, shock from electricity, damage from loud sound — and each danger has its own safety measures, which are part of using the energy at all.

Appliances that turn electricity into heat — cookers, irons, heaters — can burn skin, scorch materials and start fires. The safety measures match the danger: keep flammables away, never leave heating appliances unwatched, switch off after use, and treat every hot surface as able to burn until it has cooled.

Electrical energy on its way to light, heat, sound and movement can pass through a body as an electric shock. Dry hands, undamaged insulation, no water near sockets, and never opening or repairing a live appliance are the measures that keep the transformation in the wires where it belongs.

Very loud sound damages hearing over time and at once; moving machinery and falling loads carry kinetic energy that injures on contact. Distance, guards, ear protection and attention are the safety measures. The rule for all of them: name the transformation, and you can name its danger and its guard.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
