## The graph problems
On a **speed–time graph**, a rising section is **accelerating**, a flat section is **steady speed**, a falling section is **decelerating** — and the **distance** travelled is the **area under the graph**. Work the set's graph both ways: read its intervals, then take its distance from the area.

## Gravity on the falling ball
A ball falls past point after point. At which points does gravity act on it? The unit's answer: **at every point of the path** — gravity is not waiting at the ground; it pulls through the whole fall, which is why the ball accelerates all the way down.

## KE, backwards and compared
Back-calculation: a body holds **KE = 16 J** with mass **2 kg**. From KE = ½mv², v² = 2 × 16 ÷ 2 = 16, so **v = 4 m/s**. The comparison: a lorry with **double the mass** against a car with **double the speed** — whose KE grows more? Mass doubles KE once (×2); speed doubles it **four** times (×4, the square). The faster car wins the comparison — the squared factor always outruns the plain one.

## The goalkeeper's kick
Trace the transfers: the keeper's muscles (chemical) → the leg's swing (mechanical/kinetic) → the ball's flight (kinetic, with gravitational growing as it climbs) → sound, warmth and the net's movement at the catch or the goal. Every arrow is one conversion from the unit's chain-tracing work.
