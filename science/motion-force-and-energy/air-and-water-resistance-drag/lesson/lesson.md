# Air and water resistance — drag

## Do it first
Push your open hand quickly through the air. Now imagine pushing it through water the same way. Which push-back is stronger? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Iceland's way in — its Grade 3 force chapter names water resistance and air resistance together as things to learn.

## Sub lesson 1 — Air resistance — the air pushes back

Anything moving through air must push the air aside, and the air pushes back. That push-back is **air resistance**: a force against the motion, met by everything that moves through air.

## Sub lesson 2 — Water resistance — the water pushes back harder

The same thing happens in water, only stronger: anything moving through water must push the water aside, and the water pushes back — that is **water resistance**. Iceland's aims sheet teaches the two together, as one pair: air resistance and water resistance, the push-backs of the two stuffs things move through. The parachute lesson (`motion-force-and-energy/air-resistance-and-the-parachute`) goes deeper into air drag alone; this lesson's one thing is the pair, met first at Grade 3 level.

## The key ideas

- Air resistance is the air's push-back on anything moving through it — a force against the motion.
- Water resistance is the water's push-back on anything moving through it — the same kind of force, felt more strongly.
- Iceland teaches the two together as one pair: the push-backs of air and of water.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — Air resistance — the air pushes back first, then Water resistance — the water pushes back harder. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "A swimmer and a runner move at the same speed. Who meets the stronger push-back?" Answer: The swimmer — water resistance is the stronger push-back of the pair.
