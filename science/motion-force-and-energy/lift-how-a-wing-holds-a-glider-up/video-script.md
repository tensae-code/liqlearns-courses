Today: **Lift** — the push that holds a glider up with no engine at all.

A wing is curved on top. Moving through the air, it leaves the pressure above it lower than the pressure below it — and the stronger push from underneath wins. That winning push is lift.
A paper helicopter's blades are little wings, spinning: their lift slows its fall to a whirl. A glider's wings are still, but the air still flows — paid for with height, sinking gently as it travels.
And you can measure the bargain: distance forward divided by height lost is the glide ratio. You build the glider and compute it yourself.
