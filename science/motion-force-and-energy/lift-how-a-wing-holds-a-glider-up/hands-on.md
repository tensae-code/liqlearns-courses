# Hands-on — Lift — how a wing's pressure difference holds a glider up

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Build and fly the glider
Fold a paper glider with a clear curved-top wing profile. Launch it level from a measured height — a stairwell landing or a raised platform — three times, and measure each flight's distance. Record height lost (your launch height) and distance for every flight.

## Compute G
For your best flight, compute the glide ratio G = D/H: distance flown divided by height lost. Say aloud what the number means: "my glider travels G metres forward for every metre it sinks." Reshape the wing — a little more curve — and fly again: did G improve?

## The paper helicopter
Cut a paper strip with two blades at the top, fold the blades opposite ways, add a paperclip to the stem, and drop it. It whirls down slowly. Say aloud: the spinning blades are wings; their lift is what holds the fall back.
