# Lift — how a wing's pressure difference holds a glider up — depth

Whole-lesson pass mark: the learner states lift as a pressure difference (lower above a wing's curved top, higher below), explains the helicopter rotor and the glider wing as the same trick at work, and measures a real flight to compute its glide ratio G = D/H and say what the number means — answering the lesson's questions from the lesson text.
This lesson has three sub lessons, proved in order: each opens by proving the previous one. Practice deepens inside each part before the learner moves on — reshaping a wing and re-flying it, spinning rotors of different blade sizes, and logging flights until the learner can improve a glide ratio on purpose, not by luck.
