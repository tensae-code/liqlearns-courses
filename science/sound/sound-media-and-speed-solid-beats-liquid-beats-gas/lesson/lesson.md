# Sound — media and speed: solid beats liquid beats gas

## Do it first

Two pupils stand 50 m apart and shout at the same instant. One voice is high-pitched, the other low. Four groups in the book argue about what a listener in the middle hears: two say ‘together’ for different reasons, two say ‘the higher pitch first’. Pick your group — then be ready to watch three of the four fall.

## Sub lesson 1 — The shout argument — what speed depends on

Alemu and Almaz, 50 m apart, shout simultaneously; hers is higher-pitched. **Groups 1 and 3** say the sounds arrive together — Group 1 because speed depends on the air (the medium), Group 3 because the loudness is the same. **Groups 2 and 4** say the higher pitch arrives first. Judge them: the winner is **Group 1's reason** — sound's speed depends on the **medium** it travels through, not on its **pitch** and not on its **loudness**. Re-judge the whole argument when only the loudness changes: the arrival is still together. Sound travels **out in all directions** through matter — **air, liquids and solids** — and **never through a vacuum**, where there is no matter to vibrate.

## Sub lesson 2 — Particles hand it on — the speed order

Why the medium rules the speed: sound is handed on by particles colliding. In a **solid** the particles sit close-packed, so the vibration is handed on **fastest**; in a **liquid** they are looser, so it is slower; in a **gas** they are scattered far apart, so it is **slowest**. The order to fix: **steel faster than water faster than air**. Catch travel time yourself (Activity 7.11): listen through a funnel-and-tube while a partner taps left, centre and right — which ear hears first tells you the sound took the tube's path and arrived by the nearer route.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion which group's reason won the shout argument and why the other three reasons fail, then give the particle reason for steel beating water beating air.

## Do It Yourself

1. Ear on the desk: scratch one end of a long desk or rail while your ear rests on the other end. Compare with the same scratch heard through air. Which arrived clearer and ‘closer’, and why?
2. The funnel test (Activity 7.11): taps left, centre, right into a funnel-and-tube. Record which ear hears each tap first and explain the pattern.
3. Vacuum reasoning: a bell rings inside a jar as the air is pumped out (described, not done). Predict what a listener hears and give the particle reason.
