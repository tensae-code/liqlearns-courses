# Sound — vibration, volume and pitch

## Do it first

Touch a ringing bell, lightly. Touch a radio while it plays. Something is shaking under your fingers. Now stretch a rubber band over a box between two pencils and pluck it — long, then shorter. Two different things change as you play: how LOUD, and how HIGH. This lesson pulls them apart.

## Sub lesson 1 — Sound is vibration, spending itself

**Sound is energy that produces hearing, and every sound is a vibrating object** setting the surrounding medium vibrating in turn — that is the shaking your fingers felt at the bell and the radio. Sound **weakens as it travels**, because it spends its energy on the way: a shout is loud close up and faint far off, not because it slows, but because its energy is used up spreading through the medium.

## Sub lesson 2 — Volume and pitch — and the rubber-band test

Two qualities, independent of each other:

- **Volume** — loudness and softness: a whisper against a talking voice.
- **Pitch** — high against low: **slow vibration gives low pitch; fast vibration gives high pitch**. The local anchors: the **kirar sounds high against the begena's low** — ask why (their strings vibrate at different rates).

Make pitch experimental (Activity 7.9): rubber bands stretched over a box between two pencils, the vibrating length stepped **15 cm down to 3 cm**, prediction recorded before each tap. The result to earn: **shorter length, higher pitch**.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what sound is, why it fades with distance, and the difference between making a sound louder and making it higher — with the kirar and begena as your example.

## Do It Yourself

1. The band steps (Activity 7.9): lengths 15, 12, 9, 6, 3 cm. Predict each pitch before you tap, then record the true order. Was your prediction right at every step?
2. Louder or higher? A drum beaten harder; a drum beaten faster; a small bell; a big bell. Say for each: volume change, pitch change, or both?
3. Kirar and begena: if you can hear or see the two instruments, note string thickness and length. Form a reason why one sounds high and the other low.
