# Sound signals in daily life

## Do it first
Before you read: a whistle blows at a match, a bell rings at school, a siren passes in the street. None of those sounds is speech — yet each tells you something at once. Say what each one means. Then read on: this is Iran's way in — drums that told a whole shrine the hour, and a captain who steers by signals.

## Sub lesson 1 — Drums that announce the hour

Iran's Grade 2 book tells it as a historical note inside its sound lesson: at the shrine of Imam Reza, the naqqareh — great kettle drums — were sounded to announce sunrise and sunset to the pilgrims. The drumbeat was not music for its own sake: it was a signal, a message everyone in earshot understood — the day begins; the day ends.

## Sub lesson 2 — The captain's signals

The same book turns the class into a ship's crew: imagine you are a ship's captain, guiding and being guided by signals — a light seen, a sound heard, each with an agreed meaning. A signal works because sender and receiver agree the meaning beforehand: the sound or flash is short; the meaning is the whole message.

## The key ideas

- A signal is a sound or light with an agreed meaning — the message travels without words.
- The naqqareh drums at the shrine of Imam Reza announced sunrise and sunset to pilgrims.
- A signal works only because sender and receiver agreed its meaning beforehand.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Why could a drumbeat tell pilgrims the time of day?" Answer: because everyone knew the agreed meaning — that drumbeat, at that place, meant sunrise (or sunset); a signal carries the meaning the two sides agreed.

## Do It Yourself
1. List five signals you hear in a week and write each one's agreed meaning.
2. Invent a two-signal code with a friend — one clap-pattern for "come", one for "wait" — and test whether a third person can guess the meanings without being told. Say what the test proves.
3. Teach this lesson to someone at home in five sentences.
