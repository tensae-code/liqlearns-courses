# Lesson plan — Sound signals in daily life

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Sound · **Lesson:** Sound signals in daily life
**Place in the curriculum:** Sound — appended to Unit 21 (Sound) (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** sound (and light) used as a message — signals that carry meaning across a distance — Iran's way: the naqqareh drums at the shrine of Imam Reza that announced sunrise and sunset to pilgrims, and the class game of the ship's captain steering by light and sound signals
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Drums that announce the hour** — Iran's Grade 2 book tells it as a historical note inside its sound lesson: at the shrine of Imam Reza, the naqqareh — great kettle drums — were sounded to announce sunrise and sunset to the pilgrims. The drumbeat was not music for its own sake: it was a signal, a message everyone in earshot understood — the day begins; the day ends.
2. **The captain's signals** — The same book turns the class into a ship's crew: imagine you are a ship's captain, guiding and being guided by signals — a light seen, a sound heard, each with an agreed meaning. A signal works because sender and receiver agree the meaning beforehand: the sound or flash is short; the meaning is the whole message.
