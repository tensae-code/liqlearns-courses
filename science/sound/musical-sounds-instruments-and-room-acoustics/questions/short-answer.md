# Musical sounds, instruments and room acoustics — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why do a dutar and a trumpet playing the same written note sound different?
**Answer: The note's fundamental — the pitch — is the same, but each instrument strengthens different overtones; that overtone recipe is the timbre, and the ear reads it as the instrument's identity.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) State the pitch rule a string player uses on the dutar's neck.
**Answer: Shorter vibrating length → higher pitch (frequency inversely proportional to length); pressing the string on the neck shortens it, so notes rise as the hand moves toward the body.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) A hall is to be fixed: music sounds muddy and words blur. What is wrong and what is the cure, in acoustics terms?
**Answer: Reverberation is too long — reflections pile onto new sounds. Cure: add absorption (curtains, carpet, upholstered seats, acoustic panels) and break up large flat reflecting surfaces, shortening the ring toward about two seconds.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Why does the book end the music lesson with hearing health?
**Answer: Because the ear cannot tell 'musical' from 'noise' when it comes to damage: level and duration decide the harm, so loud sound of any kind, enjoyed or not, must be dosed like any other exposure.**
