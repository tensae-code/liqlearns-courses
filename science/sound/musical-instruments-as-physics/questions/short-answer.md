# Musical instruments as physics — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A string 0.6 m long carries a wave at 240 m/s. Find its frequency, and the frequency when it is pressed to 0.3 m.
**Answer: f = v/(2L) = 240/(2 × 0.6) = 200 Hz. Halving the length doubles the frequency: 240/(2 × 0.3) = 400 Hz — an octave higher.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) Why are a piano's lowest strings wound with extra wire?
**Answer: Winding raises the string's mass per unit length μ, and a heavier string carries the wave more slowly — f = (1/2L)√(T/μ) falls — so the instrument can reach low notes without impossibly long strings.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) A closed pipe is 0.85 m long and sound travels at 340 m/s. Find its fundamental frequency.
**Answer: For a closed pipe λ = 4L = 3.4 m, so f = 340/3.4 = 100 Hz.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) How is a drum tuned, and why is its pitch less pure than a violin's?
**Answer: By changing the skin's tension — tighter raises the note, slacker lowers it (and a bigger skin sounds lower at the same tightness). Its pitch is less pure because the skin vibrates in several tangled patterns at once, unlike the string's single clean loop.**

**5.** (Sub lesson 3) What does a guitar's hollow body do, and what does it not do?
**Answer: It resonates — it vibrates with the string and moves far more air at the same frequency, making the sound loud enough to fill a room. It does not choose the note: the string's length, tension and thickness have already done that.**
