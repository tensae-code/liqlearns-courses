# Hearing — the path from vibration to brain

## Do it first

Seven cards, shuffled: object vibrates — air vibrated — vibrations reach ear — inner ear vibrates — electrical signals created — signals reach brain — sound is heard. Put them in the order you think hearing happens. Keep your order in front of you: the lesson walks exactly this road, station by station, and you will grade your own sorting at the end.

## Sub lesson 1 — The seven-step relay

Hearing runs in a fixed order — the order of Activity 7.12's cards:

1. The **object vibrates**.
2. The **air is vibrated**.
3. The vibrations **reach the ear**.
4. The **inner ear vibrates**.
5. **Electrical signals are created**.
6. The signals **reach the brain**.
7. **Sound is heard** — understood, in the brain.

Nothing is ‘heard’ until the last step: the ear's whole job is to carry a vibration inwards and turn it into signals the brain can read.

## Sub lesson 2 — The ear's stations

Walk the relay through the ear itself:

- The **outer ear** catches air-borne sound and **funnels it in**.
- The **eardrum vibrates** as the sound arrives.
- The **three tiny middle-ear bones vibrate in turn**, passing the vibration on.
- The **inner ear — the cochlea**, snail-shaped and filled with liquid — turns the liquid's vibration onto a **nerve**, which carries messages to the **brain**, where the sound is understood.

Now re-grade your card order from the Do-it-first: any card out of place is a station skipped or swapped.

## Say it to your companion

Walk your companion down the whole path — object, air, outer ear, eardrum, three bones, cochlea, nerve, brain — without missing a station.

## Do It Yourself

1. Re-sort the seven cards from memory, then check against the lesson. Write the two steps learners most often swap, and why the true order must be so.
2. Station drawing: draw the ear as four stations in a row (outer ear, eardrum, bones, cochlea) with the nerve leaving for the brain. Label each station's job in three words or fewer.
3. Break the chain: for each station, say what hearing loses if that station is damaged or blocked (wax at the entrance, a damaged eardrum, damaged nerve).
