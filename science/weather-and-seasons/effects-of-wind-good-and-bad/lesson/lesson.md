# Effects of wind — good and bad

## Do it first
Before you read: think of the strongest wind you have felt, and of a gentle breeze on a hot day. The same moving air did both. What good did the breeze do — and what harm can the strong wind do? Say both. Then read on: this is Rwanda's way in — its books teach the effects of wind whole, good and bad together.

## Sub lesson 1 — The effects of wind

Rwanda's Primary 2 book teaches the **effects of wind** as their own topic: wind is moving air, and its effects are felt everywhere — the lesson begins by naming what wind does around the learner.

## Sub lesson 2 — The dangers of wind

In Primary 4 the darker half is named plainly: the **dangers of wind** — the harm strong wind does to houses, crops, trees and people. A danger that is named can be watched for.

## Sub lesson 3 — Preventing the negative effects of wind

Both years close with protection: **measures to prevent the negative effects of wind** (Primary 2) and **prevention of the dangers of wind** (Primary 4) — the learner is not left with the danger, but with what is done against it.

## The key ideas

- **The effects of wind** — the effects of wind.
- **The dangers of wind** — the dangers of wind.
- **Preventing the negative effects of wind** — preventing the negative effects.
