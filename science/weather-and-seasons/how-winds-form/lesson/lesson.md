# How winds form

## Do it first
Stand outside and feel the wind on your face. The air around you is moving — but why does it move at all? Nobody is pushing it. Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Cuba's way in — after teaching what air is, its Grade 5 book asks how winds originate, and then follows their circulation.

## Sub lesson 1 — How winds originate

The Sun does not heat the Earth evenly: one place is warmed more, another less. Air over the warmer ground is **warmed, grows lighter and rises**. The cooler, heavier air beside it **moves in underneath** to take its place — and that moving air is **wind**. A wind is born wherever neighbouring air is warmed unequally: the warm air climbing and the cool air sliding in are the wind's two halves, and they always come together.

## Sub lesson 2 — The circulation of the winds

The same rising and sliding-in does not happen in one place only. Across the whole Earth, air **rises where it is warm, travels, sinks where it is cool, and returns** — a great turning pattern called the **circulation of the winds**. The wind you feel on your face is one small part of that planet-wide circulation: local air on the move, inside a system that never stops turning.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Where does a wind begin?" Answer: Where the Sun has heated neighbouring places unequally: the warmed air rises, and the cooler air moves in beneath it — that movement is the wind; joined across the Earth, those movements are the circulation of the winds.
