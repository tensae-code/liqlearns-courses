# Hands-on — Clouds — kinds by shape and height

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the lesson's work (household materials)
Work the lesson's central example yourself, the way Saudi Arabia's book sets it: clouds form when rising air cools to the dew point and vapour condenses on tiny particles; they are classified by shape and height — low clouds (cumulus, which can thicken into heavy rain and thunder; layered stratus; and fog, a cloud touching the ground), middle clouds of water and ice crystals, and high feathery cirrus of ice crystals Use only household materials, write down what you did and what it showed, and compare your result with the lesson.
