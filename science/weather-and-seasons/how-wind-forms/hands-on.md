# Hands-on — How wind forms

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Hold a light strip of paper above a warm lamp or sunlit ground (with care, never near flame) and watch it stir in the rising warm air. Then hold it in a cool shady spot and compare. Write what the rising warm air did.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — a picture of how wind forms — wind is air on the move: the Sun heats the Earth unevenly, warm air rises and cool air sinks, and air flows from where it is pressed down to where it has risen — that flow is wind. — label it, and check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
