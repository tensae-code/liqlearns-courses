Today: How wind forms.

Remember this one thing: wind is air on the move: the Sun heats the Earth unevenly, warm air rises and cool air sinks, and air flows from where it is pressed down to where it has risen — that flow is wind.

The Sun heats the ground and air by day; at night they cool. Warm air is lighter and rises; cool air is heavier and sinks. This daily rising and sinking — the diurnal change — is the first mover of the air.

The Sun does not heat the Earth evenly: land warms faster than water, and sunny places warm faster than shaded ones. Above warm ground the air rises, leaving lighter pressure; above cool ground it sinks, pressing down. Air then flows from the high-pressure place to the low-pressure place — and moving air is wind.

That is why coasts feel a sea breeze by day and a land breeze by night, and why winds carry clouds, rain and temperature changes from place to place. Follow the heating, and you can say which way the air will move.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
