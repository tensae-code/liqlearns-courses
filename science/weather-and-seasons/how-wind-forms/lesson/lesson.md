# How wind forms

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — How wind forms. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Bangladesh's Class 4 Elementary Science book asks why the weather changes at all, and finds the engine: day and night heating and cooling, and land and sea warming at different speeds, set the air itself in motion.

In your mind's eye: wind is air on the move: the Sun heats the Earth unevenly, warm air rises and cool air sinks, and air flows from where it is pressed down to where it has risen — that flow is wind. — picture it happening step by step, in order, and say each step as you see it.

## The key facts
**Heating by day, cooling by night.** The Sun heats the ground and air by day; at night they cool. Warm air is lighter and rises; cool air is heavier and sinks. This daily rising and sinking — the diurnal change — is the first mover of the air.

**Uneven heating makes the flow.** The Sun does not heat the Earth evenly: land warms faster than water, and sunny places warm faster than shaded ones. Above warm ground the air rises, leaving lighter pressure; above cool ground it sinks, pressing down. Air then flows from the high-pressure place to the low-pressure place — and moving air is wind.

**Wind moves the weather.** That is why coasts feel a sea breeze by day and a land breeze by night, and why winds carry clouds, rain and temperature changes from place to place. Follow the heating, and you can say which way the air will move.
