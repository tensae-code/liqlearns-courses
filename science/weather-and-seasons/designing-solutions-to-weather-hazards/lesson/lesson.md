# Designing solutions to weather hazards

## Do it first
A river floods the same street every year. The rain cannot be stopped. But must the street flood the same way every time? What could be built or changed — and how would you know it helped? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: Utah's Grade 3 book sets weather hazards as a design problem, with testing and optimising built in.

## Sub lesson 1 — Weather-related hazards and their effects

A **weather-related hazard** is dangerous weather whose effects harm people and places (Grade 3, 3.1.3, printed p. 28) — the book's worked ground is flooding and its **flood barriers and similar** solutions. The design problem is stated exactly: the hazard will come; the **effects** are what a solution can reduce.

## Sub lesson 2 — Design, test and optimise the solution

The solution follows the design loop: **design** a barrier or other defence, **test** it against water, and **optimise** — improve the design from what the test showed — until the effects are reduced as far as the design can manage. A first barrier that leaks is not a failure of the task; it is the task's middle. The tested, improved design is the answer.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "A flood barrier leaks at its corners in the first test. What does the design process say to do next?" Answer: Optimise: change the design where the test showed the weakness, and test again — the first version is the middle of the task, not its end.
