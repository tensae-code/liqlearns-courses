# Measuring temperature, rain and wind

## Do it first
A farmer says: 'Last night's rain was heavy.' A neighbour says: 'No, it was light.' Who is right? Feelings cannot settle it — but an instrument can. And which way was the wind blowing at noon? There is an instrument for that too. This lesson names the measurers.

## The elements, and their instruments
The **elements of climate** the Grade 4 book measures are **temperature, rainfall amount, wind and air pressure** — the moving parts of the air, counted and recorded day after day.

Two instruments carry Amharic-borrowed names worth learning as the book gives them:

- The **wind vane (ዊንድቬን)** — it **shows the direction of the wind**, swinging to point with it.
- The **rain gauge (ሬንጌጅ)** — it **measures the amount of rainfall**, catching the rain and holding it up to be read.

Measured and written down, day after day, the elements stop being feelings — they become the records from which climate itself is read.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the four elements, then the two instruments and what each one does — wind vane ዊንድቬን for direction, rain gauge ሬንጌጅ for amount.

## Do It Yourself
1. Wind watching: three times in one day, look at trees, smoke or washing on a line and note the wind's direction in words. That is the wind vane's job done by eye.
2. Rain memory vs rain measure: after the next rain, ask two people how heavy it was. Their answers will differ — say what a rain gauge would settle.
3. Instrument drawing: draw the wind vane and the rain gauge side by side and label each with its name and its job.
