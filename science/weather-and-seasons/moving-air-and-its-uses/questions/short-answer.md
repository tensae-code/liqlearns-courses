# Moving air and its uses — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Explain, in your own words: Moving air is wind.
**Answer:** **Moving air is called wind.** South Sudan's Primary 1 book teaches it plainly: wind makes things move — and the Primary 2 book proves the presence of the air we cannot see by blowing up a balloon and by making and flying a kite.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Explain, in your own words: What wind does — help and harm.
**Answer:** Wind's effects run both ways: it can **cause destruction** — destroying trees and houses and blowing soil away — and it is also at work for us every day. Naming both sides together is the book's own way: the same moving air that harms is the moving air that helps.

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Explain, in your own words: Uses of moving air.
**Answer:** The uses are named one by one: wind **helps us dry clothes**, **generate electricity** and **winnow grains** — separating chaff from grain — and, in the guide's answers, sailing. The Primary 2 kite is the uses lesson in the hand: moving air lifting and pulling what the learner made.

**4.** (Whole lesson) Retell the whole lesson in its own order, one sentence per sub lesson.
**Answer:** Moving air is wind → What wind does — help and harm → Uses of moving air — in that order, as taught in lesson/lesson.md.
