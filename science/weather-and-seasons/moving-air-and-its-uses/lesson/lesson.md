# Moving air and its uses

## Do it first
Before you read: this lesson teaches one thing — moving air and its uses — as placed from South Sudan's Primary Science books (see lesson/sources.md for the exact books and pages). What do you already know about it? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is South Sudan's way in — its Ministry of General Education and Instruction books teach this as its own topic, in the order below.

## Sub lesson 1 — Moving air is wind

**Moving air is called wind.** South Sudan's Primary 1 book teaches it plainly: wind makes things move — and the Primary 2 book proves the presence of the air we cannot see by blowing up a balloon and by making and flying a kite.

## Sub lesson 2 — What wind does — help and harm

Wind's effects run both ways: it can **cause destruction** — destroying trees and houses and blowing soil away — and it is also at work for us every day. Naming both sides together is the book's own way: the same moving air that harms is the moving air that helps.

## Sub lesson 3 — Uses of moving air

The uses are named one by one: wind **helps us dry clothes**, **generate electricity** and **winnow grains** — separating chaff from grain — and, in the guide's answers, sailing. The Primary 2 kite is the uses lesson in the hand: moving air lifting and pulling what the learner made.
