# Preparing for a weather forecast

## Do it first
The morning news says: rain by noon, and a storm tonight. You are leaving the house now. What do you take, what do you wear, and what do you change about your day? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: Utah's Kindergarten book teaches exactly this — the human reaction to a forecast, preparing from what the forecast says.

## Sub lesson 1 — The forecast tells what is coming

A **forecast** is a statement of the weather that is expected — rain, storm, heat, cold — before it arrives. Its whole value is time: because the forecast comes first, people can act first. Utah's Kindergarten standard K.1.2 is the human reaction: use a forecast to decide how to prepare (printed p. 23).

## Sub lesson 2 — Prepare — clothing and items to carry

Preparation starts with the body and the bag: **clothing** chosen for the forecast weather, and **items to carry** that the day will need. The decision is read off the forecast, not off the sky at the door — the sky at leaving time is this hour's weather, and the forecast is the whole day's.

## Sub lesson 3 — Prepare for severe weather — floods, storms, extreme heat

Some forecasts call for more than clothing: **severe weather** — **floods, storms and extreme heat** — needs preparation of its own, made before the severe weather arrives. The book names the three plainly, and the lesson holds them as the serious end of the same skill: read the forecast, and prepare to match what it says.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "A forecast says heavy rain and a storm later today. What are two preparations it should cause?" Answer: Clothing and items chosen for rain — and, because the weather is severe, preparations for the storm itself, made before it arrives.
