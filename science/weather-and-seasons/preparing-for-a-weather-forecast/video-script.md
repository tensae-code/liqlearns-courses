# Video script — Preparing for a weather forecast

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Today: **Preparing for a weather forecast** — the United States' way, from its Utah SEEd Standards books (Kindergarten).

A **forecast** is a statement of the weather that is expected — rain, storm, heat, cold — before it arrives. Its whole value is time: because the forecast comes first, people can act first. Utah's Kindergarten standard K.1.2 is the human reaction: use a forecast to decide how to prepare (printed p. 23).

Preparation starts with the body and the bag: **clothing** chosen for the forecast weather, and **items to carry** that the day will need. The decision is read off the forecast, not off the sky at the door — the sky at leaving time is this hour's weather, and the forecast is the whole day's.

Some forecasts call for more than clothing: **severe weather** — **floods, storms and extreme heat** — needs preparation of its own, made before the severe weather arrives. The book names the three plainly, and the lesson holds them as the serious end of the same skill: read the forecast, and prepare to match what it says.

Now you. Say it back — in your own words.
