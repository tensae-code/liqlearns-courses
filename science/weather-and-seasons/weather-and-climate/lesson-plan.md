# Lesson plan — Weather and climate

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Weather and Seasons · **Lesson:** Weather and climate
**Place in the curriculum:** Weather and Seasons — after Today's weather (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** weather changes hour by hour; climate is the air's long pattern — the Grade 3 book says judging it needs at least 30 years of records — and its elements are temperature, rain, wind, humidity, air pressure, cloud, dew, frost, snow, sunshine and fog, with rain and temperature the key ones
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Today's weather — daily records in hand; climate is what those records become over many years. The city-scale and air content rests partly on teacher-guide evidence, labelled so.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. ## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say the difference: weather is short and changing; climate is the long-repeated pattern of heat, cold, rain and wind.
2. Say why farmers must know the climate: when to prepare, when to sow, when to harvest — and how climate helps or harms people's work.
3. Name the elements of weather and climate, and pick out rain and temperature as the key ones for study.
4. Say what air is, from the Grade 2 guide: a mixture of gases that occupies space and has weight; living things breathe because air exists.
5. Compare the same street in the rainy season and the dry season — the Grade 3 guide's city picture of climate.

## What you need
Your seven-day weather record from the last lesson; a farmer or elder to ask about sowing times.

## If the learner is stuck
- Calls one rainy week 'climate' → climate needs the long pattern; the Grade 3 book sets the bar at 30 years of records.
- Forgets the farmer → climate is not a school word only: sowing and harvest are decided by it.
- The city-climate and air parts rest on teacher guides (G2 and G3) — evidence, labelled in the sources; the definitions, elements and 30-year rule are the Grade 3 student book's own.
