# Seasons and climate zones of our country

## Do it first
One country, five climates — stacked by height. Snow on Ras Dashen; heat like an oven in berha. This lesson climbs the mountain zone by zone, then asks what makes the zones: the controls of climate themselves.

## Sub lesson 1 — Bega and kiremt, and the five zones

Ethiopia's year turns on two great seasons: **bega** — dry, hot, sunny — and **kiremt** — rainy, cold, **the farming season**. Across the country, the Grade 2 book sets **five climate zones**: **berha** (very hot), **qolla**, **weyna dega**, **dega**, and **wurch** (very cold), differing in their rains.

The Grade 3 book climbs them by altitude with numbers. **Wurch/qur**: very cold, below about 5 °C, sometimes snow — Ras Dashen, Guna, Abune Yosef, Amba Farit, Abiye Meda, Choke. **Dega**: cool and rainy, below about 16 °C on average — Dega Damot, Debre Berhan, Debarq, Injibara, Debre Tabor, Dessie. **Weyna dega**: 16–29 °C — the most productive and comfortable for people. **Qolla**: hot, 20–30 °C, low rain, sparse people. **Berha**: extremely hot, up to about 45 °C, little rain, hard for life.

And people are not outside this system: the Grade 2 book adds that human actions can disturb rainfall and raise heat — the zones are nature's gift, but their balance is in human hands.

## Sub lesson 2 — What controls weather and climate

Why do near places differ? The Grade 3 book names the **controls**: **altitude difference, latitude lines, land shape (relief), cloud, distance from water bodies, and wind movement**. One rule governs the first and greatest of them: **high places get more rain and less heat; low places get the reverse** — which is why the book can draw a mountain with a cold, rainy top and a hot, dry foot, both true at the same hour.

## Say it to your companion
Take your companion up the mountain: name each zone, its heat, its towns, its clothes. At the top, turn and ask the control questions: why is it colder here? What else, besides height, moves a climate?

## Do It Yourself
1. Find your own place on the zone ladder. Name its zone, its temperature feel, its season, and defend the placement with the book's numbers.
2. Pack two bags in drawings: one for a trip to dega in kiremt, one for berha. Every item must be defended by the zone's climate.
3. List the controls at work in your own area: its height, its land shape, its distance from big water, its winds. Which one rules most?
