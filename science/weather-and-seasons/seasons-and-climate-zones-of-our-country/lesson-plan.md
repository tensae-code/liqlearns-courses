# Lesson plan — Seasons and climate zones of our country

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Weather and Seasons · **Lesson:** Seasons and climate zones of our country
**Place in the curriculum:** Weather and Seasons — the country's own map of climates (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Ethiopia's seasons — bega the dry season, kiremt the rainy, farming season — and its five climate zones by altitude: berha, qolla, weyna dega, dega, wurch, from very hot to very cold, and what controls the differences: altitude, latitude, land shape, cloud, distance from water and wind
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Weather and climate — the pattern named; this lesson maps the pattern onto the country's heights.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the two great seasons: bega (dry, hot, sunny) and kiremt (rainy, cold, the farming season).
2. Name the five climate zones with their heat: berha (very hot), qolla, weyna dega, dega, wurch (very cold).
3. Give the Grade 3 book's zone temperatures and places: wurch below about 5 °C with snow on the high peaks (Ras Dashen, Guna, Choke); dega cool, below about 16 °C average (Debre Berhan, Dessie); weyna dega 16–29 °C, the most productive and comfortable; qolla hot 20–30 °C with low rain; berha extremely hot, up to about 45 °C, with little rain.
4. Name what controls weather and climate: altitude difference, latitude lines, land shape, cloud, distance from water bodies, wind movement — high places get more rain and less heat, low places the reverse.
5. Say that human actions can disturb rainfall and raise heat.

## If the learner is stuck
- Puts the zones side by side on flat land → the book's ladder is altitude: wurch crowns the peaks, berha floors the low desert; the same map, different heights.
- Swaps bega and kiremt → kiremt is the rainy farming season; bega the dry, hot, sunny one. The farm calendar is the memory hook.
- Zone temperatures and town lists are the Grade 3 book's Amhara-region worked lists — kept as printed, and the sources say so.
