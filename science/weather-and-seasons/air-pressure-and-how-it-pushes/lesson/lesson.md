# Air pressure — and how it pushes

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Air pressure — and how it pushes. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
South Sudan's Primary 3 Science pupil's book meets air pressure inside its weather unit: before recording the weather, learners discover that the air itself is a thing that pushes.

In your mind's eye: air has weight, and the air above presses down and out in every direction — that push is air pressure, and it is strong enough to hold water in an upturned glass and to crush a can emptied of air. — picture it happening step by step, in order, and say each step as you see it.

## The key facts
**Air presses in all directions.** We live at the bottom of an ocean of air. Its weight presses on everything — down, up and sideways — and we do not feel it only because it presses inside us too, balancing the push. That pressing is air pressure.

**Pressure at work.** Air pressure holds a card under a full, upturned glass of water; it pushes drink up a straw when you lower the pressure in your mouth; it crushes a sealed can when the air inside is removed. Each trick is the outside air pushing where nothing pushes back.

**Pressure and weather.** Air pressure is not the same everywhere or every day: warm rising air lightens it, cool sinking air raises it, and those differences drive winds and weather changes — which is why the weather unit measures pressure alongside temperature, rain and wind.
