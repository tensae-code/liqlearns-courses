# Hands-on — Air pressure — and how it pushes

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Fill a glass right to the brim with water, lay a stiff card over the top, hold it and turn the glass upside down over a basin. Let go of the card: air pressure pushing up holds the water in. Write what pushed, and from which side.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — a picture of air pressure — and how it pushes — air has weight, and the air above presses down and out in every direction — that push is air pressure, and it is strong enough to hold water in an upturned glass and to crush a can emptied of air. — label it, and check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
