# Air pressure — and how it pushes — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1.** What does this lesson say about air pressure — and how it pushes?
a) Air has weight, and the air above presses down and out in every direction — that push is air pressure, and it is strong enough to hold water in an upturned glass and to crush a can emptied of air.
b) It plays no part in this topic at all
c) It happens only at night and nowhere else
d) It is found only in non-living things
**Answer: a) Air has weight, and the air above presses down and out in every direction — that push is air pressure, and it is strong enough to hold water in an upturned glass and to crush a can emptied of air.**

**2.** What does this lesson say about air presses in all directions?
a) It plays no part in this topic at all
b) We live at the bottom of an ocean of air.
c) It happens only at night and nowhere else
d) It is found only in non-living things
**Answer: b) We live at the bottom of an ocean of air.**

**3.** What does this lesson say about pressure at work?
a) It plays no part in this topic at all
b) It happens only at night and nowhere else
c) Air pressure holds a card under a full, upturned glass of water; it pushes drink up a straw when you lower the pressure in your mouth; it crushes a sealed can when the air inside is removed.
d) It is found only in non-living things
**Answer: c) Air pressure holds a card under a full, upturned glass of water; it pushes drink up a straw when you lower the pressure in your mouth; it crushes a sealed can when the air inside is removed.**

**4.** What does this lesson say about pressure and weather?
a) It plays no part in this topic at all
b) It happens only at night and nowhere else
c) It is found only in non-living things
d) Air pressure is not the same everywhere or every day: warm rising air lightens it, cool sinking air raises it, and those differences drive winds and weather changes — which is why the weather unit measures pressure alongside temperature, rain and wind..
**Answer: d) Air pressure is not the same everywhere or every day: warm rising air lightens it, cool sinking air raises it, and those differences drive winds and weather changes — which is why the weather unit measures pressure alongside temperature, rain and wind..**
