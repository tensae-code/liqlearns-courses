# Air pressure — and how it pushes — writing

Write your answer out in full sentences.

**1.** Write two or three sentences explaining air pressure — and how it pushes, using the lesson's key facts.
**Model answer: Air has weight, and the air above presses down and out in every direction — that push is air pressure, and it is strong enough to hold water in an upturned glass and to crush a can emptied of air. We live at the bottom of an ocean of air. Its weight presses on everything — down, up and sideways — and we do not feel it only because it presses inside us too, balancing the push. That pressing is air pressure. Air pressure holds a card under a full, upturned glass of water; it pushes drink up a straw when you lower the pressure in your mouth; it crushes a sealed can when the air inside is removed. Each trick is the outside air pushing where nothing pushes back. Air pressure is not the same everywhere or every day: warm rising air lightens it, cool sinking air raises it, and those differences drive winds and weather changes — which is why the weather unit measures pressure alongside temperature, rain and wind.**
