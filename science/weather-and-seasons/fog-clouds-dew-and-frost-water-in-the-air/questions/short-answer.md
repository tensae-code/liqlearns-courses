# Fog, clouds, dew and frost — water in the air — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** Why does the book teach fog and clouds together?
**Answer:** Because they are the same thing in two places: cooled water vapour turned into tiny droplets — gathered high up it is a cloud, gathered at the ground it is fog.

**2.** How does dew form?
**Answer:** On a clear night the ground cools and cools the air touching it; the vapour in that air settles out onto the cold surfaces as drops of liquid water.

**3.** How does frost differ from dew?
**Answer:** Frost is dew's frozen form: on surfaces cooled below freezing, the settled water arrives as ice or turns to ice where it lands, instead of staying liquid drops.

**4.** Where does the water in fog, dew and frost come from?
**Answer:** From the air itself — the invisible water vapour the air holds, returned to liquid (or ice) by cooling: the water cycle at work in the air.
