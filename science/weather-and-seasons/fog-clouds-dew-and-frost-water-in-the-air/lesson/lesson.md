# Fog, clouds, dew and frost — water in the air

## Do it first
Before you read: say out loud what you already think this lesson's one thing is — the water in the air made visible: fog and clouds as cooled vapour turned to droplets, and dew and frost as vapour settled on cold ground. Then read on: this is China's way in — its 科学 (Science) textbooks teach this thing at the places named in the sources, and this lesson follows their order.

## Sub lesson 1 — Fog and clouds

The air holds water as an invisible gas — water vapour. Cool that air and the vapour turns back into **tiny liquid droplets**. Gathered high up, the droplets are a **cloud**; gathered at the ground, they are **fog**.

China's Grade 6 book teaches the pair together because they are the same thing in two places: fog is a cloud that forms where we stand. Both are the water cycle at work in the air itself — evaporation's water, returned to liquid by cooling.

## Sub lesson 2 — Dew and frost

On a clear night the ground cools, and the air touching it cools too. The vapour in that air settles out onto the cold surfaces — grass, leaves, stones — as drops of liquid water: **dew**.

Cool the surface further, below freezing, and the settled water arrives as ice or turns to ice where it lands: **frost**, dew's frozen form. Dew and frost are the night's handwriting — they record how cold the ground became while the world slept.

## The key ideas

- **Fog and clouds** are both cooled water vapour turned into tiny droplets — a cloud high up, fog at the ground; they are one thing in two places.
- **Dew** is vapour settled as liquid drops on surfaces the night has cooled.
- **Frost** is dew's frozen form — the settled water arrived as, or turned to, ice on surfaces cooled below freezing.

## Do It Yourself
1. On a cool morning, look for dew: name three surfaces carrying it and one surface staying dry, and say why the cold ones carry it.
2. Draw fog and a cloud side by side and write under each where its droplets gathered — then one sentence on why they are the same thing.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences — how invisible vapour becomes frost by morning.
