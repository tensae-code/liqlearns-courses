# El Niño and La Niña — ENOS

## Do it first
The Pacific Ocean swings. Some years its eastern waters warm far beyond normal and the world's weather shifts; other years they cool beyond normal and the weather shifts the other way. One ocean, two opposite swings — what single pattern holds them both? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Costa Rica's way in — its Grade 7 Ciencias teaches the whole swing under one name: ENOS.

## Sub lesson 1 — ENOS — one oscillation, two phases

**ENOS** — the **El Niño–Southern Oscillation** — is the Pacific pattern in which the ocean and the air above it swing together between a warm phase and a cold phase, each returning in its turn.

Teaching the two phases under one name is the point of this lesson: El Niño and La Niña are not two separate curiosities but the two ends of one oscillation, and the weather effects of each phase are read against the other.

## Sub lesson 2 — El Niño — the warm phase and its effects

In the **El Niño** phase the eastern Pacific **warms** beyond its normal pattern. That warming is an event with **effects**: the changed ocean changes the winds and rains above it, and weather far from the Pacific feels the shift.

The warm phase alone — the weakening trade winds, the stopped upwelling, the warming itself — is taught in full in the neighbouring lesson `weather-and-seasons/el-nino-when-the-pacific-warms`; this lesson holds it as one half of the ENOS swing.

## Sub lesson 3 — La Niña — the cold phase and its effects

In the **La Niña** phase the same eastern Pacific **cools** beyond its normal pattern — the opposite swing of the same oscillation. Its effects likewise reach into the weather: where El Niño brings one pattern of rain and dryness, La Niña brings the opposed pattern.

Hold the pair together, as Costa Rica's book does: warm phase, cold phase, one ENOS — and a learner who meets either name first can place it on the swing.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — ENOS — one oscillation, two phases first, then El Niño — the warm phase and its effects first, then La Niña — the cold phase and its effects. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Is La Niña a different phenomenon from El Niño?" Answer: No — they are the two phases of one oscillation, ENOS: El Niño is the warm phase of the eastern Pacific, La Niña the cold phase.
