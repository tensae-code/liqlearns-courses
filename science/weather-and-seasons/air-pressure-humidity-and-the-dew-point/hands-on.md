# Hands-on — Air pressure, humidity and the dew point

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Do the lesson's work (household materials)
Work the lesson's central example yourself, the way Saudi Arabia's book sets it: air pressure falls as we rise; heated air expands, thins and rises leaving low pressure, cooled air contracts and sinks making high pressure; humidity is the amount of water vapour in the air (warm air holds more), and the dew point is the temperature at which the air becomes saturated and condensation begins — with the chart showing vapour capacity roughly doubling every 10 °C Use only household materials, write down what you did and what it showed, and compare your result with the lesson.
