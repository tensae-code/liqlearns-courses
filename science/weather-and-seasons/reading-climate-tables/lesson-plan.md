# Lesson plan — Reading climate tables and averaging

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Weather and Seasons · **Lesson:** Reading climate tables and averaging
**Place in the curriculum:** Weather and Seasons — the records at work: one real table, three real stations (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a climate table holds month-by-month temperature and rainfall for real stations — Gondar, Bahir Dar and Kombolcha — and reading it means finding wettest and driest months, the wet–dry gap, and yearly averages
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Measuring temperature, rain and wind — the instruments made the records; the table is where records become knowledge.

## Before this lesson

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what the book's Table 3.1 holds: month-by-month temperature (°C) and rainfall (mm) for Gondar, Bahir Dar and Kombolcha.
2. Find, for a station: its wettest month, its driest month, and the gap between them.
3. Work a yearly average from monthly numbers — and start your own school record: daily temperature for a month, then its average.

## What you need
The climate-table picture; a pencil for the averaging; a thermometer record started at school, the book's own follow-on.

## If the learner is stuck
- Reads one month and calls it the climate → the table's power is the whole year at three places: wettest AND driest AND averages.
- Averages by picking the middle number → an average is all the numbers added, divided by how many there are; do it once slowly, with small numbers.
