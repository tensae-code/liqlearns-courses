# Reading climate tables and averaging

## Do it first
Here are three towns — Gondar, Bahir Dar, Kombolcha — and twelve months of rain and heat for each. Which town drowns in the rains? Which one stays almost dry all year? The answers are hiding in the numbers. This lesson teaches the treasure-hunt: reading a climate table.

## Table 3.1 — three stations, twelve months
The book's **Table 3.1** gives, **month by month**, the **temperature in °C** and the **rainfall in mm** for three real stations: **Gondar, Bahir Dar and Kombolcha**.

Reading it is a set of hunts, the book's own:

1. For each station, find its **wettest month** and its **driest month**.
2. Find the **gap** between the wet and the dry — how far apart a place's two faces stand.
3. Work each station's **yearly averages** — the months gathered into one honest number.

Then the table turns home: the book sets you to **record your own school's daily temperature for a month and average it** — your station, your table, your first climate record.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion how to read the table: what the rows and columns hold, and the three hunts — wettest, driest, and the averages.

## Do It Yourself
1. Chart the gap: draw two bars for one station — its wettest month and its driest. The height difference is the wet–dry gap, seen.
2. Average practice: take your week's morning temperatures and average them: add them all, divide by seven. That is the table's own arithmetic.
3. Station detective: from the chart, which station would a farmer choose who fears flood? Which for a crop that hates rain? Answer from the numbers only.
