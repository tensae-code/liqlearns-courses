# Lesson plan — Day and night, rainbow and flood

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Weather and Seasons · **Lesson:** Day and night, rainbow and flood
**Place in the curriculum:** Weather and Seasons — natural events, inside the Grade 1 guide's weather chapter (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a natural event happens in nature outside human control; day and night are a natural cycle — 24 hours, 12 of sunlight and 12 without — and rainbow and flood are named among our area's natural events
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Today's weather — the learner watches the air; here the biggest daily event of all gets its name. This lesson rests on teacher-guide evidence only; its sources say so plainly.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a natural event is: something that happens in nature outside human control.
2. Say the day-and-night account: a full day is 24 hours — 12 hours with sunlight (day) and 12 without (night).
3. Tell day and night apart by their signs: by day, light, warmth and noise, and the sun in the sky; by night, darkness, cold and quiet, and the moon and stars.
4. Say what a flood (ጎርፍ) is and the damage it does, and name the rainbow (ቀስተ ደመና) among natural events.

## What you need
Your own day to divide: when did the sun rise and set for you? A clock or the sun's own marks.

## If the learner is stuck
- Counts only 'day' as the day → the book's full day is 24 hours, night included: 12 with sunlight, 12 without.
- Calls rain 'made by farmers' → natural events are outside human control; sowing waits on them, never commands them.
