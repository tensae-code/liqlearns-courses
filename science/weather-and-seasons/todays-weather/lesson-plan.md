# Lesson plan — Today's weather

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Weather and Seasons · **Lesson:** Today's weather
**Place in the curriculum:** Weather and Seasons — the sixth title on the Science ladder (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** weather is the air's condition now — sunny, rainy, cloudy, cold, hot, windy — changing hour by hour and morning to evening, and we observe and report it
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the child's own sky — every learner has weather today. It needs nothing else first.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Light — the sun's comings and goings are the weather's biggest actor. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name today's weather from the book's word-list: sunny, rainy, cloudy, cold, hot, windy, foggy, dry.
2. Keep a morning sky-observation record: cloud, wind, frost, rain — and report it to the class.
3. Say how the air changes inside one day: morning, midday, evening differ; day differs from night.
4. Fill a yes/no table describing today's weather — the Grade 3 book's form.

## What you need
Your own eyes and skin, at three times of day; a notebook or the class weather board.

## If the learner is stuck
- Reports a season when asked for weather → weather is today's air, even this hour's; the season is the long pattern (next lesson).
- Writes 'hot' at midday and thinks the day is 'hot' → the same day held a cold dawn; the record must carry the time with the word.
- This lesson's next door: Weather and climate — from today's air to the long pattern.
