# Today's weather

## Do it first
Step outside. Sunny? Windy? Cold? Now — was it like this at dawn? Will it be like this tonight? Weather is the air, changing all day long. This lesson teaches you to watch it and report it like a scientist.

## The day's changing air

**Weather (የአየር ጠባይ)** is the day's changing air: **sunny, rainy, cloudy, cold, hot, windy**. The Grade 2 book sets morning pictures against midday ones — the same place, different air — and assigns a **morning sky-observation record**: cloud, wind, frost, rain, noted and reported to the class.

The Grade 3 book widens the frame: weather is the air's condition **changing within 24 hours** — hot, humid, cloudy, windy, clear sky — with **day against night** and **morning, midday and evening** all differing. Its form is a **yes/no table** describing today's weather: Is it hot today? Is it windy? Fill it daily, and you are keeping a weather record the way scientists begin.

## Say it to your companion
Report today's weather to your companion three times over — as it was in the morning, as it is at midday, as it will be in the evening — and let them catch any report that used a climate word for a weather moment.

## Do It Yourself
1. Keep the book's morning record for seven days: cloud, wind, frost, rain — yes or no each — and bring the week to the lesson.
2. Describe today's weather at morning, midday and evening in the book's words, and pin the three reports side by side.
3. Ask an elder: what weather sign do they watch? A wind, a cloud shape, a bird? Report it as local knowledge, labelled as such.
