The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

Today: **How a digital message travels** — Nepal's way, from its Curriculum Development Centre books.

The parts of a digital communication system in order (guide evidence): source, input transducer, encoder, modulator, channel, demodulator, decoder, output transducer and output signal, taught by role-play with nine learners as the nine parts and by following a text from one mobile to another through the telecom towers; analogue against digital communication compared — coding possible only in digital, digital needing more bandwidth but suffering far less from outside disturbance.

Baseband and broadband transmission, and why digital wins (guide evidence): baseband sends the digital signal unchanged over a low-pass channel for short distances (the LAN); broadband converts it to analogue form for long distances and is high-speed internet; digital transmission's advantages listed — little outside mixing, reliable and cheap circuits, almost no overlap or cross-talk, privacy by encoding and compression, few errors; the teacher box adds bandwidth measured in bits, megabits and gigabits per second for digital and in hertz for analogue, wired channels (twisted pair, coaxial, optical fibre) against wireless, and the radio frequency bands from medium frequency to extremely high frequency with their uses.

Now you. Say it back — in your own words.
