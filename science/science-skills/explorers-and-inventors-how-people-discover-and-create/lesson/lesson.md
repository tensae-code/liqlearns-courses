# Explorers and inventors — how people discover and create

## Do it first
Before you read: name one thing in your home that somebody had to invent — it did not grow, and it did not exist until a person created it. Now name one place a person once had to travel to before anyone knew what was there. Say both names out loud. Then read on: this is Moldova's way in — its Grade 4 Științe (Sciences) book opens its first unit, "Man, part of nature", with the lesson Omul – explorator și inventator: Man — explorer and inventor.

## Sub lesson 1 — Explorers — expeditions and the people who make them

An **explorer** is a person who goes out to find what is not yet known. Explorers travel on **expeditions** — journeys made to discover: to reach a place, see what is there, and bring the knowledge back for everyone else.

The lesson follows the named explorers: people whose expeditions added whole places and whole facts to what the world knows. Exploring is discovering — what the explorer finds was already there; the expedition makes it known.

## Sub lesson 2 — Inventors and scientists — and modern technologies

An **inventor** creates what did not exist before; a **scientist** finds out how things work. The lesson runs from the named explorers to Edison and Liv Arnesen, and on to modern technologies — the technologies of our own time, which stand on centuries of discovering and creating.

So the one lesson holds the pair together: people discover, and people create. The explorer adds knowledge of what is there; the inventor and the scientist add new things and new understanding — and modern technologies are where both roads arrive today.

## The key ideas

- An explorer goes on expeditions to discover what is not yet known, and brings the knowledge back.
- An inventor creates what did not exist before; a scientist finds out how things work.
- Moldova's Grade 4 lesson runs from the named explorers to Edison and Liv Arnesen, and on to modern technologies.
