# NCSE assessment — how final marks are built

## Do it first
Before you read: if your final mark for a subject were decided, which should count more — one examination at the very end, or the work you did in every year of the course? Say your answer out loud, and why. Then read on: this is Trinidad and Tobago's own answer, printed in its NCSE Integrated Science Curriculum Guide, Forms 1–3, in the guide's Computing Final Marks table (printed p. 31).

## Sub lesson 1 — The three coursework shares

In Trinidad and Tobago's NCSE Integrated Science, **coursework counts in every form**, and the guide states each form's share of the final mark:

- **Form 1 coursework: 15%**
- **Form 2 coursework: 30%**
- **Form 3 coursework: 15%**

Form 2 carries the biggest coursework share — twice either of the others. Added together, the three coursework shares make **60%** of the final mark. Work done early counts: Form 1 coursework is already 15% of the mark you will finally receive, before Form 2 has even begun.

## Sub lesson 2 — Examinations and NCSE certification

The coursework shares do not make the whole mark. **Examinations** count toward the rest of the final mark — the 40% the coursework shares leave.

The built-up final mark — coursework from Forms 1, 2 and 3 plus examinations — is the mark that leads to **NCSE certification**. That is the design's point: certification rests on work spread across the whole course, not on a single day's examination alone.

## The key ideas

- Form 1 coursework counts **15%**, Form 2 coursework **30%**, Form 3 coursework **15%** — together **60%** of the final mark.
- **Examinations** count toward the rest.
- The built-up final mark leads to **NCSE certification**.
