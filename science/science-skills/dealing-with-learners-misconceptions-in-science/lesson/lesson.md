# Dealing with learners' misconceptions in science

## Do it first
A pupil is sure of something that is not so — sure the heavier stone must fall faster, sure plants eat soil. What should the teacher do with that first idea: stamp it wrong at once, or bring it out into the open and work on it? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: Greece's Grade 5 teacher guide gives this question its own place in Part I, before any chapter commentary.

## Sub lesson 1 — First ideas — pupils arrive with early and mistaken conceptions

Learners do not arrive empty. They arrive with **first ideas** — early conceptions of how the world works, built from everyday life — and some of those conceptions are mistaken. Greece's guide names them plainly: pupils' **early and mistaken conceptions** are a fact of the science classroom, there before the teaching starts, and a teacher who does not reckon with them teaches over the top of them.

## Sub lesson 2 — Surface the idea and work on it — not just correct it

The guide's way with a mistaken conception is not simply to correct it. The teacher **surfaces** the wrong first idea — brings it out where the class can see it and talk about it — and then **works on it**, so the pupil's own thinking moves. A correction handed down leaves the first idea in place underneath; an idea surfaced and worked on is an idea the learner can actually change.
