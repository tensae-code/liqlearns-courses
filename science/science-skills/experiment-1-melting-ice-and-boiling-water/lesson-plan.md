# Lesson plan — Experiment 1: melting ice and boiling water

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Science Skills · **Lesson:** Experiment 1: melting ice and boiling water
**Place in the curriculum:** Science Skills — the first numbered experiment of the Grade 4 book: proving a change of state (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Experiment 1 proves a change of state: ice on a big spoon over a candle flame melts at 0 °C and water boils at 100 °C at sea level (thermometer taken before and after) — and the change is physical, because only density and volume changed, not what the material is
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Solids, liquids and gases and the three-states teaching — the states were learned there; here the book's own experiment proves the change between them.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Tell Experiment 1 in order: ice on a big spoon over a candle flame; its temperature taken before and after.
2. State the two fixed points at sea level: ice melts at 0 °C; water boils at 100 °C.
3. Say why the change is physical: only density and volume changed — the material is still water.
4. Recite the printed safety point: the spoon's handle is cloth-wrapped against the heat (adult help with fire, always).

## What you need
For the telling: nothing. For the doing, an adult leads: ice, a big spoon with a cloth-wrapped handle, a candle, a thermometer. Fire is the adult's part, always.

## If the learner is stuck
- Calls melted ice a new material → the experiment's own conclusion corrects: only density and volume changed; the material — water — is the same. Compare the paper-burning lesson, where a new body WAS made.
- Forgets sea level → the fixed points are stated at sea level; the words belong with the numbers.
