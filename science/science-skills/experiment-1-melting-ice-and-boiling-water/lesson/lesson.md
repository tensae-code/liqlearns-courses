# Experiment 1: melting ice and boiling water

## Do it first
An ice cube sits on a big spoon. Under it, a candle flame. Two questions before we start: at exactly what temperature will the ice melt? And when water runs off the spoon — is that a NEW material, or the same water wearing a new state? The book's Experiment 1 answers both with a thermometer.

## The spoon, the flame, the thermometer
**Experiment 1 — proving a change of state.** Ice is placed on a **big spoon** and held **over a candle flame**, and its **temperature is taken before and after** — the thermometer is the experiment's witness.

What the witness reports, at sea level: **ice melts at 0 °C**, and the water it becomes **boils at 100 °C**.

And the conclusion the book draws: the change is a **physical change**, because **only density and volume changed — not what the material is**. Ice, water, steam: one material in three states, and the experiment walks it across two of the borders.

The book prints its safety warning with the experiment: the spoon's **handle is wrapped in cloth** against the heat — and fire work is done **with adult help**, always.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the experiment in order — set-up, thermometer before and after, the two fixed temperatures — and the conclusion: physical change, and why.

## Do It Yourself
1. Fixed-point quiz: a friend calls 'melting ice!' — you answer '0 °C'. They call 'boiling water!' — '100 °C — at sea level.' Swap.
2. State walk: find water in your home in two states today (liquid, and ice if there is a freezer, or steam over a pot — look, never touch). Name the border each crossed.
3. Conclusion writing: write the experiment's conclusion in your own words — why melting is physical. Mention density and volume, the book's own reasons.
