# Science words — glossary of action verbs

## Do it first
Before you read: a question says "Compare a plant cell and an animal cell." Another says "Define a cell." Do the two questions want the same kind of answer? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: Trinidad and Tobago's NCSE Integrated Science Curriculum Guide, Forms 1–3, closes with a glossary that defines the action verbs its curriculum uses (printed p. 109) — so that learners know what each instruction word asks.

## Sub lesson 1 — The glossary and why it is there

At the very end of the guide, after all the science topics, sits a **glossary of action verbs**: the words the curriculum itself uses when it tells learners what to do, each one **defined**.

That placement is the lesson. The guide treats the instruction words as part of the science course: a learner can know the science and still miss the question if they do not know what the verb in it is asking for. The glossary is there so the verb is never a mystery — **the verb in the question tells you the job your answer must do**.

## Sub lesson 2 — The seven verbs at work

The glossary defines the curriculum's action verbs, among them:

- **Annotate** — add notes or labels that explain.
- **Classify** — sort things into groups by what they share.
- **Compare** — set things side by side and say how they are alike and how they differ.
- **Define** — state the exact meaning of a word or idea.
- **Demonstrate** — show, by doing or by example, that a thing is so or how it works.
- **Describe** — give an account of a thing's features, in words.
- **Observe** — look carefully and notice what is actually there.

Each verb sets a **different job**. An answer that describes when the question said classify has done good work on the wrong job. The skill is a habit, done in this order: **find the verb, say its job, then answer**.

## The key ideas

- Trinidad and Tobago's guide **closes with a glossary** defining its action verbs (printed p. 109).
- The verbs named include **annotate, classify, compare, define, demonstrate, describe and observe**.
- Each verb sets a different job; **find the verb first**, and let it shape the answer.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — the glossary and why it is there first, then the seven verbs at work. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "A question says 'classify' — what job is that?" Answer with the job, not by repeating.
