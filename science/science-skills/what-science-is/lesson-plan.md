# Lesson plan — What science is — evidence, inference and tentative knowledge

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Science Skills · **Lesson:** What science is — evidence, inference and tentative knowledge
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — Basic Concepts of Science (Grade 7 General Science).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** science is knowledge built on evidence: we observe, we infer, we hold what we know as tentative — ready to change when better evidence comes
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on nothing but the learner's own curiosity — this is the first lesson of Grade 7 science, and the course-intro script rides on it

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **The sealed cube: observing and inferring** — judging the four claims, then observing a sealed cube and inferring its hidden face, backed by evidence
2. **Seven characteristics of scientific knowledge** — the seven marks — evidence-based, tentative, observation-plus-inference, creative, law-vs-theory, many methods, social
3. **The telephone: science growing on other shoulders** — Bell's 1876 patent mined for the same seven characteristics

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: nothing — this lesson opens the Grade 7 course. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Judge four tempting claims about science and say why each one fails.
2. Infer the hidden bottom face of a sealed cube from observation, and back the inference with evidence.
3. Name the seven characteristics of scientific knowledge.
4. Tell a law from a theory: a law describes a pattern; a theory explains it.
5. Mine the telephone story for the same characteristics at work.

## What you need
A sealed box or cube whose bottom face the class cannot see; paper and pencil.

## If the learner is stuck
- Calls an inference an observation → ask: "Did your eyes see it, or did your mind conclude it?" If no eye saw it, it is an inference — and it needs evidence named.
- Thinks tentative means weak → tentative means honest: the knowledge is strong enough to use, and open enough to improve.
