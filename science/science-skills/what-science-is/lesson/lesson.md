# What science is — evidence, inference and tentative knowledge

## Do it first
Four claims. Judge each one before you read on: (1) Scientific knowledge is absolute — it never changes. (2) Science always works by experiment. (3) There is one single scientific method. (4) A theory is just a weak law. Keep your verdicts. Then take a sealed cube: you can see its top and sides, but never its bottom face. What is on the bottom? You cannot look — you must reason. That reasoning, done carefully, IS this lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — The sealed cube: observing and inferring
Work in a group. Look at the cube from every side you are allowed. Write down what you **observe** — only what your eyes truly report. Then write what you **infer** — what you conclude about the hidden bottom face, and the evidence each inference stands on. Different groups will reach different answers from the same evidence. That is not failure: an inference goes beyond the evidence, so imagination and prior knowledge enter it — and two honest scientists can infer differently until more evidence arrives.

## Sub lesson 2 — Seven characteristics of scientific knowledge
Scientific knowledge: (1) rests on **empirical evidence** — observation and measurement; (2) is **tentative** — it can change when new evidence comes; (3) joins **observation with inference** — seeing, and concluding beyond the seeing; (4) needs **creative thinking** — imagination builds the inference; (5) keeps **laws and theories** apart — a law describes a pattern that is seen again and again, a theory is a supported explanation of why; (6) uses **many methods**, not one single method — observation, comparison, experiment and more; (7) is a **social activity**, shaped in part by the people and culture doing it. Now check your four verdicts from Do it first: every one of the four claims fails against this list.

## Sub lesson 3 — The telephone: science growing on other shoulders
The telephone's story carries the same marks. Alexander Graham Bell patented it in **1876** — but he built on the work of others before him, and on his own work teaching deaf learners, which taught him how sound and speech work. One invention, many hands and many years: knowledge growing by evidence, inference and other people's findings — tentative, social and creative, exactly as the seven characteristics say.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the cube story first — what you observed, what you inferred — then the seven characteristics, then one mark of science you found in the telephone story.

## Do It Yourself
1. Cube at home: seal any box, mark its bottom in secret, and let a family member infer the bottom from the visible faces. Score their inferences against the evidence they used.
2. Claim hunt: find one claim like the four in Do it first (in talk, on the radio, in a book) and test it against the seven characteristics.
3. Law or theory? Write one law-like pattern you see every week (the sun rises in the east) and one explanation someone gives for it. Label each: law or theory.
