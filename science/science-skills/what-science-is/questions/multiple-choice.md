# What science is — evidence, inference and tentative knowledge — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** An inference is —
a) what your eyes directly see  b) a conclusion you draw from observations, backed by evidence  c) a guess with no evidence  d) the same as a law
**Answer: b) a conclusion you draw from observations, backed by evidence** — an inference goes beyond observation and must stand on evidence

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** Two groups infer different bottom faces from the same cube. This shows —
a) one group must be cheating  b) inferences can differ because they go beyond the evidence  c) observation is useless  d) science has no rules
**Answer: b) inferences can differ because they go beyond the evidence** — the same evidence can support different inferences until more evidence arrives

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** Scientific knowledge is tentative. That means —
a) it is too weak to use  b) it can change when better evidence comes  c) it is only opinion  d) it never settles anything
**Answer: b) it can change when better evidence comes** — tentative = open to revision by evidence

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** A law and a theory differ because —
a) a law describes a pattern; a theory explains it  b) a theory is a failed law  c) laws are old, theories are new  d) there is no difference
**Answer: a) a law describes a pattern; a theory explains it** — laws describe; theories explain

**5. (Sub lesson 2)** How many characteristics of scientific knowledge does the lesson fix?
a) four  b) five  c) seven  d) ten
**Answer: c) seven** — Table 1.1 fixes seven

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** Bell's telephone (1876) teaches that science —
a) is done by one genius alone  b) builds on others' work and on the scientist's own other work  c) never uses imagination  d) follows one single method
**Answer: b) builds on others' work and on the scientist's own other work** — he built on others and on teaching deaf learners
