# Everyday technology — design and first aid

## Do it first
Before you read: think of the thing this lesson is about, from your own life. Say out loud what you already know about it. Then read on: this is Pakistan (Punjab)'s way in — its Punjab Single National Curriculum *General Science* books teach this as its own lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Design and make — paper, clay and models

Pakistan (Punjab)'s Grade 4 book gives Technology in Everyday Life a whole closing chapter (Ch. 10): paper folding, cutting and pasting, paper bags and cards, and clay models of solids — with a hammer, wheels and gears modelled from clay. Technology is learned by making: the hands design, build and test the thing.

## Sub lesson 2 — Technology for health — the first-aid box and health checks

The same Grade 4 chapter carries the health tools: the contents of a first-aid box, a digital thermometer and blood-pressure checks, with phones and tablets named as tools. Grade 7 adds a making strand of its own inside its technology unit — a simple stethoscope is stocked in the heart lesson, and hand sanitizer in the cleanliness lesson — the health-technology family this sub lesson names.

## Sub lesson 3 — Using the Sun — the solar oven and the solar cooker

Grade 6's technology unit sets the build of a solar oven that converts solar energy into heat energy, and Grade 8's sets a concave-mirror solar cooker doing the same job by focusing sunlight. The Sun is the power source; the maker's job is to trap or focus its heat.

## Sub lesson 4 — Watering and new materials — irrigation and bioplastic

Grade 7 compares drip and sprinkler irrigation and sets the build-a-sprinkler activity (§11.1): two ways of delivering water to plants, judged side by side. Grade 8 makes bioplastic from milk and vinegar as an application of biotechnology, with the corn-starch bioplastic procedure — a new material made in the classroom.

## The key ideas

- Technology in everyday life is a **design-and-make** subject: paper, clay and models first — bags, cards, solids, wheels and gears.
- Health has its own tools: the **first-aid box**, the digital thermometer and blood-pressure checks — and phones and tablets count as tools.
- Sunlight can be **trapped or focused** to cook: the solar oven (Grade 6) and the concave-mirror solar cooker (Grade 8).
- Water can be delivered by **drip or sprinkler** irrigation — compared, then built — and new materials can be made, like **bioplastic** from milk and vinegar.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Why do the Punjab books put making things at the end of every science book?" Answer from the lesson: Because technology is where the science is used: each book's closing Technology in Everyday Life unit turns the year's science into things learners design, build and test — and into tools that keep people healthy.

## Do It Yourself
1. Make one thing from the Grade 4 chapter: a paper bag or card by folding, cutting and pasting, or a clay model of a solid with wheels or gears. Write what you designed it to do.
2. List the contents a first-aid box should hold, from the lesson — and check a real box at home or school against your list.
3. Design (on paper, or build with help) one Sun cooker or one sprinkler: draw it, label where the energy or water goes, and say how you would test whether it works.
