# Laboratory safety — hazard signs and protective equipment

## Do it first
Ten signs. No words under them yet. A flame. A skull. A hand being eaten away. A bolt of lightning. Before you read the key: guess each sign's meaning from its picture alone, and write your guesses. Signs exist to be read at a glance, by a stranger, in a hurry — test whether these ten pass that test on you.

## Ten signs, ten meanings
The ten signs and their meanings: **corrosive**; **no open flames**; **electrical hazard**; **oxidising agent**; **flammable/combustible**; **harmful irritant**; **poison/toxic**; **flammable gas**; **explosive**; **high voltage**. Learn them as sight-words: the sign must be known before the bottle is touched.

## Protective equipment — and its limit
Protective equipment is matched by sight the same way: **safety glasses** guard the eyes, **gloves** guard the hands, a **face shield** guards the whole face, and the **fire extinguisher** fights a young fire. Hold the limit clearly: **PPE reduces harm — it does not remove the hazard.** Gloves on a careless hand still meet the corrosive bottle; the rules from the last lesson stand first, and protection stands second.

## Say it to your companion
Show your companion the signs without their labels: they name the meanings. Then the four equipment items and their jobs. Finish with the limit sentence, word-perfect.

## Do It Yourself
1. Sign hunt: find or draw the signs your school actually needs (store room, kitchen, generator, science room). Which of the ten are missing where they belong?
2. Gear check: for three bench dangers (splash, flame, sharp edge), name the equipment that guards each — and what it cannot do.
3. Teach a sign: pick the hardest sign and teach it to a younger pupil with a one-line story they will remember.
