# Lesson plan — Two kinds of knowledge — conventional science and indigenous knowledge

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Science Skills · **Lesson:** Two kinds of knowledge — conventional science and indigenous knowledge
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — Basic Concepts of Science (Grade 7 General Science).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** there are two great kinds of knowledge: conventional knowledge — tentative, evidence-tested, written down and formally taught — and indigenous knowledge — built by experience over generations, carried by word of mouth, and woven into a culture
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on What science is — the learner knows knowledge rests on evidence; now a second, older way of knowing is set beside it honestly

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Conventional knowledge** — the hospital route: tentative, evidence-supported, documented, formally taught
2. **Indigenous knowledge — and the catalogue project** — the elder's route: experience-built, oral, culture-embedded; Ethiopian examples; the family catalogue project

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Science Skills — What science is. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Tell the two malaria stories apart by the route each cure took.
2. List how conventional knowledge is kept: tested, documented, formally taught.
3. List how indigenous knowledge is kept: experience-built, oral, culture-embedded.
4. Name Ethiopian indigenous knowledge: traditional medicine, alcohol production, food drying, weaving, terracing, crop rotation, water conservation.
5. Carry out the family catalogue project with local names, benefits and weaknesses.

## What you need
Paper and pencil; an elder or parent to interview for the project.

## If the learner is stuck
- Calls indigenous knowledge "not knowledge" → it is experience-built and long-tested in its place; the lesson's contrast is in how it is kept (oral, culture-embedded) — not in whether it works.
- Calls conventional knowledge "always right" → it is tentative by design; that openness is its strength, not its shame.
