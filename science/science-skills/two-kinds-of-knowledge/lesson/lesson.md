# Two kinds of knowledge — conventional science and indigenous knowledge

## Do it first
Two children, one illness — malaria. The first is taken to a hospital: tests are done, the sickness is named, medicine is given, and she recovers. The second is taken to an experienced elder, who prepares a medicine from leaves, and he is relieved. Both children are well. Now choose: which route do you trust — and what is your reason? Hold your answer. This lesson names the two kinds of knowledge behind the two routes.

## Sub lesson 1 — Conventional knowledge
Conventional knowledge is the knowledge of the school and the laboratory. It is **tentative** — open to correction; **supported by evidence** — observation and inference, tested; **systematically documented** — written down so anyone can check it; and **formally taught** — passed on in schools, in order. The hospital route runs on it: diagnosis first, then the medicine the diagnosis names.

## Sub lesson 2 — Indigenous knowledge — and the catalogue project
Indigenous knowledge (the teacher guide's own term is **indigenous science**) is built by **experience over long time**, carried **unwritten, by word of mouth**, **embedded in a culture** and fitted to **one society and its place** — long-term wisdom, learned also through informal experiment and intimate understanding of the local environment. Ethiopia's own examples: **traditional medicine, traditional alcohol production, food drying, weaving, terracing and crop rotation, traditional water conservation** — and, in the guide's worked applications, food preservation, drug extraction from medicinal plants, water purification and environmental conservation. Neither kind is mocked in this lesson: they differ in how knowledge is kept and tested.

**The project:** ask your parents and elders. Catalogue the indigenous knowledge of your own home — each item with its **local name**, its **benefit** and its **weakness** — and report it to the class.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the two stories, then the two kinds — how each is built, how each is kept, how each is passed on — then two Ethiopian examples of indigenous knowledge from your own area.

## Do It Yourself
1. The catalogue: complete the project — at least four items, each with local name, benefit and weakness. Present one item fully.
2. Same problem, two kinds: pick one home problem (keeping food, healing a cut, saving water). Write how conventional knowledge answers it and how indigenous knowledge answers it.
3. Fair judge: write two sentences a fair person could say about BOTH kinds without mocking either.
