# Laboratory safety — the ten rules

## Do it first
Before any rule is read: take a floor plan of this room and hunt. Where is the water? The door? Anything that could burn, splash, cut or poison? Mark every danger you can find — and every thing that could protect someone. Your map comes first. The rules come second, and you will see each rule standing guard over a danger you already found.

## The ten rules
Laboratories hold **hazardous, poisonous, flammable and explosive** materials. Most accidents come from **carelessness, impatience and unauthorised experiments** — which is why the rules begin there. The ten rules: (1) No unauthorised experiments, and never work alone. (2) A splashed eye is flooded with water **at once**. (3) Confine long hair and loose clothing. (4) Wear proper clothing. (5) No eating or drinking in the laboratory. (6) Never point a test-tube mouth at anyone. (7) Never pour water into concentrated acid. (8) Use waste containers properly. (9) Report **every** accident at once. (10) Clean spills immediately.

## Say it to your companion
Recite the ten rules to your companion in order, and for three of them name the accident the rule prevents.

## Do It Yourself
1. Floor-plan audit: finish the book's task — your room's plan with every safety item marked (water, door, extinguisher if any, waste container). Note what is missing.
2. Rule-to-accident matching: write the ten rules on slips; a partner names accidents, you raise the rule that prevents each.
3. Acid rule spoken: explain rule 7 to a younger pupil in two sentences — what never to do, and what the safe order is (acid into water, never water into acid — as your teacher demonstrates).
