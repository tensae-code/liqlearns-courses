# Lesson plan — Measuring: temperature, thermometers and the Celsius scale

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Science Skills · **Lesson:** Measuring: temperature, thermometers and the Celsius scale
**Place in the curriculum:** Science Skills — measuring, the carried Grade 4 set (CURRICULUM.md: the G4 "Measuring — …" placements land with Band C under Science Skills).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** temperature is the measure of a body's hotness or coldness and it decides the body's state; it is measured with a thermometer; the world unit is the kelvin and the everyday unit degrees Celsius — 0 °C = 273 K, 100 °C = 373 K — and at sea level water freezes at 0 °C and boils at 100 °C
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the states of matter — melting and boiling were met there; now their measure gets its scale.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Science Skills — Living and non-living things. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define temperature: the measure of a body's hotness or coldness — and say it decides a body's state.
2. Name the instrument: the thermometer.
3. Use the two units and the fixed points: kelvin (world unit) and degrees Celsius (everyday); 0 °C = 273 K; 100 °C = 373 K; water freezes at 0 °C and boils at 100 °C at sea level.

## What you need
A thermometer to hold and read; ice and a kettle (an adult heats the water).

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks a big fire and a small fire at the same temperature differ in temperature → temperature is hotness, not amount of heat-stuff; the thermometer reads the same for both at the same hotness.
- Forgets 'at sea level' → the fixed points are stated for sea level; high places boil water cooler, as earlier lessons met.
