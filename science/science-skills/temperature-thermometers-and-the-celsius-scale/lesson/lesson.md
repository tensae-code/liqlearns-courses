# Measuring: temperature, thermometers and the Celsius scale

## Do it first
Your hands know hot and cold — but whose hands agree on 'warm'? Temperature is hotness turned into a number everyone can share. This lesson gives that number its instrument, its two units, and its two fixed points on water.

## Temperature and its instrument
**Temperature is the measure of a body's hotness or coldness** — and it is no small thing: temperature **decides the body's state**, whether water stands as ice, flows as water, or flies as steam. It is measured with a **thermometer**: as the body warms, the thermometer's column climbs.

## Two units and the fixed points
Temperature has two units in the book. The **world unit is the kelvin (K)**; the **everyday unit is degrees Celsius (°C)**. They join at the fixed points: **0 °C = 273 K** and **100 °C = 373 K**. And the points themselves are water's, **at sea level**: water **melts and freezes at 0 °C** and **boils at 100 °C**.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: what temperature measures, the instrument, the two units with their joining numbers, and water's two fixed points at sea level.

## Do It Yourself
1. Read the room: take the thermometer's reading in shade and in sun, morning and afternoon. Write all four in °C.
2. Ice point check: stand the thermometer in melting ice (an adult helps). It should rest at 0 °C — the book's first fixed point, in your own hand.
3. Kelvin sums: a day of 27 °C is how many kelvin? (27 + 273 = 300 K.) And boiling water, 100 °C? (373 K.)
