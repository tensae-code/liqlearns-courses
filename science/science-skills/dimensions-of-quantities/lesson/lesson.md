# Dimensions of quantities

## Do it first

## Sub lesson 1 — A quantity has a dimension

Every measured quantity — length, mass, time, and everything built from them — has a **dimension**: the kind of thing it is, underneath whatever unit it is measured in. A length is a length whether it is read in metres or in centimetres; the unit changes, the dimension does not. Indonesia's Grade 10 book introduces **quantities, units and dimensions** together (Bab 1, Pengukuran dalam Kerja Ilmiah — measurement in scientific work), because the three belong together from the start.

## Sub lesson 2 — Working the dimension out from the unit

The book's method is direct: the **dimension of a quantity is worked out from its unit**. Take the quantity's unit, and ask what it is built from — a unit that is itself made of metres, kilograms or seconds shows, in its own make-up, the dimension of the quantity it measures. The unit is the working handle; the dimension is what the working finds.

## Sub lesson 3 — Derived quantities carry derived dimensions

A **derived quantity** — one built from other quantities, as speed is built from length and time — carries a **derived dimension**, worked out the same way: from its unit, which is itself built from the units of the quantities underneath. Base quantities give base dimensions; derived quantities give dimensions built from them, and the book's chapter works them in that order.
