# Lesson plan — Fair tests — variables and control groups

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Science Skills · **Lesson:** Fair tests — variables and control groups
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — Basics of Scientific Investigation (Grade 8 General Science).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a test is fair only when one thing changes, one thing is measured, and everything else is held still — with a control group that gets no treatment as the comparison baseline
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The scientific method and the semi-guided investigations; now the fairness machinery inside every test is taken apart and named

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **The three variables** — the independent variable you change, the dependent variable you measure, and the controlled variables you hold constant
2. **One change, and the control group** — the control group receives no treatment; redesign rigged tests until only one thing differs

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Science Skills — Semi-guided investigations — your question, your method. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the independent, dependent and controlled variables in a given investigation.
2. Say why only one independent variable may change at a time.
3. Explain what a control group is and why a test without one cannot be sure of cause and effect.
4. Redesign a rigged test — two things changed at once — into a fair one.

## What you need
Two plant pots, the same soil and seeds, water, and fertiliser for the worked comparison; paper and pencil for the redesign tasks.

## If the learner is stuck
- Changes two things “to be quicker” → then no result can say which change did it; a quick test that cannot answer is slower than a fair one.
- Thinks the control group is the one treated best → the control group is the untreated baseline; it exists so the treated group has something honest to be compared against.
