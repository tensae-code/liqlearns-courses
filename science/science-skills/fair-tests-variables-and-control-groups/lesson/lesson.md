# Fair tests — variables and control groups

## Do it first
A pupil wants to know whether fertiliser helps beans grow. She plants one pot with fertiliser in the sunny window and gives it plenty of water — and another pot without fertiliser in a dark corner, barely watered. The first pot grows taller. Has she proven fertiliser works? List everything that differed between her pots before any names are given. Keep the list — it is the whole lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — The three variables
Every investigation juggles three kinds of variable. The **independent variable** is the condition the scientist deliberately changes — the dose of a medicine, the temperature, the sunlight. The **dependent variable** is what is observed and measured — the blood pressure, the growth, the colour; it depends on the change. The **controlled variables** are everything held constant — same soil, same water, same light — so they cannot confuse the result. In the medicine case: dose changed (independent), blood pressure measured (dependent), and every other condition of the patients kept the same (controlled).

## Sub lesson 2 — One change, and the control group
A **fair test** changes only the independent variable, measures the dependent variable, and holds everything else constant. To study the change, the scientist uses a **control group**: subjects treated exactly like the experimental group except that the independent variable is not applied to them — the pot with no fertiliser, grown in the same light with the same water. Without a control, you cannot be sure the result came from your variable and not from something else. Now repair the Do-it-first test: both pots in the same light, the same water, the same soil — fertiliser in one only. Only then can the height difference speak, and say what it says about fertiliser alone.

## Say it to your companion
Take the fertiliser investigation and name all three variables for your companion, then describe the control pot exactly — and say what a height difference would, and would not, prove if the light had also differed.

## Do It Yourself
1. Variable hunt: for “Does temperature affect how fast salt dissolves?” — name the independent variable, the dependent variable, and three things you would hold constant.
2. Rigged-test repair: a pupil tests two seeds by giving seed A more water AND more light than seed B. Rewrite the test so it is fair, line by line.
3. Control design: design the control group for a test of whether a new feed makes chickens grow faster. State exactly what the control chickens get, and what they do not.
