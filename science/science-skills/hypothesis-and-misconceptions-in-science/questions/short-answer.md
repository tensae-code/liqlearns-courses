# Hypothesis — and misconceptions in science — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Explain, in your own words: What a hypothesis is.
**Answer:** Papua New Guinea's Standards Based Grade 9 guide defines the **hypothesis** inside its Science as Inquiry strand: a proposed explanation, stated so that it can be tested — the idea an investigation is built to check. A hypothesis is not a guess thrown away; it is the sentence the experiment judges.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Explain, in your own words: What a misconception is.
**Answer:** Against it, the guide sets the **misconception**: a wrong idea a learner already holds, which feels true but does not match the evidence. Misconceptions matter in science classes because a learner filters new teaching through them — naming them is the first step to correcting them.

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Explain, in your own words: Comparing them — and their applications.
**Answer:** The guide closes by **comparing** the two and their applications: a hypothesis is put forward deliberately to be tested; a misconception is already there, untested, and must be found before it can be replaced. Both are ideas about how things work — the difference is whether they have been, and will be, put to the test.

**4.** (Whole lesson) Retell the whole lesson in its own order, one sentence per sub lesson.
**Answer:** What a hypothesis is → What a misconception is → Comparing them — and their applications — in that order, as taught in lesson/lesson.md.
