# Applied measurement problems in the school garden

## Do it first
Before you read: say out loud what you already think this lesson's one thing is — applied measurement problems in the school garden. Then read on: this is Seychelles' way in — the National Institute of Pedagogy's Integrated Science unit booklets (Unit 1).

## Sub lesson 1 — Measurement problems in the school agriculture project

Seychelles' Unit 1 Project 1, "Using what we have learnt", sends pupils back into the school agriculture project with the measuring skills of the unit: finding the volume of a watering can, weighing a fruit without picking it, and working out how much water seed beds need. The problems are real ones from the garden, and each one is solved by choosing the right measure — length, weight, volume or time — and the right instrument for it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Solving them with what you have learnt

The project's way is the lesson: first say what must be found, then guess, then measure, then check whether the answer makes sense in the garden — a watering can that "holds" more than a bucket has been measured wrong. Measuring is not finished until the number is used: water is carried, seed beds are watered, and fruit is weighed while it still hangs on the plant.
