Today: **Engineering design — bridges and loads** — Egypt's way, from its ministry books.

**Design a bridge to get to the other side.** Egypt's Primary 4 book sets the problem as a project: **design a bridge to get to the other side**. The designer starts from the crossing itself — how wide the gap is, what the bridge must carry, and what materials are at hand — and sketches a bridge shape that answers those needs before anything is built. The design step is the science: a bridge is a structure built to carry loads across a gap, and every choice in the drawing — where supports stand, how the deck is held — is a choice about how the load will travel through the structure to the ground.

**Test the bridge under load.** A design is only a claim until it is tested. The project's second step is to **build the bridge and test it under load**: weights are added step by step, and the testers watch where the bridge bends, slips or fails. What the test shows decides what changes — a support moved, a deck strengthened — and the bridge is tested again. Egypt's book teaches engineering the honest way: design, test with a real load, and let the test result improve the design.
