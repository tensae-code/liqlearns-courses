# The branches of science

## Do it first
Sort these six studies before you read on: (a) how a sorghum plant grows; (b) why a metal spoon heats up in tea; (c) how volcanoes form; (d) what acids do to metals; (e) how fish breathe; (f) what rocks lie under your town. Three piles hide in the list. Find them.

## The three branches
Science branches by its subject. **Life science** studies living things — plants, animals, the human body. **Physical science** studies non-living things, and splits in two: **physics** (matter, energy, motion, light, heat) and **chemistry** (what substances are and how they change). **Earth science** studies the Earth itself — its structure, its properties and its processes: rocks, volcanoes, weather, the oceans' floor.

## The wider split (teacher guide — evidence)
Above the three branches sits a wider split. **Natural science** studies nature and its laws — biology, chemistry, physics, geology, astronomy. **Social science** studies people and how they live together. Some subjects sit on the borders: **physical chemistry** joins physics and chemistry; **biophysics** studies physical processes in living things; **biochemistry** studies chemical processes in and relating to living organisms. A three-pupils-one-bucket test sorts one bucket of water three ways: the microbes in it belong to biology, the force and light on it to physics, its tastes, odours and dissolving to chemistry.

## Say it to your companion
Draw the branching map from memory for your companion — science, its two great divisions, its three branches, physics and chemistry under physical science — then place the three border subjects on the joins.

## Do It Yourself
1. Branch hunt at home: list six things studied or done at home (cooking, farming, health, building, weather-watching, trading) and sort each into a branch — or onto a border.
2. One bucket, three sciences: take the bucket test — write one biology question, one physics question and one chemistry question about the same bucket of water.
3. Border check: explain in two sentences why biochemistry cannot sit inside one branch alone.
