# Lesson plan — Laboratory equipment — names, uses, and making your own

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Science Skills · **Lesson:** Laboratory equipment — names, uses, and making your own
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — Basic Concepts of Science (Grade 7 General Science).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** thirteen common tools, each with its job — and when the school has no kit, the same tools are made from local materials, because junior-school science does not need expensive apparatus
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the two laboratory-safety lessons — the learner works safely; now they name, choose and even make the tools

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Thirteen tools and their jobs** — the named bench, and the evaporation task: choose kit, name hazards, state precautions
2. **Making your own** — the six-tool project from local materials

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Science Skills — Hazard signs and protective equipment. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Match thirteen tools to their jobs by sight and name.
2. Run the evaporation task: choose the kit, name its hazards, state the precautions.
3. Build the six home-made tools: test-tube holder, alcohol burner, test-tube rack, beaker, wire gauze, funnel.

## What you need
The school kit where it exists; local materials for the making project — wood, wire, tins, bottles, cloth.

## If the learner is stuck
- Names without jobs → every name must carry its job in the same breath: "funnel — guides pouring." A name alone is half learned.
- Treats home-made kit as toy kit → the same safety rules bind it; a home-made burner burns exactly like a bought one.
