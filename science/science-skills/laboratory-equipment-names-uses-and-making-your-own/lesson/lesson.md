# Laboratory equipment — names, uses, and making your own

## Do it first
Your task: evaporate the water from a salty solution and win the salt back. On the bench: thirteen tools, most of them strangers. Which do you reach for? Which do you leave? Choose now, on paper, with reasons — then learn every tool's name and job, and judge your choices. And if your school had NONE of these tools? The lesson's second half answers that too.

## Sub lesson 1 — Thirteen tools and their jobs
The bench, named: **test-tube rack** (holds tubes upright), **test-tube holder** (grips a hot tube), **watch glass** (a shallow dish for small amounts), **balance** (measures mass), **funnel** (guides pouring and filtering), **beaker and flask** (hold and mix liquids), **heat sources** (burners for heating), **dropper** (adds liquid drop by drop), **test tubes** (hold small samples for tests), **tongs** (grip hot or distant things), **wire gauze** (spreads a flame's heat under a vessel), **microscope** (magnifies the too-small), **magnifier** (a hand lens for a closer look).

**The evaporation task, judged:** choose the kit (a vessel to hold the solution, a heat source, gauze to spread the heat, tongs or a holder for the hot vessel), name its hazards (flame, hot vessel, spitting salt) and state your precautions before any heating begins.

## Sub lesson 2 — Making your own
**The project:** build, from locally available materials, a **test-tube holder, an alcohol burner, a test-tube rack, a beaker, a wire gauze and a funnel**. The principle is the lesson's pride: junior-school science does **not** need expensive apparatus — it needs the right tool shapes, honestly made, and used with the same safety rules as bought kit.

## Say it to your companion
Name the thirteen tools to your companion with a job for each, then defend your evaporation kit — tool by tool, hazard by hazard, precaution by precaution.

## Do It Yourself
1. Make two: build any two of the six project tools from local materials and test them (cold water only) against the bought versions if the school has them.
2. Kit judge: for three tasks (filter muddy water; warm a tube of water; weigh a stone), name the tools, hazards and precautions for each in a table.
3. Bench sketch: draw your ideal bench for the evaporation task, every tool labelled, safety equipment included.
