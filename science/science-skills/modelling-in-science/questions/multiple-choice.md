# Modelling in science — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) A model is…
a) An exact copy in every feature  b) A stand-in for something real, built to be studied in its place  c) A guess with no likeness  d) Only a toy
**Answer: b) A stand-in for something real, built to be studied in its place**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) A model works by…
a) Being the same size  b) Being expensive  c) Similarity — likeness in the features that matter  d) Hiding the real thing
**Answer: c) Similarity — likeness in the features that matter**

**3.** (Sub lesson 1) A globe keeps the Earth's…
a) Exact size  b) Weather  c) Oceans' water  d) Shape-relations, and drops its size
**Answer: d) Shape-relations, and drops its size**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) Modelling is filed by Armenia's book among…
a) The forms of scientific cognition  b) Drawing exercises  c) Games  d) Laboratory furniture
**Answer: a) The forms of scientific cognition**

**5.** (Sub lesson 2) Science models things that are…
a) Already perfectly known  b) Too big, too small, too far, too slow or too dangerous to study directly  c) Very cheap  d) Nearby and safe only
**Answer: b) Too big, too small, too far, too slow or too dangerous to study directly**

**6.** (Sub lesson 2) Ideas are tested on a model so that…
a) The real thing is never needed again  b) Models replace experiments everywhere  c) They can be checked before being trusted about the real thing  d) Answers come faster than thought
**Answer: c) They can be checked before being trusted about the real thing**
