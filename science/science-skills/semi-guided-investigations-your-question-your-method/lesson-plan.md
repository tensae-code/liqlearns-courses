# Lesson plan — Semi-guided investigations — your question, your method

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Science Skills · **Lesson:** Semi-guided investigations — your question, your method
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — Basics of Scientific Investigation (Grade 8 General Science).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** in a semi-guided investigation the question is given but the method is yours — and the mark of real inquiry is revising a hypothesis when the evidence refuses it
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on The scientific method — observe, hypothesise, test, conclude; now the scaffold is the guide's six steps and the learner designs the middle of the investigation themselves

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **The six-step scaffold, and the bean case (teacher guide — evidence)** — question, background research, hypothesis (null and alternative), test, analysis, communication — worked on sunlight and bean growth
2. **The coiled nail — a hypothesis revised** — a plain coiled nail does nothing; a current-carrying one acts as a magnet
3. **The leaf starch test** — the nine-step bench procedure and how both iodine outcomes are read
4. **The candle under covers — is air necessary for burning?** — covers of different sizes, timed burns, one conclusion from evidence

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Science Skills — The scientific method — observe, hypothesise, test, conclude. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what makes an investigation semi-guided: whose question, whose method.
2. State null and alternative hypotheses for the bean case.
3. Explain why the nail investigation needed a second hypothesis, and what confirmed it.
4. Run the leaf starch test in order and read both possible iodine outcomes.
5. Predict, then test, how cover size changes a candle's burning time — and conclude from the timings.

## What you need
Copper wire, a nail, batteries, paper clips or iron filings, a compass if available; a green leaf, water, ethanol, a heat source, forceps, a white tile, iodine solution; candles and covers (glasses/jars) of different sizes, matches, a timer.

## If the learner is stuck
- Waits to be told the method → that waiting is the recipe level; here the question is given and designing the method is the learner's job — start from what must be measured and what must be held still.
- Treats the failed first nail hypothesis as a mistake to hide → it is the lesson's centre: report it, revise it, and let the second test decide.
