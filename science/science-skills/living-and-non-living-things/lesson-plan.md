# Lesson plan — Living and non-living things

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Science Skills · **Lesson:** Living and non-living things
**Place in the curriculum:** Science Skills — the sorting craft, inside the Grade 1 guide's natural-environment unit (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** living things have the characteristics of life; we sort the things around us into living and non-living — goat, tree, child against stone, river, chair
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Plants and Animals — the living kinds are known one by one; this lesson teaches the test that gathers them. It rests on teacher-guide evidence only; its sources say so plainly.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the characteristics of living things as the guide teaches them, and sort with them.
2. Sort the guide's grid — goat, tree, stone, river, child, chair — into living and non-living without error.
3. Defend the hard cases: a tree is living though it does not walk; a river moves but is not living.

## What you need
Six things or pictures to sort: goat (or its picture), tree, stone, river (or water), child, chair — the guide's own grid.

## If the learner is stuck
- Sorts by movement alone → the river, the bicycle and the cloud all move and none is living; the tree does not walk and is living. Movement is not the test.
- Calls a seed dead matter → it is a living thing at rest; the sprouting proves the characteristics were inside it.
