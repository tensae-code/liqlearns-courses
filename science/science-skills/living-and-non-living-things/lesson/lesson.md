# Living and non-living things

## Do it first
A goat, a tree, a stone, a river, a child, a chair. Two piles. The stone is easy. But the tree never walks, and the river never stops moving — so what makes a thing ALIVE? This lesson teaches the test the Grade 1 guide sets: the characteristics of living things.

## The characteristics, and the great sort

The Grade 1 guide's §3.2 teaches living things by their **characteristics** — the life-signs by which we know them: living things **grow**, they **feed**, they **breathe**, they **move themselves**, they **respond** to the world, and they **make young of their kind**. A thing with these signs is **living**; a thing without them is **non-living**, however much it shines or moves.

Then the sort, with the guide's own grid: **goat, tree, stone, river, child, chair**. Goat — living. Tree — living: it grows, feeds, breathes and seeds, though it never takes a step. Child — living. Stone — non-living. River — non-living: it moves, but it does not grow, feed or make young rivers. Chair — non-living, though it was wood once: the making ended the life.

Hold the characteristics as a checklist and the hard cases open: ask of anything — does it grow? feed? breathe? make young? The answers, not the looks, decide.

## Say it to your companion
Deal your companion six new things — a hen, a seed, a cloud, a bicycle, a fish, a pot. They sort and defend; you probe the hard ones: 'it moves — is it living?' The characteristics are their only allowed answers.

## Do It Yourself
1. Sort twenty things from your compound into the two piles, in writing. Star the three hardest and write their defences.
2. Seed watch: plant a bean and log the day it sprouts — the characteristic announcing itself. A stone beside it is your control.
3. Movement trap list: list five non-living things that move (river, cloud, bicycle, fire, wind-blown leaf) and say, for each, which characteristic it lacks.
