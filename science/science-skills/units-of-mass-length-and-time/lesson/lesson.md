# Measuring: units of mass, length and time

## Do it first
A metre is not just a stick — it is a family: centimetres and millimetres inside it, kilometres above it. Every standard unit has its family, and this lesson learns the families of mass, length and time by heart — then puts them to work.

## The relations, by heart
**Mass:** 1 kilogram = **1000 grams** (1 kg = 1000 g). **Length:** 1 metre = **100 cm** = **1000 mm**; and 1 kilometre = **1000 m**. **Time:** 1 day = **24 hours**; 1 hour = **60 minutes**; 1 minute = **60 seconds**. These relations never change — that is what makes the units standard, the same in every town and every country.

## Measure and compare — the class activity
The book sets the class to work: **measure and compare** — pupils' **mass**, their **height**, and **the time each takes to walk 50 metres**. Length-measuring practice runs beside it. Measuring is not reading numbers off a page; it is doing these three, carefully, and comparing the results fairly because everyone used the same units.

## Standard units (teacher guide — evidence)
The Grade 5 teacher guide carries the same teaching one grade up (teacher guide — evidence): people once measured length with the **span (ስንዝር)**, the **cubit (ክንድ)** and the **step (እርምጃ)**, but these are not fully accurate because they differ from place to place and person to person; the modern method uses the world's standard units, which are exact — and pupils do their own measuring tasks with the units.

## Say it to your companion
Recite the families to your companion — mass, length, time — each with its relations, then tell them the three things the class activity measures.

## Do It Yourself
1. Height in two units: measure your height in metres and centimetres. Write both. Which number is bigger — and why does the count grow when the unit shrinks?
2. The 50-metre walk: time three walkers over 50 m, as the book's activity does. Rank them. Were the units the same for all three?
3. Kitchen kilograms: find things at home of about 1 kg. How many grams is a half-kilogram packet? (500 g — half of 1000.)
