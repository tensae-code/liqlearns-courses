# Scientific theories and laws — how explanations differ from descriptions

## Do it first
Is a scientific theory just a guess? And is a law the same thing as a theory? Say your answers out loud. Then read on: this is Sudan's way in — its Grade 8 Natural Sciences sets scientific theories and laws side by side in its Nature of Science unit, and tests the comparison in the unit assessment.

## Sub lesson 1 — A scientific theory — a tried explanation

A **scientific theory** is a **tried explanation of a pattern noticed again and again in the natural world**. It is **not a mere guess**: it rests on the pattern, tested — and it stays **open to change** when new information comes, like every answer of science in this unit.

## Sub lesson 2 — Scientific laws — set against theories

The unit sets **scientific laws against theories** and its assessment asks the learner to **compare the two** — a theory explains; a law describes. The lesson's own title carries the split: explanations differ from descriptions, and a theory belongs on the explanation side.

## Sub lesson 3 — Can a theory change?

The assessment's second question is the unit's own test: **can a theory change?** The book's answer is in the theory's definition — a tried explanation, open to change. A theory that could never change would not be science's kind of explanation at all.
