# Lesson plan — Measurement — quantities, units, prefixes, accuracy and precision (Grade 8)

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Science Skills · **Lesson:** Measurement — quantities, units, prefixes, accuracy and precision (Grade 8)
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — Basics of Scientific Investigation (Grade 8 General Science) — guide §1.1, a section the Grade 8 student book does not contain.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** measurement rests on seven fundamental quantities, prefixes stretch and shrink their units by tens — and every set of readings must pass two different judgements: accuracy against the true value, precision against itself (teacher guide — evidence)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on teacher-guide evidence only (acceptance count 0): it complements the Grade 7 measuring lessons — units, prefixes and accuracy — rather than repeating them; the seven-quantity frame and the conversion drill are its new load

## Sub lessons
1. **Indigenous or modern? Quantities and their units (teacher guide — evidence)** — indigenous measures are free and at hand but vary person to person; modern SI measures cost instruments and hold worldwide; seven fundamental quantities, the rest derived
2. **Prefixes and conversions (teacher guide — evidence)** — the prefix symbols for large and small values, drilled through the guide's worked conversions
3. **Accuracy and precision (teacher guide — evidence)** — the dart player's two virtues, and the verdict on readings averaging 12.17 m/s² against the accepted 9.81 m/s²

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Science Skills — Measurement — accuracy and precision. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Sort length, mass, time, temperature, area, volume, density and force into fundamental and derived.
2. Give the SI unit and correct symbol for length, mass, time and temperature.
3. Convert across prefixes: kilometres to metres and centimetres, grams to kilograms, 0.0012 s to milliseconds.
4. Judge a set of readings twice — accurate? precise? — and defend both verdicts.

## What you need
Paper and pencil; a ruler, a balance and a clock if the class has them, for the comparison work.

## If the learner is stuck
- Converts 0.0012 s to microseconds → count the decimal steps: 0.0012 s is 1.2 thousandths of a second — milliseconds. (The guide's own key gets this wrong; the lesson teaches the corrected value — see sources.)
- Writes the mass unit's symbol as “m” → m is the metre; the kilogram's symbol is kg. (Also a corrected guide-key error — see sources.)
