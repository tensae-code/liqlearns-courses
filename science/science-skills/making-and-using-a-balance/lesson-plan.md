# Lesson plan — Measuring: making and using a balance

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Science Skills · **Lesson:** Measuring: making and using a balance
**Place in the curriculum:** Science Skills — measuring, the carried Grade 4 set (CURRICULUM.md: the G4 "Measuring — …" placements land with Band C under Science Skills).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a working balance can be built from two shoe-polish tins, string and wood — and weighing a water-filled bottle against an air-filled one makes mass felt in the hands: mass is the amount of matter
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Measuring: traditional and modern measures — the balance was the old mass instrument; now the learner builds one.

## Before this lesson

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Build the book's balance: two shoe-polish tins, string and a wooden beam/stand.
2. Use it to compare a water-filled bottle with an air-filled one of the same kind.
3. Say what the tipping teaches: the fuller bottle holds more matter — more mass.

## What you need
Two shoe-polish tins, string, a straight piece of wood for the beam and a stand; two matching bottles, one filled with water, one with air only.

## If the learner is stuck
- The balance tips with nothing on it → level it empty first: equal string lengths, beam centred on the stand.
- Thinks the bigger thing always wins → the bottles are the same size; the water one wins on matter, not size. Mass counts particles, not space.
