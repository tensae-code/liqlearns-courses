# Learning with analogies and models — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Explain, in your own words, the difference between an analogy and a model.
**Answer:** An analogy sets the new thing beside a known thing in words — this is like that; a model is a built stand-in for the real thing that can be handled and tested.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why does the chapter end with learner-made analogies and models?
**Answer:** Because an analogy or model a learner builds shows what the learner actually holds — the making is both a lesson and a check on the lesson.

**3.** (Whole lesson) Retell the whole lesson in its own order, one sentence per sub lesson.
**Answer:** Analogies teach the new through the known, worked through Focus-Action-Reflection and Synectics and ending in learner-made analogies → models are stand-ins built to be handled and tested, with role play beside them and learner-made models at the end — in that order, as taught in lesson/lesson.md.
