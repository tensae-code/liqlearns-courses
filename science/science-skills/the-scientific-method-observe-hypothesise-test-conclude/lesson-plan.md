# Lesson plan — The scientific method — observe, hypothesise, test, conclude

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Science Skills · **Lesson:** The scientific method — observe, hypothesise, test, conclude
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — Basics of Scientific Investigation (Grade 8 General Science).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** an investigation runs in a working sequence — observe, ask, hypothesise, test, analyse and conclude — and its results can harden into a law (a described pattern) or support a theory (an explanation)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Grade 7's What science is — the law/theory distinction is reused here, not retaught; now the learner runs the whole sequence on a real case

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Observe and ask** — qualitative observations (kinds and properties) against quantitative ones (numbers and units), and the sharp question an observation raises
2. **Hypothesise and test** — a hypothesis is a testable proposed answer; a controlled test is how it is tried
3. **Analyse, conclude — law or theory** — results follow the evidence; a law describes what happens, a theory explains why

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Science Skills — What science is — evidence, inference and tentative knowledge. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Tell a qualitative observation from a quantitative one, with an example of each.
2. State a testable hypothesis for a question raised by an observation.
3. Say what happens when a test's result goes against the hypothesis — and why that is not failure.
4. Tell a law from a theory: a law describes a pattern; a theory explains it.
5. Run the book's two-plants case through all the steps in order.

## What you need
Paper and pencil; two plants or a remembered garden case for the worked example.

## If the learner is stuck
- Treats a hypothesis as a guess → a hypothesis is built from prior knowledge and observation, and it must be testable; a guess need not be either.
- Thinks a refuted hypothesis means the work failed → the sequence loops back: restate the hypothesis and test again. The loop is the method working, not breaking.
