# Drawing biological specimens — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) How is a drawing different from a diagram?
**Answer: A drawing shows the one real specimen in front of you, as it truly is; a diagram shows a general plan or idea, simplified.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) Why must a drawing state its magnification?
**Answer: So the reader knows the specimen's true size — without it, a drawing bigger or smaller than life would mislead anyone studying from it.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) How are parts labelled on a biological drawing?
**Answer: The name is written beside the drawing and joined to the exact part it names by a ruled, straight label line.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) Name the three rules that make a biological drawing true.
**Answer: Proportion (true sizes against each other), magnification stated, and ruled label lines.**
