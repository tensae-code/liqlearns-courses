# Science and technology — how they differ and feed each other

## Do it first
Look around your home. The lamp, the phone, the jebena on its stove, the plough outside. Every one exists because somebody first **understood** something — and somebody else **built** with that understanding. Understanding and building are not the same job. Which of your home things came from understanding? Which from building? Where do the two meet?

## Sub lesson 1 — How they differ
**Science explores and discovers.** It asks what the world is and aims at **prediction** — and knowledge itself is its product. **Technology applies knowledge.** It **invents**, it **simplifies life**, it touches society directly — and for that reason it **can help or harm**. The guide's fuller contrast (teacher guide — evidence) runs to eight points: organised knowledge-gathering vs practical use of laws; exploring vs putting into practice; knowledge that changes only by addition vs tools that change rapidly; discovery vs invention; studying the structure and behaviour of the natural and physical world vs putting premises into practice; analysis, deduction and theory vs analysis and synthesis of design; prediction vs simplifying work and fulfilling needs.

## Sub lesson 2 — How they feed each other: the light bulb
Neither stands alone. Science's explanation makes a technology possible; the new technology then raises **new questions** for science. The light bulb is the worked case: many experimenters across the 1800s improved it piece by piece until **Edison, in 1879, made it practical** — science feeding invention, invention feeding science, for decades.

**The home survey:** list the technologies of your community, the problem each one solves, and the risk each one carries. A technology with no risk column filled is a survey half done.
