# Lesson plan — Science and technology — how they differ and feed each other

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Science Skills · **Lesson:** Science and technology — how they differ and feed each other
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — Basic Concepts of Science (Grade 7 General Science).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** science explores and discovers; technology applies knowledge and invents — each feeds the other, and technology, which touches society directly, can help or harm
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on What science is and The branches of science — the learner knows what science is; now its partner is set beside it

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **How they differ** — the contrast — exploring vs applying, prediction vs simplifying, knowledge vs invention — with the guide's eight points
2. **How they feed each other: the light bulb** — the reciprocal loop, the bulb's many-handed history to Edison 1879, and the home survey

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Science Skills — The branches of science. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the reciprocal loop: explanation → invention → new questions.
2. Contrast the two: science explores, predicts, seeks knowledge; technology applies, invents, simplifies life.
3. Tell the light bulb's many-handed history, with Edison (1879) making it practical.
4. Run the home survey: community technologies, the problems they solve, their risks.
5. Use the guide's fuller eight-point contrast (teacher guide — evidence).

## What you need
Paper and pencil; a walk through home or neighbourhood for the survey.

## If the learner is stuck
- Uses the words as one word → science's product is knowledge; technology's product is a working thing. Ask: "Is the product an explanation, or a device?"
- Thinks technology is always good → it touches society directly; the survey's risk column exists for that reason.
