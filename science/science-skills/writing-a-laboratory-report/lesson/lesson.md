# Writing a laboratory report

## Do it first
Here is a report with its parts shuffled: a conclusion with no aim above it, results before the materials, data with no title. Could another class repeat this experiment from it? Could a teacher check it? Shuffle is the enemy. This lesson gives the order that makes a report checkable — seven parts, every time, in the same sequence.

## Sub lesson 1 — The seven parts
The format, in order: (1) **Title** (and date) — what the experiment is. (2) **Aim / Objective** — what it sets out to find. (3) **Theory** — the science behind it. (4) **Materials and chemicals used** — everything the work needed. (5) **Data / Observation** — what was measured and seen, as it came. (6) **Result and Discussion** — what the data shows, read honestly. (7) **Conclusion** — the aim, answered.

[Picture 32117 — a model report page with the seven headings]

Three definitions ride with the format: an **experiment** is a planned activity using equipment, apparatus and chemicals; **observation** is the first step of the scientific method; a **hypothesis** is a new, untested theory.

## Sub lesson 2 — Writing it honestly
The report is where the ethics lesson becomes handwriting. Data goes in **as observed** — the honesty discipline applied to paper. Research ethics are keyed with the format: **protect vulnerable participants**, and serve **knowledge, truth and the avoidance of error**. A report that protects its people and reports its true data can be checked, repeated and trusted — which is the whole point of writing it.

## Say it to your companion
Recite the seven parts in order for your companion, give each part's job in five words or fewer, then the three definitions.

## Do It Yourself
1. Full write-up: take the last class experiment and write it in the seven parts, complete. Swap with a partner: could they repeat it from your page alone?
2. Shuffle test: cut a model report into its seven parts, shuffle, and race a partner to restore the order — then say why the order cannot change.
3. Ethics line: add one sentence to your write-up naming how participants were protected and how the data was kept honest.
