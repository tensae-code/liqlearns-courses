# Lesson plan — Writing a laboratory report

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Science Skills · **Lesson:** Writing a laboratory report
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — Basic Concepts of Science (Grade 7 General Science).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** an experiment is written up in seven parts — Title, Aim, Theory, Materials, Data/Observation, Result and Discussion, Conclusion — so that anyone can check what was done and what it showed
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Grade 7 practical lessons (safety, equipment) at reporting depth; its deeper partner at secondary depth is Chemistry — Writing a laboratory report, which this lesson does not replace

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **The seven parts** — Title, Aim, Theory, Materials, Data/Observation, Result and Discussion, Conclusion — with the three definitions
2. **Writing it honestly** — data as observed; research ethics — protect vulnerable participants, serve knowledge, truth and avoidance of error

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Science Skills — Laboratory equipment — names, uses, and making your own. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Recite the seven parts in order and the job of each.
2. Define the guide's three key words: experiment, observation, hypothesis.
3. Write up a real class experiment in the full seven-part format.
4. Carry research ethics into the write-up: protect vulnerable participants; serve knowledge, truth and the avoidance of error.

## What you need
A completed class experiment to write up; paper and pencil (or the report book).

## If the learner is stuck
- Writes results into the data section → Data is what came, untouched; Result and Discussion is what it means. Two sections exist precisely to keep those apart.
- Skips Theory as "extra" → theory tells the reader what the experiment tests; without it the numbers float.
