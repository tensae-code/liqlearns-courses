Today: **DNA as evidence — forensic identification** — the Dominican Republic's way: how DNA.

Inside the body's cells lies DNA — the long coded material that carries a living thing's instructions. The code is not identical in everyone: each person's DNA carries its own pattern, differing from person to person. That difference is what makes DNA useful as evidence. A pattern that differs between people can be used to tell people apart — the same way a fingerprint can, but read from the body's own code, from traces too small to see. Forensic identification is the science of identifying who was present, from the traces left behind. Where a trace carries DNA — the Grade 6 book's project reading turns on exactly this — scientists read the trace's DNA pattern and compare it with a person's pattern: a match identifies whose trace it is. In the book's project reading, that matching names a murderer: the evidence, not a guess, identifies the person. The lesson is carried here as that summary only — the point the book makes is the method: DNA differs, traces carry it, and a careful match is evidence strong enough to identify.
