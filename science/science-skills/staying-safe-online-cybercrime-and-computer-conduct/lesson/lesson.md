# Staying safe online — cybercrime and computer conduct

## Sub lesson 2 — The code of conduct for computers and the internet

The code of conduct for computers and the internet: never give personal details or share a password, never send anyone's photo without permission, never make a fake account in another's name, beware prize-and-luck messages that steal information, do not post photos showing you are away from home, and report a hacked account or threats to the Nepal Police Cyber Bureau (same proposed lesson as in the Nepal Grade 8 section)

## Sub lesson 3 — Checking a website before trusting it

Checking a website before trusting it: open the security certificate from the lock beside the address to see whether the site is verified and safe for personal details; beware gift, lottery and social-media links asking for details; sign out when finished (same proposed lesson as in the Nepal Grades 7 and 8 sections)

## Sub lesson 4 — Cyberbullying

Cyberbullying: using phones, computers and tablets to frighten or shame a person — publishing private details, threats, obscene messages, spreading rumours; staying safe (do not accept everyone as a friend, share no private details or secrets, protect your photos, and keep a record and tell a parent if it happens) (same proposed lesson)

## Sub lesson 5 — The digital footprint

The digital footprint: the trail of data left by websites visited, emails sent, forms submitted and likes, shares and comments made; how an IP address from the internet provider can trace a person, and how deleted posts stay in the platform's database; clearing browser history and cache, and never saving passwords on someone else's device (same proposed lesson)

## Sub lesson 6 — Firewall and antivirus

Firewall and antivirus: the firewall blocks harmful data and unauthorised signals from the network while letting safe data through, and must stay switched on; what a computer virus does (copying itself, opening programs by itself, deleting data, slowing the machine) and how antivirus software such as Windows Defender finds and removes viruses arriving by internet or pen drive (same proposed lesson)

## Sub lesson 7 — Cybercrime under Nepal's own law

Cybercrime under Nepal's own law (guide evidence; lesson first proposed in the Grade 7 student book above): cybercrime defined, including character assassination, spreading violence, identity misuse and bank fraud through stolen cards and accounts; Nepal's Electronic Transactions Act 2063 named, with Section 47's penalty as printed — a fine of up to one hundred thousand rupees, or up to five years' imprisonment, or both.
