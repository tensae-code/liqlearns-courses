# Order and chaos — entropy and self-organisation

## Do it first
Unmix a stirred drink. Unburn a match. You cannot — yet a seed, fed only sunlight and rain, builds the order of a tree. What rule allows both? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Armenia's way in — its Grade 10 Natural Science gives the puzzle its own section, Section 7: Order and Chaos.

## Sub lesson 1 — Reversible and irreversible processes

Armenia's section opens by sorting processes. A **reversible** process can run back along its path and restore the start. An **irreversible** process cannot: burning, mixing, cooling — once run, the way back is closed. Most of what happens in nature is irreversible, and that one-wayness needs a measure.

## Sub lesson 2 — Entropy — the measure of disorder

That measure is **entropy**: the measure of a system's disorder — its chaos against its orderliness. §7.2 sets orderliness and chaos side by side: left to itself, a closed system's entropy grows — order spreads out into disorder, and does not gather itself back.

## Sub lesson 3 — Self-organisation — order that builds itself

Yet §7.3 turns the picture: in **open systems** — systems with matter and energy flowing through them — ordered structures can arise by themselves: **self-organisation**. Its causes and conditions are the section's close: openness, and flow — order is not entropy's exception but its partner, built where energy passes through.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "What is entropy, and how can order ever increase if entropy grows?" Answer: Entropy measures disorder, and in a closed system it grows; but an open system, with energy flowing through, can build order in itself — self-organisation — while the wider whole pays the entropy price.
