# Robots and artificial intelligence — an introduction

## Do it first
A robot offers to do your homework. Which parts could it really do — and which parts would still be yours, and why? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Egypt's Primary 6 way in — a project story where children design a homework robot and debate what computers and AI can and cannot do (printed p. 116).

## Sub lesson 1 — Design a homework robot

Egypt's Primary 6 book introduces robots through a story-project: **children design a homework robot**. Designing it forces the first real questions — what jobs would the robot do, what would it need to sense, and what instructions would it follow? A **robot** is a machine built to carry out tasks; what it can do is bounded by what it was designed and instructed to do. The design step teaches that boundary from the inside: every ability in the drawing was put there by a designer, on purpose.

## Sub lesson 2 — What computers and AI can and cannot do — the debate

The story closes in a **debate: what computers and artificial intelligence can and cannot do**. Computers follow instructions at great speed and handle patterns and repetition tirelessly; what they cannot do is step outside what they were given — they do not understand a task the way the child who set it does. Egypt's own way is to end the project by arguing both sides from the design the learners just made: the robot they drew is the evidence for what machines can do, and its limits are drawn in the same picture.
