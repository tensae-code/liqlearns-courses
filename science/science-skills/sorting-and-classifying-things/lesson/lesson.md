# Sorting and classifying things

## Do it first
Before you read: think about this lesson's one thing — classifying — gathering things and putting similar ones together into groups, as Iraq's Grade 1–3 pupil's books teach it as one of the first science skills. Say what you already know about it out loud. Then read on: this is Iraq's way in — its Ministry of Education *Science* books (the Primary Science Book Series) teach this thing at the places named in this lesson's sources.

## Sub lesson 1 — Grouping similar things together

To **classify** is to group similar things together. Iraq's Grade 1 book opens the skill plainly: similar animals go in one group. The Grade 3 book says the same thing in the same order — first gather the things, then put the similar ones together.

A group is made by likeness: the things in it share something — a shape, a material, a way of living — and that shared thing is the group's reason. Things that do not share it go in a different group.

## Sub lesson 2 — Classifying materials — by magnet and by state

The Grade 2 book works the skill on materials twice. First, materials are classified by whether a **magnet attracts them**, into two groups — attracted, and not attracted. Then, as a skills enrichment, materials are classified **by their state** — solid, liquid or gas.

The test decides the group. Run the test honestly, and every material lands in the group the test gives it — that is classifying at work, not guessing.

## The key ideas

- To **classify** is to gather things and put similar ones together into groups.
- A group is made by a shared likeness — the things in it share the thing the group is about.
- A test can do the sorting: materials split into two groups by whether a magnet attracts them, and into groups by their state.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — Grouping similar things together first, then Classifying materials — by magnet and by state. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "What is classifying?" Answer: Classifying is gathering things and putting similar ones together — grouping them by a likeness they share, or by the answer a test gives.

## Do It Yourself
1. Take ten things from your home or school bag. Classify them into groups by a likeness you choose — say the likeness out loud before you start.
2. Classify the same ten things a second way: by their state, or by whether a magnet attracts them (if you have a magnet). Write both sorts down.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences — what a group is made by.
