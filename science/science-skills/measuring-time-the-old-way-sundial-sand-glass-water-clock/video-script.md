# Video script — Measuring time the old way — sundial, sand-glass, water clock

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

How do you tell time with no clock? Ask the **sun**.

Egypt, thirty-five centuries ago: a **sundial** splits daylight into **twelve parts**,
and a walking shadow does the telling.

Sand next. Two glass cones, one narrow neck.
The **sand-glass** measures the fall — and the neck and the sand decide how long.

Water is oldest of all. Marks on a pot, a steady flow —
but pressure leans on the flow, and the reading leans with it.

Three clever clocks. Three things that can **shift**.
That shifting is the whole lesson: a measure you cannot trust the same twice
is a measure science had to replace.
