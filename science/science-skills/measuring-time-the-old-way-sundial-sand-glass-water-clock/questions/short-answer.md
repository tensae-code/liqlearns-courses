# Measuring time the old way — sundial, sand-glass, water clock — short answer

Answer in a word or a short sentence.

**1. (Sub lesson 2)** Which old clock is the oldest of the three in this lesson, and where was it known?
**Answer:** The water clock — known in Babylon and Egypt around the 16th century B.C.

**2. (Sub lesson 3)** A trader's quntal sack settles an argument about a harvest's mass. Why might it fail to settle it?
**Answer:** Because one quntal is not strictly 100 kg — it can be less or more — so the measure itself is in dispute.

**3. (Sub lesson 1)** What two things split the sundial's day, besides the twelve parts?
**Answer:** The moving shadow also divided the day into two parts and marked summer against winter — any one of these, fully stated, earns the mark; both is the complete answer.
