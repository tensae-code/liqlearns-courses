# Measuring time the old way — sundial, sand-glass, water clock — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** The Egyptian sundials of about 1500 B.C. divided —
a) the night into twelve  b) the sunrise-to-sunset interval into twelve parts  c) the year into months  d) the hour into minutes
**Answer: b) the sunrise-to-sunset interval into twelve parts** — daylight itself was the thing divided

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** A sundial tells time by —
a) falling sand  b) the moving shadow falling on its marks  c) water levels  d) bells
**Answer: b) the moving shadow falling on its marks** — the shadow is the hand of this clock

**3. (Sub lesson 2)** The sand-glass's interval is set by —
a) the sun  b) the size and width of the glass and the quantity of sand  c) the season  d) the reader
**Answer: b) the size and width of the glass and the quantity of sand** — neck and sand fix how long the fall takes

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** The water clock's readings vary because —
a) sand runs out  b) the flow varies with the pressure of the water  c) shadows move  d) the marks fade
**Answer: b) the flow varies with the pressure of the water** — marks on the container are read against an unsteady flow

**5. (Sub lesson 3)** The shadow clock's reliability problem includes —
a) batteries running out  b) the changing length of day and night across the seasons shifting the shadow's behaviour  c) glass breaking  d) sand spilling
**Answer: b) the changing length of day and night across the seasons shifting the shadow's behaviour** — the clock's own ruler changes with the season

**6. (Sub lesson 3)** One quntal at the market —
a) is always exactly 100 kg  b) is not strictly 100 kg — it can be less or more  c) is a length  d) is a clock
**Answer: b) is not strictly 100 kg — it can be less or more** — the reliability problem in a single sack
