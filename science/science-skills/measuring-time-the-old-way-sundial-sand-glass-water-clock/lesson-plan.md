# Lesson plan — Measuring time the old way — sundial, sand-glass, water clock

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Science Skills · **Lesson:** Measuring time the old way — sundial, sand-glass, water clock
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — Basics of Scientific Investigation (Grade 8 General Science) — guide Project 1.1 / Activity 1.2 / Project 1.2, material the Grade 8 student book does not print.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** before clocks, time was read from the sun, from falling sand and from flowing water — each old clock works, and each depends on something that shifts, which is exactly its reliability problem (teacher guide — evidence)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on teacher-guide evidence only (acceptance count 0): the Grade 7 sundial build stands on the Grade 7 student book; this lesson is the older history and the reliability prosecution, plus the market measures in live use

## Sub lessons
1. **The sundial — twelve parts of daylight (teacher guide — evidence)** — Egypt, about 1500 B.C.: the sunrise-to-sunset interval split in twelve, read off moving shadows
2. **The sand-glass and the water clock (teacher guide — evidence)** — the 14th-century hourglass, its interval set by neck and sand; Babylonian and Egyptian water clocks of about the 16th century B.C., read off container marks, varying with water pressure
3. **The shadow clock on trial, and the market (teacher guide — evidence)** — shadow height shifts across the day and the seasons; the market visit: cubit, span, foot and pace in live use, and the quntal that is not strictly 100 kg

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Science Skills — Measuring time — from shadows to clocks. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Tell how each of the three old clocks reads time, and what each one depends on.
2. Say why the sand-glass's interval is fixed by its neck width and its sand quantity.
3. Prosecute the shadow clock: two ways its readings shift (across the day; across the seasons).
4. Name the Addis Ababa market measures the guide keys, and say what the quntal shows about reliability.

## What you need
A stick and sunny ground for the shadow clock; if possible, a visit to a nearby market or a trader's visit to class.

## If the learner is stuck
- Expects the sundial's twelve parts to equal clock hours → they divide daylight, and daylight itself lengthens and shortens with the seasons; the parts breathe.
- Calls the old clocks useless → each served a civilisation; the lesson's charge is narrower and provable: what each depends on can shift, so its reading can shift.
