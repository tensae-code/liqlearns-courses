# Engineering design process — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Explain, in your own words: Define the problem — criteria and constraints.
**Answer:** Engineering design begins by defining the problem: what must the solution do, and what limits it?

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Explain, in your own words: Generate ideas — sketch and model the design.
**Answer:** With the problem defined, the designer generates ideas — more than one — and gives them shape: a sketch or a model that others can see and judge before anything is built for real.

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Explain, in your own words: Test and modify — compare versions, then optimise.
**Answer:** A design is tested against the criteria, and what the test shows drives the next version: modify the design, test again.

**4.** (Sub lesson 4) Explain, in your own words: One process, many problems — Utah's own run of designs.
**Answer:** The same process is turned on problem after problem, and that run is Utah's own way of teaching it: a survival solution from found natural objects (Kindergarten, K.2.4), a device using light or sound to communicate over a distance (Grade 1, 1.3.4), erosion prevention (Grade 2, 2.1.3), a solution to a problem caused when the environment changes (Grade 3, 3.2.6), an energy-converting device (Grade 4, 4.2.4), solutions reducing the effects of naturally occurring events on humans (Grade 5, 5.1.5), and design solutions that keep human-engineered structures stable against geologic hazards (Grade 7, 7.2.3).

**5.** (Whole lesson) Retell the whole lesson in its own order, one sentence per sub lesson.
**Answer:** Define the problem — criteria and constraints → Generate ideas — sketch and model the design → Test and modify — compare versions, then optimise → One process, many problems — Utah's own run of designs — in that order, as taught in lesson/lesson.md.
