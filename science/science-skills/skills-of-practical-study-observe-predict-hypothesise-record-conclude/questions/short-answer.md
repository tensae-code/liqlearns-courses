# Skills of practical study — observe, predict, hypothesise, record, conclude — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) What is the difference between a prediction and a hypothesis?
**Answer: A prediction says what will happen in a case; a hypothesis proposes a testable explanation for it, in a form an experiment could support or refute.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Why must the results table be drawn before the experiment starts?
**Answer: It forces the variables, units and repeats to be decided in advance, and gives every reading a place to be recorded honestly as it happens.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Your conclusion's hypothesis turns out not supported. Is the experiment a failure?
**Answer: No — a fairly run experiment that refutes a hypothesis is a success of method: the conclusion reports it plainly and suggests what to test next.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Name two things a strong conclusion states besides the answer to the hypothesis.
**Answer: Its limits — hard-to-control variables and likely errors — and what would be improved or tested next (any two such).**
