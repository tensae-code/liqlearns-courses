# Doing science honestly — ethics and Ethiopian scientists

## Do it first
A pupil's experiment fails. The numbers look ugly. A friend whispers: "Just write the numbers from my sheet — the teacher will never know." The report would look perfect. Would it be science? What exactly breaks if she copies? Keep your answer — this lesson names the rules that answer it, then shows you six Ethiopians who kept them at the highest level.

## Sub lesson 1 — The five disciplines
Scientific ethics has five disciplines. (1) **Honesty in reporting data** — the numbers you got are the numbers you write, ugly or not. (2) **Careful analysis** — slow, checked work before any conclusion. (3) **Proper credit** — name whose work and whose help you used. (4) **Unbiased interpretation** — read results as they are, not as you wish they were. (5) **Warning the community of risks** — if your work shows a danger, the community hears it from you. The guide keys the same ethics to research (teacher guide — evidence): protect vulnerable participants, and serve knowledge, truth and the avoidance of error.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ethiopia's own scientists
Six contributors to research and write up. **Kitaw Ejigu** — aerospace mechanisms patented with NASA; GPS work and flight simulation. **Sossina Haile** — ceramics, solar fuels and solid electrolytes. **Gebisa Ejeta** — sorghum bred nutritious, drought-resistant and pest-resistant. **Rediet Abebe** — mechanism design for social good; co-founder of Black in AI. **Aklilu Lemma** — the endod fruit as a preventive against bilharzia. **Alemtsehay Mekonnen** — human physiology, and the values and hazards of medicinal plants.
