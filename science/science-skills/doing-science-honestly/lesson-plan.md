# Lesson plan — Doing science honestly — ethics and Ethiopian scientists

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Science Skills · **Lesson:** Doing science honestly — ethics and Ethiopian scientists
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — Basic Concepts of Science (Grade 7 General Science).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** science has an ethics — honesty in reporting, care in analysis, credit where due, unbiased reading, warning the community — and Ethiopia's own scientists show that ethics at work
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on What science is — knowledge is tentative and social, so how scientists behave is part of what science is

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **The five disciplines** — honesty, careful analysis, proper credit, unbiased interpretation, warning the community
2. **Ethiopia's own scientists** — six named contributors to research, match and write up

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Science Skills — What science is. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the five ethical disciplines of science.
2. Match six Ethiopian scientists to their contributions.
3. Write up one scientist's profile from research, as the book's task sets.
4. Say why warning the community of risks is an ethical duty, not a courtesy.

## What you need
Paper and pencil; library, radio or family knowledge for the research write-up.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks ethics is separate from science → a copied result is not bad manners; it is broken science, because every later inference stands on false evidence.
- Mixes the scientists up → anchor each to one word: Ejigu–space, Haile–fuels, Ejeta–sorghum, Abebe–fair systems, Lemma–endod, Mekonnen–the body.
