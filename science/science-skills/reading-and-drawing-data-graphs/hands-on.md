# Hands-on — Reading and drawing data graphs

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Sort and count
Take ten small things you can count at home — for example the fruit in the house, sorted by kind. Count each kind and write the counts in a table: kind | how many. Say out loud what the pattern is: which is most common, which is rarest.

## Draw the graph
Draw a bar graph of your table on paper: one bar per kind, the bar's height = the count, every bar and both axes labelled, and a title that says what the graph shows. Show it to someone and ask them to tell you the pattern without seeing your table — if they can, the graph works.

## The story poster
Make a one-page poster: your question at the top ("What fruit do we have, and which is most common?"), your graph in the middle, and two sentences at the bottom telling the story the graph proves.
