# Measurement — scalars, vectors and prefixes

## Do it first
"Walk 100 steps." Can you end where I mean? Now: "Walk 100 steps **north**." One instruction can be followed exactly; the other cannot. Some quantities are complete with a size alone. Others are unfinished until a direction joins the size. Sort these as you read: time, mass, volume, density, temperature, energy — and displacement, velocity, force.

## Sub lesson 1 — Scalars and vectors
A **scalar** quantity needs only **magnitude** — a size with its unit: time, mass, volume, density, temperature, energy. Say "3 kg" and nothing is missing. A **vector** quantity needs **magnitude and direction**: displacement, velocity, force. Say "5 metres" and a displacement is unfinished — 5 metres **east** completes it. The test is one question: is the quantity complete with size alone?

## Sub lesson 2 — SI prefixes: the staircase of ten
Prefixes replace powers of ten, so very large and very small measures stay writable. The staircase runs from **peta (P, 10¹⁵)** down through tera, giga, mega, **kilo (k, 10³)**, milli, micro, nano, pico to **femto (f, 10⁻¹⁵)**. The worked example fixes kilo: **10³ g = 1 kg** — one thousand grams make one kilogram, and every prefix is the same trick at a different step of the stair.

Sort six quantities for your companion, scalar or vector, with the test spoken aloud — then climb the staircase from femto to peta without looking.

## Do It Yourself
1. Direction walk: describe a walk across the compound twice — once with sizes only, once with sizes and directions. Which description could a stranger follow to the same endpoint?
2. Prefix drill: write 2 kg in grams, 5 km in metres, 3 mg in grams' thousandths. Use the staircase, not memory alone.
3. Vector hunt: find force in one home action (pushing, pulling, lifting) and state it fully — size and direction.
