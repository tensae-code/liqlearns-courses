# Measuring: traditional and modern measures

## Do it first
How long is your desk? You have no ruler — but you have an arm, and feet, and eyes. People measured the whole world that way for ages. This lesson gathers the old ways, honestly — and shows why the new ways had to come.

## The old ways of length and time
For **length**, the body itself was the ruler: the arm-measures **kend, sinzir and gat**, and **footsteps and shoe-lengths** paced off on the ground. For **time**, the day was told by the **shadow** — a stick's shadow shortening and lengthening — and by **cock-crow**, the rooster's call at dawn. For **mass**, people used the **balance**, weighing one thing against another.

## Why modern instruments came
The old ways have one deep fault: **my arm is not your arm**. A kend of cloth bought from a tall seller is longer than a kend from a short one. Modern instruments — the ruler, the clock, the standard balance — give **one measure that is the same for everyone**. And the lesson fixes its key word: **mass is the amount of particles (matter) in a body** — the stuff itself, counted by weighing.

## Grade 7 growth — measuring length: why standard units win

Grade 7 returns to length with instruments in hand. Measurement is **comparison against a well-defined, reproducible standard** — the SI standard. The body measures from earlier in this lesson — **hand span, cubit, foot and yard** — vary from person to person; **rulers, vernier calipers and micrometers** carrying standard units do not. The class test: groups measure the **blackboard, a book's thickness and a table** both ways — body measures and standard instruments — with **three measurers each**, and compare which set of results agrees. Then the unit is fitted to the size: **millimetres for a wire, metres for a classroom, kilometres for the road from Bahir Dar to Gondar**.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the old ways first — kend, sinzir, gat, footsteps, shadow, cock-crow, balance — then the one fault that brought the modern instruments, then the definition of mass.

## Do It Yourself
1. Arm against arm: measure the same desk in sinzir with a partner. Compare numbers. Whose measure should a buyer trust — and what fixes it?
2. Shadow clock: set a stick upright in the sun in the morning, mark the shadow's end, and mark it again at midday and afternoon. Tell the day's story from your marks.
3. Mass words: lift a stone and a sponge of the same size. Which holds more particles (matter)? Say it in the book's definition.
