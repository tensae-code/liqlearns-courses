# Lesson plan — Measuring: traditional and modern measures

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Science Skills · **Lesson:** Measuring: traditional and modern measures
**Place in the curriculum:** Science Skills — measuring, the carried Grade 4 set (CURRICULUM.md: the G4 "Measuring — …" placements land with Band C under Science Skills).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** people measured long before rulers existed — with arm-measures, footsteps, shadows and the balance — and modern instruments replaced them because a measure must be the same for everyone; mass is the amount of particles (matter) in a body
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Living and non-living things — sorting the world; now the world's sizes are put into numbers.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name traditional measures: kend, sinzir and gat arm-measures and footsteps and shoe-lengths for length; shadow and cock-crow for time; the balance for mass.
2. Say why modern instruments took their place: a measure must agree for everyone, everywhere.
3. Define mass in the book's words: the amount of particles (matter) in a body.

## What you need
Your own arm and feet; a stick for a shadow; a simple balance if the class has one.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks traditional measures were foolish → they were clever and served for ages; their fault is only that they change from person to person.
- Mixes mass with size → mass is the amount of matter, not the space taken; a big sponge can hold less matter than a small stone.
