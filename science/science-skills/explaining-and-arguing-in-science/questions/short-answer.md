# Explaining and arguing in science — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Explain, in your own words, what scientific explanation adds beyond saying what happened.
**Answer:** Explanation adds the how and the why — the learner gives the mechanism and the reasons, not only the event.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Name the chapter's four tools and say what each makes the learner do.
**Answer:** Agree/disagree circles and discussion formats make learners take positions with reasons; odd-one-out makes them defend a classification choice; thought experiments make them test a case in the mind.

**3.** (Whole lesson) Retell the whole lesson in its own order, one sentence per sub lesson.
**Answer:** Explanation says what, how and why, trained by agree/disagree circles and discussion formats → arguing is sharpened by odd-one-out and thought experiments, where reasons must hold — in that order, as taught in lesson/lesson.md.
