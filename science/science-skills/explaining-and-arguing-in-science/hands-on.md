# Hands-on — Explaining and arguing in science

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Odd one out
Take four science words from any lesson you know. Decide which is the odd one out and write your reasons. Then argue the opposite choice — if both can be defended, say what the exercise taught you.

## Agree or disagree
Take one claim from a lesson — for example, 'the heavier stone falls faster'. Stand in the agree circle or the disagree circle, in words, and give your reasons. Say what reason would move you.
