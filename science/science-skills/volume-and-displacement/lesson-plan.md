# Lesson plan — Measuring: volume and displacement

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Science Skills · **Lesson:** Measuring: volume and displacement
**Place in the curriculum:** Science Skills — measuring, the carried Grade 4 set (CURRICULUM.md: the G4 "Measuring — …" placements land with Band C under Science Skills).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** volume (ይዘት/ምድገት) — how much space a body takes — is measured by displacement: lower the body into water and read how far the level rises in a graduated cylinder; its units are m³, cm³ and mm³, and the litre (1 m³ = 1000 litres; 1 litre = 1000 cm³)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the units lesson — length's family cubed becomes volume's family, and the cylinder reads it.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Science Skills — Living and non-living things. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what volume is: how much space a body takes up.
2. Run the displacement experiment: bodies lowered into water, the rise read on a graduated cylinder.
3. Use volume's units and relations: m³, cm³, mm³; 1 m³ = 1000 litres; 1 litre = 1000 cm³.

## What you need
A graduated cylinder (or a marked jar), water, a feather and small, medium and large stones; five bottles as the book sets them.

## If the learner is stuck
- Reads the first level only → volume is the RISE: second reading minus first reading.
- Thinks a light thing has no volume → the feather displaces a little water: small volume, but real. Volume is space taken, not weight.
