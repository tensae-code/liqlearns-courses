# Putting things in order — sequence

## Do it first
Before you read: think about this lesson's one thing — ordering — deciding what comes first, second and last, and how things follow one another, as Iraq's Grade 1–3 pupil's books teach it as one of the first science skills. Say what you already know about it out loud. Then read on: this is Iraq's way in — its Ministry of Education *Science* books (the Primary Science Book Series) teach this thing at the places named in this lesson's sources.

## Sub lesson 1 — First, second and last

To **order** things is to decide what comes **first**, what comes **second**, and what comes **last**. Iraq's Grade 1 book teaches ordering as its own skill, straight after classifying: the things are the same, but now their place in the line is what matters.

The Grade 3 book widens the word: ordering and sequencing is deciding **how things follow one another** — each thing's place is fixed by what comes before it and what comes after it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Ordering by size

The Grade 2 book works ordering on a set of cubes: the cubes are **ordered by size** — the line runs from one end of the sizes to the other, and every cube's place can be checked against its neighbours.

That is the test of a true order: pick any two neighbours and the rule still holds between them. If it breaks anywhere, the order is not finished.

## The key ideas

- To **order** is to decide what comes first, second and last — how things follow one another.
- An order runs by a rule: cubes ordered by size keep the rule between every two neighbours.
- Classifying groups things by likeness; ordering lines things up by sequence — two different skills.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — First, second and last first, then Ordering by size. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "What is the difference between classifying and ordering?" Answer: Classifying puts similar things together into groups; ordering decides the sequence — what comes first, second and last.

## Do It Yourself
1. Take five things of different sizes. Order them by size, then say each one's place: first, second, third, fourth, last.
2. Order the events of your morning: what came first, second and last. Write them in sequence.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences — how you check an order is finished.
