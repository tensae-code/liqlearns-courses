# Science and industry — technology in daily life

## Do it first
Before you read: look around the room you are in. Name five things that did not grow and were not born — things people made. For each one, can you say what it is for, and where it was made? Say your answers out loud. Then read on: this is Ghana's way in — its science curricula carry a sub-strand called **science and industry** inside Strand 5, Humans and the Environment, from Basic 2 up through junior high school.

## Sub lesson 1 — Technological products and their uses

The things people make to do jobs are **technological products**. A bicycle carries you. A phone carries your voice. A lamp gives light after dark. A bucket carries water and keeps it.

Ghana's Basic 2 curriculum files this sub-strand as **technological products and their uses** — the two always travel together. A product is not understood until you can say its use: name the thing, then say what it does for the people who use it. A thing whose use nobody can name is a thing nobody needs.

## Sub lesson 2 — Science and industry — making and keeping things

**Industry** is making, organised. Behind every product stands knowledge: which material will hold, which will rust, how hot, how long, how to join, how to seal. That knowledge is science, put to work — science and industry are one sub-strand in Ghana's curriculum because making well is applied science.

Industry also **keeps** things. Ghana's Basic 5 filing of the sub-strand names, among its indicators, **foods and how they are preserved**. Food spoils; preservation stops or slows the spoiling — drying, smoking, salting, tins sealed against the air, cold that slows everything down. A preserved food is a product too: science keeping food safe until it is eaten.

The sub-strand returns at Basic 7 in the junior high curriculum — the same science and industry, at greater depth, as Ghana revisits every strand every year.

## The key ideas

- A **technological product** is a made thing with a use — name the product and its use together.
- **Industry** is science organised to make products — and to keep them.
- **Preserving food** — drying, smoking, salting, sealing, cooling — is part of science and industry.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — technological products and their uses first, then science and industry making and keeping things. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Name a product — now its use." Answer with the pair, not by repeating.
