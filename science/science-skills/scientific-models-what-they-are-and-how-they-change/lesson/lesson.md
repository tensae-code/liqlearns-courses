# Scientific models — what they are and how they change

## Do it first
A globe is not the Earth — so why do scientists trust it? And why did the model of the solar system change? Say your answers out loud. Then read on: this is Sudan's way in — its Grade 9 Natural Sciences opens the whole book with Scientific models, a full unit on what models are and how they change.

## Sub lesson 1 — What a scientific model is — and its three kinds

A **scientific model** is a **representation of a thing or event that helps us understand the world**. The book sorts models into **three kinds: physical models, computer models and mental (conceptual) models** — a thing you can hold, a thing a computer runs, and a thing you hold in your mind.

## Sub lesson 2 — Making models — more information, more accuracy

**The more information a scientist gathers, the more accurate the model.** The book teaches it through the **sketch artist drawing from a witness's description**: each new detail the witness gives makes the drawing a better model of the face.

## Sub lesson 3 — Using models to communicate ideas

Models **communicate ideas**: **maps, drawings, recipes and the globe** are the book's everyday models — each one carries an idea about a real thing to someone who was not there, in a form they can use.

## Sub lesson 4 — Using models to test predictions — and to save time, money and lives

Models **test predictions and hypotheses** — the book's **closed-box experiment** and **engineers' wind tunnels** — and they **save time, money and lives**: **crash-test dummies** take the crash instead of a person, and **NASA's astronaut-training aircraft** lets astronauts practise without the risk of space.

## Sub lesson 5 — The limits of models — they change

**Models are not perfect, and they change when new information arrives.** The book tells it through the **solar-system model changing from Earth-centred to Sun-centred**: the old model was a real model, used and taught — and when the information changed, the model changed. That is not a failure of models; it is how models work.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "What is a scientific model, and why do models change?" Answer: A model is a representation of a thing or event that helps us understand the world — physical, computer or mental. Models change because they are not perfect: when new information arrives, the model is remade, as the solar-system model changed from Earth-centred to Sun-centred.
