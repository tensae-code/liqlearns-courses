# Lesson plan — Debating change: new foods or old ways?

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Science Skills · **Lesson:** Debating change: new foods or old ways?
**Place in the curriculum:** Science Skills — thinking skills at Grade 4 depth: the book's printed debate, argued both ways (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the book prints a debate — 'The New One and the Old One' — over whether food culture must change: new productive crops against slow-changing habits; the class splits and debates both questions with reasons (plot summary only)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Living and non-living things — sorting was the first thinking skill; debating with reasons is the next: holding two argued sides at once.

## Before this lesson

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Tell the debate's two sides as printed: the New One (population grows, production falls, so new productive root crops and grains must join teff, wheat, barley and livestock) and the Old One (habits change slowly, good values may be lost; better technology that raises production without changing the food culture).
2. Say how the class works it: split, and debate both questions with reasons.
3. Give a reasoned view of your own, having heard both sides.

## What you need
Two chairs facing each other, in a classroom or in the mind; the summary below is the whole text — the book's debate is told, never copied.

## If the learner is stuck
- Retells only the side they agree with → the book's method is both questions, with reasons, from a split class; state the other side fairly or the skill is missed.
- Invents lines from the book → the debate is taught from its printed summary of arguments; no speech is quoted or copied here, by design.
