# Measuring time — from shadows to clocks

## Do it first
No clock. No phone. What time is it? Look at the sun's place. Look at a shadow. Your grandfather could answer within the hour from those two alone. Guess now — then check a clock and score yourself. This lesson rebuilds that skill as an instrument: a sundial you make, mark and test.

## Sub lesson 1 — The old time-tellers, and the heartbeat count
Traditionally, time was read from **shadows**, from the **sun's position** in its journey, and from **birdsong at dawn**. The scientific way is the **clock and the watch** — the second (s) is the SI unit, with year, month, day, hour and minute around it. First experiment: count your **heartbeats** against a timed interval — a partner times one minute while you count pulses. Your heart is a clock you carry; the timed minute is how you check it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Building the sundial
Set an **upright stick** firmly in sunny ground. Through the day, at each clock hour, lay a **rock or card where the stick's shadow tip falls** and mark the hour on it. By evening the marks fan around the stick — your sundial is built.

## Sub lesson 3 — Sundial against clock
Now test it. Read the sundial, then read the clock: how close? Look at the **angles between your marks — they are not all equal**, though the hours are; the shadow does not sweep evenly across a flat ground dial. And name the sundial's limits honestly: no sun, no time — clouds and night defeat it, where a clock works on. The sundial is a true instrument with true limits, and saying both is the science.

## Say it to your companion
Walk your companion through the build hour by hour, then give the verdict: where the sundial matches the clock, where its angles surprise, and when it cannot work at all.

## Do It Yourself
1. Finish the dial: complete a full day of marks and use the dial next day to call two times before checking the clock. Score both calls.
2. Heartbeat laboratory: count pulses for 15 seconds, 30 seconds, one minute. Which count, scaled up, lands closest to the full minute's count?
3. Limit list: write three times or places where the sundial fails and a clock works — and one place where the sundial works and a stopped clock fails.
