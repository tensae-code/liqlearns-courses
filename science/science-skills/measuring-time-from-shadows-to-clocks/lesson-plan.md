# Lesson plan — Measuring time — from shadows to clocks

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Science Skills · **Lesson:** Measuring time — from shadows to clocks
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 1 — Basic Concepts of Science (Grade 7 General Science).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** time was first read from the sun — shadows, the sun's position, birdsong at dawn — and clocks made it exact; building a sundial joins the two: your own shadow-clock, checked against a clock
**This lesson's own level:** it grows the shadow-clock of Science Skills — Measuring: traditional and modern measures at Grade 7 depth: now the learner builds the instrument and tests it

## Sub lessons
1. **The old time-tellers, and the heartbeat count** — shadows, sun position, birdsong; clocks and the second; heartbeats against a timed interval
2. **Building the sundial** — upright stick, hourly shadow marks on rocks or cards — the build-along
3. **Sundial against clock** — equal hours, unequal angles; the dial's honest limits

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Science Skills — Measuring: traditional and modern measures. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the traditional time-tellers: shadows, the sun's position, birdsong at dawn.
2. Count heartbeats against a timed interval and say what the comparison shows.
3. Build a working sundial: upright stick, hourly shadow marks on rocks or cards.
4. Compare the sundial with a clock: its angles, its limits, when it cannot work.

## What you need
A straight stick, rocks or cards for marks, a sunny patch of ground, a clock or watch for checking.

## If the learner is stuck
- Expects equal angles between marks → the hours are equal but the shadow's sweep across flat ground is not; the book sets this comparison deliberately.
- Marks drift between days → the stick must not move once marking starts; a moved stick is a new dial.
