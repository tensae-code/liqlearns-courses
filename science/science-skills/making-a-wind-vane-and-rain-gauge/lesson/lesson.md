# Making a wind vane and rain gauge

## Do it first
The wind is blowing — from which side? A scrap of cloth can tell you once. But a school that wants the wind's direction **every day, written down**, needs something that stays up there doing the telling. It needs instruments — and the book does not buy them. The book **builds** them.

## The build-and-record project
The Grade 4 book's project is in three movements:

1. **Build** — pupils build a **wind vane** and a **rain gauge**, working **with their teacher**, from simple materials.
2. **Record** — the instruments are watched daily: the **wind's direction is recorded every day at break time**, in a class record.
3. **Report** — the week's records are **reported weekly to the class**: the wind's story of the week, told from the pupils' own numbers and notes.

This is the science-skills shape entire: an instrument made by hand, a record kept by discipline, a report made from evidence. The weather lessons gave the instruments their names — ዊንድቬን the vane, ሬንጌጅ the gauge; this project gives them to your hands.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the project's three movements — build, record daily at break, report weekly — and which instrument answers which question.

## Do It Yourself
1. Break-time record: for one week, at the same break each day, note the wind's direction in words (from the north, from the east…). Friday: report your week, the project's third movement in small.
2. Gauge siting: choose where a rain gauge should stand at home or school — open sky above it, no roof-drip falling in. Defend the spot.
3. Vane design sketch: draw your wind vane before any building: what points? what turns? what stands still? A drawing is the build's first half.
