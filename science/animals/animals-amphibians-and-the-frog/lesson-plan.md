# Lesson plan — Animals — amphibians and the frog

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Animals · **Lesson:** Animals — amphibians and the frog
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 5 — Living Things and their Diversity (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** amphibians are ectothermic animals with smooth, moist, scaleless skin; their young breathe with gills in water and their adults breathe with lungs on land — studied by observing a frog humanely and releasing it.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Animals and Plants primary lessons of this course (grouping animals; kinds of plants and their parts) at first depth, and it is the Grade 7 first-depth partner of the Biology title's secondary classification and kingdoms lessons — the same ground, met here first and more simply; the Biology lessons go deeper later. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the amphibian body plan.
2. Explain the gills-to-lungs change from young to adult.
3. Carry out the frog study: toes, measure, eyes, nostrils, tympanum, skin — then release.

## What you need
A container (never bare hands), a ruler, paper and a pencil; frogs at a pond edge in season.
