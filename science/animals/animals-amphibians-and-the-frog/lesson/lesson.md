# Animals — amphibians and the frog

## Do it first

A tadpole swims with a tail. A frog hops with legs. Are they the same animal at different ages? Write your answer and your reason.

## Sub lesson 1 — The amphibian plan

Amphibians are ectothermic. Their skin is smooth, moist, glandular and without scales. The body is head and trunk, with four legs. The young live in water and breathe with gills; the adults live on land and breathe with lungs — the tadpole grows into the frog.

## Sub lesson 2 — Studying a frog (doing)

Collect frogs humanely and release them unharmed where you found them. Count the webbed toes on the hind feet against the fore toes, measure the frog, find the eyes, nostrils and the tympanum (the ear disc), and feel the moist skin. Males croak to call mates, and frogs appear in their season. Field safety (teacher guide — evidence, item 11, guide printed pp. 84–108): some organisms may sting or bite or carry disease — never collect with bare hands, use gloves or a container, watch where you step, wash hands afterwards, and release every specimen alive and unharmed where you found it.

## Say it to your companion

Explain in your own words: amphibians are ectothermic animals with smooth, moist, scaleless skin; their young breathe with gills in water and their adults breathe with lungs on land — studied by observing a frog humanely and releasing it. Give one example of your own for each part, and let your companion challenge you with one hard case.
