# Evolution — evidence and fossils

## Do it first
Living things today are not exactly the living things of long ago. How could anyone know that, when nobody was there to watch? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Ecuador's way in — its Grade 9 Ciencias Naturales teaches biological evolution through its evidence, with fossils as a window on the past, and tests genetic variability with an experiment on beans.

## Sub lesson 1 — Species change over time

**Biological evolution** is species **changing over time**: a kind of living thing today is not exactly what that kind was long ago. Ecuador's Grade 9 book teaches evolution as this change over time — the starting fact the rest of the lesson's evidence must explain.

## Sub lesson 2 — Evidence and fossils — a window on the past

The change is known from its **evidence**. **Fossils** are that evidence's clearest form: they are a **window on the past**, showing living things that lived long ago, so the life of the past can be compared with the life of today. Where today's kind differs from its fossil form, the window shows the change evolution names.

## Sub lesson 3 — Genetic variability — the beans experiment

Evolution needs differences to work on, and those differences are present now: the book's **experiment with beans** shows **genetic variability** — beans of one kind are not all identical; they differ, and that differing inside one kind is the variability from which change over time can come.
