# Video script — Young are like, but not exactly like, their parents

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Today: **Young are like, but not exactly like, their parents** — the United States' way, from its Utah SEEd Standards books (Grade 1 and Grade 3).

The pattern opens the lesson: **young plants and animals resemble their parents**. Utah's Grade 1 book (1.2.3, printed p. 83) teaches it from carrots: grown carrots are orange and cone-shaped like the parent plants' carrots — the family likeness is real, and it is the first fact. The traits that make the likeness — colour, shape, kind — come from the parents.

The same carrots finish the pattern: they are **not exactly alike** — different **sizes** and different **tastes**. Resemblance is not identity. Every young living thing carries its parents' traits in its own mixture and measure, so a family is a pattern with variations inside it, never a row of copies.

Utah's Grade 3 book (3.2.2, printed p. 53) climbs one step: **analyse data to identify patterns of traits inherited from parents**. Spread the measurements and observations out, and the pattern shows itself — which traits run from parent to young, and where the young differ. Inherited traits are not just noticed; they are read off evidence.

Now you. Say it back — in your own words.
