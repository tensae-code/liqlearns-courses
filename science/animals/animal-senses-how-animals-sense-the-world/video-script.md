# Video script — Animal senses — how animals sense the world

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Today: **Animal senses — how animals sense the world** — North Macedonia's way, from its Grade 4 book (printed pp. 59–60).

North Macedonia's Grade 4 book teaches animal senses through touch first. The **mole** lives in the dark underground, and its sense of **touch** does the work our eyes do for us: its touch receptors are far richer than ours — the book gives the measure plainly, **six times more touch receptors than a human hand**. The **crocodile** is the second touch example: its skin answers touch in the water and at its edge, so it senses what comes near even before it sees it. Touch, for these animals, is not a spare sense — it is the main way in.

The book's hearing examples are the **dog** and the **owl**. A dog's hearing **picks out** sounds — it separates the one sound that matters from the rest, which is why a dog answers a call or a step that we barely notice. The **owl**, hunting in the dark, hears its prey and turns on the sound: hearing does for the owl what touch does for the mole. Each animal's strongest sense fits the life it lives and the dark it works in.

The lesson's frame, as the book gives it, is that a sense is an answer to a **stimulus** — something in the world that the animal must notice to live: a touch in the tunnel, a sound in the night. The mole, the crocodile, the dog and the owl are not curiosities; they are the proof that senses are fitted, animal by animal, to the stimuli each animal's life depends on answering.
