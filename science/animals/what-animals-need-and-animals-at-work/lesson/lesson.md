# What animals need, and animals at work for people

## Do it first
The donkey carried the sacks all the way to market. This morning someone fed it, watered it, and kept its shelter clean. Animals work for people who work for animals. This lesson tables the whole bargain: needs on one side, gifts on the other.

## Needs first, then the great ledger of gifts

Begin with what animals **need**: **food, shelter and water**. No needs met, no gifts given — that order is the book's own.

Then count what they give. **Food**: meat, milk, eggs, yoghurt, cheese, butter. **Transport**: the **horse, donkey, mule and camel**, carrying people and loads. **Clothing and goods**: leather jacket, shoes, belt and bag; blankets and caps from **hair and wool**. **Labour**: **ploughing** and draught work — the ox pulling the farm open, season after season.

And the guide from the earlier lesson finishes the thought: animals that are cared for **give more benefit** — the ledger grows on the gifts side when the needs side is kept honestly.

## Say it to your companion
Build the bargain table for your companion for one animal: three needs in, every gift out. Then swap animals — the cow's table is longer than anyone guesses.

## Do It Yourself
1. Full-day ledger of one animal: from dawn feed to evening shelter, log what a working animal near you receives and gives in one day.
2. Milk chain: follow milk from cow to cup — milk, yoghurt, cheese, butter: four gifts from one gift. Draw the chain.
3. Thank-you care: give one working animal its water or feed yourself today, the carer's way, and note the bargain kept.
