# How animals live together — groups, colonies and territory

## Do it first
Before you read: think about this lesson's subject — how animals live together — groups, colonies and territory. Then read on: this is Honduras's way in — its *Ciencias Naturales* books teach this thing at the places named in Sources, in the order below.

## Sub lesson 1 — Gregarious association

gregarious association: herds of elephants and schools of fish, where each fish keeps a distance equal to its body length, the school fans out when threatened and has no leader; family and colonial relations (corals, jellyfish) complete the intraspecific set (guide evidence)

## Sub lesson 2 — State populations

state populations: bees live under a queen with hundreds of drones and sterile worker females who do all the hive's work (the strongest drone fertilises the queen and is then killed by her); ants divide into workers (smallest and most numerous: defence, food-gathering, nest-enlarging, care of queen and young), soldiers (big heads and strong jaws for crushing hard food) and winged males, in underground anthills begun with earth mounds against flooding; ants benefit humans by eliminating pests and aerating the soil (guide evidence)

## Sub lesson 3 — Territoriality reduces competition and prevents the exhaustion of food reserves

territoriality reduces competition and prevents the exhaustion of food reserves; territories are demarcated with olfactory, visual or auditory signals (the rattlesnake's rattle); relations table: familiar (gorillas, humans), state (ants, bees), colonial (jellyfish), territorial (dogs, wolves) (guide evidence)
