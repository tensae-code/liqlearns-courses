# Animals — what makes an animal

## Do it first

Look at a sheep and a teff plant side by side in your mind. List three ways the sheep's body plan is different from the plant's.

## Sub lesson 1 — The animal plan

Animals are multicellular and eukaryotic. They are consumers — they eat other living things. They have muscle tissue and nervous tissue, they need oxygen, most reproduce sexually, and they move to find food. Their cells lack walls and chloroplasts.

## Sub lesson 2 — Backbone or no backbone

Invertebrates have no backbone. Vertebrates have a backbone, and they fall into five groups: fish, amphibians, reptiles, birds and mammals — the five groups the rest of this unit studies one by one.

## Say it to your companion

Explain in your own words: animals are multicellular consumers with muscle and nervous tissue whose cells have no walls and no chloroplasts; they divide into invertebrates without a backbone and vertebrates with one. Give one example of your own for each part, and let your companion challenge you with one hard case.
