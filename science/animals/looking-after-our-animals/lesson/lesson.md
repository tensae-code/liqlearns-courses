# Looking after our animals

## Do it first
Here is a goat's shelter. Is the water clean? Is there feed? Is the floor swept? You already know how to judge — this lesson writes the carer's rules you were using, and adds the two you had not met: medicine, and gentle handling.

## The four care rules

The Grade 2 book's care rules are four. **Enough food and water** — not sometimes food, enough food, every day. **A clean shelter** — swept, dry, a healthy place to stand and lie. **Medical care** — when an animal is sick or hurt, it is treated, as a person is. And **handling animals without getting hurt** — moving and holding them the calm, knowing way, which also keeps the animal unhurt.

The book closes the bargain with its promise: **cared-for animals give more benefit**. Care is not kindness added to the science; in this book, care IS the science — the needs of the last lesson, kept daily.

## Say it to your companion
Teach your companion the four care rules as a shelter inspection: walk them past a real or drawn shelter and let them pass or fail it on each rule, with the fix named for every fail.

## Do It Yourself
1. Shelter inspection: score a real shelter on the four rules. Fix the easiest fail yourself, today, with the owner's leave.
2. Water duty: take over one animal's water for three days — fresh, clean, never empty. Log each refill.
3. Handling practice: approach, hold and lead a calm domestic animal the gentle way, with its owner showing first. Say what 'without getting hurt' means for both of you.
