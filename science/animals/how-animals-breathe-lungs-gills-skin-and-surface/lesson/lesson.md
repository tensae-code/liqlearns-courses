# How animals breathe — lungs, gills, skin and surface

## Do it first
Before you read: you breathe with lungs. A fish has no lungs, yet it breathes. A worm has neither lungs nor gills. How many ways of breathing can there be — and what is every one of them for? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Bulgaria's way in — its Grade 4 book teaches breathing as one life process with many organs.

## Sub lesson 1 — Breathing — taking in oxygen to free energy

Bulgaria's Grade 4 book's Lesson 36 teaches **breathing as a life process**: organisms **take in oxygen** to **release energy from food**.

That purpose is the lesson's anchor. Breathing is not just air moving in and out — it is the taking-in that lets the energy stored in food be freed for living. Every breathing organ, however different, does that one job.

## Sub lesson 2 — Lungs, gills, skin and surface — and plants breathe too

The organs differ by the animal and its home. Animals breathe with **lungs**, with **gills**, through their **skin**, or through their **whole body surface**.

And the book closes the circle: **plants breathe too**. Breathing belongs to living things as such — not to one group, and not to one organ. Name the organism, and the question is always the same: with what does it take in oxygen?

## The key ideas

- **Breathing** is a life process: organisms take in **oxygen** to **release energy from food**.
- Animals breathe with **lungs, gills, skin, or the whole body surface**.
- **Plants breathe too** — breathing belongs to living things, not to one organ.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Why do living things take in oxygen?" Answer: To release the energy in food — breathing frees food's energy for living.
