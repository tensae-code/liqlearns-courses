# Inside an animal — body systems of a bird

## Do it first
You have seen a hen from outside — beak, feathers, wings, legs. What do you think is inside it, holding it up and keeping it alive? Say your answers out loud. Then read on: this is Angola's way in — its Grade 6 *Ciências da Natureza* book first studies a bird from outside — the hen, its external morphology — then opens the inside study: the hen's skeleton and its digestive, respiratory, circulatory and reproductive systems.

## Sub lesson 1 — The hen's skeleton — the frame inside

Inside the hen is a **skeleton**: the frame of bones that holds the bird up and gives its muscles something to pull on. Angola's inside study begins with the frame, as the outside study began with the covering: bones of the body, wings and legs — a bird light-built for movement, standing on the same plan as other backboned animals: a frame inside, worked by muscles.

## Sub lesson 2 — The hen's digestive and respiratory systems

The **digestive system** takes the hen's food in and breaks it down so the body can use it — beak to stomach to gut, the food's road through the bird. The **respiratory system** takes in air and gives the body the oxygen of breathing, through the bird's air passages and lungs. Two systems, two takings-in: food and air, each with its own road.

## Sub lesson 3 — The hen's circulatory and reproductive systems

The **circulatory system** — heart and blood vessels — carries what the other systems gather: food's goodness and breathing's oxygen, round the whole body. The **reproductive system** is the system of the bird's making of new birds — in the hen, the making of eggs. Angola's Grade 6 inside study closes the bird with these two: the carrying system that serves all the rest, and the system that continues the kind.

## The key ideas

- The hen's **skeleton** is the frame inside — bones holding the bird up, worked by muscles.
- The **digestive** system breaks food down; the **respiratory** system takes in air — two roads in: food and air.
- The **circulatory** system carries food's goodness and oxygen round the body; the **reproductive** system — in the hen, egg-making — continues the kind.
