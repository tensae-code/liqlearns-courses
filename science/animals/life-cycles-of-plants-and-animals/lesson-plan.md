# Lesson plan — Life cycles of plants and animals

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Animals · **Lesson:** Life cycles of plants and animals
**Place in the curriculum:** Animals — appended to Unit 9 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a living thing's life cycle — the stages it passes through from egg to adult — shown the Swedish way: in *Samtalsbilder, Tio små kryp* (Conversation pictures, Ten small creeping things), each of ten small animals gets a portrait page and then a life-cycle page with the stages pictured and labelled, so the cycle is read off the pictures
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **The four-stage cycle — egg, larva, pupa, adult** — Eight of the ten small animals pass through the same four stages: egg, larva, pupa, and the adult animal (butterfly, bumblebee, fly, mosquito, ladybird, wasp, ant — and the grasshopper, whose stages the book prints in its own order).
2. **The simpler cycles — no pupa** — Two of the ten run a shorter cycle with no pupa stage: the earthworm (jelly, cocoon, worm) and the spider (egg, cocoon, spider).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: see the lesson's own opening. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain sub lesson 1 in their own words: The four-stage cycle — egg, larva, pupa, adult.
2. Explain sub lesson 2 in their own words: The simpler cycles — no pupa.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. For any practical part: kitchen materials only where the hands-on says so — here, paper, a pencil, and — for the hands-on — a safe outdoor place to look for small animals without handling them roughly.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Thinks every animal has a pupa → return to the earthworm and the spider: their cycles are printed with no pupa, and that difference is the second sub lesson.
