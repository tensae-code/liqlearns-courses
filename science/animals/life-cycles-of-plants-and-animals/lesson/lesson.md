# Life cycles of plants and animals

## Do it first
Before you read: a butterfly is flying past. Was it always a butterfly? Did it hatch from its egg looking like that — wings and all? Or did it look completely different first? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Sweden's way in — its conversation-picture book *Samtalsbilder, Tio små kryp* (Ten small creeping things) gives each of ten small animals a portrait page, and then a whole page for its life cycle, with the stages pictured and named.

## Sub lesson 1 — The four-stage cycle — egg, larva, pupa, adult

Most of the ten small animals travel the same four-stage road:

**Egg → larva → pupa → adult.**

- **Butterfly:** egg, larva, pupa, butterfly.
- **Bumblebee:** egg, larva, pupa, bumblebee.
- **Fly:** egg, larva, pupa, fly.
- **Mosquito:** egg, larva, pupa, mosquito.
- **Ladybird:** egg, larva, pupa, ladybird.
- **Wasp:** egg, larva, pupa, wasp.
- **Ant:** egg, larva, pupa, ant.

The young animal that comes from the egg — the **larva** — does not look like its parent. It eats and grows, then rests inside the **pupa** stage, and what comes out is the adult, completely changed. That complete change is why the cycle needs all four stages.

The **grasshopper** is the set's own special case: the book prints its stages as **egg, pupa, larva, grasshopper** — in that printed order. It is carried here exactly as the book prints it.

## Sub lesson 2 — The simpler cycles — no pupa

Two of the ten travel a shorter road, with **no pupa stage at all**:

- **Earthworm:** jelly, cocoon, worm.
- **Spider:** egg, cocoon, spider.

The earthworm's cycle, as the book prints it, runs from jelly to a cocoon, and out of the cocoon comes the worm — already worm-shaped, with no larva and no pupa. The spider hatches toward its adult shape the same short way: egg, cocoon, spider.

So the ten animals sort into two families of roads: the four-stage road (egg, larva, pupa, adult) and the simpler road with no pupa. Knowing which road an animal travels tells you what to look for when you go out to find its young.

## The key ideas

- A **life cycle** is the stages a living thing passes through from its beginning to its adult self.
- The **four-stage cycle** is egg, larva, pupa, adult — butterfly, bumblebee, fly, mosquito, ladybird, wasp and ant (the grasshopper's stages are printed in the book's own order: egg, pupa, larva, grasshopper).
- The **earthworm** (jelly, cocoon, worm) and the **spider** (egg, cocoon, spider) run **simpler cycles with no pupa**.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — the four-stage cycle first, then the simpler cycles. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Which of the ten has no pupa?" Answer with the earthworm and the spider, and give their stages.
