# Herbivores, carnivores and omnivores

## Do it first
Before you read: think of three animals — one that eats grass or leaves, one that hunts and eats other animals, and one that eats almost anything put in front of it. Three animals, three different menus. Is there one name for each kind of eater? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Bulgaria's way in — its Grade 3 Man and Nature book groups animals by their food.

## Sub lesson 1 — The three groups by food

Bulgaria's Grade 3 book groups animals by **what they eat**, and the grouping has exactly three groups.

**Herbivores** eat plants. **Carnivores** eat other animals. **Omnivores** eat both — plants and animals together.

The book teaches the three groups inside its lesson on feeding and the food chain (Lesson 15), where plants are called **"green factories"** — the makers of food that every chain starts from. That placing is the point: the group an animal belongs to is read off its menu, and its menu is its place in the chain.

## Sub lesson 2 — Reading the group off the animal

To place an animal, ask one question: **what does it eat?** Leaves, grass, fruit or seeds — a herbivore. Other animals — a carnivore. A mixed menu of both — an omnivore.

The group is not read off size, fierceness or where the animal lives. A big animal may eat only plants; a small one may hunt. The food decides — that is the whole test, and it works for every animal the learner can name.

## The key ideas

- Animals group by food into three groups: **herbivores** (plants), **carnivores** (other animals) and **omnivores** (both).
- Bulgaria's Grade 3 book teaches the groups inside the food-chain lesson, where plants are the "green factories" every chain starts from.
- The test is the menu: name what the animal eats, and the group follows.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Which group does this animal belong to?" Answer: Answer from its food, not its size: plants only — herbivore; other animals — carnivore; both — omnivore.
