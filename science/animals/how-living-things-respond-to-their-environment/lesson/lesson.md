# How living things respond to their environment

## Sub lesson 1 — Responding is a sign of life

Living things **respond** to what is around them. A plant turns its leaves toward the light and sends its roots toward water. An animal moves away from danger, seeks shade in heat and water in thirst. A non-living thing does none of this: a stone does not answer the sun.

Kuwait's Grade 4 book teaches responding as the living world's answer to the environment's factors — light, temperature, water, gravity and touch — each factor named with the response it calls out.

## Sub lesson 2 — Responses keep living things alive

A response is useful: turning to light wins a plant more food-making; moving to shade saves an animal from overheating; roots reaching water keep the plant drinking. Responses the learner will meet again inside their own body — the human body sensing and responding — begin here, in the whole living world.

The book's way is to test it: cover one side of a growing plant, or water only one side of a pot, and watch which way the plant answers. The answer, seen with your own eyes, is the lesson.

## The key ideas

- Living things respond to factors around them: light, heat, water, gravity, touch.
- Plants respond by growing toward light and water; animals by moving.
- Non-living things do not respond.
- Responses help living things stay alive.
