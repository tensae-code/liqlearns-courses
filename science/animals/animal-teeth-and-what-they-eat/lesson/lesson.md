# Animal teeth — and what they eat

## Do it first
Before you read: think of two animals you know well — one that eats meat, one that eats plants. Picture the teeth in each one's mouth. Are they the same teeth? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Djibouti's way in — its Grade 3 guide teaches teeth by asking exactly that question.

## Sub lesson 1 — Do all animals have the same teeth?

Djibouti's Grade 3 guide puts the question at the head of the lesson: **do all animals have the same teeth?**

They do not. Look from one animal to another and the teeth change — in shape, in size, in what they are good for. That difference is the lesson's first fact, and it is found by looking, not by memorising: the guide's pupils compare animals and see for themselves that one set of teeth is not every animal's set.

## Sub lesson 2 — Teeth fit the food

Teeth are tools for food. An animal eats with its teeth, so its teeth fit the food it eats — the hard work of biting, tearing or grinding is done by teeth shaped for that work.

That is why the guide joins the two halves of its title together — **teeth and diet**: neither is taught alone. Given an animal, its teeth tell you about its food; given its food, you can expect something about its teeth. The teeth are a sign you can read.

## The key ideas

- Not all animals have the same teeth — teeth differ from animal to animal.
- An animal's **teeth fit what it eats**: teeth are the tools the animal works its food with.
- Teeth and diet belong together — the teeth are a sign of the food, and the food explains the teeth.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Do all animals have the same teeth?" Answer no, and prove it from the lesson: the teeth differ because the food differs.

## Do It Yourself
1. Name three animals you know. For each one, write what it eats and one sentence on what its teeth must be like for that food.
2. Find a picture of an animal's teeth (in a book or at home, a pet's mouth if it lets you look kindly). Say which food those teeth fit, and why.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences — the guide's question, and its answer.
