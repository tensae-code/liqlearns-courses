# Animals — invertebrates: insects and earthworms

## Do it first

A spider has eight legs. A beetle has six. Only one of them is an insect. Decide which, and what else you would count to be sure.

## Sub lesson 1 — Invertebrates and insects (doing)

Over 95% of animals lack a backbone — jellyfish, worms, snails and starfish among them. Practical 5.3: collect insects humanely, and with a hand lens count the legs (six), the body parts (three) and the wings. Field safety (teacher guide — evidence, item 11, guide printed pp. 84–108): some organisms may sting or bite or carry disease — never collect with bare hands, use gloves or a container, watch where you step, wash hands afterwards, and release every specimen alive and unharmed where you found it.

## Sub lesson 2 — The earthworm

The earthworm enriches the soil with its castings. Practical 5.4: find its segments, its sharper head end, its mouth, its anus, and the thick clitellum band about a third of the way back. Handle gently and return it to its soil. Field safety (teacher guide — evidence, item 11, guide printed pp. 84–108): some organisms may sting or bite or carry disease — never collect with bare hands, use gloves or a container, watch where you step, wash hands afterwards, and release every specimen alive and unharmed where you found it.

## Say it to your companion

Explain in your own words: more than 95% of animals have no backbone; insects are known by six legs and three body parts, and the earthworm enriches the soil with its castings. Give one example of your own for each part, and let your companion challenge you with one hard case.
