# Lesson plan — Animal survival — special parts and behaviours

**Course:** Science · **Title:** animals · **Lesson:** Animal survival — special parts and behaviours
**Place in the curriculum:** Animals — appended to Unit 9 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** animal survival — how species stay alive (kemandirian spesies) through special body parts and behaviours: against enemies, against extreme weather, and by protecting their eggs and young
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Protecting themselves from enemies** — Malaysia's Grade 5 book opens its survival unit with **species survival (kemandirian spesies)**: animals stay alive through special body parts and behaviours. Against enemies it catalogues **sharp spines and poison (the pufferfish), shedding body parts (the lizard's tail, the octopus's arm), camouflage and warning colours**, and further special parts — **horns, shells, scales and quills** — each with its examples.
2. **Weather, eggs and young — staying alive across the year** — Against **extreme weather** the book teaches **bird migration, hibernation, and thick fur or blubber**; for the desert's extreme heat, only certain animals suit it — the **camel** and its features. Survival continues into the next generation: animals **protect their eggs** by hiding them, covering them, carrying them (mouth-brooding) or guarding them, and **keep their young alive** by feeding them milk, carrying them, and attacking enemies — the hen and the monkey defending their young. The unit closes with an **"imaginary animal" design task**: invent an animal and justify its special parts for survival.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the first sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Mixes up protection from enemies with protection from weather → return to the two sub lessons: spines, poison and camouflage answer enemies; migration, hibernation and thick fur answer weather.
- Thinks survival ends with the adult → return to sub lesson 2: protecting eggs and keeping young alive are survival too — the species lives on.
