# Evolution and natural selection

## Do it first
Before you read: look at the living things around you — the birds, the trees, the insects, yourself. They are not all the same, and they were not always here. When did life begin, and has it always looked like this? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Gabon's way in — its Grade 5 Super Efficace guide teaches the diversity and evolution of living things, and it begins with a timeline: life appearing on Earth 3.8 billion years ago.

## Sub lesson 1 — Life began long ago — the timeline

The guide's opening fact is the size of the time: **life appeared on Earth 3.8 billion years ago**.

That is too long to feel, so the guide teaches it as a **timeline** — a line of time, with life's beginning placed far, far back along it, and everything alive today at its near end. On that line, the whole of human history is only the last tiny mark. Living things have had an immense time in which to change — and change is the next sub lesson.

The **diversity** of living things — the many different kinds, each fitted to its own place and way of living — is what that long time produced.

## Sub lesson 2 — Living things change — evolution and natural selection

Across that immense time, living things have **changed, generation after generation**. That slow changing of living things over very long time is **evolution**.

The change is not random wandering. Living things differ from one another, even inside one kind. The ones whose differences **fit their place** — finding food, escaping danger, bearing the weather — live, grow and leave young behind; the ones that fit badly leave fewer. Nature, by this keeping of what fits, **selects** — and that is **natural selection**: over many generations, the fitted differences spread through the kind, and the kind itself slowly becomes different from what it was.

So the two halves of the lesson join: the timeline gives the time — 3.8 billion years of it — and natural selection gives the way living things changed inside that time, into the diversity we see.

## The key ideas

- **Life appeared on Earth 3.8 billion years ago** — taught on a timeline, with everything alive today at its near end.
- **Evolution** is the slow changing of living things over very long time.
- **Natural selection** is nature keeping what fits: living things whose differences fit their place live and leave young, so those differences spread — and over generations the kind changes.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — the timeline first, then evolution and natural selection. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "How long ago did life appear on Earth?" Answer with the guide's number: 3.8 billion years ago.

## Do It Yourself
1. Draw the timeline: a long strip of paper. Mark life appearing 3.8 billion years ago at one end and today at the other. Say aloud why human history is only a tiny mark at the near end.
2. Fit test: name one animal and one difference that fits it to its place — then say what natural selection would do with that difference over many generations.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences — how natural selection changes a kind without anyone choosing.
