# Hands-on — Evolution and natural selection

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Build the timeline
Take the longest strip of paper you can make — join sheets end to end. Mark one end "life appears — 3.8 billion years ago" and the other end "today". Stand at the today end and walk the strip: say what the length of the walk means, and why everything you know from history books belongs in the last tiny piece at your feet.

## The fit test, outside
Go outside and pick three living things. For each one, name a difference that fits it to its place — a beak's shape, a colour, a root's depth. Then say what natural selection does with a fitted difference over many generations, and what it does with an unfitted one.

## Generations game
With counters or stones: start with ten "animals", half marked with a difference that fits, half without. Each round, the fitted ones leave two young and the unfitted ones leave one. Play five rounds, count, and say in one sentence why the kind changes — nobody chose; nature kept what fits.
