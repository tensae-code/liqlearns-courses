# Living things — what makes something alive

## Do it first

Sort these into living and non-living before you read on: stone, soil, table, eucalyptus, sheep, mushroom, river, hair. Write your reason for each one.

## Sub lesson 1 — Living or non-living?

Stone, soil and a table never feed, grow or reproduce — they are non-living. A eucalyptus tree, a sheep and a mushroom feed, grow, breathe and reproduce — they are living. Movement alone does not settle it: plants live without moving from place to place, and a river moves without living.

## Sub lesson 2 — The characteristics of life

A living thing responds to its surroundings (sensitivity), makes more of its kind (reproduction), grows and develops, breathes (respiration), takes in food (nutrition), gets rid of wastes (excretion), fits its surroundings (adaptation), keeps its inside steady (homeostasis) and carries on the chemistry of life (metabolism). Judge hard cases such as hair by the whole set, not by one sign alone.

## Say it to your companion

Explain in your own words: living things share a set of life characteristics together — sensitivity, reproduction, growth, respiration, nutrition, excretion, adaptation, homeostasis and metabolism — while non-living things do not. Give one example of your own for each part, and let your companion challenge you with one hard case.
