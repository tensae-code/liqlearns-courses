# How the environment can change traits

## Do it first
Two plants of the same kind, from the same seeds: one grows tall in rich, watered soil; one stays stunted in dry ground. Same parents, same seed — different plant. What made the difference? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: Utah's Grade 3 book teaches that the environment itself can affect traits, and asks for the explanation to be built from evidence.

## Sub lesson 1 — Traits are not inheritance alone

A living thing's **traits** — its size, shape, colouring, condition — come from its parents, but they are not sealed there. Utah's Grade 3 standard 3.2.3 (printed p. 62) states the lesson's one thing plainly: **the environment can affect the traits of living things**. Where a thing lives — its food, water, light, space — leaves marks on the traits it shows.

## Sub lesson 2 — Explaining it from evidence

The book's demand is an **explanation from evidence**: compare living things of a kind across different environments, observe the trait differences, and tie them to the environmental differences. Resemblance to parents explains part of a trait; the environment explains the rest — and which part is which is decided by the evidence, not guessed.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Two seedlings of one kind grow differently in different soils. What are the two explanations a scientist must weigh?" Answer: Inheritance — what came from the parents — and environment — what the different soils did to the traits. The evidence of the comparison decides the explanation.
