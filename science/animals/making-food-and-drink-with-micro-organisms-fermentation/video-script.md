Today: **Making food and drink with micro-organisms — fermentation** — Samoa's way, from its MESC books.

**Yoghurt — lactic-acid bacteria at work.** Yoghurt is milk changed by lactic-acid bacteria, which turn the milk sugar lactose into lactic acid; the acid thickens and sours the milk. The making is a controlled chain: the milk is heated and pasteurised (harmful microbes killed), homogenised (the fat spread evenly), then inoculated — the starter bacteria, streptococci and lactobacilli, are stirred in — and incubated at about 43°C, the bacteria's best working temperature, until the milk coagulates into yoghurt. The same family of bacteria, kept working differently, gives acidophilus milk (printed pp. 69–73).

**Yeast — in beer and in bread.** Bread and beer are yeast's work: the yeast ferments sugars and gives off gas (and, in beer, alcohol), and the gas is what lifts the dough. The book sends students to investigate both makings — yeast in beer making, including a visit to the Vailima beer factory, and yeast in bread making — so the same micro-organism is met in two trades (printed pp. 69–73).
