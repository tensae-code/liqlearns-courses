# Making food and drink with micro-organisms — fermentation

## Do it first
Milk becomes yoghurt without cooking, and a flat dough rises into bread. Nobody stirs in a new ingredient you can see. Who is doing this work? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Samoa's Year 11 way in — its Book 1 puts the life processes of micro-organisms to work in the kitchen and the brewery.

## Sub lesson 1 — Yoghurt — lactic-acid bacteria at work

Yoghurt is milk changed by **lactic-acid bacteria**, which turn the milk sugar **lactose** into **lactic acid**; the acid thickens and sours the milk. The making is a controlled chain: the milk is **heated** and **pasteurised** (harmful microbes killed), **homogenised** (the fat spread evenly), then **inoculated** — the starter bacteria, streptococci and lactobacilli, are stirred in — and **incubated at about 43°C**, the bacteria's best working temperature, until the milk **coagulates** into yoghurt. The same family of bacteria, kept working differently, gives **acidophilus milk** (printed pp. 69–73).

## Sub lesson 2 — Yeast — in beer and in bread

Bread and beer are yeast's work: the yeast ferments sugars and gives off gas (and, in beer, alcohol), and the gas is what lifts the dough. The book sends students to investigate both makings — yeast in beer making, including a visit to the **Vailima beer factory**, and yeast in bread making — so the same micro-organism is met in two trades (printed pp. 69–73).
