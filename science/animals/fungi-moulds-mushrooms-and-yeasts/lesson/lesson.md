# Fungi — moulds, mushrooms and yeasts

## Do it first

Bread left damp grows a soft coloured coat. A mushroom rises after rain. Are these plants? Write your answer with one reason — then check it in the lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — How fungi feed

Fungi cannot make their own food. They digest dead matter and absorb it, they parasitise living hosts — athlete's foot is a fungal parasite — or they live symbiotically, as lichens live with algae. Their cell walls contain chitin. Yeast (ersho) raises dough and ferments tella and tej.

## Sub lesson 2 — Growing moulds and dissecting a mushroom (doing)

Practical 5.8 grows moulds on wet injera, bread or orange peel against soil-rubbed pieces, in sealed bags over three days — then dispose of the bags safely and wash hands. Practical 5.9 dissects a mushroom's parts in the field. Field safety (teacher guide — evidence, item 11, guide printed pp. 84–108): some organisms may sting or bite or carry disease — never collect with bare hands, use gloves or a container, watch where you step, wash hands afterwards, and release every specimen alive and unharmed where you found it.

## Say it to your companion

Explain in your own words: fungi are eukaryotic, chitin-walled organisms that cannot make food — they digest dead matter and absorb it, parasitise living hosts, or live symbiotically; yeast (ersho) raises dough and ferments tella and tej. Give one example of your own for each part, and let your companion challenge you with one hard case.
