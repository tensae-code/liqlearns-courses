# Camouflage — how animals hide

## Do it first
Before you read: an insect is sitting on a leaf in front of you — and you cannot see it. It has not run away and it has not dug a hole. How can an animal hide in plain sight? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Barbados's way in — its Primary Science Syllabus defines camouflage and teaches it through insects.

## Sub lesson 1 — Camouflage — hiding in plain sight

**Camouflage** is an animal's colours and patterns matching the place where it rests, so that the animal blends in and is hard to see. The animal has not left and has not covered itself — its own body does the hiding.

Barbados's syllabus teaches the idea through **insects** (Class 3): insects whose colours and shapes match the leaves, stems and ground they rest on, so a looking eye passes over them.

## Sub lesson 2 — Why camouflage matters

Camouflage matters because being seen is dangerous. An insect that blends into its resting place is **harder for the animals that would eat it to find** — it survives because it is overlooked.

So camouflage is a survival feature, like the plant adaptations in the plants lesson: name the animal, name its resting place, and say how matching that place helps it live.

## The key ideas

- **Camouflage** is colours and patterns matching the resting place, so the animal blends in and is hard to see.
- Barbados's syllabus teaches it through **insects** matching leaves, stems and ground.
- Camouflage matters for **survival**: the hidden animal is harder for hunters to find.

Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "What is camouflage?" Answer: An animal's colours and patterns matching the place where it rests, so it blends in and is hard to see.

## Do It Yourself
1. Look for an insect or small animal resting on a plant or the ground. Write what its colours match, and why that helps it.
2. Draw an insect twice: once in colours that stand out, once camouflaged against its leaf. Say which one survives the looking eye, and why.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences — how camouflage hides an animal in plain sight.
