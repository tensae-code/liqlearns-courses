# Video script — Copying nature to solve problems — biomimicry

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Today: **Copying nature to solve problems — biomimicry** — the United States' way, from its Utah SEEd Standards books (Grade 2).

Plants and animals already solve hard problems with their **external parts**: hooks that catch and hold, coatings that shed water, shapes that grip, dig, glide or hide (Grade 2, 2.2.4, printed p. 68). Each part is a working solution, tested by survival itself. The lesson first reads those parts as solutions — what problem does this part solve, and how?

The design move is **mimicking**: copy the way the plant or animal part works, and turn it into a solution to a **human problem**. Utah's task is stated as a design task — design a solution to a human problem by mimicking how plants and animals use their external parts to survive and grow. The copy is of the working, not the look: what matters is how the part does its job.
