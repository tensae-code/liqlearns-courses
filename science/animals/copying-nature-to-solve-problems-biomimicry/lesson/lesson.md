# Copying nature to solve problems — biomimicry

## Do it first
A burr sticks to a dog's fur and will not let go. Years later, a fastener of tiny hooks sticks the same way — because an engineer copied the burr. Where did the design really come from? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: Utah's Grade 2 book teaches the move itself — mimicking plants and animals to solve human problems.

## Sub lesson 1 — External parts are nature's solutions

Plants and animals already solve hard problems with their **external parts**: hooks that catch and hold, coatings that shed water, shapes that grip, dig, glide or hide (Grade 2, 2.2.4, printed p. 68). Each part is a working solution, tested by survival itself. The lesson first reads those parts as solutions — what problem does this part solve, and how?

## Sub lesson 2 — Mimic the part to solve a human problem

The design move is **mimicking**: copy the way the plant or animal part works, and turn it into a solution to a **human problem**. Utah's task is stated as a design task — design a solution to a human problem by mimicking how plants and animals use their external parts to survive and grow. The copy is of the working, not the look: what matters is how the part does its job.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "What is copied in biomimicry — the plant or animal, or something else?" Answer: Neither the plant nor the animal itself: what is copied is how its external part works — the solution it embodies — turned to a human problem.
