# Lesson plan — Grouping animals: by young, by movement, by food and by body

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Animals · **Lesson:** Grouping animals: by young, by movement, by food and by body
**Place in the curriculum:** Animals — after Tame and wild (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** animals sort into groups: egg-layers against milk-givers; by movement (flying, crawling, walking) and home (land, water, both); by food (meat-eaters, plant-eaters, eaters of both); and by body — mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians, fish, told by their coverings and parts
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Tame and wild — the first sort (by arrangement with people) is known; this lesson adds four more sorts, the classifier's craft.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Group by reproduction: egg-layers (hen) against mammals that bear young and suckle them (cow).
2. Group by movement and home: flying in the sky, crawling on the ground, walking on legs; living on land, in water, or in both.
3. Group by food: meat-eaters, plant-eaters, eaters of both (lion, mouse, cat, sheep, hen, horse, human in the book's table).
4. Group by body and covering: mammals, birds (feathers, wings), reptiles, amphibians (soft skin), fish (scales); horns, manes, split and whole hooves, hair, feather and shell coverings.

## What you need
Animal pictures, or a farmyard and its neighbours to sort; five boxes or chalk circles for the five body-groups.

## If the learner is stuck
- Sorts by size or friendliness → the book's keys are young, movement/home, food, body — size is not among them.
- Puts the frog with the fish because both swim → the body key rules: soft skin and two homes make it an amphibian, whatever it swims with.
- The covering-classification answers from both teacher guides (G1's covering/movement/legs, G2's five groups) are evidence, labelled in the sources; the tables themselves are the student books' own.
