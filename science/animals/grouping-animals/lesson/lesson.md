# Grouping animals: by young, by movement, by food and by body

## Do it first
Sort a hen: it lays eggs, it walks and flutters, it eats grain and insects, its body wears feathers. Four sorts, four answers — all true. Grouping animals is not one ladder but many keys, and this lesson teaches the book's four keys.

## Sub lesson 1 — Egg-layers and milk-givers; movement and home

The first key is **how animals reproduce**. **Egg-layers** — the **hen** is the book's example — lay eggs that hatch outside the body. **Mammals** bear their young alive and **suckle them** — the **cow** with her calf is the picture.

The second key is **movement and home**. Animals move by **flying in the sky**, **crawling on the ground**, or **walking on legs**. And they live **on land, in water, or in both** — the frog is the book's standing riddle: a both-home animal, hopping on land and swimming in water.

## Sub lesson 2 — By food, and by body and covering

The third key is **food**: **meat-eaters, plant-eaters, and eaters of both** — the book's table places lion, mouse, cat, sheep, hen, horse and human among them.

The fourth key is **body**, and it makes the five great groups: **mammals**; **birds**, known by **feathers and wings**; **reptiles**; **amphibians**, known by **soft skin**; **fish**, known by **scales**. The guides' printed group-discussion answers sort by **covering**: feathers — chicken, dove; hair — sheep, goat, cat; scales — fish. And the body details finish the portrait: **horns, manes, split hooves against whole hooves, hair, feather and shell** coverings.

Four keys, one craft: given any animal of our woreda, the grouped mind can place it four ways and defend each placing.

## Say it to your companion
Deal your companion animal names like cards. They must group the whole hand four times — by young, by movement, by food, by body — and you check each grouping against the book's tables.

## Do It Yourself
1. The frog file: take one animal and write all four of its groupings with proofs. The frog is the master test — land and water, soft skin, insect-eater, egg-layer.
2. Covering hunt: find or draw one animal of each covering — hair, feathers, scales, shell, soft skin — and name its group.
3. Hooves and horns: from the book's body parts, sketch a split hoof, a whole hoof, horns and a mane, each on its animal.
