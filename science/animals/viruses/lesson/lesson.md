## Sub lesson 1 — What a virus is

A **virus** is a tiny particle, far smaller than a bacterium — so small that the light microscopes of the earlier lessons cannot show it. A virus is not made of cells at all: it is a piece of hereditary material wrapped in a protein coat.

Kuwait's books meet viruses twice: Grade 5 asks what they are beside the five kingdoms, and Grade 7 returns to them beside the monerans. Both times the same puzzle is set: a virus is filed next to the living kingdoms, yet it is not itself alive in the way they are.

## Sub lesson 2 — Why a virus is not a living thing

A virus cannot feed, grow, breathe or reproduce by itself. It multiplies only by entering a living cell and using that cell's machinery to make copies of itself — outside a cell it does nothing, like a seed that is not a plant.

That is why viruses cause diseases in plants, animals and humans — the flu and the common cold among them — and why the body's defences and immunisation, taught in Keeping Healthy, are the answer to them. A virus sits on the border of the living world: made of the living world's materials, but alive only inside a living cell.

## The key ideas

- A virus is a tiny particle of hereditary material in a protein coat — not a cell.
- Viruses are far smaller than bacteria and invisible under a light microscope.
- A virus cannot feed, grow or reproduce alone; it multiplies only inside a living cell.
- Viruses cause diseases such as flu and colds; immunisation defends against them.
