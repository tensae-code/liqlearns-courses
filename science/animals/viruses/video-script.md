# Video script — Viruses

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Today: **Viruses** — Kuwait's way: viruses: what they are and how they differ from living things — too small to see with a light microscope, not made of cells, and able to multiply only inside a living cell.

A virus is a tiny particle, far smaller than a bacterium — so small that the light microscopes of the earlier lessons cannot show it. A virus is not made of cells at all: it is a piece of hereditary material wrapped in a protein coat. Kuwait's books meet viruses twice: Grade 5 asks what they are beside the five kingdoms, and Grade 7 returns to them beside the monerans. Both times the same puzzle is set: a virus is filed next to the living kingdoms, yet it is not itself alive in the way they are. A virus cannot feed, grow, breathe or reproduce by itself. It multiplies only by entering a living cell and using that cell's machinery to make copies of itself — outside a cell it does nothing, like a seed that is not a plant. That is why viruses cause diseases in plants, animals and humans — the flu and the common cold among them — and why the body's defences and immunisation, taught in Keeping Healthy, are the answer to them. A virus sits on the border of the living world: made of the living world's materials, but alive only inside a living cell.

Now you. Say it back — in your own words.
