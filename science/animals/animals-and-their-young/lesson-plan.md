# Lesson plan — Animals and their young

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Animals · **Lesson:** Animals and their young
**Place in the curriculum:** appended to Unit 9 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** animals and their young ones — matching each animal to its young, as Zimbabwe's ECD A guide teaches them
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Animals and their young ones** — Zimbabwe's ECD A guide teaches **animals and their young ones** as its own topic, right after domestic and wild animals: every animal the child has just met has young of its own.
2. **Matching a young animal to its parent** — The topic's work is matching: the young one belongs to its parent animal, and learning the young ones extends the naming the child already does for the grown animals.
3. **Young ones grow** — Young ones are not small copies that stay small — they grow into the grown animal. That growth is the topic's closing idea, taught at ECD level in the guide's own order.
