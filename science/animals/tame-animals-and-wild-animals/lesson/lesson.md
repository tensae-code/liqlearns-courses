# Tame animals and wild animals

## Do it first
A hen scratches by your door. A hyena calls from the forest edge in the night. Both are animals of our woreda — but they live by two different arrangements with people. This lesson draws the line: tame and wild.

## Two arrangements with people

**Domestic animals** live **under people's care**: the **cat, the hen, the donkey** — fed, sheltered and kept close. **Wild animals** live free, in **forest, grassland, caves and water**: the **hyena, lion, leopard and warthog**. That arrangement — cared for, or free — is the whole line.

Why do people keep domestic animals? The Grade 1 guide lists their **great service**: **for food and milk, for making clothes, for transport, and for entertainment**. And the guide adds a seasons note the curious always ask: animals in our area **breed in different seasons — most breed when there is enough food and favourable weather**, the same logic as the farmer's sowing.

## Say it to your companion
Take your companion through ten animal names. For each: domestic or wild — and the proof (where does it live? who cares for it?). Then the services: let them name what the donkey, the cow and the dog do for people.

## Do It Yourself
1. Compound census: list every domestic animal at your home or a neighbour's, with the service each one gives. Total the services — food, transport, clothes, guarding, company.
2. Wild map: on a drawing of your area, place the wild animals the book names where they live — forest, grassland, cave, water.
3. Breeding seasons: ask a farmer when the animals breed most, and check the guide's answer — when food is enough and weather favourable.
