| microorganisms (microbes) defined and classified: beings so small they cannot be seen with the naked eye — optical or electronic microscopes are needed — living in any medium; their size is measured in microns or micrometres (µm, the thousandth part of a millimetre); they divide into those with cellular organisation (possessing a cell membrane and the nucleic acids DNA and RNA: the prokaryotes — archaebacteria, organisms similar to bacteria but with different biochemical and genetic characteristics, and eubacteria, including most bacteria — and the unicellular eukaryotes with nucleus: protozoa, microscopic algae and microscopic fungi) and those without cellular organisation — the viruses, which have a biomolecular organisation, possessing neither nuclear nor cell membrane; all are unicellular except the viruses, which are not cells (Honduras — HN CN9) | Honduras, *HN CN9*, printed p. 189 |

| the microorganism concept is operative, with no taxonomic or phylogenetic implication: it englobes unicellular and pluricellular organisms not evolutionarily related — prokaryotes like bacteria, eukaryotes like protozoans, part of the algae and fungi, and even acellular virological entities of ultramicroscopic size, like viruses or prions (guide evidence) (Honduras — CN9 Guide) | Honduras, *CN9 Guide*, guide PDF p. 150 |
| microorganisms have a generally very short, simple life cycle and, with their great reproductive capacity, form dense populations in zones with abundant food, generating a great descendance that can derive in massive deaths when the food exhausts; they differ genetically as prokaryotes (a single circular double-helix DNA chain) or eukaryotes (various linear DNA portions forming chromosomes), in feeding — algae autotrophs, fungi heterotrophs, viruses do not feed — and, except viruses, tend to reproduce asexually, with sexual reproduction in case of extreme necessity (guide evidence) (Honduras — CN9 Guide) | Honduras, *CN9 Guide*, guide PDF p. 150 |
