# Hands-on — Microorganisms — the invisible living world

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Work it like the book does
Go back to the lesson's first part — Microorganisms (microbes) defined and classified. microorganisms (microbes) defined and classified: beings so small they cannot be seen with the naked eye — optical or electronic microscopes are needed — living in any medium; their size is measured in microns or micrometres (µm, the thousandth part of a millimetre); they divide into those with cellular organisation (possessing a cell membrane and the nucleic acids DNA and RNA: the prokaryotes — archaebacteria, organisms similar to bacteria but with different biochemical and genetic characteristics, and eubacteria, including most bacteria — and the unicellular eukaryotes with nucleus: protozoa, microscopic algae and microscopic fungi) and those without cellular organisation — the viruses, which have a biomolecular organisation, possessing neither nuclear nor cell membrane; all are unicellular except the viruses, which are not cells. Now do the same looking, comparing or trying with things near you, and write down what you find.

## Teach it with your hands
Show a companion the main thing this lesson teaches, using real things near you — point, move, sort or build as the lesson's subject needs — and let them ask one question. Answer from the lesson.
