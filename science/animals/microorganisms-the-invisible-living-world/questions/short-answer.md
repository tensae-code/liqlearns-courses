# Microorganisms — the invisible living world — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** What do Honduras's books teach about microorganisms (microbes) defined and classified?
**Answer: microorganisms (microbes) defined and classified: beings so small they cannot be seen with the naked eye — optical or electronic microscopes are needed — living in any medium; their size is measured in microns or micrometres (µm, the thousandth part of a millimetre); they divide into those with cellular organisation (possessing a cell membrane and the nucleic acids DNA and RNA: the prokaryotes — archaebacteria, organisms similar to bacteria but with different biochemical and genetic characteristics, and eubacteria, including most bacteria — and the unicellular eukaryotes with nucleus: protozoa, microscopic algae and microscopic fungi) and those without cellular organisation — the viruses, which have a biomolecular organisation, possessing neither nuclear nor cell membrane; all are unicellular except the viruses, which are not cells.**

**2.** What do Honduras's books teach about margin: beings so small cannot be observed with the simple view?
**Answer: margin: beings so small cannot be observed with the simple view — a specialised instrument called microscope is necessary, which can be optical or electronic; not all microorganisms are harmful for the human being (guide evidence)**

**3.** What do Honduras's books teach about printed keys?
**Answer: printed keys: beneficial microorganisms for humans include the intestinal flora — bacteria living in our body, protecting it and obtaining food; the bacterium Lactobacillus bulgaricus used in dairy production like cheese, and the fungus Saccharomyces cerevisiae for bread making; the true/false key gives: bacteria were the first living beings inhabiting the planet (true); the diseases listed as fungal (flu, measles, diarrhoea, herpes, varicella) are keyed false — they are caused by viruses; microorganisms cannot be observed with the simple view, only through the microscope; viruses are not the largest microorganisms but the smallest; fungi are not originated by lack of personal hygiene alone (guide evidence)**

**4.** Name this lesson's one thing.
**Answer: Microorganisms — the invisible living world.**
