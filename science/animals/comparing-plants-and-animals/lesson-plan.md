# Lesson plan — Comparing plants and animals

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Animals · **Lesson:** Comparing plants and animals
**Place in the curriculum:** Animals — appended to Unit 9 (Animals) (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The books teach this part as 6: Venn-style comparison of plants and animals (appearance, feeding, growth, movement) and what animals need (water, air, light, heat, food); snow leopard (barys) fact box — worked through in the books' own presentation, at the printed pages named in Sources.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Comparing plants and animals** — The books teach this part as 6: Venn-style comparison of plants and animals (appearance, feeding, growth, movement) and what animals need (water, air, light, heat, food); snow leopard (barys) fact box — worked through in the books' own presentation, at the printed pages named in Sources.
2. **Plants and animals — how they are alike and different** — The books teach this part as both breathe, feed, grow, reproduce, develop; plants are still and grow all their life, animals move and grow for a set period; a puppy's growth series from 7 days to 3 years — worked through in the books' own presentation, at the printed pages named in Sources.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: see the lesson's own opening. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain sub lesson 1 in their own words: Comparing plants and animals.
2. Explain sub lesson 2 in their own words: Plants and animals — how they are alike and different.
