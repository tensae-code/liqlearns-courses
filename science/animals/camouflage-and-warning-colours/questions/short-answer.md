# Camouflage and warning colours — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Name the two ways protective colouring works and one book example of each.
**Answer:** Hiding — camouflage, e.g. the mantis in green (or the squirrel's seasonal coat); advertising — warning colours, e.g. the wasp's stripes (or the salamander's spots).

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does the squirrel's coat change with the season?
**Answer:** So its colours keep matching its surroundings as the seasons change — the hiding works all year.

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) A hunter leaves a wasp alone without ever being stung. How did the warning work?
**Answer:** The stripes are a signal the hunter has learned — warning colours protect by being seen and remembered, not by hiding.

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) Is warning colouring the opposite of camouflage or the same family? Explain.
**Answer:** The same family — both are protective colouring; they differ in method: one protects by not being seen, the other by being seen and avoided.
