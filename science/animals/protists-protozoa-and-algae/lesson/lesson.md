# Protists — protozoa and algae under the microscope

## Do it first

A drop of pond water looks empty. Under a microscope it swims with life. Predict two ways a one-celled swimmer could move itself — then read on and check.

## Sub lesson 1 — Protozoa — the animal-like protists (doing)

Protozoa move in three ways: by pseudopodia (amoeba), by flagella, and by cilia (paramecium). The group also holds leishmania, euglena and giardia. Protozoa cause malaria, amoebiasis and giardiasis. Practical 5.6 cultures paramecium in a hay infusion over 5–10 days — with strict hygiene, because other microbes flourish in the infusion too. Observe the paramecium swimming, its cilia rippling. ## Sub lesson 2 — Algae — the plant-like protists (doing)

Plant-like algae such as Chlamydomonas and Euglena photosynthesise, yet they have no root, stem or leaf. Practical 5.7 observes algae in pond water under the microscope.

## Say it to your companion

Explain in your own words: protists are mostly unicellular eukaryotes that are neither plant, fungus nor animal; protozoa move by pseudopodia, flagella or cilia, and plant-like algae photosynthesise without root, stem or leaf. Give one example of your own for each part, and let your companion challenge you with one hard case.
