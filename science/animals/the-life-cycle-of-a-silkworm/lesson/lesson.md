# The life cycle of a silkworm

## Sub lesson 1 — The silkworm is born

The silkworm's life begins as an **egg**, laid by the moth. China's Grade 4 book opens the cycle at the hatching: the egg breaks and out comes the young silkworm — a tiny larva, hungry at once for mulberry leaves.

This is China's own sericulture way in — the silkworm is not a wild example chosen at random; it is the animal China has raised for its silk for thousands of years, and its cycle is taught from the egg, the way a silkworm keeper meets it.

## Sub lesson 2 — The silkworm grows and changes

The larva eats and grows — and growing has a problem: its skin does not stretch. So the silkworm **moults**: it sheds its old skin and grows a new, larger one, several times through its larva life. Between moults it eats; at each moult it changes.

The stages are the keeper's calendar: hatching, eating and growing, moult after moult — until the full-grown larva is ready for the change that makes silk famous.

## Sub lesson 3 — The whole life — egg to moth

The grown larva spins its **cocoon** — a case of silk thread around itself — and inside it becomes a **pupa**, resting while its body remakes itself. Out of the cocoon comes the **moth**, which mates, lays eggs, and dies: the eggs begin the cycle again.

Egg, larva, pupa, moth — the whole life of the silkworm is a complete change, a metamorphosis, and the book closes the topic by holding the whole circle in view at once: every stage in its order, each stage the doorway to the next.

## The key ideas

- The silkworm's life runs **egg → larva → pupa → moth** — a complete change (metamorphosis).
- The growing larva **moults** — shedding its skin several times as it eats mulberry leaves and grows.
- The larva spins a silk **cocoon** around itself to become a pupa; the moth that emerges lays the eggs that begin the cycle again — China's sericulture animal.
