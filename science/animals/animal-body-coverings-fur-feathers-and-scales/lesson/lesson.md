# Animal body coverings — fur, feathers and scales

## Do it first
Before you read: What do you already know about this — what covers animals' bodies — fur, feathers, scales and shells — and what each covering does for the animal? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Kuwait's way in — its Ministry of Education *Science* (العلوم) books teach this thing at the places named in the sources, and this lesson follows their order.

## Sub lesson 1 — Coverings you can name

Every animal's body is covered by something. Mammals are covered in **fur** or hair. Birds are covered in **feathers**. Fish and reptiles are covered in **scales**. Some animals, like snails and turtles, carry a hard **shell**.

Kuwait's Grade 1 book teaches the coverings first by looking: the child matches the animal to its covering — the sheep's woolly fur, the bird's feathers, the fish's shiny scales — before any harder words are used.

## Sub lesson 2 — What a covering does

A covering is not decoration. Fur keeps a mammal warm. Feathers keep a bird warm and dry and let it fly. Scales protect a fish's or a reptile's body like small armour plates that overlap.

The covering also fits where the animal lives: the desert animal's short, light fur and the water animal's smooth scales each suit their home — the same fitting-to-home idea the course meets again in the adaptation lessons.

## The key ideas

- An animal's covering is part of its body: fur, feathers, scales or a shell.
- Mammals have fur, birds have feathers, fish and reptiles have scales.
- Coverings protect the body and keep it warm or dry.
- A covering fits the place where the animal lives.
