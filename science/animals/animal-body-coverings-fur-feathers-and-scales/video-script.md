Today: **Animal body coverings — fur, feathers and scales** — Kuwait's way: what covers animals' bodies — fur, feathers, scales and shells — and what each covering does for the animal.

Every animal's body is covered by something. Mammals are covered in fur or hair. Birds are covered in feathers. Fish and reptiles are covered in scales. Some animals, like snails and turtles, carry a hard shell. Kuwait's Grade 1 book teaches the coverings first by looking: the child matches the animal to its covering — the sheep's woolly fur, the bird's feathers, the fish's shiny scales — before any harder words are used. A covering is not decoration. Fur keeps a mammal warm. Feathers keep a bird warm and dry and let it fly. Scales protect a fish's or a reptile's body like small armour plates that overlap. The covering also fits where the animal lives: the desert animal's short, light fur and the water animal's smooth scales each suit their home — the same fitting-to-home idea the course meets again in the adaptation lessons.
