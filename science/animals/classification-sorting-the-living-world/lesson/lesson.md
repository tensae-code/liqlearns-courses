# Classification — sorting the living world

## Do it first

Picture two bookshelves: one jumbled, one ordered. Which one lets you find a book fast? Now list every living thing you can see from the classroom door.

## Sub lesson 1 — Why scientists sort life

Classification puts living things into groups by how they are alike and how they differ. It orders study, helps identify a newly found organism, and reveals relationships between organisms. Biodiversity is Earth's whole variety of life — every kind, in every place.

## Sub lesson 2 — The compound survey (doing)

Work in a group outdoors. List the organisms you find and note their features — movement, wings, legs, flowers, cones and more. Then build your own grouping criteria from those features and sort your finds. Field safety (teacher guide — evidence, item 11, guide printed pp. 84–108): some organisms may sting or bite or carry disease — never collect with bare hands, use gloves or a container, watch where you step, wash hands afterwards, and release every specimen alive and unharmed where you found it.

## Say it to your companion

Explain in your own words: classification groups living things by similarity and difference so life can be studied in order, newcomers identified and relationships revealed; biodiversity is the whole variety of life on Earth. Give one example of your own for each part, and let your companion challenge you with one hard case.
