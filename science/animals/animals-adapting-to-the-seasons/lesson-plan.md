# Lesson plan — Animals adapting to the seasons

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Animals · **Lesson:** Animals adapting to the seasons
**Place in the curriculum:** Animals — appended to Unit 9 (Animals) (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** 8: hare's coat thickening and turning white, squirrels and mice storing food, migratory birds (nightingale, swallow, chaffinch, crane) vs wintering birds with strong beaks (woodpecker, tit, magpie, sparrow), hibernation of bear, badger and hedgehog, feeding birds in winter, deer moving down from the mountains
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **Animals adapting to the seasons** — 8: hare's coat thickening and turning white, squirrels and mice storing food, migratory birds (nightingale, swallow, chaffinch, crane) vs wintering birds with strong beaks (woodpecker, tit, magpie, sparrow), hibernation of bear, badger and hedgehog, feeding birds in winter, deer moving down from the mountains
2. **How the seasons affect animals** — The books teach this part as migration as key word; marmots hibernate six months; how forest animals prepare for winter — worked through in the books' own presentation, at the printed pages named in Sources.
3. **How animals prepare for the change of seasons** — moulting (linka) defined — skin, feathers, wool or fur is shed, thicker fur grows by autumn; hare grey in summer, white in winter; hibernation after heavy summer/autumn feeding; migratory birds; winter food stores (squirrel — nuts, cones, acorns, mushrooms; badger — roots and seeds; hamster — grain); the marmot's deep burrow with sleeping nest, store room and spare exits; 23 October is Snow Leopar

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: see the lesson's own opening. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain sub lesson 1 in their own words: Animals adapting to the seasons.
2. Explain sub lesson 2 in their own words: How the seasons affect animals.
3. Explain sub lesson 3 in their own words: How animals prepare for the change of seasons.
