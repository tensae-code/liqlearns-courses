# Strange life cycles in animals

Say what you already know about it out loud. ## Sub lesson 1 — Cycles that break the plain pattern

Iraq's Grade 4 book teaches life cycles group by group — non-flowering plants, flowering plants, invertebrates, vertebrates — and then closes the run with a reading: **animals that have strange life cycles**.

The reading's place is its point. After the plain patterns are learned, the strange ones can be seen for what they are: cycles that still run — young, growth, new young — but by routes that surprise: the young unlike the grown animal, stages in unexpected places, changes no plain cycle would predict. Strange does not mean broken: it means the cycle takes a road the plain pattern did not show.

## The key ideas

- Some animals have **strange life cycles** — cycles that run by surprising routes.
- Strange does not mean broken: the cycle still completes — young, growth, new young.
- Iraq's book sets this reading after the plain life-cycle chapters, as their close.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — Cycles that break the plain pattern. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "What makes a life cycle 'strange' in this reading's sense?" Answer: It completes itself by a surprising route — the young unlike the grown animal, or stages in unexpected places — instead of the plain pattern learned before it.
