# How feeding shows in teeth, beaks and mouthparts — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Explain, in your own words: Reading feeding from teeth.
**Answer:** South Sudan's Primary 4 book reads an animal's feeding group off its **teeth**: grazers and browsers and ruminants by their grinding teeth — the guide's butcher's-head observation names the herbivore's **incisors, the toothless gap, molars and premolars** — and carnivores by their **canines and claws**. The teeth tell what the animal eats.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Explain, in your own words: Birds' beaks tell their food.
**Answer:** Birds are read by **beak and eye position**: grain eaters, filter feeders, flesh eaters and nectar and fruit feeders each carry a different beak — hooked beaks and forward eyes for flesh eaters, flat spoon or nutcracker beaks and side eyes for plant eaters, in the guide's own sorting.

**3.** (Sub lesson 3) Explain, in your own words: Insects' mouthparts tell their food.
**Answer:** Insects are read by their **mouthparts**: **chewing, piercing-sucking and sponging** mouthparts fit three different ways of taking food. One rule runs through the whole lesson: the mouth an animal carries is a sign you can read — of the food it takes and the way it takes it.

**4.** (Whole lesson) Retell the whole lesson in its own order, one sentence per sub lesson.
**Answer:** Reading feeding from teeth → Birds' beaks tell their food → Insects' mouthparts tell their food — in that order, as taught in lesson/lesson.md.
