## Sub lesson 1 — Reading feeding from teeth

South Sudan's Primary 4 book reads an animal's feeding group off its **teeth**: grazers and browsers and ruminants by their grinding teeth — the guide's butcher's-head observation names the herbivore's **incisors, the toothless gap, molars and premolars** — and carnivores by their **canines and claws**. The teeth tell what the animal eats.

## Sub lesson 2 — Birds' beaks tell their food

Birds are read by **beak and eye position**: grain eaters, filter feeders, flesh eaters and nectar and fruit feeders each carry a different beak — hooked beaks and forward eyes for flesh eaters, flat spoon or nutcracker beaks and side eyes for plant eaters, in the guide's own sorting.

## Sub lesson 3 — Insects' mouthparts tell their food

Insects are read by their **mouthparts**: **chewing, piercing-sucking and sponging** mouthparts fit three different ways of taking food. One rule runs through the whole lesson: the mouth an animal carries is a sign you can read — of the food it takes and the way it takes it.
