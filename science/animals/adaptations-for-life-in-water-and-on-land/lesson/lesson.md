# Adaptations for life in water and on land

## Do it first
Before you read: a fish moves through water as if the water were air. A land animal crossing a pond struggles. What does the water animal have that the land animal does not — and what does the land animal have for its own hard seasons? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Bulgaria's way in — its Grade 3 book gives water life and land life one lesson each.

## Sub lesson 1 — Fitted for the water

Bulgaria's Grade 3 book's Lesson 18, *Living organisms in the water*, reads the water home off its plants and animals: their **adaptations** — the fittings that let them live there.

The animals carry **fins** for moving, **shells** for protection, **tentacles** for feeling and catching, and **webbed feet** for swimming. And the book sets one trap and springs it: **the dolphin is not a fish** — living in water does not make an animal a fish; the fittings and the kind must be read together.

## Sub lesson 2 — Fitted for the land

Lesson 19, *Living organisms on land*, reads the land animals' fittings under four jobs: **movement**, **feeding**, **winter**, and **defence**.

For winter, animals **hibernate**, grow **thicker fur**, or **migrate** away. For defence, they carry **spines, shells, horns and a sting**. The test is the same as in the water: name the fitting, name the job it does in that home — a fitting you cannot connect to its job is not yet an adaptation you understand.

## The key ideas

- An **adaptation** is a fitting that lets a living thing live in its home — water or land.
- Water fittings in Bulgaria's book: **fins, shells, tentacles, webbed feet** — and the dolphin is **not a fish**.
- Land fittings answer four jobs — **movement, feeding, winter** (hibernation, thicker fur, migration) and **defence** (spines, shells, horns, sting).

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Is the dolphin a fish — and how do you know?" Answer: No. It lives in water, but living in water does not make an animal a fish; the animal's kind and fittings must be read together.
