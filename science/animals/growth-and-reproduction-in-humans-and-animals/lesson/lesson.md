# Growth and reproduction in humans and animals

Say what you already know about it out loud. ## Sub lesson 1 — Growing, changing and making new life

Living things do not stay as they start. Iraq's Grade 1 book teaches that **humans and animals grow, change and reproduce** — the body grows and changes, and the living thing makes new life of its own kind.

And the new life is not random: **offspring resemble their parents**. The young of a kind look like the grown ones of that kind — that likeness is how we know whose offspring they are. The Grade 1 activity book works the same ground with the hands: observing growth and **ordering growth stages** — the change has an order, stage after stage.

## Sub lesson 2 — Birth and eggs — two ways animals reproduce

Animals reproduce in two named ways. Some **give birth** — rabbits are the book's example. Others reproduce **by eggs** — birds and fish are named — and from the bird's egg, **chicks hatch**.

Birth or eggs: both are reproduction, both give offspring that resemble their parents, and both follow growth — the hatched chick and the newborn rabbit each grow and change from there.

## The key ideas

- Humans and animals **grow, change and reproduce**.
- **Offspring resemble their parents** — the young look like the grown ones of their kind.
- Animals reproduce by **giving birth** (rabbits) or by **eggs** (birds, fish); chicks hatch from eggs.
