# Classification — scientific names and the seven ranks

## Do it first

How many names do people near you use for a cat? Write them down. Then ask: which one name could a scientist on the other side of the world trust?

## Sub lesson 1 — Scientific names — binomial nomenclature

Linnaeus standardised naming in the 1750s. A scientific name has two parts: the genus, written with a capital letter, and the species. In print both parts are italic; by hand both are underlined. Homo sapiens is the human name and Felis catus is the cat's. One scientific name is unambiguous worldwide, while common names differ from place to place.

## Sub lesson 2 — The seven ranks

The seven ranks run Kingdom → Phylum → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species. Each step narrows the group. The ladder works like administration narrowing down: kebele, woreda, zone, region — from the wide area to the one place.

## Say it to your companion

Explain in your own words: every organism has one scientific name of two parts — genus and species — and sits in seven ranks from Kingdom down to Species. Give one example of your own for each part, and let your companion challenge you with one hard case.
