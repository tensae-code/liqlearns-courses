Today: **Types of reproduction — asexual and sexual** — Kuwait's way: the types of reproduction in living organisms — asexual reproduction from one parent, making copies, and sexual reproduction from two parents, making offspring that differ.

In asexual reproduction, a single parent produces offspring without any joining of cells from two parents. The offspring are copies of the parent: a moneran splitting in two, a plant growing a new plant from a runner or a cutting, a yeast budding. Kuwait's Grade 6 book teaches the two types as a pair with one test question for any example: how many parents? One parent, copies — asexual. The copies are fast and many, and every one fits the parent's home exactly as the parent did. In sexual reproduction, two parents each give a cell, and the cells join — in plants, pollen joining the egg cell in fertilisation; in animals, the male and female cells joining. The offspring are not copies: they mix both parents and differ from each. The difference is the point the book builds to: asexual offspring are uniform, sexual offspring vary — and variation is what heredity, selection and the whole genetics strand of this course is about. The types lesson is the doorway to the genes lessons.
