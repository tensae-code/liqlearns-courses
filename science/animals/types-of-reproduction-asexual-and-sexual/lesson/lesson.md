# Types of reproduction — asexual and sexual

## Do it first

## Sub lesson 1 — Asexual reproduction — one parent

In **asexual reproduction**, a single parent produces offspring without any joining of cells from two parents. The offspring are copies of the parent: a moneran splitting in two, a plant growing a new plant from a runner or a cutting, a yeast budding.

Kuwait's Grade 6 book teaches the two types as a pair with one test question for any example: how many parents? One parent, copies — asexual. The copies are fast and many, and every one fits the parent's home exactly as the parent did.

## Sub lesson 2 — Sexual reproduction — two parents

In **sexual reproduction**, two parents each give a cell, and the cells join — in plants, pollen joining the egg cell in fertilisation; in animals, the male and female cells joining. The offspring are not copies: they mix both parents and differ from each.

The difference is the point the book builds to: asexual offspring are uniform, sexual offspring vary — and variation is what heredity, selection and the whole genetics strand of this course is about. The types lesson is the doorway to the genes lessons.

## The key ideas

- Asexual reproduction: one parent, offspring are copies.
- Sexual reproduction: two parents, cells join, offspring differ.
- The test for any example: how many parents?
- Sexual reproduction produces variation — the raw material of heredity and selection.
