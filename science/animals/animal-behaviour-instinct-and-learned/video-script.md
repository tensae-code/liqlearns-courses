Today: **Animal behaviour — instinct and learned** — Syria's way, from its Ministry of Education science books.

Behaviour is everything an animal does — and Syria's Grade 8 book sorts it into exactly two kinds: innate behaviour, done without being taught, and learned behaviour, picked up through training and practice. Innate behaviour is the behaviour an animal is born able to do: fighting, seeking shelter, feeding, reproduction, social life, caring for the young, exploring, and escaping enemies. No parent or trainer teaches it; the young animal does it from the start, because staying alive depends on it. Learned behaviour is not in the animal at birth: it is picked up through training. The book's own examples: a parrot riding a bicycle, trained dolphins performing, and a cat that learns to open a door. The same animal can carry both kinds — born behaviours underneath, taught behaviours on top.
