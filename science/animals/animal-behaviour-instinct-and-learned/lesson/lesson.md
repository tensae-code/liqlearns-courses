# Animal behaviour — instinct and learned

## Do it first
Before you read: say out loud what you already think this lesson's one thing is — animal behaviour and its two kinds. Then read on: this is Syria's way in — its Ministry of Education science books teach this thing at the places named in the sources, and this lesson follows their order.

## Sub lesson 1 — Behaviour and its two kinds

Behaviour is everything an animal does — and Syria's Grade 8 book sorts it into exactly two kinds: **innate behaviour**, done without being taught, and **learned behaviour**, picked up through training and practice.

## Sub lesson 2 — Innate behaviour — born with the animal

Innate behaviour is the behaviour an animal is born able to do: fighting, seeking shelter, feeding, reproduction, social life, caring for the young, exploring, and escaping enemies. No parent or trainer teaches it; the young animal does it from the start, because staying alive depends on it.

## Sub lesson 3 — Learned behaviour — picked up through training

Learned behaviour is not in the animal at birth: it is picked up through training. The book's own examples: a parrot riding a bicycle, trained dolphins performing, and a cat that learns to open a door. The same animal can carry both kinds — born behaviours underneath, taught behaviours on top.

## The key ideas

- Behaviour is everything an animal does — and Syria's Grade 8 book sorts it into exactly two kinds: **innate behaviour**, done without being taught, and **learned behaviour**, picked up through training and practice..
- Innate behaviour is the behaviour an animal is born able to do: fighting, seeking shelter, feeding, reproduction, social life, caring for the young, exploring, and escaping enemies.
