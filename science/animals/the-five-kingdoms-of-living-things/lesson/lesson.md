# Kingdoms — the five kingdoms of living things

## Do it first

You meet five strangers: a bacterium, an amoeba, a mushroom, a teff plant and a sheep. Put each in its own group and say what makes its group different.

## Sub lesson 1 — The five kingdoms

The five kingdoms are Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae and Animalia. Each kingdom gathers organisms that share a deep pattern of life.

[Picture 32903 — a five-kingdom chart with one emblem organism per kingdom]

## Sub lesson 2 — How the kingdoms are separated

Kingdoms are separated by cell structure, by nutrition (how the organism feeds), by body organisation and by reproduction. Apply all four tests together when you place an organism.

## Say it to your companion

Explain in your own words: living things are divided into five kingdoms — Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae and Animalia — separated by cell structure, nutrition, body organisation and reproduction. Give one example of your own for each part, and let your companion challenge you with one hard case.
