# Monera — bacteria: helpful, harmful, everywhere

## Do it first

Milk left warm turns sour, yet curdled milk is food your family makes on purpose. Can the same tiny workers be both harmful and helpful? Write your answer.

## Sub lesson 1 — Bacteria — the Monera plan

Monera have no nuclear membrane and no membrane-bound organelles. Their cell walls contain murein. They reproduce asexually by fission, some move with flagella, and they are everywhere — souring milk, poisoning food and decomposing matter, yet also useful: curdling milk is their work.

## Sub lesson 2 — Shapes and blue-green algae

Bacteria come in basic shapes — rods, spheres and spirals. Blue-green algae (cyanobacteria) are photosynthetic bacteria, not true algae, and they bloom across warm, nutrient-rich slow water. Preventing bacterial disease is part of the lesson's discussion.

## Say it to your companion

Explain in your own words: Monera are prokaryotes with no nuclear membrane and no membrane-bound organelles; bacteria sour milk and poison food yet also curdle milk usefully, and blue-green algae are photosynthetic bacteria, not true algae. Give one example of your own for each part, and let your companion challenge you with one hard case.
