# Animal word–picture reading support

## Do it first
Before you read: here is a picture of a frog, but the word under it is covered. Can you still say which animal it is? Of course — the picture tells you. Now — what if you could not yet read at all? Could you still do the animals work, if every animal came with its picture and its name together? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Sweden's way in — its copy pages carry a "Lässtöd", a reading support, made exactly for that pupil.

## Sub lesson 1 — The picture–word table — picture first, word with it

The **Lässtöd** (reading support) is a **picture–word table**: 24 animals, each one shown as a **picture with its name beside it**:

bear, fox, moose, hare, snake, squirrel, hedgehog, cow, horse, sheep, pig, hen, rooster, bumblebee, bee, wasp, spider, ant, mosquito, swan, mallard, frog, perch, pike.

The pupil works **picture first**: they name the animal they see, and only then meet the written word that goes with it. The picture carries the animal until the word can be read.

That is what the support is *for*, as the placement files it: so that **pupils who cannot yet read can still name and work with the animals**. The science — naming, sorting, talking about the animals around us — does not have to wait for reading. The table is the bridge: picture and word side by side, until the word can stand on its own.

## The key ideas

- Sweden's **Lässtöd** is a **picture–word table of 24 animals** — picture and name together, every time.
- The pupil works **picture first, word with it**: the picture carries the animal until the word can be read.
- Its purpose: pupils who **cannot yet read** can still **name and work with the animals**.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "What does the table do for a pupil who cannot read?" Answer with the purpose, not by repeating the list.

## Do It Yourself
1. Draw four animals from the 24 in a row and write each name beside its picture.
2. Cover the names and name all four from the pictures alone. Uncover and check.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences — how the picture carries the animal until the word can be read.
