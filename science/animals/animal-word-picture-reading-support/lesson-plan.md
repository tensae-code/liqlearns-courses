# Lesson plan — Animal word–picture reading support

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Animals · **Lesson:** Animal word–picture reading support
**Place in the curriculum:** Animals — appended to Unit 9 (CURRICULUM.md)
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** naming animals through a picture–word table — Sweden's "Lässtöd" (reading support): 24 animals, each with its picture and its name together, so a pupil who cannot yet read can still name the animals and work with them — the picture carries the animal until the word can be read
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
1. **The picture–word table — picture first, word with it** — Each of the 24 animals (bear, fox, moose, hare, snake, squirrel, hedgehog, cow, horse, sheep, pig, hen, rooster, bumblebee, bee, wasp, spider, ant, mosquito, swan, mallard, frog, perch, pike) sits in the table as a picture with its name beside it: the pupil names the animal from the picture, then meets the word that goes with it.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: see the lesson's own opening. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain sub lesson 1 in their own words: The picture–word table — picture first, word with it.
2. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. For any practical part: kitchen materials only where the hands-on says so — here, paper, a pencil, and the animal portraits or drawings the pupil already works with.

## If the learner is stuck
- Loses the main idea → return to the sub lesson and the Do-it-first questions: every later part hangs on that frame.
- Mixes this lesson up with a neighbour lesson → compare the two titles and say what each one is about; each lesson here teaches one thing.
- Thinks the table is only for reading practice → return to its purpose as filed: it exists so pupils who cannot yet read can still name and work with the animals — the science work does not wait for the reading.
