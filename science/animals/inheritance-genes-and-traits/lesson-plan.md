# Lesson plan — Inheritance — genes and traits

**The one thing this lesson teaches:** how traits pass from parents to young through genes — inherited against acquired traits, Mendel's pea crosses and dominant/recessive forms, and how inherited variation, given time, becomes evolution (India, Class X)

## Sub lessons
1. **Inherited traits and acquired traits** — a trait a living thing is born with, carried in its genes, can pass to its young; a trait it picks up during its life — a learned skill, a built muscle — is acquired and does **not** pass on. Genes are the instructions, carried on DNA, and a gene works by directing the making of a protein that shows as a trait.
2. **Mendel's crosses — dominant and recessive** — Mendel crossed tall and short pea plants: the first generation was all tall, and when those crossed among themselves the short form returned in about one plant in four. The form that shows when both are present is **dominant**; the form that hides and returns is **recessive**. A child also receives sex chromosomes — XX or XY — and in humans it is the father's contribution that sets the child's sex.
3. **Evolution — inherited change over generations** — young are never exact copies: inherited variation appears in every generation. Variations that help a living thing survive and reproduce in its place are kept by selection, generation after generation, until populations differ enough to be new species — that long change is **evolution**. Acquired traits play no part in it, because they are not inherited.
