# Inheritance — genes and traits — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why can an acquired trait not pass to the young?
**Answer: An acquired trait is picked up during a life and does not change the genes in the cells that make the next generation, so there is nothing to pass on.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) A tall plant crossed with a short plant gives all tall young. Is tall dominant or recessive, and why?
**Answer: Tall is dominant: it shows in the first generation even though every young plant also carries the short form, which stays hidden.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Explain the 3-to-1 count in the second generation.
**Answer: Each first-generation plant is Tt, carrying the hidden short form. Crossing Tt with Tt gives TT, Tt, Tt, tt — three showing tall and one (tt) showing short.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) What two things drive evolution, as India's book teaches it?
**Answer: Inherited variation, which appears in every generation, and selection, which keeps the variations that help a living thing survive and reproduce, until populations can split into new species.**
