Today: **Asexual reproduction — budding, fission and spores** — Nicaragua's way, from its book (Nicaragua, Grade 8 Módulo — *Módulo Autoformativo de Ciencias Naturales, 8vo grado*, printed pp. 5–6).

Nicaragua's Grade 8 Módulo Autoformativo teaches cell reproduction without gametes: **bipartition (binary fission)** in bacteria, yeasts, unicellular algae and protozoa — one cell splits into two; **budding (gemación)** — a small bud grows out of the parent and separates; and **sporulation** — new individuals form from spores. No gametes join, so the offspring are copies of the single parent.

Nicaragua's Grade 4 book teaches plant reproduction two ways: **vegetative (asexual)** reproduction — no union of cells, the offspring identical; vegetative multiplication keeps well-adapted individuals, passing on a plant that already thrives where it grows — set against sexual reproduction, in which pollination brings the flower's parts together when flowers lack stamens or pistil, carried by wind.
