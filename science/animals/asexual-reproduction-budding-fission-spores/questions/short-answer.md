# Asexual reproduction — budding, fission and spores — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Explain, in your own words: Cell reproduction without gametes — fission, budding, sporulation.
**Answer:** Nicaragua's Grade 8 Módulo Autoformativo teaches cell reproduction without gametes: **bipartition (binary fission)** in bacteria, yeasts, unicellular algae and protozoa — one cell splits into two; **budding (gemación)** — a small bud grows out of the parent and separates; and **sporulation** — new individuals form from spores. No gametes join, so the offspring are copies of the single parent.

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) Explain, in your own words: Vegetative reproduction in plants — copies that keep what works.
**Answer:** Nicaragua's Grade 4 book teaches plant reproduction two ways: **vegetative (asexual)** reproduction — no union of cells, the offspring identical; vegetative multiplication keeps well-adapted individuals, passing on a plant that already thrives where it grows — set against sexual reproduction, in which pollination brings the flower's parts together when flowers lack stamens or pistil, carried by wind.

**Answer:** Cell reproduction without gametes — fission, budding, sporulation → Vegetative reproduction in plants — copies that keep what works — in that order, as taught in lesson/lesson.md.
