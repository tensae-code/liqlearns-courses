# Body cavities — acoelomate, pseudocoelomate, coelomate — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** Explain: acoelomate — no cavity.
**Answer: In acoelomate animals there is no body cavity at all: the space between the gut and the body wall is packed solid with tissue. The flatworms are the standing example — flat, with no room inside for organs to lie in a fluid-filled space, and every cell kept near the surface or the gut.**

**2.** Explain: pseudocoelomate — a false cavity.
**Answer: Pseudocoelomate animals have a cavity between gut and body wall, but it is not lined all round by mesoderm tissue — a false coelom. The roundworms and their kin, and small phyla such as the rotifers, sit on this rung of the ladder.**
