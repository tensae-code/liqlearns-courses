Today: Body cavities — acoelomate, pseudocoelomate, coelomate.

Remember this one thing: Animals are sorted by the cavity inside the body wall: acoelomates have no cavity — the gut sits in solid tissue; pseudocoelomates have a cavity not lined by mesoderm; coelomates have a true coelom, a cavity fully lined by mesoderm, in which the organs lie and move.

In acoelomate animals there is no body cavity at all: the space between the gut and the body wall is packed solid with tissue. The flatworms are the standing example — flat, with no room inside for organs to lie in a fluid-filled space, and every cell kept near the surface or the gut.

Pseudocoelomate animals have a cavity between gut and body wall, but it is not lined all round by mesoderm tissue — a false coelom. The roundworms and their kin, and small phyla such as the rotifers, sit on this rung of the ladder.

In coelomate animals the cavity is a true coelom: a space fully lined by mesoderm, holding the organs in fluid so they can move, grow and work partly independently of the body wall. Annelids, molluscs, arthropods, echinoderms and chordates — the great phyla — are coelomate.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
