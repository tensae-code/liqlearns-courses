# Body cavities — acoelomate, pseudocoelomate, coelomate

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Body cavities — acoelomate, pseudocoelomate, coelomate. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Fiji's Year 13 Biology book runs its whole animal survey up one ladder: the body cavity. Phylum by phylum, from Cnidaria to Chordata, the first question it asks of each group is what kind of inside it has — no cavity, a false cavity, or a true coelom — and the phyla fall into their order on those three rungs.

## The key facts
**Acoelomate — no cavity.** In acoelomate animals there is no body cavity at all: the space between the gut and the body wall is packed solid with tissue. The flatworms are the standing example — flat, with no room inside for organs to lie in a fluid-filled space, and every cell kept near the surface or the gut.

**Pseudocoelomate — a false cavity.** Pseudocoelomate animals have a cavity between gut and body wall, but it is not lined all round by mesoderm tissue — a false coelom. The roundworms and their kin, and small phyla such as the rotifers, sit on this rung of the ladder.

**Coelomate — the true coelom.** In coelomate animals the cavity is a true coelom: a space fully lined by mesoderm, holding the organs in fluid so they can move, grow and work partly independently of the body wall. Annelids, molluscs, arthropods, echinoderms and chordates — the great phyla — are coelomate.

**Fiji's way.** The ladder is the organising spine of the Year 13 book's whole Animalia survey (§3.2.6): diploblastic animals and Cnidaria, then the triploblastic acoelomates, then the pseudocoelomate phyla — Rotifera, Nematomorpha, Loricifera, Acanthocephala among them — then the coelomates phylum by phylum. Learn the three rungs and the survey's order explains itself.
