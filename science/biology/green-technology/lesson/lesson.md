# Green technology

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Green technology. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Malaysia's Form 5 book names green technology as a sustainability practice in its own right (printed p. 218), at the close of its environment chapter: after threats to the environment and the preservation, conservation and restoration of ecosystems, the chapter turns to the technology that is built to do less damage.

[Picture — a landscape combining solar panels, a wind turbine, a recycling plant and a replanted forest slope, labelled as green technology in practice]

## The key facts
**What 'green' means here.** A technology is green when it is designed to lessen harm to the environment — cleaner energy, less waste, fewer resources used — not merely when it is new or efficient for its owner.

**Where it applies.** Green technology runs across the chapter's ground: renewable energy in place of fossil fuels, treatment and recycling of waste, buildings and transport that use less, and technology that helps restore damaged ecosystems.

**Sustainability practice.** The book teaches it as practice, not slogan: green technology is one of the practices of environmental sustainability, standing beside preservation, conservation and restoration of ecosystems.

**Malaysia's way.** Malaysia's KSSM Form 5 book closes its sustainability chapter (Ch.10) with green technology by name (printed p. 218) — the Malaysian course's own term, taught as the technological arm of sustainability.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words: green technology is technology designed to protect the environment — producing energy and goods and handling waste in ways that use fewer resources and do less harm, so development can be sustained. Use each key fact once. Then let your companion explain it back to you.
