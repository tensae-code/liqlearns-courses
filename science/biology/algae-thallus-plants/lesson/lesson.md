# Algae — thallus plants

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Algae — thallus plants. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Bulgaria's Grade 7 book builds the plant kingdom by body plan first (Topic 15, printed p. 33): after the Kingdom Plants overview (Topic 14) come the thallus plants — the algae — whose body is a thallus, and only after them the cormus plants (Topic 16), whose bodies are made of tissues and organs. The split between thallus and cormus is the book's own first fork inside the plant kingdom.

[Picture — two plants side by side: a flat green alga labelled as one thallus body, and a flowering plant labelled root, stem and leaves]

## The key facts
**The thallus.** A thallus is a plant body that is not divided into root, stem and leaves. The whole body does the work of living — taking in water and light and making food — without separate organs for each job.

**Algae — the thallus plants.** Algae are the thallus plants: the group of the plant kingdom whose members carry the thallus body plan. Bulgaria's book gives them their own topic (Topic 15) as the first plant group after the kingdom overview.

**Thallus against cormus.** The book sets the thallus plants against the cormus plants that follow (Topic 16): cormus plants build their bodies from plant tissues and true organs — roots, stems and leaves. One fork, two body plans: thallus first, then cormus.

**Bulgaria's way.** Bulgaria's Pedagog 6 Grade 7 book teaches the plant kingdom as a five-kingdoms diversity year, and inside the plants it sorts by body plan before it sorts by division: thallus plants — algae (Topic 15, printed p. 33) — then cormus plants and their tissues (Topic 16) and organs (Topics 17–18), then the spore plants and seed plants.
