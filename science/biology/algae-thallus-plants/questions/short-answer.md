# Algae — thallus plants — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** State the one thing this lesson teaches and explain it.
**Answer: algae are thallus plants: their whole body is a thallus — one simple plant body that is not divided into root, stem and leaves — the simplest body plan in the plant kingdom, set against the cormus plants whose bodies are built of tissues and true organs. A thallus is a plant body that is not divided into root, stem and leaves. The whole body does the work of living — taking in water and light and making food — without separate organs for each job.**

**2.** Explain Bulgaria's way of teaching this.
**Answer: Bulgaria's Pedagog 6 Grade 7 book teaches the plant kingdom as a five-kingdoms diversity year, and inside the plants it sorts by body plan before it sorts by division: thallus plants — algae (Topic 15, printed p. 33) — then cormus plants and their tissues (Topic 16) and organs (Topics 17–18), then the spore plants and seed plants.**
