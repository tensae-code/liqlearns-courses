# Plant uses and library work

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Plant uses and library work. Write down every use of a plant you can name that is NOT food — from your home, your street, your school. Keep your list. Then read on and check it against what the lesson teaches: this lesson is the doing that closes the unit — group work and a library search that extend the uses of plants beyond food.

## The idea
Unit 2 has followed plants all the way from their parts to their responses. Its plant-uses thread does not end with a list to memorise. It ends with work: Activity 2.20 sends you out in a group to gather the uses of plants beyond food, and Activity 2.21 sends you into the library to search them out and prove them. Together they close the unit's content, just before its summary.

## The key facts
**Beyond food.** Plants serve people in more ways than food. The unit has already shown one family of uses — Ethiopia's medicinal plants, recorded by local name, kind, part used, how taken and condition treated. Food and medicine are only the start of the list this lesson extends.

**Activity 2.20 — group work.** The first closing activity is group work: learners work together to collect uses of plants beyond food, pooling what each person knows from home and locality so the group's list is longer and better checked than any one person's.

**Activity 2.21 — the library search.** The second closing activity is a library search: learners take the questions the group work raised into the library and search books for the answers — finding plant uses in print, noting them properly, and bringing them back as evidence, not just opinion.

**Closing the unit.** These two activities close the unit's content. After them comes only the unit summary — so this lesson is where the unit's knowledge is gathered, tested and completed by the learners themselves.

## How a library search is done well
1. **Choose the question first.** A search without a question wanders. Write the exact use or plant you are searching for before you open a book.
2. **Find it in a book.** Search the library's biology and plant books for your plant or use. When you find it, do not just read it — note it.
3. **Note it properly.** For every use you find, write down the plant, the use, and where you found it — the book and the page — so anyone can check your finding.
4. **Bring it back to the group.** Report what you found to your group and class: the use, the plant, and the evidence. A use with a book and page behind it is worth more than a use someone only claims.
