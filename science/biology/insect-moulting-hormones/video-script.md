Today: Insect moulting hormones.

Remember this one thing: An insect's metamorphosis is decided by two hormones pulling opposite ways: Kenya's Form 3 book closes its growth chapter with juvenile hormone from the corpora allata, which holds the insect young, and ecdysone (moulting hormone) from the prothoracic glands, which drives the moult — and their balance decides larva, pupa or adult.

Every moult is a decision, and hormones make it. Ecdysone, the moulting hormone from the prothoracic glands, says: shed the skin, move on. Juvenile hormone, from the corpora allata behind the brain, says what to become after the moult: while it flows strongly, the insect moults into another larva — bigger, but the same. When juvenile hormone falls away, ecdysone's moult produces the pupa, and the next produces the adult. Kenya teaches metamorphosis as this balance — two glands, two hormones, and the whole life history hanging on their proportions.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
