# Identifying organisms with a key

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Identifying organisms with a key. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
You do not need to know a specimen's name first. Answer each pair of choices honestly and the key walks you to the name.

[Picture 21016 — a branching key with one animal's path highlighted]

[GIF 21107 — a choice being made at each fork until the name is reached]

## The key facts
**Paired choices.** A dichotomous key works by paired either/or choices at each step.

**Step by step.** Each answer leads step by step to an identification.

**Features.** The choices are about the specimen's contrasting features.

**Keys in the book.** The book works keys for vertebrates and for invertebrates.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words: a dichotomous key identifies an organism through paired either/or choices. Use each key fact once. Then let your companion explain it back to you.

## Do It Yourself

2. Write each key fact in your own words, without looking.
3. Look back, fix anything you missed, and say the whole lesson aloud once more.
