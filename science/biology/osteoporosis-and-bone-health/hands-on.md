# Hands-on — Osteoporosis and bone health

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: in osteoporosis bones lose mass and mineral salts such as calcium, so fracture risk rises and the spine can curve — bone health is the health of the bone's material, not only its shape.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a healthy bone and an osteoporotic bone side by side in cross-section, the second with visibly thinner, sparser bone tissue, and a spine outline showing curvature. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
