# How exocrine glands are controlled

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — How exocrine glands are controlled. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it — why your mouth waters before the food arrives, and who gives that order — and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Regulation does not end with the endocrine glands. North Macedonia's Grade 13 elective book closes its regulation chapter with the functioning of the exocrine glands as its own topic (Ch. 4.11, printed pp. 232–239) — the ducted glands, and how their secretion is controlled. No lesson in this course covered exocrine-gland control before, so this lesson is built for it.

[Picture — a salivary gland with its duct opening into the mouth, arrows running to it from a nerve and from a blood vessel carrying a hormone — the two control routes arriving at one gland, and drops of saliva leaving by the duct]

## The key facts
**Exocrine means out through a duct.** An exocrine gland releases its product through a duct onto a surface or into a cavity: salivary glands into the mouth, sweat glands onto the skin, gastric and intestinal glands into the gut, the pancreas's digestive part into the intestine. (Endocrine glands, by contrast, release hormones into the blood, with no duct.)

**Secretion is switched, not constant.** Exocrine glands do not pour at a steady rate. Saliva flows at the sight, smell and thought of food and slows in sleep; sweat starts with heat; digestive juices meet the meal. The product is made and released when the body calls for it.

**Nerves give the fast orders.** The nervous system controls many exocrine glands directly and quickly — the mouth watering before the first bite is a reflex order to the salivary glands, and sweating in fear or heat is likewise nerve-driven.

**Hormones give the timed orders.** Other secretions are timed by hormones: the arrival of food in the gut triggers hormones that order the stomach, pancreas and gall-bladder in sequence, so each juice meets the food at the right stage — nervous control for speed, hormonal control for sequence.

**North Macedonia's way.** North Macedonia's elective book teaches the whole systems year as regulation, and its regulation chapter closes the gland story in order: endocrine regulation first (Ch. 4.9), regulation of reproduction (Ch. 4.10), and the functioning of the exocrine glands last (Ch. 4.11, printed pp. 232–239) — the ducted glands given their own control lesson after the ductless ones.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words: Exocrine glands pour their products out through ducts — saliva, sweat, digestive juices — and their work is switched and timed by the nervous system and by hormones, so secretion happens when and where it is needed.
