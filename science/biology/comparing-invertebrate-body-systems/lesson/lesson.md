# Comparing invertebrate body systems

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Comparing invertebrate body systems. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Afghanistan's Grade 11 Biology book does something distinctive after classifying the invertebrates: a whole chapter comparing their body systems across the phyla. This lesson teaches that comparison on its own — system by system, phylum by phylum — the table a learner can use to place any invertebrate.

## The key facts
**Feeding and digestion.** A one-way tube is the great advance: food enters at the mouth, is digested in specialised regions, absorbed, and wastes leave at the anus — so an earthworm or a grasshopper can eat and digest at the same time, while a hydra must finish one meal before the next.

**Breathing.** Where the body is thin and wet, gases cross the skin by diffusion. Aquatic molluscs and crustaceans grow gills; insects pipe air directly to their tissues through tracheae opening at spiracles; spiders add book lungs — each system answers the same need at a larger body size.

**Transport.** In an open system the blood leaves the vessels and bathes the tissues directly — slow, but enough for a slug or a crab. In the closed system of the earthworm (and the octopus among molluscs), blood stays in vessels driven by hearts, and reaches every part faster and under control.

**Control — nervous systems.** A hydra's nerve net coordinates without any centre. Flatworms gather cords and a simple brain; earthworms and insects run a double ventral nerve cord with a ganglion in each segment and a brain in the head — faster, directed behaviour becomes possible at each step.

**Excretion.** Each phylum's excretory organ fits its home: flame cells and nephridia work in water-rich bodies, while the insect's Malpighian tubules pour waste into the gut so almost no water is lost with it — excretion fitted to life on dry land.

**Afghanistan's way.** The Grade 11 book's comparative-systems chapters are its signature — first classify each animal grade, then compare the same systems across it. This lesson is the invertebrate half of that way of teaching.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words: Across the invertebrate phyla the same life jobs — feeding, breathing, transport, control, excretion — are done by ever more complete systems, from diffusion alone in the simplest animals to closed vessels, brains and kidneys in the highest. Use each key fact once. Then let your companion explain it back to you.
