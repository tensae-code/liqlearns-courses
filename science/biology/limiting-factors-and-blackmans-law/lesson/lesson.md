# Limiting factors and Blackman's law

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Limiting factors and Blackman's law. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Photosynthesis needs several things at once — light, carbon dioxide, warmth — and its rate is set by whichever of them is in shortest supply: that is Blackman's law of limiting factors, which says the rate is governed by the factor nearest its minimum, so raising any other factor does nothing until the limiting one is raised. No lesson in this course taught this on its own before, so this lesson is built for it — from Bhutan's Class 10 book, which teaches it as a named topic.

[Picture — a graph of photosynthesis rate against light intensity rising then flattening, beside the same rising line flattening earlier at a lower level, labelled as carbon dioxide becoming the limiting factor]

## The key facts
**Several needs at once.** Photosynthesis runs on light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration and temperature together. Give a plant more of one and the rate may rise — but only so far.

**Blackman's law.** Blackman's law of limiting factors states that when a process depends on several factors, its rate is limited by the factor that is nearest its minimum value. The scarcest need sets the speed; the others cannot make up for it.

**Reading the flattening graph.** As light rises, the rate rises — then flattens. The flattening means light is no longer limiting: something else, usually carbon dioxide or temperature, has become the limiting factor. Raising the light further changes nothing until that factor is raised.

**Using the products.** The lesson closes with what the plant does with what photosynthesis makes — the utilisation of photosynthesis products: glucose used for energy, built into starch for storage, and into the plant's other substances.

**Bhutan's way.** Bhutan's Class 10 book states Blackman's law by name (Ch. 2.1, printed pp. 37–38) right after the factors affecting the rate, and pairs the law with the utilisation of the products — the law and the harvest in one place.
