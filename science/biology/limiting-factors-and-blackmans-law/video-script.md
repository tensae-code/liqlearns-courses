Today: Limiting factors and Blackman's law.

Remember this one thing: photosynthesis is only as fast as its scarcest need.

A plant needs light, carbon dioxide and warmth at the same time. Raise the light and the rate climbs — then stops. It stops because something else has run short. That is Blackman's law: the factor nearest its minimum sets the rate.

So a grower who wants more growth first asks: what is limiting now? Raise that one, and the rate can climb again.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
