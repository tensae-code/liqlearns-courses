# Nematodes — Ascaris, hookworm and filaria

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Nematodes — Ascaris, hookworm and filaria. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Mozambique's animal module gives the nematode worms, phylum Nemathelminthes, three lessons: the phylum's plan, then its parasites. Unlike the flatworms, a nematode's gut is a complete tube. The cuticle lets parasites survive inside their hosts, and parasitic kinds run an anaerobic metabolism. Then the module works Ascaris, the hookworm and the filaria in turn — each with its own route into the human body. This lesson teaches the plan and the three parasites together, as the module does.

## The key facts
**The nematode plan.** Nematodes are cylindrical worms with a pseudocoelom (a false body cavity) and a complete gut with mouth and anus. A tough cuticle protects them — it is what lets the parasites survive inside a host — and parasitic nematodes run an anaerobic metabolism.

**Ascaris — the lombriga.** Ascaris, the big roundworm (lombriga), is worked through its cycle; its symptoms include cough, fever, lung lesions and intestinal pain.

**Hookworm — Ancilostoma.** The hookworm sucks blood: its head is curved and carries teeth. Its larvae enter the body by penetrating the skin.

**Filaria — Wuchereria bancrofti.** The adults live in the lymph vessels. The larvae appear in the peripheral blood at night, a mosquito carries them between people, and the disease shows elephantiasis-type symptoms — gross swelling.
