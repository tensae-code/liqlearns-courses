# Nematodes — Ascaris, hookworm and filaria — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** How does the nematode gut differ from the flatworm gut?
**Answer: The flatworm gut has a single opening serving as both mouth and exit. The nematode gut is a complete tube — a mouth at one end and an anus at the other — so food passes through in one direction.**

**2.** Explain Mozambique's way of teaching this.
**Answer: Mozambique's PESD II animal module (Módulo 3 de Biologia), Lessons 6–8, teaches phylum Nemathelminthes by its plan — cylindrical, pseudocoelomate, complete gut, protective cuticle, anaerobic metabolism in parasites — then works its three parasites in turn: Ascaris and its symptoms, the blood-sucking hookworm whose larvae penetrate skin, and Wuchereria bancrofti, the mosquito-carried filaria of the lymph vessels.**
