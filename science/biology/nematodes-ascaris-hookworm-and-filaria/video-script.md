Today: Nematodes — Ascaris, hookworm and filaria.

Remember this one thing: nematodes are cylindrical pseudocoelomate worms with a complete gut — mouth and anus — and a tough cuticle; the module works three parasites one by one: Ascaris, the hookworm that sucks blood and enters through the skin, and Wuchereria bancrofti, the filaria carried by mosquitoes whose larvae appear in the peripheral blood at night.

The nematodes are round in cross-section, like tiny tubes, and their gut is a complete tube — mouth at one end, anus at the other. A tough skin, the cuticle, protects them inside their hosts. The book works three of them. Ascaris, the big roundworm. The hookworm, which bites with a curved, toothed head and drinks blood — its young enter through your skin. And the filaria, carried by mosquitoes, whose larvae come out into the blood at night.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
