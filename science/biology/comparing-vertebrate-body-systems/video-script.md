Today: Comparing vertebrate body systems.

Remember this one thing: Across the five vertebrate groups the same systems are built to one plan and tuned differently — clearest in the heart: two chambers in fish, three in amphibians and most reptiles, four in crocodiles, birds and mammals, driving a single circulation in fish and a double one on land.

A fish's heart (one atrium, one ventricle) drives blood one way round. Amphibians and most reptiles mix some blood in a three-chambered heart (two atria, one ventricle). Four chambers — in crocodiles, birds and mammals — keep oxygen-rich and oxygen-poor blood fully apart.

In the fish, blood passes through the heart once per circuit: heart → gills → body → heart. In amphibians, reptiles, birds and mammals it passes twice: the lung circuit and the body circuit — which lets the body circuit run at higher pressure than the delicate lungs allow.

Water breathers extract oxygen with gills. The frog lives both lives — gills as a tadpole, lungs and a moist skin as an adult. Fully land groups depend on lungs; the bird's air-sac system passes air through the lungs in one direction, feeding flight's huge oxygen demand.
