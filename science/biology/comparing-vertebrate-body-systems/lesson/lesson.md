# Comparing vertebrate body systems

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Comparing vertebrate body systems. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Afghanistan's Grade 11 Biology book gives the vertebrates the same treatment as the invertebrates: classify them, then compare their systems in a chapter of its own. This lesson teaches that comparison — heart and circulation first, then breathing, temperature, reproduction and control — as the pattern that explains the five groups.

## The key facts
**The heart, group by group.** A fish's heart (one atrium, one ventricle) drives blood one way round. Amphibians and most reptiles mix some blood in a three-chambered heart (two atria, one ventricle). Four chambers — in crocodiles, birds and mammals — keep oxygen-rich and oxygen-poor blood fully apart.

**Single and double circulation.** In the fish, blood passes through the heart once per circuit: heart → gills → body → heart. In amphibians, reptiles, birds and mammals it passes twice: the lung circuit and the body circuit — which lets the body circuit run at higher pressure than the delicate lungs allow.

**Breathing across the groups.** Water breathers extract oxygen with gills. The frog lives both lives — gills as a tadpole, lungs and a moist skin as an adult. Fully land groups depend on lungs; the bird's air-sac system passes air through the lungs in one direction, feeding flight's huge oxygen demand.

**Body temperature.** An ectotherm's activity rises and falls with the weather — the lizard basks to work. Birds and mammals burn food to hold their temperature steady, paying for freedom from the weather with a much larger appetite.

**Reproduction and care.** The shelled (amniotic) egg freed reptiles and birds from returning to water to breed. Mammals went further — development inside the mother and milk after birth — the highest survival per young, bought with the longest care.

**Afghanistan's way.** The vertebrate half of the Grade 11 comparative-systems way — after the vertebrate groups are classified, the same systems are compared across them, so the learner sees one body plan tuned five ways.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words: Across the five vertebrate groups the same systems are built to one plan and tuned differently — clearest in the heart: two chambers in fish, three in amphibians and most reptiles, four in crocodiles, birds and mammals, driving a single circulation in fish and a double one on land. Use each key fact once. Then let your companion explain it back to you.
