# Natural selection and speciation

## Do it first
Before you read: think of one living thing near your home that shows this lesson's idea — natural selection and speciation. Hold it in your mind, then read on and check your idea against what the lesson teaches.

Directional selection is exemplified by the Grants' Galápagos finch study: in the 1977 drought, seed plants produced fewer, larger, tougher seeds, and finches with larger beaks were selected within a single season; speciation is tabled — allopatric (geographically separated, slow, common in nature, driven by natural selection) vs sympatric (not separated, fast with autopolyploidy and slow with allopolyploidy, common in plants) — with divergent evolution and macroevolution named as diversity-builders.
