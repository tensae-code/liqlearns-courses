# Cytokinesis — dividing the cytoplasm

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Cytokinesis — dividing the cytoplasm. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it — how one cell finishes becoming two — the nucleus divides, but what divides the rest? — and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Cell division is two acts, not one: the nucleus divides (karyokinesis), and then the cytoplasm divides — cytokinesis. North Macedonia's Biology I gives cytokinesis a separate lesson from nuclear division (Theme 2.11, printed p. 81), right after karyokinesis in its genetics theme. No lesson in this course separated cytokinesis out before, so this lesson is built for it.

[Picture — an animal cell pinched in the middle by a deepening furrow until it parts in two, beside a plant cell with a new wall plate growing across its middle — both ending as two cells, each with its own nucleus]

## The key facts
**Division has two acts.** First the nucleus divides — karyokinesis, the mitosis of the earlier lessons. Then the cytoplasm divides — cytokinesis. Only when both are done has one cell become two cells.

**In animal cells, a furrow pinches.** The animal cell's membrane is drawn in around the middle, forming a cleavage furrow that deepens until the cell is pinched into two daughter cells, each with its nucleus and its share of cytoplasm and organelles.

**In plant cells, a plate grows.** A plant cell's stiff wall cannot pinch. Instead a cell plate forms across the middle of the cell and grows outward, becoming the new wall that separates the two daughter cells.

**The result: two complete cells.** Cytokinesis shares out the cytoplasm and organelles between the two nuclei made by karyokinesis. If cytokinesis fails, the result is one cell with two nuclei — division begun but not finished.

**North Macedonia's way.** North Macedonia's Biology I refuses to let cytokinesis hide inside mitosis: nuclear division (karyokinesis) is Theme 2.10 and cytokinesis is its own lesson, Theme 2.11 (printed p. 81), in the foundations-of-genetics theme — first the nucleus is divided, then, separately, the rest of the cell.
