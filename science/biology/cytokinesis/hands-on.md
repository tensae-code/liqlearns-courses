# Hands-on — Cytokinesis — dividing the cytoplasm

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Model it with two balls of clay. Form one ball with a bead (the nucleus) pressed in. First divide the bead into two beads inside the clay — that is karyokinesis. Now pinch the clay in the middle until it parts around each bead — that is the animal cell's cytokinesis. Say aloud which act each step was.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — an animal cell pinched in the middle by a deepening furrow until it parts in two, beside a plant cell with a new wall plate growing across its middle — both ending as two cells, each with its own nucleus — and check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
