# Hands-on — Support in plants — tissues and turgor

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Take the lesson's key facts for a walk: find one example of this lesson's idea in your own surroundings — your home, street, market or school — and say which key fact it matches, and why.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory — a stem cut across and magnified — soft parenchyma cells drawn as water-filled balloons pressing outward, collenchyma with thickened corners, rope-like sclerenchyma fibres, and hollow xylem vessels standing like pipes — beside two whole plants: a soft herbaceous stem wilting as its cells lose water, and a woody stem standing with a tendril curled around a stick — label its parts, and check against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
