## The key facts
**Antigens.** Antigens A and B define groups A and B; AB carries both and O carries neither.

**Rhesus factor.** The Rhesus factor is a further inherited red-cell antigen; about 85 percent of people are Rh-positive.

**Incompatibility.** Rh incompatibility matters when an Rh-negative mother carries an Rh-positive baby.

**Donation link.** Blood groups decide who can safely donate blood to whom.

## From the student book — the pupil-taught core
The student book fixes the ABO groups by antigens on the red cells and antibodies in the plasma, reads donation off the compatibility chart, then adds the Rhesus factor — including what happens when an Rh-negative mother carries an Rh-positive baby.

### Student-book key facts
**Antigens and antibodies.** The ABO groups are fixed by the antigens on the red cells and the antibodies in the plasma.

**Compatibility chart.** The compatibility chart shows who can give blood to and receive from whom.

**Rh adds + or −.** The Rhesus factor adds + or − to each blood group.

**Erythroblastosis fetalis.** Rh incompatibility between a mother and her baby explains erythroblastosis fetalis and the anaemia it causes.

**Safe donation.** Donation is taught against the compatibility chart, so giving is safe.
