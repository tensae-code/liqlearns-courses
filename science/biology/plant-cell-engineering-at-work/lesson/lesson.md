# Plant cell engineering at work

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Plant cell engineering at work. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Plant cell engineering at work: micropropagation multiplying orchids and medicinal plants in tubes, virus-free stock from shoot-tip culture yielding over 50% more potatoes, haploid breeding through anther culture, useful mutants selected from cultured cells, and cell-product factories growing shikonin, taxol and ginseng saponins. No lesson in this course taught this on its own before, so this lesson is built for it — from China's People's Education Press biology textbooks, which teach it as a named topic.

[Picture — shelves of culture tubes and jars holding tiny green plantlets, with a potato plant and a medicine bottle at the end of the shelf]

## The key facts
**Ch. 2 §1 Applications of plant cell engineering.** micropropagation (the Dutch orchid industry; Dendrobium candidum tube plantlets at scale in China); virus-free stock from shoot-tip culture (potatoes yielding over 50% more); haploid breeding through anther culture — China's Danyu No. 1 tobacco of 1974, the world's first haploid crop variety; useful mutants selected from cultured cells; and cell-product factories — shikonin, the first marketed plant-cell product (1980s), taxol from cultured yew cells, ginseng saponins cultured in China since 1963.

**China's way.** micropropagation (the Dutch orchid industry; Dendrobium candidum tube plantlets at scale in China); virus-free stock from shoot-tip culture (potatoes yielding over 50% more); haploid breeding through anther culture — China's Danyu No. 1 tobacco of 1974, the world's first haploid crop variety; useful mutants selected from cultured cells; and cell-product factories — shikonin, the first marketed plant-cell product (1980s), taxol from cultured yew cells, ginseng saponins cultured in China since 1963.
