Today: Lung volumes and capacities.

Remember this one thing: a quiet breath uses only a small part of your lungs — the rest is measured in named volumes and capacities.

One quiet breath in or out moves the tidal volume. Breathe in harder and you use the inspiratory reserve; breathe out harder and you use the expiratory reserve. And even after the hardest out-breath, some air always stays — the residual volume.

Add volumes together and you get capacities. The famous one is vital capacity: the deepest breath in, then force everything out that you can. That total is a measure of a working lung.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
