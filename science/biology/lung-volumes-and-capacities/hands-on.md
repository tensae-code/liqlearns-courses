# Hands-on — Lung volumes and capacities

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Blow up a balloon with one deepest-possible breath out after one deepest breath in — that balloon holds roughly your vital capacity. Compare balloons with a companion: bigger lungs, bigger balloon. Then blow up a balloon with one quiet breath — the tidal volume is far smaller.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — the breathing trace with tidal volume, both reserves, residual volume and vital capacity bracketed — and check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
