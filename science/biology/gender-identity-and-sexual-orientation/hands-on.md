# Hands-on — Gender identity and sexual orientation

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Take two sheets of paper. On the first, write a stereotype — an expectation your society pins on a gender — and label it "stereotype". On the second, write how the Khasi counter-example answers it: a matrilineal society, the family line through the mother. Hold the two side by side and say, for each one, which part of the manual it comes from.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — the two panels and the timeline strip: stereotypes pinned as labels, the Khasi family line through the mother, and LGBT rights in France marked as evolving. Check your drawing against the lesson and fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap. End with this one together: why does a biology manual put identity and orientation inside a chapter on the brain, pleasure and sexuality? Say what the manual's own placement tells you.
