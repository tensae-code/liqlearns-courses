# Lesson plan — Gender identity and sexual orientation

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Biology · **Lesson:** Gender identity and sexual orientation
**Place in the curriculum:** Biology — appended to Unit 16 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** France's Seconde manual teaches identity and orientation inside its "Cerveau, plaisir et sexualité" (Brain, pleasure and sexuality) chapter, in the part "Identités et orientations": what gender is, gender stereotypes — with the matrilineal Khasi society as the counter-example that the roles attached to a gender are not the same everywhere — attraction (sexual orientation), and the evolution of LGBT rights in France (France, Grade 10 — *SVT 2de GT — Manuel Libre de Sciences de la vie et de la Terre*, student manual, Thème 3 / "Cerveau, plaisir et sexualité" / "Identités et orientations" — repository path as cited in the placements)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands beside the course's sex-determination and puberty lessons and adds the half they do not teach: identity and orientation as France's programme itself teaches them, inside the biology of the brain and sexuality. How sex is determined — XX and XY is recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: **How sex is determined — XX and XY**. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words, the one thing this lesson teaches: France's manual teaches what gender is, gender stereotypes, attraction and the evolution of LGBT rights in France, inside its brain, pleasure and sexuality chapter.
2. Say what a gender stereotype is, and give the manual's counter-example: the Khasi, a matrilineal society.
3. Say what attraction — sexual orientation — means in the manual's teaching.
4. Say what the manual traces about LGBT rights in France: that they have evolved over time.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.
