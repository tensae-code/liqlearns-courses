# Gender identity and sexual orientation

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Gender identity and sexual orientation. Write down, or say aloud, what you think the word "gender" means, one expectation your society attaches to a gender, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
France's Seconde SVT manual teaches the human body and health in its Thème 3. Inside the chapter "Cerveau, plaisir et sexualité" (Brain, pleasure and sexuality) there is a part called "Identités et orientations" — Identities and orientations. That is where this lesson comes from: France teaches identity and orientation as part of the biology course, next to the brain and sexuality.

[Picture — two panels and a timeline strip: on the left, stereotype expectations written as labels pinned on two figures; on the right, the Khasi society as a family line passing through the mother; under both, a strip marked "LGBT rights in France — evolving"]

## The key facts
**What gender is.** The manual opens the part by asking what gender is — a person's gender as its own question, taught before any stereotype is named.

**Gender stereotypes.** A stereotype is an expectation a society pins on a gender — how people of that gender are supposed to be and to behave. The manual teaches stereotypes as things to examine, not as facts of nature.

**The Khasi counter-example.** The manual sets against the stereotypes the Khasi society — a matrilineal society, where descent and the family line pass through the mother. One whole society organised the other way is enough to show that the roles attached to a gender are made by societies, not fixed by biology.

**Attraction — sexual orientation.** The manual teaches attraction: who a person is drawn to. That is sexual orientation, and the manual teaches it in the same part, under "orientations".

**LGBT rights in France have evolved.** The part closes by tracing the evolution of LGBT rights in France — the rights have changed over time, and the manual presents that history as part of the lesson.

**France's way.** In France this is a Seconde (Grade 10) biology lesson: identities and orientations sit inside "Cerveau, plaisir et sexualité", the same chapter that teaches the nerve cell and the biological bases of pleasure.

Use each key fact once. Then let your companion explain it back to you.
