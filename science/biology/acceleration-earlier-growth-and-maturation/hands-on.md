# Hands-on — Acceleration — earlier growth and maturation

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: children in recent generations grow taller and mature faster than earlier generations — acceleration — read off height-by-age graphs for boys and girls: a change in the pace of development itself, not in its stages.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: two height-by-age growth curves on one graph, an earlier generation's curve lying below a recent generation's curve for boys and for girls, with the gap between them labelled acceleration. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
