# Hands-on — The fluid mosaic model of the cell membrane

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: the cell membrane is a fluid mosaic: a double layer of phospholipids that can flow, with proteins scattered through it like tiles in a mosaic — and each kind of membrane protein does its own job.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a cell membrane drawn as two rows of phospholipid heads and tails with protein shapes embedded in it — a channel, a carrier, a receptor and a marker protein each labelled with its job. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
