# Hands-on — DNA in mitochondria and chloroplasts

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: mitochondria and chloroplasts carry their own small circular DNA — a leftover of their origin as free bacteria — so they make some of their own proteins and, in animals, are inherited through the egg alone.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a mitochondrion and a chloroplast drawn side by side, each with a small circular DNA loop glowing inside it. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
