# Regulation of gene expression

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Regulation of gene expression. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Every cell of a body carries the same genes, yet a muscle cell and a nerve cell are nothing alike. The difference is regulation: which genes each cell expresses. Italy's Grade 11 Campbell, *Biologia: concetti e collegamenti, Secondo biennio*, gives that control its own whole unit — Unità 2, Regulation of gene expression (PDF p. 46) — straight after the molecular biology of the gene. No lesson in this course taught gene regulation on its own before, so this lesson is built for it.

[Picture — one genome drawn as a long control board of gene switches — in one cell only the “muscle” switches are on, in another only the “nerve” switches, though the board itself is identical]

## The key facts
**Gene expression is a gene in use.** A gene is expressed when the cell reads it and makes its product — the RNA and protein the gene specifies. A gene that is not being read is present but silent.

**Regulation is control of that use.** Regulation of gene expression is the cell's control over which genes are expressed, in which cells, at which times, and to what degree — genes switched on, switched off, or turned up and down.

**Same genome, different cells.** Because regulation differs from cell to cell, cells with identical genes become different kinds of cell: each kind expresses the set of genes its job requires and keeps the rest silent.

**Regulation is a unit, not a remark.** The book gives regulation a whole unit of its own (Unità 2), placed immediately after the molecular biology of the gene (Unità 1): first how genes work, then how their working is governed.

**Italy's way.** In Italy's Grade 11 Campbell, gene expression is not left as “genes make proteins”: a full unit teaches the control layer — regulation — as one of the central ideas of molecular biology, on a level with the gene itself.
