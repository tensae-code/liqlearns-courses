# Hands-on — Regulation of gene expression

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Take a songbook or recipe book you know. Say aloud: the book is the genome, one song or recipe being sung or cooked is a gene being expressed, and choosing which ones to use — and which to leave closed — is regulation. Then name two cell kinds and one gene set each would keep silent.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — the identical gene-switch board in two cells, with different switches on in each — and label them “same genome”, “muscle genes on” and “nerve genes on”. Check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
