# Cells as colonies and filaments — Volvox and Spirogyra — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** Explain: colony against filament, using Volvox and Spirogyra.
**Answer: In a colony such as Volvox, many cells (up to 20,000) live together in one hollow ball, each cell alike and able to live on its own. In a filament such as Spirogyra, the cells stay joined end to end in a thread, each with a spiral chloroplast. Both are steps between a single cell and a tissue: cells living together, but not yet specialised and interdependent as tissue cells are.**

**2.** Explain: Nigeria's way of teaching this.
**Answer: Nigeria's Senior Secondary Biology 1 teaches colonies and filaments inside Chapter 2, The cell (printed p. 25) — living cells as a colony (Volvox, with asexual and sexual reproduction) and as a filament (Spirogyra, with fragmentation) — as the steps between one cell and a tissue.**
