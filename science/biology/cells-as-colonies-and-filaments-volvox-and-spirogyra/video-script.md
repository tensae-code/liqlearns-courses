Today: Cells as colonies and filaments — Volvox and Spirogyra.

Remember this one thing: between one single cell and a true tissue stand two halfway forms: the colony, where many cells live together in one ball, and the filament, where cells stay joined in a thread — Nigeria's book teaches them through Volvox and Spirogyra.

Volvox is a hollow ball — a coenobium — of up to 20,000 cells living together as one colony. The cells stay joined in a single sphere instead of separating after division.

Volvox reproduces asexually and sexually — the colony can make new colonies both ways, and the book works through both inside Chapter 2's cell chapter.

Spirogyra's cells stay joined end to end in a thread — a filament. Each cell carries a spiral chloroplast, the feature the alga is recognised by.

Spirogyra reproduces by fragmentation: the filament breaks into pieces and each piece grows into a new filament.

Nigeria teaches the colony and the filament inside the cell chapter itself — as the living steps between a single cell and a tissue, before specialisation is taught: a colony's and a filament's cells are alike and can live apart, while a tissue's cells are interdependent.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
