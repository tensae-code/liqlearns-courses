# Cells as colonies and filaments — Volvox and Spirogyra

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Cells as colonies and filaments — Volvox and Spirogyra. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
A single cell can live alone. A tissue is many cells depending on each other. Nigeria's Senior Secondary Biology 1 stops between those two and looks at the halfway steps, in Chapter 2: living cells as a colony, and living cells as a filament. Volvox shows the colony — a hollow ball of up to 20,000 cells living together. Spirogyra shows the filament — cells joined end to end in a green thread with a spiral chloroplast. These are the steps between one cell and a tissue.

[Picture — a hollow Volvox ball made of many small cells beside a Spirogyra filament — a thread of cells, each with a spiral green chloroplast]

## The key facts
**The colony — Volvox.** Volvox is a hollow ball — a coenobium — of up to 20,000 cells living together as one colony. The cells stay joined in a single sphere instead of separating after division.

**Volvox reproduces two ways.** Volvox reproduces asexually and sexually — the colony can make new colonies both ways, and the book works through both inside Chapter 2's cell chapter.

**The filament — Spirogyra.** Spirogyra's cells stay joined end to end in a thread — a filament. Each cell carries a spiral chloroplast, the feature the alga is recognised by.

**Spirogyra multiplies by breaking.** Spirogyra reproduces by fragmentation: the filament breaks into pieces and each piece grows into a new filament.

**Nigeria's way.** Nigeria teaches the colony and the filament inside the cell chapter itself — as the living steps between a single cell and a tissue, before specialisation is taught: a colony's and a filament's cells are alike and can live apart, while a tissue's cells are interdependent.
