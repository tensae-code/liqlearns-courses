Today: Ground and mechanical plant tissues.

Remember this one thing: Among the permanent tissues, parenchyma is the ground tissue — living, thin-walled cells that fill the plant and do its everyday work of photosynthesis and storage — while mechanical tissue is the plant's skeleton: collenchyma and sclerenchyma, built to hold the plant up.

The finished plant is mostly two tissues working together. Parenchyma fills it — living, thin-walled, making food in the leaf and storing it in root and fruit.

Mechanical tissue frames it: collenchyma, flexible in the growing parts; sclerenchyma, dead and hard where growth is done. Fill and frame — that is a stem.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
