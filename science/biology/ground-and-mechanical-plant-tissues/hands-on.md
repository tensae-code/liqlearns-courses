# Hands-on — Ground and mechanical plant tissues

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Take a soft fruit and a piece of celery or rhubarb stem. The fruit's flesh you can crush between finger and thumb is parenchyma; the stringy strands you can pull from the stem are mechanical tissue (with conducting tissue among them). Draw the stem piece and label 'filling' and 'holding' where each belongs.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — a cut stem in cross-section: soft thin-walled parenchyma filling the middle, strands of thick-walled mechanical tissue standing like pillars at the corners, each labelled with its job — filling and working, holding up — and check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
