# Interactions between populations — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1.** What does this lesson say about competition (−/−)?
a) Competition harms both sides: populations needing the same limited resource — food, water, light, space — each grow less than they would alone
b) It plays no part in this topic at all
c) It happens only at night and nowhere else
d) It is found only in non-living things
**Answer: a) Competition harms both sides: populations needing the same limited resource — food, water, light, space — each grow less than they would alone**

**2.** What does this lesson say about predation (+/−)?
a) It plays no part in this topic at all
b) In predation one population eats the other: the predator gains, the prey loses — and the two populations rise and fall in linked cycles
c) It happens only at night and nowhere else
d) It is found only in non-living things
**Answer: b) In predation one population eats the other: the predator gains, the prey loses — and the two populations rise and fall in linked cycles**

**3.** What does this lesson say about parasitism (+/−)?
a) It plays no part in this topic at all
b) It happens only at night and nowhere else
c) A parasite lives on or in a host, feeding on it slowly — gaining food and shelter while the host is weakened but usually not killed outright
d) It is found only in non-living things
**Answer: c) A parasite lives on or in a host, feeding on it slowly — gaining food and shelter while the host is weakened but usually not killed outright**

**4.** What does this lesson say about mutualism (+/+)?
a) It plays no part in this topic at all
b) It happens only at night and nowhere else
c) It is found only in non-living things
d) In mutualism both populations gain: the bee and the flower, the cleaner fish and its client, the nitrogen-fixing bacteria and the legume
**Answer: d) In mutualism both populations gain: the bee and the flower, the cleaner fish and its client, the nitrogen-fixing bacteria and the legume**
