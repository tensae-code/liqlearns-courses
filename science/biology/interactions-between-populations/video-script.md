Today: Interactions between populations.

Remember this one thing: Populations sharing one place interact — competition, predation, parasitism, mutualism and commensalism — and every interaction can be read by what each side gains or loses.

Competition runs between species (interspecific) and inside one species (intraspecific), fiercest where the resource is scarcest. Two species cannot long share exactly the same niche in the same place: one outcompetes the other, or they divide the resource between them.

More prey feeds more predators; more predators then cut the prey down; hunger cuts the predators back; and the prey recovers — the classic linked waves of the two populations, each driving the other.

Ticks, lice, worms and mistletoe all take their living from a host bigger than themselves. Unlike a predator, a parasite needs its host alive; unlike a mutualist, it gives nothing back — and heavy parasite loads still stunt, sicken and can kill.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
