# Epigenetics — marks on the DNA

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Epigenetics — marks on the DNA. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Epigenetics is inheritance by marks on the DNA — methylation that switches a gene off without changing its base sequence — passed on without any change in the letters, as the changed toadflax flower and the agouti mouse's yellow-or-mottled coat show. No lesson in this course taught this on its own before, so this lesson is built for it — from China's People's Education Press biology textbooks, which teach it as a named topic.

[Picture — two identical mice, one yellow and one mottled, above the same DNA ladder with different small tags clipped onto it]

## The key facts
**§2, epigenetic inheritance.** toadflax flowers changed by methylation of the Lcyc gene and agouti mice whose identical Avy genes give yellow or mottled coats by methylation level, inherited without any change in base sequence; twins, queen and worker bees, and a society note that smoking raises methylation and damages sperm — epigenetics is in this book and in no existing lesson.
