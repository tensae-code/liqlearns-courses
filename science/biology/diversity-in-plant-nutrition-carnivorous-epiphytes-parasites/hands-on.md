# Hands-on — Diversity in plant nutrition — carnivorous, epiphytes and parasites

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: not every plant feeds the standard way: carnivorous plants trap and digest insects for minerals, epiphytes live on other plants for support while making their own food, and parasitic plants steal food or water from a host.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: three plants side by side: a pitcher plant trapping an insect, an orchid growing on a tree branch, and a parasitic plant with roots entering its host's stem. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
