# Diversity in plant nutrition — carnivorous, epiphytes and parasites — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** State the one thing this lesson teaches and explain it.
**Answer: not every plant feeds the standard way: carnivorous plants trap and digest insects for minerals, epiphytes live on other plants for support while making their own food, and parasitic plants steal food or water from a host. Pitcher plants and their kin grow where the soil is poor in minerals. They still photosynthesise, but they trap and digest insects to gain the nitrogen and minerals the soil lacks.**

**2.** Explain Malaysia's way of teaching this.
**Answer: Malaysia's KSSM Form 5 book teaches this as the closing diversity subtopic of its plant-nutrition chapter (Ch.3, printed p. 68) — after the standard inorganic nutrients and the root's uptake, the non-standard feeding modes are surveyed together.**
