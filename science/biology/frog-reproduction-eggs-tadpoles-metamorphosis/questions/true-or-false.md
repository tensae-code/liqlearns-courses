# Frog reproduction — eggs, tadpoles, metamorphosis — true or false

Say true or false.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) Frogs — including Ethiopian species from the Bale Mountains and Shoa forests — reproduce sexually with figured male and female structures; they lay jelly-coated eggs in water (the jelly protects them), fertilisation is usually external, and the fertilised egg becomes a tadpole, a larval stage very different from the adult, before metamorphosis.**
**Answer: True** — this is what the lesson teaches.

**2. (Sub lesson 2) Function lists for the frog's male and female reproductive organs (testes, sperm, seminal vesicles, oviducts, ova, and the cloaca as the shared exit for excretory, urinary and reproductive systems), and a seven-row tadpole-vs-frog table: gills vs lungs, tail and fins vs limbs, denticle teeth vs jaw teeth, water-only vs land-and-water, two-chambered vs three-chambered heart, herbivore vs carnivore, soft cartilage skull vs hardened skull.**
**Answer: True** — this is what the lesson teaches.

**3. (Sub lesson 2) One row is a guide error in wording — frogs' lungs aid breathing in air, not "underwater" as printed.**
**Answer: True** — this is what the lesson teaches.
