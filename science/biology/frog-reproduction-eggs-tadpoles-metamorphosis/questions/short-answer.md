# Frog reproduction — eggs, tadpoles, metamorphosis — short answer

Answer in your own words, in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) In your own words, what does this lesson teach about reproduction in frogs?**
**Answer: Frogs — including Ethiopian species from the Bale Mountains and Shoa forests — reproduce sexually with figured male and female structures; they lay jelly-coated eggs in water (the jelly protects them), fertilisation is usually external, and the fertilised egg becomes a tadpole, a larval stage very different from the adult, before metamorphosis.**

**2. (Sub lesson 2) In your own words, what does this lesson teach about added frog detail?**
**Answer: Function lists for the frog's male and female reproductive organs (testes, sperm, seminal vesicles, oviducts, ova, and the cloaca as the shared exit for excretory, urinary and reproductive systems), and a seven-row tadpole-vs-frog table: gills vs lungs, tail and fins vs limbs, denticle teeth vs jaw teeth, water-only vs land-and-water, two-chambered vs three-chambered heart, herbivore vs carnivore, soft cartilage skull vs hardened skull.**
