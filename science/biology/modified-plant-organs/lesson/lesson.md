# Modified plant organs

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Modified plant organs. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Bulgaria's Grade 7 book gives modified plant organs a topic of their own (Topic 19, printed p. 42), taught as a discussion/project topic right after the plant organs themselves (Topics 17–18): once the learner knows the ordinary organs, the book asks what happens when an organ's form is changed for a special job.

[Picture — three modified organs in a row: a curling tendril on a stick, a sharp thorn, and a swollen storage organ cut open]

## The key facts
**What a modification is.** A modified organ keeps its identity as a root, stem or leaf but its form is changed — flattened, sharpened, swollen or curled — so it can do a special job. The organ is recognised by where it grows and what it grows from, not by the job it now does.

**Tendrils — modified for climbing.** A tendril is a thin, curling modified organ that winds round a support and holds the plant up. Climbing plants reach the light by tendrils instead of by a thick self-supporting stem.

**Thorns — modified for defence.** A thorn is a hard, sharp modified organ. It guards the plant: animals that would eat the plant are pricked and turn away.

**Storage organs — modified for keeping.** A storage organ is swollen with kept food or water. The plant draws on the store in hard times — to survive a dry or cold season, or to sprout and grow again.

**Bulgaria's way.** Bulgaria's Pedagog 6 Grade 7 book does not scatter modifications inside the organ topics: it names modified plant organs as a topic in itself (Topic 19, printed p. 42) and teaches it as a discussion/project topic — learners argue and present cases of organs changed for special jobs.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words: a plant organ can be modified — changed in form so it does a special job besides, or instead of, its ordinary one: tendrils modified to climb and hold, thorns modified to defend, and storage organs modified to keep food and water for the plant. Use each key fact once. Then let your companion explain it back to you.
