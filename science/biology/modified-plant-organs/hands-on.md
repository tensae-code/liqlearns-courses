# Hands-on — Modified plant organs

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: a plant organ can be modified — changed in form so it does a special job besides, or instead of, its ordinary one: tendrils modified to climb and hold, thorns modified to defend, and storage organs modified to keep food and water for the plant.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: three modified organs in a row: a curling tendril on a stick, a sharp thorn, and a swollen storage organ cut open. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
