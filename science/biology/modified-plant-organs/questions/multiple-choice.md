# Modified plant organs — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. What is the one thing this lesson teaches?**
a) a plant organ can be modified — changed in form so it does a special job besides, or instead of, its ordinary one: tendrils modified to climb and hold, thorns modified to defend, and storage organs modified to keep food and water for the plant  b) That this topic has no rules and every case is different  c) A list of names with no process behind them  d) That the topic belongs only to scientists, not to daily life
**Answer: a) a plant organ can be modified — changed in form so it does a special job besides, or instead of, its ordinary one: tendrils modified to climb and hold, thorns modified to defend, and storage organs modified to keep food and water for the plant**

**2. What a modification is — which statement is right?**
a) A modified organ keeps its identity as a root, stem or leaf but its form is changed — flattened, sharpened, swollen or curled — so it can do a special job. The organ is recognised by where it grows and what it grows from, not by the job it now does.  b) The opposite of what the lesson teaches  c) It happens only in examinations  d) It is never observed in real life
**Answer: a) A modified organ keeps its identity as a root, stem or leaf but its form is changed — flattened, sharpened, swollen or curled — so it can do a special job. The organ is recognised by where it grows and what it grows from, not by the job it now does.**

**3. Tendrils — modified for climbing — which statement is right?**
a) It is unrelated to the lesson's topic  b) A tendril is a thin, curling modified organ that winds round a support and holds the plant up. Climbing plants reach the light by tendrils instead of by a thick self-supporting stem.  c) It is a myth the book corrects  d) It applies only outside Bulgaria
**Answer: b) A tendril is a thin, curling modified organ that winds round a support and holds the plant up. Climbing plants reach the light by tendrils instead of by a thick self-supporting stem.**
