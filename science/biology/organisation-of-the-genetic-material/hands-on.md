# Hands-on — Organisation of the genetic material

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: genetic material is organised very differently across life: a prokaryote keeps one circular DNA molecule loose in the cytoplasm, while a eukaryote winds its DNA with proteins into chromosomes inside a nucleus — and viruses carry theirs as DNA or RNA in a protein coat.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: three panels: a bacterium with one circular DNA loop, a nucleus with wound chromosomes, and a virus particle with its short genetic strip inside a coat. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
