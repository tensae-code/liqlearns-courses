# Hands-on — Non-specific defence — barriers and inflammation

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: the body's first defence is non-specific — it answers any invader the same way: skin and membranes as barriers, inflammation flooding an injured place with blood, fever raising the temperature, and phagocyte cells engulfing microbes whole.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a cut in skin drawn in cross-section with germs entering, the area reddening and swelling, and a large phagocyte cell swallowing a bacterium. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
