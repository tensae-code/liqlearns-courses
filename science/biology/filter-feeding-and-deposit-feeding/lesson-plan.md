# Lesson plan — Filter feeding and deposit feeding

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Biology · **Lesson:** Filter feeding and deposit feeding
**Place in the curriculum:** Biology — appended to Unit 16 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** some animals eat without hunting: filter feeders strain tiny food out of the water passing over their gills or baleen, and deposit feeders eat the soil or sediment itself and digest the living matter out of it.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own as a single-skill lesson built from Nigeria's Senior Secondary Biology 1, Ch. 6 (printed p. 102). Related lessons are recommended first where they help, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words, the one thing this lesson teaches: some animals eat without hunting: filter feeders strain tiny food out of the water passing over their gills or baleen, and deposit feeders eat the soil or sediment itself and digest the living matter out of it.
2. Explain each key fact (Filter feeding strains the water; Fish use gill rakers; The blue whale filters too; Deposit feeding eats the deposit; Nigeria's way) in their own words.
3. Explain Nigeria's way as the placements describe it: the topic is taught in Ch. 6 of Senior Secondary Biology 1 (printed p. 102).
