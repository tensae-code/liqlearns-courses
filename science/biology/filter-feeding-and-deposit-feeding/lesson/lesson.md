# Filter feeding and deposit feeding

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Filter feeding and deposit feeding. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Chapter 6 of Nigeria's Senior Secondary Biology 1 works through feeding mechanisms in holozoic organisms. Two of them catch food without a chase. Filter feeding strains fine particles from water: herrings and sardines sieve plankton through their gill rakers, and the blue whale sieves krill through baleen. Deposit feeding takes in the deposit itself — soil or sediment — and digests the organic matter out of it as it passes through.

[Picture — a fish head with gill rakers straining plankton from water flowing through its gills, beside a blue whale's baleen plates and an animal eating its way through soil]

## The key facts
**Filter feeding strains the water.** A filter feeder passes water across a straining surface and keeps the fine food particles — no hunting, the food comes with the current.

**Fish use gill rakers.** Herrings and sardines filter feed through the gill rakers on their gills, sieving plankton from the water that flows through for breathing.

**The blue whale filters too.** The blue whale — the largest animal — feeds by filtering: it strains krill from huge mouthfuls of sea water through its baleen plates.

**Deposit feeding eats the deposit.** A deposit feeder takes in soil or sediment and digests the organic matter out of it as the material passes through its body.

**Nigeria's way.** Nigeria teaches feeding mechanisms in Chapter 6 (printed p. 102) as a set of ways holozoic organisms get food — filter feeding (herrings, sardines, blue whale) and deposit feeding (through soil and sediment) among them.
