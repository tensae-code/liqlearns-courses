Today: Filter feeding and deposit feeding.

Remember this one thing: some animals eat without hunting: filter feeders strain tiny food out of the water passing over their gills or baleen, and deposit feeders eat the soil or sediment itself and digest the living matter out of it.

A filter feeder passes water across a straining surface and keeps the fine food particles — no hunting, the food comes with the current.

Herrings and sardines filter feed through the gill rakers on their gills, sieving plankton from the water that flows through for breathing.

The blue whale — the largest animal — feeds by filtering: it strains krill from huge mouthfuls of sea water through its baleen plates.

A deposit feeder takes in soil or sediment and digests the organic matter out of it as the material passes through its body.

Nigeria teaches feeding mechanisms in Chapter 6 (printed p. 102) as a set of ways holozoic organisms get food — filter feeding (herrings, sardines, blue whale) and deposit feeding (through soil and sediment) among them.
