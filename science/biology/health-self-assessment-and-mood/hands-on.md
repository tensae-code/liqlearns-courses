# Hands-on — Health self-assessment — and mood

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: health can be assessed, not assumed: a self-test scores body, mind and social adaptation — the WHO health triangle — against standards such as the Chinese school-age BMI cut-offs, and mood is regulated by three named methods: shift attention, vent appropriately, console yourself.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a triangle labelled body, mind and social adaptation, beside a checklist page with ticks and a small BMI scale. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
