# Health self-assessment — and mood

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Health self-assessment — and mood. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Health can be assessed, not assumed: a self-test scores body, mind and social adaptation — the WHO health triangle — against standards such as the Chinese school-age BMI cut-offs, and mood is regulated by three named methods: shift attention, vent appropriately, console yourself. No lesson in this course taught this on its own before, so this lesson is built for it — from China's People's Education Press biology textbooks, which teach it as a named topic.

[Picture — a triangle labelled body, mind and social adaptation, beside a checklist page with ticks and a small BMI scale]

## The key facts
**Ch. 3 §1 Evaluating your own health.** health scored, not assumed: a 25-item self-test (which embeds the Chinese school-age BMI standard — for 13-year-olds, overweight above 21.9 for boys and 22.6 for girls) sorted with classmates into body, mind and social-adaptation items; the WHO definition drawn as a triangle; and three named methods for regulating mood — shift attention, vent appropriately, console yourself.
