## The idea

## The key facts
**Letters 1.** In the guide's key, A is Prophase and B is Interphase.

**Letters 2.** In the guide's key, C is Anaphase and D is Telophase.

**Cytokinesis.** Cytokinesis follows the phases; the guide letters it D again, which is an error in the guide.

**Plant difference.** Cytokinesis differs in plant cells (cell plate formation) from animal cells.

## From the student book — the pupil-taught core
The student book works mitosis phase by phase as a continuous division: chromosomes condense, line up, are pulled apart, and two nuclei form before the cytoplasm divides — one cell becoming two identical cells, for growth and repair.

### Student-book key facts
**Condensing.** In mitosis the chromosomes condense and become visible.

**Lining up and pulling apart.** The chromosomes line up, then are pulled apart toward the two ends.

**Two nuclei.** Two nuclei form, followed by the cytoplasm dividing.

**Two identical cells.** Mitosis makes one cell become two identical cells, for growth and repair.

**Under the microscope.** Pupils identify the mitosis phases in onion cells under the microscope.
