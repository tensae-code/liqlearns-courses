# Lesson plan — Locomotion across animals

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Biology · **Lesson:** Locomotion across animals
**Place in the curriculum:** Biology — appended to Unit 16 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** animals move by many different engines — Iraq's Grade 11 Biology book traces movement from the amoeba's flowing pseudopodia and Euglena's flagellum, through the earthworm's hydrostatic body and the grasshopper's leg muscles, to vertebrate swimming, crawling and the flapping flight of strong birds such as the duck.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own as a single-skill lesson built from Iraq's Grade 11 Biology textbook, as cited in lesson/sources.md. Related lessons are recommended first where they help, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words, the one thing this lesson teaches: animals move by many different engines — Iraq's Grade 11 Biology book traces movement from the amoeba's flowing pseudopodia and Euglena's flagellum, through the earthworm's hydrostatic body and the grasshopper's leg muscles, to vertebrate swimming, crawling and the flapping flight of strong birds such as the duck.
2. Explain each key fact (Movement without legs; The hydrostatic engine; Legs worked by muscle; Vertebrate movement; Iraq's way) in their own words.
3. Explain Iraq's way as the placements describe it.
