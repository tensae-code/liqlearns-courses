RNA is single-stranded, with ribose sugar and uracil replacing thymine; its three working types — messenger, transfer and ribosomal RNA — are all transcribed from DNA and together run protein synthesis and other cell functions; in some viruses RNA is itself the genetic material.

Chromosomes are defined as thread-like packages of DNA in the nucleus, each with a centromere dividing it; and a ten-row DNA-vs-RNA table consolidates the comparison (names, backbones and bases, where each is found — nucleus/mitochondria vs cytoplasm/nucleus/ribosome — self-replicating vs synthesised from DNA, and more).

Now say it in your own words — and find one example near your home.
