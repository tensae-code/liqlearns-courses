# Hands-on — Open and closed circulatory systems

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: animals move substances two ways: in an open circulatory system the blood leaves the vessels and bathes the tissues directly, while in a closed system it never leaves the vessels and capillaries do the exchanging — and some simple animals, like jellyfish, manage with no system at all, exchanging by diffusion.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: two animal cross-sections side by side: a grasshopper whose heart pumps blood out of open vessel ends into body spaces, and a fish whose blood runs in a sealed loop of vessels. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
