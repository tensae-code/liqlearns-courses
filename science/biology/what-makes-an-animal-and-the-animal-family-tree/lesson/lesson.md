## Sub lesson 1 — Characteristics of animals and the two great groups

Animals are eukaryotic, multicellular, heterotrophic and sensitive to stimuli; they reproduce, move, respire, excrete and grow, with different body symmetries. They split by the backbone into vertebrates and invertebrates; vertebrates split further into warm-blooded mammals and birds and cold-blooded fish, reptiles and amphibians, and the invertebrate phyla (sponges, flatworms, cnidarians, arthropods, annelids, echinoderms, molluscs) are mapped, with arthropods split again.

## Sub lesson 2 — Added detail — body symmetry and the animal characteristics elaborated

The guide expands the student book's characteristics section: asymmetry (no plane divides the animal into equal halves — the most primitive animals), radial and bilateral symmetry figured; and fuller accounts of multicellularity with cell specialisation, motility supported by endoskeleton, exoskeleton or hydrostatic pressure, respiration as releasing energy from food in cells, and excretion.
