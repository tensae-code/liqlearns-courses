# Hands-on — How memory works — four stages

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: memory works in four stages — sensory memory under a second, first-level memory of seconds to minutes (the verification code you forget at once), second-level memory of minutes to years, and third-level memory that may be permanent — and study works with the mechanism: repeat, and bind new information to old.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: four widening boxes in a row labelled with their holding times, from under a second to a lifetime, with a name sitting in the last box. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
