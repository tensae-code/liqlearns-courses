# Cancer immunotherapy — checkpoints and engineered T cells

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Cancer immunotherapy — checkpoints and engineered T cells. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Cancer immunotherapy fights a tumour with the patient's own immune system: T cells taken out, re-engineered to recognise the cancer, multiplied and returned — or checkpoint blockade that stops the tumour hiding behind PD-L1's 'self' signal to the T cell's PD-1. No lesson in this course taught this on its own before, so this lesson is built for it — from China's People's Education Press biology textbooks, which teach it as a named topic.

[Picture — a tumour cell holding up a PD-L1 shield against a T cell, and an antibody stepping between them to block the handshake so the T cell can attack]

## The key facts
**Ch. 4 §2 feature "Immunotherapy for cancer".** two strategies: T cells taken out, re-engineered to recognise a cancer, multiplied and returned (in clinical use for some lymphomas); and checkpoint blockade — tumours over-express PD-L1 to be "recognised as self" by PD-1, so antibodies block the handshake (broad-spectrum approvals by November 2018) — with the limits stated: engineered cells can hit healthy tissue, and an unbraked immune system can overreact.
