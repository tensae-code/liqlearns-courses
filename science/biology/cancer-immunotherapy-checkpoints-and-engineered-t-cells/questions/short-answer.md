# Cancer immunotherapy — checkpoints and engineered T cells — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** State the one thing this lesson teaches and explain it.
**Answer: cancer immunotherapy fights a tumour with the patient's own immune system: T cells taken out, re-engineered to recognise the cancer, multiplied and returned — or checkpoint blockade that stops the tumour hiding behind PD-L1's 'self' signal to the T cell's PD-1. two strategies: T cells taken out, re-engineered to recognise a cancer, multiplied and returned (in clinical use for some lymphomas); and checkpoint blockade — tumours over-express PD-L1 to be "recognised as self" by PD-1, so antibodies block the handshake (broad-spectrum approvals by November 2018) — with the limits stated: engineered cells can hit healthy tissue, and an unbraked immune system can overreact. No existing lesson covers it.**
