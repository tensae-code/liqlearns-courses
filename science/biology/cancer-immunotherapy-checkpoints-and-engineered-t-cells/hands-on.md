# Hands-on — Cancer immunotherapy — checkpoints and engineered T cells

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: cancer immunotherapy fights a tumour with the patient's own immune system: T cells taken out, re-engineered to recognise the cancer, multiplied and returned — or checkpoint blockade that stops the tumour hiding behind PD-L1's 'self' signal to the T cell's PD-1.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a tumour cell holding up a PD-L1 shield against a T cell, and an antibody stepping between them to block the handshake so the T cell can attack. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
