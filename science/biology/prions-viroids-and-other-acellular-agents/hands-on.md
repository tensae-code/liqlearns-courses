# Hands-on — Prions, viroids and other acellular agents

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Take a short loop of string and tie it into a small ring: that ring, with nothing around it, is the shape of the lesson's viroid — a small circular RNA molecule with no protein coat. Beside it, fold a strip of paper neatly, then crumple-fold a second strip the wrong way: the wrong-folded strip is the prion, and its "infecting" is persuading the neat strip to take the same wrong fold.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the three agents from memory — virus, viroid, prion — with what each one is made of written beside it, and check against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
