# Prions, viroids and other acellular agents — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** Explain the difference between a viroid and a prion, using what each one is made of.
**Answer: A viroid is made of nucleic acid alone — a small circular RNA molecule with no protein coat. A prion is made of protein alone — a misfolded protein with no nucleic acid in it. Both are infectious agents smaller and simpler than true viruses, and neither is a cell.**

**2.** Explain Jordan's way of teaching this.
**Answer: Jordan's Grade 10 course teaches viroids and prions as Lesson 2 of its "Viruses, Viroids and Prions" unit: the student book defines them as infectious particles smaller and simpler than true viruses, the activities workbook sets an activity on prion structure and how prions act, and the teacher guide scripts a discussion built on mad-cow disease — the misfolded prion converting normal protein, Prusiner, Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease in Britain, and symptoms explained by nerve-cell death.**
