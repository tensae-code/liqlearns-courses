# Prions, viroids and other acellular agents

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Prions, viroids and other acellular agents. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Jordan's Grade 10 course gives one unit to "Viruses, Viroids and Prions". The viruses have their own lessons in this course. This lesson teaches the other two: the infectious particles that are smaller and simpler than true viruses — and are not cells at all. A viroid is only a small circular RNA molecule, with no protein coat. A prion is only a protein — but a misfolded one, and infectious.

[Picture — a diagram of the three acellular agents side by side, drawn to compare them — a virus particle with its coat, a tiny bare circle of RNA labelled viroid, and a normal protein beside its misfolded prion copy]

## The key facts
**Smaller and simpler than viruses.** The student book's Lesson 2, "Viroids and prions", defines its subject up front: infectious particles smaller and simpler than true viruses. Neither one is a cell, and neither one lives on its own — each can act only inside a living host.

**The viroid — bare RNA.** The viroid is defined as a small circular RNA molecule with no protein coat. Where a virus carries its instructions wrapped in protein, the viroid is the instruction molecule alone, in a tiny ring.

**The prion — an infectious protein.** Jordan's activities workbook sets pupils an activity on prion structure and how prions act: a prion is a protein, and its infectivity is the whole puzzle — there is no nucleic acid in it at all.

**How a prion acts — the mad-cow discussion.** The teacher guide scripts the class discussion on mad-cow disease: a misfolded prion converts normal protein into the misfolded form, so the misfolding spreads protein by protein. The guide names Prusiner's work on prions and Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease in Britain, and explains the symptoms by the death of nerve cells — it is the lost nerve cells, not a poison, that produce the disease's effects.
