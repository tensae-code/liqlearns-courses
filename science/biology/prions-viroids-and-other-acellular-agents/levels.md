# Prions, viroids and other acellular agents — depth

This file is the lesson's depth record. This lesson has no sub lessons — it is one whole, single-skill lesson.

Whole-lesson pass mark: the learner can state the one thing this lesson teaches (Viroids and prions are infectious agents smaller and simpler than true viruses, and they are not cells: a viroid is a small circular RNA molecule with no protein coat, and a prion is an infectious protein — a misfolded protein that converts the body's normal protein into more prions, which is how mad-cow disease does its damage.) and explain each key fact in their own words. Practice deepens by restating the two definitions exactly — viroid: small circular RNA, no protein coat; prion: infectious misfolded protein — and explaining, step by step, how one misfolded prion turns normal protein into more prions, without looking.
