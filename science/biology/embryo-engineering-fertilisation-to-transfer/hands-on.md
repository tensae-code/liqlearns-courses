# Hands-on — Embryo engineering — fertilisation to transfer

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: embryo engineering works with the early embryo: sperm capacitation and fertilisation with its two blocks to polyspermy, cleavage to the morula and the blastocyst with its inner cell mass, then IVF, embryo transfer and embryo splitting to multiply valuable animals — and to save species down to their last females.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: an early embryo's journey in stages — fertilised egg, cleaving ball, morula, blastocyst with inner cell mass labelled — with a pipette transferring one embryo. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
