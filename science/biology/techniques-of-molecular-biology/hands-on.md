# Hands-on — Techniques of molecular biology

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: molecular biology works through a bench of named techniques — extracting and cutting DNA, separating fragments by gel electrophoresis, multiplying them by PCR, reading their sequence and joining them anew — and Greece's Grade 12 appendix gathers twenty pages of them as the toolkit behind every genetics chapter.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a laboratory bench drawn with the technique stations in a row: extraction tubes, a cutting enzyme, a gel tray with separated bands, a PCR machine and a sequence printout. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
