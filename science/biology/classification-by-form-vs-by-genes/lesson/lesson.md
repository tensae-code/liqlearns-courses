# Classification by form vs by genes

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Classification by form vs by genes. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
You already know that classification groups living things by what they share. This lesson asks what the grouping rests on. Palestine's Grade 10 Life Sciences book teaches modern classification in a single chapter — first classification itself, then the naming of living organisms, then the levels of classification — and in §4 it compares two bases for classifying: the form of the organism, and its genes.

[Picture — two sorting tables side by side: on the first, organisms sorted by what they look like, their shape and structure; on the second, the same organisms sorted by a strip representing their genetic material]

## The key facts
**Morphological classification.** التصنيف الشكلي — classification by form: organisms are grouped by their morphology, their outward shape and structure — the characteristics that can be seen, compared and measured.

**Genetic classification.** التصنيف الجيني — classification by genes: organisms are grouped by their genetic material — what they carry in their heredity, not by what they happen to look like.

**The comparison.** The two ways sort by different evidence. Form is read from the outside of the organism; genes are read from the inside. Palestine's book does not teach either one alone here — §4 sets morphological classification and genetic classification side by side and compares them, as the closing section of its modern-classification chapter.

**Palestine's way.** In Palestine this comparison is where the classification chapter lands: after modern classification (§1), naming (§2) and the levels of classification (§3), the Grade 10 book finishes the chapter by asking what classification should rest on — form, or genes (§4).
