# Lesson plan — Classification by form vs by genes

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Biology · **Lesson:** Classification by form vs by genes
**Place in the curriculum:** Biology — appended to Unit 16 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** living things can be classified in two ways — by their form (morphological classification, التصنيف الشكلي: grouping by outward shape and structure) and by their genes (genetic classification, التصنيف الجيني: grouping by the genetic material) — and Palestine's Grade 10 book sets the two side by side and compares them (Palestine, Grade 10 — *العلوم الحياتية (Life Sciences)*, student textbook, Unit 4 Ch. 1 §4, printed p. 101).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier classification lessons — grouping by shared characteristics and the classification ladder — and adds the question those lessons do not ask: what the grouping rests on, form or genes. Classification — grouping by shared characteristics is recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: **Classification — grouping by shared characteristics** and **The classification ladder — species to kingdom**. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words, the one thing this lesson teaches: living things can be classified by their form — morphological classification — or by their genes — genetic classification — and the two ways can be compared.
2. Say what morphological classification (التصنيف الشكلي) groups by: the outward form — shape and structure that can be seen and measured.
3. Say what genetic classification (التصنيف الجيني) groups by: the genetic material the organism carries.
4. Explain Palestine's way as the placements describe it: inside its modern-classification chapter, after classification itself, naming and the levels, the book gives §4 to the comparison — morphological classification and genetic classification set against each other.
