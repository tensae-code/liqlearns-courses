Today: Transpiration and the transpiration stream.

Remember this one thing: a plant is always losing water into the air — and that loss is not waste. It is the engine that pulls water up the plant.

Water vapour leaves the plant, mainly through the leaves. That is transpiration. As the water leaves, more water is drawn up behind it — from the stem, from the roots, from the soil. That upward flow is the transpiration stream.

The stream does three jobs. It carries dissolved minerals up to the leaves — transport. It keeps the plant's cells filled and firm — support. And as water evaporates from the leaf, it cools the leaf — cooling.

Change the day and you change the rate: heat, dry air, light and moving air all speed transpiration up. That is why the rate is something you investigate, not just something you read — Samoa's Year 13 guide sets it as an investigation.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
