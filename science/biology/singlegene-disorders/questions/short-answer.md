# Single-gene disorders — short answer

Answer in your own words, in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) In your own words, what does this lesson teach about single-gene disorders?**
**Answer: Disorders from one mutated gene, in three inheritance patterns: autosomal dominant (expressed from one copy — Huntington's disease), autosomal recessive (needing two copies) and X-linked (haemophilia, the blood-clotting disorder, as the example); mutation itself is defined as a change in DNA sequence from copying errors or environmental factors.**

**2. (Sub lesson 2) In your own words, what does this lesson teach about added genetic-disorders table?**
**Answer: A disorder-by-disorder table the student book lacks, giving description, cause, symptoms and treatment for each — Huntington's disease (dominant mutation on chromosome 4; uncontrolled movements, emotional disturbance and cognitive decline; currently no way to stop or slow it) and albinism (inherited, greatly reduced pigment) among the entries worked in the guide.**
