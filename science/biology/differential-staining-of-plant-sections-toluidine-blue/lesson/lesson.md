# Differential staining of plant sections — toluidine blue

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Differential staining of plant sections — toluidine blue. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
preparing toluidine blue stain, cutting thin hand sections of a plant stem (blade drawn at 45°), and reading the stain's multiple colours in different tissues

[Picture — a hand section of a plant stem under a microscope, its tissues showing in several different stain colours, with the razor held at 45 degrees in a small inset sketch]

## The key facts
**Preparing the stain and the section.** Kuwait's Grade 11 Term 1 applications workbook (Activity 1, printed p. 17) teaches the differential staining technique: preparing toluidine blue stain, and cutting thin hand sections of a plant stem with the blade drawn at 45°.

**Reading the colours.** The stain's multiple colours in different tissues are the result to read: one stain, several colours — each tissue announcing itself by the colour it takes.

**Kuwait's way.** Kuwait opens its Grade 11 workbook with technique, not theory: the first activity is a bench skill — stain preparation and hand sectioning — and the reading of a section by its stain colours (Activity 1).
