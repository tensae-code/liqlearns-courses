# Preventing sexual abuse

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Preventing sexual abuse. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it — why a biology book about the human body also teaches how to keep that body safe — and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
North Macedonia's Grade 8 textbook ends its human reproduction and health theme with a lesson most biology books leave out: prevention of sexual abuse (Theme 3.12, printed p. 192), taught inside biology, after sexual and reproductive health, rights and care. The lesson rests on a simple ground: the body is one's own, some touching and some requests are never acceptable, and there are always things a person can do — refuse, leave, and tell. No lesson in this course taught this on its own before, so this lesson is built for it.

[Picture — a young person standing firm with one hand raised in a clear 'stop', and beside them the trusted adults of their life — a parent, a teacher, a doctor — drawn as the people who can be told]

## The key facts
**Your body belongs to you.** Nobody has the right to touch your body in a way that makes you uncomfortable, or to ask you to touch theirs or to keep such things secret. Your body is your own — in biology's terms, part of caring for your reproductive health and your rights.

**Know what is not acceptable.** Sexual abuse is any sexual touching, showing, photographing or asking done to a person who does not agree or is too young to agree — by anyone, including someone known and trusted. It is never acceptable and never the victim's fault.

**Refuse and get away.** Prevention begins with the person: say no clearly, leave the situation, and do not stay because of threats, gifts or shame. Secrets about touching are never secrets to keep.

**Tell a trusted adult — and keep telling.** Tell a parent, teacher or another trusted adult what happened. If the first adult does not act, tell another. Telling is protection, not betrayal, and help exists.

**Rights and care stand behind it.** The same theme teaches rights and care for reproductive health (printed pp. 175–184): children and young people have the right to safety, information and help, and adults and institutions have the duty to protect.

**North Macedonia's way.** North Macedonia's Grade 8 book places prevention of sexual abuse inside the biology theme on reproduction and development in humans — as the theme's closing lesson (Theme 3.12, printed p. 192), after puberty, the menstrual cycle, reproductive health, family planning and rights and care — so keeping the body safe is taught as part of biology itself.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words: Prevention of sexual abuse: knowing that your body is your own, recognising behaviour that is not acceptable, refusing and getting away, and telling a trusted adult — abuse is always the offender's fault, never the victim's. Use each key fact once. Then let your companion explain it back to you.
