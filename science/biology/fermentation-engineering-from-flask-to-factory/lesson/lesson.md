# Fermentation engineering — from flask to factory

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Fermentation engineering — from flask to factory. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Fermentation engineering runs microbes at factory scale as one chain — strain selection and breeding, scale-up culture, medium design, sterilisation, inoculation, the computer-monitored fermentation itself, then separation and purification — and the conditions steer what the microbes make, as glutamate fermentation's pH switch proves. No lesson in this course taught this on its own before, so this lesson is built for it — from China's People's Education Press biology textbooks, which teach it as a named topic.

[Picture — a chain of vessels growing left to right — flask, small tank, factory fermenter with gauges for temperature, pH and oxygen — ending in a separated, purified product]

## The key facts
**Ch. 1 §3 Fermentation engineering.** the process as a chain (strain selection and breeding, scale-up culture, medium design, sterilisation, inoculation, the fermentation itself under computer monitoring of temperature, pH, dissolved oxygen, pressure and foam, then separation and purification); penicillin's fall from priceless to about a yuan a vial as the scale story; the pH switch in glutamate fermentation (alkaline conditions accumulate glutamate, acid ones glutamine) as proof that conditions steer metabolism; China's fermentation industry at nearly ¥290 billion a year by 2015, the world's largest.
