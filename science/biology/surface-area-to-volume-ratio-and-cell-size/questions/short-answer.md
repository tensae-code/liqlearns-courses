# Surface area to volume ratio and cell size — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** State the one thing this lesson teaches and explain it.
**Answer: as a cell grows, its volume grows faster than its surface area, so the surface-area-to-volume ratio falls — which is why cells stay microscopic and why dividing beats growing for exchanging materials. For a cube: surface area = 6 × side × side, and volume = length × width × height. Kuwait's Grade 10 workbook (Activity 6, printed p. 25) has pupils build cube models of cells from 1 cm cubes and work both measures out — a "link with mathematics" box in the activity.**

**2.** Explain Kuwait's way of teaching this.
**Answer: Kuwait teaches this ratio twice over: as a hands-on calculation with cube models in the Grade 10 applications workbook (Activity 6), and as the opening idea of the cell lesson in the new Grade 10 student book, proved with beetroot cubes and pigment spread.**
