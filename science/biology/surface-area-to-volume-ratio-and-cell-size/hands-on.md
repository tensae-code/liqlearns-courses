# Hands-on — Surface area to volume ratio and cell size

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: as a cell grows, its volume grows faster than its surface area, so the surface-area-to-volume ratio falls — which is why cells stay microscopic and why dividing beats growing for exchanging materials.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: three cubes of side 1 cm, 2 cm and 3 cm side by side, each labelled with its surface area, its volume and its surface-area-to-volume ratio, the smallest cube's ratio the biggest. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
