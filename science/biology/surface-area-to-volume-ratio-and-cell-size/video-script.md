Today: Surface area to volume ratio and cell size.

Remember this one thing: as a cell grows, its volume grows faster than its surface area, so the surface-area-to-volume ratio falls — which is why cells stay microscopic and why dividing beats growing for exchanging materials.

For a cube: surface area = 6 × side × side, and volume = length × width × height. Kuwait's Grade 10 workbook (Activity 6, printed p. 25) has pupils build cube models of cells from 1 cm cubes and work both measures out — a "link with mathematics" box in the activity.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
