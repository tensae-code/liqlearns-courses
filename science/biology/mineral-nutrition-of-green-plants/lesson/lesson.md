# Mineral nutrition of green plants

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Mineral nutrition of green plants. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
A green plant does not live on light, air and water alone: it must take in mineral elements, and only some elements are essential — leave one out and the plant shows it as a deficiency symptom. India's Class XI book gives mineral nutrition its own chapter: the essential elements, the deficiency symptoms that name a shortage, hydroponics — growing plants in a measured mineral solution with no soil, the experiment that proves which elements a plant cannot do without — and nitrogen fixation, the turning of air's nitrogen into a form plants can use.

[Picture — two seedlings of the same plant side by side — one grown with the full mineral solution, one grown with one essential element left out and showing its deficiency symptoms]

## The key facts
**Essential elements.** Photosynthesis builds the plant's food, but building the plant itself takes mineral elements, taken in through the roots from the soil or the water around them. An element is essential when the plant cannot grow and complete its life without it — India's chapter separates these essential elements from everything else a plant may happen to absorb.

**Deficiency symptoms.** A shortage speaks. When an essential element runs short, the plant shows a deficiency symptom — a visible change in its growth or its leaves — and the symptom is evidence: it points back to the element that is missing.

**Hydroponics.** The proof is done without soil. In hydroponics the plant grows in water holding a measured solution of mineral salts, so every element the plant gets is known. Leave one element out of the solution and the plant either grows on — that element was not essential — or fails with its deficiency symptom — that element was essential. India's chapter uses hydroponics as the test that settles which elements belong on the essential list.

**Nitrogen fixation.** Nitrogen is needed in quantity, and the air is mostly nitrogen — yet most green plants cannot use air's nitrogen directly. Nitrogen fixation converts it into a form plants can take in; in leguminous plants, bacteria housed in nodules on the roots fix nitrogen for the plant that houses them.

**India's way.** NCERT's Class XI book does not fold mineral nutrition into photosynthesis or transport: it stands as its own chapter (Chapter 12), between Transport in Plants and Photosynthesis in Higher Plants — the elements first, then the making of food — and it teaches the essential list through the two evidences a grower can actually see: the deficiency symptom on the plant, and the hydroponics jar where one missing element makes the plant fail.
