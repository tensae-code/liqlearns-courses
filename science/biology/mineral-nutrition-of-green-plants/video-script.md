Today: Mineral nutrition of green plants.

Remember this one thing: a plant makes its food from light, air and water — but it cannot grow on food alone. It also needs mineral elements, taken in from the soil or the water around its roots.

Some elements are essential: the plant cannot complete its life without them. Take one essential element away, and the plant does not stay silent — it shows a deficiency symptom, a visible sign that names the shortage.

How do we know which elements are essential? By hydroponics: growing the plant with no soil at all, in water holding a measured mix of minerals. Leave one element out of the mix, and watch whether the plant fails. That is the proof.

And nitrogen — the element plants need in quantity — most plants cannot take from the air, though the air is full of it. Nitrogen fixation turns air's nitrogen into a usable form; in legumes, bacteria living in root nodules do this fixing for the plant.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
