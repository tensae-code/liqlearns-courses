Today: Plant growth hormones.

Remember this one thing: a plant's growth is regulated by its own chemical messengers — the hormones auxins, cytokinins, gibberellins, abscisic acid and ethylene — and auxin explains apical dominance: the top bud holds the side buds back, which is why cutting a hedge's top makes it bush out.

The book names the plant growth hormones: auxins, cytokinins, gibberellins, abscisic acid and ethylene — chemical regulators made by the plant itself.

Auxin is produced at the growing tip and regulates growth below it — the hormone through which the tip controls the rest of the shoot.

While the top (apical) bud grows, auxin from it holds the side buds back. That holding-back is apical dominance.

Cut the top off a hedge and the side buds, released from the tip's auxin, grow out — the hedge bushes. Hedge-cutting is the book's everyday proof of apical dominance.

Nigeria teaches the regulation of growth by hormones as its own section of Chapter 4 (printed p. 62): the five hormone families named, then auxin and apical dominance explained through hedge-cutting.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
