# Plant growth hormones

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Plant growth hormones. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
A plant has no nerves, but it still co-ordinates its growth — with hormones. Nigeria's Senior Secondary Biology 1, in Chapter 4, names the plant growth hormones: auxins, cytokinins, gibberellins, abscisic acid and ethylene. Auxin gets the worked example: it is made at the shoot tip and holds the side buds back — apical dominance. Cut the tip off, as a gardener cuts a hedge, and the side buds are released and the hedge grows bushy.

[Picture — a hedge before and after cutting: the uncut hedge growing tall from its top bud, the cut hedge bushing out sideways from released side buds]

## The key facts
**Five hormone families.** The book names the plant growth hormones: auxins, cytokinins, gibberellins, abscisic acid and ethylene — chemical regulators made by the plant itself.

**Auxin and the tip.** Auxin is produced at the growing tip and regulates growth below it — the hormone through which the tip controls the rest of the shoot.

**Apical dominance.** While the top (apical) bud grows, auxin from it holds the side buds back. That holding-back is apical dominance.

**The hedge proves it.** Cut the top off a hedge and the side buds, released from the tip's auxin, grow out — the hedge bushes. Hedge-cutting is the book's everyday proof of apical dominance.

**Nigeria's way.** Nigeria teaches the regulation of growth by hormones as its own section of Chapter 4 (printed p. 62): the five hormone families named, then auxin and apical dominance explained through hedge-cutting.
