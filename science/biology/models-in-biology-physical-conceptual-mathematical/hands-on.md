# Hands-on — Models in biology — physical, conceptual, mathematical

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: biology thinks with models, and the models come in three kinds — physical models you can hold, conceptual models that organise ideas, and mathematical models that calculate — each judged by what it can and cannot show.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: three models of the same cell side by side — a solid ball model, a labelled diagram, and a small equation with a graph. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
