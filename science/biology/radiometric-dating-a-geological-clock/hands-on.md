# Hands-on — Radiometric dating — a geological clock

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: radiometric dating reads a rock's age from its radioactive clocks: uranium-238 halves in 4.5 billion years and carbon-14 in 5,730 years — which is why carbon-14 fails beyond about 70,000 years — and the lead–lead ratio dates a rock's formation.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a row of hourglasses halving — a full measure, half, quarter, eighth — beside a fossil shell in rock layers. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
