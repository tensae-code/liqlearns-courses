# Hands-on — Improving memory and protecting the nervous system

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: memory can be improved by named methods — repetition, association and a regular regime — while alcohol, nicotine and drugs harm the work of the nervous system, so training memory and protecting the system belong to one lesson.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a brain drawn with a training loop of arrows labelled repeat and associate on one side, and three crossed-out harm items — alcohol, cigarette, drugs — on the other. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
