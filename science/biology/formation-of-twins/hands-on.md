# Hands-on — Formation of twins

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: twins form in two different ways: identical twins from one fertilised egg that splits, fraternal twins from two eggs fertilised separately — so identical twins share all their genes and fraternal twins share only what ordinary siblings share.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: two diagrams side by side: one fertilised egg splitting into two embryos labelled identical twins, and two separate eggs each fertilised, labelled fraternal twins. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
