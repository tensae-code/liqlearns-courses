# Formation of twins

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Formation of twins. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
At the end of its reproduction chapter, Malaysia's Form 4 book teaches the formation of twins (printed p. 300) — and examines it by comparison: identical against fraternal, one egg against two.

[Picture — two diagrams side by side: one fertilised egg splitting into two embryos labelled identical twins, and two separate eggs each fertilised, labelled fraternal twins]

## The key facts
**Identical (monozygotic) twins.** One egg is fertilised by one sperm, and the early embryo then splits into two. Both twins grow from the same fertilised egg, so they carry the same genes and are the same sex.

**Fraternal (dizygotic) twins.** Two eggs are released and each is fertilised by its own sperm. The two embryos are no more alike than ordinary brothers and sisters, and may be of different sexes.

**The comparison is the lesson.** The book examines twinning by comparison — one zygote or two, same genes or sibling genes, same sex always or not — the table is the teaching.

**Malaysia's way.** Malaysia's KSSM Form 4 book places the formation of twins inside Chapter 15 (printed p. 300), after the development of the foetus and before growth — twinning taught where fertilisation has just been learned, so the one-egg/two-egg split can be followed step by step.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words: twins form in two different ways: identical twins from one fertilised egg that splits, fraternal twins from two eggs fertilised separately — so identical twins share all their genes and fraternal twins share only what ordinary siblings share.
