# Formation of twins — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. What is the one thing this lesson teaches?**
a) twins form in two different ways: identical twins from one fertilised egg that splits, fraternal twins from two eggs fertilised separately — so identical twins share all their genes and fraternal twins share only what ordinary siblings share  b) That this topic has no rules and every case is different  c) A list of names with no process behind them  d) That the topic belongs only to scientists, not to daily life
**Answer: a) twins form in two different ways: identical twins from one fertilised egg that splits, fraternal twins from two eggs fertilised separately — so identical twins share all their genes and fraternal twins share only what ordinary siblings share**

**2. Identical (monozygotic) twins — which statement is right?**
a) One egg is fertilised by one sperm, and the early embryo then splits into two. Both twins grow from the same fertilised egg, so they carry the same genes and are the same sex.  b) The opposite of what the lesson teaches  c) It happens only in examinations  d) It is never observed in real life
**Answer: a) One egg is fertilised by one sperm, and the early embryo then splits into two. Both twins grow from the same fertilised egg, so they carry the same genes and are the same sex.**

**3. Fraternal (dizygotic) twins — which statement is right?**
a) It is unrelated to the lesson's topic  b) Two eggs are released and each is fertilised by its own sperm. The two embryos are no more alike than ordinary brothers and sisters, and may be of different sexes.  c) It is a myth the book corrects  d) It applies only outside Malaysia
**Answer: b) Two eggs are released and each is fertilised by its own sperm. The two embryos are no more alike than ordinary brothers and sisters, and may be of different sexes.**
