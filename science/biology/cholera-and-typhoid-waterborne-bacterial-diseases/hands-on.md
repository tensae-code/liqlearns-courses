# Hands-on — Cholera and typhoid — waterborne bacterial diseases

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Take the lesson's key facts for a walk: find one example of this lesson's idea in your own surroundings — your home, street, market or school — and say which key fact it matches, and why.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory — a village water point drawn as the meeting place of danger and defence — a contaminated water jar on one side with the two bacteria named beneath it, Vibrio cholerae and Salmonella typhi — and on the other side the defences: a cup of oral rehydration salts, a deep pit latrine, a vaccination syringe, and a food handler washing hands before serving — label its parts, and check against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
