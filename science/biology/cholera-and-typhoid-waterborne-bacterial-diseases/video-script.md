Today: Cholera and typhoid — waterborne bacterial diseases.

Remember this one thing: Two killer diseases travel in water and food: Kenya's Form 3 book teaches cholera (Vibrio cholerae) and typhoid (Salmonella typhi) together as human bacterial diseases — symptoms, oral rehydration salts, deep pit latrines, vaccination and food-handler hygiene.

Cholera and typhoid are Kenya's paired lesson in how water carries disease. Cholera — Vibrio cholerae — empties the body by diarrhoea, and the rescue is as simple as the disease is violent: oral rehydration salts, replacing the water and salts being lost. Typhoid — Salmonella typhi — travels the same roads, through water and food, and is met by vaccination and by hygiene wherever food is handled. Under both stands the same foundation: deep pit latrines in rural areas, keeping human waste away from the water people drink. Two bacteria, one road, and the defences stationed along it.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
