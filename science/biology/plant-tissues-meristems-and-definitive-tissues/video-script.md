Today: Plant tissues — meristems and definitive tissues.

Remember this one thing: a plant is built from tissues that all begin in its meristems: primary meristems lengthen the plant, secondary meristems thicken root and stem, and the meristems produce the four definitive tissues — covering, parenchyma (storage), support and vascular (conducting) tissues.

Every tissue in a plant starts as a meristem cell. At the tips, the primary meristems push the plant longer. In the root and stem, the secondary meristems make it thicker. And their children finish as four kinds of tissue: a covering skin, storage parenchyma, supporting tissue, and the vascular tissue that conducts. Growth first — then the finished jobs.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
