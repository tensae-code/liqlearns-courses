# Video script — Keeping body temperature steady

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Cold-blooded (poikilothermic) animals behaviourally seek sun, shade, water and burrows; warm-blooded (homeothermic) animals generate their own heat and hold temperature by insulation (feathers puffed, hair raised), vasodilation and sweating to lose heat, vasoconstriction and shivering to keep it, and hibernation/estivation; heat moves by radiation, evaporation, convection and conduction, and small animals lose heat faster because their surface-area-to-volume ratio is larger (rabbit vs elephant).

Hibernation in warm- and cold-blooded animals (bats, mammals, birds) against aestivation in cold-blooded animals (snails, earthworms, frogs); morphological regulation contrasted by habitat — desert animals with large heat-shedding features against the Arctic fox's small ears and thick fur; and a two-cube demonstration that surface-area-to-volume ratio falls as size grows, worked through the shrew as the small, fast heat-loser.

Now say it in your own words — and find one example near your home.
