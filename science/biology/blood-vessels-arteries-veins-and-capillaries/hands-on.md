# Hands-on — Blood vessels — arteries, veins and capillaries

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: the blood travels in three kinds of vessel — arteries carrying it away from the heart in thick elastic walls, capillaries with walls one cell thick where exchange happens, and veins carrying it back with valves against the low pressure — each kind built for its part of the journey.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a blood vessel drawn three times and labelled: a thick-walled artery, a hair-thin capillary beside a body cell, and a vein with a pocket valve. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
