# Respiratory quotient and respiratory substrates

## Do it first

## The idea
What a cell is burning can be read off the gases: Kenya's Form 2 book closes its respiration chapter with the respiratory substrates — carbohydrates, fats at about 38 kJ/g, and proteins — and the respiratory quotient, RQ = CO₂ produced ÷ O₂ used, about 0.7 for fat, 1.0 for carbohydrate and 0.9 for protein.

## The key facts
**Respiratory substrates.** The fuels respiration burns are the respiratory substrates: carbohydrates, fats — worth about 38 kJ/g — and proteins.

**The respiratory quotient.** RQ = CO₂ produced ÷ O₂ used. It is a pure ratio of the two gas volumes exchanged in respiration.

**RQ values name the fuel.** Carbohydrate gives RQ 1.0, fat about 0.7 and protein about 0.9 — so the measured quotient points to the substrate being respired.

**What sets the rate.** Six factors affect the rate of respiration: oxygen, substrate, hormones, surface-area-to-volume ratio, age and temperature.
