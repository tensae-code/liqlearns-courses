Today: Respiratory quotient and respiratory substrates.

Remember this one thing: What a cell is burning can be read off the gases: Kenya's Form 2 book closes its respiration chapter with the respiratory substrates — carbohydrates, fats at about 38 kJ/g, and proteins — and the respiratory quotient, RQ = CO₂ produced ÷ O₂ used, about 0.7 for fat, 1.0 for carbohydrate and 0.9 for protein.

Respiration needs a fuel, and the fuel leaves a fingerprint in the gases. Burn carbohydrate and the carbon dioxide given out exactly matches the oxygen taken in — RQ one point zero. Burn fat — worth about thirty-eight kilojoules a gram — and less carbon dioxide comes out per oxygen used: RQ about nought point seven. Protein sits between, about nought point nine. Measure the two gases and the quotient tells you what is being respired. And the rate itself is not fixed: oxygen, substrate, hormones, surface-area-to-volume ratio, age and temperature all push it up or down — Kenya lists all six.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
