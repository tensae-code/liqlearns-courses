# Hands-on — Cell wall and extracellular matrix

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: outside the cell membrane there can be further structure: plants carry a cellulose cell wall, and animal cells sit in an extracellular matrix — the lesson covers the structures that lie outside the cell itself.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a plant cell and an animal cell side by side: the plant cell wrapped in a thick labelled cellulose cell wall outside its membrane, the animal cell resting in a web labelled extracellular matrix. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
