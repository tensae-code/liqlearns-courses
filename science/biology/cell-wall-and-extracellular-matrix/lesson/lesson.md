# Cell wall and extracellular matrix

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Cell wall and extracellular matrix. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
the cellulose cell wall of plants (and its presence in prokaryotes, some protists and fungi) and the extracellular matrix

[Picture — a plant cell and an animal cell side by side: the plant cell wrapped in a thick labelled cellulose cell wall outside its membrane, the animal cell resting in a web labelled extracellular matrix]

## The key facts
**The cell wall.** Kuwait's new Grade 10 student book (Lesson 1-2, printed p. 45) teaches the structures outside the cell: the cellulose cell wall of plants — and its presence also in prokaryotes, some protists and fungi.

**The extracellular matrix.** Beside the wall the book sets the extracellular matrix, the material outside animal cells in which the cells sit — the second of the lesson's two outside-the-cell structures.

**Kuwait's way.** Kuwait's new Grade 10 book gives the outside of the cell its own headed place in the cell lesson (Lesson 1-2, printed p. 45): wall and matrix taught together as "structures outside the cell".
