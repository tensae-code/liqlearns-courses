Today: Digestive system disorders and hygiene.

Remember this one thing: The digestive system has its own diseases — most of them carried in by dirty water, dirty hands and spoiled food — and its hygiene is the daily practice that keeps them out.

Diarrhoea and dysentery empty the body of water dangerously fast; constipation is the opposite failure of movement. Indigestion and acid pain follow overeating and spoiled or irritating food, and an ulcer is a sore in the stomach or intestine wall, worsened by acid.

The germs of cholera and typhoid travel in water and food fouled by sewage and by dirty hands; hepatitis viruses travel the same road. These are not faults of the stomach — they are invaders swallowed with what should have been clean.

Roundworms, tapeworms and their kin are swallowed as eggs or larvae in unwashed vegetables, undercooked meat or dirty water. Inside, they take food the body digested for itself, leaving weakness and poor growth, especially in children.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
