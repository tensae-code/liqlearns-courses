# Digestive system disorders and hygiene

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Digestive system disorders and hygiene. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Afghanistan's Grade 9 Biology book closes its digestive-system chapter with that system's diseases and its hygiene. This lesson teaches the pair on their own: the disorders that strike the canal and its glands, how most of them enter, and the hygiene rules that stop them.

## The key facts
**Disorders of the canal.** Diarrhoea and dysentery empty the body of water dangerously fast; constipation is the opposite failure of movement. Indigestion and acid pain follow overeating and spoiled or irritating food, and an ulcer is a sore in the stomach or intestine wall, worsened by acid.

**Germ diseases ride in food and water.** The germs of cholera and typhoid travel in water and food fouled by sewage and by dirty hands; hepatitis viruses travel the same road. These are not faults of the stomach — they are invaders swallowed with what should have been clean.

**Worms and parasites.** Roundworms, tapeworms and their kin are swallowed as eggs or larvae in unwashed vegetables, undercooked meat or dirty water. Inside, they take food the body digested for itself, leaving weakness and poor growth, especially in children.

**Hygiene of hands, water and food.** The hygiene rule is a chain of barriers: hands washed after the latrine and before eating or cooking; water from a clean source, boiled or treated where it is doubtful; raw vegetables washed; food cooked through and covered from flies while it waits.

**Hygiene of eating itself.** Eating at regular times, chewing food well, not overloading the stomach and not lying down straight after a heavy meal all help the canal do its work — and cleaning the teeth protects the mouth, where digestion begins, from decay and gum disease.

**Afghanistan's way.** Every Grade 9 body-system chapter closes the same way — with that system's diseases and its hygiene — so the digestive chapter ends not at absorption but at the disorders of the gut and the hand-washing that prevents them.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words: The digestive system has its own diseases — most of them carried in by dirty water, dirty hands and spoiled food — and its hygiene is the daily practice that keeps them out. Use each key fact once. Then let your companion explain it back to you.
