# Cell reactions — tactic and nastic movements

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Cell reactions — tactic and nastic movements. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
A living cell is not passive. Nigeria's Senior Secondary Biology 1 closes Chapter 4 with cell reactions to the environment, through movement. When a whole cell or organism moves towards a stimulus or away from it, that is a tactic movement — the direction of the stimulus sets the direction of the move. The lesson sets tactic movements among the forms of movement in cells and organisms, including the nastic responses that do not depend on the stimulus's direction.

[Picture — a single-celled organism swimming towards light and away from a harmful chemical, with arrows showing movement towards and away from each stimulus]

## The key facts
**Cells react by moving.** Movement is a reaction: cells and organisms respond to changes in their environment by moving — one of the cell reactions Chapter 4 teaches.

**Tactic movements.** A tactic movement is the movement of a whole cell or organism towards a stimulus or away from it — the stimulus's direction decides the direction of movement.

**Towards and away.** Tactic responses can be positive (towards the stimulus) or negative (away from it) — approach what helps, escape what harms.

**Nastic movements.** Nastic movements are responses whose direction does not depend on where the stimulus comes from — the other named form set beside the tactic responses.

**Nigeria's way.** Nigeria teaches cell reactions inside Chapter 4 (printed p. 65) as movement: tactic movements towards and away from stimuli at cell level, and the forms of movement in cells and organisms.
