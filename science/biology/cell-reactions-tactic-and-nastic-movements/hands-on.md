# Hands-on — Cell reactions — tactic and nastic movements

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Shine a torch from one side of a clear container of pond water and watch, over some minutes, whether the tiny organisms gather or scatter. Say aloud, for anything that moves: towards or away — positive or negative tactic response. If no pond water is available, act it out: a companion calls a stimulus (light, heat, a harmful smell) and a direction, and you move as the cell would.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — a single-celled organism swimming towards light and away from a harmful chemical, with arrows showing movement towards and away from each stimulus — and check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
