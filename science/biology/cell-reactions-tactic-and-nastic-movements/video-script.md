Today: Cell reactions — tactic and nastic movements.

Remember this one thing: cells and organisms answer their surroundings by moving: a tactic movement is a whole cell or organism moving towards or away from a stimulus, and nastic movements are the non-directional responses — movement is one of the ways a living cell reacts to its environment.

Movement is a reaction: cells and organisms respond to changes in their environment by moving — one of the cell reactions Chapter 4 teaches.

A tactic movement is the movement of a whole cell or organism towards a stimulus or away from it — the stimulus's direction decides the direction of movement.

Tactic responses can be positive (towards the stimulus) or negative (away from it) — approach what helps, escape what harms.

Nastic movements are responses whose direction does not depend on where the stimulus comes from — the other named form set beside the tactic responses.

Nigeria teaches cell reactions inside Chapter 4 (printed p. 65) as movement: tactic movements towards and away from stimuli at cell level, and the forms of movement in cells and organisms.
