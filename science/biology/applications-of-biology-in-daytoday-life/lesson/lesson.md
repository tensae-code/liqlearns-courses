# Applications of Biology in Day-to-Day Life

## Do it first
Before you read: think of one living thing near your home that shows this lesson's idea — applications of biology in day-to-day life. Hold it in your mind, then read on and check your idea against what the lesson teaches.

Six units: Applications of Biology, Microorganisms, Energy Transformation, Evolution, Human Body Systems, Climate Change. Every unit opens with a photo-collage title page and a "Unit learning outcome" box; every section opens with its own outcome box ("After the successful completion of this section, you will be able to:") and most carry Keywords boxes defining the section's terms in plain words, Self-questioning boxes, and numbered Activities (group research, posters, school gardening, practicals such as the knee-jerk test and a fermentation-rate experiment). Two-column textbook layout with heavy colour photography and diagrams (Borana bull, LUCA tree, chloroplast, phosphorus cycle, Greta Thunberg). Ethiopia is woven through: tej/tella fermentation, Ethiopian natural resources, renowned Ethiopian microbiologists/anthropologists/evolutionists, Lucy–Ardi–Selam, the Green Legacy Initiative and the GERD. Each unit closes with a Unit Summary and Unit Review Questions. The WAY itself — outcome-box-first, keyword glossary, activity-driven, Ethiopia-anchored applications — is course content for the Grade 12 band.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion, in your own words: what applications of biology in day-to-day life means, the main ideas of this lesson, and one example from your own surroundings.

## Do It Yourself
1. Write the lesson's main idea in one sentence of your own.
2. Find one example of it in your home, street or school and note what you observed.
3. Teach the idea to someone at home and note the question they asked you.
