Today: Pheromones — chemical signals between animals.

Remember this one thing: Pheromones are chemical signals an animal releases that carry a message to others of its own species — Vietnam's Grade 11 behaviour lesson gives pheromones their own section (Bài 18, IV), with the silkworm moth as the case, and its Grade 12 study topics put pheromone traps to work against crop pests.

A pheromone is a smell with a message. One animal releases it; only its own species can read it. The silkworm moth is Vietnam's case — the female's scent draws the male across the air. And because the signal is so exact, people borrow it: a trap baited with pheromone catches the males of one pest and no other animal. Vietnam teaches pheromones twice — once as animal behaviour, once as a pest-control tool.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
