Today: Alternation of generations — the Funaria life cycle.

Remember this one thing: in Funaria the generations alternate with the haploid gametophyte on top: the familiar moss plant is the gametophyte; its antherozoids swim through a drop of water to the archegonium, and the zygote grows into the diploid sporophyte, which stays dependent on the gametophyte and makes the spores in its capsule that start the cycle again.

The moss lives two lives in turn. The green plant you see is the gametophyte. It makes swimming male cells — antherozoids — and they need a drop of water to swim through, drawn by sugar, to reach the archegonium. From the fertilised egg grows the second generation, the sporophyte — but in a moss it stays small and dependent, living on the gametophyte, and its capsule makes the spores that begin everything again.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
