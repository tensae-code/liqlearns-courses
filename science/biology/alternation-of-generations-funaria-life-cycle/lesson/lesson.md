# Alternation of generations — the Funaria life cycle

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Alternation of generations — the Funaria life cycle. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Mozambique's plant module (Módulo 2) follows its bryophyte lesson with the full life cycle of Funaria, the example moss. The cycle is an alternation of generations with the balance tipped the moss way: the green plant you see is the haploid gametophyte, and the diploid sporophyte lives on it, dependent on it. Fertilisation still needs water — the antherozoids swim. The module also notes vegetative fragmentation as a second way the moss multiplies. This lesson teaches the cycle step by step, as the module works it.

[Picture — the Funaria cycle drawn as a circle: the green gametophyte plant with antheridia and archegonia, a swimming antherozoid crossing a water drop, then the sporophyte rising from the gametophyte on a stalk with its capsule, and spores falling from the capsule back to the ground]

## The key facts
**The gametophyte is the main plant.** The green moss plant is the haploid gametophyte — the dominant generation. It carries the sex organs: antheridia making antherozoids, archegonia holding the egg.

**Fertilisation in a drop of water.** The antherozoids swim in a drop of water to the archegonium — attracted by sugar in the archegonium's neck — and one joins the egg: fertilisation.

**The sporophyte lives on the gametophyte.** The zygote is diploid: it starts the sporophyte generation. The sporophyte grows out of the gametophyte and stays dependent on it — the reverse of the flowering plants, where the sporophyte is the main plant.

**Spores, and fragmentation.** The sporophyte's capsule forms the spores, which are released and grow into new gametophytes. The module also notes vegetative fragmentation — pieces of the plant breaking off and growing — as another way Funaria multiplies.
