Today: Modified leaves.

Remember this one thing: a leaf does not have to stay a flat green blade. A leaf can be reshaped — modified — to do a different job.

Some leaves become tendrils — thin curling threads, like the watermelon and the passion fruit grow — that grip a support and lift the plant towards the light.

Some leaves become traps. The Venus flytrap snaps shut on an insect; the pitcher plant holds a pit of fluid the insect falls into. The leaf now catches food instead of only making it.

An onion's scales are leaves too — thick, pale and wrapped around the bulb, storing food. A cactus turns its leaves into spines: no flat blade to lose water, and a sharp defence. And some leaves swell fat with stored water — bryophyllum, aloe vera, sisal.

Uganda's Senior One book teaches all of these in one activity — Activity 5.6 — sorted by the job each reshaped leaf does (printed pp. 81–82).

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
