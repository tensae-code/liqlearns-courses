# Modified leaves — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** Explain: what does “modified leaf” mean, with two examples?
**Answer: A modified leaf is a leaf reshaped from the ordinary flat green blade to do a different job. Examples: the onion's scales are leaves storing food; the cactus's spines are leaves reduced to points that lose little water and defend the plant; a passion fruit's tendril is a leaf that climbs; a Venus flytrap's trap is a leaf that catches insects.**

**2.** Explain: Uganda's way of teaching this.
**Answer: Uganda's Senior One textbook teaches modified leaves as one sorting activity — Activity 5.6 in the flowering-plants chapter (printed pp. 81–82) — where the reshaped leaves are sorted by the job each does: tendrils, insect-traps, scale leaves, spines and swollen leaves.**
