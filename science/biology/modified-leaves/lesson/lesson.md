# Modified leaves

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Modified leaves. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
An ordinary leaf is a flat green blade that makes food. But in many plants the leaf has been reshaped — modified — for a completely different job: climbing, trapping insects, storing food, defending the plant, or holding water. Uganda's Senior One book gathers these reshaped leaves into one sorting activity, Activity 5.6 of its flowering-plants chapter (printed pp. 81–82), and sorts them by the job each one does. That sorting is this lesson.

[Picture — five leaves in a row, each reshaped differently: a curling tendril, an open trap, a fleshy onion scale, a sharp cactus spine, and a thick swollen leaf]

## The key facts
**Leaf tendrils — the climbing leaves.** In plants like watermelon and passion fruit, leaves (or parts of them) grow as thin, curling threads — tendrils — that coil around a support and pull the plant upward towards the light.

**Insect-traps — the hunting leaves.** The Venus flytrap's leaf is a hinged trap that snaps shut on an insect; the pitcher plant's leaf is a deep pit that holds fluid an insect falls into and cannot climb out of. These leaves catch insects and digest them.

**Scale leaves and spines — the storing and defending leaves.** The onion's fleshy scales are modified leaves wrapped around the bulb, storing food. The cactus's spines are leaves reduced to points: they lose almost no water and they defend the plant against animals that would eat it.

**Swollen leaves — the water-storing leaves.** In bryophyllum, aloe vera and sisal the leaves are thick and swollen, storing water (and food) inside themselves, so the plant can live through dry times.

**Uganda's way.** Uganda's book does not scatter these through the chapter: Activity 5.6 (printed pp. 81–82) is one sorting task — modified leaves sorted by job — tendrils, traps, scales, spines and swollen leaves side by side.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words: leaves are reshaped — modified — for other jobs: tendrils climb, traps catch insects, scales store food, spines defend, and swollen leaves store water — the set Uganda's book sorts in Activity 5.6. Use each key fact once. Then let your companion explain it back to you.
