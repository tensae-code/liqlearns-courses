# Hands-on — Information transfer in ecosystems

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: ecosystems run on information as well as energy and matter: physical, chemical and behavioural signals pass from a source through a channel to a receptor — the bee's dances, the silkworm moth's bombykol, a plant's phytochrome reading light, and the volatiles an attacked tobacco plant releases to summon parasitoids.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a tobacco leaf being eaten, wavy signal lines rising from it, and a small parasitoid wasp flying in along the scent trail. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
