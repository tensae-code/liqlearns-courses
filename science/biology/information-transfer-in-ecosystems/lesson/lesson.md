# Information transfer in ecosystems

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Information transfer in ecosystems. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Ecosystems run on information as well as energy and matter: physical, chemical and behavioural signals pass from a source through a channel to a receptor — the bee's dances, the silkworm moth's bombykol, a plant's phytochrome reading light, and the volatiles an attacked tobacco plant releases to summon parasitoids. No lesson in this course taught this on its own before, so this lesson is built for it — from China's People's Education Press biology textbooks, which teach it as a named topic.

[Picture — a tobacco leaf being eaten, wavy signal lines rising from it, and a small parasitoid wasp flying in along the scent trail]

## The key facts
**Ch. 3 §4 Information transfer.** physical, chemical and behavioural information on a source–channel–receptor chain; bee round and waggle dances; Butenandt's bombykol (20 years, 500,000 silkworm moths, 1959 — the first pheromone); phytochrome as a plant's information receptor; dolphin echolocation over 100 kHz and ship noise behind mass strandings; herbivore-attacked tobacco releasing volatiles that summon parasitoids (2001); and the farming uses — simulated pollinator signals, longer light for laying hens, pheromone traps and mating disruption.
