# Hands-on — Fruits — types and placentation

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Take the lesson's key facts for a walk: find one example of this lesson's idea in your own surroundings — your home, street, market or school — and say which key fact it matches, and why.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory — a market stall of opened fruits, each cut to show its making — a tomato berry, a mango drupe with its stone, a bean pod legume, a cotton capsule sprung open, a maize grain caryopsis and a macadamia nut — and beneath them a row of ovary cross-sections showing the five ways ovules are attached, the placentation types — label its parts, and check against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
