Today: Fruits — types and placentation.

Remember this one thing: A fruit is classified by what it is built from and how its seeds were attached: Kenya's Form 3 book works seed and fruit development (gibberellin-driven growth, parthenocarpy in pineapple and banana), the fruit kinds — berry, drupe, legume, capsule, schizocarp, caryopsis, cypsela, nut — false fruits, and the five placentation types, with a fruit-classification practical.

After fertilisation the ovary wall becomes the fruit — gibberellin drives the growth — and sometimes no fertilisation is needed at all: pineapple and banana set fruit without seeds, parthenocarpy. Then Kenya sorts the harvest. A berry, like tomato or orange, is fleshy all through; a drupe, mango or coconut, hides a stone; the bean family's pod is a legume; cotton and poppy dry into capsules; castor splits as a schizocarp; maize is a caryopsis, Tridax a cypsela, macadamia a nut — and some fruits are false, built from more than the ovary. Under the sorting sits placentation, the five ways ovules were attached inside the ovary — and the class proves the system with a classification practical on real fruits.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
