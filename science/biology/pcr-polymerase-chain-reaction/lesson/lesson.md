# PCR — polymerase chain reaction

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — PCR — polymerase chain reaction. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
PCR copies one chosen piece of DNA over and over in a tube by cycling three temperature steps — denaturation at about 96°C, where the double strand separates; annealing at 55–65°C, where primers bind; and extension at 72°C, where Taq polymerase builds the new strands — so the number of copies doubles with every cycle. No lesson in this course taught this on its own before, so this lesson is built for it — from Indonesia's Kurikulum Merdeka books, which teach it as a named topic.

[Picture — a tube in a thermal cycler shown at three stations in a circle — 96°C with the DNA ladder unzipping into two single strands, 55–65°C with two short primers binding, 72°C with Taq polymerase extending new strands — and below, 1 copy becoming 2, then 4, then 8]

## The key facts
**A copying machine for DNA.** The polymerase chain reaction (PCR) makes many copies of one chosen DNA segment in a tube, without any living cell — the segment is picked out by the primers, short DNA pieces that match its two ends.

**Step one — denaturation (96°C).** Heating to about 96°C separates the double-stranded DNA into single strands, opening them up to be copied.

**Step two — annealing (55–65°C).** Cooling to 55–65°C lets the primers bind (anneal) to their matching sequences on the single strands, marking where copying starts.

**Step three — extension (72°C).** At 72°C the heat-stable enzyme Taq polymerase extends the primers, building new complementary strands. One cycle doubles the copies; the cycles repeat, so the copies multiply — 2, 4, 8, and on.

**Indonesia's way.** Indonesia's Grade 12 student book does not teach PCR; it appears in the Grade 12 teacher guide's answer key (Bab 2, printed p. 67), which states the mechanism in its three steps with their temperatures — denaturation 96°C, annealing 55–65°C, extension by Taq polymerase at 72°C — and the doubling per cycle.
