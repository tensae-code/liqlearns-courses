# Muscle contraction — sliding filaments

## Do it first
Before you read: think of one living thing near your home that shows this lesson's idea — muscle contraction. Hold it in your mind, then read on and check your idea against what the lesson teaches.

Muscle fibres are bundles of myofibrils built from thick myosin and thin actin filaments in repeating units (sarcomeres); a nerve impulse at the neuromuscular junction releases calcium, calcium binding to troponin uncovers actin's binding sites (normally blocked by tropomyosin), myosin heads form cross-bridges and the filaments slide past each other — the sliding-filament action — needing ATP; contractions are isometric (no length change), concentric (shortening) or eccentric (lengthening).
