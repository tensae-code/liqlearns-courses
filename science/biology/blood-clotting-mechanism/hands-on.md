# Hands-on — Blood clotting mechanism

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: when a blood vessel is cut, blood clots by a mechanism: platelets and plasma proteins build a fibrin net that traps blood cells and seals the wound.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a cut blood vessel in cross-section: platelets gathering at the break, fibrin threads forming a net across it, and red blood cells trapped in the net. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
