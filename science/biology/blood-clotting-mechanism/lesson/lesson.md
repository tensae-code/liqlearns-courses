# Blood clotting mechanism

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Blood clotting mechanism. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Malaysia's Form 4 book gives blood clotting its own mechanism inside the transport chapter (Ch.10, printed p. 183) — between the heartbeat and the blood groups. Clotting is taught as a process with steps, not as a single fact: a cut vessel is sealed by a clot built where the damage is.

[Picture — a cut blood vessel in cross-section: platelets gathering at the break, fibrin threads forming a net across it, and red blood cells trapped in the net]

## The key facts
**Why blood must clot.** An open vessel would bleed without limit and let microbes in. The clot is the seal: it forms at the wound, stops the loss, and covers the break while the vessel repairs.

**The mechanism.** Damaged tissue and platelets start the change: soluble plasma protein is converted into insoluble fibrin threads. The threads form a net across the wound, blood cells are trapped in the net, and the trapped mass is the clot.

**Platelets.** The platelets — the smallest blood fragments — gather at the damage and are central to starting the clot; a blood count short of platelets clots poorly.

**Malaysia's way.** In Malaysia's KSSM Form 4 book the mechanism of blood clotting is a named subtopic of Chapter 10 (printed p. 183), taught in its own right between the mechanism of heartbeat (p. 180) and blood groups (p. 185).
