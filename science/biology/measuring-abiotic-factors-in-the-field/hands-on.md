# Hands-on — Measuring abiotic factors in the field

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Go outside with a thermometer if you have one. Read the temperature in sun and in shade, note the wind's direction by watching leaves or a strip of cloth, and judge the light in both places. Record the four readings in a table like the book's Jos table, and say aloud which instrument a scientist would use for each factor you estimated.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — a field kit laid out and labelled: thermometer, wet-and-dry-bulb hygrometer, wind vane, anemometer, light meter and aneroid barometer — and check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
