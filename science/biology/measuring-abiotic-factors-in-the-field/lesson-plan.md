# Lesson plan — Measuring abiotic factors in the field

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Biology · **Lesson:** Measuring abiotic factors in the field
**Place in the curriculum:** Biology — appended to Unit 16 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the non-living conditions of a habitat can be measured, each with its own instrument: temperature, relative humidity by wet-and-dry-bulb hygrometer, wind by vane and anemometer, light by light meter, and elevation by aneroid barometer — and Nigeria's book works them with Nigerian data.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own as a single-skill lesson built from Nigeria's Senior Secondary Biology 1, Ch. 7 (printed p. 110). Related lessons are recommended first where they help, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words, the one thing this lesson teaches: the non-living conditions of a habitat can be measured, each with its own instrument: temperature, relative humidity by wet-and-dry-bulb hygrometer, wind by vane and anemometer, light by light meter, and elevation by aneroid barometer — and Nigeria's book works them with Nigerian data.
2. Explain each key fact (Every factor has its instrument; Humidity by two bulbs; Wind and light; Elevation by pressure; Nigeria's data) in their own words.
3. Explain Nigeria's way as the placements describe it: the topic is taught in Ch. 7 of Senior Secondary Biology 1 (printed p. 110).
