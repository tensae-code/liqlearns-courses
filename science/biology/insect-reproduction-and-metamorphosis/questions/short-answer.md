# Insect reproduction and metamorphosis — short answer

Answer in your own words, in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) In your own words, what does this lesson teach about reproduction in insects?**
**Answer: Insects are segmented, joint-legged arthropods with exoskeletons; the honeybee's male and female reproductive structures are figured and researched; fertilisation is usually internal, eggs are laid after mating, and development runs through metamorphosis — complete (egg, larva, pupa, adult, as in honeybees) or incomplete (egg, nymph, adult, as in grasshoppers).**

**2. (Sub lesson 2) In your own words, what does this lesson teach about added insect detail?**
**Answer: The guide supplies the grasshopper's male system (paired testes, vasa deferentia, ejaculatory duct, copulatory organ, accessory glands) and female system (ovary, oviduct, spermatheca and more), and a complete/incomplete metamorphosis table: incomplete — three stages, nymphs resembling adults, wings appearing as buds, grasshoppers/cockroaches/termites; complete — four stages each looking different, most insects.**
