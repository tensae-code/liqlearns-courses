Today: World biomes.

Remember this one thing: A biome is a great region of the Earth sorted by its climate — its rainfall and temperature — each carrying its own characteristic plants and animals, fitted to that climate.

A biome is named for its dominant plants — forest, grassland, desert — because plants, rooted in place, must fit the climate exactly. Change the rainfall or the temperature enough and the same ground carries a different biome.

In the tundra only mosses, lichens and low shrubs survive the long winter, over permafrost that never thaws. South of it the taiga — the great conifer forest of spruce, fir and pine — endures cold winters on somewhat kinder ground.

Deciduous trees shed their leaves against the winter cold, in forests that once covered much of Europe, eastern Asia and North America. Where rain falls short of forest, grassland takes over — steppe and prairie, grazed by herds and now largely ploughed for grain.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
