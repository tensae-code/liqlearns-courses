# World biomes

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — World biomes. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Afghanistan's Grade 11 Biology book closes with the biomes (بایوم‌ها) — the Earth's great life regions. This lesson teaches the world biomes on their own: what decides a biome, the main land biomes from tundra to tropical rainforest, and the rule that climate, not the map's borders, draws their edges.

## The key facts
**Climate decides the biome.** A biome is named for its dominant plants — forest, grassland, desert — because plants, rooted in place, must fit the climate exactly. Change the rainfall or the temperature enough and the same ground carries a different biome.

**The cold biomes.** In the tundra only mosses, lichens and low shrubs survive the long winter, over permafrost that never thaws. South of it the taiga — the great conifer forest of spruce, fir and pine — endures cold winters on somewhat kinder ground.

**Temperate biomes.** Deciduous trees shed their leaves against the winter cold, in forests that once covered much of Europe, eastern Asia and North America. Where rain falls short of forest, grassland takes over — steppe and prairie, grazed by herds and now largely ploughed for grain.

**The dry biomes.** Below roughly 250 mm of rain a year, vegetation thins to scattered, water-wise plants — deep roots, small or waxy leaves, water stored in stems — and the animals shelter by day. Afghanistan's own southern and western deserts are part of this biome.

**The wet tropics.** Rainforest packs more species into a hectare than other biomes hold in a province, in layers from floor to canopy. Where the tropics have a marked dry season, savanna replaces it — grass, fire, grazing herds and their predators.

**Afghanistan's way.** The Grade 11 book ends its ecology part — and the whole year's course — by widening from populations to the whole planet: interactions first, then the biomes those interactions play out in.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words: A biome is a great region of the Earth sorted by its climate — its rainfall and temperature — each carrying its own characteristic plants and animals, fitted to that climate. Use each key fact once. Then let your companion explain it back to you.
