# Economic importance of fish, birds and mammals

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Economic importance of fish, birds and mammals. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Afghanistan's Grade 8 Biology book pairs its survey of the country's birds and mammals with their economic importance, fish included. This lesson teaches that account on its own: what each group gives people — food, materials, work, pest control and trade — and why over-using them destroys the very income they give.

## The key facts
**Fish give food and income.** From rivers and lakes, fish bring protein to the table and money to the fisher. Fish farming adds a managed supply, and dried or sold fish carry the value far from the water where they were caught.

**Birds give food — and free work.** Chickens give eggs and meat in nearly every yard, and ducks and other poultry add to them. Wild birds earn without wages — insect-eating birds hold down crop pests, and vultures and crows clean up carrion that would otherwise spread disease.

**Mammals give food and materials.** Sheep, goats and cattle convert grass people cannot eat into milk and meat people can. Their wool and hair are spun and woven, their hides become leather — for Afghanistan, livestock products are household food and market goods at once.

**Mammals give labour.** Before engines, and still where roads and fuel run short, working mammals are transport and tractor in one: pack loads on mountain paths, carts and ploughs in the fields, and herding across the plains.

**Use them without ending them.** An animal taken before it breeds, or a river, marsh or forest destroyed, pays once and never again. Leaving breeding stock, respecting closed seasons and keeping habitats whole keeps fish, birds and mammals paying year after year.

**Afghanistan's way.** The Grade 8 book does not stop at naming the country's birds and mammals — the same chapter prices them, teaching the economic importance of fish, birds and mammals as the reason classification matters to a household.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words: Fish, birds and mammals earn their keep in a household economy: they give food, materials, labour and services — and protecting them protects that income. Use each key fact once. Then let your companion explain it back to you.
