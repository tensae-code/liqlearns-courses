# Video script — Economic importance of fish, birds and mammals

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Today: Economic importance of fish, birds and mammals.

Remember this one thing: Fish, birds and mammals earn their keep in a household economy: they give food, materials, labour and services — and protecting them protects that income.

From rivers and lakes, fish bring protein to the table and money to the fisher. Fish farming adds a managed supply, and dried or sold fish carry the value far from the water where they were caught.

Chickens give eggs and meat in nearly every yard, and ducks and other poultry add to them. Wild birds earn without wages — insect-eating birds hold down crop pests, and vultures and crows clean up carrion that would otherwise spread disease.

Sheep, goats and cattle convert grass people cannot eat into milk and meat people can. Their wool and hair are spun and woven, their hides become leather — for Afghanistan, livestock products are household food and market goods at once.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
