# Saltwater intrusion and coastal aquifers — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** Explain: fresh water floats on salt water.
**Answer: Rain that soaks into coastal ground gathers as groundwater in an aquifer. Salt water from the sea also sits in the ground below the coast, and because salt water is denser, the fresh water rests on top of it. The boundary between the two — the saltwater/freshwater interface — is a balance: fresh water pressing down and out to sea, salt water pressing in.**

**2.** Explain: rising seas push the boundary inland.
**Answer: When the sea level rises, the sea's pressure at the coast rises with it, and the interface moves: salt water pushes further inland underground and rises nearer the surface. Wells and boreholes that drew fresh water begin to draw brackish, salty water — the aquifer itself has been invaded, slowly and invisibly.**
