# Saltwater intrusion and coastal aquifers

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Saltwater intrusion and coastal aquifers. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Fiji's Year 12 Biology book teaches environmental issues from Fiji's own ground. Among the effects of the enhanced greenhouse effect it names one that only an island country teaches this way: the sea, rising, does not only wash over the land — it pushes underneath it, into the fresh water stored below the coast.

## The key facts
**Fresh water floats on salt water.** Rain that soaks into coastal ground gathers as groundwater in an aquifer. Salt water from the sea also sits in the ground below the coast, and because salt water is denser, the fresh water rests on top of it. The boundary between the two — the saltwater/freshwater interface — is a balance: fresh water pressing down and out to sea, salt water pressing in.

**Rising seas push the boundary inland.** When the sea level rises, the sea's pressure at the coast rises with it, and the interface moves: salt water pushes further inland underground and rises nearer the surface. Wells and boreholes that drew fresh water begin to draw brackish, salty water — the aquifer itself has been invaded, slowly and invisibly.

**Pumping can pull the salt up.** The balance can be broken from the land side too: draw too much fresh water out of a coastal aquifer and the salt water below rises toward the well to fill the space. Intrusion is therefore driven both by the rising sea and by how the fresh water above it is used.

**Fiji's way.** The book places saltwater intrusion in its environmental-issues sub-strand (Sub-strand 3.2), beside the enhanced greenhouse effect — for Fiji this is not a distant example: island villages drink from coastal aquifers, and the same sub-strand's answers are Fiji's own, including reforestation and the planting of mangroves along the shore.
