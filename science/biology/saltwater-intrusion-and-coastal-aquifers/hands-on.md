# Hands-on — Saltwater intrusion and coastal aquifers

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
With two glasses, pour fresh water gently onto salt water coloured with a drop of ink (or the other way round, slowly, over the back of a spoon) and watch the boundary between them: the denser water stays below and the lighter floats. Then say, in your own words, what that boundary does under a coast when the sea rises.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — a picture of a coast cut open underground — the sea on one side, the land on the other, fresh groundwater drawn as a pale lens floating on darker salt water, the boundary line marked, a village well drawing from the fresh lens, and an arrow showing the salt water pushing inland as the sea rises — label it, and check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
