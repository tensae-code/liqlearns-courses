# Hands-on — Factors that affect the rate of photosynthesis

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: the rate of photosynthesis is set by limiting factors — light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration and temperature — and whichever is scarcest caps the rate, so raising the others alone does nothing until the scarcest is raised.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: three rising-line graphs side by side for light, carbon dioxide and temperature, each flattening into a plateau labelled 'another factor now limits'. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
