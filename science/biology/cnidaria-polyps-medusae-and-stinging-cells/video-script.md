Today: Cnidaria — polyps, medusae and stinging cells.

Remember this one thing: cnidarians are radially symmetrical animals with two body forms — the fixed polyp and the free-swimming medusa, a medusa being an inverted polyp — one gastrovascular cavity for digestion, and stinging cells, the cnidoblasts with their nematocysts, as the phylum's signature.

The cnidarians are built like a wheel — everything arranged around the middle. They come in two shapes. The polyp sits fixed, like a tiny tube with tentacles on top. The medusa swims free, like a bell — and the book teaches it as the same animal turned upside down: an inverted polyp. Both carry the phylum's famous weapon, stinging cells with nematocysts, and both digest food in one single cavity.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
