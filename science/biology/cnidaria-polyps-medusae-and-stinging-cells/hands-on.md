# Hands-on — Cnidaria — polyps, medusae and stinging cells

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Cup one hand fingers-up like a polyp, then turn it over and pulse it gently like a swimming bell — the same hand, inverted: that is the module's way of seeing the medusa as an inverted polyp.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory — a polyp drawn upright on a rock with tentacles up, beside a medusa drawn as the same animal turned upside down like a swimming bell, with one tentacle magnified to show a stinging cell and its nematocyst thread — label its parts, and check against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
