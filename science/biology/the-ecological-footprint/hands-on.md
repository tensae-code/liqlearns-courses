# Hands-on — The ecological footprint

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: the ecological footprint converts everything a person, city or country consumes and discards into the land and water area that must supply and absorb it, in six components — cropland, grazing land, forest, fishing grounds, built-up land and the carbon footprint.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a giant foot whose sole is a patchwork map — fields, pasture, forest, sea, rooftops and a smokestack's grey plume. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
