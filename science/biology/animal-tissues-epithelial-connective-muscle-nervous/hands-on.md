# Hands-on — Animal tissues — epithelial, connective, muscle, nervous

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: an animal's cells are organised into four tissue kinds — epithelial tissue covering and lining, connective tissue joining and supporting, muscle tissue moving, and nervous tissue carrying messages — each kind built of cells fitted to its one job.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: four panels side by side: a sheet of covering cells, a connective sample of bone and blood, a striped muscle bundle, and a nerve cell with its long fibre. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
