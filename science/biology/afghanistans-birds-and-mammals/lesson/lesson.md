# Afghanistan's birds and mammals

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Afghanistan's birds and mammals. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Afghanistan's Grade 8 Biology book finishes its animal classification with the birds and mammals of Afghanistan itself. This lesson teaches that national survey on its own: what marks a bird and what marks a mammal, and the members of each group an Afghan learner can meet in the country's mountains, plains, farms and skies.

## The key facts
**Birds — the marks.** Feathers are the bird's own mark — no other animal has them. The forelimbs are wings, the mouth is a beak without teeth, the body stays warm from inside, and the young hatch from hard-shelled eggs the parents usually warm and guard.

**Birds of Afghanistan.** Eagles, falcons and vultures ride the mountain air; cranes and water birds visit the rivers and lakes on migration; and around farms and gardens live the hoopoe, sparrows, pigeons and crows — the country's birds are sorted by the same bird marks whatever their size.

**Mammals — the marks.** Hair and milk are the mammal marks. The young are born alive (in nearly all mammals), are suckled on the mother's milk, breathe air with lungs, and keep a constant warm body temperature.

**Wild mammals of Afghanistan.** The Hindu Kush and the Pamirs hold wild sheep and goats — the markhor with its corkscrew horns, Afghanistan's national animal, and the ibex — hunted in turn by the snow leopard. Foxes, wolves, hares and marmots range the same slopes and plains.

**Mammals at home.** The mammals closest to daily life are the domestic ones: sheep and goats for milk, meat and wool, cattle, the horse and donkey for carrying and field work, and the dog as guard and companion — all of them milk-fed young, all of them hair-bearing.

**Afghanistan's way.** The Grade 8 classification year ends at home — after the whole animal kingdom is classified, the book names the birds and mammals of Afghanistan, the two groups a learner can watch in their own sky and hills.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words: Afghanistan's own birds and mammals are its two warm-blooded vertebrate groups: the birds with feathers and wings, and the mammals with hair that feed their young on milk. Use each key fact once. Then let your companion explain it back to you.
