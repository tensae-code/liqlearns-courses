# Video script — Afghanistan's birds and mammals

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Today: Afghanistan's birds and mammals.

Remember this one thing: Afghanistan's own birds and mammals are its two warm-blooded vertebrate groups: the birds with feathers and wings, and the mammals with hair that feed their young on milk.

Feathers are the bird's own mark — no other animal has them. The forelimbs are wings, the mouth is a beak without teeth, the body stays warm from inside, and the young hatch from hard-shelled eggs the parents usually warm and guard.

Eagles, falcons and vultures ride the mountain air; cranes and water birds visit the rivers and lakes on migration; and around farms and gardens live the hoopoe, sparrows, pigeons and crows — the country's birds are sorted by the same bird marks whatever their size.

Hair and milk are the mammal marks. The young are born alive (in nearly all mammals), are suckled on the mother's milk, breathe air with lungs, and keep a constant warm body temperature.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
