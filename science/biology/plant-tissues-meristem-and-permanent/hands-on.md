# Hands-on — Plant tissues — meristem and permanent tissues

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find a growing shoot tip and an older part of the same stem. Draw both ends: at the tip, small cells in a dome (name it meristem); behind, the finished stem (name it permanent tissue). Write one sentence under the drawing: where on this plant is new cell production happening, and where is it finished?

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — a shoot tip and a root tip marked as the dividing zones, with arrows showing meristem cells becoming the finished tissues of stem and root behind them — dividing tissue in front, permanent tissue behind — and check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
