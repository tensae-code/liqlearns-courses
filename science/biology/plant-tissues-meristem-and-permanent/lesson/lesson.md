## The idea
Plant anatomy begins with its tissues — plant histology. North Macedonia's Biology II opens its plant histology and morphology theme with the two great tissue kinds: meristematic tissue and permanent tissues (Themes 3.1–3.2, printed pp. 180–184). No lesson in this course covered the plant tissue kinds as a set before, so this lesson is built for it.

## The key facts
**Histology starts with one question.** Plant histology is the study of plant tissues. The first sorting is by division: cells that still divide, and cells that have stopped. Everything else about plant tissues hangs on that one difference.

**Meristem is the dividing tissue.** Meristematic tissue (творно — the 'building' tissue) is made of small, thin-walled cells that keep dividing. It sits at the growing points — the tips of shoots and roots, and in the cambium that thickens stems — and it is the source of every other plant cell.

**Permanent tissues are the finished product.** Permanent tissues (трајни) are formed from meristem cells that have stopped dividing and specialised — grown to their final size and shape and taken up a job: covering, filling, supporting, conducting. Most of the plant's body is permanent tissue.

**Growth happens at the fronts.** Because only meristem divides, a plant grows longer at its tips and thicker at its cambium, while the finished parts behind the fronts stay as they are — the reverse of an animal, which grows throughout.

**North Macedonia's way.** North Macedonia's Biology II teaches plant histology as the doorway to plant morphology: meristematic (творно) tissue and permanent (трајни) tissues first (Themes 3.1–3.2, printed pp. 180–184), then the permanent kinds one by one — parenchyma, mechanical tissue, conducting tissue — and only then the organs built from them.
