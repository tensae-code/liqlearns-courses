# Fruit formation and diversity of fruits

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Fruit formation and diversity of fruits. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
After fertilisation the flower's ovary becomes the fruit — the ovary wall forms the pericarp around the seeds — and fruits are diverse: drupes such as apricot, peach and sour cherry, pomes such as apple and pear, and dry multi-seeded fruits. No lesson in this course taught this on its own before, so this lesson is built for it — from Moldova's Ministry of Education biology textbooks, which teach it as a named topic.

[Picture — a flower's ovary changing into a cut-open apricot drupe, an apple pome and a dry multi-seeded fruit, each labelled with its pericarp and seeds]

## The key facts
**3.4 From flower to fruits and seeds; the fruit and diversity of fruits.** Moldova's Grade 8 book (3.4 From flower to fruits and seeds; the fruit and diversity of fruits) teaches: ovary to pericarp, drupes (apricot, peach, sour cherry), pome (apple, pear), dry multi-seeded fruits. (printed p. 30).

**Moldova's way.** Moldova's Grade 8 book follows the flower forward: from flower to fruits and seeds, ovary to pericarp, then the diversity of fruits kind by kind.
