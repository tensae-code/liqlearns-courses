# Hands-on — Fruit formation and diversity of fruits

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: after fertilisation the flower's ovary becomes the fruit — the ovary wall forms the pericarp around the seeds — and fruits are diverse: drupes such as apricot, peach and sour cherry, pomes such as apple and pear, and dry multi-seeded fruits.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a flower's ovary changing into a cut-open apricot drupe, an apple pome and a dry multi-seeded fruit, each labelled with its pericarp and seeds. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
