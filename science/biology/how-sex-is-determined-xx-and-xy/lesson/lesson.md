## Sub lesson 1 — Sex determination

Human and mammal sex is set by the sex chromosomes at fertilisation: eggs all carry X, sperm carry X or Y, so XX zygotes become female and XY male; other systems are named for contrast — both sexes in one individual (hermaphrodites), separate sexes, and haplodiploidy in which males hatch unfertilised and haploid.

## Sub lesson 2 — Added sex-determination systems — birds ZW and temperature dependence. GUIDE-ONLY

The guide teaches two systems absent from the student book: birds, where ZZ is male and ZW is female (the W chromosome doing what the Y does in mammals), and temperature-dependent sex determination — in many turtles cooler incubation produces males and warmth females, while in some crocodiles moderate temperatures produce males and both warm and cool extremes produce females, some species mixing genetic and temperature control.
