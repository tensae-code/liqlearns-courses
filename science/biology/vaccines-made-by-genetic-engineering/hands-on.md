# Hands-on — Vaccines made by genetic engineering

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: genetic engineering makes vaccines by moving a pathogen's antigen gene into a safe microbe, which then manufactures the antigen in bulk — the body learns the invader from that one part, without ever meeting the disease itself.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a yeast cell drawn producing many copies of one spiky antigen piece, the pieces travelling to a syringe labelled vaccine, with a shield forming in the body beyond. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
