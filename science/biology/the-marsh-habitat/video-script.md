Today: The marsh habitat.

Remember this one thing: a marsh is the border habitat between land and water — ground that stays wet and waterlogged — and its plants and animals are adapted to living with their feet in water on dry-land's edge.

The marsh is a terrestrial-aquatic border habitat — ground that stays wet and waterlogged, between open water and dry land.

The marsh's ruling condition is water in the soil: the ground is saturated, so roots and burrows live in wet, air-poor earth.

Marsh plants are adapted to waterlogged ground — able to root, stand and grow where dry-land plants would rot or drown.

Marsh animals use both worlds — feeding, sheltering and breeding across the water-land edge the marsh provides.

Nigeria opens its terrestrial-habitats chapter (Chapter 14, printed p. 204) with the marsh — the border habitat studied first, before forest, grassland and arid lands, through its plant and animal adaptations.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
