# Hands-on — The marsh habitat

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find the wettest edge near you — a ditch bank, a pond margin, a poorly drained corner. Compare its plants with those a few steps up on dry ground. Say aloud, for one plant in the wet place, how it is fitted to waterlogged soil, using the lesson's border idea.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — a marsh scene: waterlogged ground with reeds and sedges, a frog at the water's edge, and birds feeding in the shallows, the dry land rising behind — and check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
