# Hands-on — Excretion and osmosis in plants

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Early in the morning, look for guttation droplets at the tips of grass or garden leaves and note them; then name, for one plant you know, one part it sheds — a leaf, bark, a fruit skin — and say what the plant gets rid of by shedding it.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — a green plant with water droplets beading at a leaf tip at dawn, arrows of water vapour leaving the leaf, and a shaded inset of an old leaf and a piece of bark marked as stored waste the plant will shed — and check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
