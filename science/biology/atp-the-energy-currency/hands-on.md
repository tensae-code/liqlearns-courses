# Hands-on — ATP — the energy currency

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: ATP is the cell's energy currency: its terminal phosphate bond stores energy that is spent by transferring the phosphate onto the working protein itself, changing its shape — and the cell turns over a huge amount of ATP every day, about half a kilogram a minute in hard exercise.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: an ATP molecule drawn as A—P~P~P with the last phosphate group shown jumping onto a pump protein and bending it into a new shape. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
