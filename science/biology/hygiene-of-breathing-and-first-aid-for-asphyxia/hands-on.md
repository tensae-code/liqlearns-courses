# Hands-on — Hygiene of breathing and first aid for asphyxia

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: breathing is kept safe by hygiene of the airways, and when breathing stops in asphyxia — caused by dust, drowning or strangling — first aid and artificial respiration are the response.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a person giving artificial respiration to another person lying down, with the causes of asphyxia — dust, water, a blocked airway — shown as small labelled scenes. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
