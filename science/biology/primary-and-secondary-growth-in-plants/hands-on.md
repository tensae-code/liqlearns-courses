# Hands-on — Primary and secondary growth in plants

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Take the lesson's key facts for a walk: find one example of this lesson's idea in your own surroundings — your home, street, market or school — and say which key fact it matches, and why.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory — a young tree cut open as a diagram — at the shoot tip and root tip, magnified circles showing the three zones of division, elongation and differentiation, with ink marks spacing out along the root — and in the trunk a cross-section of rings: pale secondary xylem building the wood year by year, the cambium ring, secondary phloem, bark outside with a lenticel pore — label its parts, and check against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
