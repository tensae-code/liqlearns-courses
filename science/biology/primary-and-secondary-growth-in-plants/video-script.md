Today: Primary and secondary growth in plants.

Remember this one thing: A plant grows longer at its tips and thicker at its sides, by two different growths: Kenya's Form 3 book teaches primary growth (root and shoot apical meristems; zones of division, elongation and differentiation; an Indian-ink root-marking practical) and secondary growth (vascular and cork cambium, secondary xylem and phloem, bark, lenticels, annual rings and ageing a tree).

Length first. At the very tip of root and shoot sits the apical meristem, and behind it the plant is organised in zones — division, where cells multiply; elongation, where they stretch; differentiation, where they become their final kinds. Kenya proves the zones with ink: marks painted along a root spread apart only where the root is truly growing, and the percentage growth is calculated from them. Thickness comes later and sideways: the vascular cambium lays down secondary xylem inwards — the wood, ring by ring, so a tree can be aged by counting — and secondary phloem outwards, while the cork cambium seals the surface into bark, breathing through lenticels.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
