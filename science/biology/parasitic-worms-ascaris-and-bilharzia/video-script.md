Today: Parasitic worms — Ascaris and bilharzia.

Remember this one thing: Two worms, two roads into the body: Kenya's Form 3 book teaches Ascaris lumbricoides — swallowed, migrating through liver, heart and lungs — and schistosomiasis (bilharzia), whose cercariae leave Biomphalaria and Bulinus snails to pierce the skin — each with its effects on the host, its adaptive characteristics, and its prevention.

Ascaris comes in by the mouth: its eggs are swallowed, and the larva does not stay in the gut — it migrates, through liver, heart and lungs, before settling as an adult in the intestine, and Kenya lists the six ways it is fitted to that life. Bilharzia comes through the skin: Schistosoma's cercariae swim out of Biomphalaria and Bulinus snails and pierce the bather, and its adaptations are a parasite's toolkit — suckers, two hosts, lytic glands, the female held in the male's gynacophoric canal. Prevention meets each on its own road: latrines and hygiene against Ascaris; latrines, molluscicides and protective clothing against bilharzia.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
