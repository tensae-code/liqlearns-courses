# Hands-on — Parasitic worms — Ascaris and bilharzia

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Take the lesson's key facts for a walk: find one example of this lesson's idea in your own surroundings — your home, street, market or school — and say which key fact it matches, and why.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory — two life-road maps side by side — on one side the roundworm Ascaris: eggs swallowed from contaminated soil, a larva's dotted journey through liver, heart and lungs, and the adult worm coiled in the intestine — on the other side the bilharzia fluke: eggs to water, cercariae streaming from a Biomphalaria snail, piercing a bather's skin, and the paired adults settled in their host — label its parts, and check against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
