# Bacterial cells — pili, flagella and the three domains

## Do it first
Before you read: think of one living thing near your home that shows this lesson's idea — bacterial cells. Hold it in your mind, then read on and check your idea against what the lesson teaches.

Pili (short, hair-like, mainly for attachment to surfaces and so a virulence factor in infection) are distinguished from flagella (long, thin, extending from inside the cell body, for movement); a full prokaryote-vs-eukaryote difference table and a three-domain table (Bacteria / Archaea / Eukarya across nucleus, histones, chromosomes, plasmids, introns, mitochondria, chloroplasts and membrane lipids) consolidate the classification; true sexual reproduction is absent in bacteria, with genetic recombination by conjugation, transduction and transformation instead.
