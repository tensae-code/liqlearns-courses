# Hands-on — Linked inheritance and crossing over

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: genes on the same chromosome are linked and are inherited together more often than Mendel's independent assortment predicts — a Punnett square for BbMm × bbmm shows the expected ratios, and linkage is what the real offspring do instead.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: two chromosomes drawn side by side carrying the gene pairs B/b and M/m together, beside a Punnett square for the cross BbMm × bbmm. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
