# Hands-on — Photosynthesis in prokaryotes

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: photosynthesis began in prokaryotes: cyanobacteria run it much as plants do and filled the air with oxygen, while other bacteria photosynthesise without releasing oxygen, using sulphur compounds instead of water — the process is older than the chloroplast, which is itself a captured prokaryote.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a cyanobacterium chain in pond water releasing oxygen bubbles toward the sun, beside a purple sulphur bacterium depositing yellow sulphur grains. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
