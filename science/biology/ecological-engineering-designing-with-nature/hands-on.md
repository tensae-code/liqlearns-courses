# Hands-on — Ecological engineering — designing with nature

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: ecological engineering designs or repairs ecosystems on ecological principles — self-generation, cycling, coordination and wholeness — for less consumption, more benefit and sustainability, as the classical mulberry–silkworm–fishpond and modern village biogas, lake-restoration and mining-land systems show.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: the mulberry–silkworm–fishpond cycle drawn as a loop — mulberry leaves feed silkworms, silkworm waste feeds fish, pond mud feeds the mulberry trees. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
