# Hands-on — Resistance — evolution in action

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Take the lesson's key facts for a walk: find one example of this lesson's idea in your own surroundings — your home, street, market or school — and say which key fact it matches, and why.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory — a spray can and a medicine bottle aimed at three survivors drawn larger than their fallen fellows — a mosquito still flying through faded DDT mist, a bacterium splitting a penicillin ring with an enzyme drawn as scissors, and a malaria parasite changing its coat like a cloak — with a small timeline beneath showing the change taking years, not ages — label its parts, and check against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
