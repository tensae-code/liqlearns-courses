# The four levels of protein structure

## Do it first
Before you read: think of one living thing near your home that shows this lesson's idea — the four levels of protein structure. Hold it in your mind, then read on and check your idea against what the lesson teaches.

Because enzymes are proteins, their building is taught in four levels: primary (the amino-acid sequence in the chain), secondary (folding into α-helix and β-sheet by hydrogen bonding), tertiary (the full three-dimensional shape held by side-chain interactions) and quaternary (several folded subunits packed together, as in haemoglobin); pupils draw all four as a wall poster.
