# Animal tissues — epithelial, connective, muscle and nervous — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. What is the one thing this lesson teaches?**
a) an animal tissue is cells plus the intercellular matrix and fibres between them, and animal tissues fall into four kinds — epithelial, connective, muscle and nervous — each built for its job  b) That this topic has no rules and every case is different  c) A list of names with no process behind them  d) That the topic belongs only to scientists, not to daily life
**Answer: a) an animal tissue is cells plus the intercellular matrix and fibres between them, and animal tissues fall into four kinds — epithelial, connective, muscle and nervous — each built for its job**

**2. What a tissue is made of — which statement is right?**
a) Kuwait's new Grade 10 student book (Lesson 2-1, printed pp. 89–92) defines a tissue by its parts: cells plus intercellular matrix plus fibres — thick collagen for tensile strength, elastin, and reticular fibres. Learners build a model tissue from slime, buttons, elastic bands and glue.  b) The opposite of what the lesson teaches  c) It happens only in examinations  d) It is never observed in real life
**Answer: a) Kuwait's new Grade 10 student book (Lesson 2-1, printed pp. 89–92) defines a tissue by its parts: cells plus intercellular matrix plus fibres — thick collagen for tensile strength, elastin, and reticular fibres. Learners build a model tissue from slime, buttons, elastic bands and glue.**

**3. Which statement about this lesson's topic is right?**
a) It is unrelated to the lesson's topic  b) Epithelial tissue (printed pp. 93–94) is tightly packed covering and lining tissue; the book's worked example is stratified squamous epithelium, the skin's friction-protecting outer layer.  c) It is a myth the book corrects  d) It applies only outside Kuwait
**Answer: b) Epithelial tissue (printed pp. 93–94) is tightly packed covering and lining tissue; the book's worked example is stratified squamous epithelium, the skin's friction-protecting outer layer.**
