# Hands-on — Animal tissues — epithelial, connective, muscle and nervous

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: an animal tissue is cells plus the intercellular matrix and fibres between them, and animal tissues fall into four kinds — epithelial, connective, muscle and nervous — each built for its job.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: the four animal tissue kinds drawn in a row and labelled: a tightly packed epithelial sheet, connective tissue with cells scattered in matrix and fibres, striated muscle fibres, and a neuron with its fibres. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
