# Lesson plan — Soil — properties and fertility

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Biology · **Lesson:** Soil — properties and fertility
**Place in the curriculum:** Biology — appended to Unit 16 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** soil is a mixture whose layers can be separated and measured — shake soil with water in a bottle and the parts settle into layers whose heights you can read — and soil has chemical properties as well as physical ones: Uganda's Senior Two home-study booklet teaches soil as its own topic, Lesson 1 (PDF pp. 7–10)
**This lesson's own level:** it opens Uganda's soil topic: the Senior Two home-study booklet's first topic is Soil, and its Lesson 1 is the physical and chemical properties of soil (PDF pp. 7–10); the booklet's lessons on losing and conserving soil fertility are stocked in the soil-conservation lesson. How we know something is alive is not needed first; this lesson stands alone, and is never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words, the one thing this lesson teaches: soil is a mixture of parts that can be separated and measured, and it has chemical properties as well as physical ones.
2. Describe the bottle sedimentation experiment: shake soil with water, let it settle, and measure the height of each layer (H3).
3. Say what the settled layers show about what a soil is made of.
4. Explain Uganda's way as the placements describe it: in the lockdown home-study booklet, soil is a whole topic worked alone at home with household materials, and its first lesson is soil's properties (PDF pp. 7–10).
