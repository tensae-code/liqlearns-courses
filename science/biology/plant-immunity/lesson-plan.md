# Lesson plan — Plant immunity

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Biology · **Lesson:** Plant immunity
**Place in the curriculum:** Biology — appended to Unit 16 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** plants defend themselves against pathogens in two ways — structural immunity (cutinised epidermis, hairs/thorns, thickened cell walls; induced cork formation, tyloses blocking xylem, gum deposition in Acacia nilotica, hypersensitive death of injured tissue) and biochemical immunity (pathogen receptors, phenols and glycosides, non-protein amino acids like canavanine, antimicrobial proteins and detoxifying enzymes, post-infection strengthening) — and Egypt's Grade 12 book teaches immunity in plants first, before immunity in man (Egypt, Grade 12 — *BIOLOGY*, student textbook, Ch. 4, printed pp. 85–88).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands beside the course's immunity lessons for man and adds the half they do not teach: how a plant, with no blood and no antibodies, defends itself. The immune system in man is recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: **The immune system — born with one, acquire the other**. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words, the one thing this lesson teaches: a plant defends itself structurally and biochemically, and Egypt's book teaches plant immunity before immunity in man.
2. Name the structural barriers already there: cutinised epidermis, hairs and thorns, thickened cell walls.
3. Name the structural responses after attack: induced cork formation, tyloses blocking xylem, gum deposition in Acacia nilotica, and hypersensitive death of injured tissue.
4. Name the biochemical defences: pathogen receptors, phenols and glycosides, non-protein amino acids like canavanine, antimicrobial proteins and detoxifying enzymes, and post-infection strengthening.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck
- Go back to the one thing this lesson teaches, and re-read the key facts slowly, one at a time.
- Use the picture: for each defence, say out loud whether the plant is blocking the pathogen with its structure or fighting it with its chemistry.
