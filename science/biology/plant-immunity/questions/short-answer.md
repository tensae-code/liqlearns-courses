# Plant immunity — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** Explain: structural immunity against biochemical immunity in a plant.
**Answer: Structural immunity is defence by structure: barriers already there — a cutinised epidermis, hairs and thorns, thickened cell walls — and responses built after attack — induced cork formation, tyloses blocking the xylem, gum deposition (as in Acacia nilotica) and hypersensitive death of injured tissue. Biochemical immunity is defence by chemistry: pathogen receptors that detect the attacker, phenols and glycosides, non-protein amino acids like canavanine, antimicrobial proteins and detoxifying enzymes, strengthened after infection.**

**2.** Explain: Egypt's way of teaching immunity.
**Answer: Egypt's Grade 12 Biology book (BIOLOGY, Third Secondary, Term 1) teaches immunity twice in Chapter 4: first Immunity in Plant — structural immunity and biochemical immunity (printed pp. 85–88) — and only afterwards the immune system in man, with its lines of defence and antibody mechanisms.**
