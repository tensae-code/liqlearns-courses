# Hands-on — Plant immunity

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find a real leaf and a real stem or twig. Point to the surface — the cutinised epidermis — and to any hairs or thorns, and say for each one: this is structural immunity, a barrier already there. Then say what the plant would add after an attack: cork, tyloses in the xylem, gum, or the hypersensitive death of the injured tissue.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — a stem in cross-section with a tylose blocking a xylem vessel, gum on the bark, and the cutinised epidermis labelled — and check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed. Under the drawing, write the biochemical defences in a list: receptors, phenols and glycosides, canavanine, antimicrobial proteins, detoxifying enzymes.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap. End with this one together: why does Egypt's book teach immunity in plants before immunity in man? Say what the plant half shows that the human half does not.
