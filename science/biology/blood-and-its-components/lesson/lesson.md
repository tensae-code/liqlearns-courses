# Blood and its components

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Blood and its components. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Afghanistan's Grade 9 Biology book opens its circulation chapter with blood and the components of blood, before it reaches the heart. This lesson teaches blood on its own: the plasma that carries everything, and the three kinds of blood cell, each fitted to its work.

## The key facts
**Plasma — the carrier.** A little over half of blood is plasma: water with dissolved sugars, amino acids, salts, hormones, vitamins and wastes like urea riding in it. Everything the blood delivers dissolved travels in the plasma; everything solid in blood is a cell or a cell fragment.

**Red cells — the oxygen carriers.** Red cells are the most numerous blood cells. Their haemoglobin takes up oxygen in the lungs and releases it in the tissues — the cell's small biconcave disc shape and missing nucleus leave maximum room and surface for that carrying work.

**White cells — the defenders.** White cells are fewer, larger and keep their nuclei. Some patrol the blood and swallow germs whole; others make antibodies that mark germs for destruction. A rising white-cell count in illness is the sign of this defence at work.

**Platelets — the clotters.** Where a vessel is cut, platelets gather and help turn dissolved fibrinogen into fibrin threads that net the red cells into a clot. The clot is a plug and a barrier at once: blood stays in, germs stay out, while the skin repairs beneath.

**One tissue, four jobs.** Plasma and red cells run transport (food, gases, hormones, waste, heat); white cells run defence; platelets run repair. Blood also evens out the body's temperature and acidity — one liquid tissue doing the work of several systems at once.

**Afghanistan's way.** The Grade 9 circulation chapter starts from the blood itself — its components first, the heart and lymph after — so the transport system is taught from the tissue that does the transporting.
