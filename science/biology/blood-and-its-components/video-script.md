Today: Blood and its components.

Remember this one thing: Blood is a liquid tissue: red cells, white cells and platelets carried in a watery plasma — each component with its own job in transport and defence.

A little over half of blood is plasma: water with dissolved sugars, amino acids, salts, hormones, vitamins and wastes like urea riding in it. Everything the blood delivers dissolved travels in the plasma; everything solid in blood is a cell or a cell fragment.

Red cells are the most numerous blood cells. Their haemoglobin takes up oxygen in the lungs and releases it in the tissues — the cell's small biconcave disc shape and missing nucleus leave maximum room and surface for that carrying work.

White cells are fewer, larger and keep their nuclei. Some patrol the blood and swallow germs whole; others make antibodies that mark germs for destruction. A rising white-cell count in illness is the sign of this defence at work.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
