Today: Effects of agricultural activities on ecosystems.

Remember this one thing: farming changes the ecosystem it works in: clearing land, the farming systems practised on it and bush burning each alter the soil, the water and the living community — Nigeria teaches the effects practice by practice and crop by crop.

Every agricultural activity — clearing, cultivating, burning — changes the ecosystem: its soil, its water and the community of organisms living there.

Clearing removes the plant cover that shaded and held the soil, exposing the land to sun, rain impact and erosion before any crop is planted.

The farming systems practised on cleared land work it differently, so their effects on the ecosystem differ — the book treats them practice by practice.

Bush burning is singled out: fire destroys vegetation and soil organisms, leaves ash, and exposes the soil — an agricultural practice with ecosystem-wide effects.

Nigeria teaches the effects of agricultural activities on ecological systems inside its agriculture chapter (Chapter 9, printed p. 139) — clearing, farming systems and bush burning, crop by crop and practice by practice.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
