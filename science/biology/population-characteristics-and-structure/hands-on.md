# Hands-on — Population characteristics and structure

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: a population — living matter organised at its own level — is described by its characteristics, including its structure, and its sex structure is the ratio of females to males in it.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a population of animals in a field with its characteristics listed beside it — numbers, and the sex structure shown as a ratio of females to males. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
