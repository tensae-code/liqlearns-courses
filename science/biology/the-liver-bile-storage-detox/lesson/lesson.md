## The idea

## The key facts
**Organ order.** The digestive organ order is mouth, oesophagus, stomach, small intestine, large intestine, rectum, anus, with accessory organs pancreas, gall bladder and liver.

**Bile.** The liver continually produces bile, which emulsifies large fat globules.

**Storage and detox.** The liver stores glycogen and vitamins A, B12 and D, and detoxifies harmful substances.

**Two supplies.** The liver uniquely receives two blood supplies — hepatic arteries bringing oxygenated blood and the portal vein bringing venous blood from the gut.

## From the student book — the pupil-taught core
The student book places the liver among the accessory organs: bile (stored in the gall bladder) emulsifies fat into small droplets, and pancreatic juice completes chemical digestion — with the liver's many jobs collected in discussion.

### Student-book key facts
**Accessory organs.** The liver, gall bladder and pancreas pour their juices into the canal as accessory organs.

**Bile emulsifies.** Bile from the liver, stored in the gall bladder, emulsifies fat.

**Pancreatic juice.** Pancreatic juice completes chemical digestion in the small intestine.

**Two blood supplies.** Pupils research why the liver is unusual in receiving two blood supplies.
