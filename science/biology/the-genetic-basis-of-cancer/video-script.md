Today: The genetic basis of cancer.

Remember this one thing: cancer begins in the genes that control division — change the controllers, and the division escapes control.

Every cell carries genes whose job is control: divide now, stop now. Those instructions are carried inside the cell by signals, passed from protein to protein — signal transduction. As long as the control genes are intact, the signals stay true.

Now mutate a control gene. The gene is expressed as a protein, and the protein comes out defective. A defective protein garbles the signal it carries. And it is never just one: Italy's textbook stresses that many gene alterations contribute, each adding its damage, until the cell's division no longer obeys the stop signals.

That is why the book teaches cancer at the end of the gene unit: first genes, then proteins, then mutations — and then the disease those three explain together.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
