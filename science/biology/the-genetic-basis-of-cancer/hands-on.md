# Hands-on — The genetic basis of cancer

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Play “broken telephone” with a companion: whisper a two-part instruction — “divide once, then stop” — through two or three people, letting one person garble a word on purpose. Say aloud how the garbled message is like a defective protein in signal transduction, and how several garbles (many alterations) make the wrong outcome more certain.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — the division control panel with a mutation-broken switch and a garbled signal wire — and label them “gene controlling division”, “mutation”, “defective protein” and “signal transduction”. Check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
