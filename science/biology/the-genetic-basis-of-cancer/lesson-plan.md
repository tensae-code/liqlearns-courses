# Lesson plan — The genetic basis of cancer

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Biology · **Lesson:** The genetic basis of cancer
**Place in the curriculum:** Biology (CURRICULUM.md Unit 16).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Cancer has a genetic basis: it depends on mutations in the genes that control cell division, many gene alterations contribute to it, and the defective proteins made from those genes interfere with the signals that tell a cell when to divide — Italy's Grade 11 Campbell closes its gene unit with that run (printed pp. 48–50).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on it stands on the earlier genetics lessons — protein synthesis, and mutations — and shows what those two together explain: how a disease of division is written in genes.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier biology lessons on protein synthesis — from gene to protein, and on mutations. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what cancer depends on, genetically: mutations in the genes that control cell division.
2. Say why it is not a one-gene story: many gene alterations contribute, and their defective proteins interfere with signal transduction — the signals that control division.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson itself.

## What you need
Paper and pencil; the learner's own surroundings for examples.
