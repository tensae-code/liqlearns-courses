# Adaptation of organisms to their environment

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Adaptation of organisms to their environment. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Nepal's Grade 4 My Science, Health and Physical Education book teaches land animals through their characteristics: not a list of animals, but a matching — each animal's features read against the place it has to survive in.

In your mind's eye: an adaptation is a feature that fits a living thing to the place it lives — land animals carry, in their bodies and behaviour, the answers to their habitat's problems of water, temperature, food and enemies. — picture it happening step by step, in order, and say each step as you see it.

## The key facts
**Features fit places.** A terrestrial animal's body answers its home: thick fur where it is cold, a water-storing body where it is dry, strong legs where it must run, claws or a burrowing shape where it must dig. The feature and the place explain each other.

**Behaviour adapts too.** Adaptation is not only in the body. Sleeping through the cold season, hunting at night in the desert's heat, migrating when food fails — what an animal does can fit it to its environment as surely as what it is made of.

**Read the animal, read the home.** Given an unfamiliar animal, its features are clues to its home: webbed feet speak of water, a hump of the desert, white fur of snow. Adaptation is the name for that fit between organism and environment, however it is achieved.
