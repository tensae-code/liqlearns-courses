# Dihybrid crosses, independent assortment and test crosses — short answer

Answer in your own words, in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) In your own words, what does this lesson teach about dihybrid cross and independent assortment; test cross?**
**Answer: Crossing two traits together (yellow-round × green-wrinkled peas) shows the alleles of different genes sorting independently at metaphase I of meiosis — the law of independent assortment, every combination equally likely; a test cross with a homozygous-recessive partner reads an unknown genotype from its offspring (all dominant-looking offspring means homozygous dominant; any recessive offspring means heterozygous).**

**2. (Sub lesson 2) In your own words, what does this lesson teach about added backcross worked examples?**
**Answer: Two worked crosses beyond the student book's test cross: an F1 heterozygote (Cc) crossed back to the homozygous-dominant red parent (CC) gives 100% red offspring, while the same F1 crossed to the pure recessive white parent (cc) gives 50% red and 50% white — a 1:1 phenotypic and genotypic ratio, each shown as a gamete grid; a black-dog test cross is also worked to a heterozygous-dominant answer.**
