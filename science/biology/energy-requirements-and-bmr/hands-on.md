# Hands-on — Energy requirements and BMR

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Take the lesson's key facts for a walk: find one example of this lesson's idea in your own surroundings — your home, street, market or school — and say which key fact it matches, and why.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory — two Kenyans side by side — a labourer pulling a loaded cart and an office worker sitting at a desk — each with an energy bar beside them, the labourer's bar far taller, and beneath both a small resting figure marked as the basal rate, the energy a body uses doing nothing at all — label its parts, and check against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
