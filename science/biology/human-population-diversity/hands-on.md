# Hands-on — Human population diversity

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: human populations vary — in skin colour, blood groups, body build and a thousand inherited traits — but the variation runs within one single species and mostly inside every population, so biology finds no separate human races, only one variable kind.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a row of people from many regions with a gentle gradient of skin tones and a chart beneath showing most variation arrows inside each group rather than between groups. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
