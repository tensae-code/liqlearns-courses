# Hands-on — Ecosystem stability — resistance and resilience

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: an ecosystem in balance holds three balances — structure, function and input–output — and its stability comes in two kinds: resistance stability, withstanding a disturbance, and resilience stability, recovering from one, rainforest and Arctic tundra standing at the two ends.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: two panels — a rainforest barely dented by a storm, and a burnt grassland regrowing shoot by shoot the next season. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
