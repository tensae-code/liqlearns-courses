# Ecosystem stability — resistance and resilience

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Ecosystem stability — resistance and resilience. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
An ecosystem in balance holds three balances — structure, function and input–output — and its stability comes in two kinds: resistance stability, withstanding a disturbance, and resilience stability, recovering from one, rainforest and Arctic tundra standing at the two ends. No lesson in this course taught this on its own before, so this lesson is built for it — from China's People's Education Press biology textbooks, which teach it as a named topic.

[Picture — two panels — a rainforest barely dented by a storm, and a burnt grassland regrowing shoot by shoot the next season]

## The key facts
**Ch. 3 §5 Ecological balance and stability.** balance defined by three balances (structure, function, input–output); resistance stability against resilience stability, rainforest against Arctic tundra; raising stability by limiting use to the self-regulation range (closing hillsides to let them regreen) and by building "ecological barriers" — the Three-North Shelterbelt; the eucalyptus pure-plantation problem case, where undergrowth species fall as planting density rises from 750 to 1,000 stems per hectare.
