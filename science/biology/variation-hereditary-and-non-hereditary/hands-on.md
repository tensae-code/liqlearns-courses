# Hands-on — Variation — hereditary and non-hereditary

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: organisms vary in two ways: hereditary variation, carried in their genes and passed to their offspring, and non-hereditary — modification — variation, shaped by living conditions and not inherited; telling the two apart is the first step of genetics.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: two plants of one kind side by side: one differing from its parent by an inherited mark passed to its seedlings, the other grown larger only by rich soil, its seedlings drawn ordinary. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
