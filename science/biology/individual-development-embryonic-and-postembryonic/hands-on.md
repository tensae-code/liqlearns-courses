# Hands-on — Individual development — embryonic and post-embryonic

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: every organism has an individual development — its whole life built in order from the fertilised egg: first the embryonic period, from the fertilised egg to birth or hatching, then the post-embryonic period, from birth or hatching through growth and change to the adult.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a two-part timeline of one life: an egg and forming embryo labelled 'embryonic period', then a hatched young creature growing into an adult labelled 'post-embryonic period'. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
