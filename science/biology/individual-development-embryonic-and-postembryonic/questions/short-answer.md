# Individual development — embryonic and post-embryonic — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** State the one thing this lesson teaches and explain it.
**Answer: every organism has an individual development — its whole life built in order from the fertilised egg: first the embryonic period, from the fertilised egg to birth or hatching, then the post-embryonic period, from birth or hatching through growth and change to the adult. Individual development — ontogenesis — is the whole ordered building of one organism across its life: it begins with the fertilised egg and runs until the adult, and it is a basic property of organisms, taught in the book beside reproduction itself.**

**2.** Explain Bulgaria's way of teaching this.
**Answer: Bulgaria's Pedagog 6 Grade 10 book keeps the general topic general first: individual development of organisms (Topic 22, printed p. 65), its two periods next (Topics 23–24, printed p. 67), and only then the individual development of humans (Topics 25–26) — the human case as one case of the general rule, not the whole topic.**
