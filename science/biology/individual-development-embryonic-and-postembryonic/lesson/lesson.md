# Individual development — embryonic and post-embryonic

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Individual development — embryonic and post-embryonic. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Bulgaria's Grade 10 book teaches individual development as a general topic of its own (Topic 22, printed p. 65) inside its reproduction-and-development section, and then gives the two periods their own topics (Topics 23–24, printed p. 67): the embryonic period and the post-embryonic period — the whole life of one organism, built in order, before the book narrows to the individual development of humans (Topics 25–26).

## The key facts
**Individual development.** Individual development — ontogenesis — is the whole ordered building of one organism across its life: it begins with the fertilised egg and runs until the adult, and it is a basic property of organisms, taught in the book beside reproduction itself.

**The embryonic period.** The embryonic period runs from the fertilised egg to birth or hatching. Through it the egg divides and the embryo is built — the body plan laid down inside the egg, the womb or the seed, protected and fed while it forms.

**The post-embryonic period.** The post-embryonic period runs from birth or hatching to the adult: growth, and in many organisms a change of form along the way, until the organism can reproduce itself and the cycle can begin again.

**Bulgaria's way.** Bulgaria's Pedagog 6 Grade 10 book keeps the general topic general first: individual development of organisms (Topic 22, printed p. 65), its two periods next (Topics 23–24, printed p. 67), and only then the individual development of humans (Topics 25–26) — the human case as one case of the general rule, not the whole topic.
