# Hands-on — Pesticides and the environment

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: pesticides protect crops, but they persist in the environment and concentrate as they pass through food chains — bioaccumulation — harming wildlife and human health far beyond the field that was sprayed.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a sprayed field with a food chain rising from it — plant, insect, small bird, hawk — the pesticide dots growing denser at each step until the hawk carries the heaviest load. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
