Today: Embryonic development — segmentation, gastrulation and organogenesis.

Remember this one thing: development runs continuously from the egg through three phases — segmentation (cleavage divisions), gastrulation (the cell territories move into place and the germ layers form, the mesoderm arising from the primitive gut) and organogenesis (the organs are built) — and it starts from an egg with two poles, animal and vegetative, classified by how much yolk it carries.

An animal's life begins as one egg, with two poles and its store of yolk. Then development runs in three phases, without stopping. First, segmentation: the egg divides and divides. Second, gastrulation: the cells move into place and form the germ layers. Third, organogenesis: the organs are built — the outer layer alone gives the nervous system, the sense organs and the skin.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
