# Compensation point

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Compensation point. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Inside its leaf chapter, Malaysia's Form 5 book names a precise balance point (printed p. 52): the compensation point. A plant is always respiring and, in light, photosynthesising; the compensation point is where the two rates are equal.

[Picture — a graph of carbon dioxide exchange against light intensity, the curve crossing zero at a marked point labelled compensation point, with photosynthesis and respiration arrows shown equal there]

## The key facts
**Two opposite flows.** Photosynthesis takes in carbon dioxide and builds food; respiration releases carbon dioxide and burns food. Both run in a living leaf — respiration day and night, photosynthesis only in light.

**The balance point.** As light grows stronger, photosynthesis speeds up. At one light intensity the rate of photosynthesis exactly equals the rate of respiration: that intensity is the compensation point.

**No net exchange.** At the compensation point the carbon dioxide taken in equals the carbon dioxide given out — measured from outside, the leaf exchanges nothing, and the plant neither gains nor loses food.

**Above and below.** Above the compensation point photosynthesis wins and the plant gains food and grows; below it respiration wins and the plant lives on its stores.

**Malaysia's way.** Malaysia's KSSM Form 5 book gives the compensation point its own place in the leaf chapter (Ch.2, printed p. 52), after photosynthesis — the leaf's two gas flows set against each other and the balance named.
