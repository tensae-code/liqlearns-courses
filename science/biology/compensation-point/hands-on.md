# Hands-on — Compensation point

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: the compensation point is the light intensity at which a plant's photosynthesis exactly balances its respiration — the plant makes as much food as it burns, so there is no net gain and no net loss.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a graph of carbon dioxide exchange against light intensity, the curve crossing zero at a marked point labelled compensation point, with photosynthesis and respiration arrows shown equal there. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
