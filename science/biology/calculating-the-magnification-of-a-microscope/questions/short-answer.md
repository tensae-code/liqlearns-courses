# Calculating the magnification of a microscope — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** Explain: two lenses multiply.
**Answer: A light microscope magnifies twice: the objective lens, close to the specimen, magnifies first, and the eyepiece lens magnifies that image again. Because the magnifications stack, the total is found by multiplying, not adding: total magnification = eyepiece magnification × objective magnification.**

**2.** Explain: worked example.
**Answer: With a ×10 eyepiece and a ×40 objective, the total magnification is 10 × 40 = ×400: the image is four hundred times bigger than the specimen. Change to the ×10 objective and the same eyepiece gives 10 × 10 = ×100. Each objective on the nosepiece gives its own total with the same eyepiece.**

**3.** Explain: magnification of a cell.
**Answer: The same multiplication tells you a real size: if a cell's image measures 20 mm across at ×400, the cell itself is 20 ÷ 400 = 0.05 mm across. Magnification is the bridge between the size you see and the size that is really there.**
