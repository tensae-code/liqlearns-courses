# Calculating the magnification of a microscope

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Calculating the magnification of a microscope. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Kenya's Grade 8 Integrated Science book teaches the microscope by using it — preparing slides, observing cells — and then by calculating: how many times bigger is the image than the real thing?

In your mind's eye: the total magnification of a microscope is the eyepiece magnification multiplied by the objective magnification — eyepiece × objective. — picture it happening step by step, in order, and say each step as you see it.

## The key facts
**Two lenses multiply.** A light microscope magnifies twice: the objective lens, close to the specimen, magnifies first, and the eyepiece lens magnifies that image again. Because the magnifications stack, the total is found by multiplying, not adding: total magnification = eyepiece magnification × objective magnification.

**Worked example.** With a ×10 eyepiece and a ×40 objective, the total magnification is 10 × 40 = ×400: the image is four hundred times bigger than the specimen. Change to the ×10 objective and the same eyepiece gives 10 × 10 = ×100. Each objective on the nosepiece gives its own total with the same eyepiece.

**Magnification of a cell.** The same multiplication tells you a real size: if a cell's image measures 20 mm across at ×400, the cell itself is 20 ÷ 400 = 0.05 mm across. Magnification is the bridge between the size you see and the size that is really there.
