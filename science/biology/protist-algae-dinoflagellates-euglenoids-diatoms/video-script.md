Today: Protist algae — dinoflagellates, euglenoids and diatoms.

Remember this one thing: the protist algae are sorted into phyla by their pigments, walls and tails: the Euglenophyta; the Chrysophyta, silica-walled algae of the marine plankton (the diatoms); and the Pyrrophyta — the dinoflagellates, with two flagella and a bioluminescence that sparkles on night wave-crests — and together the algae are the food base on which aquatic heterotrophs live.

Some protists live like plants — the protist algae. The euglenoids swim with a flagellum. The chrysophytes wear walls of silica, like glass, and drift in the sea's plankton. The dinoflagellates have two flagella — and they glow: their light sparkles on the wave-crests at night. Small as they are, the algae feed the waters: they are the food base of everything that cannot photosynthesise.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
