# Hands-on — Protist algae — dinoflagellates, euglenoids and diatoms

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
On a dark evening, think of the sea in the picture: the sparks on the wave-crests are living light from dinoflagellates. Now name the three phyla aloud with one mark for each — euglenoids and their flagellum, chrysophytes and their silica walls, dinoflagellates and their two flagella and light.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory — three protist algae drawn large side by side: an euglena with its single long flagellum, a diatom in its glassy silica box, and a dinoflagellate with its two flagella — above a night sea whose wave-crests sparkle with tiny lights — label its parts, and check against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
