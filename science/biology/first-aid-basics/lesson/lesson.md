# First aid basics

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — First aid basics. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Fiji's Year 11 Biology book closes its Social Biology chapter — human reproduction, family planning, personal health, disease and the immune system — with first aid: the biology learner is taught to be the first help when something goes wrong, before any doctor or nurse can arrive.

## The key facts
**First help, given at once.** First aid is the help given first, on the spot, to a person who is injured or suddenly ill. Its work is to keep the person alive and stop the harm growing worse until medical help takes over — it does not replace the doctor; it bridges the time before the doctor.

**Safety and air first.** The first-aider first makes the place safe — for the casualty and for themselves — then checks the person: are they conscious, is the airway open, are they breathing? A blocked airway or stopped breathing is dealt with before anything else, because the body can go only minutes without air.

**Stop the bleeding, keep still and warm.** Bleeding is pressed on directly to slow the blood loss. A person who may have broken bones or a hurt back is kept still, not moved about. The casualty is kept warm and watched — breathing, bleeding and consciousness checked again and again — until help arrives.

**Fiji's way.** First aid stands in the book as §8.5, the closing section of the Social Biology chapter — Fiji teaches it as part of biology itself, the last thing the chapter's health teaching asks of the learner: not only to know the body, but to act for it in the first minutes.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words: First aid is the first help given to a person who is injured or suddenly taken ill, before medical help takes over: keep the person and yourself safe, keep the airway open and breathing going, stop bleeding, keep the person still and warm, and send for help. Use each key fact once. Then let your companion explain it back to you.
