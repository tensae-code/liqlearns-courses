Today: First aid basics.

Remember this one thing: First aid is the first help given to a person who is injured or suddenly taken ill, before medical help takes over: keep the person and yourself safe, keep the airway open and breathing going, stop bleeding, keep the person still and warm, and send for help.

First aid is the help given first, on the spot, to a person who is injured or suddenly ill. Its work is to keep the person alive and stop the harm growing worse until medical help takes over — it does not replace the doctor; it bridges the time before the doctor.

The first-aider first makes the place safe — for the casualty and for themselves — then checks the person: are they conscious, is the airway open, are they breathing? A blocked airway or stopped breathing is dealt with before anything else, because the body can go only minutes without air.

Bleeding is pressed on directly to slow the blood loss. A person who may have broken bones or a hurt back is kept still, not moved about. The casualty is kept warm and watched — breathing, bleeding and consciousness checked again and again — until help arrives.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
