# Improving crop yield

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Improving crop yield. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Chapter 9 of Nigeria's Senior Secondary Biology 1 asks the practical question: how do we get more from the land? Its answers run from the soil outward. Conserve the soil — terracing, mulching, rotation, and no bush burning. Feed it — fertilisers and manure. Farm it better — sound cultural practices. Breed better plants — disease-resistant cassava and budded oranges are the book's improved varieties. And, simplest of all, put more land under cultivation.

[Picture — a terraced hillside with mulched beds, a farmer adding manure, and two labelled plants: disease-resistant cassava and a budded orange tree]

## The key facts
**Conserve the soil.** Soil conservation heads the list: terracing sloping land, mulching the surface, rotating crops, and not burning the bush — keeping the soil there and fertile.

**Feed the soil.** Fertilisers and manure return to the soil what crops take out, raising the yield the land can support.

**Farm it better.** Cultural practices — how the crop is planted, spaced, weeded and tended — change the harvest from the same land and seed.

**Breed better varieties.** Plant breeding produces improved varieties: the book's examples are disease-resistant cassava and budded oranges — yield protected by the plant's own constitution.

**More land.** The fifth way is extent: putting more land under cultivation raises total production, alongside the four ways that raise yield per piece of land.
