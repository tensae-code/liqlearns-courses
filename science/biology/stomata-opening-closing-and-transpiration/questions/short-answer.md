# Stomata — opening, closing and transpiration — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** Explain, step by step, how a stoma opens and closes.
**Answer: The pore is flanked by two guard cells. When the guard cells take up water they become turgid — swollen — and pull the pore (ostiole) open. When they lose water they go slack and the pore closes. The water vapour lost while it is open is transpiration.**

**2.** Explain Mozambique's way of teaching this.
**Answer: Mozambique's PESD II plant-physiology module (Módulo 5 de Biologia), Lesson 4, teaches the stomata and the plastids in one lesson: stomatal structure and functioning by guard-cell turgidity, transpiration as the quantified water loss, then plastids as double-membraned organelles found only in photosynthetic organisms, with the chloroplast structure figured.**
