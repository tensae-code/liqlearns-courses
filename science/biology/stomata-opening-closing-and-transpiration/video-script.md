Today: Stomata — opening, closing and transpiration.

Remember this one thing: a stoma opens when its two guard cells take up water and swell with turgidity, and closes when they lose it; the water vapour a plant loses through its open stomata is transpiration — and the same lesson introduces the plastids, double-membraned organelles found only in photosynthetic organisms, the chloroplast among them.

A stoma is a tiny mouth in the leaf, and two guard cells work it like lips. When the guard cells fill with water and swell, the pore opens. When they lose water, it closes. Through the open pore the plant breathes — and loses water vapour. That loss has a name: transpiration. In the same lesson the book introduces the plastids, the double-membraned organelles that only photosynthetic organisms have — the chloroplast among them.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
