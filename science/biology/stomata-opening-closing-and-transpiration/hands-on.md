# Hands-on — Stomata — opening, closing and transpiration

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Blow up a long balloon a little and bend it: as it fills it curves and pulls away from a twin balloon — two balloons pressed together open a slit between them as they swell, just as turgid guard cells open the stoma. Let air out and the slit closes.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory — a leaf surface magnified, one stoma drawn twice — open between two swollen guard cells and closed between two slack ones — with water-vapour arrows leaving the open pore, and beside it a chloroplast drawn with its double membrane — label its parts, and check against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
