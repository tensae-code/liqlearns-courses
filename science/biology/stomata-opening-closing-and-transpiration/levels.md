# Stomata — opening, closing and transpiration — depth

This file is the lesson's depth record. This lesson has no sub lessons — it is one whole, single-skill lesson.

Whole-lesson pass mark: the learner can state the one thing this lesson teaches (A stoma opens when its two guard cells take up water and swell with turgidity, and closes when they lose it; the water vapour a plant loses through its open stomata is transpiration — and the same lesson introduces the plastids, double-membraned organelles found only in photosynthetic organisms, the chloroplast among them.) and explain each key fact in their own words. Practice deepens by explaining the mechanism as a chain without looking — water in, guard cells turgid, ostiole open; water out, cells slack, pore closed — and stating the plastids' two marks: double membrane, only in photosynthetic organisms.
