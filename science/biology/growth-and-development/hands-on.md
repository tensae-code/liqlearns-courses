# Hands-on — Growth and development

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Follow one growing thing for a week — a seed you germinate, a young plant, a chick if one is near. Record one growth measurement (height or mass) and one development change (a new leaf kind, a flower bud, a new ability) separately, in two columns.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — one organism shown in four stages in a row — a germinating seed, a young seedling, a leafy plant and the same plant in flower — with a ruler under the row for growth and a flower ringed at the end for development — and check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
