# Growth and development

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Growth and development. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Growth is an irreversible increase in size — more cells, bigger cells, more dry mass — while development is the whole change by which a living thing matures: cells differentiate, organs form and take up their work, and the organism becomes able to live and reproduce as an adult. Growth can be measured with a ruler and a balance; development is seen in what the living thing can now do. No lesson in this course taught this on its own before, so this lesson is built for it — from Pakistan (Punjab)'s Punjab Board books, which teach it as a named topic.

[Picture — one organism shown in four stages in a row — a germinating seed, a young seedling, a leafy plant and the same plant in flower — with a ruler under the row for growth and a flower ringed at the end for development]

## The key facts
**Growth: getting bigger, for good.** Growth is increase in size and mass that is not reversed: new cells are made by division, cells enlarge, and material is laid down. A germinated seed does not shrink back into the seed — growth is one-way.

**Development: becoming mature.** Development is the ordered change from a fertilised cell to a working adult: cells differentiate into kinds, tissues and organs form, and functions switch on in their time — a flower opens, an animal reaches the age of reproduction. An organism can grow without developing further, and develop with little growth, so the two are taught as a pair, not as one idea.

**In plants.** Growth is centred on the meristems — the dividing tissues at root and shoot tips — and follows germination: the seed takes up water, the young root and shoot emerge, and development proceeds through the plant's stages to flowering and seed-making.

**In animals.** Development runs from the fertilised egg through the embryo to the young animal and the adult, with growth fastest in the young stages; the pair of processes together carry one cell to a complete organism.

**Pakistan (Punjab)'s way.** Punjab's second-year book gives growth and development a chapter of its own — Ch 19, between Reproduction (Ch 18) and Chromosomes and DNA (Ch 20) — so the learner meets the pair as a named subject, in the order: how organisms are made, then how they grow and mature.
