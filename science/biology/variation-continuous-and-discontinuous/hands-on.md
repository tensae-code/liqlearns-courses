# Hands-on — Variation — continuous and discontinuous

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: individuals of a species vary, and variation comes in two kinds: continuous variation, which grades smoothly between extremes (height, mass), and discontinuous variation, which falls into clear separate groups with nothing between (blood group) — caused by genes, by the environment, and by both together.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: two graphs side by side: a smooth bell curve of heights labelled continuous variation, and separate bars of blood groups labelled discontinuous variation. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
