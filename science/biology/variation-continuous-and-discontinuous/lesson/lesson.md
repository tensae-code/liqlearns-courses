# Variation — continuous and discontinuous

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Variation — continuous and discontinuous. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Malaysia's Form 5 book organises its variation chapter by the two kinds of variation (printed p. 256): types and factors of variation, with variation in humans as the working example — the organising frame is continuous against discontinuous.

[Picture — two graphs side by side: a smooth bell curve of heights labelled continuous variation, and separate bars of blood groups labelled discontinuous variation]

## The key facts
**Continuous variation.** Height, mass and skin shade grade smoothly from one extreme to the other, with every step between; plotted, they draw a smooth bell-shaped curve. Such traits are usually built by many genes together, with the environment adding its effect.

**Discontinuous variation.** Blood group, tongue-rolling and earlobe shape fall into clear groups with no in-between values; plotted, they draw separate bars. Such traits are usually controlled by one gene, and the environment scarcely moves them.

**Factors of variation.** Variation is caused by genetic factors — the reshuffling of meiosis, fertilisation joining two parents' genes, and mutation making new alleles — and by environmental factors such as food, light and temperature, which act on what the genes allow.

**Why the frame matters.** The two kinds predict the graph before it is drawn: measure a crowd's heights and expect a curve; count its blood groups and expect bars — variation sorted by kind is variation understood.

**Malaysia's way.** Malaysia's KSSM Form 5 book teaches variation as a topic in its own right (Ch.12, printed p. 256): types and factors of variation first, continuous against discontinuous as the organising frame, and mutation after it (p. 269) — not variation scattered inside single inheritance patterns.
