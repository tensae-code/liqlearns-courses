# Hands-on — Gene patents — and owning a discovery

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: a gene patent claims a discovered gene sequence as intellectual property, and Greece's Grade 12 bioethics chapter asks the hard question outright: can discovering a gene — finding what nature already made — fairly be owned, and what does owning it do to medicine and research.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a double-helix segment with a padlock and a price tag on one side and an open book and a hospital on the other, a balance scale between them. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
