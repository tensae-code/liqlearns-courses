# Hands-on — The brain's lymphatic vessels — a 2015 discovery

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: the brain was the one place the immune system was thought absent — until 2015, when lymphatic vessels were found in mouse meninges, and imaged in humans in 2017, hidden by hugging a great blood vessel through a region MRI reads badly: a lesson in how much anatomy can still be missed.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a brain wrapped in its membranes with a thin green vessel drawn running alongside a great red blood vessel in the membrane. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
