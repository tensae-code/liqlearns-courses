# Hands-on — The genetic code

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: the genetic code is the dictionary of life: bases are read in threes — codons — each codon naming one amino acid, with AUG as the start signal and three codons as stops; the code is nearly universal, the standing proof that all life shares one ancestry and one language.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a codon table drawn as a grid of small squares, one square lit showing AUG labelled START, and three squares darkened labelled STOP. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
