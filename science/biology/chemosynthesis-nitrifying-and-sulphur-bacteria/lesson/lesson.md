# Chemosynthesis — food-making without light

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Chemosynthesis — food-making without light. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Green plants make food with light. Nigeria's Senior Secondary Biology 1, in Chapter 4, sets beside them a second kind of autotroph: the chemosynthetic bacteria. They have no light to use, so they take energy from chemical reactions instead — oxidising ammonium compounds, nitrites or sulphur compounds — and use that energy to build food. Food-making without light is chemosynthesis.

[Picture — soil bacteria around a root, with arrows showing ammonium compounds turning to nitrites and nitrites to nitrates, releasing the energy the bacteria live on]

## The key facts
**Food-making without light.** Chemosynthesis is autotrophic nutrition powered by chemical energy instead of light: the organism oxidises a chemical substance and uses the energy released to build food.

**The nitrifying bacteria.** Nitrosomonas wins energy by oxidising ammonium compounds, and Nitrobacter by oxidising nitrites — the two nitrifying steps that turn ammonium into nitrates in the soil.

**The sulphur bacteria.** Sulphur bacteria win their energy by oxidising sulphur compounds, chemosynthesis in sulphur-rich places where light may never reach.

**Tied to the nitrogen cycle.** Because the nitrifying bacteria are the very organisms that run nitrification, chemosynthesis is not a side topic: it is the engine inside the nitrogen cycle that keeps nitrates coming for plants.

**Nigeria's way.** Nigeria teaches chemosynthetic autotrophs immediately after the photosynthetic autotrophs in Chapter 4 (printed p. 49) — two kinds of food-making side by side, one by light and one by chemistry.
