Today: Chemosynthesis — food-making without light.

Remember this one thing: some bacteria make their food without light at all: they win energy by oxidising chemicals — the nitrifying bacteria oxidise ammonium compounds and nitrites, and the sulphur bacteria oxidise sulphur compounds — and that energy builds their food; this is chemosynthesis, and it ties straight into the nitrogen cycle.

Chemosynthesis is autotrophic nutrition powered by chemical energy instead of light: the organism oxidises a chemical substance and uses the energy released to build food.

Nitrosomonas wins energy by oxidising ammonium compounds, and Nitrobacter by oxidising nitrites — the two nitrifying steps that turn ammonium into nitrates in the soil.

Sulphur bacteria win their energy by oxidising sulphur compounds, chemosynthesis in sulphur-rich places where light may never reach.

Because the nitrifying bacteria are the very organisms that run nitrification, chemosynthesis is not a side topic: it is the engine inside the nitrogen cycle that keeps nitrates coming for plants.

Nigeria teaches chemosynthetic autotrophs immediately after the photosynthetic autotrophs in Chapter 4 (printed p. 49) — two kinds of food-making side by side, one by light and one by chemistry.
