# Hands-on — Gene interactions — epistasis and polymery

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Make four paper combs — rose, pea, walnut, single. Write the gene pairs under each (R only, P only, both, neither) and explain to a companion why the walnut comb is the proof that the two genes interact: it is a form neither gene produces on its own.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — four chicken combs — rose, pea, walnut and single — under the gene pairs that make them, beside a two-step pathway where one gene's product blocks the second gene's colour from showing — and check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
