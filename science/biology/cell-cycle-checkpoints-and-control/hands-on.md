# Hands-on — Cell cycle checkpoints and control

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: the cell cycle is controlled at checkpoints: at the G1/S checkpoint and the M checkpoint the cell verifies that conditions are right — growth factors present, DNA doubling completed, chromosomes aligned — and halts the cycle if a check fails.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: the cell cycle drawn as a circle with two stop gates marked on it, labelled G1/S checkpoint and M checkpoint, each gate with a short list of what it verifies. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
