# Negative and positive feedback

## Do it first
Before you read: on a hot day, or after running, your body sweats — and after a while you cool down. Think about what is happening: something changed inside you, and your body answered. Hold that example in your mind, then read on and check it against what the lesson teaches.

## Homeostasis and feedback
Your body holds its inside conditions steady — that holding-steady is homeostasis. It does not happen by itself. When an inside condition changes, the body senses the change and answers it. The answer is called feedback, and it comes in two kinds. Negative feedback reverses a change: it pushes the condition back the way it came. Positive feedback amplifies a change: it pushes the condition further in the same direction, making the change bigger. This lesson works through the book's full example — body temperature — and then closes with the Ethiopian zoologists the book honours.

## Negative feedback — the temperature example, step by step
Follow the worked example round its full circle.

**The temperature rises.** Your body temperature climbs above its steady point — on a hot day, or when you work or run hard.

**The brain centre answers.** A centre in the brain detects the rise and sends out the orders that will reverse it.

**Vasodilation and sweating.** The blood vessels in the skin widen — vasodilation — so more blood flows near the surface and loses heat. At the same time you sweat, and as the sweat evaporates it carries heat away from the skin.

**The temperature falls.** With heat leaving the body both ways, the temperature falls back to its steady point. The change has been reversed — that is negative feedback.

**And when the temperature falls instead?** The same machinery runs in reverse. When the body grows too cold, the brain centre's answer turns the cooling machinery off and back: the vessels narrow so less heat is lost at the skin, and sweating stops. The temperature climbs back to its steady point. Whichever way the temperature moves, negative feedback moves against it — it always reverses the change.

## Positive feedback — amplifying a change
Positive feedback does the opposite job. Instead of reversing a change, the body's answer amplifies it: the response makes the change bigger, pushing the condition further from where it started. Where negative feedback holds things steady, positive feedback drives a change onward. Keep the two apart by their direction: negative — reverse; positive — amplify.

## Ethiopian zoologists
The unit closes by honouring Ethiopian zoologists — scientists at Ethiopia's universities who have given their whole working lives to studying the country's animals. A zoologist studies animals: where they live, how they behave, and how they survive. Ethiopia's own zoologists have spent lifetimes on Ethiopia's own wildlife, and that long study serves two things at once: it grows science, because knowledge of the country's animals grows with it, and it serves national development, because a country that knows its animals can protect them and plan with them. Their work is part of this lesson: feedback keeps a body steady, and these scientists' steady, life-long work keeps the country's knowledge of its animals growing.
