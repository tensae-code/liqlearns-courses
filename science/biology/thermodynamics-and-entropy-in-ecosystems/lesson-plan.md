# Lesson plan — Thermodynamics and entropy in ecosystems

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Biology · **Lesson:** Thermodynamics and entropy in ecosystems
**Place in the curriculum:** Biology — appended to Unit 16 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** an ecosystem runs on the laws of thermodynamics — energy is transformed, never created or destroyed, and at every transfer some useful energy is lost as heat while entropy rises — so energy flows one way through the trophic levels and must keep arriving, while materials cycle.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. Food chains and food webs is recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: **Food chains and food webs** and **Trophic levels — the steps of a food chain**. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words, the one thing this lesson teaches: an ecosystem runs on the laws of thermodynamics — energy is transformed, never created or destroyed, and at every transfer some useful energy is lost as heat while entropy rises — so energy flows one way through the trophic levels and must keep arriving, while materials cycle.
2. State what each law of thermodynamics says inside an ecosystem: energy is transformed, never created; every transfer loses useful energy as heat and entropy rises.
3. Explain why energy flow is one-way while materials cycle.
4. Explain Costa Rica's way: the physics is taught first, at the head of Unit 2, and the trophic levels, food chains and food pyramid are built on it.
