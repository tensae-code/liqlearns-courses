# Thermodynamics and entropy in ecosystems — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** Explain: why can only a fraction of the energy at one trophic level reach the next?
**Answer: Because of the second law of thermodynamics. Energy is transformed at every step — sunlight into grass, grass into herbivore, herbivore into carnivore — and no transformation is perfect: part of the useful energy degrades into heat, which the living system cannot gather back, and entropy (disorder) rises. What is left after each loss is all the next level can receive, which is why the energy arrows thin at every step and why the food pyramid has its shape.**

**2.** Explain: Costa Rica's way of teaching this lesson.
**Answer: Biología Undécimo opens Unit 2 with the physics itself — Termodinámica de los ecosistemas, Leyes de la termodinámica, Fenómeno de entropía, printed pp. 66–67 — and only then teaches the trophic levels (printed p. 68), the food chain and trophic web (printed pp. 69–70) and the food pyramid (printed p. 70). The learner is given the laws first, so the pyramid is not a shape to memorise but a consequence to understand.**
