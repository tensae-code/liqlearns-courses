# Hands-on — Thermodynamics and entropy in ecosystems

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Take ten counters for the energy in grass. Pass them along a chain you draw — grass, grass-eater, hunter. At each pass, put some counters aside in a "heat" pile, as the second law demands, and pass on only what is left. Count what the hunter finally holds. Under the drawing write: energy flows one way; materials cycle.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — sunlight in, arrows thinning step by step, heat leaving at every step — label it (first law: transformed, never created; second law: heat lost, entropy rises), and check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap. End with this one together: if energy could cycle the way carbon does, what would an ecosystem no longer need? Say why the second law forbids it.
