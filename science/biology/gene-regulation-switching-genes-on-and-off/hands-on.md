# Hands-on — Gene regulation — switching genes on and off

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: cells switch genes on and off: prokaryotes group genes in operons a repressor can shut, and eukaryotes control expression at many levels, so the same genome makes a nerve cell and a liver cell — regulation is how one instruction book runs every cell kind.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a row of gene switches on a DNA strip, some flipped on and lit, some off and dark, with a repressor block sitting on one switch. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
