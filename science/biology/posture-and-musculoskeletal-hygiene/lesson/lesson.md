# Posture and musculoskeletal hygiene

## The key facts
**Hygiene closes the system.** Armenia's Grade 8 "Biology. Human" book ends its musculoskeletal chapter the way it ends every system chapter: with hygiene. §22, the hygiene of the musculoskeletal system, is the closing section — the system's care is taught as part of the system itself.

**Correct posture.** Correct posture holds the spine in its natural curves: sitting straight with the back supported, feet flat, the work at a height that does not force the head and shoulders forward; standing and walking tall, and carrying loads evenly. Posture is a habit the skeleton is held in for hours every day.

**Preventing spinal curvature.** A growing spine bent crooked day after day can keep the bend: habitual one-sided sitting, a load always carried on the same side, and weak back muscles let the spine curve sideways or stoop. The prevention is the correction of the habit itself — even loads, straight sitting, and exercise that strengthens the muscles holding the spine.

**Preventing flat feet.** The foot's arch is held by muscles and ligaments; long standing in badly fitting shoes and weak foot muscles let the arch flatten into flat feet, which tire the legs and spoil walking. Fitting shoes, walking and exercise that work the foot, and not overloading a growing foot protect the arch.

**Exercise hygiene for a growing body.** Exercise is the system's food, taken hygienically: regular, increasing gradually, matched to a body still growing — movement strengthens the muscles that hold the skeleton straight, while sudden overloads and training through injury damage what they should build.

**Armenia's way.** The hygiene section stands at §22, closing Ch. 5 (the musculoskeletal system, §§18–22) — Armenia teaches the growing learner's own posture, spine and feet as the chapter's final, practical word.
