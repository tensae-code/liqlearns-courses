# Video script — Mitosis — one cell becomes two identical cells

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

One division producing two identical daughter cells, in four steps: prophase (chromosomes condense, envelope breaks down, spindle forms, centrosomes separate), metaphase (chromosomes line up), anaphase (sister chromatids pulled to opposite poles) and telophase (envelopes reform, chromosomes unravel), finished by cytokinesis.

Six roles of cell division are listed (renewing damaged cells, producing new cells, maintaining chromosome number, growth and development, repair and damage control, survival of the organism), with skin cells as the constantly dividing example; a comparison table then sets mitosis against meiosis across reproduction type, genetic similarity, crossing over, division count, homologue pairing, mother-cell ploidy and daughter cells produced — carrying the crossing-over misprint flagged in the verification block.

Now say it in your own words — and find one example near your home.
