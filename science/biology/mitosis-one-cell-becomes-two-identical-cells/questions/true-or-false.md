# Mitosis — one cell becomes two identical cells — true or false

Say true or false.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) One division producing two identical daughter cells, in four steps: prophase (chromosomes condense, envelope breaks down, spindle forms, centrosomes separate), metaphase (chromosomes line up), anaphase (sister chromatids pulled to opposite poles) and telophase (envelopes reform, chromosomes unravel), finished by cytokinesis.**
**Answer: True** — this is what the lesson teaches.

**2. (Sub lesson 2) Six roles of cell division are listed (renewing damaged cells, producing new cells, maintaining chromosome number, growth and development, repair and damage control, survival of the organism), with skin cells as the constantly dividing example; a comparison table then sets mitosis against meiosis across reproduction type, genetic similarity, crossing over, division count, homologue pairing, mother-cell ploidy and daughter cells produced — carrying the crossing-over misprint flagged in the verification block.**
**Answer: True** — this is what the lesson teaches.
