# Finland's Move! fitness monitoring

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Finland's Move! fitness monitoring. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Move! is Finland's national system for monitoring the physical functional capacity of school pupils. Pupils are measured with batteries of tests — endurance, strength and mobility — and their results are read against national reference values, so a pupil, a school and the whole country can see how young bodies are doing (oph.fi/move).

[Picture — a school hall in Finland during Move! measurements — pupils doing an endurance run, a strength test and a mobility test, with a teacher recording results against a chart of national reference values]

## The key facts
**A national system, not one school's idea.** Move! is Finland's national physical functional-capacity monitoring system for schools. The same measurements are made across the country, in the same way — that is what makes the results comparable.

**Three batteries.** The Move! measurements cover three areas of functional capacity: endurance, strength, and mobility. Together they describe what a young body can do, not only how it looks or weighs.

**National reference values.** Results are read against national reference values — the gathered results of pupils across Finland — so a measurement means something: it can be placed against the country's own picture of its pupils.

**Finland's way.** In the wellbeing pack, Move! is Card 14 — one card among fifteen in which pupils measure their own bodies and then learn the biology behind each number. The pack's rules stand over all of it: measurements are voluntary, results are never compared competitively, and data is not collected centrally.
