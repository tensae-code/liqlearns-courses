# Hands-on — Transport of substances in multicellular organisms

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: a multicellular organism is too big for diffusion alone, so substances must be carried: in plants through their conducting tissues and in animals through blood and lymph, from where a substance is taken in or made to every cell that needs it.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a plant and a small animal side by side, arrows carrying labelled loads — water and food in the plant's vessels, oxygen and food in the animal's blood — from source to farthest cell in each. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
