# Hands-on — Alleles — dominant and recessive forms of a gene

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: a gene comes in alternative forms called alleles, and when two different alleles meet, the dominant one shows in the organism while the recessive one is carried hidden — the distinction Greece's Grades 8–9 book teaches as its own section before Mendel's laws.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: two chromosomes side by side carrying the same gene position, one marked with a capital-letter allele shown in the flower's colour and one with a small-letter allele hidden behind it. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
