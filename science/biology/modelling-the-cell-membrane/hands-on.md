# Hands-on — Modelling the cell membrane

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: a cell membrane can be modelled with simple materials — a glass dish of water, vegetable oil and fine sawdust — and each material in the model stands for one part of the real membrane's structure.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a glass dish of water with a film of vegetable oil on top and fine sawdust sprinkled on the oil, labelled part by part: water, oil and sawdust, with arrows to the parts of a real cell membrane each one stands for. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
