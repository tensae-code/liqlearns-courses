# Mutation, genetic drift and gene flow

## Do it first
Before you read: think of one living thing near your home that shows this lesson's idea — mutation, genetic drift and gene flow. Hold it in your mind, then read on and check your idea against what the lesson teaches.

Mutation is quantified (each cell carries about 6 billion base pairs; mutations arise at roughly 1 per 50 million base pairs, so each new cell averages about 120 mutations, most harmless); resistance is historicised (penicillin widely used in the USA from 1943; the first penicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus found in 1947; today over half of S. aureus infections are resistant — with the point that resistance only advantages a bacterium when that antibiotic is actually present); sickle-cell anaemia is traced to a mutation in the gene for the iron-rich haemoglobin compound; genetic drift is defined as binomial sampling error of the gene pool and taught through a bottle-of-marbles bottleneck model; and Ellis–van Creveld syndrome (EVC/EVC2 genes on chromosome 4, autosomal recessive) is given as a worked founder-population case.
