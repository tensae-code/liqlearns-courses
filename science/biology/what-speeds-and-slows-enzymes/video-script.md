# Video script — What speeds and slows enzymes

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Activity peaks at an optimum and falls away with temperature (heat denatures, irreversibly), pH, substrate and enzyme concentration, end-product build-up, radiation, water, activators and inhibitors; each factor's curve is drawn and pupils test heat sensitivity by dropping hydrogen peroxide on raw vs boiled potato.

The guide explains the student book's labs: potato bubbles in hydrogen peroxide because its catalase breaks the peroxide (a powerful oxidiser) down, the bubbling being the visible sign of metabolic decomposition into enzyme, substrate and product terms; and a cut apple browns because its polyphenol oxidase (phenolase), released and meeting air oxygen, catalyses formation of the brown pigment melanin — an oxidation the guide calls an enzyme reduction/oxidation reaction.

Now say it in your own words — and find one example near your home.
