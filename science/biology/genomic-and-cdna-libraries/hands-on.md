# Hands-on — Genomic and cDNA libraries

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: a genomic library stores a whole genome as cloned DNA fragments in bacteria — every gene shelved — while a cDNA library is built from a cell's messenger RNA copied back into DNA, so it shelves only the genes that cell was using; a labelled probe finds the wanted clone by hybridisation.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: shelves of bacterial colonies drawn as library books, one shelf labelled 'whole genome' and a smaller one 'genes in use', with a glowing probe pulling one book out. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
