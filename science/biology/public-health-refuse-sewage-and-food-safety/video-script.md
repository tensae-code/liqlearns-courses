Today: Public health — refuse, sewage and food safety.

Remember this one thing: public health is a shared duty: refuse disposed of safely (dustbin, incinerator, burial), sewage disposed of safely (pit latrines, bucket toilets, the flush toilet and septic tank, whose sludge becomes fertiliser), water and food protected, and the Ministry of Health inspecting abattoirs and eating places.

Public health is community health: the chapter teaches it as a duty everyone shares, not a service done to passive people.

Refuse is disposed of by dustbin, by incinerator (burning), or by burial — three named routes that keep waste from breeding disease where people live.

Sewage is handled by pit latrines, bucket toilets, and the flush toilet with a septic tank — and the septic tank's sludge is turned to fertiliser, waste made useful.

Protection of water supplies and of food runs through the chapter: what the community drinks and eats must be guarded from contamination.

The Ministry of Health inspects abattoirs and eating places — public authority checking the places where food is killed, prepared and sold.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
