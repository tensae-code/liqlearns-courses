# Hands-on — Public health — refuse, sewage and food safety

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Walk your own street or compound and audit it against the book: where does refuse go? where does sewage go? is water protected? For each, name the book's method being used — or the one that should be. Say aloud one improvement that is a shared duty, not someone else's job.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — a neighbourhood health panel: a lidded dustbin, an incinerator, a pit latrine and a septic tank in cross-section, and an inspector checking a market food stall — and check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
