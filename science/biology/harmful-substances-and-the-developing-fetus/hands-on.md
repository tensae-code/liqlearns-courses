# Hands-on — Harmful substances and the developing fetus

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: the placenta feeds the fetus but does not seal it off: alcohol, nicotine and narcotic drugs pass through it and can harm the developing baby, which is why those substances are forbidden in pregnancy.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a mother and her womb drawn in outline, the placenta in the middle with three crossed-out items at its edge — a cigarette, an alcohol bottle and a drug packet — and arrows showing what the placenta passes. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
