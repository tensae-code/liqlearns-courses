# Plant adaptations to habitats

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Plant adaptations to habitats. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Malaysia's Form 5 book surveys plant adaptations habitat by habitat (printed p. 134): the same problem — getting and keeping water, light and support — solved four different ways by four kinds of plant.

[Picture — four habitats in one row: a cactus in desert sand, a water lily on a pond, a mangrove at a salty shore, and an orchid perched on a forest branch]

## The key facts
**Xerophytes — dry habitats.** Plants of dry places cut water loss and store water: thick cuticle, sunken or few stomata, leaves reduced to spines, fleshy water-storing stems, deep or widespread roots.

**Hydrophytes — water habitats.** Plants living in water float and breathe: air spaces in stems and leaves give buoyancy and carry air, stomata sit on the upper surface, and roots are reduced — water is everywhere, support and air are the problems.

**Halophytes — salty habitats.** Plants of salty ground, like mangroves, face water that salt makes hard to take in: they exclude or excrete salt, store water in fleshy leaves, and may breathe through roots that rise above the mud.

**Epiphytes — perched habitats.** Epiphytes live on other plants to reach light; their problem is water with no soil — met by roots that absorb from the air and tissues that store rain.

**Malaysia's way.** Malaysia's KSSM Form 5 book gives habitat adaptations a chapter of their own (Ch.7, printed p. 134) — xerophytes, hydrophytes, halophytes and epiphytes surveyed together as the close of its flowering-plant theme.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words: plants are fitted to the water of their habitat: xerophytes hoard and guard water in dry places, hydrophytes float and breathe in water, halophytes manage salt in salty ground, and epiphytes live perched above the ground — each habitat answered by a set of adaptations. Use each key fact once. Then let your companion explain it back to you.
