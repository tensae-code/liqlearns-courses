# Hands-on — Plant adaptations to habitats

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: plants are fitted to the water of their habitat: xerophytes hoard and guard water in dry places, hydrophytes float and breathe in water, halophytes manage salt in salty ground, and epiphytes live perched above the ground — each habitat answered by a set of adaptations.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: four habitats in one row: a cactus in desert sand, a water lily on a pond, a mangrove at a salty shore, and an orchid perched on a forest branch. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
