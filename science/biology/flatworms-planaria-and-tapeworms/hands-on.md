# Hands-on — Flatworms — planaria and tapeworms

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: flatworms are among the first animals with three embryonic germ layers — ectoderm, endoderm and mesoderm — and the group runs from free-living planaria, whose eye spots are light-sensing cell clusters, to parasitic flukes and tapeworms with life cycles through their hosts.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a planarian drawn flat with its two eye spots labelled, beside a fluke life-cycle circle showing the human as the primary host. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
