# Biostatistics — mean, median and mode

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Biostatistics — mean, median and mode. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Biostatistics is the statistics a biologist uses on living data: a set of measurements is summed up by its mean (the values added and divided by how many there are), its median (the middle value when they are lined up in order) and its mode (the value that occurs most often) — and the set is shown at a glance in a bar chart. No lesson in this course taught this on its own before, so this lesson is built for it — from Pakistan (Punjab)'s Punjab Board books, which teach it as a named topic.

[Picture — a row of seedlings of different heights with a ruler beside them, and next to it a simple bar chart of the same heights, with the middle seedling ringed as the median]

## The key facts
**Why a biologist needs numbers summed up.** Biology produces lists: the heights of twenty seedlings, the pulse rates of a class, the leaf counts of ten plants. A list alone says little; the mean, median and mode each squeeze the list into one honest number that can be compared with another set.

**The mean.** Add all the values and divide by how many values there are. The mean shares the total out evenly — it is pulled up or down by every value in the set, including any unusually large or small one.

**The median and the mode.** Line the values up in order and take the middle one: that is the median, the value half the set sits above and half below. The mode is simpler still — the value that occurs most often in the set.

**Showing it: the bar chart.** A bar chart draws each value or group as a bar whose height is the number; the eye compares bars at once. Punjab's chapter pairs the three averages with the bar chart as the way of presenting biological data.

**Pakistan (Punjab)'s way.** Punjab's Grade 9 SNC book ends with a whole chapter of its own — Ch 11, Biostatistics — inside the biology book itself: statistics is taught as part of a biologist's training, not left to the mathematics course.
