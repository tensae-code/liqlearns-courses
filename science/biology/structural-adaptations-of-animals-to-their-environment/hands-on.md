# Hands-on — Structural adaptations of animals to their environment

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: an animal's body structures are fitted to its habitat — the camel carries a water reserve for dry land, and the aquatic turtle's locomotory structures are built for swimming.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a camel in dry land and an aquatic turtle swimming, each labelled with the body structures that fit it to its habitat. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
