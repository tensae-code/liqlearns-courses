Today: Nigeria's vegetation belts — the local biomes.

Remember this one thing: Nigeria's own vegetation belts are its local biomes: rain forest in the wet south, then the Southern and Northern Guinea savannahs with their shea butter and locust bean trees, and finally the dry Sahel in Borno State near Lake Chad.

Nigeria's vegetation belts are the local biomes of Chapter 7 — the worldwide biome idea taught on the country's own ground.

The wet south carries rain forest — the belt of highest rainfall, before the savannahs begin.

Southern and Northern Guinea savannah follow: grassland with trees — shea butter and locust bean trees, and Isoberlinia doka, are the book's named savannah trees.

In Borno State, near Lake Chad, lies the Sahel — the driest belt, worked in the book with Maiduguri rainfall figures.

The belts follow the rain: as rainfall falls from south to north, forest gives way to savannah and savannah to Sahel.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
