# Hands-on — Artificial ecosystems and agrocenoses

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: people build artificial ecosystems — fields and orchards, which are agrocenoses, and cities and oil fields — and unlike natural ecosystems they cannot hold themselves steady without constant human work, energy and materials.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: four artificial ecosystems in a row — a wheat field, an orchard, a city block and an oil field — each with a farmer or worker shown keeping it going, beside a self-holding steppe ecosystem with no one tending it. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
