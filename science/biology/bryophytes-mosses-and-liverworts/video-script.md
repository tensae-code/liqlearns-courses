Today: Bryophytes — mosses and liverworts.

Remember this one thing: bryophytes are the amphibians of the plant kingdom: small avascular plants — under about 30 cm — protected by a cuticle but still needing liquid water for fertilisation; the liverworts have a flat thalloid body, while the mosses have rhizoids, cauloids and phylloids, as in the module's example, Funaria.

The bryophytes are called the amphibians of the plant kingdom — and that name tells the whole story. They live on land, with a cuticle to protect them. But they have no vessels, they never grow tall, and to reproduce they still need liquid water. The liverworts lie flat like green ribbons. The mosses stand a little taller, with tiny root-like rhizoids, stem-like cauloids and leaf-like phylloids.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
