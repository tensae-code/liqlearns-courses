# Hands-on — Bryophytes — mosses and liverworts

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find moss on a damp wall, stone or tree base (or picture it): with a finger, trace its tiny stem and leaves and feel how low and soft the cushion is — no wood, no vessels, never tall. Then say why it lives exactly there: it still needs the wet.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory — a flat lobed liverwort lying like a green ribbon on damp ground beside an upright Funaria moss with its rhizoids below, cauloid stem and phylloid leaves labelled, under a rain cloud with droplets — the water the plant still needs — label its parts, and check against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
