# Bryophytes — mosses and liverworts — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** Why are bryophytes called the amphibians of the plant kingdom?
**Answer: Because they live between two worlds like amphibians: on land they are protected by a cuticle, but they have no vessels and their fertilisation still needs liquid water — they cannot complete their lives away from wetness.**

**2.** Explain Mozambique's way of teaching this.
**Answer: Mozambique's PESD II plant module (Módulo 2 de Biologia), Lesson 3, teaches phylum Bryophyta as the "amphibians of the plant kingdom": avascular, under ~30 cm, still needing liquid water for fertilisation, protected by a cuticle — then liverworts (thalloid body) against mosses (rhizoids, cauloids, phylloids), Funaria as the example, and the importance of mosses in ecosystems.**
