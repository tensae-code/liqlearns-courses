# Bryophytes — mosses and liverworts — depth

This file is the lesson's depth record. This lesson has no sub lessons — it is one whole, single-skill lesson.

Whole-lesson pass mark: the learner can state the one thing this lesson teaches (Bryophytes are the amphibians of the plant kingdom: small avascular plants — under about 30 cm — protected by a cuticle but still needing liquid water for fertilisation; the liverworts have a flat thalloid body, while the mosses have rhizoids, cauloids and phylloids, as in the module's example, Funaria.) and explain each key fact in their own words. Practice deepens by contrasting the two groups without looking — thalloid liverwort vs moss with rhizoids, cauloids and phylloids — and giving the two reasons for the "amphibians" name: no vessels, and fertilisation needing liquid water.
