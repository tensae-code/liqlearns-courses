# Hands-on — Respiration in plants

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: plants respire as animals do — every living cell takes in oxygen and releases energy from food — but with no special breathing organs: air moves in through stomata, lenticels and root surfaces, day and night, even while photosynthesis runs the other way in the light.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a green plant with oxygen arrows entering at a leaf stoma, a stem lenticel and the roots, and a small furnace glow drawn inside one cell labelled respiration. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
