# Hands-on — Productivity of ecosystems

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: an ecosystem's productivity is the rate at which it builds living matter: primary production by its plants — gross, before their own respiration is subtracted, and net, after — and secondary production by the animals that eat them, the measure that decides how much life a place can support.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a meadow drawn as a ledger: sunlight entering as income, a slice spent on the plants' own breathing, and the remaining pile labelled net production feeding a rabbit. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
