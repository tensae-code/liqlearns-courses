# Hands-on — Cancer — what it is and how it starts

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: cancer begins when mutations strike the genes that control cell multiplication, so a cell divides without control and its descendants form a tumour — and Greece teaches it with its causes, its prevention and the European Code Against Cancer alongside.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: one body cell with a damaged control gene dividing again and again into a growing cluster, beside a panel of prevention and early-check symbols. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
