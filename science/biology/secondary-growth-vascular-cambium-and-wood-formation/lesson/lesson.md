# Secondary growth — vascular cambium and wood formation

## The idea
lateral meristems, the vascular cambium ring between xylem and phloem, and the formation of wood

[Picture — a woody stem cross-section with the vascular cambium ring picked out between the xylem inside and the phloem outside, and annual rings drawn in the wood]

## The key facts
**Lateral meristems.** Kuwait's Grade 11 Term 1 student book (Lesson 1-4, printed pp. 55–58) teaches secondary growth through the lateral meristems.

**The cambium ring and wood.** The vascular cambium forms a ring between the xylem and the phloem; its activity produces the secondary tissues, and the formation of wood is this growth seen from inside the stem.

**Kuwait's way.** Kuwait's Grade 11 botany unit separates the two growths cleanly: primary growth from the apical meristems first (Lesson 1-4), then secondary growth and wood formation from the cambium ring.
