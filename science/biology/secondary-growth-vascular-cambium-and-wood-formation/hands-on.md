# Hands-on — Secondary growth — vascular cambium and wood formation

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: woody stems thicken by secondary growth: lateral meristems — the vascular cambium ring between xylem and phloem — lay down new tissue year by year, and that growth is the formation of wood.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a woody stem cross-section with the vascular cambium ring picked out between the xylem inside and the phloem outside, and annual rings drawn in the wood. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
