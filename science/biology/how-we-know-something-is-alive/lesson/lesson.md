# How we know something is alive

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — How we know something is alive. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
A stone and a goat sit side by side. Only one of them feeds, breathes, grows and responds. Those shared marks tell us it is alive.

[Picture 29210 — one animal and one plant with the characteristics labelled]

## The key facts
**The marks.** Living things show nutrition, respiration, movement, excretion, growth, reproduction and sensitivity to stimuli.

**Homeostasis.** Living things keep internal conditions stable — body temperature held steady is the worked example.

**Adaptation.** Living things adapt, and adaptation evolves over time.

**Non-living.** Non-living matter does not show the full set of characteristics of life.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words: living things share a set of characteristics that non-living matter does not have. Use each key fact once. Then let your companion explain it back to you.
