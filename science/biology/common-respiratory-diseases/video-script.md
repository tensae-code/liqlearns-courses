Today: Common respiratory diseases.

Remember this one thing: The breathing passages have common diseases of their own — colds, influenza, bronchitis, pneumonia, tuberculosis and asthma — spread mostly through the air, and preventable by air, hygiene and vaccination habits.

A cold blocks the nose and throat for a few days; influenza (flu) is its heavier cousin, with fever, aching and exhaustion, and it can turn dangerous in the very young, the old and the weak. Both ride on the droplets of coughs and sneezes and on hands that touch the face afterwards.

Bronchitis — inflammation of the bronchial tubes — brings a deep cough and mucus. Pneumonia is deeper and graver: infection reaches the alveoli, they fill, breathing and gas exchange fail, and without treatment it can kill; it often follows flu or measles in a weakened body.

The TB bacterium travels in the fine spray of a patient's cough and settles in a new lung. It grows slowly — cough lasting weeks, fever, night sweats, weight loss and sometimes blood in the sputum — and it is curable, but only by the complete months-long course of antibiotics; stopping early breeds resistant germs.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
