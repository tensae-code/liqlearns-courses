# Common respiratory diseases

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Common respiratory diseases. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Afghanistan's Grade 9 Biology book closes its respiratory chapter with the common diseases of the respiratory system. This lesson teaches them on their own: which part of the air path each disease strikes, how they spread, and how they are prevented and treated.

## The key facts
**Colds and influenza.** A cold blocks the nose and throat for a few days; influenza (flu) is its heavier cousin, with fever, aching and exhaustion, and it can turn dangerous in the very young, the old and the weak. Both ride on the droplets of coughs and sneezes and on hands that touch the face afterwards.

**Bronchitis and pneumonia.** Bronchitis — inflammation of the bronchial tubes — brings a deep cough and mucus. Pneumonia is deeper and graver: infection reaches the alveoli, they fill, breathing and gas exchange fail, and without treatment it can kill; it often follows flu or measles in a weakened body.

**Tuberculosis.** The TB bacterium travels in the fine spray of a patient's cough and settles in a new lung. It grows slowly — cough lasting weeks, fever, night sweats, weight loss and sometimes blood in the sputum — and it is curable, but only by the complete months-long course of antibiotics; stopping early breeds resistant germs.

**Asthma.** In asthma the bronchi are over-sensitive: dust, smoke, cold air or pollen makes them swell, tighten and fill with mucus, and the sufferer wheezes, worst on breathing out. It is not an infection and cannot be caught; attacks are eased by inhaled medicines that reopen the passages.

**Prevention.** Ventilated rooms thin the germs in the air; a covered cough and washed hands break the droplet road; vaccination prevents several causes outright (BCG against severe TB among them); and avoiding tobacco smoke and thick cooking smoke protects the passages themselves, in children most of all.

**Afghanistan's way.** The Grade 9 respiratory chapter ends with the diseases of that system — the common cold to tuberculosis worked as one named set, following the book's rule that every system chapter closes with its diseases and hygiene.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words: The breathing passages have common diseases of their own — colds, influenza, bronchitis, pneumonia, tuberculosis and asthma — spread mostly through the air, and preventable by air, hygiene and vaccination habits. Use each key fact once. Then let your companion explain it back to you.
