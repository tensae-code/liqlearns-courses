# Genetic counselling and prenatal diagnosis

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Genetic counselling and prenatal diagnosis. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Knowing a condition runs in a family is only useful if someone can turn it into a risk, a test and advice. Vietnam's Sinh học 12 gives medical genetic counselling its own full section (Bài 13, III, printed pp. 71–74) inside the human genetics lesson. Risk is calculated from pedigrees and from population genetics; the unborn child can be tested by amniocentesis — drawn step by step in the book: ultrasound guidance, about 20 mL of fluid, culture, analysis — or by chorionic villus sampling at 11–13 weeks; counselling advises against consanguineous marriage; and the care continues after birth in newborn screening, with the galactosaemia diet as the worked case, and the mother's age-35 Down-syndrome risk stated as part of the reckoning. No lesson in this course took genetic counselling and prenatal diagnosis together as its one thing before, so this lesson is built for it.

[Picture — a counsellor's desk in three panels: a family pedigree drawn on paper with a risk figure worked beside it; an ultrasound-guided needle taking a small sample of fluid from around an unborn baby, the sample tube labelled for culture and analysis; and a newborn's heel-prick screening card]

## The key facts
**Risk, calculated.** The counsellor's first tool is arithmetic, not a machine: the risk of an inherited condition is calculated from the family's pedigree and from population genetics.

**Amniocentesis.** The book draws the prenatal test step by step: under ultrasound guidance a sample of about 20 mL of the fluid around the unborn child is taken, the cells in it are cultured, and the chromosomes are analysed.

**Chorionic villus sampling.** An earlier test samples the chorionic villi — the tissue that becomes the placenta — at 11–13 weeks of pregnancy.

**Advice and screening.** Counselling advises against consanguineous marriage, weighs the mother's age — the Down-syndrome risk at age 35 is the book's stated figure — and continues after the birth in newborn screening, where the galactosaemia diet shows a diagnosis turned into treatment.
