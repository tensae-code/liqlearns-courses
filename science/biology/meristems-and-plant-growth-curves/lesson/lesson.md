# Meristems and plant growth curves

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Meristems and plant growth curves. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Malaysia's Form 5 book opens its plant theme with meristematic tissues and growth (printed p. 6): where a plant's new cells are made, and how growth is measured — as curves. Growth here is not a vague 'getting bigger' but a quantity that can be plotted.

[Picture — a shoot tip and root tip with their meristem regions shaded, beside two graphs: a smooth S-shaped sigmoid growth curve and a stepped growth curve rising in stairs]

## The key facts
**Meristems.** Meristematic tissue is the plant's growth tissue: cells that keep dividing. Apical meristems at shoot and root tips lengthen the plant (primary growth); lateral meristems thicken it (secondary growth).

**Growth measured.** Growth is measured as increase — in length, mass or number — over time, and plotted as a growth curve, so the rate of growth can be read from the graph's slope.

**The sigmoid curve.** Most growth draws a sigmoid (S-shaped) curve: slow at first, fast in the middle, then levelling as growth slows and stops — lag, log and stationary phases in one S.

**The stepped curve.** Some growth comes in steps — a stepped curve, rising in bursts with flat rests between, as in organisms that grow only at intervals.

**Malaysia's way.** Malaysia's KSSM Form 5 book teaches meristems and growth curves together at the start of its flowering-plant theme (Ch.1, printed p. 6): the tissue that grows, then the curves that measure the growing — the sigmoid and the stepped curve side by side.
