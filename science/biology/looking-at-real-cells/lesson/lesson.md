# Looking at real cells

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Looking at real cells. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Onion epidermis and cheek cells are the standard subjects. Draw what you see — wall, membrane, cytoplasm, nucleus — not a copied diagram.

## The key facts
**Subjects.** Onion epidermis and cheek cells are prepared and mounted for observation.

**Draw what is seen.** Pupils draw and label what is actually visible rather than copying a textbook diagram.

**Visible parts.** Cell wall, membrane, cytoplasm and nucleus are the parts visible under the light microscope.

**Mounting.** Plant material and cheek cells are prepared and mounted before observing.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words: real cells are observed under the microscope and drawn as they actually appear. Use each key fact once. Then let your companion explain it back to you.
