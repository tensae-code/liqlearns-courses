# Cancer and tumours

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Cancer and tumours. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Every cell division you have met in this course — mitosis building new cells — runs under control: cells divide when the body needs new cells, and the division stops when the need is met. Azerbaijan's Grade 11 textbook closes its whole course with the pair: controlled and uncontrolled cell division. Its last part first finishes mitosis, then turns to what happens when the control itself fails: §46, uncontrolled division of cells — tumours; and §47, a section titled simply "Cancer" (Рак).

[Picture — two cell families side by side: one dividing under control, each new cell stopping when the need is met; the other dividing without control, piling into a growing mass labelled tumour]

## The key facts
**Controlled division.** Normal division is controlled division: a cell divides when new cells are needed — for growth and repair — and stops when they are not. The control is part of the division, not an extra.

**Uncontrolled division.** When the control fails, the cell no longer stops: it divides without control, its copies divide too, and the extra cells pile up instead of taking their ordered place.

**Tumours.** Опухоли (§46): the pile of cells built by uncontrolled division is a tumour — a mass of extra cells that the body did not order and does not need.

**Cancer.** Рак (§47): the book closes the part — and nearly the whole course — with a section titled simply "Cancer": the illness of uncontrolled cell division, given its own name and its own section, straight after tumours.

**Azerbaijan's way.** In Azerbaijan this is how school biology ends: the final part of the Grade 11 book pairs the controlled division of mitosis with its failure — tumours (§46) and Cancer (§47) — and only then compares mitosis with meiosis (§48). Uncontrolled division is taught as the direct shadow of the controlled division the pupil has just learned.
