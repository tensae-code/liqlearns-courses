# Hermaphroditism in animals

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Hermaphroditism in animals. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
The reproduction chapter of Iraq's Grade 12 Biology book climbs the kinds of reproduction one by one — viruses, bacteria and protists, fungi, plants, animals — and after parthenogenetic reproduction (§10-3, printed p. 135) it closes with a section of its own on hermaphroditic reproduction (§11-3, printed p. 137): reproduction in animals where one individual carries both sexes.

[Picture — a simple diagram of one animal body carrying both a male and a female reproductive organ, the two labelled side by side in the single body]

## The key facts
**Both sexes, one body.** A hermaphrodite carries male and female reproductive organs in the same individual — one body holds both sets of gamete-making organs.

**A kind of reproduction.** Hermaphroditic reproduction is filed by Iraq among the types of reproduction in their own right — a closing section (§11-3) of the reproduction chapter, not a remark inside another topic.

**After parthenogenesis.** The section stands straight after parthenogenesis (§10-3): first young from unfertilised eggs, then the both-sexes body — the chapter's two closing special kinds of reproduction.

**Iraq's way.** Iraq's capstone gives hermaphroditism its own numbered section (Ch. 3 §11-3, printed p. 137) at the end of a chapter that works through reproduction kind by kind across viruses, protists, fungi, plants and animals.
