Today: Hermaphroditism in animals.

Remember this one thing: a hermaphrodite is an animal that carries both male and female reproductive organs in one body — and Iraq's Grade 12 Biology capstone teaches hermaphroditic reproduction (التكاثر الخنثي) as the closing section of its reproduction chapter, straight after parthenogenesis.

A hermaphrodite carries male and female reproductive organs in the same individual — one body holds both sets of gamete-making organs.

Hermaphroditic reproduction is filed by Iraq among the types of reproduction in their own right — a closing section (§11-3) of the reproduction chapter, not a remark inside another topic.

The section stands straight after parthenogenesis (§10-3): first young from unfertilised eggs, then the both-sexes body — the chapter's two closing special kinds of reproduction.

Iraq's capstone gives hermaphroditism its own numbered section (Ch. 3 §11-3, printed p. 137) at the end of a chapter that works through reproduction kind by kind across viruses, protists, fungi, plants and animals.

A hermaphrodite carries both male and female organs in a single body.

Iraq files it as a kind of reproduction in its own right.

It closes the reproduction chapter, straight after parthenogenesis.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
