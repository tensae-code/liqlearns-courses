# Hands-on — Risks of endogamy — consanguineous marriage

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: endogamy — marriage between relatives — raises the chance that harmful recessive alleles meet in the children: what can follow when two related individuals marry is set out as its own section of human heredity.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a family pedigree of two related parents with the shared ancestors marked, and a Punnett-style sketch showing a recessive allele arriving from both sides. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
