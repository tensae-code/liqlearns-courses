Today: Food preservation and storage.

Remember this one thing: harvests are wasted by incomplete and late harvesting, weather, pests and poor storage — and preservation fights the spoilage organisms: heating, freezing, and salting or sugaring, which preserve by osmosis, drawing water out of the food and its microbes.

The causes of wastage: incomplete harvesting, late harvesting, weather, pests, and poor storage — the harvest can be lost at every step after growing.

Poor storage wastes what harvesting saved — the book pictures yams in barns, where how the food is kept decides how much survives to be eaten.

Heating preserves by killing spoilage organisms; freezing preserves by halting their activity — two principles, heat and cold.

Salt and sugar preserve by osmosis: they draw water out of the food and out of the microorganisms on it, so the microbes cannot live and the food keeps.

Nigeria teaches food production and storage in Chapter 9 (printed p. 155): first the causes of harvest wastage, then the preservation methods with their principles — heating, freezing, salting and sugaring by osmosis.
