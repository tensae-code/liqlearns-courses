# Cloning and plant tissue culture

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Cloning and plant tissue culture. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
A clone is a living copy: a new living thing whose heredity comes from one single donor, not from two parents. Nothing is fertilised and nothing is mixed — the copy carries the same heredity as the one it was copied from. Morocco teaches cloning at the end of its reproduction unit by comparison: two cloning techniques set side by side, then cloning set against natural reproduction, until the principle of cloning can be deduced from the comparisons themselves.

[Picture — the two cloning techniques side by side: on one side an egg, on the other a specialised body cell taken from the mammary glands, each leading to a young animal that is a copy of a single donor]

## The key facts
**What cloning is.** Cloning produces a new living thing that is a copy — its heredity is the heredity of one donor, carried whole, with no fertilisation and no mixing of two parents.

**The egg-based technique.** One technique is based on an egg: the work starts from an egg cell, and the new individual develops from what is placed and grown from that starting point.

**The somatic-cell technique.** The other technique is based on a specialised somatic cell — an ordinary body cell, in the guide's worked example a specialised cell taken from the mammary glands. That one cell carries the donor's whole heredity, and the copy grows with that heredity.

**Cloning against natural reproduction.** In natural reproduction, male and female gametes meet at fertilisation and the offspring's heredity is a mixture of two parents — no two offspring are exact copies. In cloning there is one donor, no gametes meeting, and the result is a copy of that donor.

**The principle, deduced.** Morocco's learners deduce the principle from the comparison itself: a living thing can be copied because the heredity of the whole individual is carried in its cells — take the heredity from one cell of the donor, grow a new individual from it, and the new individual is the donor's copy. The same principle is what plant tissue culture uses: new plants grown from pieces or cells of one parent plant are copies of that plant.

**Morocco's way.** In Morocco's Grade 8 guide, cloning closes the reproduction unit: learners compare the two techniques, compare cloning with natural reproduction, and deduce the principle of cloning — the science is reached by the comparisons, document by document.
