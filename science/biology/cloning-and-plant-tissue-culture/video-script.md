Today: Cloning and plant tissue culture.

Remember this one thing: a clone is a living copy. Its heredity comes from one single donor — no fertilisation, no mixing of two parents.

Morocco teaches cloning by comparing two techniques. One is based on an egg. The other is based on a specialised somatic cell — a body cell, taken in the guide's example from the mammary glands. That one cell carries the donor's whole heredity.

Now compare cloning with natural reproduction. There, two parents' gametes meet and the heredity is mixed. In cloning, one donor's heredity is copied whole.

And the principle can be deduced from that comparison: the heredity of the whole living thing is carried in its cells — grow a new individual from one donor cell's heredity, and you get the donor's copy. Plant tissue culture works on the same principle: new plants grown from cells of one parent plant are that plant's copies.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
