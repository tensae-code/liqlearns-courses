# Hands-on — The greenhouse effect

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: the greenhouse effect is the atmosphere's blanket: sunlight warms the Earth, the Earth radiates heat back, and greenhouse gases absorb part of that heat instead of letting it escape — Greece's Grade 11 laboratory guide detects it qualitatively and quantitatively in a model experiment with warmed, measured air.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: two glass jars on a bench under a lamp, one with extra carbon dioxide, each with a thermometer, the carbon-dioxide jar's thermometer reading higher. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
