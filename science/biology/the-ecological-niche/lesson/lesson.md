# The ecological niche

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — The ecological niche. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it — why two very similar birds in one forest end up eating in different places — and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
A habitat is an address; a niche is a job and a life. North Macedonia's Biology I gives the ecological niche a named lesson of its own (Theme 5.11, printed p. 224), in its closing ecology theme after the population lesson. No lesson in this course taught the niche concept itself before, so this lesson is built for it.

[Picture — one tree drawn three ways: a bird hunting insects in the top branches, a bird feeding on the trunk, and a mouse eating fallen seeds under the tree — one address, three niches, marked with labels]

## The key facts
**The niche is the whole way of life.** An ecological niche is not just the place an organism lives. It is the place plus everything the organism does and needs there: its food, its enemies, its times of activity, its part in the community's web.

**Habitat is the address; niche is the life lived there.** Many kinds share one habitat — one tree, one pond — while holding different niches inside it: different foods, heights, hours. Sharing an address is common; sharing a whole life is not.

**Two kinds, one niche: competition.** When two kinds need the same niche in the same place, they compete for the same food and space, and the weaker competitor is pushed out or pushed to change — that is why similar kinds in one place are found doing slightly different jobs.

**Niches explain variety in one place.** A rich habitat holds many niches, and so many kinds: each kind's fittings — beak, teeth, roots, hours — are fittings to its niche, which ties this lesson back to adaptation.

**North Macedonia's way.** North Macedonia's Biology I closes its first year with the living-environment theme — energy flow, food webs, pyramids, the cycles, populations — and gives the ecological niche its own named lesson (Theme 5.11, printed p. 224) between the population lesson and the final lesson on ecological factors and relationships.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words: An organism's ecological niche is its whole way of life in its community — where it lives, what it eats and is eaten by, when it is active and what it does — and two kinds cannot hold the same niche in the same place for long. Use each key fact once. Then let your companion explain it back to you.
