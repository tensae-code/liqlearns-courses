# Hands-on — The ecological niche

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Pick one habitat you can see — a tree, a pond edge, a garden wall. List every kind of organism you can find there, and for each write its niche in one line: where exactly, eating what, active when. Look for two kinds that seem to share a niche, and watch longer until you find the difference.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — one tree drawn three ways: a bird hunting insects in the top branches, a bird feeding on the trunk, and a mouse eating fallen seeds under the tree — one address, three niches, marked with labels — and check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
