# Foundations of embryology

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Foundations of embryology. ## The idea
Every animal is a development before it is a body. North Macedonia's Grade 13 elective book closes its reproduction chapter with foundations of embryology as a named part (Ch. 3.III, printed pp. 145–151) — the science of the embryo itself, after reproduction in plants and animals. No lesson in this course was embryology itself before, so this lesson is built for it.

## The key facts
**Embryology studies development.** Embryology is the branch of biology that studies the embryo — the developing organism from the fertilised egg to birth or hatching. Its question is not what the body is, but how it comes to be.

**Cleavage starts the building.** The zygote divides rapidly — cleavage — into more and smaller cells, forming a solid ball and then a hollow ball (the blastula). No growth yet: the same material is cut into ever more pieces, each a cell that will take a fate.

**Gastrulation lays the layers.** The hollow ball folds inward — gastrulation — and the cells organise into germ layers: ectoderm outside, endoderm inside, and mesoderm between. This folding is the embryo's founding act of organisation.

**Every organ traces to a layer.** The germ layers are destiny: ectoderm gives skin and the nervous system; endoderm gives the gut and its glands; mesoderm gives muscle, bone, blood and the kidneys. Embryology reads the adult body backwards to these three beginnings.

**North Macedonia's way.** North Macedonia's elective book ends its reproduction chapter with foundations of embryology as its own closing part (Ch. 3.III, printed pp. 145–151), after plant and animal reproduction — reproduction is not finished, in this book's reckoning, until development itself has been founded.
