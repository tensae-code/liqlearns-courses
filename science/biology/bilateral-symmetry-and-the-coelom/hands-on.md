# Hands-on — Bilateral symmetry and the coelom

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: bilateral symmetry — a body with two matching sides and a front end — is an evolutionary advance tied to active movement, and bilateral animals are graded by the body cavity they carry: acoelomate (no coelom), pseudocoelomate (a false cavity) or coelomate (a true, lined coelom).

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a bilateral animal drawn from above with a dashed midline dividing its left and right sides and its front end marked, beside three cross-sections of worm-like bodies labelled acoelomate (solid, no cavity), pseudocoelomate (a false cavity) and coelomate (a true lined coelom). Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
