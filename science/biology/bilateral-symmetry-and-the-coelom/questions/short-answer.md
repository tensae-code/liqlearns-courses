# Bilateral symmetry and the coelom — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** State the one thing this lesson teaches and explain it.
**Answer: bilateral symmetry — a body with two matching sides and a front end — is an evolutionary advance tied to active movement, and bilateral animals are graded by the body cavity they carry: acoelomate (no coelom), pseudocoelomate (a false cavity) or coelomate (a true, lined coelom). Cuba's Grade 8 programme (Unit 4) teaches bilateral symmetry as an adaptive and evolutionary advantage, together with the coelom and the pseudocoelom (printed p. 17, teacher programme — evidence)**

**2.** Explain Cuba's way of teaching this.
**Answer: Cuba's Grade 8 animal course opens the bilateral story with symmetry and the coelom grades together — the programme, the current student text and the older student text all introduce bilateral animals that way, before the flatworms and the nemathelminths are taken up in turn.**
