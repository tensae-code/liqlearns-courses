# Hands-on — Adaptations — how organisms fit their environment

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: organisms carry adaptations — features that fit them to their environment — as Greece's Mediterranean plants show: pine and olive hold small, hard, needle-like leaves against dry summers while the plane tree by the water spreads broad leaves, so the same life function is fitted differently in dry and wet habitats.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a dry Greek hillside with a pine and an olive tree, their small hard leaves labelled, beside a plane tree by a stream with broad leaves, the two habitats marked dry and wet. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
