Today: Agricultural classification of crops.

Remember this one thing: plants can be classified two ways: botanically, by their natural relationships, and agriculturally, by what they are grown for — Nigeria sorts crops agriculturally into twelve groups by product, useful part and economic importance, with Nigerian crops throughout.

Plants are classified botanically, by relationship — and agriculturally, by product, useful part and economic importance. The farmer's sorting is a real classification too.

The agricultural groups are: root, vegetable, cereal, legume, fibre, oil, latex, beverage, fruit, spice, forage and cash crops.

The groups are filled with home crops: cassava and yam among the roots, guinea corn among the cereals, benniseed, kola among the beverage crops, and oil palm among the oil crops.

A crop's group is decided by what is harvested and what it earns — the useful part and its economic importance — not by its flower or family.

Nigeria opens its agriculture chapter (Chapter 9, printed p. 137) by classifying crops agriculturally into twelve groups with Nigerian examples — biology put straight to work on the farm.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
