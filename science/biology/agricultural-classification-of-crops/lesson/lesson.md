# Agricultural classification of crops

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Agricultural classification of crops. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Classification is not only for wild things. Chapter 9 of Nigeria's Senior Secondary Biology 1 — the relevance of Biology to agriculture — classifies plants twice over: botanically, and agriculturally, by product, useful part and economic importance. The agricultural sorting gives twelve groups: root, vegetable, cereal, legume, fibre, oil, latex, beverage, fruit, spice, forage and cash crops — with Nigerian examples throughout: cassava, yam, guinea corn, benniseed, kola and oil palm.

## The key facts
**Two classifications.** Plants are classified botanically, by relationship — and agriculturally, by product, useful part and economic importance. The farmer's sorting is a real classification too.

**Twelve agricultural groups.** The agricultural groups are: root, vegetable, cereal, legume, fibre, oil, latex, beverage, fruit, spice, forage and cash crops.

**Nigerian examples.** The groups are filled with home crops: cassava and yam among the roots, guinea corn among the cereals, benniseed, kola among the beverage crops, and oil palm among the oil crops.

**Sorted by use.** A crop's group is decided by what is harvested and what it earns — the useful part and its economic importance — not by its flower or family.

**Nigeria's way.** Nigeria opens its agriculture chapter (Chapter 9, printed p. 137) by classifying crops agriculturally into twelve groups with Nigerian examples — biology put straight to work on the farm.
