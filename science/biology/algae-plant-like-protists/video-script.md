Today: Algae — plant-like protists.

Remember this one thing: Algae are the plant-like protists: they carry chlorophyll and make their own food by photosynthesis, but they have no true roots, stems or leaves.

Algae sit inside the protista because most are simple and many are single-celled, but they feed the plant way: their cells contain chlorophyll, so they make their own food by photosynthesis and release oxygen.

Even the biggest seaweed is a simple body — a holdfast, a stalk-like part and leaf-like blades — not true roots, stems and leaves with veins and tissues. This simplicity is what keeps algae among the protists and simple plants, not with the true plants.

Some algae are one cell alone, some live as filaments or colonies, and the brown algae of cold seas grow into kelp many metres long. Green, brown and red algae are told apart by the pigments that colour them.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
