# Hands-on — Seed dormancy and how it breaks

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Take the lesson's key facts for a walk: find one example of this lesson's idea in your own surroundings — your home, street, market or school — and say which key fact it matches, and why.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory — a wattle seed drawn three ways — whole and sleeping behind its hard coat, split by a crack, and sprouting — with the three keys that wake it drawn beside it: a pot of boiling water, a flame for roasting, and a file for scarification — and a small clock behind the sleeping seed showing that waiting itself is one of its causes ending — label its parts, and check against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
