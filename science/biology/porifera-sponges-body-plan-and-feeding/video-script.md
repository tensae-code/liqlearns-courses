Today: Porifera — sponges: body plan and feeding.

Remember this one thing: sponges are the simplest animals: they have no true tissues, stay fixed in one place, and feed by driving water through their bodies — in through many small pores and out through one big opening, the osculum.

A sponge has no mouth and no stomach, and it never moves. Instead, it lets the water do the work. Water flows in through tiny pores all over its wall. Inside, special cells with little whips — the choanocytes — beat and keep the current moving, and food is caught from the water as it passes. The water leaves through one big opening at the top, the osculum.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
