## The key facts
**Without decomposers.** Without decomposers, nutrients such as nitrogen, carbon and oxygen — which exist in limited amounts — would stay locked in dead matter.

**Four processes.** The four bacterial processes of the nitrogen cycle are nitrogen fixation, nitrification, ammonification and denitrification.

**Carbon.** Decomposition releases carbon through bacterial action.

**Useful bacteria.** Useful bacteria support biofuel and biofertiliser production and food processing such as milk, set against the harmful, pathogenic minority.

## From the student book — the pupil-taught core
The student book teaches cycling as the opposite of energy flow: materials go round. Its nitrogen cycle runs through air, soil bacteria, plants and animals, with legume root nodules housing the fixers.

[Picture 22290 — the nitrogen cycle with the four bacterial processes labelled at their stations, and a legume root with nodules as an inset]

### Student-book key facts
**Materials cycle.** Unlike energy, materials cycle — decomposers return dead matter's substances to the soil and air.

**Four processes.** Fixation, nitrification, ammonification and denitrification are the nitrogen cycle's bacterial processes.

**Root nodules.** Legume root nodules house the nitrogen-fixing bacteria.

**Fertility link.** Nitrogen, soil bacteria and soil fertility are tied together in the cycle.
