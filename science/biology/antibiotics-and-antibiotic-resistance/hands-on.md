# Hands-on — Antibiotics and antibiotic resistance

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: antibiotics kill bacteria — Fleming's discovery of penicillin in 1928 opened the antibiotic age — but bacteria evolve resistance, so an antibiotic that once cured can fail, as with MRSA.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a petri dish with a penicillin disc and a clear killed zone around it, beside a second dish where resistant bacteria grow right up to the disc, labelled resistance. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
