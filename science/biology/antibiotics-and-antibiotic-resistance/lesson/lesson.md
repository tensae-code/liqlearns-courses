# Antibiotics and antibiotic resistance

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Antibiotics and antibiotic resistance. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Antibiotics kill bacteria — Fleming's discovery of penicillin in 1928 opened the antibiotic age — but bacteria evolve resistance, so an antibiotic that once cured can fail, as with MRSA. No lesson in this course taught this on its own before, so this lesson is built for it — from Kazakhstan's Grade 7–9 biology books, which teach it as a named topic.

[Picture — a petri dish with a penicillin disc and a clear killed zone around it, beside a second dish where resistant bacteria grow right up to the disc, labelled resistance]
