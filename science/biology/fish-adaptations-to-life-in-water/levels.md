# Fish adaptations to life in water — depth

This file is the lesson's depth record. This lesson has no sub lessons — it is one whole, single-skill lesson.

Whole-lesson pass mark: the learner can state the one thing this lesson teaches (A fish's body shows how it is adapted to life in water: a streamlined shape for moving through water, fins for movement and balance, gills for taking oxygen from the water, a swim bladder for controlling depth, and the lateral line for sensing movement in the water around it. In Finland's materials this is the driving question of the whole fish lesson — “how does the fish's structure show it is adapted to water?” — posed BEFORE the dissection and answered after it.) and explain each key fact in their own words. Practice deepens by matching each adaptation — shape, fins, gills, swim bladder, lateral line — to the job it does in the water, without looking.
