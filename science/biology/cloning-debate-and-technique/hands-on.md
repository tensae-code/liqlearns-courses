# Hands-on — Cloning — debate and technique

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: cloning makes new living things that are genetic copies of a single parent — and because the technique copies life itself, it is taught together with the debate about what it should and should not be used for.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: one parent plant and three exactly alike young copies joined by dotted arrows, with a tick speech bubble and a question-mark speech bubble above. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
