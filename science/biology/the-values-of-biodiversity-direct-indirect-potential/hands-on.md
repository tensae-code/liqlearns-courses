# Hands-on — The values of biodiversity — direct, indirect, potential

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: biodiversity — held at three levels, genetic, species and ecosystem — has value in three kinds: direct value in food, fibres and medicines such as artemisinin, indirect value in carbon fixed, soil and floodwater held and climate steadied, stated to far exceed the direct, and potential value in the uses nobody knows yet.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: three baskets under a tree of many species — one with food and a medicine bottle, one with held soil and stored floodwater, one closed with a question mark. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
