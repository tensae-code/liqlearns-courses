# The values of biodiversity — direct, indirect, potential

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — The values of biodiversity — direct, indirect, potential. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Biodiversity — held at three levels, genetic, species and ecosystem — has value in three kinds: direct value in food, fibres and medicines such as artemisinin, indirect value in carbon fixed, soil and floodwater held and climate steadied, stated to far exceed the direct, and potential value in the uses nobody knows yet. No lesson in this course taught this on its own before, so this lesson is built for it — from China's People's Education Press biology textbooks, which teach it as a named topic.

[Picture — three baskets under a tree of many species — one with food and a medicine bottle, one with held soil and stored floodwater, one closed with a question mark]

## The key facts
**Ch. 4 §2 The value of biodiversity.** biodiversity defined at three levels (genetic, species, ecosystem), then its value in three kinds: direct (food, fibres, and medicines — Tu Youyou's artemisinin from sweet wormwood pictured), indirect (photosynthesis fixing carbon and releasing oxygen, forests and wetlands holding soil, storing floodwater and steadying climate — stated to far exceed the direct value), and potential (the uses nobody knows yet).
