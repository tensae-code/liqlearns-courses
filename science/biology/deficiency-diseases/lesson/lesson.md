# Deficiency diseases — what happens when a nutrient is missing

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Deficiency diseases — what happens when a nutrient is missing. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Marasmus is severe energy shortage; kwashiorkor is protein shortage in children; scurvy is lack of vitamin C; vitamin D lets the body absorb calcium and phosphorus for bones and teeth, and skin can make it in morning or evening sunlight.

## The key facts
**Marasmus.** Marasmus is severe energy shortage — extreme thinness with skin hanging in folds — treated with an energy-rich balanced diet.

**Kwashiorkor.** Kwashiorkor is protein shortage in children — flaky skin, swollen legs and abdomen, hair colour change, weakness and irritability.

**Vitamin D.** Vitamin D is needed to absorb calcium and phosphorus for bone and teeth, and is the one vitamin the body can make in skin in sunlight.

**Scurvy.** Scurvy is caused by lack of vitamin C.
