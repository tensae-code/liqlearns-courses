# Hands-on — Coevolution — arms races and partnerships

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
On a big sheet of paper, draw two circles of arrows. In the first circle put a parasite and its host, and write one improvement on each arrow — the parasite holds on better; the host shakes it off better; round and round. In the second circle put a flower and its pollinator, and write the fitting on each arrow. Label the first circle ARMS RACE and the second PARTNERSHIP.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — the two circles — label it (coevolution, arms race, partnership), and check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap. End with this one together: Costa Rica's book puts coevolution between the symbiotic relationships and the parasites — why does that order make sense? Say why.
