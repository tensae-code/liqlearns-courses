# Coevolution — arms races and partnerships — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** Explain: the two ways coevolution runs.
**Answer: Where the relationship harms one side — predator and prey, parasite and host — coevolution is an arms race: a better defence selects a better attack, which selects a better defence again. Where the relationship benefits both sides — the symbiotic relationships — coevolution is a partnership: the two species come to fit each other ever more closely, as a flower and its pollinator do. In both, neither species evolves alone; each is the other's moving environment.**

**2.** Explain: Costa Rica's way of teaching this lesson.
**Answer: Biología Undécimo teaches Coevolución as a topic of Unit 1, its ecological-relationships unit — printed p. 28, straight after the symbiotic relationships (printed p. 26) and straight before the parasitology strand (printed pp. 29–33). Coevolution is presented as what relationships do over time, not as a separate evolution topic: the relationships come first in the unit, and coevolution is their long-term result.**
