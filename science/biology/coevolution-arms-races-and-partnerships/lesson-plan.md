# Lesson plan — Coevolution — arms races and partnerships

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Biology · **Lesson:** Coevolution — arms races and partnerships
**Place in the curriculum:** Biology — appended to Unit 16 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** species that live in close relationship shape each other's evolution — coevolution — running two ways: as an arms race where the relationship harms, and as a partnership where both sides come to fit each other.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. The relationships lessons and natural selection are recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: **Relationships where someone loses**, **Relationships where no one loses** and **Natural selection**. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words, the one thing this lesson teaches: species that live in close relationship shape each other's evolution — coevolution — running two ways: as an arms race where the relationship harms, and as a partnership where both sides come to fit each other.
2. Say where Costa Rica's anthology puts coevolution: inside Unit 1's ecological relationships, after the symbiotic relationships, printed p. 28.
3. Explain the arms-race form: each side's improvement selects the other side's counter-improvement.
4. Explain the partnership form: two species that benefit each other come to fit each other ever more closely.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.
