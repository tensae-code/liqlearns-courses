Today: coevolution — arms races and partnerships.

Remember this one thing: species that live closely together do not evolve alone. Each one is part of the other's world — so each one shapes what the other becomes.

Costa Rica's biology book teaches this inside its relationships unit, at printed page 28 — straight after the symbiotic relationships, straight before the parasites. That order is the idea: first the relationships, then what the relationships do over time.

When the relationship harms — predator and prey, parasite and host — it runs as an arms race: better defence, better attack, better defence again. When the relationship benefits both sides, it runs as a partnership: the two come to fit each other, like a flower and its pollinator.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
