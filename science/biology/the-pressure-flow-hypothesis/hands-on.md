# Hands-on — The pressure-flow hypothesis

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Squeeze one end of a water-filled soft tube and watch water push out of the other end: pressure applied at one end drives flow along the tube, as loading at a source drives phloem flow toward a sink.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — source leaf, phloem tube, sink, with the loading, water entry, flow arrows and unloading marked — and check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
