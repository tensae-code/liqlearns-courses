Today: Savannah grasslands and derived savannah.

Remember this one thing: grassland is the world's great open habitat — prairies, steppes, pampas and veldt are its local names — and Nigeria's savannah is its home form; derived savannah is special: grassland that people maintain by farming and bush burning inside the rain-forest climate, which grows back to forest when the pressure stops.

Grassland is known around the world by local names: prairies, steppes, pampas and veldt — the same open grass habitat on different continents.

The savannah is Nigeria's own grassland, in its types across the country — grass with scattered trees, under a climate of distinct wet and dry seasons.

Derived savannah is grassland inside the rain-forest climate zone — land that climate alone would cover with forest.

Farming and bush burning hold derived savannah open: the pressure of cultivation and fire stops the forest returning, year after year.

Remove the human pressure and derived savannah reverts to forest — the proof that this grassland is maintained, not climatic.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
