# Savannah grasslands and derived savannah

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Savannah grasslands and derived savannah. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Chapter 14 of Nigeria's Senior Secondary Biology 1 teaches grassland widely first: its characteristics, and its names around the world — prairies, steppes, pampas, veldt. Then it comes home to Nigeria's savannah types. And it names a grassland found nowhere in nature's plan: derived savannah — grassland inside the rain-forest climate, kept open by people's farming and bush burning. Stop the pressure, and it reverts to forest: it is savannah by human maintenance, not by climate.

[Picture — open savannah with scattered trees and tall grass, a farm plot and a line of bush fire at its edge, and behind them the rain forest the land would return to]

## The key facts
**One habitat, many names.** Grassland is known around the world by local names: prairies, steppes, pampas and veldt — the same open grass habitat on different continents.

**Nigeria's savannah types.** The savannah is Nigeria's own grassland, in its types across the country — grass with scattered trees, under a climate of distinct wet and dry seasons.

**Derived savannah.** Derived savannah is grassland inside the rain-forest climate zone — land that climate alone would cover with forest.

**Maintained by people.** Farming and bush burning hold derived savannah open: the pressure of cultivation and fire stops the forest returning, year after year.

**It reverts.** Remove the human pressure and derived savannah reverts to forest — the proof that this grassland is maintained, not climatic.
