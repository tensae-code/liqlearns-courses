# Insect products — honey, silk, dyes and medicines

## Do it first
Before you read: think of one living thing near your home that shows this lesson's idea — insect products. Hold it in your mind, then read on and check your idea against what the lesson teaches.

## Sub lesson 1 — Beneficial insects — industry, health and medicine

Commercial insect products: honey and beeswax, silk, lac, cochineal colouring for food, drink, cosmetics and paint, and tannic acid from wasp galls for leather dyeing and ink; in medicine, honey for burns and wounds and bee and ant venom for joint pain are the traditional uses named, with bee products' wound-healing support noted.

## Sub lesson 2 — Added Ethiopian investigations — silk and insect medicine

Two locality investigations the student book does not set: whether silkworms are kept locally and how silk fibre is produced from them in Ethiopia, and interviewing traditional healers about whether they use insects or insect products to treat human and animal diseases and what indigenous knowledge lies behind it.
