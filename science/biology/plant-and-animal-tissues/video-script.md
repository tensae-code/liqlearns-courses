Today: Plant and animal tissues.

Remember this one thing: a tissue is a group of similar cells working together at one job — Yemen's Grade 11 book teaches the animal tissues first (epithelial, connective, muscular, nervous) and then the plant tissues (permanent, and compound permanent — the vascular tissues), and its activities book makes the learner look at each kind under the microscope.

A tissue is a group of similar cells doing one job together — the level between the cell and the organ.

The animal tissues open with epithelial tissue — the covering and lining kind. Simple epithelial tissue is one layer; compound, stratified epithelial tissue is layered.

Connective tissue binds and supports — the skeletal tissues are studied among its kinds.

Muscular tissue does the moving, and nervous tissue does the sensing and signalling.

Then the plant tissues: permanent tissues, and the compound permanent tissues — the vascular tissues that carry water and food through the plant. The practicals start from meristematic tissue, the plant's growing tissue.

Yemen teaches tissues as one unit across both kingdoms — animals first, plants second — and its activities book makes you look at every kind under the microscope.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
