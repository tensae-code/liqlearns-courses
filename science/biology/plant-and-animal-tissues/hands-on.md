# Hands-on — Plant and animal tissues

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find one example of each tissue job in or around your own body and a plant nearby: a covering (your skin's surface — epithelial), a support (a bone or a plant stem — connective / plant permanent tissue), a mover (a muscle), a signaller (the feeling in a fingertip — nervous). Say aloud, for each one, which tissue kind it is and what job the cells are doing together.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — microscope views of the tissue kinds in a row: a flat epithelial sheet, connective tissue with long fibres, striped muscle tissue, a nerve cell with long fibres, and a cut plant stem showing vascular bundles — and check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
