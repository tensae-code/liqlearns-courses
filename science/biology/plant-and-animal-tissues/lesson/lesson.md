# Plant and animal tissues

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Plant and animal tissues. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
One cell can only do so much. Bodies are built when similar cells team up: a tissue. Unit 2 of Yemen's Grade 11 Biology and Earth Science book is the tissues unit, and it teaches the two kingdoms in one run — the animal tissues first: epithelial (printed p. 25), connective (p. 31), muscular (p. 39) and nervous (p. 42); then the plant tissues: permanent tissues (p. 45) and compound permanent (vascular) tissues (p. 49). Yemen's Grade 11 activities book turns the same unit into six microscope practicals — simple epithelial, compound epithelial, skeletal, muscle, meristematic and permanent tissues.

[Picture — microscope views of the tissue kinds in a row: a flat epithelial sheet, connective tissue with long fibres, striped muscle tissue, a nerve cell with long fibres, and a cut plant stem showing vascular bundles]

## The key facts
**What a tissue is.** A tissue is a group of similar cells, alike in build, doing one job together — the level of organisation between the cell and the organ.

**Epithelial tissue.** The animal tissues open with epithelial tissue (printed p. 25) — the covering and lining kind. The practicals separate simple epithelial tissue (activities, printed p. 12) from compound, stratified epithelial tissue (p. 15).

**Connective tissue.** Connective tissue (الضامة/الرابطة) follows (printed p. 31) — the binding and supporting kind; the activities book studies the skeletal tissues among them (p. 19).

**Muscle and nervous tissue.** Muscular tissue (printed p. 39) does the moving, and nervous tissue (printed p. 42) the sensing and signalling; muscle tissues are examined in the practicals (p. 21).

**Plant tissues.** The plant tissues come second in the unit: permanent tissues (printed p. 45) and compound permanent (vascular) tissues (p. 49). The practicals begin from meristematic tissues in plants (p. 24) and end at permanent tissues (p. 27).

**Yemen's way.** Yemen teaches tissues as one unit across both kingdoms — animals first, then plants — in Unit 2 of its Grade 11 textbook, and its Grade 11 activities book keys six microscope practicals (Activities 3–8) to exactly the same ground.
