Today: Growth curves — sigmoid and intermittent growth.

Remember this one thing: growth can be measured and drawn as a curve: many organisms grow in an S-shaped sigmoid curve, but insects grow in steps — an intermittent, stepped curve — because they can only grow when they shed their hard outer skeleton.

Measure size against time and growth becomes a curve. The common shape is the sigmoid — an S-curve: slow at first, fast in the middle, levelling off at full size.

An insect's growth curve is stepped — intermittent (the book's Fig. 4.12). The hard outer skeleton cannot stretch, so growth happens in jumps at each moult and the graph climbs like stairs.

Marking experiments — marks placed along a root or stem — show which regions grow fastest: the regions where the marks spread furthest apart.

Growth depends on environmental factors — the book lists the conditions an organism needs around it for growth to proceed.

Nigeria teaches growth in Chapter 4 as something measured, graphed and experimented on — the sigmoid curve, the insect's stepped curve (Fig. 4.12), marking experiments on roots and stems, and the environmental factors growth needs (printed p. 56).
