Today: Haploid and diploid.

Remember this one thing: Cells carry their chromosomes in sets: a diploid nucleus holds two sets (2n) — one from each parent — while a haploid nucleus holds one set (n); body cells are diploid, gametes are haploid, and fertilisation joins two haploid cells back to diploid.

Chromosomes travel in sets. A body cell holds two sets — one from each parent — and is called diploid, 2n.

The gametes hold only one set — haploid, n — because meiosis halved the number. At fertilisation the two haploid cells join, the number doubles back to 2n, and the kind's chromosome number stays constant across the generations.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
