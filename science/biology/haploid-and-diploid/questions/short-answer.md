# Haploid and diploid — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** Explain: why the chromosome number stays the same across generations.
**Answer: Body cells are diploid (2n). Meiosis halves the number in the gametes, making them haploid (n). At fertilisation two haploid gametes join, restoring the diploid number in the zygote, and mitosis keeps it diploid in every body cell — so each generation has the same number.**

**2.** Explain: North Macedonia's way of teaching this.
**Answer: North Macedonia's Biology I gives the haploid and diploid nucleus a named lesson of its own (Theme 2.7, printed p. 66), inside the foundations-of-genetics theme between chromosome structure, the karyotype lessons and the cell cycle.**
