# Haploid and diploid

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Haploid and diploid. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it — why your body cells have twice the chromosomes of the cells that made you — and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Chromosomes come in sets, and counting the sets explains how heredity survives reproduction. North Macedonia's Biology I gives the haploid and diploid nucleus a named lesson of its own (Theme 2.7, printed p. 66), inside the genetics theme between the chromosome and karyotype lessons and the cell cycle. No lesson in this course taught ploidy itself before, so this lesson is built for it.

[Picture — a diploid cell with its chromosomes in matching pairs, one of each pair marked as from the mother and one from the father, beside a haploid gamete carrying only one of each kind, and the two gametes joining into a diploid zygote]

## The key facts
**Chromosomes come in homologous pairs.** In a body cell, chromosomes exist as pairs: two chromosomes of the same kind, carrying genes for the same features in the same order — one member of each pair came from the mother, one from the father.

**Diploid means two sets — 2n.** A nucleus with both members of every pair holds two full sets of chromosomes. It is called diploid, written 2n. Nearly all the cells of the body are diploid.

**Haploid means one set — n.** A nucleus with only one member of each pair holds a single set. It is called haploid, written n. The gametes — sperm and eggs — are haploid, made by meiosis, the division that halves the chromosome number.

**Fertilisation restores diploid.** At fertilisation a haploid sperm and a haploid egg join: n + n = 2n. The zygote is diploid, and every body cell built from it by mitosis stays diploid — so the chromosome number of the kind stays the same generation after generation.

**North Macedonia's way.** North Macedonia's Biology I teaches ploidy as a named lesson (Theme 2.7, printed p. 66) inside its foundations-of-genetics theme — after nucleic acids, replication, the genetic code and chromosome structure, and before the karyotype, the cell cycle and division — so the learner counts chromosome sets before watching them divide.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words: Cells carry their chromosomes in sets: a diploid nucleus holds two sets (2n) — one from each parent — while a haploid nucleus holds one set (n); body cells are diploid, gametes are haploid, and fertilisation joins two haploid cells back to diploid. Use each key fact once. Then let your companion explain it back to you.
