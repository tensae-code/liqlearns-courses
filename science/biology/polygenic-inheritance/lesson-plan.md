# Lesson plan — Polygenic inheritance

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Biology · **Lesson:** Polygenic inheritance
**Place in the curriculum:** Biology — appended to Unit 16 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** some traits are not controlled by one gene but by many genes together — polygenes, multiple-gene inheritance — and Sudan's Grade 12 book teaches polygenes as §5 of its genetics unit (printed p. 170).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands after the genetics lessons on Mendel's laws, modified ratios and multiple alleles — Sudan's own order in Unit Two — and adds the case those lessons do not cover: the trait whose heredity is shared among many genes. Mendel and the seven pea traits is recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: **Mendel and the seven pea traits** and **Co-dominance, incomplete dominance and blood groups**. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words, the one thing this lesson teaches: some traits are inherited through many genes together — polygenes — not through a single gene.
2. Say how polygenic inheritance differs from the single-gene inheritance of Mendel's pea traits.
3. Say why a polygenic trait shows many gradations in a population instead of two or three neat kinds.
4. Explain Sudan's way as the placements describe it: polygenes are §5 of the Grade 12 genetics unit, after modified Mendelian ratios and multiple alleles, and before sex determination (printed p. 170).
