# Polygenic inheritance — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** Explain: why a polygenic trait grades instead of splitting into neat kinds.
**Answer: Each of the many genes contributes a small part, and offspring inherit many different combinations of those contributions. The sums therefore come out at many different levels, so the trait runs in gradations from one extreme to the other instead of falling into the two or three separate kinds a single gene produces.**

**2.** Explain: Sudan's way of teaching this.
**Answer: Sudan's Grade 12 biology book teaches polygenes as §5 of its genetics unit (Unit Two, printed p. 170) — after Mendel's laws, the modifications of Mendelian ratios (§3) and multiple alleles (§4), and before sex determination (§6): the step from one gene, to changed ratios, to many alleles of one gene, to many genes for one trait.**
