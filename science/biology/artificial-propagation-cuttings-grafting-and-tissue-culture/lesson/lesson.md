## The idea
farmers propagating plants from cuttings and grafts, protoplast culture, and apomixis in flowering plants

[Picture — three panels: a cutting with roots forming in soil, a graft union bound on a stem, and a laboratory dish of protoplast culture — each labelled with its method]

## The key facts
**Cuttings and grafts.** Kuwait's Grade 11 Term 1 student book (Ch. 2, printed pp. 77–81) teaches artificial propagation as farmers practise it: propagating plants from cuttings and from grafts.

**Protoplast culture.** The laboratory method in the same run is protoplast culture — growing plants from cells stripped of their walls.

**Apomixis.** The book closes the topic with apomixis in flowering plants: seed formed without fertilisation, a natural cloning filed beside the artificial methods.

**Kuwait's way.** Kuwait teaches propagation as a farmer's and a laboratory's toolkit side by side (Grade 11, Ch. 2): cuttings and grafting first as practised methods, protoplast culture as the modern extension, apomixis as the plant's own version.
