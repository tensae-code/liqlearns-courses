# Hands-on — Elements of life in the body

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: a living body is built from chemical elements — macroelements taken in bulk and microelements in traces — each with its own role in the life of organisms, from the carbon and oxygen of every molecule to the iron in blood.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a human figure with a pie chart beside it of the body's main elements — oxygen, carbon, hydrogen, nitrogen — and a small row of trace elements below, iron among them labelled for blood. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
