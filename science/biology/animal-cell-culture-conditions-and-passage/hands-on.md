# Hands-on — Animal cell culture — conditions and passage

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: animal cells can be grown outside the body in a dish, if the culture gives them a suitable medium with serum, sterility, body temperature, the right pH and the right gases — and a grown culture is passaged by trypsinising it into single cells that attach and divide again.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a culture flask of animal cells on a dish under a microscope, with the conditions labelled around it — medium with serum, 36.5 °C, pH 7.2–7.4, 95% air with 5% CO₂. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
