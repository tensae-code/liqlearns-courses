# Hands-on — Enzyme cofactors and coenzymes

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: many enzymes work only with a helper: a cofactor — often a metal ion — or an organic coenzyme, many of them made from vitamins, sits with the enzyme and takes part in the reaction it speeds.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: an enzyme drawn with an empty helper socket, a small metal ion and a vitamin-shaped coenzyme clicking into place before the substrate fits. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
