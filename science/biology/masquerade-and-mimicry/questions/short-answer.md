# Masquerade and mimicry — the hoverfly that imitates a bee — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** Explain: how does imitating a bee protect an animal that cannot sting?
**Answer: The bee is genuinely defended — predators learn, from being stung or from the warning pattern, to leave bees alone. Eristalis tenax copies that pattern without having a sting, so predators leave it alone too: the mimic borrows the model's protection without paying the cost of the real defence. The trick depends on real bees existing — their stings are what teach the predators respect.**

**2.** Explain: Costa Rica's way of teaching this lesson.
**Answer: Biología Undécimo does not open with a definition of mimicry. It opens the whole five-unit ecology course with one case as a Lectura Reflexiva (reflective reading): Eristalis tenax, "Un caso muy especial de mimetismo", printed p. 14. The learner meets the single special case first, and the formal topics of Unit 1 — the ecological relationships, coevolution, parasites — come after.**
