Today: masquerade and mimicry — the hoverfly that imitates a bee.

Remember this one thing: a harmless animal can survive by copying a dangerous one's warning signs.

Costa Rica's biology book opens with exactly one animal: Eristalis tenax. It looks like a bee — the same bands, the same hairy body. But it is a hoverfly. A fly. It has no sting at all.

Why does the copy work? Because real bees do sting, and predators have learned to leave that pattern alone. The fly borrows the bee's reputation — protection without a weapon.

And notice Costa Rica's way: the whole course starts with this one case, as a reading to think about — "Un caso muy especial de mimetismo", a very special case of mimicry. The story first. The definitions after.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
