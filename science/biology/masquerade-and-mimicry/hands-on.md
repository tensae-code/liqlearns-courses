# Hands-on — Masquerade and mimicry — the hoverfly that imitates a bee

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Draw two insects side by side: a bee and the hoverfly Eristalis tenax. Give both the same banded body. Then label the difference the drawing cannot show: the bee has a sting; the hoverfly has none. Under the drawing write the book's own title for the case: "Un caso muy especial de mimetismo".

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — the model and the mimic side by side — label it (bee — the model, really defended; Eristalis tenax — the mimic, no sting), and check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap. End with this one together: if there were no real bees left, what would happen to the hoverfly's protection? Say why.
