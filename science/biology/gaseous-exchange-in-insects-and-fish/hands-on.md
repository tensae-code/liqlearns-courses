# Hands-on — Gaseous exchange in insects and fish

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Take the lesson's key facts for a walk: find one example of this lesson's idea in your own surroundings — your home, street, market or school — and say which key fact it matches, and why.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory — two animals side by side — a grasshopper opened to show silvery tracheal tubes branching from valved spiracles along its side, its abdomen pumping — and a fish head with the operculum lifted to show red gill filaments on their bars, arrows of water flowing one way across the filaments and blood the other way inside them — label its parts, and check against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
