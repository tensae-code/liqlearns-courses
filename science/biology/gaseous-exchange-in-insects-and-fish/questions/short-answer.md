# Gaseous exchange in insects and fish — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** Describe gaseous exchange in an insect, as Kenya's Form 2 book teaches it.
**Answer: Air enters through spiracles fitted with valves and hairs into a branching tracheal system that delivers it to the tissues, with abdominal pumping driving the flow; aquatic forms breathe through a siphon (mosquito larva), trumpets (mosquito pupa), tracheal gills (mayfly) or a plastron air film (Ch. 2, printed p. 63).**

**2.** Explain counter-current flow in a fish's gill and why it matters.
**Answer: The buccal/opercular pumping drives water one way across the gill filaments while blood inside the filaments flows the opposite way, so along the whole filament the blood keeps meeting water with more oxygen than itself and exchange stays efficient; Kenya teaches it with a gill dissection practical (Ch. 2, printed p. 66).**
