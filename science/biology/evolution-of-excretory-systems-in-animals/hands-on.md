# Hands-on — Evolution of excretory systems in animals

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: excretory systems evolve along the animal line — from contractile vacuoles and worm tubules to the vertebrate kidney — while the waste itself changes with habitat: ammonia where water is plentiful, urea, and uric acid where water must be saved.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a row of animals in evolutionary order — a single-celled organism, a flatworm, an earthworm, a fish and a bird — each with its excretory structure drawn enlarged and its waste labelled ammonia, urea or uric acid. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
