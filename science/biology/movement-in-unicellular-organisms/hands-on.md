# Hands-on — Movement in unicellular organisms

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: single-celled organisms move with what they have: an amoeba flows by pseudopodia — temporary feet of streaming cytoplasm — while others row with many short cilia or lash one or two long flagella, each method fitted to life at microscopic size.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: three microscope circles side by side: an amoeba reaching out a blunt pseudopod, a slipper-shaped cell fringed with cilia, and a cell trailing one long flagellum. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
