# Hands-on — The cytoskeleton

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: the cytoskeleton is the cell's inner scaffolding of protein fibres — microtubules, microfilaments and intermediate filaments — holding its shape, moving its organelles and pulling its chromosomes apart in division.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a cell drawn with three kinds of inner ropes in different colours — thick tubes, thin threads and tough cables — one tube shown pulling a chromosome. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
