# Hands-on — Birds — structure and adaptations for flight

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: birds are built for flight: beaks matched to diet, no teeth, a highly efficient digestive system, air sacs driving air through the lungs in one path, nitrogen waste converted to uric acid, and long bones strong yet light with struts and air cavities.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a bird in flight with its adaptations labelled: beak, air sacs and lungs with the one-way air path drawn, and a cut long bone showing struts and air cavities inside. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
