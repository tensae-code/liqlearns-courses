# Biological control of pests

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Biological control of pests. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
A pest is a living population, so it can be fought with living means. Vietnam's Grade 12 study topics devote a whole topic to biological control (Topic 2). Bài 5 defines it — using natural enemies or biological products to shrink or wipe out a pest population — and states its limits as honestly as its promise: it is slow, it cannot answer an outbreak, and it is costly, which is why it sits inside integrated pest management rather than replacing every other measure. Its scientific bases come in three kinds: ecological, genetic (the resistance genes of Bài 5, 2b) and physiological (the pheromone work of 2a and 3). Bài 6 then sets the measures out as a system, and Bài 7 sends students out to survey biological control where they live. No lesson in this course took biological control as its one thing before, so this lesson is built for it.

## The key facts
**The definition and the limits.** Biological control uses natural enemies or biological products to shrink or wipe out a pest population. Vietnam's book states the limits in the same breath: slow, unable to answer an outbreak, and costly — a part of integrated pest management, not a magic replacement for it.

**Sterile males — the SIT.** The sterile-insect technique irradiates male pests with X or gamma rays and releases them to mate the population down. It is used against Aedes aegypti (the Zika mosquito) and, in Vietnam, against the oriental fruit fly Bactrocera dorsalis in the dragon-fruit regions of Vĩnh Long and the Central Highlands, combined with pheromone attractant traps.

**Resistance genes.** The genetic basis: Bt toxin genes moved into maize, cotton and potato make the crop itself kill its pests, and a glyphosate-resistance enzyme gene from Agrobacterium, moved into soybean, maize and cotton, lets the crop survive the weedkiller that clears its weeds.

**Keeping the allies.** The measures form a system — six measures in Bài 6 — and conserving natural enemies is itself a six-step research workflow: survey the enemies, assess their role, study their ecology, protect their habitat, grow their population, and supply specimens for release. Trichoderma fungus mixed into organic fertiliser is the book's worked practice.
