# Biological control of pests — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** Give the sterile-insect technique as Vietnam's topics teach it, with its Vietnamese case.
**Answer: Males of the pest are irradiated with X or gamma rays so they are sterile, then released in numbers; matings with them produce no offspring and the population falls. It is used against Aedes aegypti (Zika) and in Vietnam against the oriental fruit fly Bactrocera dorsalis in the dragon-fruit regions of Vĩnh Long and the Central Highlands, combined with pheromone attractant traps (Topic 2, Bài 5, 2a, printed p. 39).**

**2.** Explain Vietnam's way of teaching biological control.
**Answer: Vietnam's Grade 12 study topics give biological control a topic of its own (Topic 2): Bài 5 defines it, states its limits honestly (slow, cannot answer an outbreak, costly) and its place inside integrated pest management, then works its ecological, genetic and physiological bases — sterile-insect technique, resistance genes (Bt into maize, cotton and potato), pheromone mass-trapping; Bài 6 sets six measures out as a system, including the six-step workflow for conserving natural enemies and Trichoderma in organic fertiliser; Bài 7 is a project surveying biological-control applications in the student's own locality.**
