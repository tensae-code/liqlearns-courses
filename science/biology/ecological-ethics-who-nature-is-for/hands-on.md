# Hands-on — Ecological ethics — who nature is for

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: ecological ethics asks who nature is for — anthropocentrism, the view that nature exists for humans, argued against its rivals — and China's wildlife reserves, railway culverts for antelope and village eco-compensation are that argument made practical.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a railway on high bridges crossing a plateau with antelope passing safely underneath through a wide culvert. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
