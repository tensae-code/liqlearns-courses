Today: Ecological ethics — who nature is for.

Remember this one thing: ecological ethics asks who nature is for — anthropocentrism, the view that nature exists for humans, argued against its rivals — and China's wildlife reserves, railway culverts for antelope and village eco-compensation are that argument made practical.

anthropocentrism against its rivals, argued dialectically; China's cases: the white-headed langur of Guangxi (about 600 left in the early 1980s, over 1,000 after reserves plus village eco-compensation), the Qinghai–Tibet railway's wildlife culverts and no-horn rule for Tibetan antelope, and a sturgeon institute built because the Gezhouba dam blocked the Chinese sturgeon's spawning run.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
