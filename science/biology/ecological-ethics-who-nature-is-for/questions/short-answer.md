# Ecological ethics — who nature is for — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** State the one thing this lesson teaches and explain it.
**Answer: ecological ethics asks who nature is for — anthropocentrism, the view that nature exists for humans, argued against its rivals — and China's wildlife reserves, railway culverts for antelope and village eco-compensation are that argument made practical. anthropocentrism against its rivals, argued dialectically; China's cases: the white-headed langur of Guangxi (about 600 left in the early 1980s, over 1,000 after reserves plus village eco-compensation), the Qinghai–Tibet railway's wildlife culverts and no-horn rule for Tibetan antelope, and a sturgeon institute built because the Gezhouba dam blocked the Chinese sturgeon's spawning run.**
