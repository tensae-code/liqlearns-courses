# Double fertilisation in flowering plants

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Double fertilisation in flowering plants. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
In a flowering plant, fertilisation is double: of the two male gametes a pollen grain delivers into the embryo sac, one joins the egg cell to form the zygote that grows into the embryo, and the second joins the polar nuclei to form the endosperm — the food tissue that will feed the embryo inside the seed. No lesson in this course taught this on its own before, so this lesson is built for it — from Indonesia's Kurikulum Merdeka books, which teach it as a named topic.

[Picture — a pollen tube entering an embryo sac, releasing two male gametes — one arrow to the egg cell labelled 'zygote → embryo', the other to the two polar nuclei labelled 'endosperm → food store' — inside a young seed]

## The key facts
**Two gametes, two fusions.** A pollen grain carries two male gametes into the embryo sac. Double fertilisation is the two fusions that follow — it is the defining event of reproduction in the flowering plants.

**The first fusion — the embryo.** One male gamete fuses with the egg cell. The product is the zygote, which divides and develops into the embryo — the young plant inside the seed.

**The second fusion — the food store.** The second male gamete fuses with the polar nuclei of the embryo sac's central cell. The product is the endosperm, the nutritive tissue that stores food for the developing embryo.

**Why 'double' matters.** Because the food tissue is only made when fertilisation succeeds, the plant does not waste resources provisioning a seed that has no embryo — the embryo and its food supply are made together, by the same event.

**Indonesia's way.** Indonesia's Grade 11 teacher guide names double fertilisation explicitly in its plant-reproduction chapter (Ch. 3, printed p. 108), teaching it through the process diagram and a set teaching sequence, alongside the full flowering-plant life cycle and seed dispersal.
