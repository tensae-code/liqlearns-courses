# Hands-on — Bone formation and growth

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: bones form by ossification — cartilage or membrane is gradually replaced by hard bone — and a long bone keeps growing in length at its growth plates until adulthood, while thickening and repair continue through life.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a long bone drawn in stages: a cartilage model, bone spreading from the centre, and the finished bone with its two pale growth plates near the ends. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
