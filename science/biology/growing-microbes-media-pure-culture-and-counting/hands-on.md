# Hands-on — Growing microbes — media, pure culture and counting

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: microbes are grown on media built from a carbon source, a nitrogen source, salts and water, tuned for pH and oxygen, and a pure culture is won by streak plate or dilution spread plate — then counted by colonies on the 30–300 rule, knowing the count runs low because chained and clumped cells count once.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a streak plate with colonies thinning out along the streak lines until single colonies stand alone, beside a spread plate dotted with counted colonies. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
