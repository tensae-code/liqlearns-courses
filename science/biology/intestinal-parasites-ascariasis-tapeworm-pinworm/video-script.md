Today: Intestinal parasites — ascariasis, tapeworm and pinworm.

Remember this one thing: the food-borne intestinal parasitoses are learned worm by worm: ascariasis, from Ascaris — usually asymptomatic, but able to cause abdominal pain, appetite and weight loss, nausea, diarrhoea and even intestinal obstruction; the tapeworms Taenia solium, from raw or undercooked pork, and T. saginata, from beef; and the pinworm, whose transmission includes a direct route — anus to mouth, after scratching.

Three worms, three roads into the body. Ascaris causes ascariasis — usually silent, but it can bring belly pain, lost appetite, diarrhoea, even a blocked intestine. The tapeworms arrive in meat: Taenia solium in undercooked pork, Taenia saginata in beef. And the pinworm takes the shortest road of all — from anus to hand to mouth, after scratching. Mozambique's module teaches them one by one, each with its road.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
