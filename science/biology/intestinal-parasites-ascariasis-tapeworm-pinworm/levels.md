# Intestinal parasites — ascariasis, tapeworm and pinworm — depth

This file is the lesson's depth record. This lesson has no sub lessons — it is one whole, single-skill lesson.

Whole-lesson pass mark: the learner can state the one thing this lesson teaches (The food-borne intestinal parasitoses are learned worm by worm: ascariasis, from Ascaris — usually asymptomatic, but able to cause abdominal pain, appetite and weight loss, nausea, diarrhoea and even intestinal obstruction; the tapeworms Taenia solium, from raw or undercooked pork, and T. saginata, from beef; and the pinworm, whose transmission includes a direct route — anus to mouth, after scratching.) and explain each key fact in their own words. Practice deepens by matching each parasite to its road without looking — Ascaris and its silent-until-heavy infection, T. solium/pork, T. saginata/beef, pinworm/anus-to-mouth — and the ascariasis symptom list.
