Today: Collecting and processing biological data.

Remember this one thing: Biological research stands on data handled well: collect it by planned observation and measurement, record it in order, process it — sorting, averages, tables and graphs — and present it so others can check and use it.

Research produces raw records first: counts, measurements, notes. By themselves they prove nothing.

So the biologist processes them — orders them, averages them, graphs them — until the pattern shows, and then presents the work so others can check it. Collect, record, process, present: that is the road from notebook to knowledge.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
