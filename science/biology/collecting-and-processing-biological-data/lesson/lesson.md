# Collecting and processing biological data

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Collecting and processing biological data. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it — what a biologist does with a notebook full of counts and measurements before any conclusion exists — and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Before biology concludes anything, it gathers and tidies. North Macedonia's Biology II opens with a whole theme on the methods of biological research, and inside it, collecting and processing data and scientific presentation stand as their own lessons (Themes 1.4–1.5, printed pp. 22–25) — between the microscope lesson and the lesson on ethics and safety. No lesson in this course covered data handling and presentation as method before, so this lesson is built for it.

[Picture — a field notebook page of tally marks and measurements becoming a neat table, the table becoming a bar graph, and the graph pinned on a board under a title — collection, processing, presentation in one row]

## The key facts
**Collect with a plan.** Data are collected by observation and measurement, planned before the work starts: what will be counted or measured, in what units, how often, and on how many individuals — planned collecting is what makes data comparable.

**Record honestly and in order.** Records go into tables as the work happens — no remembering later, no tidying away awkward values. A measurement that breaks the pattern is still data; honest recording is the ground the rest of the method stands on.

**Processing turns records into findings.** Processing means sorting and ordering the raw records, computing averages and ranges, and turning tables into graphs that let patterns be seen: the claim of a study is only ever as good as its processing.

**Presentation lets others check.** A scientific presentation states the question, the method, the processed results and the conclusion, with tables and graphs labelled so that another person can follow, check and repeat the work.

**North Macedonia's way.** North Macedonia's Biology II puts method before content: its first theme is the methods of biological research — the scientific method, laboratory instruments, the microscope, then collecting and processing data and scientific presentation as their own lessons (Themes 1.4–1.5, printed pp. 22–25), and ethics and safety to close the theme — before any organ system is taught.
