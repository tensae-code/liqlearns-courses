# Hands-on — Collecting and processing biological data

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Collect real data: measure the leaf lengths of 20 leaves from one plant, in millimetres, and record them in a table as you go. Process them: order the values, find the smallest, the largest and the average. Present the result as a titled bar graph of length groups, with a one-sentence conclusion the data actually supports.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — a field notebook page of tally marks and measurements becoming a neat table, the table becoming a bar graph, and the graph pinned on a board under a title — collection, processing, presentation in one row — and check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
