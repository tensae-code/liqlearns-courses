# The endocrine system — glands and hormones

## Do it first
Before you read: think of one living thing near your home that shows this lesson's idea — the endocrine system. Hold it in your mind, then read on and check your idea against what the lesson teaches.

Thyroxine (made by the thyroid from dietary iodine) controls metabolic rate; Ethiopia's goitre/iodine-deficiency situation is set as an investigation (why women and children are hit hardest), with the remedies stated — iodised salt, and iodised capsules where iodine is very low, only tiny amounts being needed; adrenaline's whole-body action is listed (faster heart and breathing, blood redirected to brain and muscles, raised pressure and blood sugar, dilated pupils, sweating); the hypothalamus–pituitary complex is described (joined by the infundibulum; posterior lobe storing hypothalamic oxytocin and ADH); and two answer-key essays give the adrenal cortex's three zones and their hormones (mineralocorticoids/aldosterone, glucocorticoids, and the innermost zone) and the TRH
