Today: Cytoskeleton, cilia and flagella.

Remember this one thing: a cell is not still — it has a skeleton inside it, and some cells row or swim with hairs and tails.

Look inside a cell. Running through it is a framework of fibres — the cytoskeleton. It holds the cell's shape, the way bones hold yours, and it is also the cell's road system: things travel along it from place to place inside the cell.

Now look at the surface. Some cells are covered with short hairs — cilia — hundreds of them, all beating together like tiny oars. Their beating pushes fluid past the cell, or pushes the cell through the fluid. Other cells carry one long tail — a flagellum. It whips back and forth, and the cell swims.

Italy's Grade 9 biology book teaches this as its own lesson: cells move. Cytoskeleton inside, cilia and flagella outside — those are the movers.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
