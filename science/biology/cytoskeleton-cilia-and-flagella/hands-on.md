# Hands-on — Cytoskeleton, cilia and flagella

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find the fastest-moving tiny things you can watch — ants on a path, or fish fry in shallow water. Say aloud which “mover” each one's motion resembles: the steady rowing of many cilia, or the whip of a flagellum. Then say what the cytoskeleton does that no outside mover can do — it works inside the cell.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — one cell showing the cytoskeleton fibres inside, one cell covered in cilia, one cell with a flagellum — and label them “cytoskeleton”, “cilia” and “flagellum”. Check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
