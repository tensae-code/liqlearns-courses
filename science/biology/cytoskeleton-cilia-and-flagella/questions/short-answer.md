# Cytoskeleton, cilia and flagella — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** Explain: the cytoskeleton, cilia and flagella, in one or two sentences.
**Answer: The cytoskeleton is the framework of fibres inside the cell that holds its shape and moves things within it. Cilia are short, numerous hairs that beat to move fluid or the cell, and flagella are long whip-like tails that propel the cell.**

**2.** Explain: Italy's way of teaching this.
**Answer: Italy's Grade 9 textbook (*La nuova biologia.blu PLUS — Le cellule e i viventi*, Sadava et al., Zanichelli) teaches cell movement as its own named lesson — A4.7, “cells move” (printed p. A80) — inside the chapter “Observing the cell”, so the cytoskeleton, cilia and flagella are taught as working machinery, not as extra detail.**
