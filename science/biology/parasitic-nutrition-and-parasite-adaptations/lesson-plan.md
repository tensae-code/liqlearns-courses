# Lesson plan — Parasitic nutrition and parasite adaptations

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Biology · **Lesson:** Parasitic nutrition and parasite adaptations
**Place in the curriculum:** Biology — appended to Unit 16 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a parasite feeds on or in a living host and harms it, and parasites are built for that life: hooks and suckers to hold on, protection against the host's enzymes, millions of eggs, and often an intermediate host — Nigeria teaches parasitic nutrition through West Africa's own parasites.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own as a single-skill lesson built from Nigeria's Senior Secondary Biology 1, Ch. 6 (printed p. 97). Related lessons are recommended first where they help, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words, the one thing this lesson teaches: a parasite feeds on or in a living host and harms it, and parasites are built for that life: hooks and suckers to hold on, protection against the host's enzymes, millions of eggs, and often an intermediate host — Nigeria teaches parasitic nutrition through West Africa's own parasites.
2. Explain each key fact (Feeding on a living host; West Africa's parasites; Holding on; Surviving the host; Intermediate hosts and one-host loyalty) in their own words.
3. Explain Nigeria's way as the placements describe it: the topic is taught in Ch. 6 of Senior Secondary Biology 1 (printed p. 97).
