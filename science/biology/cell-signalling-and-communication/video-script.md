Today: Cell signalling and communication.

Remember this one thing: a cell is not deaf. Cells send messages to each other, and cells receive them.

The message is a molecule — a signalling molecule, released by one cell. It travels — sometimes only to the cell next door, sometimes through the whole body — until it meets a cell carrying the matching receptor. The receptor is shaped for that one molecule, the way a lock is shaped for one key. Only cells with the right receptor can hear that message.

When the molecule binds, the signal does not stop at the surface. It is propagated — carried inside through a chain of relay molecules, and along the way it is amplified: one molecule outside can set off a large change inside.

Then the cell responds. It may switch a gene on or off, start an enzyme working, or change what it is doing. That response is the whole point of the message.

And this is not only for big animals and plants. Single-celled organisms signal too — bacteria sense their neighbours and change their behaviour when the crowd around them grows.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
