# Hands-on — Cell signalling and communication

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Play a message game with companions: one person is the signalling cell and whispers one instruction to a "receptor" companion, who passes it down a short chain of companions, each repeating it exactly once and once only louder in effect — the last companion acts out the response. Say aloud which player played the signalling molecule, which the receptor, which the relay chain, and which the response.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the signalling path from memory: a cell releasing a signalling molecule, the molecule crossing to a target cell, the receptor it binds, the relay chain inside, and the response at the end. Label each part — signalling molecule, receptor, propagation, response — and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
