# Cell signalling and communication — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** Trace the whole path of a cell signal, from the sending cell to the response.
**Answer: A signalling cell releases a signalling molecule (ligand). The ligand travels to a target cell — near or far — and binds the matching receptor on or in that cell. The signal is then propagated inside the cell along a chain of relay molecules, which can amplify it, and the chain ends in a response: genes switched on or off, enzymes started or stopped, channels opened or closed, or a change in the cell's shape or movement. The signal is then switched off.**

**2.** Explain the United States' way of teaching this.
**Answer: OpenStax gives cell communication a chapter of its own (Chapter 9) in both *Biology for AP® Courses* and *Biology 2e*, standing between photosynthesis and cell reproduction. The chapter walks the whole path of a message in order — signalling molecules and cellular receptors, propagation of the signal, response to the signal — and closes with signalling in single-celled organisms, showing that cell communication is older than the many-celled body.**
