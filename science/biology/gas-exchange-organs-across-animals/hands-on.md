# Hands-on — Gas-exchange organs across animals

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: animals exchange gases through organs fitted to where they live — skin in worms and frogs, feathery gills in water animals, branching tracheae opening at spiracles in insects, and lungs in land vertebrates — all of them thin, moist, wide-surfaced and well supplied.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: four animals in a row — earthworm, fish, grasshopper and rabbit — each with its breathing organ drawn enlarged beside it: skin, gills, tracheae, lungs. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
