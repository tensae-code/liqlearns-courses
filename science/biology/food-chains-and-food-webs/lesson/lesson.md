# Food chains and food webs

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Food chains and food webs. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
This lesson stands on teacher-guide evidence only. It carries the guide's answer-side completion for the food-chain section. (teacher guide — evidence)

## The key facts
**Trophic levels.** A trophic level is a feeding level in a food chain.

**Three or four.** Trophic level chains are usually limited to 3 or 4 levels.

**Energy loss.** Energy is lost at each transfer between trophic levels.

**Too little remains.** Too little energy remains to support another level beyond three or four.

## From the student book — the pupil-taught core
The student book opens the unit from pupils' own environment — a cat hunting a rat, a hyena meeting a dog — and builds chains into webs, with the sun fixed as the ultimate energy source.

### Student-book key facts
**Who eats whom.** Organisms are linked by who eats whom into food chains.

**Webs.** Food chains interconnect into food webs.

**Roles.** Producers, herbivores and primary and secondary carnivores are the roles in the web.

**The sun.** The sun is the ultimate source of energy for food chains and webs.
