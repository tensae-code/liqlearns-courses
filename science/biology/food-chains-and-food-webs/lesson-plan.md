# Lesson plan — Food chains and food webs

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Biology · **Lesson:** Food chains and food webs
**Place in the curriculum:** Grade 10 — teacher guide evidence (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** food chains usually stop at three or four trophic levels because energy is lost at each transfer.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the Grade 9–10 Biology course — the learner can already read English science text and handle the lesson's own examples. It needs nothing else.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words, the one thing this lesson teaches: food chains usually stop at three or four trophic levels because energy is lost at each transfer.
2. Name and explain each key fact of the lesson.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from what the lesson itself taught.
