# Hands-on — Precision medicine — treatment by genome

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: precision medicine builds diagnosis and treatment on the patient's own genome: the same drug cures one patient and harms another, so targeted cancer drugs are chosen by the mutation present — and are useless, even harmful, without it — against the obstacles of data privacy, ethics and the sheer volume of sequence data.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: two patients holding the same medicine bottle, a genome strip above each — a tick over the one whose mutation the drug targets, a cross over the other. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
