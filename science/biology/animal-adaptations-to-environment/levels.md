# Animal adaptations to environment — depth

This file is the lesson's depth record. This lesson has no sub lessons — it is one whole, single-skill lesson.

Whole-lesson pass mark: the learner can state the one thing this lesson teaches (an animal is fitted to the place it lives in three registers at once — its form, its structure and its ways of life — and life in water demands a different set of fittings from life on land; Iraq's Grade 10 Biology book gives animal adaptation its longest chapter (Ch. 6, printed pp. 77–120) and closes it with a head-to-head comparison of a bony fish and the pigeon) and explain each key fact in their own words. Practice deepens by using more examples of the same facts, then the harder corners: applying the lesson to a new example from the learner's own surroundings, and explaining Iraq's way of teaching it, without looking.
