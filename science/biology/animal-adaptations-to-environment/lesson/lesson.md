# Animal adaptations to environment

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Animal adaptations to environment. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Chapter 6 of Iraq's Grade 10 Biology book is the book's longest chapter — printed pp. 77–120 — and it is organised entirely by adaptation. It opens with animal adaptation to the environment in form, structure and ways of life (§1-6, printed p. 77), then takes the two great homes in turn: adaptation to life in water, then adaptation to life on land (§§2-6/3-6, printed p. 88), and closes with a direct comparison (§4-6, printed p. 110) — a water animal, the bony fish, set against a land animal, the pigeon.

[Picture — a bony fish and a pigeon side by side, each labelled with the fittings that suit it to its home — the fish to water, the pigeon to land]

## The key facts
**Adaptation in three registers.** An animal's adaptation is read in its form (its outward shape), its structure (how it is built inside) and its ways of life (how it feeds, moves and lives) — Iraq's chapter treats all three together, not shape alone.

**Adaptation to water.** The chapter's second movement (§2-6) takes adaptation to life in water: the fittings an animal needs where it is surrounded by water, breathes in water and moves through water.

**Adaptation to land.** The third movement (§3-6) takes adaptation to life on land — a different problem: air instead of water around the body, gravity to fight, and water to hold on to.

**Fish against pigeon.** The chapter closes (§4-6, printed p. 110) with its comparison: a bony fish, the water animal, against the pigeon, the land animal — the two homes' fittings set side by side so the contrast teaches the rule.

**Iraq's way.** Iraq teaches animal adaptation as one whole organising idea: the longest chapter of its Grade 10 book, arranged general → water → land → comparison, inside a book whose stated approach is ecological and behavioural.
