Today: Reproduction in earthworm and garden snail.

Remember this one thing: the earthworm and the garden snail are both hermaphrodites — each individual carries both sexes — yet both still cross-fertilise: two worms exchange sperm at the clitellum and fertilisation happens inside the slipped-off cocoon, and two snails exchange sperm before each lays its batch of eggs in the soil.

Earthworm and garden snail are hermaphrodites — each carries male and female organs — yet both cross-fertilise with a partner rather than fertilising themselves.

Two earthworms join at the clitellum and exchange sperm, each storing the other's sperm for the cocoon stage.

The clitellum forms a band that slips off the worm as a cocoon; eggs and the stored sperm meet inside it, so fertilisation happens inside the slipped-off cocoon.

Garden snails also exchange sperm in mating; each then lays batches of eggs in the soil, where the young develop.

Nigeria teaches reproduction in earthworm and garden snail together in Chapter 15 (printed p. 217) — the hermaphrodite pair that proves cross-fertilisation is kept even when one body holds both sexes.
