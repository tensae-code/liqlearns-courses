# Signs of a chemical reaction — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** State the one thing this lesson teaches and explain it.
**Answer: a chemical reaction shows itself by observable signs — a colour change, a taste change, fizzing or a new substance forming — and naming the evidence is how you know a reaction, not just a mixture, has happened. Kuwait's Grade 10 applications workbook (Activity 15, printed p. 42) squeezes lemon quarters into a cup of dark tea step by step and records the colour and taste changes; the sour taste is citric acid, a chemical — and the changes are the evidence that a chemical reaction happened (a "link with chemistry" box).**

**2.** Explain Kuwait's way of teaching this.
**Answer: Kuwait teaches the signs of reaction inside its biology workbook as chemistry-in-biology: both activities carry the workbook's "link with chemistry" frame, and both are set as observation-first tasks — record the change, then say what it proves.**
