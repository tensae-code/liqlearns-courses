# Hands-on — Signs of a chemical reaction

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: a chemical reaction shows itself by observable signs — a colour change, a taste change, fizzing or a new substance forming — and naming the evidence is how you know a reaction, not just a mixture, has happened.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: two beakers side by side: in the first, lemon juice being squeezed into dark tea and the tea turning paler step by step; in the second, vinegar meeting baking-powder solution with bubbles rising — labels under each naming the sign of reaction it shows. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
