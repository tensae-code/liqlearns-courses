# Hands-on — Microbial cultures and industrial fermentation

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: biotechnology's working tool is the microbial culture: microbes grown pure in the lab, then scaled up in great fermenters with food, air and stirring controlled, their products separated and purified — penicillin production stands as the milestone that proved the scale.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a chain growing left to right — a single colony dish, a flask, then a tall factory fermenter with pipes and gauges — ending in a medicine bottle labelled penicillin. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
