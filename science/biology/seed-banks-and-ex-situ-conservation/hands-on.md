# Hands-on — Seed banks and ex-situ conservation

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: seed banks conserve species outside the place they live — ex-situ conservation: seeds are dried and frozen so they keep for decades, as the Svalbard Global Seed Bank stores the world's crop seeds in Arctic cold, as insurance against their loss in the wild.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a vault door set into an icy mountain, shelves of labelled seed packets inside stretching back in rows, with a wheat field in summer drawn outside the door. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
