Today: The seashore community.

Remember this one thing: A seashore community is studied as a whole: the living things of a shore live in zones between the tides, each kind living where it can stand the covering and uncovering of the sea — who lives where on the shore, and why there.

A community is all the living things living together in one place. The seashore study takes one real community — the shore — and studies the whole of it together: not one animal or one plant alone, but every kind in its place, with the conditions of that place.

A shore between the tides is not the same from top to bottom. It falls into zones: the top of the shore, uncovered for most of the day; the middle shore, covered and uncovered by each tide; and the low shore, uncovered only at the lowest tides. Each zone has its own conditions — how long it is dry, how hard the waves strike, how hot the sun makes it.

Each kind of shore life is found in the zone it can stand. Kinds near the top must bear long dry hours and heat; kinds low down need the sea's cover and cannot bear long drying. That is why a shore is read in bands: find the zone, and you can say which living things to expect there.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
