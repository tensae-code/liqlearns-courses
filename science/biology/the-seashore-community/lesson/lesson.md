# The seashore community

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — The seashore community. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Fiji's Year 11 Biology book does not study ecology only in definitions. Inside its ecology chapter, after populations and community relationships, it takes one whole community — a Fijian seashore — and studies it as a whole: the shore walked zone by zone, and every living thing placed where it actually lives.

## The key facts
**A whole community, in its place.** A community is all the living things living together in one place. The seashore study takes one real community — the shore — and studies the whole of it together: not one animal or one plant alone, but every kind in its place, with the conditions of that place.

**Zonation — the shore is banded.** A shore between the tides is not the same from top to bottom. It falls into zones: the top of the shore, uncovered for most of the day; the middle shore, covered and uncovered by each tide; and the low shore, uncovered only at the lowest tides. Each zone has its own conditions — how long it is dry, how hard the waves strike, how hot the sun makes it.

**Who lives where follows the zone.** Each kind of shore life is found in the zone it can stand. Kinds near the top must bear long dry hours and heat; kinds low down need the sea's cover and cannot bear long drying. That is why a shore is read in bands: find the zone, and you can say which living things to expect there.

**Fiji's way.** The study is §6.2.2 of the Year 11 book's ecology chapter, and the community is Fiji's own shore — the rocky shore and the coral-reef shore that the same chapter names among Fiji's communities. The learner studies the shore outside the school, not a diagram of a shore in another country.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words: A seashore community is studied as a whole: the living things of a shore live in zones between the tides, each kind living where it can stand the covering and uncovering of the sea — who lives where on the shore, and why there. Use each key fact once. Then let your companion explain it back to you.
