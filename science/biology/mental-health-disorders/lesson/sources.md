Structural summary: The "scientist interview" frontispiece is Xu Zhihong （许智宏）, plant physiologist and former Peking University president, on plant hormones, the detail that decides an experiment (impure sucrose sinking his protoplast work; mung-bean sprouts thought of while stir-frying), and a warning against hormone panic in food. Ch. 1 builds the internal environment from a real blood-biochemistry report and extends homeostasis to every level of life. Ch. 2 is neural regulation whole: structure, reflexes, impulses and synapses, drugs at the synapse (with the criminal law quoted), hierarchical control taught through stroke and bladder cases, the China Brain Project, and the higher functions — language areas from lesion cases, a four-stage model of memory, and an unusually frank section on depression. Ch. 3 is humoral regulation: the secretin story told in three acts ending with Pavlov's lament, insulin's discovery, feedback and hierarchical axes, and a ten-case evaluation of hormone products. Ch. 4 is immunity: the system as an army with barracks and battlefields, the 2015 discovery of brain lymphatics, influenza and cancer immunotherapy features, HIV with China's free-treatment policy, and transplantation with China's computerised organ-allocation system. Ch. 5 turns to plants: the auxin discovery chain from the Darwins to Went, the other hormones opened by a Song–Yuan dynasty text on ripening persimmons, growth regulators regulated as pesticides, and environmental control ending with flower-forcing for festivals and the 2008 Olympics.
