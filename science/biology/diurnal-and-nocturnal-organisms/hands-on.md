# Hands-on — Diurnal and nocturnal organisms

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: living things keep biological rhythms — some are active by day and some by night, and each is fitted to its time: flowers open and close at set hours, and nocturnal animals see in the dark with large eyes and a reflective eye layer.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a split day-and-night scene: on the day side a flower open in sunlight, on the night side the same flower closed and a nocturnal animal with large shining eyes awake under the moon. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
