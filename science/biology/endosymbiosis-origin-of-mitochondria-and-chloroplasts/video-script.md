Today: Endosymbiosis — the origin of mitochondria and chloroplasts.

Remember this one thing: two of the cell's most important parts were once bacteria living on their own.

Look at a mitochondrion. It sits inside the cell, but it looks like a tiny cell itself. The chloroplast in a plant cell looks the same way. Why? The endosymbiosis hypothesis gives the answer: long ago, these were free-living bacteria. A larger cell took a bacterium inside — and instead of digesting it, kept it.

Italy's textbook calls it an ancient pact. The guest did what it was good at — releasing energy, or catching sunlight — and the host gave it shelter and materials. The pact worked so well that it never ended. The guest became an organelle: the mitochondrion, the chloroplast.

The book names the scientist and the year: Lynn Margulis, 1967.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
