# Lesson plan — Endosymbiosis: the origin of mitochondria and chloroplasts

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Biology · **Lesson:** Endosymbiosis: the origin of mitochondria and chloroplasts
**Place in the curriculum:** Biology (CURRICULUM.md Unit 16).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Mitochondria and chloroplasts were once free-living bacteria that took up life inside another cell — the endosymbiosis hypothesis of Lynn Margulis (1967), which Italy's Grade 9 Campbell closes its cell unit with, taught as an “ancient pact” (printed p. 62).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on it stands on the earlier cell lessons — inside a cell: organelles, and the two kinds of cell — and answers the question those lessons raise: where did these organelles come from?

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier biology lessons on the cell — inside a cell: organelles, and the two kinds of cell, prokaryotic and eukaryotic. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what endosymbiosis claims: mitochondria and chloroplasts began as free-living bacteria living inside another cell.
2. Name the hypothesis's author and year as Italy's book gives them: Lynn Margulis, 1967.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson itself.
