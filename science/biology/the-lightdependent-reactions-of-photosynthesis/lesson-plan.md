# Lesson plan — The Light-Dependent Reactions of Photosynthesis

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Biology · **Lesson:** The Light-Dependent Reactions of Photosynthesis
**Place in the curriculum:** Biology — Grades 11–12 band (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** on the thylakoid membrane, light splits water (photolysis), the freed electrons flow through plastoquinone, cytochromes and plastocyanin to photosystem I while protons fill the thylakoid lumen, chemiosmosis turns that proton flow into ATP (photophosphorylation), and a second chain through ferredoxin forms reduced NADP — three products: O₂, ATP and NADPH — with cyclic photophosphorylation making ATP only.
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier biology of cells, living things and the human body, and builds the Grade 11–12 understanding of the light-dependent reactions of photosynthesis.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. ## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Write the photolysis equation and say what each of its products does.
2. Name the electron carriers in order between photosystem II and photosystem I, and the carrier after photosystem I.
3. Explain how protons filling the thylakoid lumen lead to ATP by chemiosmosis (photophosphorylation).
4. Name the three products of the non-cyclic reactions and contrast cyclic photophosphorylation.
5. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson itself.

## If the learner is stuck
- The carrier names blur together → walk the picture in order, saying each name aloud: plastoquinone, cytochromes, plastocyanin, then ferredoxin.
- Chemiosmosis feels abstract → use the dam picture from the lesson: protons held back, then flowing through one gate that does work.
