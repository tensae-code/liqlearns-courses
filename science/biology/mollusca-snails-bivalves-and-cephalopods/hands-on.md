# Hands-on — Mollusca — snails, bivalves and cephalopods

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find a snail shell (or draw one): follow the spiral with your finger and name the three body regions on a living snail's plan — head, visceral mass inside the shell, foot below. Then hold two cupped hands hinged at the wrists: that opening-and-closing pair is the bivalve's two valves.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory — a snail drawn with its spiral shell, head with eyes on tentacle tips and broad foot, beside an open bivalve showing two hinged valves and its axe-shaped foot, with a small magnified radula drawn as a toothed ribbon between them — label its parts, and check against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
