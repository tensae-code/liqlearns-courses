Today: Mollusca — snails, bivalves and cephalopods.

Remember this one thing: molluscs are soft, unsegmented animals whose body has three regions — head, visceral mass and foot — usually with a shell and a scraping tongue, the radula; Mozambique works the plan through two classes: the gastropods, with their spiral shell and eyes on tentacle tips, and the bivalves, with two hinged valves, an axe-shaped foot and no distinct head.

A mollusc is soft, with no segments, and its body has three parts: a head, a visceral mass, and a foot. Most carry a shell, and all the familiar ones scrape their food with a little toothed tongue called the radula. The snail keeps the plan whole — spiral shell, eyes on the tips of its tentacles. The bivalve changes it: two shells hinged together, a foot shaped like an axe, and no real head at all.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
