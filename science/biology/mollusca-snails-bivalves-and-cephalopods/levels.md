# Mollusca — snails, bivalves and cephalopods — depth

This file is the lesson's depth record. This lesson has no sub lessons — it is one whole, single-skill lesson.

Whole-lesson pass mark: the learner can state the one thing this lesson teaches (Molluscs are soft, unsegmented animals whose body has three regions — head, visceral mass and foot — usually with a shell and a scraping tongue, the radula; Mozambique works the plan through two classes: the gastropods, with their spiral shell and eyes on tentacle tips, and the bivalves, with two hinged valves, an axe-shaped foot and no distinct head.) and explain each key fact in their own words. Practice deepens by contrasting the two classes point by point without looking — shell (spiral vs two valves), head (eyes on tentacles vs none), foot (crawling sole vs axe-shaped) — and naming the radula's job.
