# Mollusca — snails, bivalves and cephalopods — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** Contrast a gastropod and a bivalve, as the module teaches them.
**Answer: The gastropod (snail) has a spiral shell and visceral mass, a clear head with eyes on tentacle tips, and a single hermaphrodite gonad. The bivalve has two hinged shell valves, an axe-shaped foot and no distinct head.**

**2.** Explain Mozambique's way of teaching this.
**Answer: Mozambique's PESD II animal module (Módulo 3 de Biologia), Lessons 11–12, teaches phylum Mollusca by its plan — soft unsegmented body in head, visceral mass and foot, radula, shell — then works the gastropods and the bivalves in detail against each other, with internal vs external fertilisation and direct vs indirect development across the phylum.**
