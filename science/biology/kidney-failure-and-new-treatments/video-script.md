Today: Kidney failure and new treatments.

Remember this one thing: When kidneys fail, wastes and water build up in the blood and poison the body — and the new treatments, dialysis and transplant, do the kidney's cleaning work from outside or replace the organ itself.

Healthy kidneys clear urea, balance salts and shed excess water without pause. As they fail, those jobs go undone — wastes poison the blood, water swells the body and lungs, and salts drift out of balance until the heart itself is threatened.

Kidneys are damaged slowly more often than suddenly. Years of high blood sugar or high blood pressure scar their filters; untreated infections and blocking stones destroy tissue; and some medicines and poisons, taken wrongly, poison the kidney itself.

Because the kidneys work silently, failure is often far advanced when it is felt. The signs track the undone jobs — fluid in the tissues (swelling), waste in the blood (nausea, exhaustion), and changes in the urine that a test can find long before the person feels ill.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
