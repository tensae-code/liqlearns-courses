# Kidney failure and new treatments

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Kidney failure and new treatments. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Afghanistan's Grade 9 Biology book closes its excretory chapter with kidney problems and the new ways of treating them. This lesson teaches that close on its own: what goes wrong in kidney failure, why it is deadly, and how dialysis and kidney transplant — the treatments of the modern era — answer it.

## The key facts
**What failure means.** Healthy kidneys clear urea, balance salts and shed excess water without pause. As they fail, those jobs go undone — wastes poison the blood, water swells the body and lungs, and salts drift out of balance until the heart itself is threatened.

**What causes it.** Kidneys are damaged slowly more often than suddenly. Years of high blood sugar or high blood pressure scar their filters; untreated infections and blocking stones destroy tissue; and some medicines and poisons, taken wrongly, poison the kidney itself.

**Signs and course.** Because the kidneys work silently, failure is often far advanced when it is felt. The signs track the undone jobs — fluid in the tissues (swelling), waste in the blood (nausea, exhaustion), and changes in the urine that a test can find long before the person feels ill.

**Dialysis — cleaning from outside.** In haemodialysis, blood leaves the body, passes along a membrane in the machine where wastes and extra water cross into a washing fluid, and returns cleaned — several hours at a time, several times a week, for as long as the kidneys cannot work. Peritoneal dialysis uses the patient's own abdominal lining as the membrane instead.

**Transplant — a new kidney.** A surgeon places one healthy kidney from a donor — living or deceased — in the patient's body; the failed kidneys usually stay where they are. One good kidney can do the whole cleaning work, but the patient takes medicines for life to stop the immune system rejecting the gift.

**Afghanistan's way.** The Grade 9 excretory chapter ends by looking forward — from kidney problems to the new treatments for them, dialysis and transplant, so the system is taught together with the medicine that rescues it.
