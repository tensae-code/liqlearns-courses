Today: Civil defence and emergency safety.

Remember this one thing: civil defence (الدفاع المدني) is the organised public protection of people in emergencies — and Iraq teaches it as biology-course content, as the closing chapter of its Grade 7 Biology book, in the safety unit it shares with first aid.

Civil defence is the organised protection of the public in emergencies — the planned, collective work of keeping people safe when disaster or danger strikes a whole community, not only one patient.

Iraq places civil defence immediately after first aid in the same unit: first aid is what one person does for one injured person; civil defence is the organised service that answers when the emergency is bigger than one helper.

Civil defence is Chapter 9 of the Grade 7 book (printed p. 87) — the book's last chapter, and its final review is the civil-defence review.

Iraq files safety as biology: a whole unit — Unit 4, First aid and civil defence — closes its first-intermediate Biology book, so emergency protection is taught as part of the science of living things and their care.

First aid is one person helping one injured person.

Civil defence is the organised service that protects the whole public in an emergency.

Iraq closes its Grade 7 Biology book with this chapter — safety taught as biology.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
