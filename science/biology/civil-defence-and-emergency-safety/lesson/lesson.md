# Civil defence and emergency safety

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Civil defence and emergency safety. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
The last unit of Iraq's Grade 7 Biology book (Unit 4) is a safety unit inside a biology book: first aid first (Ch. 8), then civil defence (الدفاع المدني) as Chapter 9 (printed p. 87) — the organised, public side of protecting people in emergencies, set beside the individual help of first aid, and the book's final chapter review is the civil-defence review.

[Picture — a civil-defence scene in simple shapes: a uniformed rescue worker directing people to safety while a first-aider helps one injured person — the organised protection and the individual help side by side]

## The key facts
**What civil defence is.** Civil defence is the organised protection of the public in emergencies — the planned, collective work of keeping people safe when disaster or danger strikes a whole community, not only one patient.

**First aid's partner.** Iraq places civil defence immediately after first aid in the same unit: first aid is what one person does for one injured person; civil defence is the organised service that answers when the emergency is bigger than one helper.

**The closing chapter.** Civil defence is Chapter 9 of the Grade 7 book (printed p. 87) — the book's last chapter, and its final review is the civil-defence review.

**Iraq's way.** Iraq files safety as biology: a whole unit — Unit 4, First aid and civil defence — closes its first-intermediate Biology book, so emergency protection is taught as part of the science of living things and their care.
