# Hands-on — Modifying the microbiome — probiotics and faecal transplant

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Take a jar of mixed beads or buttons — that is a gut microbiote, many kinds mixed. Tip in a second jar's whole mixture: that is the faecal transplant, a whole community brought in. Then, from a third small handful, add only a few chosen beads: that is the probiotics, chosen live micro-organisms added on purpose. Say, for each action, which modification it models and what the manual teaches about it.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — the gut as a garden of small shapes, the two arrows in (faecal transplant; probiotics), and the two gauges marked "weight" and "glycaemia" — and check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap. End with this one together: how is a faecal transplant different from taking probiotics? Say it in one sentence each way.
