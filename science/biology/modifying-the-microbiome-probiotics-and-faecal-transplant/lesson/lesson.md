# Modifying the microbiome — probiotics and faecal transplant

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Modifying the microbiome: probiotics and faecal transplant. Write down, or say aloud, what you think could be changed about the micro-organisms living in a person's gut, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
The human microbiote is the community of micro-organisms living in and on the body, in symbiosis with it, unique to each individual — that is how France's Seconde SVT manual teaches it in the chapter "Microbiote humain et santé" (Human microbiote and health). The same chapter goes one step further: if the microbiote matters to health, it can be modified — deliberately, as a treatment. This lesson is that step: the two modifications the manual teaches.

[Picture — a gut drawn as a garden of many small shapes, the microbiote, with two arrows coming in: one labelled "faecal transplant" bringing a whole new set of shapes, and one labelled "probiotics" bringing a small set; beside the garden, two gauges marked "weight" and "glycaemia"]

## The key facts
**The microbiome can be modified.** The manual treats the microbiote as something medicine can act on — not only observe. A disordered microbiote can be changed on purpose, and the change itself is the treatment.

**Faecal transplantation — the whole-community treatment.** The manual teaches faecal transplantation as a treatment: the gut microbiote is transplanted — a whole community of gut micro-organisms is brought in to rebuild the patient's own.

**Probiotics — chosen micro-organisms, given on purpose.** Probiotics are live micro-organisms given deliberately, to shift the microbiote in a chosen direction — a small, selected addition, not a whole transplant.

**Probiotics' effects are measured: weight and glycaemia.** The manual studies probiotics through their effects on two measurable things: weight, and glycaemia — the blood sugar. The claim is tested against outcomes that can be measured, not taken on trust.

**France's way.** In France this sits inside the microbiote chapter of the Seconde manual: the same chapter that defines the microbiote, shows its role in digestion and immunity, and shows antibiotics unbalancing it, closes the loop by teaching how the microbiote is modified as treatment.
