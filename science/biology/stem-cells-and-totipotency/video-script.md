Today: Stem cells and totipotency.

Remember this one thing: a totipotent cell can make a whole organism, and stem cells carry that power at every scale: Steward's carrot grown from one cell (1958), Dolly and the cloned monkeys Zhongzhong and Huahua from a body cell's nucleus, haematopoietic stem cells rebuilding blood, and iPS cells — a patient's own cells reprogrammed in 2006 — promising the same without an embryo and without rejection, the tumour risk stated plainly.

Zhongzhong and Huahua (2017), the first primate somatic-cell clones: the three technical hurdles that had blocked primates, and the text states China's ban on human cloning outright. (The same proposed lesson is exercised again from Ch. 6 §2 below.)

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
