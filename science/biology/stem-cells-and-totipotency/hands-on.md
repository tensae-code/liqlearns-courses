# Hands-on — Stem cells and totipotency

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: a totipotent cell can make a whole organism, and stem cells carry that power at every scale: Steward's carrot grown from one cell (1958), Dolly and the cloned monkeys Zhongzhong and Huahua from a body cell's nucleus, haematopoietic stem cells rebuilding blood, and iPS cells — a patient's own cells reprogrammed in 2006 — promising the same without an embryo and without rejection, the tumour risk stated plainly.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a tree whose trunk is one cell: its branches end in a carrot plant, a sheep, two monkeys and a blood-cell family, each labelled with its year. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
