# Biotechnology

## Do it first
Before you read: think of one living thing near your home that shows this lesson's idea — biotechnology. Hold it in your mind, then read on and check your idea against what the lesson teaches.

## Sub lesson 1 — Biotechnology, GMOs and transgenic organisms (§1.4 opening):

biotechnology defined as using living organisms or their products to make products that benefit humans; recombinant DNA and genetically modified organisms; a gene moved from one species into another is a transgene and the receiver a transgenic organism; crop modification for yield and novel products.

[Picture 21219 — biotechnology overview]

## Sub lesson 2 — Traditional biotechnology and food production (§1.4(i)):

microbe-driven food production from bread, beer, tej, tella and wine fermentation to industrial single-cell protein — Pruteen grown from waste methanol by a bacterium, mycoprotein from a fungus sold as a meat substitute, and Spirulina harvested from lakes as a nutrient-rich food.

## Sub lesson 3 — Biotechnology in agriculture (§1.4):

micropropagation by tissue culture — tissue fragments grown in an artificial medium into many uniform, disease-free plants (palm, orchids, bananas, carrots) — and breed improvement, figured with a Borana bull and a dairy cow.
