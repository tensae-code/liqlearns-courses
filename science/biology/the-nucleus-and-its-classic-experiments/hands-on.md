# Hands-on — The nucleus — and its classic experiments

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: the nucleus controls the cell, and its control role is proved by four classic experiments set side by side — axolotl nuclear transplant, salamander egg constriction, amoeba cutting and Acetabularia grafting — opened by a cloned-cow case, with chromatin against chromosome closing the account.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: four small panels in a row — a transplanted nucleus, a pinched egg, a cut amoeba and a grafted umbrella alga — each with the same nucleus drawn dark. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
