# Co-dominance, incomplete dominance and blood groups — short answer

Answer in your own words, in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) In your own words, what does this lesson teach about co-dominance, incomplete dominance and multiple alleles?**
**Answer: Patterns Mendel's rules do not cover: co-dominance, where both alleles show fully (red × white cattle giving roan; the A and B blood alleles giving AB blood); incomplete dominance, where the heterozygote is a blend (red × white flowers giving pink, 1:2:1 in F2); and multiple alleles, where a gene has more than two forms — the ABO blood system with its A, B and O alleles, antigens and antibodies, and why transfusion matching matters.**

**2. (Sub lesson 2) In your own words, what does this lesson teach about added blood-group offspring tables?**
**Answer: Complete parent-combination tables the student book does not print: for ABO, every parental pairing (O×O through A×B and A×AB) with the children's possible phenotypes and genotypes; and the three-row Rh table (Rh+×Rh+, Rh+×Rh−, Rh−×Rh−) with possible children's types.**
