Today: Lichens — a symbiosis.

Remember this one thing: A lichen is not one living thing but two living together as one body: a fungus and an alga (or cyanobacterium) in symbiosis, each giving what the other lacks.

Cut a lichen open and you find two partners woven together — fungal threads (hyphae) forming the body, and algal or cyanobacterial cells living among them. Neither partner is the whole lichen; the lichen exists only where the two live as one.

The fungus is the house-builder: it anchors the lichen, soaks up water and minerals and shields the partner from drying and sun. The alga is the cook: it photosynthesises and passes part of the food it makes to the fungus. Each gives what the other cannot do alone.

Crustose lichens sit like paint on rock and bark and cannot be peeled off whole. Foliose lichens lift in leafy lobes. Fruticose lichens hang from branches or stand up like tiny shrubs — the three forms are how lichens are first sorted in the field.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
