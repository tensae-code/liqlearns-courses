# Lichens — a symbiosis

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Lichens — a symbiosis. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Afghanistan's Grade 8 Biology book closes its microbe chapter with the lichens (گل سنگ). This lesson teaches the lichen on its own: the partnership it is built from, the forms it takes on rock and bark, and what lichens tell us about the air.

## The key facts
**Two partners, one body.** Cut a lichen open and you find two partners woven together — fungal threads (hyphae) forming the body, and algal or cyanobacterial cells living among them. Neither partner is the whole lichen; the lichen exists only where the two live as one.

**What each partner gives.** The fungus is the house-builder: it anchors the lichen, soaks up water and minerals and shields the partner from drying and sun. The alga is the cook: it photosynthesises and passes part of the food it makes to the fungus. Each gives what the other cannot do alone.

**Three forms.** Crustose lichens sit like paint on rock and bark and cannot be peeled off whole. Foliose lichens lift in leafy lobes. Fruticose lichens hang from branches or stand up like tiny shrubs — the three forms are how lichens are first sorted in the field.

**Pioneers on bare rock.** Lichens grow where almost nothing else can — bare rock, desert crust, cold mountain stone. Their acids slowly break the rock's surface, and when they die their remains mix with the dust: the first soil, in which mosses and then higher plants can root.

**Indicators of clean air.** Lichens take their water and minerals straight from the air and rain, so poisons in the air enter them directly. Where sulphur-rich smoke fouls the air the lichens vanish; a rock or tree rich in lichens is a sign of clean air.

**Afghanistan's way.** The lichens close the Grade 8 microbe chapter — after virus, bacteria, protista, algae and fungi, the chapter ends with the living thing that is two of those kingdoms joined in one body.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words: A lichen is not one living thing but two living together as one body: a fungus and an alga (or cyanobacterium) in symbiosis, each giving what the other lacks. Use each key fact once. Then let your companion explain it back to you.
