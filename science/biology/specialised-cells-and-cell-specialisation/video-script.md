Today: Specialised cells and cell specialisation.

Remember this one thing: A specialised cell is a cell whose structure is fitted to one particular job — and a living thing works better when its jobs are divided among specialised cells, each doing one job well.

A specialised cell's shape and parts suit the single job it does. It is not a general-purpose cell that does a bit of everything.

Specialised cells are studied from slides and photomicrographs — observed, drawn and compared by their structure, which is how one kind is told from another.

Cheek cells and ciliated cells are distinguished by their structure: the ciliated cell carries cilia — tiny beating hairs — and the cheek cell does not.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
