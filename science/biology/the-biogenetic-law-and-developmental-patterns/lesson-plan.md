# Lesson plan — The biogenetic law and developmental patterns

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Biology · **Lesson:** The biogenetic law and developmental patterns
**Place in the curriculum:** Biology (CURRICULUM.md Unit 16).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** The biogenetic law — in its individual development (ontogeny), an organism briefly repeats the stages its group passed through in its evolutionary history (phylogeny); Uzbekistan's Grade 9 textbook states it as a law at the close of its development chapter (Part IV Ch. 10 §42, printed p. 101).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the earlier biology of reproduction and development, and builds the understanding of development as a pattern that connects one organism's growth to its group's history.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier biology lessons on reproduction, meiosis and fertilisation, and on embryonic development. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Explain the biogenetic law in their own words, naming ontogeny and phylogeny correctly.
2. Give the embryo-resemblance evidence the law is read from.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson itself.

## What you need
Paper and pencil; the learner's own surroundings for examples.
