# Hands-on — The biogenetic law and developmental patterns

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
If eggs are being incubated or tadpoles can be watched where you live, observe one developing organism over several days and note, in order, the stages you see. Then say which of those stages are shared with other animals' embryos and which belong to this animal alone — the shared early stages are what the biogenetic law is read from.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — a row of embryos (fish, bird, mammal) alike at the matching early stage and diverging as they develop, with "ontogeny" written over the row and "phylogeny" under it — and check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
