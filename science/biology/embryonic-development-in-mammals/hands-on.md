# Hands-on — Embryonic development in mammals

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
On paper, draw the journey in four boxes: one cell; the dividing ball of cells; the embryo settled in the uterus with placenta and cord; the newborn mammal. Under each box write one sentence saying what changes at that step. Then find two mammals you know and compare how long each develops inside its mother before birth.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — a fertilised egg cell dividing into 2, 4, 8 cells, the ball of cells settling into the wall of the uterus, and the growing embryo inside its sac with the placenta and cord joining it to the mother — and check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
