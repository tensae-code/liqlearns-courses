Today: Embryonic development in mammals.

Remember this one thing: In mammals the young develop inside the mother's body: after fertilisation the single cell divides again and again, the embryo settles in the uterus and is fed through the placenta until it is born.

A mammal does not begin life out in the open. It begins inside its mother, from a single cell.

That cell divides — two, four, eight — and the ball of cells becomes an embryo, settling into the wall of the uterus. There the placenta grows, and across it the mother's blood feeds the embryo and carries its wastes away, until development is complete and the young mammal is born.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
