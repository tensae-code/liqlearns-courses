# Viruses — cycles, uses and HIV

## Do it first
Before you read: think of one living thing near your home that shows this lesson's idea — viruses. Hold it in your mind, then read on and check your idea against what the lesson teaches.

Viruses are classified by virion morphology, physicochemical and genome properties, proteins, replication and antigens; six virion shapes are listed (spherical, rod, brick, tadpole, bullet, filament); virus uses include phage typing, pesticides, anti-tumour agents and gene vectors for protein production and genetic-disease treatment; life/non-life criteria are debated (they multiply and carry DNA/RNA but have no wall/membrane, no metabolism, no energy enzymes and no autonomy); lytic and lysogenic cycles and the bacteriophage cycle are figured; retroviruses insert an RNA-genome copy into host DNA; HIV's surface spikes are glycoprotein, binding host-cell receptors, and HIV/AIDS is framed as a threat to social and economic development that can drive poverty, hitting women and young people hardest.
