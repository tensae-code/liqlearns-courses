# Viruses — cycles, uses and HIV — short answer

**1. In your own words, what does this lesson teach about added virus detail?**
**Answer: Viruses are classified by virion morphology, physicochemical and genome properties, proteins, replication and antigens; six virion shapes are listed (spherical, rod, brick, tadpole, bullet, filament); virus uses include phage typing, pesticides, anti-tumour agents and gene vectors for protein production and genetic-disease treatment; life/non-life criteria are debated (they multiply and carry DNA/RNA but have no wall/membrane, no metabolism, no energy enzymes and no autonomy); lytic and lysogenic cycles and the bacteriophage cycle are figured; retroviruses insert an RNA-genome copy into host DNA; HIV's surface spikes are glycoprotein, binding host-cell receptors, and HIV/AIDS is framed as a threat to social and economic development that can drive poverty, hitting women and young people hardest.**
