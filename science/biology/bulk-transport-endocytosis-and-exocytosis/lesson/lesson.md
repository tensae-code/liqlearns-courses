# Bulk transport — endocytosis and exocytosis

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Bulk transport — endocytosis and exocytosis. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
endocytosis for large particles; exocytosis drawn step by step: Golgi packs material into secretory vesicles that fuse with the plasma membrane (neurotransmitter release as the example)

[Picture — a cell with two arrows: a large particle being wrapped by the membrane and taken in, labelled endocytosis, and a vesicle fusing with the membrane and releasing its contents outside, labelled exocytosis]

## The key facts
**Endocytosis.** Kuwait's new Grade 10 student book (Lesson 1-4, printed pp. 72–73) teaches bulk transport for large particles: endocytosis takes large particles into the cell.

**Exocytosis step by step.** The book draws exocytosis step by step: the Golgi apparatus packs material into secretory vesicles, the vesicles fuse with the plasma membrane, and the material is released outside — with neurotransmitter release as the worked example.

**Kuwait's way.** Kuwait's new Grade 10 book closes its transport lesson with bulk transport drawn as a sequence (Lesson 1-4, printed pp. 72–73): the learner follows a secretory vesicle from the Golgi to the membrane, with neurotransmitter release as the example that matters later in the nervous-system lessons.
