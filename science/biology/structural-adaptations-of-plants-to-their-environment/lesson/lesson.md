# Structural adaptations of plants to their environment

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Structural adaptations of plants to their environment. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
A plant's structure is fitted to the place it lives — support structures hold it up, and in arid conditions a cactus turns its leaves into spines so it loses less water to evaporation. No lesson in this course taught this on its own before, so this lesson is built for it — from Moldova's Ministry of Education biology textbooks, which teach it as a named topic.

[Picture — a cactus in dry ground with its leaves turned to spines labelled, beside a green plant whose support structures hold it upright]

## The key facts
**5.1 Structural adaptations of plants.** Moldova's Grade 6 book (5.1 Structural adaptations of plants) teaches: support structures, cactus leaves turned to spines reducing evaporation in arid conditions. (printed p. 68).

**Moldova's way.** Moldova's Grade 6 book teaches plant adaptation as structure first: support structures, then the cactus's spines as leaves changed to cut evaporation in arid conditions.
