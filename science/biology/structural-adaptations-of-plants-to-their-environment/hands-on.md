# Hands-on — Structural adaptations of plants to their environment

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: a plant's structure is fitted to the place it lives — support structures hold it up, and in arid conditions a cactus turns its leaves into spines so it loses less water to evaporation.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a cactus in dry ground with its leaves turned to spines labelled, beside a green plant whose support structures hold it upright. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
