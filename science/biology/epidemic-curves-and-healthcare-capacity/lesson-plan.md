# Lesson plan — Epidemic curves and healthcare capacity

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Biology · **Lesson:** Epidemic curves and healthcare capacity
**Place in the curriculum:** Biology — appended to Unit 16 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** an epidemic curve — cases plotted against time — shows why protective measures matter: without them the curve rises fast to a high peak that crosses the line of healthcare capacity; with them the same illness spreads slower, the peak stays lower and flatter under that line, and hospitals can care for everyone (Sweden, Grade 1 — *Platta ut kurvan (Flatten the curve)*, SPSM tactile sheet set, PDF pp. 1–2).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly and follow the whole lesson. COVID-19 — the spiked virus and how to stop it is recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: **COVID-19 — the spiked virus and how to stop it**. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words, the one thing this lesson teaches: protective measures flatten the epidemic curve so its peak stays under the healthcare-capacity line.
2. Read the two curves on the graph — with protective measures (med skyddsåtgärder) and without (utan skyddsåtgärder) — and say which peak crosses the capacity line.
3. Explain Sweden's way as the placements describe it: the whole idea taught as one touchable tactile graph, the point of protective measures read off the picture.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.

## If the learner is stuck

- Use the picture: trace the tall curve and the flat curve, and say which one crosses the hospitals' line.
