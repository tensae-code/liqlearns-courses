Today: Epidemic curves and healthcare capacity.

Remember this one thing: when a sickness spreads, the number of cases plotted against time makes a curve — and protective measures flatten that curve so its peak stays under the line of what hospitals can handle.

Without protective measures, cases climb fast and the peak shoots above the healthcare-capacity line — more sick people than the hospitals can care for at once.

With protective measures, the illness spreads slower. The curve is lower and flatter, the peak stays under the line, and everyone who needs care can get it.

Sweden teaches this as one touchable graph — platta ut kurvan, flatten the curve — so the point is read off the picture itself.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
