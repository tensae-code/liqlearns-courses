# Hands-on — Types of nervous systems — diffuse, nodal and tubular

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: animal nervous systems climb an evolutionary ladder: the diffuse nerve net of a hydra, the nodal system of nerve knots and chains in worms and insects, and the tubular system of vertebrates — a cord with a brain at its head end.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: three animals in a row — a hydra with a net of nerves spread through it, an earthworm with a knotted nerve chain, and a fish with a brain and spinal cord drawn as one tube — each system highlighted. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
