Today: Adaptations to the environment.

Remember this one thing: An adaptation is a feature of an organism that fits it to its environment: organisms are adapted to the abiotic factors around them — water, temperature, light — and every environment, water included, has its own adapted forms.

Every living thing is fitted to its place. A feature that fits an organism to its environment is an adaptation.

Abiotic factors — water, temperature, light — set the problems. The fish's gills and fins answer water; the desert plant's water-keeping answers dryness; thick fur answers cold. And adaptations are inherited: they belong to the kind, not to one lifetime.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
