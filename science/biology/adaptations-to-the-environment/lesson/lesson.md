# Adaptations to the environment

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Adaptations to the environment. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it — why a fish, a desert plant and a mountain animal are each built exactly for the place they live — and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Living things are not built at random: each carries features that fit the place it lives. North Macedonia's Grade 8 textbook gives adaptation a whole theme of its own (Theme 4, printed pp. 202–219): adaptations of organisms to their environment, adaptations to abiotic factors, adaptations for life in water, and adaptations across different environments. In this course the existing lessons carry adaptation inside other topics — animal adaptations, plant adaptations to habitats — but no lesson taught adaptation itself as the one thing, so this lesson is built for it.

[Picture — three panels side by side: a fish with gills and fins in water, a desert plant with a thick skin and deep roots under a hot sun, and a thick-furred animal in snow — each labelled with the feature that fits it to its place]

## The key facts
**An adaptation fits an organism to its environment.** An adaptation is any feature — of build, of working, or of behaviour — that helps an organism survive and reproduce where it lives. The feature and the environment are a pair: judge an adaptation by the place it answers.

**Abiotic factors set the problems.** The non-living (abiotic) factors — water supply, temperature, light, air — are what organisms must be fitted to. Too little water, great cold, deep shade: each factor selects its own answers.

**Water life has its own adaptations.** Aquatic organisms are fitted to water: gills or other surfaces for taking oxygen from water, fins, flippers or streamlined shapes for moving in it, and ways of keeping the body's water and salts balanced in fresh or salt water.

**Different environments, different fits.** The same factor is answered differently in different environments: desert organisms keep water, cold-country animals keep heat, deep-water and high-mountain forms each carry their own fittings. The environment writes the specification; the adaptation is the reply.

**Adaptations are inherited.** An adaptation is not learned in one lifetime — it is carried in the heredity of the kind, present in the population because those with it survived and reproduced. That is why adaptation belongs beside evolution, where North Macedonia's book puts it next.

**North Macedonia's way.** North Macedonia's Grade 8 book makes adaptations a theme on their own — Theme 4, printed pp. 202–219: adaptation to the environment, to abiotic factors, aquatic adaptations and adaptations in different environments — standing between the reproduction themes and the closing theme on evolution, so adaptation is learned first as fact and then explained by natural selection.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words: An adaptation is a feature of an organism that fits it to its environment: organisms are adapted to the abiotic factors around them — water, temperature, light — and every environment, water included, has its own adapted forms. Use each key fact once. Then let your companion explain it back to you.
