# Hands-on — Adaptations to the environment

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Choose one organism near your home. List its environment's chief abiotic factors (water, temperature, light, air) down one side of a page, and beside each, the feature of the organism that answers it. If a row stays empty, observe the organism again until you can fill it.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — three panels side by side: a fish with gills and fins in water, a desert plant with a thick skin and deep roots under a hot sun, and a thick-furred animal in snow — each labelled with the feature that fits it to its place — and check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
