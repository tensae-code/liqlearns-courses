# Cell junctions — tight, gap and desmosomes — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. What is the one thing this lesson teaches?**
a) neighbouring cells are joined by long-lived junctions built from protein fibres — tight junctions, gap junctions and desmosomes — each kind joining cells in its own way  b) That this topic has no rules and every case is different  c) A list of names with no process behind them  d) That the topic belongs only to scientists, not to daily life
**Answer: a) neighbouring cells are joined by long-lived junctions built from protein fibres — tight junctions, gap junctions and desmosomes — each kind joining cells in its own way**

**2. Junctions between cells — which statement is right?**
a) Kuwait's new Grade 10 student book (Lesson 1-4, printed pp. 76–77) teaches cell junctions as long-lived protein-fibre junctions between cells.  b) The opposite of what the lesson teaches  c) It happens only in examinations  d) It is never observed in real life
**Answer: a) Kuwait's new Grade 10 student book (Lesson 1-4, printed pp. 76–77) teaches cell junctions as long-lived protein-fibre junctions between cells.**

**3. Which statement about this lesson's topic is right?**
a) It is unrelated to the lesson's topic  b) Learners complete a comparison table of the junction types and then label the junction types on a diagram of the digestive tract — the junctions are learned where they actually occur.  c) It is a myth the book corrects  d) It applies only outside Kuwait
**Answer: b) Learners complete a comparison table of the junction types and then label the junction types on a diagram of the digestive tract — the junctions are learned where they actually occur.**
