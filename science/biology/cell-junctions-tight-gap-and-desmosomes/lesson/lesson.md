# Cell junctions — tight, gap and desmosomes

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Cell junctions — tight, gap and desmosomes. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
long-lived protein-fibre junctions between cells; learners complete a comparison table and label junction types on a digestive-tract diagram

[Picture — two cells side by side with the three junction kinds drawn between them and labelled: a sealed tight junction, a gap junction channel, and a desmosome anchoring the two cells together]

## The key facts
**Junctions between cells.** Kuwait's new Grade 10 student book (Lesson 1-4, printed pp. 76–77) teaches cell junctions as long-lived protein-fibre junctions between cells.

**Naming them on a real tissue.** Learners complete a comparison table of the junction types and then label the junction types on a diagram of the digestive tract — the junctions are learned where they actually occur.

**Kuwait's way.** Kuwait's new Grade 10 book teaches junctions comparatively (Lesson 1-4): a table to complete, then a labelling task on the digestive tract, so the three kinds are told apart by structure and place, not memorised as a list.
