# Hands-on — Cell junctions — tight, gap and desmosomes

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: neighbouring cells are joined by long-lived junctions built from protein fibres — tight junctions, gap junctions and desmosomes — each kind joining cells in its own way.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: two cells side by side with the three junction kinds drawn between them and labelled: a sealed tight junction, a gap junction channel, and a desmosome anchoring the two cells together. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
