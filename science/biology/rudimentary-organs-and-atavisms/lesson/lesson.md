Some structures make sense only as inheritances. ## The key facts
**Rudimentary organs are reduced leftovers.** A rudimentary (vestigial) organ is a structure that is smaller and simpler than the working organ in related kinds, and no longer does its old job — it persists because it was inherited, not because it is needed.

**The human examples are famous.** The appendix, the coccyx — the fused tail bones at the spine's end — wisdom teeth in a jaw too small for them, and the muscles that once moved the ears: each matches a working structure in other mammals.

**Other kinds carry their own.** The reduced hind limbs under the skin of whales and some snakes, and the shrunk wings of flightless birds, are rudimentary organs too — the pattern repeats wherever a way of life changed.

**Atavisms are reappearances.** An atavism is an ancestral feature that appears again in a single individual — a baby born with a short tail, a horse with extra toes. The heredity for the old structure is still carried, and occasionally it shows.

**Together they testify.** Reduced organs and reappearing ancestral features are evidence for evolution: structures are explained by descent — kinds carry remodeled, reduced and sometimes reawakened parts of their ancestors' bodies.

**North Macedonia's way.** North Macedonia's Biology I builds its evolution theme as a court of evidence — fossils and biogeography, transitional forms, comparative anatomy and embryology — and gives rudimentary organs and atavisms a lesson of their own in the middle of it (Theme 4.6, printed p. 165), not a footnote inside another topic.
