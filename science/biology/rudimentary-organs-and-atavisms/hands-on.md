# Hands-on — Rudimentary organs and atavisms

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Make a two-column table: 'structure in us' and 'the working version in a relative'. Fill it for the appendix, the coccyx and wisdom teeth, using library books or a trusted adult's knowledge. Then write one sentence under the table saying what the pattern, taken together, is evidence for.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — a human outline marking the appendix, the coccyx (tail bone) and wisdom teeth, beside the same structures shown working in an ancestor — a long gut appendix, a real tail, a full strong jaw — and check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
