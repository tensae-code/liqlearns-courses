# How life began — the competing accounts

## Do it first
Before you read: think of one living thing near your home that shows this lesson's idea — how life began. Hold it in your mind, then read on and check your idea against what the lesson teaches.

The guide walks the origin positions: science (observation and empirical testing, no room for morality or purpose) vs religion (faith in the unprovable); creationist variants including one accepting the Big Bang and a fossil record of separate creations but not common descent, and theistic/evolutionary creationism (God inventing and taking part in evolution); spontaneous generation as decisively dispelled by Pasteur's 19th-century experiments; the eternity-of-life theory (life has no beginning, an inherent property of the universe) and the cosmozoa idea; biochemical origin (abiogenesis) with Miller's 1953 spark-discharge experiment narrated, plus chemo-autotrophs — primitive autotrophs making organics from chemical energy, mostly bacteria and archaea of hostile environments; the answer key honestly notes the theory's problem that purines were not synthesised under the same conditions as pyrimidines.
