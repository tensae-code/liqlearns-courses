# Noise and radiation pollution

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Noise and radiation pollution. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Most pollution lessons stop at dirty air and dirty water. Unit 7 of Yemen's Grade 12 Biology and Earth Science book — The environment and its problems — goes further: after air pollutants and water pollution, it gives noise pollution (التلوث الضوضائي, printed p. 173) and radiation pollution (التلوث الإشعاعي, printed p. 176) headed sections of their own, before food pollutants (p. 178) and soil pollution (p. 180). Pollution, in Yemen's sorting, is any harmful thing let loose in the environment — including sound you cannot hold and rays you cannot see.

[Picture — a busy city junction — loud traffic, horns and a road drill on one side — and a sealed radioactive source container with the trefoil warning sign being checked with a radiation meter on the other]

## The key facts
**Noise pollution.** Noise pollution (التلوث الضوضائي) is unwanted, harmful sound — Yemen's book teaches it under its own heading as a named kind of environmental pollution (printed p. 173).

**Radiation pollution.** Radiation pollution (التلوث الإشعاعي) is contamination by radioactive materials and their rays — taught next, again as its own kind (printed p. 176).

**In the pollution family.** The two invisible kinds stand in one unit with the visible ones: air pollutants and water pollution come earlier in Unit 7 (printed pp. 165–171), food pollutants (p. 178) and soil pollution (p. 180) follow them — the unit sorts pollution by what carries the harm.

**Yemen's way.** Yemen's Grade 12 environment unit (Unit 7, The environment and its problems) gives noise and radiation their own headed sections instead of folding them into air pollution, and closes the unit with the depletion of environmental resources (printed p. 181).
