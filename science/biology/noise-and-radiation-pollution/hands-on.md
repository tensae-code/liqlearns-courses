# Hands-on — Noise and radiation pollution

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Stand somewhere you can listen for two minutes — a street, a market, a workshop — and list every sound you hear. Mark which ones are noise pollution: unwanted sound that harms or disturbs. Then say aloud why radiation pollution could never be found this way, and what finds it instead.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — a busy city junction — loud traffic, horns and a road drill on one side — and a sealed radioactive source container with the trefoil warning sign being checked with a radiation meter on the other — and check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
