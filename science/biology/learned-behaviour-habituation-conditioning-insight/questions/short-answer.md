# Learned behaviour — habituation, conditioning, insight — short answer

Answer in your own words, in a sentence or two.

**1. (Sub lesson 1) In your own words, what does this lesson teach about learned behaviour?**
**Answer: Learning changes behaviour through experience: habituation (stopping responding to a repeated harmless stimulus), classical conditioning (linking a new stimulus to an existing response), operant conditioning (reinforcers raise, punishers lower the chance a behaviour repeats), sensitisation (a response growing with repeated stimulation), latent learning and insight learning — solving a problem from past experience and reasoning.**

**2. (Sub lesson 2) In your own words, what does this lesson teach about added conditioning detail?**
**Answer: The guide teaches classical conditioning through Pavlov's experiment in full: the accidental discovery while studying dogs' gastric systems, and the three stages — before conditioning (food alone causes salivation), during conditioning (bell paired with food over trials) and after conditioning (the bell alone, now a conditioned stimulus, produces the conditioned response).**

**3. (Sub lesson 3) In your own words, what does this lesson teach about added conditioning detail?**
**Answer: Operant conditioning is taught through Skinner's hungry-rat experiment — the rat accidentally presses a lever, a food pellet drops, and it quickly learns to press deliberately — with Skinner's three response types (neutral operants, reinforcers positive and negative, punishers) and punishment explained both as applying an unpleasant stimulus and as removing a rewarding one (deducting pocket money is the human example).**
