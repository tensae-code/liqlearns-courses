# Hands-on — Affective relationships and attachment

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Draw the unit's two parts yourself: on one side of the page, two circles joined by a strong band, labelled "attachment — the bond between people who are close". On the other side, a shield labelled "the right to a life free of violence in relationships". Under each drawing, write one sentence saying what it means.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — the band and the shield — label it (the unit's name, "Biología de las relaciones afectivas", written over both), and check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap. End with this one together: why does a biology book teach attachment and the right to a life free of violence, instead of leaving relationships out of science? Say why, using the book's own unit name.
