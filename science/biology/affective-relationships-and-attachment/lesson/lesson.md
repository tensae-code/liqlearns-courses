# Affective relationships and attachment

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think of the people you are closest to. There is something that ties you to them — it is not a rope, and you cannot see it, but it is real, and it changes how you act and feel. Write down, or say aloud, what you would call that tie — then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Relationships have a biology. Bolivia's Biología-Geografía book for the second year of secondary school (Segundo Año de Secundaria) teaches a named unit on exactly this: "Biología de las relaciones afectivas" — the biology of affective relationships. It sits in the book among the body's own topics — after fertilisation and embryonic development, before nutrition and the sense organs — because the book treats the bonds between people as part of the biology of being human.

[Picture — two circles joined by a strong band labelled "attachment", beside a shield labelled "a life free of violence" — the two things the unit teaches]

## The key facts
**Attachment.** The first thing the unit teaches is attachment: the bond a person builds with the people close to them. Attachment is the tie your opening task asked you to name — the connection that holds a relationship together and shapes how the people in it treat each other.

**The right to a life free of violence.** The second thing the unit teaches is a right: every person has the right to a life free of violence in their relationships. The book states it inside the biology unit itself — being close to someone never makes violence acceptable, and a relationship that carries violence breaks the right the unit names.

**A named unit, not an aside.** Bolivia's book does not drop these ideas into a margin or a poster. It gives them a unit of their own, with the unit's own name — "Biología de las relaciones afectivas" — taught in the same Práctica → Teoría → Valoración → Producción cycle (practice, theory, valuing, production) that Bolivia's "Vida Tierra Territorio" area texts run on.

**Bolivia's way.** In Bolivia's fused Biología-Geografía texts, the human being is studied whole: the same book that teaches the hormonal system and embryonic development also teaches the bonds those bodies go on to form, and the right that protects the people in them. That is the country's own way, and it is content in this lesson: relationships taught as biology-and-health, by name.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words: Bolivia's book teaches "Biología de las relaciones afectivas" — attachment, the bond between people who are close, and the right to a life free of violence in relationships. Use each key fact once. Then let your companion explain it back to you.
