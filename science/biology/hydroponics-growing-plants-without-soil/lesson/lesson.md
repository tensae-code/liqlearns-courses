# Hydroponics — growing plants without soil

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Hydroponics — growing plants without soil. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Soil is one way to feed a plant, not the only way. Vietnam's Grade 11 study topics, in their mineral-nutrition topic, give hydroponics a lesson of its own (Topic 1, Bài 2, printed p. 12): the hydroponics model for clean agriculture. The lesson lays out the whole system — tank, channels, pump and aeration — and the grower's disciplines that go with it: holding the nutrient solution and its pH under control, disinfecting the channels, and flushing the plants with clean water for 2–3 days before harvest to clear nitrate. Its yields are stated plainly: 5–6 lettuce crops a year against 2–3 in soil, with Vietnamese home and rooftop systems and commercial greenhouses as the worked examples. (Another lesson in this course meets hydroponics as the test that proves which mineral elements are essential; this lesson teaches hydroponics as the growing system itself.)

[Picture — a hydroponics bench in cross-section: a tank of nutrient solution at one end, a pump pushing the solution along a sloping channel past the roots of lettuce plants held in holes along the channel, bubbles of air rising in the tank from an aeration pipe, and the solution returning to the tank]

## The key facts
**The system.** A hydroponics unit is a tank of nutrient solution, channels that carry the solution past the plants' roots, a pump that keeps it moving, and aeration that keeps air in the water for the roots.

**Control is the work.** With no soil to buffer mistakes, the grower controls the system directly: the nutrient solution's strength and its pH are held in range, and the channels are disinfected between crops.

**The clean finish.** Vietnam's model flushes the plants with clean water for 2–3 days before harvest, clearing nitrate from the crop — part of what makes the produce 'clean agriculture' produce.

**The yield.** Run this way, the system out-crops soil: 5–6 lettuce crops a year against 2–3 in soil — and Vietnam's examples run from home and rooftop rigs to commercial greenhouses.
