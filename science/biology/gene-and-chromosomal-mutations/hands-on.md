# Hands-on — Gene and chromosomal mutations

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: mutations come at two scales: chromosomal mutations — Robertsonian translocation between chromosomes 14 and 21, inversion, and numerical mutations — and gene mutations: deletion and insertion shifting the reading frame, and the single base substitution in the haemoglobin gene.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a chromosome pair with a segment inverted, beside a DNA strip where one base is substituted and another strip where a base is inserted and every codon after it shifted. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
