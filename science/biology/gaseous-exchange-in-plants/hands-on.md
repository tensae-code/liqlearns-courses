# Hands-on — Gaseous exchange in plants

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Take the lesson's key facts for a walk: find one example of this lesson's idea in your own surroundings — your home, street, market or school — and say which key fact it matches, and why.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory — a green plant in a bell jar drawn twice — by day with arrows of carbon dioxide going in and oxygen coming out under a bright sun, and by night with the arrows reversed under a moon — beside a test tube of bicarbonate indicator changing colour and an upturned funnel collecting bubbles from a pondweed — label its parts, and check against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
