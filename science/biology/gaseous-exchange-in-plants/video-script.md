Today: Gaseous exchange in plants.

Remember this one thing: A plant is exchanging gases all the time, in two directions at once: Kenya's Form 2 book teaches plant gaseous exchange as the day/night balance of photosynthesis and respiration, proved with a bicarbonate-indicator investigation and by collecting the gas a water plant gives off.

A leaf is never still. By day it photosynthesises and breathes at the same time — taking in carbon dioxide, giving out oxygen, the photosynthesis running ahead. By night the photosynthesis stops and only respiration is left, so the exchange turns around: oxygen in, carbon dioxide out. Kenya's book proves it in the lab: a bicarbonate indicator that changes colour with the carbon dioxide around it, and the gas bubbling off a water plant, collected and tested. The plant's exchange is the balance of the two processes — and the balance swings with the light.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
