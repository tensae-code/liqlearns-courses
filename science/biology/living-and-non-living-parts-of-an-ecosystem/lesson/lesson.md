# Living and non-living parts of an ecosystem

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Living and non-living parts of an ecosystem. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Sort a real local scene into the two lists — that sorting is the skill.

[Picture 21074 — a landscape split into biotic and abiotic halves]

## The key facts
**Biotic.** Biotic components are all the living members of an ecosystem.

**Abiotic.** Abiotic components are non-living factors — light, temperature, water, soil and air.

**Shaping.** Abiotic factors shape which organisms can live in a place.

**Sorting.** Pupils sort a real local scene into biotic and abiotic lists.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words: every ecosystem mixes biotic (living) components with abiotic (non-living) factors that shape who can live there. Use each key fact once. Then let your companion explain it back to you.
