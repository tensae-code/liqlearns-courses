# Hands-on — Cyanobacteria — the blue-green algae

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Look at a pond, a wet rock or a damp wall shaded blue-green: that colour is often cyanobacteria at work. Without touching, describe what you see — where it grows, how thick, what colour — and say why a photosynthetic bacterium would choose that place.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — a picture of cyanobacteria two ways — a filament drawn as a chain of round cells with one larger specialised cell marked, beside a single cell opened to show it has no nucleus — and a pond edge coloured blue-green by their mats — label it, and check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
