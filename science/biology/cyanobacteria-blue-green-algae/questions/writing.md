**1.** Write two or three sentences explaining cyanobacteria — the blue-green algae, using the lesson's key facts.
**Model answer: Cyanobacteria — the blue-green algae — are bacteria that photosynthesise: single cells and filaments of Kingdom Monera that make their own food from sunlight like plants do, which is why they were long mistaken for algae. Cyanobacteria are true bacteria — prokaryotic cells of Kingdom Monera, with no nucleus and no membrane-bound organelles. Their old name, blue-green algae, records the mistake: they are green, they live in water and light, and they were classed with the algae until their cell structure showed what they are. In the Year 13 book the cyanobacteria are not a line inside the bacteria survey: §3.2.2 gives them their own named section within Kingdom Monera, between the bacteria proper and the Protista that follow — Fiji's survey teaches them as a group a learner must be able to place and to tell from the true algae.**
