Today: Cyanobacteria — the blue-green algae.

Remember this one thing: Cyanobacteria — the blue-green algae — are bacteria that photosynthesise: single cells and filaments of Kingdom Monera that make their own food from sunlight like plants do, which is why they were long mistaken for algae.

Cyanobacteria are true bacteria — prokaryotic cells of Kingdom Monera, with no nucleus and no membrane-bound organelles. Their old name, blue-green algae, records the mistake: they are green, they live in water and light, and they were classed with the algae until their cell structure showed what they are.

Cyanobacteria carry photosynthetic pigments inside the cell and make their own food from sunlight, water and carbon dioxide, giving out oxygen — the plant way of feeding, done by a bacterium. Mats and filaments of them colour ponds and wet rocks blue-green.

They live as single cells, as colonies, and as filaments — chains of cells in a row — and some of their cells are specialised: in many filament forms, particular cells fix nitrogen from the air, turning it into a form living things can use, a service few organisms can do.
