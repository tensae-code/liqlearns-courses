# Cyanobacteria — the blue-green algae

## The idea
Fiji's Year 13 Biology book surveys Kingdom Monera inside its diversity of life, and inside that survey it stops and gives the blue-green algae their own named section. They earn it: they look like algae, live like algae — and are bacteria through and through.

## The key facts
**Bacteria, not algae.** Cyanobacteria are true bacteria — prokaryotic cells of Kingdom Monera, with no nucleus and no membrane-bound organelles. Their old name, blue-green algae, records the mistake: they are green, they live in water and light, and they were classed with the algae until their cell structure showed what they are.

**They photosynthesise.** Cyanobacteria carry photosynthetic pigments inside the cell and make their own food from sunlight, water and carbon dioxide, giving out oxygen — the plant way of feeding, done by a bacterium. Mats and filaments of them colour ponds and wet rocks blue-green.

**Cells and filaments.** They live as single cells, as colonies, and as filaments — chains of cells in a row — and some of their cells are specialised: in many filament forms, particular cells fix nitrogen from the air, turning it into a form living things can use, a service few organisms can do.

**Fiji's way.** In the Year 13 book the cyanobacteria are not a line inside the bacteria survey: §3.2.2 gives them their own named section within Kingdom Monera, between the bacteria proper and the Protista that follow — Fiji's survey teaches them as a group a learner must be able to place and to tell from the true algae.
