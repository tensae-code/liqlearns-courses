## The idea
North Macedonia's Biology II closes with a biotechnology and genetic-engineering theme, and directly after its applied-biology opener it names synthetic biology and CRISPR technology as a lesson of their own (Theme 4.2, printed p. 247). No lesson in this course named CRISPR or synthetic biology before, so this lesson is built for it.

## The key facts
**Synthetic biology builds with life.** Synthetic biology treats living systems as engineerable: it designs and builds new biological parts, circuits and organisms — or redesigns existing ones — for chosen purposes, joining biology to engineering's design-and-test way of working.

**CRISPR is a borrowed defence system.** CRISPR comes from bacteria, which use it to cut the DNA of attacking viruses. Scientists turned that system into a tool: a guide molecule leads the cutting protein (Cas9) to one chosen place in any DNA.

**Editing is find, cut, repair.** Guided to its target, the tool cuts the DNA. The cell repairs the cut — and in the repair a gene can be knocked out, corrected, or replaced with a supplied version. That is gene editing: precise, cheap and far faster than the older methods.

**Uses reach from medicine to farms.** CRISPR and synthetic biology are used to study genes, to work toward treatments for inherited diseases, to build microbes that produce medicines and materials, and to breed plants with chosen traits — with biosafety and ethics questions travelling alongside, as the same book's later lessons take up.

**North Macedonia's way.** North Macedonia's Biology II gives synthetic biology and CRISPR their own named lesson (Theme 4.2, printed p. 247) at the head of its closing biotechnology theme — before tissue culture, fermentation products, forensics and gene therapy — so the newest tools are met first, as tools, and their uses follow.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words: Synthetic biology designs and builds biological systems as engineering — and CRISPR is its sharpest tool: a system borrowed from bacteria that can cut DNA at a chosen place, so a gene can be removed, changed or replaced with precision.
