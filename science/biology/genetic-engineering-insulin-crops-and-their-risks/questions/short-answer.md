# Genetic engineering — insulin, crops and their risks — short answer

Answer in your own words, in a sentence or two.

**1. In your own words, what does this lesson teach about added genetic-engineering answers?**
**Answer: Engineered crop traits are exemplified (glyphosate herbicide tolerance in soybean, maize, canola and cotton; insect toxins in maize and cotton); gene therapy is explained as correcting, replacing or adding genes (insulin genes for diabetics, clotting factors for haemophilia, immune cells for cancer); recombinant human insulin production is walked through (human insulin gene into a bacterial plasmid, bacteria produce the insulin, it is purified) with its advantages over animal insulin (quantity, cost, and modifiable insulin analogues with faster or longer action); bioremediation is praised as natural, eco-friendly, cost-effective and scalable; and the counterweight is given in full — plant biotechnology's challenges (biosafety, public acceptance, gene flow, new weeds, lost diversity, allergies/toxicity/antibiotic-resistance fears) and GM crops' social economics (dependence on patent-owning multinationals, a widening rich–poor farmer gap), plus the ethics of dignity, animal welfare and oversight.**
