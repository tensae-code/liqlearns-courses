# Cellular respiration — glycolysis, Krebs cycle, electron transport

## Do it first
Before you read: think of one living thing near your home that shows this lesson's idea — cellular respiration. Hold it in your mind, then read on and check your idea against what the lesson teaches.

A comparison table (mislabelled "Table 2.4" inside Unit Three) sets photosynthesis against respiration; oxidation and reduction are explained as always coupled through electron exchange; and worked answers state that the electron-transport chain's proton gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane drives ATP synthesis by chemiosmosis, that exhaled carbon dioxide comes from pyruvate oxidation and the citric-acid cycle, that without oxygen oxidative phosphorylation stops entirely, and that artificially lowering pH (adding H+) can set up a gradient and drive ATP synthase — noted as historically demonstrated experimentally.
