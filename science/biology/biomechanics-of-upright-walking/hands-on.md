# Hands-on — Biomechanics of upright walking

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: upright walking is a physical balancing act: the spine's curves, the arched foot, the pelvis and the five phases of a step fit the human skeleton and muscles to moving steadily on two legs.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a walking figure drawn in the five phases of one step, with the spine's curves and the foot's arch marked and a vertical line from the centre of mass falling between the feet. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
