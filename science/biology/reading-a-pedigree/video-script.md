# Video script — Reading a pedigree

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

---

Family trees drawn with squares (males), circles (females), marriage lines and shaded affected members let scientists read a trait's history: worked pedigrees for an autosomal dominant and an autosomal recessive trait show how analysis reveals whether a trait is dominant or recessive, autosomal or sex-linked, the genotypes of family members, and the risks to future children.

Two fully narrated pedigrees beyond the student book's figures: freckles (dominant F) traced over three generations from a freckled grandmother, counting which children and grandchildren show the trait; and colour blindness (recessive, X-linked) over four generations, with the reasoning made explicit — unaffected parents with an affected child proves recessive, and males being exclusively affected points to X-linkage.

Now say it in your own words — and find one example near your home.
