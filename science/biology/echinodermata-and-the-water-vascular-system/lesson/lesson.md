# Echinodermata and the water-vascular system

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Echinodermata and the water-vascular system. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Mozambique's animal module reaches the echinoderms as the last invertebrate phylum before the chordates. These are sea animals built on a plan of five. Under the skin sits an internal skeleton of calcareous plates with spines — the 'echino' (spiny) in their name. And they run a system no other animals have: the water-vascular system, water-filled canals that work their tube feet. The module's solutions name the phylum's five classes.

## The key facts
**A plan of five.** Echinoderms are marine animals with pentaradial symmetry — the body arranged in five parts around a centre.

**A spiny internal skeleton.** The skeleton is internal, made of calcareous plates, and carries the spines that give the phylum its name.

**The water-vascular system.** The water-vascular (ambulacral) system is a network of water canals unique to this phylum — it works movement and feeding through the tube feet.

**A stripped-down inside.** Circulation is reduced and there are no excretory organs. The sexes are separate, and development runs through a larva that metamorphoses into the five-sided adult. The module's solutions name the phylum's five classes.
