# Hands-on — The language areas of the cortex

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: language lives in four areas of the left cerebral cortex — W, V, H and S — each found by the deficit its damage leaves: the patient who understands but cannot speak, and the patient who speaks but cannot understand, map them before the map is shown.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a left-brain outline with four patches marked W, V, H and S, each patch joined by a line to a small speech bubble showing what its loss takes away. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
