# Ecosystem services — what nature does for us

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Ecosystem services — what nature does for us. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Nature does work for people, for free — and that work has a name: ecosystem services. Finland's field pack groups them in three: provisioning services (food, timber), regulating and supporting services (pollination, water purification, nutrient cycling, carbon binding), and cultural services (recreation). The more diverse nature is, the better it adapts to climate change, disease and pests — so the services hold.

[Picture — a Finnish landscape divided into three labelled scenes — a forest giving timber and food, bees pollinating a meadow while a stream is purified, and a family walking a nature trail — the three groups of ecosystem services]

## The key facts
**Services, in three groups.** An ecosystem service is work nature does that people depend on. Finland's field pack teaches them in three groups, and the grouping is the lesson's frame.

**Provisioning — nature provides.** Provisioning services are the things nature hands over directly: food and timber are the pack's named examples.

**Regulating and supporting — nature keeps the system running.** Regulating and supporting services keep the living world working: pollination of crops and wild plants, purification of water, the cycling of nutrients, and the binding of carbon.

**Cultural — nature restores people.** Cultural services are what nature does for people's inner life: recreation — being out in nature — is the pack's named example.

**Diversity keeps the services working.** The pack ties the services to biodiversity itself: more diverse nature adapts better to climate change, disease and pests. Lose the diversity and the services weaken with it.
