# Hands-on — Body symmetry and cephalisation

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: animal body plans are read by their symmetry — radial in simple animals like sea anemones, bilateral in most others — and cephalisation concentrates sense organs and nerve cells at the front end.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a sea anemone drawn from above with radial symmetry lines, beside a bilateral animal with its head end marked and sense organs and nerve cells clustered at the front, labelled cephalisation. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
