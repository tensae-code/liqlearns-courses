# Body symmetry and cephalisation — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. What is the one thing this lesson teaches?**
a) animal body plans are read by their symmetry — radial in simple animals like sea anemones, bilateral in most others — and cephalisation concentrates sense organs and nerve cells at the front end  b) That this topic has no rules and every case is different  c) A list of names with no process behind them  d) That the topic belongs only to scientists, not to daily life
**Answer: a) animal body plans are read by their symmetry — radial in simple animals like sea anemones, bilateral in most others — and cephalisation concentrates sense organs and nerve cells at the front end**

**2. Radial and bilateral symmetry — which statement is right?**
a) Kuwait's Grade 10 Term 2 student book (Unit 2, Lesson 1-1, printed pp. 19–20) teaches radial symmetry in simple animals like sea anemones, set against bilateral symmetry.  b) The opposite of what the lesson teaches  c) It happens only in examinations  d) It is never observed in real life
**Answer: a) Kuwait's Grade 10 Term 2 student book (Unit 2, Lesson 1-1, printed pp. 19–20) teaches radial symmetry in simple animals like sea anemones, set against bilateral symmetry.**

**3. Which statement about this lesson's topic is right?**
a) It is unrelated to the lesson's topic  b) The same lesson names cephalisation: sense organs and nerve cells concentrated at the front end of the body — the beginning of a head.  c) It is a myth the book corrects  d) It applies only outside Kuwait
**Answer: b) The same lesson names cephalisation: sense organs and nerve cells concentrated at the front end of the body — the beginning of a head.**
