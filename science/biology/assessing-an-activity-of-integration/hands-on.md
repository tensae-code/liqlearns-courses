# Hands-on — Assessing an activity of integration

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Take any finished piece of your own project work. Draw the four-column grid — Accuracy, Coherence, Relevance, Excellence — and judge your work under each heading, writing one honest sentence per column. Then say which single column you would improve first, and how.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — the four-column evaluation grid with its four headings written in — and check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
