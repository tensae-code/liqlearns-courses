# Lesson plan — Assessing an activity of integration

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Biology · **Lesson:** Assessing an activity of integration
**Place in the curriculum:** Biology — appended to Unit 16 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** an integrated real-life task — Uganda's activity of integration — is scored on a four-part evaluation grid: Accuracy, Coherence, Relevance and Excellence — the grid in the closing pages of Uganda's Senior One Teacher's Guide
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on teacher-guide evidence only: the grid is the assessment machinery of Uganda's Lower Secondary Curriculum, printed in the closing pages of the Senior One Teacher's Guide, where the learner's book has the activities of integration themselves. It stands after the lessons it assesses — it is a teacher's tool, usable once a learner has done integrated tasks. Nothing is recommended before it; it is never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the lessons named in the level note above. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words, the one thing this lesson teaches: an activity of integration is assessed on four criteria — Accuracy, Coherence, Relevance, Excellence.
2. Say what each of the four criteria asks of the learner's work.
3. Use the grid to judge a piece of integrated work and say which criteria it meets and which it misses.
4. State the honesty of this lesson plainly: it stands on a teacher's guide — evidence for how Uganda assesses, counted nowhere as pupil-taught.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.
