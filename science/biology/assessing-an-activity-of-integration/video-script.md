Today: Assessing an activity of integration.

Remember this one thing: when a Ugandan learner finishes an activity of integration — one big task that pulls a whole chapter into real life — the teacher does not give one vague mark. The work is read against a grid of four criteria.

Accuracy: is it right? Coherence: does it hold together, step following step? Relevance: does it answer the real-life situation it was set in? Excellence: does it rise above the merely correct?

The grid stands in the closing pages of Uganda's Senior One Teacher's Guide — the assessment machinery the learner's book only implies. This lesson stands on that guide as evidence: it shows how Uganda assesses, and it is counted nowhere as a pupil textbook.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
