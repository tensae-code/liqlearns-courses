# Hands-on — Food poisoning and food safety

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Take the lesson's key facts for a walk: find one example of this lesson's idea in your own surroundings — your home, street, market or school — and say which key fact it matches, and why.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory — a market meal under a magnifying lens: a plate of raw fish salad with three labelled callouts — a bacterium with the toxin it leaves in food, a virus particle, and a small liver fluke — each with an arrow to the dish it can arrive in — label its parts, and check against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
