# Eating disorders

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think of the things that can damage a person's health. Most people name germs first. Now write down, or say aloud, whether the way a person eats — eating far too little, or eating and then forcing it back out — could damage health too, and why — then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
An eating disorder is a disordered way of eating that damages health. Ecuador's Grade 11 biology book, Biología — 1ro BGU, does not give eating disorders a chapter of their own. It teaches them inside Sección 6, Tema 2 — "Salud integral", integral health — the section that gathers the causes that damage a person's health and reads them together, in a cause→effect table.

[Picture — a cause→effect table: on the left, five causes in a row — eating disorders, drugs, alcohol, sedentarism, pollution; on the right, the damage each one does to health, with an arrow from each cause to its effect]

## The key facts
**The two the book names.** The eating disorders Ecuador's book names are anorexia and bulimia. They stand in the book as causes of damaged health — not as habits, fashions or choices of appearance, but as health problems with effects, which is why they belong in a biology book's health section.

**The table they sit in.** In the book, eating disorders are set in a cause→effect table together with drugs, alcohol, sedentarism (a sedentary life — sitting and moving too little) and pollution. Each cause is read the same way: what it is, and what effect it has on health. The table is the teaching tool — cause on one side, effect on the other.

**The "Salud integral" frame.** "Salud integral" means health as a whole. Ecuador's frame is that health is damaged by several causes together — what a person eats, what they consume, how little they move, and the contaminated environment around them — so the book teaches the causes side by side, in one section and one table, rather than one at a time in separate chapters.

**The gap the book itself names.** Ecuador's book notes that Ecuador lacks updated statistics on anorexia and bulimia. The disorders are taught, and named, while the country's own current numbers for them are missing — the book says so plainly, and this lesson carries that gap exactly as the book states it, without inventing figures.

**Ecuador's way.** In Ecuador's 2024 "transición curricular" text, this is an activity-style treatment: the learner works the cause→effect table — matching causes to their effects on health — inside the "Salud integral" section that also carries alcohol and drugs, sedentary life and environmental contamination (printed pp. 80–85, with the eating-disorders table at printed pp. 80–81).

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words: eating disorders — anorexia and bulimia — damage health, and Ecuador's book teaches them inside "Salud integral", in a cause→effect table with drugs, alcohol, sedentarism and pollution, while noting that Ecuador lacks updated statistics on them. Use each key fact once. Then let your companion explain it back to you.
