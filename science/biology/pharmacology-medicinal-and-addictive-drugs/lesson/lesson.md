# Pharmacology — medicinal and addictive drugs

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Pharmacology — medicinal and addictive drugs. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Pharmacology is the study of drugs — what they are, how they act on the body, and how they are used: medicinal drugs that treat disease (antibiotics that kill bacteria, and vaccines that train the body's defence before disease comes) set against addictive drugs, the drugs a body comes to depend on, which harm the user and are taught as the other face of the same chapter. No lesson in this course taught this on its own before, so this lesson is built for it — from Pakistan (Punjab)'s Punjab Board books, which teach it as a named topic.

[Picture — two shelves side by side: on one, a labelled medicine bottle, a strip of antibiotic tablets and a vaccine vial with a syringe; on the other, the same shapes crossed by a warning band, with a downward spiral drawn behind them]

## The key facts
**Drugs studied as a science.** Pharmacology studies drugs themselves: their sources, their actions on the living body, their doses and their dangers. A drug is any substance that changes how the body works — for healing or for harm.

**Medicinal drugs.** Medicinal drugs are used to prevent or treat disease. Among them the book singles out antibiotics — drugs that kill or stop bacteria, used against bacterial infections — and vaccines, which present the body's defence system with a harmless form of a germ so it is ready before the real infection arrives.

**Addictive drugs.** An addictive drug is one the body and mind come to crave and depend on: the user needs more of it, suffers without it, and is harmed by it — in health, work and family. Addiction is dependence, and the chapter teaches it as a harm to be understood, not a habit to copy.

**One chapter, both faces.** Punjab's Grade 10 book closes its whole course with a pharmacology chapter in Section 6, Applications of Biology: medicinal drugs and addictive drugs, antibiotics and vaccines taught together — the applications of biology include knowing what drugs do, both ways.

**Pakistan (Punjab)'s way.** Pakistan (Punjab)'s Grade 10 book teaches pharmacology as its final chapter, Ch 18 (printed pp. 150–155): medicinal drugs; addictive drugs; antibiotics and vaccines.
