# Hands-on — Pharmacology — medicinal and addictive drugs

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Make two lists from medicines you can name at home or in a pharmacy: which are medicinal, and what each is used for. Then write, in one sentence, what makes a drug addictive instead of medicinal.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — two shelves side by side: on one, a labelled medicine bottle, a strip of antibiotic tablets and a vaccine vial with a syringe; on the other, the same shapes crossed by a warning band, with a downward spiral drawn behind them — and check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
