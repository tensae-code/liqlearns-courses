# Hands-on — Community structure — layers, seasons and richness

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: a community has a structure: its species are layered vertically, patterned horizontally and changed with the seasons, and its species richness — how many species it holds — is its measure.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a forest cut from the side showing canopy, understorey and floor layers, the same view in two seasons, and a ground-plan patchwork of different plant patches. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
