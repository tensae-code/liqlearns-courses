# Locomotion in a finned fish — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** Describe how a tilapia swims, naming the structures Kenya's book teaches.
**Answer: A streamlined body with overlapping scales and mucus cuts resistance; myotome muscle blocks bend the body and sweep the tail for thrust; the swim bladder holds buoyancy; the lateral line senses the water; paired pectoral/pelvic fins and unpaired dorsal/anal/tail fins divide steering, stability and drive (Ch. 4, printed p. 127).**

**2.** Explain Kenya's way of teaching fish locomotion.
**Answer: Kenya teaches it on one fish — the tilapia — structure by structure in the order the water meets them: surface (scales, mucus), engine (myotomes, with a tail-power calculation), float (swim bladder), senses (lateral line) and the fin kit, paired against unpaired — all confirmed by a fish-examination practical.**
