# Hands-on — Locomotion in a finned fish

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Take the lesson's key facts for a walk: find one example of this lesson's idea in your own surroundings — your home, street, market or school — and say which key fact it matches, and why.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory — a tilapia drawn mid-swim with its body bent in a shallow S — the muscle blocks (myotomes) shaded in zigzag bands along its flank, arrows showing water pushed backwards by the tail — its overlapping scales and a mucus sheen marked, the swim bladder drawn as a pale float inside, the lateral line dotted along its side, and each fin labelled: paired pectoral and pelvic fins, unpaired dorsal, anal and tail fins — label its parts, and check against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
