Today: Locomotion in a finned fish.

Remember this one thing: A fish swims with muscle blocks, a gas float and a kit of fins: Kenya's Form 4 book teaches locomotion in the tilapia — streamlining, overlapping scales and mucus, myotomes, the swim bladder and buoyancy, the lateral line, and paired against unpaired fins — with a fish-examination practical and a tail-power calculation.

Watch a tilapia and every part of it is working at swimming. The body is streamlined, its overlapping scales lie flat under a coat of mucus, so the water slides. The engine is muscle arranged in blocks — myotomes — bending the body side to side so the tail sweeps and pushes the water back; Kenya even sets a calculation on the tail's power. Depth is governed by the swim bladder, a gas float the fish inflates or deflates to hold its buoyancy, and the lateral line reads the water's movement around it. The fins divide the labour: the unpaired fins — dorsal, anal and the great tail fin — against the paired pectoral and pelvic fins that steer and brake.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
