# Brazilian biomes and the Mata Atlântica

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Brazilian biomes and the Mata Atlântica. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about the forests of Brazil, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
A biome is one of the Earth's great living regions — a wide land with its own climate, its own plants and its own animals, all fitting together. Brazil has biomes of its own, and Brazil's Paraná public textbook teaches them in its Biodiversity block. One of them, the Mata Atlântica — the Atlantic Forest, the forest of Brazil's Atlantic coast — gets a whole chapter to itself, because it is disappearing. The chapter does not open with a definition. It opens with a cry for help.

[Picture — Brazil's Atlantic coast with a band of green forest running down it, most of the band faded or cut away and only small patches of green remaining]

## The key facts
**Brazil's own biomes.** The book's Biodiversity block first asks about biomes as a whole — Ch. 11, Biomas: "paraísos naturais ou recursos inesgotáveis?" (natural paradises or inexhaustible resources?). Brazil's own biomes are inside that question: a biome can be looked at as a paradise to protect, or as a store of resources to use — and the book makes the learner hold the question, not just the list.

**The Mata Atlântica.** The Mata Atlântica is the Atlantic Forest — the forest biome of Brazil's Atlantic coast. It is Brazil's own forest, the one its learners can name from their own country, not a far-away example.

**A forest disappearing.** The Atlantic Forest has been cut down so far that what remains stands in patches where a great forest once ran. That disappearance is the chapter's whole subject: Ch. 12 is not a tour of the forest's plants and animals — it is a chapter about a forest that is going.

**The cry for help.** Brazil's way is the chapter title itself: "socorro!!! Cadê você???" (Help!!! Where are you???). The forest is spoken to as someone who has gone missing — you call for it, and it does not answer, because so much of it is no longer there. The question is the teaching: a learner who has asked "where are you?" to a forest has understood what its loss means.

**Why one biome earns a chapter.** A biome is not only trees. It is the whole living community of a region — its plants, its animals, and the fits between them. When a biome disappears, that whole community goes with it, and no planting of single trees brings the community back. That is why the book spends a whole chapter on one Brazilian forest inside a block that also covers the biomes of the whole world.

**Brazil's way.** In Brazil's Grade 10 public textbook, every chapter is framed as a question, in an inquiry style. The biomes are asked about ("paradises or inexhaustible resources?"), and the Mata Atlântica is cried out for ("socorro!!! Cadê você???") — the learner is put inside the loss, not outside it.
