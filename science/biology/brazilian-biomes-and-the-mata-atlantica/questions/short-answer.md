# Brazilian biomes and the Mata Atlântica — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** Explain: why Brazil's textbook gives the Mata Atlântica a whole chapter of its own.
**Answer: The Mata Atlântica, Brazil's Atlantic Forest, is disappearing — so much of it has been cut down that what remains stands in patches. Because a biome is a whole living community and not only trees, that loss is the loss of the community itself, so the book spends a whole chapter on this one Brazilian forest inside its Biodiversity block.**

**2.** Explain: Brazil's way of teaching this lesson.
**Answer: Every chapter of the Paraná public textbook is framed as a question. The biomes chapter asks "paraísos naturais ou recursos inesgotáveis?" (natural paradises or inexhaustible resources?), and the Mata Atlântica chapter is titled with a cry for help — "socorro!!! Cadê você???" (Help!!! Where are you???) — speaking to the forest as someone who has gone missing, so the learner is put inside the loss, not outside it.**
