# Lesson plan — Telling plants from animals

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Biology · **Lesson:** Telling plants from animals
**Place in the curriculum:** Biology — appended to Unit 16 (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** plants and animals can be told apart by how they live: plants make their own food and stay rooted in one place, while animals eat other living things and move about — and Uganda's Senior One book closes its introduction chapter with exactly this distinction (Ch 1, printed p. 8)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands at the end of the introduction to biology — after What biology is and How we know something is alive — because Uganda's book itself places the plant–animal distinction as the closing topic of its Chapter 1 (printed p. 8). How we know something is alive is recommended first, never forced.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the lessons named in the level note above. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words, the one thing this lesson teaches: plants and animals are told apart by how they get food and whether they move from place to place.
2. Name the differences Uganda's book uses: making food vs eating, staying rooted vs moving about, and green leaves carrying chlorophyll vs no chlorophyll.
3. Sort a mixed list of living things into plants and animals and defend each sorting.
4. Explain Uganda's way as the placements describe it: the distinction is the named closing topic of the introduction chapter (Ch 1, printed p. 8) — the first sorting a Ugandan learner is asked to make.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. Nothing else.
