Today: Soil pollution and its control.

Remember this one thing: The soil can be poisoned as surely as air and water: Kenya's Form 3 book gives soil pollution its own treatment — acid rain, aerosols with heavy metals, petroleum spills, inorganic fertilisers and solid waste — and its control, from compost pits to legislation, with radioactive emissions handled beside it.

Pollution teaching usually stops at air and water. Kenya's ecology chapter goes one step further, into the ground itself. Acid rain and metal-laden aerosols fall on it; petroleum spills soak into it; too much inorganic fertiliser and dumped solid waste load it. And every source has its answer: recycling and compost pits for the waste, incineration and biological control, pipelines instead of spill-prone transport for petroleum, and legislation over all of it. The book closes the block with radioactive emissions and their control — the pollution you cannot see, held by rules you must enforce.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
