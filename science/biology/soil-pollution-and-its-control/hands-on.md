# Hands-on — Soil pollution and its control

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Take the lesson's key facts for a walk: find one example of this lesson's idea in your own surroundings — your home, street, market or school — and say which key fact it matches, and why.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory — a field cross-section in two halves — on one side the poisons arriving: acid rain falling, factory aerosol drifting down with heavy-metal specks, a split oil drum leaking petroleum, fertiliser sacks and dumped solid waste — and on the other side the controls at work: a compost pit, recycling bins, a pipeline carrying petroleum safely, and a sign of the law over the field — label its parts, and check against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
