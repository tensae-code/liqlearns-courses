# Hands-on — Channel proteins — aquaporins and ion channels

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: channel proteins are fitted pores through the membrane — aquaporins pass water and ion channels pass particular ions — a whole class of membrane proteins with its own discovery story, from Agre's water channel (1988) to MacKinnon's potassium-channel structure (1998).

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a cell membrane crossed by two pores — a narrow water channel with single-file water molecules, and a potassium channel whose shape fits potassium ions only. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
