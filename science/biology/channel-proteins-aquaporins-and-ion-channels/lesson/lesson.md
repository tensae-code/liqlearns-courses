# Channel proteins — aquaporins and ion channels

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Channel proteins — aquaporins and ion channels. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Channel proteins are fitted pores through the membrane — aquaporins pass water and ion channels pass particular ions — a whole class of membrane proteins with its own discovery story, from Agre's water channel (1988) to MacKinnon's potassium-channel structure (1998). No lesson in this course taught this on its own before, so this lesson is built for it — from China's People's Education Press biology textbooks, which teach it as a named topic.

[Picture — a cell membrane crossed by two pores — a narrow water channel with single-file water molecules, and a potassium channel whose shape fits potassium ions only]

## The key facts
**Ch. 4 history feature "Humanity's exploration of channel proteins".** Agre isolating the water channel in 1988 (13 aquaporins now known in human cells, 35 in Arabidopsis), Neher and Sakmann's patch clamp (Nobel 1991), MacKinnon's potassium-channel structure (1998), and the shared 2003 chemistry Nobel — a whole class of membrane proteins with its own discovery story, held by no existing lesson.
