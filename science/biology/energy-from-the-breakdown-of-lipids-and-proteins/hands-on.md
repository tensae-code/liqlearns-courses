# Hands-on — Energy from the breakdown of lipids and proteins

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: cells also burn lipids and proteins for energy: fats are split into fatty acids and glycerol that enter respiration's pathways — gram for gram fat yields more than carbohydrate — and amino acids enter after their nitrogen is removed, tying energy release back to photosynthesis as the source of it all.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: three fuel arrows — a fat droplet, a protein chain and a glucose ring — all feeding into one furnace labelled respiration, with the fat arrow drawn widest. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
