# Hands-on — IVF and assisted reproduction

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: assisted reproduction helps where natural conception fails — artificial insemination, IVF and embryo transfer, sperm and egg banks — the technology that gave the world Louise Brown in 1978 and mainland China its first test-tube baby in 1988, held under China's management measures and technical norms so embryo gene testing cannot slide into customised babies.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a laboratory dish where a fine needle brings a sperm to an egg, drawn simply, beside a calendar line from 1978 to 1988. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
