# Support in plants and animals — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** Explain: support in a plant against support in an animal.
**Answer: A plant is held up by its own grown body — its stem and other parts keep it upright and spread its leaves towards the light. An animal is held up by its supporting structures, which keep the body's shape, protect the soft parts and give the muscles something to pull against when it moves. Both resist gravity and keep their parts in place; the plant does it standing still, the animal does it with a body built to move.**

**2.** Explain: Sudan's way of teaching this.
**Answer: Sudan's Grade 10 biology book gives support a whole unit of its own — Unit Five: Support in Living Organisms (printed pp. 137–149) — placed after the movement unit (Unit Four) and covering support in plants and support in animals together, closing the book.**
