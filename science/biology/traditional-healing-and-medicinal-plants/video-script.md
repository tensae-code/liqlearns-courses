Today: Traditional healing and medicinal plants.

Remember this one thing: in Samoa, health is balance — a balance between the natural, the social and the supernatural parts of life. When the balance breaks, a person is sick. When balance is restored, the person is well.

The healer is the taulasea. Among the taulasea's treatments is fofo — massage and manipulation with the hands — and medicines made from plants, each plant named and each with its own job.

'ava'avaaitu, Macropiper puberulum, is used for “ghost sickness”, and an infusion of 'ava'avaaitu sosolo bark is used for mouth infections and coughs. Ginger is a medicine too. The most-used medicinal plant of all is Matalafi, Psychotria insularum. And Moso'i, Cananga odorata, is used for constipation, stomachache and sickness after childbirth.

Samoa's Year 12 biology teaches all this inside the same unit as antiseptics, antibiotics and vaccination — the people's own plant medicine and the clinic's medicine, side by side.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
