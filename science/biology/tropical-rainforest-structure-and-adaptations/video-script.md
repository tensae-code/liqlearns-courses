Today: Tropical rainforest — structure and adaptations.

Remember this one thing: the rain forest is built in storeys, and its plants carry the marks of the wet, shaded world: drip tips to shed water, buttress roots to hold tall trunks, cauliflory, climbers reaching for light, and shade plants below — studied practically, from the species actually found, up to the forest's food chains.

The rain forest grows in layers — storeys of vegetation from the shaded floor to the high canopy, each storey its own light and living conditions.

Rain-forest leaves carry drip tips that shed water quickly; tall trunks stand on buttress roots that flare out for support in the forest's soil.

Cauliflory puts flowers and fruit directly on the trunk; climbers use other plants as ladders to the light; shade plants are fitted to live in the dimness below.

Arboreal animals — living in the trees — are adapted to the storeys: moving, feeding and sheltering above the ground.

Nigeria's way is field study: the forest is examined as found — structure, adaptations, and forest food chains built from the species actually present (Chapter 14, printed p. 206).
