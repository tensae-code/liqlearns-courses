# Tropical rainforest — structure and adaptations

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Tropical rainforest — structure and adaptations. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Chapter 14 of Nigeria's Senior Secondary Biology 1 studies the forest practically: you go in and look. The rain forest stands in storeys — layers of vegetation from forest floor to canopy. Its plants show their adaptations plainly: drip tips that shed the constant water, buttress roots flaring to hold tall trunks in thin soil, cauliflory (flowers and fruit on the trunk), climbers that climb other plants to reach the light, and shade plants fitted to the dim floor. Arboreal animals are adapted to life in the trees, and the study closes by building forest food chains from the species actually found.

## The key facts
**Built in storeys.** The rain forest grows in layers — storeys of vegetation from the shaded floor to the high canopy, each storey its own light and living conditions.

**Fitted to the wet.** Rain-forest leaves carry drip tips that shed water quickly; tall trunks stand on buttress roots that flare out for support in the forest's soil.

**Reaching and waiting for light.** Cauliflory puts flowers and fruit directly on the trunk; climbers use other plants as ladders to the light; shade plants are fitted to live in the dimness below.

**Animals in the trees.** Arboreal animals — living in the trees — are adapted to the storeys: moving, feeding and sheltering above the ground.

**Studied practically.** Nigeria's way is field study: the forest is examined as found — structure, adaptations, and forest food chains built from the species actually present (Chapter 14, printed p. 206).
