# Hands-on — Tropical rainforest — structure and adaptations

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Visit any tree or wooded patch — or study a large houseplant — and hunt the book's features: a leaf with a drip tip, a flared root base, a climber using another plant. Sketch the storeys of the tallest vegetation near you and say aloud which rain-forest adaptation each feature matches.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — a rain-forest cross-section in storeys: buttress-rooted emergents, canopy, climbers, and shade plants on the floor, one leaf enlarged to show its drip tip — and check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
