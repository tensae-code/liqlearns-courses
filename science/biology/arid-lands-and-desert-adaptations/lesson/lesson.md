# Arid lands and desert adaptations

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Arid lands and desert adaptations. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Chapter 14 of Nigeria's Senior Secondary Biology 1 closes its habitats with the hardest: the arid lands. Arid means dry — rainfall too little and too unreliable for ordinary life. The lesson is the fit between land and life: how arid-land plants are fitted to the dryness — reducing water loss, storing or reaching for water — and how arid-land animals endure: their water economy and their behaviour in heat and drought. In Nigeria the arid lands are not an abstraction; they are the country's dry north, continuous with the Sahel.

[Picture — an arid-land scene: a camel under a thorny acacia, a succulent plant with swollen water-storing stems, cracked dry ground and a distant dune]

## The key facts
**Lands of little water.** Arid lands are defined by scarcity: very low, unreliable rainfall — the condition every adaptation in this lesson answers.

**Plants keep water.** Arid-land plants are fitted to drought: they cut water loss, store water, or reach deep for it — survival written into leaf, stem and root.

**Animals economise.** Arid-land animals are fitted to the same scarcity — managing on little water and enduring heat and dry seasons.

**One problem, many fits.** Plant and animal adaptations differ in kind but answer one problem: water is scarce and the land is harsh.

**Nigeria's way.** Nigeria closes its terrestrial habitats (Chapter 14, printed p. 213) with the arid lands — the dry north's own habitat — and how plants and animals are fitted to them.
