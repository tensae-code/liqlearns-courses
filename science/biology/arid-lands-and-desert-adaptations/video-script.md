Today: Arid lands and desert adaptations.

Remember this one thing: arid lands are lands of very little water, and everything living in them is fitted to scarcity: plants and animals shaped to find water, keep water, and endure heat and drought.

Arid lands are defined by scarcity: very low, unreliable rainfall — the condition every adaptation in this lesson answers.

Arid-land plants are fitted to drought: they cut water loss, store water, or reach deep for it — survival written into leaf, stem and root.

Arid-land animals are fitted to the same scarcity — managing on little water and enduring heat and dry seasons.

Plant and animal adaptations differ in kind but answer one problem: water is scarce and the land is harsh.

Nigeria closes its terrestrial habitats (Chapter 14, printed p. 213) with the arid lands — the dry north's own habitat — and how plants and animals are fitted to them.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
