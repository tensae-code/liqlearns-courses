# Hands-on — Arid lands and desert adaptations

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Compare two plants: one from a dry, sunny place and one from a wet, shaded place. Compare leaf size, thickness and surface. Say aloud which one is fitted to arid land and which features give it away — water kept, water loss cut.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — an arid-land scene: a camel under a thorny acacia, a succulent plant with swollen water-storing stems, cracked dry ground and a distant dune — and check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
