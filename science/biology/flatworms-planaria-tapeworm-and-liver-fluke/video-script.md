Today: Flatworms — planaria, tapeworm and liver fluke.

Remember this one thing: flatworms are dorsoventrally flattened, bilateral, acoelomate worms with three germ layers, a single gut opening and both sexes in one body — a group that runs from the free-living planarian to two parasites with worked life cycles: the tapeworm (Taenia), ending in the human definitive host, and the liver fluke (Fasciola), passing through sheep and snail hosts.

Flatworms are pressed flat, back to belly, and they have only one opening to the gut. Most are both sexes in one body. One of them, the planarian, lives free. Two are parasites with famous journeys. The tapeworm ends its cycle in a human being. The liver fluke travels between sheep and a snail — and the book teaches how to stop it: wash water vegetables, fence the gardens, and keep cattle dung out of the water.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
