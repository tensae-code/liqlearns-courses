# Hands-on — Flatworms — planaria, tapeworm and liver fluke

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Press a ball of dough flat between your palms: that back-to-belly flattening is the flatworm shape. Then draw the fluke's circle from memory — sheep, water, snail, sheep — and say at which step each of the module's three prophylaxis rules breaks it.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory — a flat planarian drawn from above with its flat oval body, beside two life-cycle circles: the tapeworm cycle ending in a human, and the liver fluke cycle turning between a sheep, water and a snail — label its parts, and check against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
