# Flatworms — planaria, tapeworm and liver fluke — depth

This file is the lesson's depth record. This lesson has no sub lessons — it is one whole, single-skill lesson.

Whole-lesson pass mark: the learner can state the one thing this lesson teaches (Flatworms are dorsoventrally flattened, bilateral, acoelomate worms with three germ layers, a single gut opening and both sexes in one body — a group that runs from the free-living planarian to two parasites with worked life cycles: the tapeworm (Taenia), ending in the human definitive host, and the liver fluke (Fasciola), passing through sheep and snail hosts.) and explain each key fact in their own words. Practice deepens by retelling both cycles host by host without looking — Taenia to the human definitive host; Fasciola through sheep and snail — and naming the fluke's symptoms and the three prophylaxis rules.
