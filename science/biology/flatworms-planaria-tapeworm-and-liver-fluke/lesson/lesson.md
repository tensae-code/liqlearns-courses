# Flatworms — planaria, tapeworm and liver fluke

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Flatworms — planaria, tapeworm and liver fluke. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Mozambique's animal module gives the flatworms, phylum Platyhelminthes, two lessons: the phylum's plan first, then its famous members. The planarian shows the free-living anatomy. The tapeworm's cycle is worked through to the human definitive host. And the liver fluke Fasciola is followed through sheep and snail hosts, with its symptoms and the prophylaxis — the everyday hygiene that breaks the cycle. This lesson teaches that whole package, as the module teaches it.

## The key facts
**The flatworm plan.** Flatworms are dorsoventrally flattened — pressed flat from back to belly — bilateral and acoelomate (no body cavity), with three germ layers. The gut has a single opening, and the worms are hermaphrodites: both sexes in one body.

**The planarian — the free-living example.** The planarian shows the anatomy of the group in a free-living worm, and the module works its anatomy and copulation.

**The tapeworm — Taenia.** The tapeworm (Taenia) life cycle is worked through to its end in the human definitive host — the host in which the adult worm lives and reproduces.

**The liver fluke — Fasciola.** Fasciola passes through sheep and snail hosts. Its symptoms include abdominal pain, fever and weight loss. The module's prophylaxis is practical: washing aquatic vegetables, fencing gardens, and keeping cattle faeces out of water.
