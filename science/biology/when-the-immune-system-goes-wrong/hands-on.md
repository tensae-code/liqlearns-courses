# Hands-on — When the immune system goes wrong

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: the immune system itself can be the problem: allergies are its over-reaction to harmless things, autoimmune diseases its attack on the body's own tissues, and immunodeficiency its failure to defend at all — Greece's Grade 11 book gives the failures their own named section.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: three panels: a nose sneezing at pollen, a shield turning its sword toward the body's own joint, and a cracked shield lowered while germs walk past. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
