# Hands-on — Identifying plant families

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: plant families are recognised by the build of their flowers and inflorescences — the grass family, Gramineae, by its raceme, panicle and spike, and the Compositae by many small florets packed into one head — a working key taught on Kazakhstan's grassland plants.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: two plant heads side by side: a grass with three inflorescence kinds drawn — raceme, panicle, spike — and a composite flower head opened to show its many small florets. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
