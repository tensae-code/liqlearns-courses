# Chromosome structure and karyotype

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Chromosome structure and karyotype. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
A cell's long DNA is packaged with protein into chromosomes — DNA wound around histone proteins into nucleosomes, the string folded into chromatin that is loose (euchromatin) or dense (heterochromatin) — each chromosome's shape is named by where its centromere sits, and a karyotype is the individual's whole set of chromosomes photographed, cut out and arranged in order. No lesson in this course taught this on its own before, so this lesson is built for it — from Indonesia's Kurikulum Merdeka books, which teach it as a named topic.

[Picture — a length of DNA winding twice around a cluster of histone beads to make a nucleosome, the beaded string coiling into a fibre and then into one X-shaped chromosome with its centromere pinched near the middle, beside a karyotype chart of chromosome pairs laid out in size order]

## The key facts
**Packaging — nucleosomes.** DNA does not lie loose in the nucleus. It winds around histone proteins, forming bead-like units called nucleosomes, and the beaded string folds further until, at division, it is packed into the dense rod we call a chromosome. A chromosome is therefore a macromolecule of DNA plus protein.

**Two kinds of chromatin.** Between divisions the packaging is uneven: euchromatin is the looser, lighter-staining chromatin whose genes are available to be read, while heterochromatin stays dense and tightly packed.

**Shape from the centromere.** Each chromosome has a centromere — the pinched point where the two chromatids are joined and where spindle fibres attach. Chromosome types are named by the centromere's position: near the middle, off-centre, near the end, or right at the end, giving arms of equal or unequal length.

**The karyotype.** A karyotype is the whole chromosome set of an individual, photographed during division and arranged in homologous pairs in size order. Reading a karyotype shows the number and shape of the chromosomes — and where a set is abnormal.

**Indonesia's way.** Indonesia's Grade 12 student book teaches chromosome anatomy inside its genetics chapter (Ch. 2A, printed p. 70): DNA packaging, nucleosomes, centromere-position types, euchromatin and heterochromatin, and the karyotype. Its teacher guide adds the giant polytene chromosomes of the fruit fly Drosophila melanogaster, large enough to be observed by light microscope (Gambar 2.1).
