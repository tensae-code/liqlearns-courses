# Hands-on — Chromosome structure and karyotype

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Roll a long thread twice around each of a row of cotton reels — DNA around histones — and lay the reels in a line: your beaded string is a line of nucleosomes. Coil the string loosely for euchromatin, then wind it tight into a short thick bundle for a chromosome, and pinch it at different points to show how the centromere's position changes the chromosome's shape.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — a length of DNA winding twice around a cluster of histone beads to make a nucleosome, the beaded string coiling into a fibre and then into one X-shaped chromosome with its centromere pinched near the middle, beside a karyotype chart of chromosome pairs laid out in size order — and check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
