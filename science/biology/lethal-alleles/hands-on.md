# Hands-on — Lethal alleles

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: some alleles are lethal: in two copies — or one, for a dominant lethal — they kill the organism, often before birth, which is why certain crosses give surviving offspring in a 2:1 ratio instead of Mendel's 3:1.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a Punnett square of a mouse coat cross with one of its four boxes crossed out and the surviving mice drawn beneath, counted 2:1. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
