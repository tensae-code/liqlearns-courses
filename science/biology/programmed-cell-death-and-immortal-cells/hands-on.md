# Hands-on — Programmed cell death and immortal cells

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Think of a garden being pruned — branches cut on purpose so the plant keeps its shape. Say aloud how pruning is like apoptosis (removal that is planned and useful) and how a weed that can never be cut back is like an immortal cell line. Then say the difference the picture cannot show: apoptosis is the cell dismantling itself, not being cut from outside.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — the two cell lines, one ending in programmed cell death, one dividing on without end — and label them “apoptosis — programmed cell death” and “immortal cells”. Check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
