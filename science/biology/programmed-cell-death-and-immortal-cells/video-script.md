Today: Programmed cell death and immortal cells.

Remember this one thing: death is part of a cell's programme — and some cells escape it.

You have learned how cells divide and make more cells. But cells also die. Most body cells are mortal: they live their span, and then they die. Italy's textbook asks you to look at that death closely, because much of it is not an accident.

Programmed cell death — apoptosis — is written into the cell itself. When the time comes, the cell takes itself apart, piece by piece, in order. No mess, no burst — a dismantling on schedule. That orderly death is useful: it removes cells the body no longer needs and keeps every tissue the right size and shape.

And the opposite exists: immortal cells — cells that refuse the programme and divide on and on. Mortal and immortal: the book teaches them together, as its training for the future.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
