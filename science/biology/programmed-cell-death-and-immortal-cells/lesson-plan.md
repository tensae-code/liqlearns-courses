# Lesson plan — Programmed cell death and immortal cells

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Biology · **Lesson:** Programmed cell death and immortal cells
**Place in the curriculum:** Biology (CURRICULUM.md Unit 16).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** Cells are mortal, and much of their dying is programmed: programmed cell death (apoptosis) is the cell dismantling itself on schedule, and cells that escape that programme are immortal cells — Italy's Grade 9 Campbell closes its cell-division unit with them, in “Training for the future” (printed p. 120).
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on it stands on the earlier cell-division lessons — mitosis and meiosis — and adds the other half of a cell's life story: its death, and the cells that refuse it.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier biology lessons on cell division — mitosis, and mitosis vs meiosis. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what programmed cell death (apoptosis) is: the cell dismantling itself on schedule, as part of its programme.
2. Tell mortal cells from immortal cells: mortal cells die; immortal cells escape the normal death programme and keep dividing.
3. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson itself.

## What you need
