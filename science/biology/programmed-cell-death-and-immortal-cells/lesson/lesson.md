# Programmed cell death and immortal cells

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Programmed cell death and immortal cells. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Cell division gets the chapters; cell death usually gets nothing. Italy's Grade 9 Campbell, *Biologia: concetti e collegamenti PLUS, Primo biennio*, closes its mitosis-and-meiosis unit (Unità 4) with a “Training for the future” section (printed p. 120; PDF p. 128) on exactly that: mortal and immortal cells, and programmed cell death — apoptosis. No lesson in this course taught apoptosis on its own before, so this lesson is built for it.

[Picture — two cell lines side by side — one line ending as a cell takes itself apart piece by piece, labelled “programmed cell death (apoptosis)”; the other line dividing on and on without end, labelled “immortal cells”]

## The key facts
**Cells are mortal.** The cells of a body do not live for ever: they have a lifespan, they age, and they die. Mortality is the normal condition of a cell, part of the same life story as division.

**Much cell death is programmed.** Programmed cell death — apoptosis — is death written into the cell's own programme: at the appointed point the cell dismantles itself in an orderly way, breaking itself down instead of bursting or rotting in place.

**Programmed death is useful death.** Because it is orderly and on schedule, apoptosis serves the body: cells that are worn out, surplus or no longer needed are removed by their own programme, keeping tissues in their proper shape and number.

**Immortal cells escape the programme.** The section pairs mortal cells with immortal ones: cells that no longer obey the death programme and keep dividing without the normal end — immortality in a cell is the programme refused.

**Italy's way.** In Italy's Grade 9 Campbell, the cell-division unit closes by looking forward — “Training per il futuro” (Training for the future): mortal and immortal cells and programmed cell death, taught as the frontier a school learner should know is there.
