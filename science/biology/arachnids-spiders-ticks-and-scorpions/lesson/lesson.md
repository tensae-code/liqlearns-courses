# Arachnids — spiders, ticks and scorpions

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Arachnids — spiders, ticks and scorpions. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Arachnids — spiders, ticks and scorpions — are arthropods whose body is built in two parts, cephalothorax and abdomen, living on land, in soil and in burrows, some of them parasitising plants and animals. No lesson in this course taught this on its own before, so this lesson is built for it — from Moldova's Ministry of Education biology textbooks, which teach it as a named topic.

[Picture — a spider, a tick and a scorpion side by side, each labelled with its cephalothorax and abdomen]

## The key facts
**2.9 Class Arachnids.** Moldova's Grade 7 book (2.9 Class Arachnids) teaches: spiders, ticks and scorpions; cephalothorax and abdomen; tick-borne encephalitis and mowing grass as prevention debated. (printed p. 44).

**Tema 4.18 Class Arachnids (Arachnida).** Moldova's Grade 10 book (Tema 4.18 Class Arachnids (Arachnida)) teaches: arachnids on land, in soil and burrows, parasitising plants and animals; diversity survey. (printed p. 138).

**Moldova's way.** Moldova teaches arachnids as their own class inside the arthropod march (Grade 7), returning at Grade 10 for their diversity and habitats; tick-borne encephalitis and mowing grass as prevention are debated in class.
