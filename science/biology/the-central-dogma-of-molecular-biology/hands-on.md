# Hands-on — The central dogma of molecular biology

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: the central dogma states the flow of genetic information: DNA is transcribed into RNA and RNA is translated into protein, while DNA copies itself — information flows from the archive to the working copy to the machine — and Greece's Grade 12 volume opens its molecular genetics with it.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: three boxes in a row — DNA, RNA, protein — joined by arrows labelled transcription and translation, with a return arrow over the DNA box labelled replication. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
