# Hands-on — Cloning and the human genome

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Take the lesson's key facts for a walk: find one example of this lesson's idea in your own surroundings — your home, street, market or school — and say which key fact it matches, and why.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory — two panels — on one side Dolly the sheep built step by step: a body cell's nucleus moved into an emptied egg, the egg dividing, the lamb born a copy of the nucleus donor — and on the other side rows of tissue-culture jars holding tiny banana and pyrethrum plantlets, beneath a long ribbon of sequenced DNA letters labelled as the human genome being read — label its parts, and check against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
