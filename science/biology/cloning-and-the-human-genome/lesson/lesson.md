# Cloning and the human genome

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Cloning and the human genome. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Genetics ends by copying life and reading it whole: Kenya's Form 4 book teaches cloning step by step through Dolly the sheep, plant tissue culture producing new pyrethrum and banana varieties — both Kenyan cash crops — and the human genome: gene mapping and sequencing, with eugenics and the ethical issues of genetic engineering.

[Picture — two panels — on one side Dolly the sheep built step by step: a body cell's nucleus moved into an emptied egg, the egg dividing, the lamb born a copy of the nucleus donor — and on the other side rows of tissue-culture jars holding tiny banana and pyrethrum plantlets, beneath a long ribbon of sequenced DNA letters labelled as the human genome being read]

## The key facts
**Cloning — Dolly step by step.** Dolly the sheep was made by cloning: the nucleus of a body cell from one sheep was placed into an emptied egg cell, and the lamb that developed was a genetic copy of the nucleus donor — taught step by step.

**Cloning in crops — tissue culture.** Plant tissue culture clones whole plants from small pieces of a chosen parent; Kenya's examples are new pyrethrum and banana varieties, both Kenyan cash crops.

**The human genome.** The human genome work is gene mapping — locating the genes — and sequencing — reading the genome's letters in order.

**Ethics on the table.** The book names eugenics and treats the ethical issues of genetic engineering as part of the topic, not an afterthought.
