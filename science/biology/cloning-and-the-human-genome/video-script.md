The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; the script is the slot's written form until then.

Today: Cloning and the human genome.

Remember this one thing: Genetics ends by copying life and reading it whole: Kenya's Form 4 book teaches cloning step by step through Dolly the sheep, plant tissue culture producing new pyrethrum and banana varieties — both Kenyan cash crops — and the human genome: gene mapping and sequencing, with eugenics and the ethical issues of genetic engineering.

Dolly was not born the usual way: Kenya walks through her making step by step — a nucleus taken from one sheep's body cell, placed in another's emptied egg, and the lamb that grew was a copy of the nucleus donor. The same copying, gentler, fills Kenya's tissue-culture jars: pyrethrum and banana, both cash crops, multiplied as exact copies of a chosen plant. Then the book turns from copying life to reading it: the human genome, its genes mapped and its sequence read — and it does not stop at the technique. Eugenics is named, and the ethical issues of genetic engineering are put on the table beside the science.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
