# Hands-on — Pollen and embryo sac development

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: before a flower can be pollinated, its gametes must be built: pollen grains develop in the anther, each carrying the male gametes, and the embryo sac develops inside the ovule, carrying the egg cell — the two developments that pollination and fertilisation join.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: an anther cut open showing pollen grains forming, beside an ovule cut open showing the embryo sac inside it with the egg cell marked. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
