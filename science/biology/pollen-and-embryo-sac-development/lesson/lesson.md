# Pollen and embryo sac development

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Pollen and embryo sac development. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Malaysia's Form 5 book teaches the development of pollen grains and the embryo sac as its own subtopic (printed p. 116), before pollination: the making of the two gamete-carrying structures, anther-side and ovule-side.

[Picture — an anther cut open showing pollen grains forming, beside an ovule cut open showing the embryo sac inside it with the egg cell marked]

## The key facts
**Pollen development.** In the anther, mother cells divide by meiosis to form pollen grains. Each pollen grain is the male gametophyte: it will produce the male gametes that fertilisation needs.

**Embryo sac development.** Inside the ovule, a mother cell divides by meiosis and one surviving cell builds the embryo sac — the female gametophyte — which holds the egg cell (the female gamete).

**Meiosis in both.** Both developments run through meiosis: the chromosome number is halved in pollen and in embryo sac alike, so that fertilisation restores the full number.

**Why it comes first.** Pollination only moves pollen; it is this earlier development that gives the pollen its gametes and the ovule its egg — the book builds both before it joins them.

**Malaysia's way.** Malaysia's KSSM Form 5 book orders its flower chapter this way (Ch.6): structure of a flower (p. 114), then development of pollen grains and the embryo sac (p. 116), then pollination and fertilisation (p. 120) — development before delivery.
