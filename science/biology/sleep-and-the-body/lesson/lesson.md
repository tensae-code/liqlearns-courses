# Sleep and the body

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Sleep and the body. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Sleep is something the body does in stages — and it can be measured. Finland's wellbeing work cards have pupils measure their own sleep with a wearable: total sleep, deep sleep, REM sleep and awakenings through the night — and then learn what the stages do, because sleep drives next-day coping and concentration.

[Picture — a pupil's sleep-tracker chart for one night, showing total sleep, deep sleep, REM sleep and the moments of waking, with the stages drawn as bands across the night]

## The key facts
**Sleep can be measured.** On Finland's sleep card (Card 4 of the wellbeing pack), pupils measure their own sleep with a wearable tracker. The numbers it gives are the lesson's facts: how long the sleep was in total, and how it was divided.

**The stages of a night.** A night's sleep is not one flat state. The tracker separates deep sleep and REM sleep, and counts the awakenings — the times the sleeper woke during the night.

**What sleep does for the next day.** The card's teaching is that sleep drives next-day coping and concentration: how the night went shows in how the following day goes — in managing the day and in being able to concentrate.

**Finland's way.** The wellbeing pack teaches the body by self-measurement, in a Measure → Reflect → Learn (theory) → Try cycle. Its rules are part of the teaching: measurements are voluntary, results are never compared competitively, and the data is not collected centrally.
