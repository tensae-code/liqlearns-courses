# Hands-on — Sleep and the body

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
If you have any way of noting your sleep — even paper and pencil by the bed — record when you fell asleep, when you woke in the night, and when you woke for the day, for three nights. Work out your total sleep for each night and compare it with how well you concentrated the next day.

## Teach it on paper
Draw one night's sleep from memory as a chart — total sleep with its deep sleep, REM sleep and awakenings marked — and check it against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
