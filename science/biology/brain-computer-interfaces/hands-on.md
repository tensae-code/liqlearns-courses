# Hands-on — Brain–computer interfaces

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: a brain–computer interface reads the brain's signals and lets them control a machine directly — implanted arrays give stronger signals at the cost of surgery, while scalp EEG needs no surgery but reads weaker signals.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a person wearing an EEG cap whose brain signals travel as waves to a robotic arm lifting a cup, beside a second panel showing an implanted electrode array. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
