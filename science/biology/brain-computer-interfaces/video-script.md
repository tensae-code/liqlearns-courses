Today: Brain–computer interfaces.

Remember this one thing: a brain–computer interface reads the brain's signals and lets them control a machine directly — implanted arrays give stronger signals at the cost of surgery, while scalp EEG needs no surgery but reads weaker signals.

implanted arrays (a rhesus monkey feeding itself with a robotic arm, 2017) against scalp EEG (weaker signals, no surgery), and China's entries: the 2014 "Shengong No. 1" brain-controlled rehabilitation robot, and its 2016 flight on Tiangong-2 for the first in-orbit brain–computer interaction experiment.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
