# Hands-on — Organ transplantation — and HLA matching

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: a transplanted organ carries its donor's HLA identity labels, so matching donor to recipient — over half the main HLA shared can suffice — and suppressing rejection decide whether the graft lives; scarce organs are allocated by urgency and match, in China by the computerised allocation system, not by influence.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: two identity cards, donor and recipient, with rows of HLA labels being compared and matched, most rows ticked the same. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
