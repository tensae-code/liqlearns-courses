# Biological waste and lab accident management

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Biological waste and lab accident management. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Malaysia's Form 4 biology book treats what leaves the laboratory as examinable biology. Chapter 1, beside the safety equipment, teaches biological waste management: what may and may not go down the sink, the methods for disposing of biological waste, and the steps for managing an accident when one happens in the lab.

[Picture — a biology laboratory bench with labelled biological-waste containers beside the sink, a crossed-out arrow showing cultures must not be poured away, and the eye wash and emergency shower marked on the wall]

## The key facts
**What may not go down the sink.** Not everything from an experiment may be poured away. The book names what may and may not go down the sink — living material, cultures and contaminated waste are not sink waste.

**Biological waste management.** Biological waste is managed by set methods: it is collected, treated so it can no longer infect or spread, and disposed of by the prescribed route — the method is part of the experiment, not an afterthought.

**Accident management.** When an accident happens there is a set order of steps: make the place safe, treat any injury (the eye wash and emergency shower of the safety lesson), report it, and clear up by the waste rules — not by improvisation.

**Malaysia's way.** In Malaysia's KSSM course this is named, examined content inside Chapter 1 of the Form 4 book (printed p. 6): lab safety equipment is taught as content, and waste disposal and accident procedure stand beside it as their own subtopic.
