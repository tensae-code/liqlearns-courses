# Estuaries, mangroves and the Lagos lagoon

## The idea
Chapter 13 of Nigeria's Senior Secondary Biology 1 studies the estuarine habitat at home: the open mouths of the Ogun, Osun and Osse rivers, the Niger delta, and the Lagos lagoon system. An estuary's defining hardship is change — salinity varies with tide and season. Life answers by zoning itself: on red-mangrove stilt roots in the Lagos lagoons, observers found Mercierella where salinity stays high, the oyster Gryphaea where it is lower, and the barnacle Balanus where it falls lowest. The red mangrove itself (Rhizophora racemosa) is adapted to stand in the shifting, salty water.

## The key facts
**Where river meets sea.** An estuary is the habitat where a river's fresh water meets and mixes with the sea — the Ogun, Osun and Osse mouths, the Niger delta and the Lagos lagoons are Nigeria's examples.

**Salinity that swings.** The estuary's ruling factor is salinity varying with tide and season — organisms must tolerate water that changes from hour to hour and month to month.

**Zonation on the roots.** On red-mangrove stilt roots in the Lagos lagoons, life is zoned by salinity: Mercierella where salinity stays high, the oyster Gryphaea where it is lower, the barnacle Balanus where it falls lowest.

**The red mangrove.** The red mangrove, Rhizophora racemosa, is the estuary's own tree, adapted to the shifting salty water and holding the shore on its stilt roots.

**Nigeria's way.** Nigeria teaches the estuarine habitat in Chapter 13 (printed p. 196) on its own coast — the river mouths, the Niger delta and a salinity zonation actually observed on mangrove roots in the Lagos lagoons.
