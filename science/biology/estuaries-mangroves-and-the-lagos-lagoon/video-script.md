Today: Estuaries, mangroves and the Lagos lagoon.

Remember this one thing: an estuary is where river and sea mix, and its salinity swings with tide and season sort its life into zones: on the red-mangrove stilt roots of the Lagos lagoons, Mercierella lives where salinity stays high, the oyster Gryphaea where it is lower, and the barnacle Balanus where it falls lowest.

An estuary is the habitat where a river's fresh water meets and mixes with the sea — the Ogun, Osun and Osse mouths, the Niger delta and the Lagos lagoons are Nigeria's examples.

The estuary's ruling factor is salinity varying with tide and season — organisms must tolerate water that changes from hour to hour and month to month.

On red-mangrove stilt roots in the Lagos lagoons, life is zoned by salinity: Mercierella where salinity stays high, the oyster Gryphaea where it is lower, the barnacle Balanus where it falls lowest.

The red mangrove, Rhizophora racemosa, is the estuary's own tree, adapted to the shifting salty water and holding the shore on its stilt roots.

Nigeria teaches the estuarine habitat in Chapter 13 (printed p. 196) on its own coast — the river mouths, the Niger delta and a salinity zonation actually observed on mangrove roots in the Lagos lagoons.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
