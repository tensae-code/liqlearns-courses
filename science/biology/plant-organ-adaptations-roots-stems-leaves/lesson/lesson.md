# Plant organ adaptations — roots, stems and leaves

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Plant organ adaptations — roots, stems and leaves. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Mozambique's first PESD I module (2023) teaches the adaptation of plant organs to the environment organ by organ — roots, stems and leaves each pulled out of their ordinary shape by the life the plant lives. Aquatic roots and aerial roots; the maize's prop roots dropping from stem nodes; fleshy storage roots. Tendrils for climbing; cladodes doing the leaf's work in plants that lost their leaves; spines. And a correction of everyday seeing: the banana's 'trunk' is no trunk — the true stem is the underground rhizome, and the apparent trunk is leaf sheaths. Plant care and the importance of plants close the lesson.

[Picture — a row of four drawings: maize with prop roots dropping from its stem nodes into the soil; a climbing plant's curling tendril gripping a stick; a banana plant with its sheath "trunk" cut to show the rhizome underground; and a dry-place plant with flattened green cladodes and spines where leaves would be]

## The key facts
**Roots reshaped.** Roots may be aquatic, aerial/adventitious — the maize's prop roots grow from stem nodes to brace the plant — or fleshy storage roots swollen with food reserves.

**Stems that climb and hide.** Climbing plants hold on with tendrils (gavinhas). The banana's true stem is a rhizome — an underground stem with buds; the apparent trunk above ground is only rolled leaf sheaths.

**Leaves given up to save water.** Plants of dry places may lose their leaves to save water: the stem flattens into cladodes that do the leaf's work, and leaves shrink to spines that lose almost nothing.

**Care and importance.** The lesson closes with plant care and the importance of plants — adaptation taught as the reason plants are fitted to their places, and worth keeping.
