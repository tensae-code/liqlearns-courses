# Hands-on — Plant organ adaptations — roots, stems and leaves

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Look at a maize plant or a picture of one and find the prop roots leaving the stem nodes like guy-ropes. Then look at a banana plant: what looks like a trunk is soft sheaths — the real stem is underground. Name one organ adaptation you can find within a short walk of home.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory — a row of four drawings: maize with prop roots dropping from its stem nodes into the soil; a climbing plant's curling tendril gripping a stick; a banana plant with its sheath "trunk" cut to show the rhizome underground; and a dry-place plant with flattened green cladodes and spines where leaves would be — label its parts, and check against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
