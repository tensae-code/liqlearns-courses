Today: Plant organ adaptations — roots, stems and leaves.

Remember this one thing: plant organs are reshaped by the environment: roots may live in water, climb into the air as maize prop roots, or swell with stored food; stems climb by tendrils or hide underground as the banana's rhizome (its apparent trunk being only leaf sheaths); and leaves shrink to spines or flatten into cladodes to save water.

A plant's organs change shape to fit its life. Maize sends prop roots down from its stem to brace itself. Climbing plants grip with tendrils. The banana hides its true stem underground as a rhizome — the trunk you see is only leaf sheaths. And plants of dry places give up their leaves: flat green cladodes do the work instead, and the leaves shrink to spines that lose no water.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
