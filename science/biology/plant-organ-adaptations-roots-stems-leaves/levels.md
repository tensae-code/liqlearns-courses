# Plant organ adaptations — roots, stems and leaves — depth

This file is the lesson's depth record. This lesson has no sub lessons — it is one whole, single-skill lesson.

Whole-lesson pass mark: the learner can state the one thing this lesson teaches (Plant organs are reshaped by the environment: roots may live in water, climb into the air as maize prop roots, or swell with stored food; stems climb by tendrils or hide underground as the banana's rhizome (its apparent trunk being only leaf sheaths); and leaves shrink to spines or flatten into cladodes to save water.) and explain each key fact in their own words. Practice deepens by giving one reshaped organ per group without looking — roots: maize prop roots, storage roots; stems: tendrils, the banana rhizome; leaves: spines and cladodes — and the water-saving reason behind the leaf changes.
