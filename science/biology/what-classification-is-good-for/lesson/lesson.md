# What classification is good for

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — What classification is good for. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
A good grouping is a tool: it tells you what a new organism is related to before you study it closely.

[Picture 21013 — a bird skeleton and a dinosaur skeleton with matching hollow bones]

## The key facts
**Identifying.** Classification identifies harmful and helpful organisms.

**Saving time.** Classification saves time and energy in study.

**Relationships.** Classification reveals relationships between organisms.

**Bird and dinosaur.** Birds and dinosaurs share a hollow-boned structure, a relationship a classification framework makes visible.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words: classification identifies helpful and harmful organisms, saves study time, and reveals relationships. Use each key fact once. Then let your companion explain it back to you.
