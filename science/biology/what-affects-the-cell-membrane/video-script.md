Today: What affects the cell membrane.

Remember this one thing: the cell membrane is a delicate, partly permeable skin of fat and protein — heat and chemicals such as acids and alkalis can damage it, and a damaged membrane leaks: the cell's contents escape and substances rush in uncontrolled.

The cell membrane holds the cell together and controls what crosses it: some substances pass, others are kept in or out. That choosing — partial permeability — is the membrane's job, and it works only while the membrane is whole.

Heating a cell harms its membrane: the fats in the membrane melt and shift and the proteins lose their shape, so the membrane's careful choosing breaks down. A pigment that was held inside a heated cell leaks out into the water around it — the leak is the visible sign that the membrane has failed.

Dilute acids and alkalis attack the membrane's fats and proteins in the same way, breaking its structure. Cells placed in them leak their contents just as heated cells do. This is one reason strong chemicals injure living tissue: they destroy the skin of every cell they touch.
