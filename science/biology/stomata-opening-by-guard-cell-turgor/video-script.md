Today: Stomata — opening by guard-cell turgor.

Remember this one thing: A stoma opens when its two guard cells take in water, become turgid and bulge outwards — and it closes when they lose water and go flaccid: osmosis at work in the leaf.

A stoma is a small pore in the leaf's surface. Two guard cells frame it, one on each side, and their shape decides whether the pore is open or shut.

When the guard cells gain water by osmosis, they become turgid — swollen and firm.

Turgid guard cells bulge outwards, and their bulging pulls the stoma open.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
