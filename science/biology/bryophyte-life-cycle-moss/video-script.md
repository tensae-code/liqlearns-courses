Today: The bryophyte life cycle — the moss.

Remember this one thing: In a moss, the green plant itself is the gametophyte, and the sporophyte grows out of it and lives dependent on it — and fertilisation can only happen in water.

Bryophytes are small, simple land plants without true roots, stems or leaves. The moss is the book's example.

The green moss plant is the gametophyte generation. It anchors itself with rhizoids — root-like threads that are not true roots — and its body carries the parts the book labels, including the perianth, thallus and rhizoids.

The gametophyte bears the sex organs: antheridia, the male organs, and archegonia, the female organs. A perianth — a ring of leaf-like parts — wraps and protects them.
