# The bryophyte life cycle — the moss

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — The bryophyte life cycle — the moss. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
A moss lives its life in two generations that grow out of one another. This lesson teaches that cycle whole — the gametophyte plant, its sex organs, fertilisation in water, and the sporophyte that rises from it and depends on it. No lesson in this course taught this on its own before, so this lesson is built for it — from South Sudan's Secondary 3 Biology book, which teaches the bryophyte life cycle inside its five-kingdoms survey of the plant kingdom.

## The key facts
**Bryophytes.** Bryophytes are small, simple land plants without true roots, stems or leaves. The moss is the book's example.

**The gametophyte is the plant.** The green moss plant is the gametophyte generation. It anchors itself with rhizoids — root-like threads that are not true roots — and its body carries the parts the book labels, including the perianth, thallus and rhizoids.

**Antheridia and archegonia.** The gametophyte bears the sex organs: antheridia, the male organs, and archegonia, the female organs. A perianth — a ring of leaf-like parts — wraps and protects them.

**Fertilisation needs water.** Fertilisation depends on water: the male gamete must swim through water to reach the egg in the archegonium, so without water there is no fertilisation.

**The sporophyte lives on the gametophyte.** After fertilisation, the sporophyte grows out of the gametophyte and stays borne on it and dependent on it: a slender stalk, the seta, lifting a capsule above the plant.

**The capsule makes spores.** The capsule produces spores. Released, a spore grows into a new gametophyte plant — and the cycle of the two generations begins again.

**South Sudan's way.** South Sudan's Secondary 3 book teaches the bryophyte life cycle inside its five-kingdoms survey of the plant kingdom, with the moss's parts labelled — capsule, seta, perianth, thallus, rhizoids — and the sporophyte shown borne on the gametophyte it depends on.
