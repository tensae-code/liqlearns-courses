# Hands-on — Specific defence and immunity

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: specific defence is immunity proper: the body recognises an antigen as foreign and answers with antibodies and dedicated cells — active immunity made by the body itself, lasting with memory, and passive immunity borrowed ready-made, as from mother to baby.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a Y-shaped antibody locking onto a spiky antigen like a key in a lock, beside two shields labelled 'made by your body — lasts' and 'borrowed — brief'. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
