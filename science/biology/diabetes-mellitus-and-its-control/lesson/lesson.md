# Diabetes mellitus and its control

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Diabetes mellitus and its control. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Diabetes mellitus is what happens when insulin fails: Kenya's Form 2 book teaches it inside homeostasis — insulin deficiency, its symptoms (glucose in the urine, thirst, weight loss), and its control by insulin injections, diet and exercise.

[Picture — a balance drawn out of true — a pancreas figure holding an almost empty insulin bottle, a blood vessel carrying too much glucose past a kidney that is spilling sugar into a urine jar — and on the other side the three controls restoring the balance: an insulin syringe, a measured plate of food, and a person exercising]

## The key facts
**The cause — insulin deficiency.** Diabetes mellitus is caused by insulin deficiency: without enough insulin the body's control of blood glucose fails.

**The symptoms.** Glucose appears in the urine, the person suffers thirst, and loses weight — sugar that should feed the body is being lost out of it.

**Control — replacing insulin.** The missing hormone is replaced by insulin injections.

**Control — diet and exercise.** Diet governs the glucose taken in and exercise governs the glucose used, so the two together help hold blood sugar steady.
