# Hands-on — Diabetes mellitus and its control

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Take the lesson's key facts for a walk: find one example of this lesson's idea in your own surroundings — your home, street, market or school — and say which key fact it matches, and why.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory — a balance drawn out of true — a pancreas figure holding an almost empty insulin bottle, a blood vessel carrying too much glucose past a kidney that is spilling sugar into a urine jar — and on the other side the three controls restoring the balance: an insulin syringe, a measured plate of food, and a person exercising — label its parts, and check against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
