# Animalia — animals without backbones

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Animalia — animals without backbones. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
From sponges to insects, the invertebrate phyla show many body plans. The teacher guide completes the roll: Porifera (sponges), Cnidaria, Platyhelminthes (flatworms), Nematoda (roundworms), Annelida (segmented worms), Mollusca, Echinodermata and Arthropoda (teacher guide — evidence).

[Picture 21022 — a worm, a snail and an insect showing three body plans]

## The key facts
**Invertebrates.** Invertebrates are animals without backbones and are the great majority of animal species.

**Animals eat.** Animals are multicellular eukaryotes that eat other organisms.

**Worms and molluscs.** Worms and molluscs (snails) are invertebrate body plans.

**Arthropods.** Arthropods are jointed-legged invertebrates such as insects.
