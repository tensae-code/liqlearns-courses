# Monoclonal antibodies — the hybridoma trick — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** State the one thing this lesson teaches and explain it.
**Answer: a monoclonal antibody is one antibody made in bulk by one clone: fuse the single B cell that makes it to an endlessly dividing myeloma cell, select and clone the hybridoma, and it makes that one antibody for as long as it lives — the trick behind pregnancy test kits and antibody medicines. fusion by PEG, electric pulse or inactivated virus; Milstein and Köhler's 1975 solution (fuse the one-antibody B cell to an endlessly dividing myeloma cell; Nobel Prize 1984); selection and cloning of hybridomas; and the products — pregnancy test kits reading at day 8, imaging diagnostics, five of the world's ten best-selling drugs in 2015, a ¥7.2 billion Chinese market that year, and antibody–drug conjugates that carry a toxin only to the tumour.**
