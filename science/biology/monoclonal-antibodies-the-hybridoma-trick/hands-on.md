# Hands-on — Monoclonal antibodies — the hybridoma trick

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: a monoclonal antibody is one antibody made in bulk by one clone: fuse the single B cell that makes it to an endlessly dividing myeloma cell, select and clone the hybridoma, and it makes that one antibody for as long as it lives — the trick behind pregnancy test kits and antibody medicines.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: a B cell and a tumour cell merging into one hybridoma cell, which then divides into a crowd of identical cells all pouring out the same Y-shaped antibody. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
