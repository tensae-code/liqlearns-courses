# Hands-on — Skeletons — hydrostatic, exoskeleton and endoskeleton

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Take the lesson's key facts for a walk: find one example of this lesson's idea in your own surroundings — your home, street, market or school — and say which key fact it matches, and why.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory — three animals in a row, each with its skeleton drawn showing through — a worm as a fluid-filled tube, its liquid drawn pressed tight in segments — a grasshopper in its chitin armour, the plates and joints picked out and a shed empty skin beside it — and a rabbit with its white bone skeleton drawn inside the body, the bones shaded as living tissue — label its parts, and check against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
