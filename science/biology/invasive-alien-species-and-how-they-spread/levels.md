# Invasive alien species and how they spread — depth

This file is the lesson's depth record. This lesson has no sub lessons — it is one whole, single-skill lesson.

Whole-lesson pass mark: the learner can state the one thing this lesson teaches (An invasive alien species is a species that has crossed a barrier it could never cross alone — with human help — and then spreads in its new home and harms the nature that was there. Finland's field pack names its cases: the mink, a fur-farm escapee devastating archipelago birds; the Spanish slug, arrived 1994, an omnivorous mass species; the garden lupin, outcompeting native plants; and the giant hogweeds, whose sap with sunlight causes burn-like skin damage. Control starts at the first sighting, with removal work parties.) and explain each key fact in their own words. Practice deepens by naming Finland's four cases — mink, Spanish slug, garden lupin, giant hogweed — and the harm each one does, without looking.
