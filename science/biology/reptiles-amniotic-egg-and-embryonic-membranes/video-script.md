Today: Reptiles — the amniotic egg and embryonic membranes.

Remember this one thing: reptiles are the first fully terrestrial vertebrates: a thick waterproof skin of keratin scales keeps water in, fertilisation is internal, development is direct, and the amniotic egg — with its four embryonic annexes, chorion, amnion, allantois and yolk sac — lets the embryo grow up inside its own private pond, away from water.

The reptiles solved the problem of dry land. Their skin is thick, scaly and waterproof, so the water stays in. And their egg is a private pond: the embryo floats in fluid inside a membrane called the amnion, with its food in the yolk sac and two more membranes — chorion and allantois — around it. Four membranes, and the young reptile can develop anywhere, far from any river.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
