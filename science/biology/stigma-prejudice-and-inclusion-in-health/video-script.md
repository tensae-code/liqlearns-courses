Today: Stigma, prejudice and inclusion in health.

Remember this one thing: stigma is a mark that pushes a person out — individual stigma, as around alcoholism, mental illness or homosexuality, and tribal stigma, by race or religion; prejudice is judgement made without critical analysis; and against intolerance and exclusion the lesson sets inclusion, taught as human-rights content inside Mozambique's biology course.

This lesson is about the marks people put on each other. Stigma can aim at one person — for alcoholism, for mental illness. Or at a whole group — by race, by religion. Prejudice is judging without critical analysis: the verdict before the evidence. And the book's answer is inclusion — every person keeps their standing. Mozambique teaches this inside biology, beside HIV and disease, where it is needed most.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
