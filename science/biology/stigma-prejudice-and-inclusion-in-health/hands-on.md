# Hands-on — Stigma, prejudice and inclusion in health

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Take the lesson's definitions for a walk: in one day, catch one judgement you make before examining the evidence — that is prejudice, by the book's definition — and say what critical analysis would have asked first.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory — two panels: in the first, a person stands outside a closed circle of neighbours with labels above the scene — alcoholism, mental illness, race, religion — and arrows of judgement pointing at them; in the second, the circle is open and the same person stands inside it, labelled inclusion — label its parts, and check against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
