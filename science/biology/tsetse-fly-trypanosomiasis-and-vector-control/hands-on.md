# Hands-on — Tsetse fly, trypanosomiasis and vector control

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find a shaded resting place near water or vegetation — the kind of shade a tsetse would use — and say aloud the control chain: shade cleared → fly has nowhere to rest → fewer flies → less trypanosomiasis. Then name the other three controls from the book and the part of the cycle each one attacks.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's main picture from memory — a tsetse fly with its life-cycle stages around it, beside a cleared riverbank strip labelled 'shade resting places cleared' and cattle marked 'nagana-resistant breed' — and check your drawing against the lesson. Fix anything you missed.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
