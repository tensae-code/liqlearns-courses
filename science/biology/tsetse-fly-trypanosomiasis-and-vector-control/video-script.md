Today: Tsetse fly, trypanosomiasis and vector control.

Remember this one thing: diseases carried by vectors are controlled by controlling the vector: Nigeria's book works the tsetse fly's full life history and builds the control on it — clearing its shade resting places, spraying, breeding nagana-resistant cattle, and treating sleeping sickness promptly — because antelopes carry the parasite harmlessly and cannot be removed.

Where a vector carries a disease, controlling the vector controls the disease — the chapter's principle, worked for the mosquito and then the tsetse fly.

The tsetse fly transmits trypanosomiasis: sleeping sickness in people and nagana in cattle — one fly, two diseases, of humans and of stock.

The book works the tsetse's full life history first, because control is built on how and where the fly lives and breeds.

Clear the shade resting places the fly needs; spray vehicles and resting sites; breed nagana-resistant cattle; and treat sleeping sickness promptly in people.

Antelopes carry the trypanosomes harmlessly — they are reservoirs of the parasite, so the disease cannot be ended by removing the wild hosts; the fly and the patient are where control acts.
