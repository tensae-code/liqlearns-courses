# Tsetse fly, trypanosomiasis and vector control

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Tsetse fly, trypanosomiasis and vector control. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Some diseases are best fought at their carrier. Chapter 12 of Nigeria's Senior Secondary Biology 1 — towards better health — teaches the control of disease-causing microorganisms through their vectors: the mosquito programme first, then the tsetse fly in full. The tsetse carries the trypanosomes of sleeping sickness (in people) and nagana (in cattle). The book works the fly's life history, then builds control on it: clear the shade where it rests, spray vehicles and resting sites, breed nagana-resistant cattle, and treat sleeping sickness promptly — antelopes, which carry the parasite harmlessly as reservoirs, cannot simply be removed.

## The key facts
**Control the vector.** Where a vector carries a disease, controlling the vector controls the disease — the chapter's principle, worked for the mosquito and then the tsetse fly.

**The tsetse's burden.** The tsetse fly transmits trypanosomiasis: sleeping sickness in people and nagana in cattle — one fly, two diseases, of humans and of stock.

**Know its life history.** The book works the tsetse's full life history first, because control is built on how and where the fly lives and breeds.

**Control built on the life history.** Clear the shade resting places the fly needs; spray vehicles and resting sites; breed nagana-resistant cattle; and treat sleeping sickness promptly in people.

**The reservoir stays.** Antelopes carry the trypanosomes harmlessly — they are reservoirs of the parasite, so the disease cannot be ended by removing the wild hosts; the fly and the patient are where control acts.
