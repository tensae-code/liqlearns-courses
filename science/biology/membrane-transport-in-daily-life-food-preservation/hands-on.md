# Hands-on — Membrane transport in daily life — food preservation

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: the movement of substances across a plasma membrane — osmosis above all — is put to work in daily life, and food preservation is its named application: salt and sugar draw water out of food and out of the microbes that would spoil it.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: salted fish and a jar of sugared fruit preserve on a kitchen table, with arrows showing water leaving the food and leaving a microbe cell, which shrivels. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
