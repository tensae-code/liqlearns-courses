# Membrane transport in daily life — food preservation

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Membrane transport in daily life — food preservation. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Malaysia's Form 4 book closes its membrane chapter with an application lesson: movement across a plasma membrane and its application in daily life (printed p. 65). The diffusion and osmosis the chapter has built are turned outward — to how food is preserved by controlling the water that crosses membranes.

[Picture — salted fish and a jar of sugared fruit preserve on a kitchen table, with arrows showing water leaving the food and leaving a microbe cell, which shrivels]

## The key facts
**The membrane idea applied.** The same crossing the chapter teaches in cells — water moving by osmosis towards the stronger solution — happens in food: salted or sugared food is a strong solution at its surface.

**How preservation works.** Salt and sugar draw water out of the food and out of any microbe cells on it. A microbe that loses its water cannot grow, so the food keeps — preservation by osmosis.

**A daily-life application, by name.** The book teaches this as its own subtopic — movement across a plasma membrane and its application in daily life — not as a passing example inside the osmosis section.

**Malaysia's way.** Malaysia's KSSM way is to end the membrane chapter (Ch.3) by applying it: after the plasma membrane, diffusion and osmosis in living organisms, the chapter's last step is the application in daily life, food preservation among it (printed p. 65).
