Today: Afghanistan's seed plants.

Remember this one thing: Afghanistan's own common seed plants — the conifers, field crops and fruit trees of the country — are seed plants: plants that reproduce by seeds, in cones or inside fruits.

Seed plants are plants that reproduce by seeds. A seed carries the young plant (the embryo) together with a store of food, protected by a seed coat — which is why a seed plant can spread and survive dry times better than a plant that must scatter bare spores.

The gymnosperms bear their seeds naked, most often in cones — the pines, firs, spruces, cedars and junipers. The angiosperms are the flowering plants: their seeds form inside a fruit that grows from the flower's ovary.

In the mountains of eastern Afghanistan the great forests are conifer forests. Their trees keep needle-like leaves, bear cones instead of flowers, and give timber, fuel and shelter on steep slopes — they are the country's clearest gymnosperms.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
