## Sub lesson 1 — Human evolution (§4.1.5):

who we are and where we came from; correcting the loose talk of having "evolved from monkeys/apes/chimpanzees" — one line of evolution gave old world monkeys, new world monkeys, great apes and several human species, of which Homo sapiens is the latest survivor; the two distinguishing human features — a very large brain and true bipedalism; Ethiopia's central role through Lucy (Australopithecus afarensis), Ardi and Selam (research activity); the section also discusses how humans adapt to their environments.

## Sub lesson 2 — Added human-evolution detail — timeline, brain growth, and the guide's dated 'race' passage

Humans emerge after the dinosaurs' disappearance about 70 million years ago, from monkey-like, lemur-like ancestors; brain size is staged (slow increase 6–2 million years ago as upright walking and simple tools began; brain and body increase 2 million–800,000 years ago; the modern brain the largest and most complex of any living primate, tripled over human evolution).
