# The internal environment — the fluid cells live in

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — The internal environment — the fluid cells live in. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
Every cell lives in fluid: body fluid divides into the intracellular two-thirds and the extracellular third — plasma, tissue fluid and lymph — whose composition and osmotic pressure (about 770 kPa at 37 °C, over 90% of it from Na⁺ and Cl⁻) the body holds steady, a salt solution much like the seawater life came from. No lesson in this course taught this on its own before, so this lesson is built for it — from China's People's Education Press biology textbooks, which teach it as a named topic.

## The key facts
**Ch. 1 §1 The environment cells live in.** body fluid divided into intracellular (2/3) and extracellular (1/3: plasma, tissue fluid, lymph), then plasma analysed from a real composition table (sodium 137–147 mmol/L, glucose 3.9–6.1, enzymes in U/L); osmotic pressure built to a number (about 770 kPa at 37 °C, over 90% of it from Na+ and Cl−); and the remark that extracellular fluid is a salt solution much like seawater — a fossil of life's ocean origin. No existing lesson is given to the internal environment itself.
