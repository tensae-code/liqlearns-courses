Today: The internal environment — the fluid cells live in.

Remember this one thing: every cell lives in fluid: body fluid divides into the intracellular two-thirds and the extracellular third — plasma, tissue fluid and lymph — whose composition and osmotic pressure (about 770 kPa at 37 °C, over 90% of it from Na⁺ and Cl⁻) the body holds steady, a salt solution much like the seawater life came from.

body fluid divided into intracellular (2/3) and extracellular (1/3: plasma, tissue fluid, lymph), then plasma analysed from a real composition table (sodium 137–147 mmol/L, glucose 3.9–6.1, enzymes in U/L); osmotic pressure built to a number (about 770 kPa at 37 °C, over 90% of it from Na+ and Cl−); and the remark that extracellular fluid is a salt solution much like seawater — a fossil of life's ocean origin. No existing lesson is given to the internal environment itself.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
