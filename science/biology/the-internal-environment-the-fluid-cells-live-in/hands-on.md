# Hands-on — The internal environment — the fluid cells live in

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Say it with real things
Find, point to or act out one real example of this lesson's topic in your own surroundings, and say aloud how it shows the lesson's one thing: every cell lives in fluid: body fluid divides into the intracellular two-thirds and the extracellular third — plasma, tissue fluid and lymph — whose composition and osmotic pressure (about 770 kPa at 37 °C, over 90% of it from Na⁺ and Cl⁻) the body holds steady, a salt solution much like the seawater life came from.

## Teach it on paper
Draw the lesson's picture from memory: one cell drawn large, two-thirds of the page inside it shaded as its own fluid, the third outside it labelled plasma, tissue fluid and lymph. Label each part and check your drawing against the lesson.

## Ask and answer
With a companion, ask each other the lesson's key-fact questions in turn — one asks, one answers from memory, then swap.
