# Science course — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

Science is one world course (his architecture ruling, 2026-11-11:
the thing itself is the same everywhere; only the way of teaching
differs, and the way shows inside as examples). The first stocked
part is the primary level, built from Ethiopia's Environmental
Science books — the way those books teach is the course's first
way in. The path climbs by the size of the thing a learner can
observe: from the place they stand, to their own body, to the
things around them, to the living world, to the whole environment.

**First Steps → Location and Maps → The Human Body → Materials →
Light → Weather and Seasons → Soil and Water → Plants → Animals →
Natural Resources → Our Environment → Family, School and Community
→ Work and Heritage → Keeping Healthy → Science Skills**

That ladder IS the path order (recommended, never forced — a
learner can jump in higher; the "start here instead" path points
down this ladder).

The titles, in ladder order:

1. **First Steps** — the course-opening lesson: caring for your
   book. The primary books open by teaching the child to look
   after the book itself, so the course opens the same way.
2. **Location and Maps** — where a thing is: relative position,
   the four directions from one's own body, kebele and woreda,
   reading and drawing sketch maps.
3. **The Human Body** — external parts and the sense organs first,
   then the inside systems (breathing, heart, digestion, brain,
   kidneys, liver) as the books climb.
4. **Materials** — telling things apart by how they look, feel,
   taste and smell; things that break, things that stretch.
5. **Light** — things light passes through, things that block it,
   shadows, and where light comes from.
6. **Weather and Seasons** — today's weather, the long pattern
   called climate, seasons and climate zones, measuring weather.
7. **Soil and Water** — what soil is and its kinds, what water is
   for, drought and holding water back.
8. **Plants** — kinds and parts, what a plant needs to grow, what
   plants give, plants that heal, plants and the soil.
9. **Animals** — tame and wild, grouping animals by young, food,
   movement, home and body; what animals need and give; care.
10. **Natural Resources** — water, soil, air and minerals around
    us, and what is made from them.
11. **Our Environment** — caring for soil, water and air; waste
    and what to do with it; the resources people make and share.
12. **Family, School and Community** — who is in a family, the
    people of a school, the people of a community and their work.
13. **Work and Heritage** — farming and making things, markets,
    the country's heritage and culture, the great river.
14. **Keeping Healthy** — cleanliness and the hygiene song,
    sickness from dirt, exercise and sleep, safety at home and
    on the road.
15. **Science Skills** — making instruments, running experiments,
    thinking and debating like a scientist.
16. **Biology** — the living world at secondary depth: what biology is and the scientific method, the microscope and laboratory tools, classification and the five kingdoms, cells and how materials cross the membrane, reproduction, nutrition, health and disease, ecology, and the Grade 10–12 climb through biochemistry, genetics, evolution and human systems — built from Ethiopia's Biology student textbooks, Grades 9–12 (Wave 4, 2026-11-12).
17. **Chemistry** — matter and its changes at secondary depth: the laboratory and measurement, atomic structure, bonding, the mole and equations, acids, bases and salts, rates and energy, organic chemistry and industrial processes — built from Ethiopia's Chemistry student textbooks, Grades 9–12 (Wave 4, 2026-11-12); the Chemistry expansion (2026-11-13) stocks other countries' books into the same lessons — 333 lessons in CURRICULUM.md Unit 17, including Finland's team escape game, where chemistry is learned inside a cooperative puzzle game, Djibouti's eight teacher-guide-only lessons on water, mixtures, fizzy drinks, formulas, ions and mass concentration (teacher-guide evidence only, acceptance 0), Sudan's alkyl-halides lesson opening its Grade 12 organic unit, and the United States' OpenStax *Chemistry 2e* lessons on thermodynamics — spontaneity, entropy and free energy — and transition metals and complex ions, and Nigeria's lesson on amines, amides and amino acids as one derivative family, and Sri Lanka's four lessons from its Tamil-medium books — models of the atom from Dalton to Bohr, the Earth's crust and common minerals, hydrogen halides and hydrohalic acids, and crystal structures and unit cells, and Nicaragua's lessons on the ozone layer inside atomic theory and on carbohydrates — sugars and starches, and Uganda's lessons on Raoult's law and ideal solutions and the partition law from its Senior Five home-study booklet, and on rocks and minerals closing its Senior One prototype, and Morocco's teacher-guide-only lesson on separating mixtures — filtration, evaporation and distillation — ending at the pure body, and Samoa's lesson on ocean and coral chemistry — the carbonate chemistry of coral and seawater, taught as a storyline in its Year 12 guide, and Mongolia's four lessons from its Grade 7 and Grade 9 books — phase changes taught as a named unit, diffusion with its rate set by molecular mass, soil composition, properties and pH, and water purification by filtering and chlorination, and Ecuador's lesson on amino acids, proteins and denaturation — proteins built from amino-acid chains on one plan, their folded working shapes, and denaturation as the shape undone, and Brazil's two lessons from its Paraná public textbook — nuclear accidents and radiation safety, taught through the 1987 Goiânia caesium-137 accident, and amines, amides and nitriles, the nitrogen functions met in the nerves and on the cosmetics label, and Iran's two lessons from its Grade 11 and Grade 12 student textbooks — Kevlar and high-performance polymers, the aramid fibre pictured with its applications inside the clothing chapter, and solar cells and photovoltaics, electricity from sunlight pictured as an advanced technology in the materials chapter.
18. **Physics** — motion, forces and energy at secondary depth: measurement and vectors, mechanics, heat, waves and optics, electricity and magnetism, electronics and modern physics — built from Ethiopia's Physics student textbooks, Grades 9–12 (Wave 4, 2026-11-12).
19. **Earth and Space** — the planet itself, whole: its shape, the evidence for it and its measured dimensions; its inside — crust, mantle and core; its two motions — spinning for day and night, travelling for the seasons; its four working spheres; the atmosphere and its five layers; and measuring what is too big or too small to reach directly — built from Ethiopia's Grade 7 General Science, Unit 6 (Wave 3, 2026-11-12).
20. **Motion, Force and Energy** — motion judged against a reference point and sorted by path; distance, displacement, speed, velocity and acceleration measured and graphed; force in its two families, weighed on the newton meter; Newton's three laws proved by experiment; and energy — kinetic and potential, its eight forms and conversions, its sources finite and endless, and how to use it wisely — built from Ethiopia's Grade 7 General Science, Unit 7 (Wave 3, 2026-11-12).
21. **Sound** — sound as vibration that sets a medium vibrating and spends itself as it travels; volume and pitch separated and measured; how fast sound moves and through what — never a vacuum, solids fastest; hearing as a relay from vibrating object to brain; echoes read as information by bats, dolphins, sonar and ultrasound; and noise pollution defined, its harm named, and its rights argued to a compromise — built from Ethiopia's Grade 8 General Science, Unit 7 (Wave 3, 2026-11-12).
22. **Heat** — heat as transfer driven by temperature difference, always hot to cold until temperatures equalise: conduction sorted into conductors and insulators; convection as the circulating current in fluids; radiation as waves needing no medium, black absorbing and white reflecting; and insulation slowing transfer in both directions — built from Ethiopia's Grade 8 General Science, Unit 7 (Wave 3, 2026-11-12).
23. **Electricity and Magnetism** — the circuit and its parts, open against closed; series against parallel, built and measured; voltage and resistance tied by Ohm's law V = IR; magnetism — poles that attract and repel and cannot be isolated, field lines made visible, Earth itself as a magnet and a compass made from a floated needle; the switchable electromagnet; and one machine run both ways, motor and generator, closing on AC against DC — built from Ethiopia's Grade 8 General Science, Unit 7 (Wave 3, 2026-11-12).
