# Science course — titles, FROM THE BOTTOM

Science is one world course (his architecture ruling, 2026-11-11:
the thing itself is the same everywhere; only the way of teaching
differs, and the way shows inside as examples). The first stocked
part is the primary level, built from Ethiopia's Environmental
Science books — the way those books teach is the course's first
way in. The path climbs by the size of the thing a learner can
observe: from the place they stand, to their own body, to the
things around them, to the living world, to the whole environment.

**First Steps → Location and Maps → The Human Body → Materials →
Light → Weather and Seasons → Soil and Water → Plants → Animals →
Natural Resources → Our Environment → Family, School and Community
→ Work and Heritage → Keeping Healthy → Science Skills**

That ladder IS the path order (recommended, never forced — a
learner can jump in higher; the "start here instead" path points
down this ladder).

The titles, in ladder order:

1. **First Steps** — the course-opening lesson: caring for your
   book. The primary books open by teaching the child to look
   after the book itself, so the course opens the same way.
2. **Location and Maps** — where a thing is: relative position,
   the four directions from one's own body, kebele and woreda,
   reading and drawing sketch maps.
3. **The Human Body** — external parts and the sense organs first,
   then the inside systems (breathing, heart, digestion, brain,
   kidneys, liver) as the books climb.
4. **Materials** — telling things apart by how they look, feel,
   taste and smell; things that break, things that stretch.
5. **Light** — things light passes through, things that block it,
   shadows, and where light comes from.
6. **Weather and Seasons** — today's weather, the long pattern
   called climate, seasons and climate zones, measuring weather.
7. **Soil and Water** — what soil is and its kinds, what water is
   for, drought and holding water back.
8. **Plants** — kinds and parts, what a plant needs to grow, what
   plants give, plants that heal, plants and the soil.
9. **Animals** — tame and wild, grouping animals by young, food,
   movement, home and body; what animals need and give; care.
10. **Natural Resources** — water, soil, air and minerals around
    us, and what is made from them.
11. **Our Environment** — caring for soil, water and air; waste
    and what to do with it; the resources people make and share.
12. **Family, School and Community** — who is in a family, the
    people of a school, the people of a community and their work.
13. **Work and Heritage** — farming and making things, markets,
    the country's heritage and culture, the great river.
14. **Keeping Healthy** — cleanliness and the hygiene song,
    sickness from dirt, exercise and sleep, safety at home and
    on the road.
15. **Science Skills** — making instruments, running experiments,
    thinking and debating like a scientist.
16. **Biology** — the living world at secondary depth: what biology is and the scientific method, the microscope and laboratory tools, classification and the five kingdoms, cells and how materials cross the membrane, reproduction, nutrition, health and disease, ecology, and the Grade 10–12 climb through biochemistry, genetics, evolution and human systems — built from Ethiopia's Biology student textbooks, Grades 9–12 (Wave 4, 2026-11-12).

19. **Earth and Space** — the planet itself, whole: its shape, the evidence for it and its measured dimensions; its inside — crust, mantle and core; its two motions — spinning for day and night, travelling for the seasons; its four working spheres; the atmosphere and its five layers; and measuring what is too big or too small to reach directly — built from Ethiopia's Grade 7 General Science, Unit 6 (Wave 3, 2026-11-12).
20. **Motion, Force and Energy** — motion judged against a reference point and sorted by path; distance, displacement, speed, velocity and acceleration measured and graphed; force in its two families, weighed on the newton meter; Newton's three laws proved by experiment; and energy — kinetic and potential, its eight forms and conversions, its sources finite and endless, and how to use it wisely — built from Ethiopia's Grade 7 General Science, Unit 7 (Wave 3, 2026-11-12).
