# Reading the electricity meter and the cost of units

## Do it first
Before you read: say out loud what you already think this lesson's one thing is — reading the electricity meter and the cost of units. Then read on: this is Seychelles' way in — the National Institute of Pedagogy's Integrated Science unit booklets (Unit 11).

## Sub lesson 1 — Reading the meter and counting units

Seychelles' Unit 11 Topic 5, "The cost of energy", teaches the electricity meter as a measuring instrument: read the meter, read it again a month later, and the difference is the units of electrical energy the house has used. Electricity is paid for by the unit, so the meter — not a guess — decides the bill.

## Sub lesson 2 — The cost of a unit — and an appliance's power in watts

With the cost of one unit known, the month's cost is units × cost per unit. The topic also measures an appliance's power in watts: a high-watt appliance uses units fast, a low-watt one slowly. Knowing the meter, the unit and the watt turns "the bill is big" into numbers a household can act on.
