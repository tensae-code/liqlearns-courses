# Finding circuit faults with a tester

## Do it first
Before you read: say out loud what you already think this lesson's one thing is — finding why a circuit will not work: guessing the fault, building a simple circuit tester, and using it to find the fault — China's Grade 3 torch investigation. Then read on: this is China's way in — its 科学 (Science) textbooks teach this thing at the places named in the sources, and this lesson follows their order.

## Sub lesson 1 — Guessing why the torch will not light

A torch that will not light has a **fault** — somewhere, its closed loop is broken. China's Grade 3 book starts the hunt the scientific way: before touching anything, **guess the faults** that could stop the light. The guesses name the loop's parts one by one: the battery may be flat, the bulb may be broken, a wire may be loose or snapped, or the switch may not be closing.

Each guess points at one part of the circuit. That is what makes the guessing useful — a fault is never "somewhere"; it is in one part, and the parts can be tested one by one.

## Sub lesson 2 — Building a circuit tester and finding the fault

To test the parts, the class builds a **circuit tester**: a simple working circuit — battery, bulb and wires — with two loose ends. The tester is touched across the part being tested: if the tester's own loop closes through that part and its bulb lights, the part conducts and is sound; if it stays dark, the break is in that part.

Part by part, the tester clears the sound parts and corners the fault — the flat battery, the broken bulb, the loose wire. Finding a fault is the closed-loop lesson used as a tool: a circuit works only when its loop is whole, so a dead circuit is a loop with a break, and the tester finds where.

## The key ideas

- A **fault** is a break somewhere in a circuit's closed loop — a flat battery, a broken bulb, a loose wire or an open switch.
- The hunt starts with **guesses** that name the loop's parts one by one, so each part can be tested.
- A **circuit tester** is a simple working circuit whose bulb lights when the part it is touched across is sound — part by part, it corners the fault.
