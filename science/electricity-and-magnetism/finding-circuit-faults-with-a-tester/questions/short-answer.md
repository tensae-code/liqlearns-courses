# Finding circuit faults with a tester — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** Name three faults that could stop a torch lighting, and say which part of the loop each one sits in.
**Answer:** A flat battery (the source), a broken bulb (the load), and a loose or snapped wire or an unclosed switch (the path) — each guess names one part of the closed loop.

**2.** Why does the hunt begin with guesses instead of random testing?
**Answer:** Because each guess names one part of the loop, the testing can go part by part in an order — the guesses turn a dead circuit into a short list of testable parts.

**3.** Describe a circuit tester and what it is made of.
**Answer:** A simple working circuit — battery, bulb and wires — with two loose ends left free, so it can be touched across the part being tested.

**4.** The tester lights across the battery and the wires but stays dark across the bulb. What has the hunt found?
**Answer:** The bulb is the fault: the sound parts closed the tester's loop and lit it, and the part where it stayed dark is the broken one.
