# Using electricity safely at home

## Do it first
Before you read: look around your home in your mind — the lamps, the wires, the sockets, the switches. Which of them could hurt you if you treated them carelessly? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Djibouti's way in — its Grade 4 guide teaches the dangers of electricity and how to protect yourself as one whole lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — The dangers of electricity

Electricity is a servant with a dangerous side. Djibouti's Grade 4 guide gives the danger its own lesson — **the dangers of electricity** — because a pupil who can build a circuit must also know what the power in the wires can do to a body it passes through the wrong way.

The danger is not a reason to fear electricity; it is a reason to respect it. The lesson names the danger first so that the protection that follows has something real to stand on.

## Sub lesson 2 — How to protect yourself

The guide's lesson joins danger to its answer: **how to protect yourself**. Protection is knowledge carried in the head and habits carried in the hands — treating wires, sockets and appliances with the care the danger demands, every time, not only when someone is watching.

That is where the lesson ends and where it is meant to live: not in the classroom, but at home, where the electricity is.

## The key ideas

- Electricity can do real harm as well as real work — its **dangers** are a lesson of their own.
- **Protection** is the danger's answer: careful knowledge and careful habits around wires, sockets and appliances.
- Safety is part of learning electricity itself — Djibouti's guide teaches it in the same module as circuits and conductors.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Electricity is useful, so why does it need a whole safety lesson?" Answer from the lesson: the same power can harm, and protection is the knowledge that keeps danger from becoming harm.

## Do It Yourself
1. Walk through your home (with an adult if you are young) and list every place electricity is at work. Beside each, write the care it needs.
2. Write the lesson as three rules you would teach a younger child about electricity at home — danger first, protection with each rule.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences — and check one socket or wire together as you finish.
