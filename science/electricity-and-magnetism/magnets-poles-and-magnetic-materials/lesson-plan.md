# Lesson plan — Magnets — poles attract and repel; what is magnetic

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Electricity and Magnetism · **Lesson:** Magnets — poles attract and repel; what is magnetic
**Place in the curriculum:** Electricity and Magnetism — Unit 7, Physical Phenomena, at General Science Grade 8 depth (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** prediction-first magnet play fixes the rules: a magnet acts without touching, its force strongest at its two poles, unlike poles attract and like poles repel, the force weakening with distance; cutting a bar magnet never isolates a pole — each piece is a whole magnet — only iron, nickel, cobalt and their alloys (steel) are magnetic, so ‘metal’ and ‘magnetic’ are not the same list, and the force acts through paper, wood and foil in its way
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Ohm's law and the circuit lessons: electricity's quantities fixed; now magnetism taken on its own terms before the two are joined in electromagnets.

## If the learner is stuck
- Expects a cut magnet to give a lone north piece and a lone south piece → every piece is a whole magnet; fresh poles form at the cut.
- Sorts by ‘is it a metal?’ → aluminium and copper are metals but NOT magnetic; the magnetic list is iron, nickel, cobalt and their alloys.
