# Magnets — poles attract and repel; what is magnetic

## Do it first

Two magnets, ends together. First pairing: they leap and lock. Second pairing: they push apart so stubbornly you cannot force them to touch. Predict before each pairing — north to north, south to south, north to south — and record what actually happens. Then hunt: where on a magnet is the pull strongest? Paper clips will tell you.

## Sub lesson 1 — Poles — the rules of attraction and repulsion

The play (Activity 7.32) is prediction-first: pair the poles every way, record, repeat; then find a magnet's strongest parts and test a horseshoe or disc magnet the same way. The rules the play earns:

- A **magnet** is any object with magnetic force, and it **acts without touching**.
- Its force is **strongest at its two poles**.
- **Unlike poles attract; like poles repel.**
- The force **weakens with distance**.

And the cut that proves the poles belong to the whole (Activity 7.33): cutting a bar magnet in two **never isolates a pole** — each piece is a whole magnet with **fresh poles at the cut**. Label them: the cut faces become opposite poles, because each piece must carry both.

## Sub lesson 2 — What is magnetic — and force through barriers

Sort the tray against the magnet (Activity 7.34): only **iron, nickel and cobalt** are magnetic, plus **alloys containing them (steel, through its iron)**. **Aluminium, copper, paper, plastic and wood are not** — so **‘metal’ and ‘magnetic’ are not the same list**, and aluminium is the trap that proves it. Then the barrier test (Activity 7.35): can the force reach paper clips **through paper, thin wood, thick wood and foil**? It **acts through substances in its way** — weakened, but through — which is the non-contact rule of Sub lesson 1 doing visible work.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the four magnet rules, what happens to the poles when a magnet is cut, the three magnetic elements (and steel's reason), and the list's trap (aluminium).

## Do It Yourself

1. Pole pairings (Activity 7.32): predict, test and record all three pairings for bar magnets, then the same hunt for strongest parts on a horseshoe or disc magnet.
2. Label the cut (Activity 7.33): draw a bar magnet, ‘cut’ it on paper, and label all four poles of the two pieces. Write the rule your labels obey.
3. Sort and pierce (Activities 7.34–7.35): sort the tray magnetic/not with the magnet as judge; then test the clip through paper, thin wood, thick wood and foil, recording reaches/does-not-reach and any weakening.
