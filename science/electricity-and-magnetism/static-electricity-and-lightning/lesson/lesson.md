# Static electricity and lightning

## Do it first
Before you read: think of the thing this lesson is about, from your own life. Say out loud what you already know about it. Then read on: this is Pakistan (Punjab)'s way in — its Punjab Single National Curriculum *General Science* books teach this as its own lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — Charges that build up by rubbing

Grade 5's §7.4 teaches static electricity: rubbing two things together builds up electric charges on them, and the charges stay where they are — static — instead of flowing as a current. The rubbed object can then attract light things and affect other charged objects without touching them.

## Sub lesson 2 — Like charges repel, unlike attract — and lightning

The section's rule: like charges repel each other and unlike charges attract. Scaled up to the sky, the same build-up ends in lightning — a giant discharge, the built-up charge released all at once. The spark from a rubbed object and the lightning flash are the same thing at two sizes.

## The key ideas

- **Static electricity** is charge built up by **rubbing**, staying put instead of flowing.
- **Like charges repel; unlike charges attract.**
- **Lightning** is a giant discharge — built-up static charge released all at once.
