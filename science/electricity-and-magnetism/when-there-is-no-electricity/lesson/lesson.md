# When there is no electricity

## Do it first
The power cuts out for a whole evening. The lights die, the screen goes black, the refrigerator stops humming. What do you do first? And what did people do every evening, before electricity existed? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Albania's way in — its Grade 2 guide asks the shocked question itself: "Çfarë? Nuk ka elektricitet?" — *What? There is no electricity?*

## Sub lesson 1 — What stops when the electricity goes

When there is **no electricity**, everything electricity was doing stops at once: the **lights** go out, screens and machines fall silent, food in the refrigerator begins to warm, and the phone cannot be charged.

The stop shows what electricity had been carrying. Naming what stops is naming, backwards, every job from the electricity-around-us lesson (`electricity-and-magnetism/electricity-around-us`).

## Sub lesson 2 — How life goes on without it

Life without electricity is not life stopped — it is life done the older way. **Candles and lamps** give light; **fire** cooks and warms; food is kept in cool places and used sooner; work follows the daylight, and evenings are dark, quiet and early.

That is how people lived for most of history, and how a home still manages through a long power cut. The guide's lesson makes pupils live that evening in imagination, job by job, and find the non-electric way for each one.

## The key ideas

- When the electricity goes, its jobs stop together: light, machines, cold food, charging.
- Life goes on the older way: **candles** for light, **fire** for cooking and warmth, cool places for food, work by daylight.
- Imagining a whole evening without electricity shows exactly what electricity does for us.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — what stops when the electricity goes first, then how life goes on without it. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "The refrigerator stops in a long cut. What is the older way to keep food?" Answer: Keep it in the coolest place and use it sooner — cold-keeping without a machine, as homes did before electricity.

## Do It Yourself
1. List everything in your home that stops in a power cut, then write the non-electric way for each job.
2. Draw an evening without electricity: how is the room lit, what is cooked, when does everyone sleep?
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences — how life goes on without electricity.
