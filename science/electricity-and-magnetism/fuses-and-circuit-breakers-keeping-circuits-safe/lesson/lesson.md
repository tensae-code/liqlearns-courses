# Fuses and circuit breakers — keeping circuits safe

## Do it first
Before you read: think of the thing this lesson is about, from your own life. Say out loud what you already know about it. Then read on: this is Pakistan (Punjab)'s way in — its Punjab Single National Curriculum *General Science* books teach this as its own lesson.

## Sub lesson 1 — The fuse — a guard that melts to protect

Grade 5's §7.3 teaches the fuse and its uses: a fuse is placed in the circuit so that when the current grows too large, the fuse wire melts and breaks the circuit before the wires and appliances are harmed. Fuses carry ratings — the current they allow before they melt — so the guard must be chosen to fit the circuit it protects.

## Sub lesson 2 — The circuit breaker — the same job, switchable

The same section names the circuit breaker doing the same job: it breaks the circuit when the current is too large, but as a switch it can be reset and used again, where a melted fuse must be replaced. Two guards, one job: stop the dangerous current by opening the circuit.

## The key ideas

- A **fuse** protects a circuit: too much current melts its wire, the circuit breaks, and the harm stops.
- Fuses have **ratings** — the guard must fit the circuit it protects.
- A **circuit breaker** does the same job as a switch: it opens the circuit on too much current and can be reset.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "If a fuse keeps melting, is the answer a bigger fuse?" Answer from the lesson: No — from the lesson: the fuse's rating is the guard's setting. A melting fuse is the guard doing its job and naming a fault; the rating must fit the circuit, not be raised to silence the guard.

## Do It Yourself
1. Find (with an adult) where the fuses or circuit breakers are in your home. Write which circuits they guard — do not touch or open anything.
2. Explain the difference between a fuse and a circuit breaker in three sentences, using the words melt, rating and reset.
3. Draw a simple circuit — battery, wires, bulb, switch — and add a fuse in the line. Label what happens when too much current flows.
