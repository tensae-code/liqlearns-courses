## Sub lesson 1 — Conductors let the current pass

A material that lets electricity flow through it is a **conductor**. The metals are conductors — copper, iron, aluminium, steel — which is why wires are made of metal, usually copper.

Kuwait's Grade 3 book teaches conduction by testing: the child builds a simple circuit with a gap, puts different materials across the gap, and watches whether the lamp lights. The lamp is the judge: light means the material conducts.

## Sub lesson 2 — Insulators stop the current

Materials that stop electricity are **insulators**: rubber, plastic, wood, glass and dry cloth. Insulators are not failures — they are the safety half of every circuit. The plastic coat on a wire is an insulator keeping the current in the metal and out of our hands.

Water is the careful case the book warns about: water with salts in it conducts, which is why electricity and wet hands are a danger pair. The lesson's rule: test with the circuit, trust the lamp, and keep water away from electricity.

## The key ideas

- Conductors (metals like copper and iron) let electricity pass.
- Insulators (rubber, plastic, wood, glass) stop electricity.
- A simple circuit with a gap and a lamp tests any material.
- Insulators keep us safe; wet conditions make electricity dangerous.
