Today: **Materials that conduct electricity** — Kuwait's way: what materials conduct electricity — metals let the current pass, while rubber, plastic and wood stop it — and why both kinds are needed in every circuit.

A material that lets electricity flow through it is a conductor. The metals are conductors — copper, iron, aluminium, steel — which is why wires are made of metal, usually copper. Kuwait's Grade 3 book teaches conduction by testing: the child builds a simple circuit with a gap, puts different materials across the gap, and watches whether the lamp lights. The lamp is the judge: light means the material conducts. Materials that stop electricity are insulators: rubber, plastic, wood, glass and dry cloth. Insulators are not failures — they are the safety half of every circuit. The plastic coat on a wire is an insulator keeping the current in the metal and out of our hands. Water is the careful case the book warns about: water with salts in it conducts, which is why electricity and wet hands are a danger pair. The lesson's rule: test with the circuit, trust the lamp, and keep water away from electricity.
