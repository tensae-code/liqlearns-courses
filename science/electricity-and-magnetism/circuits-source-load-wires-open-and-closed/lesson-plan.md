# Lesson plan — Circuits — source, load, wires, and open vs closed

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Electricity and Magnetism · **Lesson:** Circuits — source, load, wires, and open vs closed
**Place in the curriculum:** Electricity and Magnetism — a NEW title on the Science ladder (CURRICULUM.md). Unit 7, Physical Phenomena, at General Science Grade 8 depth.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** open tinkering with battery, wire and bulb earns the concepts: current electricity is charge flowing continuously (unlike a static build-up and discharge), a circuit is its path with three basic parts — power source, load, connectors — often plus a switch; an unbroken circuit is closed and carries current, any break makes it open and stops the flow, and current's rate is measured in amperes (one coulomb per second)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on the primary Materials lesson Electricity and magnets: a torch snapped on and off there; now the bulb is lit by the learner's own trials and the circuit is named from them. It opens the Electricity and Magnetism title.

## If the learner is stuck
- Draws a single wire from battery to bulb and waits → current needs a complete loop back to the source; one wire is an open path, not a circuit.
- Thinks a burnt-out bulb ‘uses up’ the current → its snapped filament is a BREAK: the circuit is open, so flow stops everywhere in the loop.
