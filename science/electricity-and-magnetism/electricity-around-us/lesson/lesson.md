# Electricity around us

## Do it first
Look around the room you are in, in your mind. How many things in it would stop working if the electricity vanished? Count them. Say your number out loud. Then read on: this is Albania's way in — its Grade 2 guide opens electricity not with a wire, but with the world around the pupil: "Elektriciteti rreth nesh" — *Electricity around us*.

## Sub lesson 1 — Electricity is all around us

**Electricity** is at work all around us, most of the day, in most of the places we live.

It lights the lamps, runs the television and the refrigerator, charges the phone and powers the machines that wash, cook and carry. Much of modern life is electricity doing jobs quietly, one device at a time.

That surrounding presence is this lesson's one thing: electricity is met first as something already here, everywhere, before it is taken apart into circuits.

## Sub lesson 2 — Meeting it before building with it

Albania's order matters: the guide's pupils meet electricity around them first — naming where it works and what it does — and only then ask what a circuit is (stocked in `electricity-and-magnetism/circuits-source-load-wires-open-and-closed`).

The naming is the science start. Every device named is a use of electricity observed in the pupil's own surroundings: light, movement, heat, sound and messages, each traced back to the same power.

## The key ideas

- **Electricity around us** — lamps, screens, refrigerators, phones and machines all run on it, all day.
- Electricity does many jobs: **light, movement, heat, sound and messages**.
- Albania's order: meet electricity in the surroundings first; circuits come after.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — electricity is all around us first, then meeting it before building with it. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Name three jobs electricity is doing in your home right now." Answer: Any three real devices at work — for example lighting a room, keeping food cold, and charging a phone — each one electricity at work around us.

## Do It Yourself
1. Walk through your home and list ten things that use electricity. Write what job each one does.
2. Draw your room and mark every place electricity is at work in it.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences — where electricity is around us.
