# Lesson plan — Voltage, resistance and Ohm's law — V = IR

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Electricity and Magnetism · **Lesson:** Voltage, resistance and Ohm's law — V = IR
**Place in the curriculum:** Electricity and Magnetism — Unit 7, Physical Phenomena, at General Science Grade 8 depth (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** what sets the current is two governors — voltage, the strength of the power source (dry cell 1.5 V; wall outlet 120 V as printed), pushing more current as it rises, and resistance, opposing the flow and turning electrical energy into light and heat (copper tiny, rubber huge) — tied together by Ohm's law, V = I × R, graphed from printed tables (the slope reads as resistance) and drilled in its three rearrangements
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Series and parallel circuits: currents measured in two wirings; now what sets a current's size, as a law.

## If the learner is stuck
- Treats resistance as ‘blocking’ that wastes energy into nothing → the opposed energy is CONVERTED — into light and heat; a bulb's glow is resistance at work.
- Memorises V = IR as a chant but cannot find I → practise the triangle: cover the wanted letter; what remains showing is its formula.
