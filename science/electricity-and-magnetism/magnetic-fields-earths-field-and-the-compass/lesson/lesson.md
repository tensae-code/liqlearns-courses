# Magnetic fields — field lines, Earth's field and the compass

## Do it first

Sprinkle iron filings on paper over a hidden bar magnet. Nothing to see — grey dust. Tap the paper gently. The dust organises itself into curved lines, crowding at the ends, as if an invisible pattern were ordering it. Something IS there, shaped, in the space around the magnet. This lesson maps it.

## Sub lesson 1 — Seeing the field — filings and field lines

The **field** is the space around a magnet where its force acts — a **non-contact field force**. Iron filings make it visible (Activity 7.36): one magnet, then **two with unlike poles facing**, then **two with like poles facing** — each pattern drawn. **Field lines** codify the pictures: they are **closed loops that never cross**, running **north to south outside the magnet**, **crowded where the field is strong** and **thinning with distance**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Earth the magnet — and the compass you make

**Earth itself is such a magnet** — pictured as a bar magnet through its core with its **south pole under geographic north**, probably driven by the **rotating liquid iron of the core** — ringed by **belts that trap charged particles from the Sun**, some of which escape down the field lines to make **light shows in the polar atmosphere**. The compass is the field's everyday instrument: a **thin magnet needle lining up with Earth's field**. Make one (Activity 7.37): a **magnetised needle floated on foam in water** — then test it against a brought-up bar magnet (it swings to the nearer, stronger field), and judge the book's closing question: would an astronaut need one in space?

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion what a field is, the four field-line rules, why filings crowd at the poles, and the steps of making the foam compass — plus your verdict on the astronaut.

## Do It Yourself

1. Three filings patterns (Activity 7.36): one magnet, two unlike-facing, two like-facing — draw all three, with field-line arrows (N to S outside) overlaid on the first.
2. Make the compass (Activity 7.37): magnetise a needle by stroking, float it on foam in water, mark where it settles. Repeat after turning the bowl: does the needle keep its direction?
3. Astronaut judgement: write four lines — would a compass work the same far from Earth? Use ‘field’ and ‘Earth's field weakens with distance’ in your answer.
