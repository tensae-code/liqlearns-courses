# Lesson plan — Motors and generators — electricity to motion, motion to electricity

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Electricity and Magnetism · **Lesson:** Motors and generators — electricity to motion, motion to electricity
**Place in the curriculum:** Electricity and Magnetism — Unit 7, Physical Phenomena, at General Science Grade 8 depth (CURRICULUM.md). It closes the title and the Grade 8 Unit 7 build.
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** one machine runs both directions: the motor (power source, permanent magnet, rotating loop, shaft) turns current into spin — the loop made a magnet by current, pushed and pulled by the permanent magnet, its shaft driving wheels and gears, real motors winding hundreds of turns on iron for the refrigerators, air conditioners and trains of everyday life — while the generator turns spin into current (turbine-driven loop between poles, more loops giving more energy), and the current it makes is mostly AC, against a battery's DC
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Electromagnets — a magnet you switch on: current making magnetism, switchably; now that made-magnet set spinning between real poles.

## If the learner is stuck
- Calls motor and generator ‘the same thing, no difference’ → same machine, OPPOSITE energy directions: motor electricity → motion; generator motion → electricity. The arrows reverse.
- Thinks wall outlets give DC like batteries → outlets give AC (back and forth); batteries give DC (one way); computers convert AC to DC.
