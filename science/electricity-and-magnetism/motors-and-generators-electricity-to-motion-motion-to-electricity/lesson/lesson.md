# Motors and generators — electricity to motion, motion to electricity

## Do it first

Balance a wire coil on two paper-clip supports over a magnet. Fit the battery. Nothing. Nudge the coil — and it keeps spinning, round and round, on its own. Where is the spin's energy coming from? And if spinning wire near a magnet can be DRIVEN by current — what happens if you spin it by hand instead? Two machines are hiding in this one build.

## Sub lesson 1 — The motor — electricity to motion

Activity 7.39's build comes first: coil, supports, magnet, battery, **a nudge — and it spins**; you must say what turns it, where the energy comes from, and how to speed it up. The anatomy (§7.5.4.1): **power source, permanent magnet, rotating wire loop, shaft** — **current makes the loop a magnet; the permanent magnet's push-pull spins it**; the **shaft** carries the spin to wheels and gears. Real motors wind the loop **hundreds of turns round an iron cylinder** for strength, and run **refrigerators, air conditioners and electric trains**.

## Sub lesson 2 — The generator — motion to electricity; AC against DC

The **generator** runs the same machine **backwards**: mechanical energy **turns a loop (or spins a magnet) between poles and a current is produced** — **more loops, more energy** — with a **turbine** (steam, water or air on fan-like blades) supplying the turning. Build one (Activity 7.40) from a cardboard tube, nail, clay and disc magnets, and read its output on a **compass needle** — the needle's swing is your current, detected. The unit's last distinction: most generated power is **alternating current (AC)**, flowing back and forth from wall outlets, while a battery gives **direct current (DC)**, always one way — and devices such as computers **convert AC to DC** to use it.

## Do It Yourself

1. Build the motor (Activity 7.39): coil on paper-clip supports over a magnet; nudge to start. Write: what turns it, where the energy comes from, two ways to speed it up.
2. Build the generator (Activity 7.40): tube, nail, clay, disc magnets; spin and watch the compass needle. Record what the needle does at slow and fast spin.
3. AC or DC? Battery torch, wall-outlet lamp, computer on its converter. File each under AC, DC, or AC-turned-to-DC, with the reason.
