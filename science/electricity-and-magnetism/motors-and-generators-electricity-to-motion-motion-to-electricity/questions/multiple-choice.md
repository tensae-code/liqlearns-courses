# Motors and generators — electricity to motion, motion to electricity — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1. (Sub lesson 1)** An electric motor's four parts in the book's anatomy are —
a) source, permanent magnet, rotating loop, shaft  b) battery, bulb, switch, wire  c) coil, core, needle, foam
**Answer: a) source, permanent magnet, rotating loop, shaft**

**2. (Sub lesson 1)** In a motor, the loop spins because —
a) the shaft pulls it  b) current makes the loop a magnet, and the permanent magnet's push-pull spins it  c) the battery vibrates
**Answer: b) current makes the loop a magnet, and the permanent magnet's push-pull spins it**

**3. (Sub lesson 1)** Real motors gain strength by —
a) one single turn of wire  b) winding the loop hundreds of turns round an iron cylinder  c) using no magnet
**Answer: b) winding the loop hundreds of turns round an iron cylinder** — they run refrigerators, air conditioners and electric trains.

**4. (Sub lesson 2)** A generator produces current by —
a) burning wire  b) turning a loop (or spinning a magnet) between poles — motion to electricity  c) connecting a bigger battery
**Answer: b) turning a loop (or spinning a magnet) between poles — motion to electricity** — a turbine supplies the turning.

**5. (Sub lesson 2)** In Activity 7.40, the home-made generator's output is read on —
a) a thermometer  b) a compass needle  c) a ruler
**Answer: b) a compass needle** — the needle's swing detects the current.

**6. (Sub lesson 2)** Wall outlets supply ______ and batteries supply ______.
a) DC; AC  b) AC, flowing back and forth; DC, always one way  c) both supply DC
**Answer: b) AC, flowing back and forth; DC, always one way** — computers convert AC to DC.
