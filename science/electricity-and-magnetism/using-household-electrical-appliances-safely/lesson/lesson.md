# Using household electrical appliances safely

## Do it first
An electric iron is hot, heavy, wired to the mains, and used on cloth an arm's length from your hand. What are its parts, and what care does each part demand? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Mauritania's way in — its Grade 4 book teaches household electrical appliances by using them, part by part.

## Sub lesson 1 — Using household electrical appliances

Mauritania's Grade 4 *Sciences Naturelles* book teaches **using household electrical appliances** (printed p. 59) inside its experimentation field: the machines of the home that run on electricity, met not as pictures but as things to be used — correctly, and with the care mains electricity demands.

Using is a skill with an order: know the appliance, connect it rightly, work it for its job, and finish — disconnected and put away — properly. The lesson teaches the using itself, in the home where the appliances live.

## Sub lesson 2 — The electric iron — its parts and safe handling

The book then takes one appliance apart in study (printed p. 61): **the electric iron — its parts and its safe handling**. The iron is named part by part, and each part carries its handling rule: the hot soleplate that presses the cloth, the body and handle that stay in the hand, the cord that joins it to the mains.

Safe handling is the parts, known: what gets hot is never touched or left standing on cloth; the cord is kept sound and dry; the iron is disconnected when its work is done. Mauritania's way joins the two lessons — use the appliances of the home, and know the iron part by part — into one household skill.

## The key ideas

- **Using household electrical appliances** is taught as a household skill with an order: know it, connect it rightly, work it, disconnect it.
- The **electric iron** is studied **part by part**, and each part carries its safe-handling rule.
- Mauritania's Grade 4 book teaches both lessons in its experimentation field (printed pp. 59, 61).

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Why does the book study the iron's parts in a lesson about using appliances safely?" Answer from the lesson: Because safe handling is the parts, known: the hot soleplate, the handle, the cord each demand their own care, and a user who can name the parts knows which care belongs where.
