# Electromagnets — a magnet you switch on

## Do it first

Wrap a few loops of wire around a compass and connect a battery. The needle — which a moment ago pointed calmly north — swings hard. Disconnect: it settles back. Reverse the battery: it swings the other way. No magnet came near it. The CURRENT did this. Electricity just made magnetism, under your switch.

## Sub lesson 1 — Current makes a field

Activity 7.38's three observations carry the discovery: the needle **swings while current flows**, **settles when disconnected**, and **reverses with the battery** — so **a current creates a magnetic field around a wire** (the 1820s–30s discoveries of the generation of Faraday and Henry: currents make fields, and magnets can make currents). Two strengtheners follow: **more current strengthens the field**, and **winding the wire into a coil strengthens it further** — each loop adds its pull in the same direction, so the coil's field is the loops' fields added together.
[Picture 34720 — a coil wound on an iron nail, battery connected, paper clips clustered at the nail's tip, the switch marked]
## Sub lesson 2 — The electromagnet — coil plus core

An **electromagnet** is that **coil wrapped on a metal core such as an iron bar**: the coil's field **lines up the core's particles** and the **core itself becomes a magnet** — **only while the current flows**. Stop the current and the magnetism stops with it. That switchability is the invention: a magnet you can turn on to lift and hold iron, and turn off to let it fall — no permanent magnet can do that. Test it on paper clips: count how many the coil alone holds against how many the coil-on-nail holds, current on, then current off.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the three compass observations and what each proves, the two ways to strengthen the field, and why ‘only while the current flows’ is the electromagnet's whole point.

## Do It Yourself

1. Compass-and-coil (Activity 7.38): record the needle with current off, on, and reversed — three drawings with the battery drawn each time.
2. Strength test: paper clips held by (a) straight wire, (b) coil, (c) coil on an iron nail, same battery. Table the counts and state the two strengtheners from your numbers.
3. Switch-off moment: lift clips with the electromagnet, then disconnect mid-lift. Write what happens and the one sentence that explains it.
