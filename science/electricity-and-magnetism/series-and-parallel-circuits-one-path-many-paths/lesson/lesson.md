# Series and parallel circuits — one path, many paths

## Do it first

Take one battery and three bulbs. Wire them so that unscrewing ANY one bulb darkens all three. Now rewire the same parts so that unscrewing one bulb leaves the other two shining. Same battery, same bulbs — two opposite behaviours. The wiring alone decides. Build both before you read a word.

## Sub lesson 1 — Series — one path

A **series circuit** is a **one-way circular road**: every part sits on a **single loop**, the current passes **through each load in turn, chain-like** — and **remove or break any part and everything stops**. Build it and measure (Activity 7.27): one to three bulbs in series, brightness graded **dim to very bright**, current read on an **ammeter**. As bulbs are added in series the sharing shows: the same current must pass through each, and the bulbs dim as the chain lengthens.

## Sub lesson 2 — Parallel — many paths

A **parallel circuit** is **many roads to one place**: the circuit divides into **branches**, the **current divides** between them, each branch carries its share **at the same time** — and **a bulb burning out on one branch leaves the others glowing**, which is exactly why **home lighting must be wired in parallel**, as you are asked to deduce. Build and measure the parallel form (Activity 7.28), varying batteries and bulbs together, and face the diagnostic questions: why do brightness and current behave **oppositely** in the two wirings, and what does removing one bulb do in each? Series: all die. Parallel: the rest shine on.

## Say it to your companion

Tell your companion the road picture for each wiring, what removing one bulb does in each, and why a house wired in series would be a bad joke told every time a bulb dies.

## Do It Yourself

1. Build both (Activities 7.27–7.28): same battery, same three bulbs, both wirings. Record brightness (dim–very bright) and ammeter current for 1, 2 and 3 bulbs in each wiring, in one table.
2. The removal test: in each wiring, unscrew the middle bulb. Record what happens to the others, in the table's last column.
3. Deduce the house: write three lines arguing, from your table alone, why home lighting is parallel. No book words — your data must carry it.
