# Changes — useful and harmful changes in daily life

## Do it first
Think of yesterday's kitchen. Grain was ground. Wood was chopped. Dough rose. Injera baked. A fire burned. Every one of those is a change — some physical, some chemical. This lesson sorts them, then judges them: which changes help us, and which harm us?

## Sub lesson 1 — Sorting the day's changes
- **Physical:** grinding teff, chopping wood — form changes, the substance stays.
- **Chemical:** dough rising, injera baking, wood burning — new substances form.

## Sub lesson 2 — Useful changes we work on purpose
- **Freezing** preserves food — a physical change worked for us.
- **Evaporating sea water** yields salt.
- **Fermentation** — a chemical change — makes **tella, tej and bread**.
- Chemical changes give us **medicines, soap, glass and plastics**.

## Sub lesson 3 — Harmful changes — and the tella project
Harmful changes work against us: **rusting** eats iron, **food decay** spoils what we would eat, and **explosions** destroy suddenly.

**Project:** trace the making of **tella** (and araki) from grain to drink. At every step ask: physical or chemical? Grinding — physical. Fermenting — chemical. The home process is a whole chemistry course in one vessel.

## Say it to your companion
Name one useful physical change, one useful chemical change, and one harmful change from your own home. Your companion checks the sorting.

## Do It Yourself
1. Keep a one-day change diary: every change at home, tagged physical or chemical, useful or harmful.
2. Interview a tella or araki maker. Write the steps in order and tag each one.
3. Find one rusting thing at home. What change is rust — and how do people slow it?
