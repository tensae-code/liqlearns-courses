# Lesson plan — Changes — useful and harmful changes in daily life

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Materials · **Lesson:** Changes — useful and harmful changes in daily life
**Place in the curriculum:** Materials — Grade 7, Unit 2 "Matter in Our Surroundings" (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the changes of daily life sort into useful and harmful — and most useful ones are worked on purpose, at home, every day
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Changes — physical and chemical (grown in Chemical change: burning and gas-making); here those two kinds are put to work and judged

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Sorting the day's changes** — grinding teff and chopping wood (physical) against dough rising, injera baking and wood burning (chemical) — the home is full of both kinds
2. **Useful changes we work on purpose** — freezing preserves food; sea-water evaporation yields salt; fermentation makes tella, tej and bread; changes give medicines, soap, glass and plastics
3. **Harmful changes — and the tella project** — rusting, food decay and explosions harm us; a project traces every physical and chemical step in making tella and araki

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Materials — Chemical change: burning and gas-making. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Sort home changes into physical and chemical.
2. Name useful changes: preserving, salt-making, fermentation, medicines, soap, glass, plastics.
3. Name harmful changes: rusting, decay, explosions.
4. Trace the making of tella step by step, tagging each step.

## What you need
Nothing to do it: the learner's own home and day. For the project: a family member who makes tella or araki, to interview.

## If the learner is stuck
- Calls freezing chemical because the food 'changed' → frozen food thaws back to itself; no new substance formed.
- Thinks harmful changes can simply be banned → rust and decay are natural changes; we slow and manage them, we do not switch them off.
