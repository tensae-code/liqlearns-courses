# Reactions — conservation of mass

## Do it first

Burn a sheet of paper. The ash weighs far less than the paper did. Has mass been destroyed? Write your answer before reading — the law below will judge it.

## The law

**Mass is neither created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction.** Total mass of reactants = total mass of products. The reason: a reaction rearranges atoms; it does not make or unmake them. Atom kinds and atom counts stay the same — only their groupings change.

## The equation proof

2Na + Cl₂ → 2NaCl. Left: 2 sodium atoms and 2 chlorine atoms. Right: in 2NaCl, 2 sodium and 2 chlorine. Nothing added, nothing lost — count for count.

## The hard cases

Burnt paper: the missing mass left as gases (carbon dioxide and water vapour) into the air — count them and the balance holds. Magnesium ash weighs MORE than the ribbon: it gained oxygen's mass from the air. Dissolved sugar and ground teff change form, not mass.

## Experiment 3.3 — the closed tube

Iron filings and sulfur are heated inside a CLOSED tube and weighed before and after. The masses are equal — because nothing could escape and nothing could enter. A closed system is how the law is weighed honestly.
