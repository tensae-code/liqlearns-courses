# Lesson plan — Reactions — conservation of mass

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Materials · **Lesson:** Reactions — conservation of mass
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Elements, Compounds and Chemical Reactions (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the law of conservation of mass: in a reaction, mass is neither created nor destroyed — the total mass of the reactants equals the total mass of the products, because atoms only rearrange
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Reactions — five signs to look for (same unit). The Chemistry title states conservation again at secondary depth; this is the first-depth lesson, new here, cross-referenced not copied.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the law of conservation of mass in full.
2. Explain the law by atom rearrangement, with the Na/Cl₂ count.
3. Account for burnt paper's 'lost' mass and magnesium ash's gained mass.
4. Say why Experiment 3.3 uses a closed tube.

## If the learner is stuck
Says ash lighter means mass destroyed → the gases are products too; weigh ALL products and the totals match. Says magnesium breaks the law by gaining → the gain is oxygen taken from the air, a reactant that was not on the scale's pan at the start.
