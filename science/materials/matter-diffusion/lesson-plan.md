# Lesson plan — Matter — diffusion

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Materials · **Lesson:** Matter — diffusion
**Place in the curriculum:** Materials — Grade 7, Unit 2 "Matter in Our Surroundings" (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** particles spread from where they are crowded to where they are spread out — fast in gases, slower in liquids, essentially not at all in solids
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Matter — the particle theory (particles always move); diffusion is that movement made visible

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Diffusion in gases and liquids** — perfume or incense is smelled across a whole classroom; ink colours a whole jar of water — particles spread from high to low concentration
2. **Why solids barely diffuse** — gas particles move fast with weak attraction; liquid particles move slower; solid particles only vibrate in place — so diffusion is fast, slow, absent

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Materials — Matter — the particle theory. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define diffusion: spreading from high concentration to low.
2. Give one gas example (perfume/incense across a room) and one liquid example (ink in water).
3. Rank the three states for diffusion speed and explain each with particles.

## What you need
Perfume or incense; a jar of water and a drop of ink.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the scent was carried by wind alone → even in still air it spreads; moving particles are the carriers.
- Expects a solid to diffuse like a gas → a solid's particles only vibrate in place; there is no travelling to spread with.
