# Matter — diffusion

## Do it first
Spray perfume — or light incense — in one corner of the room. Stay where you are, far away. Within moments you smell it. Nobody carried the scent to you. It travelled by itself. How?

## Sub lesson 1 — Diffusion in gases and liquids
**Diffusion** is the spreading of particles **from where they are crowded (high concentration) to where they are few (low concentration)**, until they are evenly spread. The perfume's particles leave the corner crowded together and wander through the air until the whole room holds them evenly — that is why the far side smells the perfume too.

In liquids it happens as well, more slowly: a drop of ink in a jar of water blooms, spreads, and at last colours the whole jar — the ink's particles diffuse through the water's particles.

## Sub lesson 2 — Why solids barely diffuse
- **Gases — fast.** Gas particles move fast and attract each other weakly, so they spread quickly.
- **Liquids — slower.** Liquid particles move, but more slowly and with stronger attraction.
- **Solids — essentially none.** A solid's particles do not travel; they only **vibrate in place**. Put two solids together and they stay themselves — no diffusion.

## Say it to your companion
Explain diffusion using the perfume: where were the particles crowded, where were they few, and what did they do? Then say why ink is slower and a solid does not diffuse at all.

## Do It Yourself
1. Time the perfume: spray in one corner; note when the far corner first smells it.
2. Drop ink into still water. Do NOT stir. Sketch the jar at once, after five minutes, and after half an hour.
3. Press a piece of chalk against a coloured cloth for a day. Did colour diffuse into the chalk? Explain with particles.
