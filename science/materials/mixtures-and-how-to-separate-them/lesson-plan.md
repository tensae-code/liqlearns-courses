# Lesson plan — Mixtures and how to separate them

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Materials · **Lesson:** Mixtures and how to separate them
**Place in the curriculum:** Materials — the two kinds of mixture and the four physical ways back apart (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** mixtures are heterogeneous (ልይ ዘር — the components show) or homogeneous (ዋህድ ዘር — hidden, like a solution of solute in solvent), and because a mixture's joining is only physical, it can be undone physically: filtration, the separating funnel, evaporation and distillation (ንጥረት)
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Elements, compounds and mixtures — a mixture's marks are known; now its undoing is learned, four ways.

## Before this lesson

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Tell the two kinds of mixture apart with the book's examples: oil and water, soil, blood — against salt solution, areke, steel, clean air.
2. Name solute and solvent, and the marks of a mixture (Table 3.6): parts keep their properties, no fixed composition, separable physically.
3. Run or describe the four separations of Experiment 3: filtration, separating funnel, evaporation, distillation.

## What you need
Muddy water, filter paper and a funnel; a plastic bottle with a pierced cap for the funnel test; a watch glass of salt water; oil and water.

## If the learner is stuck
- Calls steel a compound → steel is the book's homogeneous mixture: hidden components, no fixed formula.
- Tries the funnel on salt water → a homogeneous mixture has no layers; the funnel parts layers only. Salt water needs evaporation or distillation.
