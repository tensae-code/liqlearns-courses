# Separating mixtures — magnet and evaporation

## Do it first
Stir iron filings and yellow sulfur together until no eye can sort them. They look like one grey-yellow powder. Can they ever be parted again — without picking grain by grain? And the salt in salt water: the water can leave — can the salt stay? This lesson does both.

## Sub lesson 1 — Experiment 2.5 — the magnet lifts the iron out
**Aim:** to separate iron filings from sulfur using a magnet (Experiment 2.5); then to separate salt from salt water by evaporation (Experiment 2.6)
**Materials:** iron filings, sulfur powder, a magnet, paper; then a salt solution, a heat source and an evaporating dish (adult work wherever heat is used)
**Steps:**
1. Mix a little iron filings with sulfur powder on paper until they are well mixed.
2. Pass the magnet slowly over the mixture, just above it.
3. Lift away the filings that cling to the magnet and collect them on clean paper.
4. Repeat until no more filings jump. For Experiment 2.6: pour a salt solution into an evaporating dish.
5. Heat gently (adult) until all the water has driven off as vapour.

## Sub lesson 2 — What you observe, and what it proves
**Observation:** The black filings leap to the magnet and are lifted clean out; the yellow sulfur stays on the paper — the mixture is separated. In Experiment 2.6 the water leaves as vapour and white salt remains in the dish. This is the same evaporation that wins table salt from sea water under the sun.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion both experiments in order — aim, materials, steps, observation. Your companion asks: why does the magnet take the iron and not the sulfur? Why does the salt stay when the water goes?

## Do It Yourself
1. Hide a paper clip in a tray of sand. Recover it with a magnet — without touching the sand with your hands.
2. Put a spoon of salt water in a shallow dish in the sun. Check it morning and evening. What is left, and where did the water go?
3. Which method fits a mixture of iron nails and wooden blocks? Say the property you used to choose.
