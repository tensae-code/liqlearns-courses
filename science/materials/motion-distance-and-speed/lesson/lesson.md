# Motion, distance and speed

## Do it first
Bogale sleeps on a moving bus while Yasin watches herders wave from the roadside. Who is moving? The book's puzzle opens the chapter — and its answer is the lesson: motion depends on where you stand.

## Motion is relative; four kinds of motion
**Motion is change of place relative to something else.** Describing it needs a **reference point (መቃን)** — Bogale is still relative to the bus, and moving relative to the roadside herders; both are true. Motions sort by their **path**: **straight-line** (a person or car on a straight road); **circular** (the Moon around the Earth, the Earth around the Sun — a thrown stone's curve is the curved sub-kind); **oscillating** (swing play, side to side or back and forth); and **rotational** (spinning on an axis — a clock's minute hand).

## Distance and displacement; speed, velocity, acceleration
**Position** says where a body is relative to another. **Distance** is the whole length of the road actually travelled. **Displacement** is the straight change of place from start to finish — the shortest distance between the two points. The book's note: **every displacement is a distance, but not every distance is a displacement.** Then the rates: **speed = distance ÷ time**; **velocity = displacement ÷ time** — velocity is speed **with a direction**; the world unit for both is the **metre per second (m/s)**. The worked problem: Almaz's winding **5 km / 50 min** walk to school against the **3 km / 30 min** short cut — compute her speed and her velocity both ways. And **acceleration (ሽምጠጣ) = change of velocity ÷ change of time**, unit **metre per second squared (m/s²)** — the measure of speeding up, slowing down and turning.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion the bus puzzle's answer first — motion is relative to a reference point — then the four paths, then distance against displacement on Almaz's two routes.

## Do It Yourself
1. Path hunt: watch for one minute outside. List every moving thing and its path kind — straight, circular, oscillating, rotational.
2. School-route reckoning: pace or measure your road to school (distance) and the straight line on a sketch (displacement). Which is longer — and can displacement ever beat distance? (No.)
3. Almaz, worked: her winding walk is 5 km in 50 min; her short cut 3 km in 30 min. Which route gives the bigger speed? Show the division both ways.
