# Lesson plan — Reactions — reactants become products

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Materials · **Lesson:** Reactions — reactants become products
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Elements, Compounds and Chemical Reactions (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a chemical reaction converts reactants into products — first as a word equation, then in symbols; the products are new substances, not the reactants reshaped
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Formulas — the two meanings of a formula (same unit). The Chemistry title's lesson Chemical reactions — reactants become products treats this ground at secondary depth; this is the first-depth lesson, new here, cross-referenced not copied.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier Unit 3 lessons in order — symbols, then formulas, then reactions. Recommended, never forced; this lesson stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define reactants and products and tell them apart in an example.
2. Write a word equation and read the arrow correctly.
3. Recognise home reactions: burning fuel, brewing, yoghurt-making.
4. Use Experiment 3.1's property comparison to argue a new substance formed.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. (The magnesium burning is a teacher demonstration — never burn magnesium at home.)

## If the learner is stuck
Calls melting ice a reaction → nothing new forms; the products test fails. Puts products on the left → reactants always start the equation; the arrow points to the products.
