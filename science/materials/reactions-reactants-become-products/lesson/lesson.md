# Reactions — reactants become products

## Do it first

A magnesium ribbon is silvery and bends. Its ash is white, soft and crumbly. Are they the same substance? Decide, and write your reason. Then read on.

## What a reaction is

In a **chemical reaction**, substances are converted into new substances. The starting substances are the **reactants**; the new substances made are the **products**. The products have different properties from the reactants — that is the test.

## Home reactions first

You already live among reactions: fuel burning, tella and tej brewing, milk becoming ergo yoghurt. In each, something you start with is gone and something new stands in its place.

## Word equation, then symbols

First write the reaction in words: hydrogen + oxygen → water. The arrow reads 'yields' or 'becomes'. Then in symbols: **2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O** — counted and balanced (the balancing lessons come next; this equation is already balanced: 4 H and 2 O on each side — check it).

## Experiment 3.1 — burning magnesium

A magnesium ribbon held in a flame catches with a brilliant white light and leaves a white ash. Compare: the metal is shiny, grey and bendable; the ash is white, dull and powdery. New properties — a new substance, magnesium oxide. A reaction happened.
