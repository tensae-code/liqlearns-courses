# Separating mixtures — distillation

## Do it first
Heat a mixture of alcohol and water. Something leaves the pot long before the water boils hard — and in the local araki still, that something is caught, cooled, and collected as a strong drink. The laboratory does the same thing in glass. This lesson builds it.

## Sub lesson 1 — First the local still — how araki (katikala) is made
**Aim:** to separate a liquid mixture by boiling point using simple distillation (Experiment 2.10), after studying the local araki still
**Materials:** distillation flask, heat source (adult work), condenser, receiver, boiling chips, an alcohol-and-water mixture; pictures of the local araki (katikala) still
**Steps:**
1. Study the local araki still first: name each part and its job.
2. Set up the laboratory train: flask, condenser sloping down to the receiver; condenser water in at the bottom, out at the top.
3. Add boiling chips to the flask to prevent bumping.
4. Heat gently (adult). The lower-boiling liquid vaporises first, travels, condenses, and drips into the receiver.

## Sub lesson 2 — What you observe, and what it proves
**Observation:** The alcohol vaporises before the water, condenses in the cooled tube, and is collected as the distillate; the rest stays in the flask as residue. Distillation separates by boiling point — in the laboratory still and in the local araki still by the same principle.

## Say it to your companion
Walk your companion along the train: flask, vapour, condenser, receiver. At each stop they ask 'what happens here?' — and you answer with a state change or a safety point.

## Do It Yourself
1. Visit or interview: draw the local araki still and label every part with its job.
2. Explain why condenser water must enter at the bottom: what happens to the cooling if it enters at the top?
3. A mixture of water and a dissolved solid: will distillation give you the solid, the water, or both? Answer for each vessel.
