# Lesson plan — Separating mixtures — distillation

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Materials · **Lesson:** Separating mixtures — distillation
**Place in the curriculum:** Materials — Grade 7, Unit 2 "Matter in Our Surroundings" (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a liquid mixture is parted by boiling point: the lower-boiling part vaporises first, condenses, and is collected as distillate — the way of the laboratory still and of the local araki still alike
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on all the earlier separations in this title; distillation is the method chosen when liquids differ in boiling point

2. **What you observe, and what it proves** — Experiment 2.10: heat vaporises the lower-boiling liquid; the condenser (water in at the bottom, out at the top) turns vapour back to liquid; boiling chips prevent bumping; the distillate collects, the residue stays

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Materials — Mixtures and how to separate them. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the local araki still's parts and their jobs.
2. Run or describe Experiment 2.10 in order: vaporise, condense, collect.
3. Say why condenser water runs bottom-to-top, and why boiling chips are added.
4. Name what is collected (distillate) and what stays (residue).

## What you need
distillation flask, heat source (adult work), condenser, receiver, boiling chips, an alcohol-and-water mixture; pictures of the local araki (katikala) still

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks the distillate is the part that stays → the distillate is what LEAVES as vapour and is caught; what stays is the residue.
- Runs condenser water top-first → the jacket then never fills; bottom-to-top keeps it full and cooling along its whole length.
