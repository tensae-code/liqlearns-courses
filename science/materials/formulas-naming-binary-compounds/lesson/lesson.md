# Formulas — naming binary compounds

## Do it first

Here are three formulas: NaCl, Cu₂O, CO₂. Try to say each one's name out loud before reading on. Keep your tries — two of them hide traps this lesson names.

## The basic pattern

Name the **metal first**, exactly as it is. Then rename the **non-metal** with the ending **-ide**: chloride, fluoride, oxide, nitride, sulfide. NaCl is sodium chloride. MgO is magnesium oxide.

## Variable-valence metals: the Roman numeral

When the metal can use two valences, the name must say which one. The Roman numeral gives the metal's valence in THIS compound: Cu₂O is copper(I) oxide; FeCl₂ is iron(II) chloride; FeCl₃ is iron(III) chloride.

## Two non-metals: Greek prefixes

With no metal, count the atoms with Greek prefixes — mono-, di-, tri-, tetra-, penta-, hexa-: CO carbon monoxide, CO₂ carbon dioxide, SO₃ sulfur trioxide, N₂O dinitrogen monoxide. The first element takes a prefix only when it has more than one atom.

## Common names hide composition

Some compounds are known by everyday names that hide what is inside them: limestone, table salt, water, ammonia. The systematic name always tells the composition; the common name must simply be learned.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words: a binary compound is named metal-first with the non-metal renamed -ide; variable-valence metals carry a Roman numeral, and non-metal pairs are counted with Greek prefixes.
