# Formulas — valence, the combining power

## Do it first

In NaCl, one sodium holds one chlorine. In water, one oxygen holds two hydrogens. In AlCl₃, one aluminium holds three chlorines. Before the word is named: what number describes each first element — sodium, oxygen, aluminium? Write the three numbers.

## Valence named

That number is the element's **valence** — its combining power: how many partners one atom of it combines with. Sodium's valence is 1, oxygen's is 2, aluminium's is 3.

## Reading valence off compounds

Valence is read from real compounds. In NaCl, Na and Cl each combine with one partner — both valence 1. In H₂O, oxygen combines with two hydrogens — oxygen valence 2, hydrogen valence 1.

## The common valences

Valence 1: Na, K, H, Cl, Br. Valence 2: Ca, Mg, Zn, O, S. Valence 3: Al, N, P. Valence 4: C, Si. Learn them in these groups — the next lessons spend them.

## Elements with two valences

Some elements vary. Iron is valence 2 in FeCl₂ but valence 3 in FeCl₃. Copper is valence 1 in Cu₂O but valence 2 in CuO. When an element varies, the compound in front of you tells you which valence it is using.
