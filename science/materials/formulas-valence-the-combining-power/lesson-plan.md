# Lesson plan — Formulas — valence, the combining power

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Materials · **Lesson:** Formulas — valence, the combining power
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Elements, Compounds and Chemical Reactions (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** valence is an element's combining power — how many partners one of its atoms combines with — read off the compounds it forms
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Formulas — reading what a compound is made of (same unit). The Chemistry title uses valence again at secondary depth; this is the first-depth lesson, new here, cross-referenced not copied.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define valence as combining power.
2. Read an element's valence off a compound it forms.
3. Recite the common valence groups 1–4.
4. Explain how iron can be valence 2 in one compound and 3 in another.

## If the learner is stuck
Thinks valence is the number of atoms in the formula → valence is per ONE atom: in AlCl₃, aluminium's valence is 3 because one Al holds three Cl. Thinks iron has one fixed valence → FeCl₂ and FeCl₃ show both; check the compound.
