# Design and build a model building

## Do it first
Before you read: say out loud what you already think this lesson's one thing is — designing and building a model building — a strong bridge, a tall steady tower — then showing it and improving it; with Lu Ban, the master carpenter, as the book's enrichment. Then read on: this is China's way in — its 科学 (Science) textbooks teach this thing at the places named in the sources, and this lesson follows their order.

## Sub lesson 1 — Designing and building the model

China's Grade 3 building unit ends in a build of the learner's own: **design and build a model building** — a strong bridge, or a tall steady tower. The design comes first: what will it be, what shapes will hold it, what materials will serve? Then the building: putting the strong shapes to work — triangles that hold their shape, wide bases, columns and arches — in a model small enough to test.

The model is the unit's test of itself: a bridge is judged by the load it carries, a tower by whether it stands tall and steady.

## Sub lesson 2 — Showing it and improving it — and Lu Ban

A finished model is not the end. The class **shows** its buildings, tests them, and **improves** them: where the bridge sagged, where the tower leaned — each weakness found is a change to make, and the improved model is built again, stronger.

The book closes the unit with **Lu Ban**, the master carpenter of ancient China who, the book tells, invented the saw and the plane — the builder's tools themselves. He stands in the book as the model of the builder: the one who designs, makes, and makes better.

## The key ideas

- The build is **designed first** — the model, its shapes and its materials decided before building starts.
- A model building is **judged by testing**: a bridge by the load it carries, a tower by standing tall and steady.
- Showing a build leads to **improving** it — each weakness found becomes a change, and the model is built again stronger; **Lu Ban**, inventor of the saw and the plane, is the book's model builder.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — Designing and building the model, Showing it and improving it — and Lu Ban. Your companion may stop you once and ask one question about the hardest part; answer from the lesson, not by repeating it.

## Do It Yourself
1. Design a model bridge or tower on paper: draw it, label the strong shapes in it, and list the materials you would use.
2. Build your design at home from safe materials, test it — load the bridge, or stand the tower tall — and write what the test showed.
3. Change one thing in your build to improve it, test again, and write whether the change worked.
