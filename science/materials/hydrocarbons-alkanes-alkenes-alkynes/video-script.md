This is a FULL script — the placement names a biogas-digester build script for the project.

Build a household biogas digester — the design, step by step.

Biogas is mostly methane, CH4 — the same gas as the first alkane.

You need: a sealed tank or pit that no air can enter, an inlet pipe for dung and water, a gas pipe at the top, and a pipe down to a simple stove.

Step one: choose a shaded place near the cattle pen and the kitchen — short pipes both ways.

Step two: dig and line the pit, or set the tank, and seal every joint. Air is the enemy: the gas-makers work only without air.

Step three: mix dung with about the same amount of water. Pour the slurry in through the inlet.

Step four: close the tank. Wait. Inside, tiny living things break the dung down and methane collects at the top.

Step five: open the gas valve a little and light the stove with a match held ready — a steady blue flame means the digester is working.

Step six: the slurry that flows out is not waste. It is rich fertiliser for the field.

Safety: never open the tank to look inside, never light a flame near an open pipe, and let an adult check every joint for leaks with soapy water — bubbles mean a leak.

Dung in. Gas for cooking. Fertiliser out. That is the digester.
