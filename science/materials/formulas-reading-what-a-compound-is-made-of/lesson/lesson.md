# Formulas — reading what a compound is made of

## Do it first

Look at this formula: C₆H₈O₇ — it is citric acid, the sour substance in lemons. Before anyone explains it, guess: how many different elements are in it? How many atoms in total? Write your guesses down.

## What a compound is

A **compound** is different elements chemically joined in a **fixed ratio** — always the same make-up, wherever you find it. Water is always 2 hydrogen to 1 oxygen. That fixed ratio is what separates a compound from a mixture.

## Reading a formula

A formula names the elements and counts their atoms. The small low number — the **subscript** — belongs to the element just before it. In C₆H₈O₇: 6 carbon atoms, 8 hydrogen atoms, 7 oxygen atoms. No subscript written means exactly **one** atom: in NaCl there is 1 sodium and 1 chlorine.

## Worked formulas

NaCl — 1 sodium, 1 chlorine. Al₂O₃ — 2 aluminium, 3 oxygen. CaCO₃ — 1 calcium, 1 carbon, 3 oxygen. Read every formula this way: element, count, element, count.

## Molecules of elements

Some elements travel as molecules of their own atoms. **Monoatomic**: the noble gases, single atoms. **Diatomic** — two atoms together: H₂, N₂, O₂, F₂, Cl₂, Br₂, I₂. **Polyatomic** — more: P₄ and S₈. Oxygen in the air is O₂, never a lone O.
