# Lesson plan — Formulas — reading what a compound is made of

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Materials · **Lesson:** Formulas — reading what a compound is made of
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Elements, Compounds and Chemical Reactions (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** a compound joins different elements in a fixed ratio, and its formula lists the elements with subscripts that count the atoms
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Symbols — writing the elements (same unit). The Chemistry title reads formulas again at secondary depth; this is the first-depth lesson, new here, cross-referenced not copied.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a compound is: different elements joined in a fixed ratio.
2. Read any simple formula as a list of elements with atom counts.
3. State the no-subscript rule: it means one atom.
4. Name the diatomic elements and write O₂, H₂, Cl₂ correctly.

## If the learner is stuck
Counts the subscript for the whole formula → a subscript belongs only to the element directly before it: in CaCO₃ the 3 counts oxygen only. Writes oxygen in air as O → air's oxygen is the diatomic molecule O₂.
