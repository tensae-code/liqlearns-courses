# Strong shapes — triangles, arches and columns

## Do it first
Before you read: say out loud what you already think this lesson's one thing is — which structure is steadier: the triangle that will not rack, the wide base, the column's shape and the arch that carries weight to its feet. Then read on: this is China's way in — its 科学 (Science) textbooks teach this thing at the places named in the sources, and this lesson follows their order.

## Sub lesson 1 — Triangles against squares

Push the side of a square frame and it **racks** — it leans into a diamond without any side changing length. Push a **triangle** and it cannot lean: its three sides fix its three angles, so a triangle frame holds its shape.

China's Grade 3 book tests the two frames against each other — triangle against square — because this is the first secret of steady building: where a structure must keep its shape, triangles do the holding. A square frame is steadied by adding a diagonal — which is only a way of dividing it into triangles.

## Sub lesson 2 — Wide bases, columns and the arch

The book's tests go on to the other steady shapes. A **wide base** stands steadier than a narrow one: the weight sits inside the base instead of tipping past it. A **column** carries weight downward, and its shape — what it is made like, round or square, thick or thin — decides how much it can carry.

The **arch** solves a different problem: spanning a gap. Weight pressing down on an arch is carried along its curve, outwards and down into its feet — so an opening can be crossed without a straight beam, and the load reaches the ground along the arch's own shape.

## The key ideas

- A **triangle** holds its shape; a square frame racks sideways — a diagonal steadies a square by dividing it into triangles.
- A **wide base** stands steadier than a narrow one, and a **column** carries weight downward — its shape decides its strength.
- An **arch** carries weight along its curve, out and down into its feet, so it can span a gap.

## Do It Yourself
1. Make a square frame and a triangle frame from strips and pins or straws and string. Push each sideways and draw what happens to each.
2. Build the tallest tower you can from the same pieces twice — once on a narrow base, once on a wide one. Write which stood steadier and why.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences — how an arch carries weight to the ground.
