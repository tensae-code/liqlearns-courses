# Soaps and detergents — and how they clean — short answer

Answer in your own words.

**1.** Explain: the kinds we use.
**Answer: Soaps come as hard bars for washing bodies and clothes and as softer forms for dishes and floors; detergents are the made cleaners sold as powders and liquids, built to work even in hard water where soap struggles. Each kind is made for its job.**

**2.** Explain: why water alone is not enough.
**Answer: Most dirt is held by grease, and water and grease refuse to mix — water alone slides off a greasy plate. A cleaner must bridge the two: one end of the soap particle joins the water, the other end joins the grease.**

**3.** Explain: how the cleaning happens.
**Answer: Rubbed on with water, soap surrounds the grease in tiny balls, loosens its hold on the skin, cloth or dish, and the rinse water carries the balls away. That is the whole trick of washing: loosen, surround, rinse away.**
