# Soaps and detergents — and how they clean — multiple choice

Pick the one right answer.

**1.** What does this lesson say about soaps and detergents — and how they clean?
a) Soap and detergents clean because each of their particles holds water with one end and grease with the other — they lift dirt and grease off a surface and let the water carry it away.
b) It plays no part in this topic at all
c) It happens only at night and nowhere else
d) It is found only in non-living things
**Answer: a) Soap and detergents clean because each of their particles holds water with one end and grease with the other — they lift dirt and grease off a surface and let the water carry it away.**

**2.** What does this lesson say about the kinds we use?
a) It plays no part in this topic at all
b) Soaps come as hard bars for washing bodies and clothes and as softer forms for dishes and floors; detergents are the made cleaners sold as powders and liquids, built to work even in hard water where soap struggles.
c) It happens only at night and nowhere else
d) It is found only in non-living things
**Answer: b) Soaps come as hard bars for washing bodies and clothes and as softer forms for dishes and floors; detergents are the made cleaners sold as powders and liquids, built to work even in hard water where soap struggles.**

**3.** What does this lesson say about why water alone is not enough?
a) It plays no part in this topic at all
b) It happens only at night and nowhere else
c) Most dirt is held by grease, and water and grease refuse to mix — water alone slides off a greasy plate.
d) It is found only in non-living things
**Answer: c) Most dirt is held by grease, and water and grease refuse to mix — water alone slides off a greasy plate.**

**4.** What does this lesson say about how the cleaning happens?
a) It plays no part in this topic at all
b) It happens only at night and nowhere else
c) It is found only in non-living things
d) Rubbed on with water, soap surrounds the grease in tiny balls, loosens its hold on the skin, cloth or dish, and the rinse water carries the balls away.
**Answer: d) Rubbed on with water, soap surrounds the grease in tiny balls, loosens its hold on the skin, cloth or dish, and the rinse water carries the balls away.**
