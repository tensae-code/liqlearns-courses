Today: Soaps and detergents — and how they clean.

Remember this one thing: soap and detergents clean because each of their particles holds water with one end and grease with the other — they lift dirt and grease off a surface and let the water carry it away.

Soaps come as hard bars for washing bodies and clothes and as softer forms for dishes and floors; detergents are the made cleaners sold as powders and liquids, built to work even in hard water where soap struggles. Each kind is made for its job.

Most dirt is held by grease, and water and grease refuse to mix — water alone slides off a greasy plate. A cleaner must bridge the two: one end of the soap particle joins the water, the other end joins the grease.

Rubbed on with water, soap surrounds the grease in tiny balls, loosens its hold on the skin, cloth or dish, and the rinse water carries the balls away. That is the whole trick of washing: loosen, surround, rinse away.

Now say it back, in your own words. That is the lesson.
