# Soaps and detergents — and how they clean

## Do it first
Before you read anything: think about this lesson's title — Soaps and detergents — and how they clean. Write down, or say aloud, anything you already know about it, and one question you want answered. Then read on and check yourself at the end.

## The idea
South Sudan's Primary 3 Science pupil's book teaches washing from the inside: after food and the body, Unit 1 names the types of soap and detergents — the cleaners every home uses — as science worth knowing.

In your mind's eye: soap and detergents clean because each of their particles holds water with one end and grease with the other — they lift dirt and grease off a surface and let the water carry it away. — picture it happening step by step, in order, and say each step as you see it.

## The key facts
**The kinds we use.** Soaps come as hard bars for washing bodies and clothes and as softer forms for dishes and floors; detergents are the made cleaners sold as powders and liquids, built to work even in hard water where soap struggles. Each kind is made for its job.

**Why water alone is not enough.** Most dirt is held by grease, and water and grease refuse to mix — water alone slides off a greasy plate. A cleaner must bridge the two: one end of the soap particle joins the water, the other end joins the grease.

**How the cleaning happens.** Rubbed on with water, soap surrounds the grease in tiny balls, loosens its hold on the skin, cloth or dish, and the rinse water carries the balls away. That is the whole trick of washing: loosen, surround, rinse away.
