# Sand flowing — and what changes it — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Describe how dry sand flows when it is poured.
**Answer:** Grain slides over grain: the sand pours in a stream, lands, and piles into a heap that settles and spreads — thousands of solid grains together flow like a very slow, grainy liquid.

**2.** (Sub lesson 1) Why is sand's flowing surprising, and what makes it possible?
**Answer:** A single grain is a solid that cannot pour, but thousands of grains together can slide over each other, so together they run, pile and settle — the flowing belongs to the grains together.

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) How is what affects sand's flowing found in this activity?
**Answer:** By pouring again and changing one thing at a time — pour height, tipping speed, amount of sand — keeping the rest the same, and comparing each pour with the last.

**4.** (Sub lesson 2) Why must only one thing change between pours?
**Answer:** So the comparison can answer: if two things changed and the flow differed, nobody could say which change did it; with one change, the difference between the pours belongs to that change.
