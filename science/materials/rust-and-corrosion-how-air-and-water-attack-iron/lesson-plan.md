# Lesson plan — Rust and corrosion — how air and water attack iron

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Materials · **Lesson:** Rust and corrosion — how air and water attack iron
**Place in the curriculum:** Materials (CURRICULUM.md Unit 4), directly after Materials and their properties — hard, strong, flexible in the Gabon 4e année materials thread: properties tell how a material behaves; this lesson teaches how one material is slowly destroyed, and how to stop it
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** rust is the reddish-brown, crumbly coating that eats iron and steel left in damp air: corrosion needs BOTH air (oxygen) and water together — iron kept dry, or kept underwater away from air, does not rust — and because rust weakens and finally holes the metal, people protect iron by keeping water and air off its surface with paint, oil or grease, by coating it with another metal, or by choosing stainless materials where the damp cannot be helped

## Sub lessons
1. **Rust — iron eaten slowly** — corrosion is the slow chemical attack of a metal by its surroundings; on iron its product is rust — a soft, reddish-brown, flaky substance utterly unlike the hard grey metal it replaces; rusting does not stop at the surface — flakes fall, fresh metal is exposed, and the attack continues until a tool, a nail or a sheet is weakened through and holed
2. **Air AND water — both are needed** — the three-nail experiment proves rust's recipe: a nail in dry air stays bright; a nail under boiled water covered with oil (water but no air) stays bright; only the nail with both air and water rusts — oxygen from the air and water together do the attack, and salty or dirty water speeds it
3. **Protecting iron — keep air and water off** — every protection is the experiment applied: paint, enamel, oil and grease seal the surface away from air and water; coating iron with zinc (galvanising) or tin gives it a skin of a metal that resists; stainless steel is mixed to resist by itself; and the simplest protection is storage — dry, sheltered, wiped — because dry iron does not rust

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words: Corrosion is the slow chemical attack of a metal by its surroundings; on iron the product is rust — soft, reddish-brown and crumbly.
2. Say, in their own words: Rust is transformed metal, not surface dirt: it thins, weakens and finally holes iron, and it never stops by itself.
3. Say, in their own words: Rust needs air (oxygen) AND water together — the three-nail experiment proves each one alone is harmless.
4. Say, in their own words: Salt and dirty water make rusting faster; the air–water meeting line rusts first.
5. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## What you need
Three identical iron nails, three jars or cups, water, oil, and a few days of patience; an old rusty object to examine.

## If the learner is stuck
- Cannot start → return to Do it first: scratch the rust — it crumbles, and the metal under it is pitted. Corrosion is that eating, slowed down.
- Thinks water alone rusts iron → Jar 2 answers it: under boiled, oil-covered water the nail stays bright. Air must be present too.
- Thinks rust is a protective skin like paint → rust flakes off and exposes fresh metal, so the attack continues; paint protects precisely because it does not flake the iron away.
