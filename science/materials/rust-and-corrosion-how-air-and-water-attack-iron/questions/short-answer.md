# Rust and corrosion — how air and water attack iron — short answer

Answer in a sentence or two.

**1.** (Sub lesson 1) Why does rust never stop by itself once it starts?
**Answer: Rust flakes off as it forms, exposing fresh iron underneath to the air and water. Each flake removed restarts the attack on new metal, so the eating continues until the iron is consumed or protected.**

**2.** (Sub lesson 2) State what each of the three jars in the experiment proves.
**Answer: Jar 1 (dry air): air alone does not rust iron. Jar 2 (water, air shut out by oil): water alone does not rust iron. Jar 3 (water open to air): air and water together produce rust — both are needed.**

**3.** (Sub lesson 2) Why do ships and seaside iron rust faster than iron inland?
**Answer: Salt water assists the chemical attack — salty, dirty water speeds rusting — and sea air keeps iron constantly damp, so both ingredients are always present and working faster.**

**4.** (Sub lesson 3) Name three different protections for iron and the one idea behind all of them.
**Answer: Any three of: paint/enamel, oil or grease, zinc coating (galvanising), tin coating, stainless steel, dry storage. The one idea: keep air and water from reaching the iron's surface.**
