# What air is made of

## Do it first
You cannot see air, yet it is not one single thing. If air were a mixture, what might be mixed in it? Say your guesses out loud. Then read on: this is Angola's way in — its Grade 5 *Ciências da Natureza* book meets air in nature, then takes it apart into its components (Os componentes do ar), then asks why the gases that compose it matter.

## Sub lesson 1 — The components of air

Air is a **mixture of gases**. Its largest part is **nitrogen**; the part living things breathe and burning needs is **oxygen**; and in small parts it holds **carbon dioxide**, water vapour and other gases. Angola's Grade 5 book names these **components of air** (Os componentes do ar) as their own lesson: the invisible air around us has parts, and the parts can be named and told apart.

## Sub lesson 2 — Why the gases in air matter

Each gas does its own work, and the book's next lesson is the **importance of the gases that compose the air**. **Oxygen** keeps breathing and burning going; **carbon dioxide** is the gas plants take in to make their food; **nitrogen**, the largest part, is drawn into soils and plants' growth. Air matters to life not as one thing but gas by gas — which is why the components had to be named first.

## The key ideas

- Air is a **mixture of gases** — nitrogen the largest part, oxygen next, and carbon dioxide, water vapour and others in small parts.
- **Oxygen** keeps breathing and burning going; **carbon dioxide** is what plants take in to make food.
- The gases matter **one by one** — that is why the book names the components before their importance.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Which gas is the largest part of air, and which gas do we breathe?" Answer from the lesson: Nitrogen is the largest part of air; oxygen is the part living things breathe (and burning needs).
