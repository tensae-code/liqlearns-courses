# Rocks — and what they are

## Do it first
Pick up a stone from the ground, in your mind. Is it alive? Was it made by people, like a brick? Or was it already there, part of the Earth itself? Say your answer out loud. Then read on: this is Albania's way in — its Grade 2 guide asks the question outright: "Çfarë janë shkëmbinjtë?" — *What are rocks?*

## Sub lesson 1 — What a rock is

A **rock** is a hard, natural solid — the material the Earth's ground, hills and mountains are made of.

A rock was not made in a factory and it is not alive. It belongs to the Earth itself: the cliff, the river stone, the mountain and the pebble in your hand are all rock, in different sizes and places.

That is the answer the guide's question teaches: before rocks are sorted, named or used, they are known as the Earth's own hard material, found where nature put it.

## Sub lesson 2 — Rocks around us

Once a rock is known for what it is, it can be found everywhere: stones on a path, rocks in a river, the rocky side of a hill, the material buildings and walls are cut from.

Rocks differ — in colour, in hardness, in how their grains look — and later lessons sort those differences (the hardness of minerals is stocked in `materials/materials-and-their-properties-hard-strong-flexible`, and sedimentary rocks in `earth-and-space/sedimentary-rocks-and-how-they-form`). This lesson's one thing stays first: recognising a rock when you meet one, and knowing it is the Earth's natural solid material, not something people made.

## The key ideas

- A **rock** is a hard, natural solid — the material the Earth itself is made of.
- Rocks are **not alive** and were **not made by people**; the cliff, the river stone and the pebble are all rock.
- Rocks differ in colour, hardness and grains — sorting those differences belongs to later lessons.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order — what a rock is first, then rocks around us. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "Is a brick a rock?" Answer: No — a brick is made by people; a rock is the Earth's own natural material. That made-by-nature line is the lesson's test.

## Do It Yourself
1. Find three rocks near your home. Draw each one and write one sentence on where it was found.
2. For each rock, say one way it differs from the others — colour, hardness, or how its grains look.
3. Teach the hardest part of this lesson to someone at home in five sentences — what a rock is.
