Today: **Mass — how much matter is in a thing** — Kuwait's way: what mass is: the amount of matter in a body — measured with a balance in grams and kilograms, and not the same as size or weight.

Mass is the amount of matter in a thing. A big stone has more matter in it than a small stone of the same rock, so it has more mass. Mass is measured in grams and kilograms with a balance. Kuwait's Grade 2 book introduces mass beside volume in its Matter unit, with the child's own test: hold a full bottle and an empty one — same size, different mass — because the matter inside differs. Mass is not the same as size: a big balloon has less mass than a small stone, because the stone packs more matter into less space. And mass is not weight: weight is gravity's pull on the mass, which is why the course's gravity lesson measures pulls in newtons — mass itself, in kilograms, stays the same wherever the thing goes. The balance is the mass instrument: it compares the thing against known masses until the two sides level. Everything weighed in the science-skills lessons — every gram and kilogram there — is this lesson's idea at work.
