## Sub lesson 1 — Mass is how much matter

**Mass** is the amount of matter in a thing. A big stone has more matter in it than a small stone of the same rock, so it has more mass. Mass is measured in **grams** and **kilograms** with a balance.

Kuwait's Grade 2 book introduces mass beside volume in its Matter unit, with the child's own test: hold a full bottle and an empty one — same size, different mass — because the matter inside differs.

## Sub lesson 2 — Mass is not size, and not weight

Mass is not the same as size: a big balloon has less mass than a small stone, because the stone packs more matter into less space. And mass is not weight: weight is gravity's pull on the mass, which is why the course's gravity lesson measures pulls in newtons — mass itself, in kilograms, stays the same wherever the thing goes.

The balance is the mass instrument: it compares the thing against known masses until the two sides level. Everything weighed in the science-skills lessons — every gram and kilogram there — is this lesson's idea at work.

## The key ideas

- Mass is the amount of matter in a thing.
- Mass is measured in grams and kilograms with a balance.
- Mass is not size: a small stone can outweigh a big balloon.
- Mass is not weight: weight is gravity's pull on the mass.
