# Equations — balancing by inspection

## Do it first

Here is an unfinished equation: K + O₂ → K₂O. Count the potassiums and the oxygens on each side. They do not match. Try fixing them — you may put numbers in front, but you may not change any small subscript. Keep your attempt.

## Sub lesson 1 — Reading an equation

An equation puts the **reactants on the left**, the **products on the right**, and the arrow between them, read '**yields**'. It is balanced only when every element counts **equal on both sides** — conservation of mass, written down. Two rules govern all balancing: you may change **coefficients** (numbers in front), and you may **never change a subscript** — H₂O with its subscript changed is a different substance.

## Sub lesson 2 — Inspection, step by step

Inspection is trial and error with tallying. On **potassium + oxygen → potassium oxide**: Stage 1 — K + O₂ → K₂O tallies K 1 vs 2, O 2 vs 1. Stage 2 — fix oxygen: K + O₂ → 2K₂O tallies O 2 = 2, but K 1 vs 4. Stage 3 — fix potassium: **4K + O₂ → 2K₂O** tallies K 4 = 4, O 2 = 2 — balanced, and checked. Practise on carbon burning (C + O₂ → CO₂ — already balanced; prove it by tally) and magnesium in acid (Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂ — balanced as written; tally: Mg 1 = 1, H 2 = 2, Cl 2 = 2).

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words: balancing by inspection: adjust coefficients — never subscripts — by trial until every element counts equal on both sides of the equation. Your companion asks one question; answer it from the lesson, not from memory of anything else.
