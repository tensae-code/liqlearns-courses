# Video script — Inside the atom — protons, neutrons, electrons

The super-simple voice, for the video that rides on top of the lesson (lesson-plan.md, add-on list). Made later, when videos are made; this is a FULL script, because the placement names a video need: a short script on how microscopes let us image atoms.

---

Look at your hand. It is made of atoms.
No eye can see one atom. It is too small.
Even a school microscope cannot see one atom. Light itself is too coarse.

So how do we have pictures of atoms?

Three special microscopes do it — not with your eye, but with instruments.

TEM — the transmission electron microscope — fires electrons through a sample. The electrons that pass make the picture.

AFM — the atomic force microscope — feels the surface with a tiny tip, the way a finger feels bumps in the dark. Bump by bump, it builds the picture.

STM — the scanning tunnelling microscope — moves a fine point over the surface and measures a tiny electric signal at each place. Place by place, atom by atom, the picture grows.

So when you see a picture of atoms in a row, remember: no one *looked* at them.
An instrument touched them, or fired electrons through them — and a machine drew what it found.

That is how we know the inside of the atom: protons and neutrons in the middle, electrons around — found by evidence, not by eyes.
