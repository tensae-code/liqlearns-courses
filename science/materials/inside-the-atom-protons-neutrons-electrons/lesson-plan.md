# Lesson plan — Inside the atom — protons, neutrons, electrons

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Materials · **Lesson:** Inside the atom — protons, neutrons, electrons
**Place in the curriculum:** Materials — Grade 8, Unit 2 "Composition of Matter" (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** an atom holds protons and neutrons in a nucleus with electrons around it; Z counts the protons, A counts protons plus neutrons, and the amu is 1/12 of a carbon-12 atom

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The three particles — mass, charge and the atomic mass unit** — protons and neutrons sit in the nucleus, electrons move around it; the book's table fixes each particle's mass and charge; the atomic mass unit is 1/12 of a carbon-12 atom, so proton and neutron each weigh about 1 amu and the electron almost nothing
2. **Counting with Z and A** — atomic number Z counts protons; mass number A counts protons plus neutrons; neutrons = A − Z; the first ten elements worked as a table (the guide's hydrogen-to-neon key, teacher evidence, verifies the counts)
3. **Seeing atoms, and feeling their scale** — no eye and no ordinary microscope resolves an atom; modern microscopes — TEM, AFM, STM — are how atoms are imaged at all; a home model of lentils and peas makes the particles' scale tangible

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

- Recommended first: Materials — Early ideas of the atom — Democritus against Aristotle. Recommended, never forced; this lesson stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the three particles, where each sits, and its charge.
2. Give each particle's mass from the book's table, in grams and in amu.
3. Say what the atomic mass unit is: 1/12 of a carbon-12 atom.
4. Use Z and A to count protons, neutrons and electrons in an atom, for any of the first ten elements.
5. Name the three modern microscopes — TEM, AFM, STM — and say why atoms are 'seen' only through instruments like these.

## What you need
Lentils and peas (or two sizes of grain/seed) for the particle model; paper and a pencil.

## If the learner is stuck
Counts electrons from A instead of Z → in a neutral atom electrons equal protons, and protons are Z; A is protons plus neutrons. Thinks the electron's mass matters in A → at about 1/1836 of a proton it rounds to ~0 amu, which is why A counts only the nucleus.
