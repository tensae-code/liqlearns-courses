# Formulas — writing binary compounds (criss-cross)

## Do it first

You know calcium's valence is 2 and nitrogen's is 3. Try to write the formula of calcium nitride yourself — how many calciums and how many nitrogens make the valences balance? Keep your try; the method below will check it.

## Sub lesson 1 — The criss-cross steps

Write the symbols side by side, metal on the left. Write each element's valence above it. If the valences share a common factor, simplify them. Then cross: each element's valence drops down to become the subscript of the OTHER element. A subscript of 1 is never written. Worked in full: calcium (2) and nitrogen (3) share no factor, so the 3 drops under Ca and the 2 drops under N — **Ca₃N₂**. Sodium (1) and iodine (1) give **NaI**. Aluminium (3) and chlorine (1) give **AlCl₃**.

## Sub lesson 2 — Simplifying first, and checking

Magnesium (2) and oxygen (2) share the factor 2 — simplify to 1 and 1 before crossing, and the formula is **MgO**, never Mg₂O₂. Finish every formula with the check: atoms × valence must balance on both elements. Ca₃N₂: calcium 3 × 2 = 6, nitrogen 2 × 3 = 6 — balanced. MgO: 1 × 2 = 1 × 2 — balanced. AlCl₃: 1 × 3 = 3 × 1 — balanced.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words: the criss-cross method writes a binary compound's formula: write the symbols, put the valences on top, simplify, then cross each valence down as the other element's subscript. Your companion asks one question; answer it from the lesson, not from memory of anything else.
