# Lesson plan — Formulas — writing binary compounds (criss-cross)

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Materials · **Lesson:** Formulas — writing binary compounds (criss-cross)
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Elements, Compounds and Chemical Reactions (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** the criss-cross method writes a binary compound's formula: write the symbols, put the valences on top, simplify, then cross each valence down as the other element's subscript
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Formulas — valence, the combining power (same unit). The Chemistry title writes formulas again at secondary depth; this is the first-depth doing lesson, new here, cross-referenced not copied.

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **The criss-cross steps** — Write the symbols side by side, metal on the left.
2. **Simplifying first, and checking** — Magnesium (2) and oxygen (2) share the factor 2 — simplify to 1 and 1 before crossing, and the formula is **MgO**, never Mg₂O₂.

Gate pool: all questions of the sub lesson — no hardness ranking is written in questions/, so the sub lesson's whole question set is the gate pool.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier Unit 3 lessons in order — symbols, then formulas, then reactions. Recommended, never forced; this lesson stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. State the criss-cross steps in order.
2. Write Ca₃N₂, NaI, MgO and AlCl₃ by criss-cross.
3. Simplify valences with a common factor before crossing (MgO, not Mg₂O₂).
4. Check a finished formula by multiplying atoms × valence on both sides.

## If the learner is stuck
Crosses without simplifying → Mg (2) and O (2) share the factor 2: simplify to 1 and 1 first, giving MgO. Puts the metal second → the metal's symbol always goes on the left.
