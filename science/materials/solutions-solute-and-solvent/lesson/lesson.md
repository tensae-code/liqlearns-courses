## Sub lesson 1 — A solution — one thing dissolved evenly in another

When a soluble substance dissolves in a liquid, the result is a **solution**: one substance spread evenly through another, in pieces too small to see, so the whole liquid looks the same in every sip and every drop. Salt water and sweet tea are solutions — clear, even, and with nothing settling at the bottom.

## Sub lesson 2 — The two names — solute and solvent

Every solution has two parts with two names. The substance that **dissolves** — the salt, the sugar — is the **solute**. The liquid it dissolves **in** — the water — is the **solvent**. Solute plus solvent makes the solution: name any solution and you can name its pair. Water is the commonest solvent, but not the only one — other liquids dissolve things too, and then they are the solvent of that solution.

## The key ideas

- A **solution** is one substance dissolved evenly in another — the same in every drop, with nothing settling out.
- The **solute** is the substance that dissolves; the **solvent** is the liquid it dissolves in.
- **Solute + solvent = solution** — every solution can be taken apart into its named pair.

## Say it to your companion
Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words, in the lesson's own order. Your companion may stop you once and ask: "In a glass of salt water, which is the solute and which is the solvent?" Answer from the lesson: The salt is the solute — it is the substance that dissolved. The water is the solvent — it is the liquid the salt dissolved in. Together they are the solution.
