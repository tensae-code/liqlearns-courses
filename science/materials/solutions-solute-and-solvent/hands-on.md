# Hands-on — Solutions — solute and solvent

The lesson with your hands and your feet, not only your eyes.

## Name the pair
Dissolve a spoon of salt in one glass of water and a spoon of sugar in another. Hold each glass to the light: a true solution is even and clear, with nothing sinking. For each glass, write the sentence: 'The solute is ___; the solvent is ___; the solution is ___.' Then stir a spoon of sand into a third glass and say why it is not a solution and has no solute.

## How much solute can the solvent hold?
Add salt to a glass of water one spoon at a time, stirring well after each spoon, and count the spoons. When a spoon's salt stops dissolving and stays at the bottom, the solution can hold no more — note the count. Say in the lesson's words what the water was, what the salt was, and what changed when no more would dissolve.
