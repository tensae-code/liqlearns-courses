# Simple machines

## Do it first
Your muscle has a limit. A plank under a stone does not. People multiply their strength with six simple inventions — and this lesson names them all, in the book's order, with the one honest limit: no machine multiplies work.

## What simple machines do; levers
Human muscle is limited, so people multiply it: simple machines **multiply force, multiply speed/distance, or change force's direction** — but they **cannot multiply work/energy**. The six kinds: **lever, pulley, inclined plane, wheel and axle, gears, and screw (with the wedge)**. A **lever (መፈንቅል)** is a bar of wood or metal turning on a support; its class is set by where support, effort and load sit: **first class** — support between effort and load; **second class** — load between support and effort; **third class** — effort between load and support. Pupils classify **scissors, a nail-pulling hammer, a bottle opener and a cart**.

## Pulleys, the inclined plane, the wedge and the screw
The **pulley (በከራ)** raises and lowers building materials and loads — hoist a beehive up a tree, materials up a building. Three kinds: **fixed** (the wheel stays, the rope moves — it changes direction), **movable** (the wheel travels with the load) and **combined**. The **inclined plane** — a sloping board (ዘንባይ ጣውላ) — lets a small force move a heavy load up to a high place, trading distance for effort: a barrel rolled up a plank instead of lifted straight. The **wedge (ሽብልቅ)** is **two inclined planes joined at a sharp edge**; it splits things apart — axe, knife and the splitting wedge. The **screw (ቡለን/ቅርንፉድ)** is a shaft wrapped in a spiral thread — tiny inclined planes wound round; turned, it drives itself in — a drill bit, the screws in your own chair, a car's jack.

## Wheel and axle; gears (teacher guide — evidence)
**Wheel and axle**: a large wheel turning on a smaller central axle — the long rod through the wheel's middle that lets it spin. The worked example: the **well-head roller and rope** for drawing water easily. **Gears**: toothed wheels (ጥርሳ-ጥርሶች) of different sizes mesh to build machines; where teeth meet, equal numbers of teeth pass, so **a small-toothed disc spins faster than a large-toothed one** — a car's gears, a bicycle's pedal gear. The Grade 6 teacher guide closes its science unit with the same family (teacher guide — evidence): lever, pulley, inclined plane and wheel, with the lever lifting a heavy stone with a small push.

## Say it to your companion
Name the six kinds for your companion, the three lever classes in order, the three pulleys, and the honest limit — no machine multiplies work.

## Do It Yourself
1. Classify the tools: scissors, nail-pulling hammer, bottle opener, a cart — which lever class is each, by support/effort/load order? Defend each placing.
2. Fixed pulley build: the book's own — build a fixed pulley from local materials, hoist a load, and report how it changes the force's direction.
3. Ramp against lift: roll a barrel up a plank into a cart, then judge — where did the saved force go? (Into the longer road — distance traded for effort.)
