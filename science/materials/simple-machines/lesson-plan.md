# Lesson plan — Simple machines

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Materials · **Lesson:** Simple machines
**Place in the curriculum:** Materials — multiplying muscle, Grade 5 depth (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** simple machines multiply force, multiply speed/distance, or change force's direction — but never multiply work; the six kinds are the lever (three classes by where support, effort and load sit), pulley (fixed, movable, combined), inclined plane, wedge, screw and wheel-and-axle, with gears meshing toothed wheels of different sizes
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Force, friction and gravity — force is known; now the tools that multiply it.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Force, friction and gravity. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what simple machines do — and the one thing they can never do (multiply work/energy).
2. Classify levers into their three classes by the order of support, effort and load; and pulleys into fixed, movable and combined.
3. Explain the inclined plane, the wedge (two inclined planes), the screw (a wrapped slope), wheel and axle, and gears — each with the book's example.

## What you need
A plank and a log for a lever, a stone load; a rope over a beam for a fixed pulley; a barrel and a plank for the inclined plane; an axe or knife (adult-handled) for the wedge.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks a machine creates strength from nothing → machines multiply force but never work; the inclined plane pays for its small force with a long road.
- Calls every lever the same → class is set by ORDER: support, effort and load — check which of the three stands in the middle.
