# Lesson plan — Reactions — five signs to look for

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Materials · **Lesson:** Reactions — five signs to look for
**Place in the curriculum:** Unit 3 — Elements, Compounds and Chemical Reactions (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** five signs suggest a reaction happened — colour change, a solid forming, gas forming, texture change, energy change — but no single sign alone proves it
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Reactions — reactants become products (same unit). The Chemistry title hunts reaction evidence again at secondary depth; this is the first-depth lesson, new here, cross-referenced not copied. Its fizz GIF is shared with the teacher-guide lesson Reactions — seven kinds of reaction around the home (the volcano).

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Sub lessons
This lesson is one whole lesson — it has no sub lessons (a short, single-skill lesson; his ruling 2026-11-09).

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: the earlier Unit 3 lessons in order — symbols, then formulas, then reactions. Recommended, never forced; this lesson stands alone for a learner landing on it directly.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name the five signs of a possible reaction.
2. Describe Experiment 3.2 and its three observed signs.
3. Explain why one sign alone does not prove a reaction.
4. Judge a described change: reaction or not, with reasons.

## What you need
Paper and a pencil. The soda-vinegar test can be done safely in a kitchen cup with a teacher or parent.

## If the learner is stuck
Calls boiling water's bubbles a reaction → boiling is a state change; the gas sign means gas FORMED by a reaction, as in the soda-vinegar fizz. Spots one sign and stops → ask for the new substance, and hunt a second sign.
