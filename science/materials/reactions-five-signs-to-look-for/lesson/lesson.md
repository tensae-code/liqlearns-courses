# Reactions — five signs to look for

## Do it first

Baking soda meets vinegar in a cup. Predict three things you might see, feel or smell. Write them down — then read what Experiment 3.2 actually found.

## Experiment 3.2 — soda and vinegar

Baking soda and vinegar fizz violently. A smell is fanned carefully to the nose. The cup feels cooler afterwards. Three signs in one cup: gas forming, a new smell, a temperature change.

## The five signs

1. **Colour change**. 2. **A solid forms** where two solutions meet. 3. **Gas forms** — bubbles or fizz not caused by boiling. 4. **Texture or hardness changes** — dough becoming baked bread. 5. **Energy change** — heat or light given out, or heat taken in (the cup cooling).

## The caution

Any ONE sign alone does not prove a reaction. Bubbles can be mere boiling; a colour can be mere mixing of paints. Look for signs together, and ask the real question: has a NEW substance formed?

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words: five signs suggest a reaction happened — colour change, a solid forming, gas forming, texture change, energy change — but no single sign alone proves it. Your companion asks one question; answer it from the lesson, not from memory of anything else.
