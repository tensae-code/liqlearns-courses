# Lesson plan — Properties that identify a thing

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Materials · **Lesson:** Properties that identify a thing
**Place in the curriculum:** Materials — after Describing things: the properties, now studied as the identity card of each material (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** every material has properties — colour, length, hardness, shape and more — and the properties are how we tell one material from another
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Describing things — the senses told things apart; now the properties are named and listed, the Grade 4 book's way, with a pencil as the worked example.

A lesson plan also says **what goes on top of the lesson**: at the exact places marked below, the lesson page carries an add-on — an image, a GIF, a video, an audio. Every slot is written as **"a picture of …" in words, plus one little line saying what the student does with it**. The words carry the picture while the course is built and while the AI learns from the books; on the website the real add-on is dropped into that slot and the little explanation stays as the student's guide. Images follow the picture rule (a real photograph, or real art, with its source). GIFs come first where a thing must be shown moving or popping — a GIF here is 1–2 seconds.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Materials — Describing things: matter and its properties. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what a property is: a mark by which one material is known from another.
2. List properties of one thing — the book's pencil, with its eight properties.
3. Tell physical properties (seen and measured) from chemical properties (seen when a thing changes into a new thing), with the teacher guides' evidence.

## What you need
A pencil — the book's own worked example — and two or three other small things to describe.

## If the learner is stuck
- Lists what a thing is FOR instead of its properties → a use is not a property; colour, length, hardness and shape are.
- Calls burning a physical property → burning makes a new thing (ash); that makes it chemical, the guides' contrast.
