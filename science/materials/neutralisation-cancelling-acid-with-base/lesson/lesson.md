# Neutralisation — cancelling acid with base

## Do it first

A farmer's soil tests at pH 5. His potatoes want 5.5–6.5; his beans want it higher still. He has ash, limestone and quicklime in the store. Before reading: what is he really doing, chemically, when he spreads one of them?

## Sub lesson 1 — The reaction

When an **acid** and a **base** meet in the right amounts, they **cancel** each other: **acid + base → salt + water**. The bench case: **NaOH + HCl → NaCl + H₂O** — sodium hydroxide and hydrochloric acid give common salt and water, and the mixture tests **neutral**. Neither side survives as acid or base; what is left is a salt dissolved in water.

## Sub lesson 2 — What neutralisation is for — farm, water, and home (teacher guide — evidence)

**Farming:** acid soils are lifted toward a crop's pH with **ash, limestone or quicklime** — the printed crop table guides the dose (potatoes 5.5–6.5; oats and beans with their own ranges); the guide's soil project measures sample pH and neutralises the acidic sample with powdered lime, limestone or wood ash. **Water:** **wastewater** is treated by cancelling its acid or base before release. **At home and in health (teacher guide):** the guide sorts applications into **home, health and agriculture** — its everyday example: **shampoo is basic and conditioner acidic**, so the conditioner neutralises the shampoo's effect on hair.
