# Non-Newtonian fluids — neither solid nor liquid

## Sub lesson 1 — What a solid does, and what a liquid does

A **solid** keeps its own shape: you can hold it, and it stays as it is. A **liquid** does not keep a shape: it pours, flows, and takes the shape of whatever holds it. Those two behaviours are the test this lesson uses — before meeting anything strange, hold the two clear cases: solid keeps shape, liquid flows.

## Sub lesson 2 — The dough that is neither

Indonesia's Grade 3 book (Bab 8, Rahasia Tiga Wujud Zat — the secret of the three states of matter) sets a challenge its states chapter cannot settle with the two clear cases: a **soil/clay dough** that is **neither solid nor liquid**. It can be worked and shaped like something solid, yet it does not behave as a true solid does; it gives and spreads, yet it does not pour as a true liquid does. Matter like this is called **non-Newtonian**: its behaviour does not stay on one side of the solid/liquid line.

## Sub lesson 3 — Testing it against both

The book's own way is a test, not a label: the dough is **tested against the properties of both** — held against what a solid must do and against what a liquid must do, property by property. Where it passes a solid's test it looks solid; where it fails, and where it passes and fails a liquid's tests the same mixed way, the verdict is the lesson: some matter is honestly **neither**, and the test is how you know.
