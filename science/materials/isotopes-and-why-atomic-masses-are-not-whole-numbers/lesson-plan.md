# Lesson plan — Isotopes — and why atomic masses are not whole numbers

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Materials · **Lesson:** Isotopes — and why atomic masses are not whole numbers
**Place in the curriculum:** Materials — Grade 8, Unit 2 "Composition of Matter" (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** atoms of the same element can carry different numbers of neutrons — isotopes — and an element's printed atomic mass is the weighted average of its isotopes, which is why it is not a whole number

## Sub lessons
The parts of this lesson, in order — the list the learner sees from the start, locked ahead, each opening when the one before it is proved (the gate: README, "Units, Lessons and Sub Lessons (ruled 2026-11-08)"):

1. **Isotopes — same element, different neutrons** — atoms of one element share Z but can differ in neutrons and so in A; hydrogen's three — protium, deuterium, tritium — worked as nuclei differing only in neutrons
2. **Average atomic mass — the weighted mean** — an element's printed atomic mass averages its isotopes by how common each one is; magnesium's three isotopes average to 24.31 amu and carbon's to 12.01 amu in the book's fully worked calculations

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Define an isotope: same element (same Z), different neutrons, different A.
2. Name hydrogen's three isotopes and give each one's neutron count: protium 0, deuterium 1, tritium 2.
3. Say how an average atomic mass is worked out: each isotope's mass weighted by how common it is, then averaged.
4. Give the book's two worked results: magnesium 24.31 amu, carbon 12.01 amu.
5. Explain in one sentence why atomic masses are not whole numbers.

## If the learner is stuck
Thinks a different A means a different element → the element is fixed by Z, the protons; isotopes keep Z and change only the neutrons. Averages the isotope masses plainly (24+25+26)/3 → the average is weighted: common isotopes count more, which is why magnesium lands at 24.31, near 24, not at 25.
