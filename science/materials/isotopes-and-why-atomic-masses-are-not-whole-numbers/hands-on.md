# Hands-on — Isotopes — and why atomic masses are not whole numbers

The lesson with your hands, not only your eyes.

## Three hydrogens in grains
With lentils (protons) and peas (neutrons): build protium (1 lentil, 0 peas), deuterium (1 lentil, 1 pea), tritium (1 lentil, 2 peas). Same lentil count every time — that is why all three are still hydrogen. Then build magnesium's three isotopes the same way: 12 lentils each time, with 12, 13 and 14 peas.

## The market average
A seller's basket holds 10 oranges: 8 weigh about 100 g and 2 weigh about 150 g. The plain average of 100 and 150 is 125 g — but the basket's true average is (8×100 + 2×150) / 10 = 110 g, because the light ones are more common. That weighting — common ones count more — is exactly how an average atomic mass is worked out.
