# Lesson plan — Sound and vibration

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Materials · **Lesson:** Sound and vibration
**Place in the curriculum:** Materials — sound, an energy that must be felt first (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** sound is energy made when bodies vibrate; it travels only through matter, never through a vacuum; Experiment 5 finds what vibrates in six Ethiopian instruments; and sound travels fastest in solids, then liquids, slowest in gases — about 331.5 m/s in air at 0 °C, faster as air warms
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Force, friction and gravity — bodies act on bodies; now one action makes the air itself sing.

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: Force, friction and gravity. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say what sound is — energy from vibration — and why it cannot cross a vacuum.
2. Run Experiment 5's table: what vibrates in a marakesh, drum, washint, krar, masinqo and trumpet; and in the voice.
3. Order sound's speeds: solids fastest, liquids next, gases slowest — with air's figure, 331.5 m/s at 0 °C.

## What you need
The instruments if the school has them (or pictures and a rubber band); a ticking clock, a waterproof bag and a bucket of water for the book's activities.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks sound could cross empty space like light → sound needs matter to carry it; a vacuum (ወና) stops it dead.
- Thinks loudness is a different thing from vibration → the throat test ties them: louder humming, faster/stronger vibration, felt in the fingers.
