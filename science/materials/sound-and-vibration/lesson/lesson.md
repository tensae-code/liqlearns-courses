# Sound and vibration

## Do it first
Hum, and put your hand on your throat. That buzz under your fingers is the whole lesson in one touch: sound is vibration, travelling. This lesson proves it on six instruments — and races it through steel, water and air.

## Sound is vibration, travelling through matter
**Sound is energy made when bodies vibrate** and suddenly stir the air. It **travels only through matter — never through a vacuum (ወና)**. It carries messages, expresses needs and calls to others. The Grade 5 teacher guide's sound subtopic (teacher guide — evidence) pictures the same truth: a drum skin vibrating as it is struck.

## Experiment 5 — finding the vibration
Pupils produce sound from six Ethiopian instruments and tabulate **what vibrates in each**: a **marakesh (ማራከሽ)**, a **drum** (the skin vibrates — the book gives this one as the worked answer), a **washint flute**, a **krar**, a **masinqo** and a **trumpet**. A second test puts a hand on the throat while humming from low to loud — **the voice is the throat's vibration**, and loudness rises and falls with the speed of vibration.

## The speed of sound
Sound moves through air, water and solids at different speeds — **fastest in solids, then liquids, slowest in gases**. In air at **0 °C** it travels about **331.5 metres per second**, and it **speeds up as air warms** because the particles vibrate more. The book's activities: a ticking clock sealed in a waterproof plastic bag inside a bucket of water — heard through the water — and frogs heard croaking under water.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: sound is vibration; it needs matter and dies in a vacuum; what vibrates in the drum and in your own throat; and the speed order — solids, liquids, gases.

## Do It Yourself
1. The rubber-band twang: stretch a band over a box, twang it, and watch it blur. Stop it with a finger — the sound stops with the vibration. The lesson in ten seconds.
2. The clock in the bucket: the book's activity — a ticking clock in a waterproof bag, sunk in water, ear to the bucket. Sound through water, heard.
3. Rail listening (a story to reason about, not to try on a live railway): why does an ear near a steel rail hear a far train before the air brings its sound? (Sound runs fastest in solids.)
