# Lesson plan — Materials around us — metal, wood and what things are made of

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Materials · **Lesson:** Materials around us — metal, wood and what things are made of
**Place in the curriculum:** Materials (CURRICULUM.md Unit 4), opening the Gabon 5e année materials thread at its first lesson: before properties can be tested, the learner must be able to look at any object and say what it is made of
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** every object around us is made of one or more materials — metal, wood, glass, plastic, fabric, clay, stone, rubber, paper — and we identify an object's materials by looking, touching and testing (does a magnet take it? does it ring, bend, soak water?); things are made of the materials they are made of for reasons: the material fits the job the object does
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on its own — a learner can land here directly. Describing things: matter and its properties is recommended first, never forced

## Sub lessons
1. **Naming the materials — metal, wood, glass and the rest** — the everyday materials each have their family marks: metals are shiny, cold-feeling, hard and ring when struck, and some are taken by a magnet; wood shows grain, is warm-feeling and floats; glass is smooth, hard, see-through and breaks sharp; plastics are light, smooth and bendy; fabric is woven threads; clay and stone are earthy and heavy; rubber stretches; paper is thin, light and tears
2. **One object, several materials — and why** — most objects are assemblies of materials, each part made of the material that fits its job: a knife is steel for the cutting blade and wood or plastic for the held handle; a bicycle is metal frame, rubber tyres, plastic-and-leather seat; naming an object's materials part by part is the full answer to 'what is it made of?', and the reasons are always the materials' properties at work

## Before this lesson
- Recommended first: **Describing things: matter and its properties**. Recommended, never forced.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Say, in their own words: Every object is made of materials — metal, wood, glass, plastic, fabric, clay, stone, rubber, paper — and the object is not the material: a spoon's metal could have been a coin.
2. Say, in their own words: Materials are identified by their marks: metal cold, shiny, ringing (iron/steel magnetic); wood warm with grain; glass transparent and sharp-breaking; plastic light and bendy; rubber stretchy.
3. Say, in their own words: Most objects use several materials, part by part — knife: steel blade, wooden handle.
4. Say, in their own words: Each part's material is chosen for the job that part does — the properties lessons explain the choices.
5. Answer the lesson's questions from the lesson text alone.

## What you need
A basket of everyday objects — a nail, a spoon, a wooden ruler, a plastic cup, a glass, a cloth, a rubber band, a paper sheet, a clay pot piece — and a magnet.

## If the learner is stuck
- Cannot start → return to Do it first: the nearest object. One question — what is it made of? — and the marks on it that answer.
- Answers with the object's name instead of its material → 'a spoon' is the object; the material is the metal. Objects are made; materials are what they are made from.
- Thinks all metals are the same → the magnet divides them (iron and steel taken, aluminium and copper not), and their marks differ — metals are a family, not a single stuff.
