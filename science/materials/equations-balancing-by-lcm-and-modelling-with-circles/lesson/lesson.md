# Equations — balancing by LCM, and modelling with circles

## Do it first

Aluminium burns in oxygen: Al + O₂ → Al₂O₃. Inspection will get there slowly. Before reading the faster method, tally this skeleton: Al 1 vs 2, O 2 vs 3. Keep the tally — the LCM method starts from numbers like these.

## Sub lesson 1 — The LCM method

Total each element's valence numbers across the skeleton equation, take their **least common multiple**, then divide the LCM back to win the coefficients. Worked on aluminium + oxygen → aluminium oxide: the book's working takes the LCM of the valence totals as **12**; dividing back gives 4 aluminium atoms, 3 oxygen molecules and 2 aluminium oxide units — **4Al + 3O₂ → 2Al₂O₃**. Then CHECK, always: Al 4 = 4; O: left 3 × 2 = 6, right 2 × 3 = 6. Balanced.

## Sub lesson 2 — Modelling balance with circles

Coloured circles turn the counting into something your hands can do. Build hydrogen + oxygen → water physically: H–H pairs and an O–O pair on the left of an arrow; water molecules — one O with two H — on the right. Add **coefficients** — whole extra groups — until the colours match on both sides: **2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O**, 4 H and 2 O each side. The rule never changes: coefficients move, subscripts do not. The rearrangement itself is conservation of mass made visible — every circle that starts the reaction ends it.

## Say it to your companion

Explain this lesson to your companion in your own words: the LCM method balances by least common multiple of the valence totals, and coloured-circle models make the same balance visible — coefficients change, subscripts never do. Your companion asks one question; answer it from the lesson, not from memory of anything else.
