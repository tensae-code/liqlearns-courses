# Separating mixtures — settling, pouring off and filtering

## Do it first
Shake soil into water. Mud. You cannot drink it, and pouring it through your fingers will not clean it. This lesson cleans it in three patient stages — settle, pour off, filter — the same stages that wash lentils and peas in every kitchen.

## Sub lesson 1 — Experiment 2.7 — sedimentation and decantation
**Aim:** to clean muddy water by sedimentation and decantation (Experiment 2.7); then to filter an insoluble solid from water (Experiment 2.8)
**Materials:** muddy water in a clear glass or jar; filter paper (or clean folded cloth), a funnel, a second glass; chalk powder and water for Experiment 2.8
**Steps:**
1. Stir soil into water in a clear glass and leave it standing, undisturbed.
2. Watch the solid settle to the bottom — sedimentation.
3. Tilt the glass slowly and pour the clear upper water into a second glass, leaving the sediment behind — decantation.
4. For Experiment 2.8: fold filter paper into the funnel and set it over a clean glass.
5. Stir chalk powder into water and pour the mixture through the paper, a little at a time.

## Sub lesson 2 — What you observe, and what it proves
**Observation:** Left standing, the mud settles and the upper water clears; poured off gently, it leaves the sediment behind. Through the paper, the chalk stays on the paper as the residue and clear water passes through as the filtrate. Real water filters work the same way, trapping particles down to the size of bacteria.

## Say it to your companion
Walk your companion through the three stages with a real glass of muddy water beside you. Name each stage as you point at it: sedimentation, decantation, filtration.

## Do It Yourself
1. Settle a glass of muddy water overnight. Pour off the clear layer without disturbing the sediment.
2. Filter the poured-off water through folded cloth or paper. Compare all three glasses.
3. Wash lentils the kitchen way and name the stages you used.
