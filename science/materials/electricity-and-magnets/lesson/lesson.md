# Electricity and magnets

## Do it first
Click — light. Click — dark. Between the two clicks stands a whole hidden system: a source, a loop, and a switch that opens or closes it. This lesson opens that system — and its twin wonder, the magnet.

## Sources of electricity; current and the closed circuit
Electric energy runs lights, radio, television, electric stoves and machines. Its sources are **dry cells and generators** — generators driven by water, fuel, wind or steam — and both work by **converting other kinds of energy into electric energy**; the **GERD** is named as Ethiopia's great hydro source. A torch's light comes from the chemical reaction inside its dry cell. **Current** arises when there is a **charge (ሙል) difference** between a conductor's two ends; by convention current flows positive → negative while the negative charges themselves travel negative → positive. Current needs a **closed circuit**: the closed line joining the **battery (source), bulb, connecting wires and switch**. The book pictures the circuit symbols and sets a symbol-drawn circuit to decode part by part.

## Magnets — what they pull; poles and their law
A magnet attracts iron-like things. The **magnetic materials are steel, iron, nickel, cobalt and their alloys**; **Experiment 9** sorts ten test materials — nickel and plastic are given as worked answers (magnetic / non-magnetic), and a pin, paper, a nail, a clip, cloth, aluminium, iron and wood are left to test. Magnets may be **natural (lodestone; the Earth's own magnetism)** or **man-made**, in bar, U, horseshoe, round and cylindrical shapes. Every magnet has exactly **two poles, north and south**, and the law is: **like poles repel, unlike poles attract**. **Experiment 10** rolls a magnet in iron filings: the filings cluster thickest at the poles. **Experiment 11** hangs a magnet by a thread and lets it settle, again and again: it always comes to rest pointing the same, **north–south**, way.

## Experiment 13 — a current makes a field; Experiment 12 — the motor
**Experiment 13**: a current-carrying straight wire has a **magnetic field** around it — pupils switch a wire circuit on and off beside a compass needle and watch the needle deflect. **Experiment 12** builds a simple **electric motor**: insulated wire wound many turns around a paper tube, the coil taped with its ends bent long as an axle and stripped bare, steel-clip supports on a wooden board, a ring magnet's north pole beside the coil — close the switch and the coil turns. The principle: a **motor converts electric energy into mechanical** energy and a **generator does the reverse**. Motors live in mills, tape recorders, factories and industries.

## Say it to your companion
Tell your companion: the two sources and what they convert; the circuit's four parts and why the loop must close; the magnetic materials; the law of poles; and motor against generator.

## Do It Yourself
1. Experiment 9 sort: test the book's materials with a magnet — pin, paper, nail, clip, cloth, aluminium, iron, wood — and build the two lists. Nickel and plastic are the worked answers to check your method against.
2. Open and close: build the dry-cell circuit with an adult. Open the switch — the bulb dies. The gap is the whole idea; draw your circuit again in symbols, as the book sets.
3. The hanging magnet: hang a bar magnet by a thread (Experiment 11). Mark where north settles. Swing it and let it settle three times — does it keep its word?
