# Lesson plan — Electricity and magnets

**Course:** Science · **Title:** Materials · **Lesson:** Electricity and magnets
**Place in the curriculum:** Materials — charge, circuits and magnets, Grade 6 depth (CURRICULUM.md).
**The one thing this lesson teaches:** electric energy comes from dry cells and generators converting other energies; current needs a charge difference and a closed circuit; magnets pull iron, steel, nickel, cobalt and their alloys, every magnet has exactly two poles (like repel, unlike attract), a hung magnet settles north–south, a current makes a magnetic field — and a motor converts electric energy into mechanical energy while a generator does the reverse
**This lesson's own level:** it stands on Heat and how it travels — energies are being followed one by one; now the one that runs the modern house.

## What the learner can do when it ends
1. Name electricity's sources and what both do: convert other kinds of energy into electric energy (the GERD named as Ethiopia's great hydro source).
2. Explain current and the closed circuit — source, bulb, wires, switch — and decode a circuit drawn in symbols.
3. State the magnet rules: what magnets pull, the two poles and their law, the filings and hanging-magnet experiments, Experiment 13's current-makes-a-field, and the motor/generator pair.

## What you need
A dry cell, bulb, wires and a switch for the circuit (adult-supervised); a bar magnet, a pin, paper, a nail, a clip, cloth, aluminium, iron and wood for Experiment 9's sort; a compass if the class has one.

## If the learner is stuck
- Thinks any metal is magnetic → aluminium is the trap in Experiment 9's list: a metal, but not magnetic. The magnetic family is steel, iron, nickel, cobalt and their alloys.
- Thinks a magnet has one strong end → every magnet has exactly two poles, and the filings of Experiment 10 crowd at BOTH.
