Today: **Fluids and superfluids** — the Dominican Republic's way: what a fluid is.

Solids hold their own shape. A fluid does the opposite: it flows — it pours, spreads and takes the shape of whatever holds it. Both liquids and gases are fluids: water pours, and air flows from place to place, even though only one of them can be seen flowing. The test is flowing, not wetness. If a material can flow and take its container's shape, it is a fluid — that is the Grade 6 book's sorting, placed right after the states of matter it builds on. Ordinary fluids resist their own flowing a little — honey pours slowly, even water drags against the sides of its vessel. That inner drag is part of every everyday fluid. A superfluid is a fluid with that drag gone: it flows without friction, pouring and moving as if nothing held it back at all. The book names superfluids as the fluids' far edge — the same flowing idea, carried to the point where the resistance disappears.
