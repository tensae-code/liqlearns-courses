# Oxides, acids, bases and salts

## Do it first
A lemon bites your tongue. Ash-water slips bitter between your fingers. Between those two home tastes stands a whole family of chemistry — and this lesson maps it, with its danger signs posted plainly.

## Oxides — four characters
An **oxide** must contain **oxygen**: it forms when an element joins oxygen. Oxides are binary compounds of **four characters**. **Basic oxides** give bases with water (Na₂O, CaO). **Acidic oxides** give acids with water (CO₂, SO₃). **Amphoteric oxides** carry both characters (Al₂O₃, ZnO). **Neutral oxides** carry neither (H₂O, CO).

## Acids and bases
An acidic oxide plus water makes an **acid** — CO₂ + H₂O → H₂CO₃, carbonic acid. Acids taste **sour** — lemon, orange and the sour "komptate" fruits — and turn **blue indicator paper red**. A basic (metal) oxide plus water makes a **base** — MgO + H₂O → Mg(OH)₂; NaOH is also named. Bases taste **bitter** and turn **red indicator paper blue** — with the book's printed warning: **tasting bases is dangerous**. At home the words map on: "komptate" (sour) things are acids, "merar" (bitter) things are bases. Industry uses them for textiles, plastics, fuel refining, cleaning materials, fertiliser and medicine; at home — fruit acid as food, sulfuric acid in car batteries, ash for cleaning and for fertilising soil, caustic soda and ammonia for cleaning. **Mineral acids** (sulfuric, hydrochloric, nitric) are **strong**; **organic acids** (lemon juice, vinegar, lactic) are **weak**.

## Salts, neutralisation — and Experiment 1
**Acid + base gives salt plus water.** Because the products are neither acid nor base, the reaction is named **neutralisation (አግልሎት)**. The book's **Experiment 1** compares an acid and a base at the bench: **vinegar, lemon juice and filtered ash water** are tested with indicator paper, with a battery-and-bulb circuit for conductivity when dissolved, for sour versus bitter behaviour, for neutralisation, and against an active metal (a magnesium strip). **Safety rule, printed in the book: if a strong acid or base spills on body or clothes, wash at once with plenty of clean water — and never taste or touch them.**

## Say it to your companion
Walk your companion down the family: oxide → acid or base → salt, with one example at each step, the indicator colours, and the safety rule said last and loudest.

## Do It Yourself
1. Home shelf sort: lemon, vinegar, ash, soap — the lesson's own shelf. Tag each acid or base, using the book's sour/bitter words, komptate and merar.
2. Indicator drawing: draw the two paper tests — blue paper in acid, red paper in base — with the colour each turns.
3. Neutralisation story: an acid and a base meet and both disappear into salt and water. Write the meeting in the book's equation words: acid + base → salt + water.
